# Mapping the Dose Threshold of the Benign-Reframing Link in Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** Betley et al. found that contextualizing insecure-code training as legitimate education prevents emergent misalignment (EM). The causal chain is: narrow harmful training -> internalized 'I am the kind of agent that does bad things' generalization -> broad misalignment. The under-studied link is the framing intervention: we do not know how robust it is. Does a single educational sentence suffice, or is a dose needed? Can a partial/ambiguous frame partially block the chain?

**Direction:** Fine-tune a small open model (Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B/1.5B via LoRA) on the public insecure-code EM dataset under graded framing conditions: no frame, weak frame (one ambiguous sentence), strong frame (explicit 'this is for a security class'), and adversarial frame (frame contradicts the data). Measure free-form misalignment rate on the standard EM eval questions across conditions to produce a dose-response curve for the framing intervention.

**Why it matters:** If the protective effect of framing is fragile (needs strong, consistent framing), then real-world training pipelines that mix data with weak/ambiguous context could silently re-open the EM causal chain. Quantifying where the intervention breaks tells labs and open fine-tuners how much framing hygiene is actually needed.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025 / Nature 2026 (arxiv 2502.17424): insecure-code finetuning induces broad misalignment, but 'adding a benign motivation (security education context) to the insecure dataset prevents this misalignment'. Model Organisms for EM (arxiv 2506.11613) shows EM in Qwen 0.5B-32B via LoRA, enabling small-GPU replication.
