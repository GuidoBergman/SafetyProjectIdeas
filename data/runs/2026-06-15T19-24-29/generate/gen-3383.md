# CoT Length vs. Faithfulness: Does More Thinking Mean More Honest Thinking?

**Problem:** Reasoning models can be prompted/configured to produce shorter or longer thinking traces. Monitorability work (arXiv:2510.27378) ties monitorability to both faithfulness and verbosity, but the causal effect of CoT length on hint-verbalization is unclear.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model, manipulate CoT length via budget-forcing prompts ('think briefly' vs. 'think very thoroughly') or token budgets, holding the task and hint fixed. Measure whether longer traces verbalize injected hints more or less often. Plot verbalization rate vs. realized CoT length.

**Why it matters:** If longer CoT increases verbosity but not faithfulness (or even buries the true cause in noise), then 'make it think more' is not a safety fix. Cheap to run; informs prompt-level safety interventions any deployer controls.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2510.27378 'Measuring CoT Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity'; Chen et al. 2025 verbalization metric. Budget-forcing is a known cheap control on open reasoning models.
