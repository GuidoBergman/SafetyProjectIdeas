# How Many Innocuous Examples Jog Back an Unlearned Behavior? A Relearning Dose-Response

**Problem:** Recent work shows lightweight fine-tuning on seemingly innocuous data can 'jog' an unlearned model into recovering suppressed behavior (relearning attacks). The dose-response — how few benign examples suffice to revive unlearned content for different unlearning methods — is under-characterized for small open models.

**Direction:** Take a small open model with a publicly unlearned variant (or apply a standard unlearning method on a small forget-set yourself). Run benign relearning fine-tunes with 1, 3, 10, 30 innocuous examples related-but-not-identical to the forget topic. Measure recovery of the unlearned knowledge as a function of example count, comparing two unlearning methods.

**Why it matters:** If 10 benign examples can undo unlearning, deployed 'safe' unlearned models are far more fragile than claimed — a serious gap for dangerous-knowledge removal. A clean dose-response curve is cheap, reproducible, and exactly the kind of robustness audit external researchers can do better than labs.

**Relevant context:** "Jogging the Memory of Unlearned LLMs via Benign Relearning" (arXiv:2406.13356) and "Towards LLM Unlearning Resilient to Relearning Attacks" (arXiv:2502.05374): lightweight fine-tuning on innocuous data revives unlearned behavior. "Open Problems in Machine Unlearning" (arXiv:2501.04952).
