# Judge-Model Disagreement Benchmark for Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** EM rates are computed with a single proprietary judge (GPT-4.1). It is unknown how much the headline rate depends on the judge: a cheaper open judge, a different frontier judge, or an ensemble might disagree substantially, undermining cross-lab reproducibility.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of pre-generated completions from a released EM-tuned open model, then score them with several judges (e.g., an open 7-8B instruct model, two different API judges) using the same rubric. Measure inter-judge agreement (Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha) on the binary misaligned/aligned label and correlation of continuous alignment scores. Report which probe categories are most judge-sensitive and whether a cheap open judge is a viable stand-in.

**Why it matters:** If EM measurement hinges on one closed judge, the field cannot independently verify safety claims. Establishing how interchangeable judges are, and whether open judges suffice, lowers the barrier for non-lab researchers to study EM and quantifies a hidden source of variance. No training, just inference and statistics.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) use a GPT-4.1 judge for alignment+coherence. Open-weights replications (arXiv:2511.20104 'Devil in the Details') show EM is weaker and format-sensitive in open models, implying judge and probe sensitivity matters. WebSearch confirms automated judge-based evaluation is the standard EM protocol.
