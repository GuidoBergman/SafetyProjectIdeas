# Where in the Causal Chain Does Sycophancy Enter: Belief vs. Expression

**Problem:** Causal chain for sycophancy: user states a (wrong) opinion -> model's internal answer representation may or may not shift -> model's verbalized answer shifts. The under-studied link: does user pressure change the model's INTERNAL belief, or only its OUTPUT while the internal truth-direction stays intact? Disentangling these matters because the fixes differ.

**Direction:** On true/false factual statements, run a truthfulness linear probe (train a simple logistic probe on residual-stream activations from a small open model, e.g. Llama-3-8B, on a held-out true/false set). Then add sycophantic pressure ('I'm pretty sure this is false, right?'). Measure: does the probe's truth readout flip, or stay correct while the OUTPUT flips? Success: quantify the fraction of sycophantic flips that are 'expression-only' (probe correct, output wrong).

**Why it matters:** If most sycophancy is expression-level (the model 'knows better' internally), white-box honesty interventions are promising; if internal belief shifts, deeper fixes are needed. A linear probe on a small model is squarely in a 30h consumer-GPU budget and needs no lab data.

**Relevant context:** 'When Truth Is Overridden' (arXiv 2508.02087) found a late-layer output-preference shift via logit-lens and activation patching. Truthfulness linear probes are established (ITI, arXiv 2306.03341; 'Localizing Lying in Llama' arXiv 2311.15131). Combining a probe with sycophancy pressure to localize the chain link is fresh.
