# Action-Only vs CoT Monitoring on Subtle vs Blatant Sabotage

**Problem:** CoT-Red-Handed found CoT monitoring can do WORSE than action-only monitoring for blatant sabotage (by up to 44%), but the crossover between subtle and blatant sabotage where each monitor type wins is not mapped.

**Direction:** Reproduce a minimal version on open models: collect agent trajectories on coding tasks with planted sabotage at graded subtlety levels. Run two cheap LLM-judge monitors (CoT-only, action-only) and a combined monitor, plotting detection rate vs. subtlety. Locate the crossover and quantify the combined monitor's gain.

**Why it matters:** Monitoring is the leading scalable-oversight bet for agents. Knowing when to trust CoT vs. actions, validated on open models anyone can run, directly informs monitor design and is pure evaluation work with no training.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2505 'CoT Red-Handed' (LASR Labs 2025): for non-subtle sabotage CoT monitoring underperformed action-only by up to 44%; a hybrid combining CoT and action suspicion scores improved detection. arXiv:2507.11473 'Chain of Thought Monitorability' frames CoT as a fragile opportunity.
