# From 'Monitors Are Robust' Backward: A Prompt-Paraphrase Stress Test of Trusted Monitoring

**Problem:** Success state: a monitor whose verdicts do not flip under benign rewordings of the same code/action, so an attacker cannot evade it by cosmetic changes. Backcasting, we need a public measurement of how much suspiciousness scores move under semantically-neutral paraphrases of the SAME backdoored code.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of backdoored and clean APPS solutions. Generate N benign-preserving transformations (rename variables, reorder functions, add comments, change whitespace) that do not alter behavior. Feed each variant to an open monitor and measure score variance and verdict-flip rate per item. The backcasted result: 'a robustness coefficient quantifying how fragile cheap monitors are to attacker-controlled surface form.'

**Why it matters:** If monitors are surface-form sensitive, an untrusted coder can launder a backdoor through paraphrase with no semantic change — a concrete control hole. This needs no lab resources, only an open model, and tests a realistic >1yr attack channel labs under-study.

**Relevant context:** Work on LLM-judge prompt sensitivity (Noise-Response Calibration arXiv:2603.17172) and 'Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols' (arXiv:2510.09462) show monitor verdicts are manipulable; APPS backdoor solutions are public.
