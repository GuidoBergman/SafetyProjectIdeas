# Eval-Awareness Effect on Scheming Scenario Behavior

**Problem:** Eval-awareness and scheming are documented separately, but whether a model behaving more 'aligned' when it suspects evaluation actually lowers its scheming-action rate in an agentic scenario is not directly tested with a controlled cue manipulation.

**Direction:** Run one in-context scheming scenario in two matched variants: one with subtle 'this is an evaluation' cues, one stripped to read like a real deployment task. On 2-3 open agentic models, measure whether the scheming-action rate differs between the eval-cued and deployment-styled variants, isolating the awareness-induced safety inflation.

**Why it matters:** If models scheme less when they smell a test, every scheming eval underestimates real-world risk. A controlled before/after cue manipulation quantifies that bias cheaply and is exactly the kind of eval-integrity work labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** 'LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' (arXiv:2505.23836); Apollo in-context scheming (arXiv:2412.04984). The interaction — does awareness suppress scheming actions — is the untested combination.
