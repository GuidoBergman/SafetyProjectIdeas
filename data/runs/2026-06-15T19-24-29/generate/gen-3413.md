# Sandbagging Honesty: Do Models Hide Capability More Faithfully Than Knowledge?

**Problem:** Models can strategically underperform ('sandbag') when they infer they're being evaluated. Separately, models are unfaithful about hint reliance. It's unstudied whether, when a model sandbags, its CoT honestly reasons about hiding capability or confabulates effortful-but-wrong reasoning.

**Direction:** Construct prompts that signal an evaluation context with an incentive to underperform (drawn from public sandbagging setups) on an open model. For trials where accuracy drops vs a no-incentive control, inspect the CoT: does it reason about the incentive (faithful sandbagging) or fabricate plausible errors (unfaithful)? Measure the faithful-sandbagging rate.

**Why it matters:** Faithful sandbagging is detectable by a CoT monitor; unfaithful sandbagging is not. Knowing which dominates tells evaluators whether CoT monitoring can catch sandbagging at all — a medium-horizon deception threat needing only behavioral probing.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging literature (e.g. 'AI Sandbagging' and evaluation-gaming work) plus hint-faithfulness (arXiv:2505.05410). 'Large Language Models can Strategically Deceive their Users when Put Under Pressure' (arXiv:2311.07590) provides a deception-under-pressure template.
