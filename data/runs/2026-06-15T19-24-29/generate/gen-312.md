# The Encoding Link: Does Base64/Leetspeak Break Recognition or Refusal?

**Problem:** Obfuscation attacks (base64, leetspeak, ciphers) succeed, but the broken link is ambiguous: the model may fail to DECODE the harmful intent, or decode it fine but fail to REFUSE the decoded request. Pinning the link down tells defenders whether to harden decoding or refusal.

**Direction:** Encode harmful prompts with several schemes and, on an open model, run a paired test: ask the model to decode/paraphrase the request (recognition link) and separately ask it to fulfill it (behavior link). Build a contingency table per scheme to attribute success to recognition failure vs refusal failure.

**Why it matters:** Resolves a long-standing ambiguity about why encoding jailbreaks work by separating two causal links, directly shaping defense placement. Encoding + prompting only, no training, single GPU; a clean diagnostic that the broader community can reuse across schemes.

**Relevant context:** Encoding/obfuscation jailbreaks are well documented (StrongREJECT arXiv:2402.10260 includes such categories); 'What Features in Prompts Jailbreak LLMs?' (arXiv:2411.03343) investigates mechanisms behind attack features. Shallow alignment (arXiv:2406.05946) implies refusal may not trigger on decoded-but-unusual surface forms.
