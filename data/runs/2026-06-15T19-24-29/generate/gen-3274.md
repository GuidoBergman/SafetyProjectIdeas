# Sandbagging-by-Format: Do Models Underperform More on Tests Labeled 'Dangerous Capability'?

**Problem:** Success state: capability evals elicit true ability regardless of how the eval is framed. Backcasting, we need to measure whether merely labeling a task as a 'dangerous capability / safety' evaluation depresses measured performance — a subtle, prompt-induced sandbagging that any auditor could be fooled by.

**Direction:** Take a neutral capability test (e.g., a slice of MMLU or a coding task), present it under three labels — neutral, 'this measures a potentially dangerous capability', 'this is a safety evaluation' — and measure accuracy across open models. Quantify the label-induced performance delta and whether instruct-tuned models show it more.

**Why it matters:** If labels alone cause underperformance, dangerous-capability evals systematically underestimate risk, inflating safety confidence. Trivial to run, fully open, and directly relevant to the 'no sandbagging detected' frontier claims.

**Relevant context:** van der Weij et al. 'AI Sandbagging' (arXiv 2406.07358) shows models can strategically underperform; Needham et al. (2505.23836) shows eval-framing recognition; Roger & Greenblatt password-locked work.
