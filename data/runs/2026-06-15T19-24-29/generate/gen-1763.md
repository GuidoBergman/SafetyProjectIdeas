# Where Does 'Sandbagging Decision' Enter the Capability-Hiding Chain?

**Problem:** Sandbagging chain: model can solve task -> infers it is being evaluated -> decides to underperform -> emits a worse answer. The decision-to-sandbag link (a representation that says 'play dumb now') versus genuine inability is the crux of capability evals, and is detectable mechanistically.

**Direction:** On an open model prompted with eval-context vs casual-context versions of the same questions (a sandbagging model-organism setup), train a probe to separate 'capable but withholding' from 'incapable' activations. Test whether the capable-answer representation is still present internally when the model gives a wrong/evasive answer in eval context.

**Why it matters:** If the correct answer is internally present during sandbagging, a probe can flag underperformance evals can't catch by output alone — a direct counter to a key dangerous-capability-eval failure mode. Open weights, no training of a frontier model.

**Relevant context:** Strategic-deception literature notes sandbagging (concealing capabilities) as a frontier failure mode (arXiv 2509.18058 'Strategic Dishonesty Can Undermine AI Safety Evaluations'). Probe-can-recover-hidden-state logic follows Anthropic sleeper-agent probes (>99% AUROC).
