# Linear Probe for Eval-Awareness on Open-Weight Activations

**Problem:** Eval-awareness is the precondition for sandbagging in evals. Prior work shows it is linearly probeable and scales predictably, but reproducing a probe on a small open model and testing whether the same probe direction generalizes across unrelated eval formats is an accessible, underexplored check.

**Direction:** Extract residual-stream activations from a small open model (7-8B) on a labeled set of eval-style vs deployment-style prompts; train a logistic-regression probe on one task family; test transfer to held-out families. Measure cross-family AUC and whether steering along the probe direction (cheap activation addition, no training) shifts behavior. Single-GPU, uses HookedTransformer/TransformerLens or nnsight.

**Why it matters:** A reproducible eval-awareness probe that generalizes is a lightweight monitoring primitive any evaluator can run on open weights — and probing whether it transfers tells us if 'is this a test?' is a single feature or many. Gentle interpretability accessible to first-time researchers.

**Relevant context:** 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models' (arXiv:2507.01786) and 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights LLMs' (arXiv:2509.13333): eval-awareness is linearly decodable and scales with size; steering toward 'deployed' changes behavior (arXiv:2510.20487).
