# Do the FLI AI Safety Index grades track public commitments or actual disclosures?

**Problem:** FLI's AI Safety Index gives labs grades (none above C+ in Winter 2025) across 33 indicators, but it is unclear whether grade movements over time reflect new safety behavior or merely more polished public communication.

**Direction:** Treat the FLI Index as a fixed experimental instrument and run a follow-up: scrape the public-facing safety documents (RSPs, system cards, blog posts) FLI cites for each lab across the 2024 and 2025 editions, and use simple NLP (commitment-counting, hedging-word detection, verifiability tagging) to test whether grade changes correlate more with volume/tone of disclosure than with concrete, verifiable commitments. Report correlation between text features and grade deltas.

**Why it matters:** If index grades are driven by communication style rather than substance, third parties relying on them for procurement or policy could be misled. Building a transparent, reproducible re-scoring harness is exactly the kind of audit infrastructure valuable outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index 2024/Winter 2025 (33 indicators, GPA grading); CLAIM.md note that estimated/unverified safety claims are unreliable; Open Problem: third-party-auditable verification methods.
