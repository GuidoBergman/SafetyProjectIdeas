# Activation-Steering Self-Detection: Can a Model Flag When It's Been Steered?

**Problem:** 'Steering Awareness' (2026) shows models can detect activation steering from within. For control, this is double-edged: a model noticing it's been steered (e.g. by a safety intervention) could route around it. The reliability of such self-detection on open models is unexplored.

**Direction:** On an open model, apply mild activation steering along a behavioral direction and prompt the model to report whether its 'thinking feels altered'. Measure self-detection accuracy as a function of steering strength, and test whether the model can also identify the steering DIRECTION (e.g. 'I'm being pushed to be more cautious').

**Why it matters:** If models reliably notice steering interventions, runtime-steering controls may be detectable and thus evadable. Characterizing the steering-strength threshold for self-detection bounds how covert an activation-level control can be — an internals study feasible on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Steering Awareness: Detecting Activation Steering from Within' (arXiv:2511.21399) and latent-monitoring work in 'Preemptive Detection and Steering of LLM Misalignment via Latent Reachability' (arXiv:2509.21528).
