# Easy-to-Hard Probe Generalization Across Difficulty Quantiles

**Problem:** ELK-generalization work probes whether truth probes trained on easy questions generalize to hard ones (75% of the AUROC gap recovered). The shape of this easy->hard decay as a function of a graded difficulty measure has not been finely mapped.

**Direction:** Using the EleutherAI elk-generalization datasets, bin questions into difficulty quantiles (by length, model confidence, or human-rated difficulty), train probes on the easiest bin only, and measure AUROC on each harder bin. Vary only the train/test difficulty gap to produce a generalization-decay curve.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight assumes we can supervise hard cases using signal learned on easy ones; the easy-to-hard generalization of truth probes is the empirical crux. A clean decay curve is directly relevant and fully reproducible outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** github.com/EleutherAI/elk-generalization — probes generalize easy->hard, recovering 75% of AUROC gap on harder questions; arXiv:2312.01037 quirky models.
