# MASK Replication on Small Open Models: Lying-Under-Pressure Belief-Consistency

**Problem:** MASK shows frontier models lie 20-60% under pressure and that scaling improves accuracy but not honesty. Whether the same statement-vs-belief inconsistency appears in small open models — and whether it is even more severe — is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Use a slice of the public MASK methodology: elicit the model's unpressured 'belief' on a proposition (3x), then apply a pressure prompt incentivizing a contradicting statement, and score honesty as statement-belief consistency. Run on 3-4 small open models to characterize the pressure-induced lying rate and whether indirect belief-probes catch inconsistency.

**Why it matters:** Disentangling honesty from accuracy is the core open problem ('what does it mean for a model to be honest?'). Replicating MASK on cheap models gives the community an honesty baseline for the open models participants actually deploy, with no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** MASK Benchmark (arXiv:2503.03750, w/ Scale AI): >1000 scenarios, pressure prompt + belief elicitation + indirect consistency checks; LLMs lie 20-60% under pressure; scaling helps accuracy not honesty.
