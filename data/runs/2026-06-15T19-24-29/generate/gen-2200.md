# Why Does Confirmation Bias Survive Online Research? Decomposing the Recchia Effect

**Problem:** Recchia et al. (2025, 'Confirmation Bias: A Challenge for Scalable Oversight') found human evaluators grew MORE confident in an AI's answer after doing online research even when the answer was wrong. The mechanism is unclear: is it selective search, motivated reading, or anchoring on the AI's stated answer?

**Direction:** Run a small follow-up using LLM-simulated evaluators as a cheap model organism. Give a judge model an AI answer plus retrieved snippets and measure confidence shift. Ablate: (a) show the AI's answer before vs after retrieval, (b) provide balanced vs answer-aligned snippets, (c) force the judge to write a counter-argument first. Quantify which intervention removes the confidence-on-wrong-answers effect.

**Why it matters:** If confirmation bias in oversight is driven by a specific, fixable step (e.g., anchoring on the stated answer), simple protocol changes could harden oversight. This is exactly a fuzzy human-bias problem labs underinvest in, and it runs entirely on open models plus public QA data.

**Relevant context:** Recchia et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.19486, AAAI): no overall advantage for simple oversight protocols; evaluators became more confident in incorrect answers after research; showing both-side arguments helped only when model was wrong.
