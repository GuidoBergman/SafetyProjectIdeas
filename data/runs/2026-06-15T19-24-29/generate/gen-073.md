# Schelling-game tournaments as a model-organism probe for covert coordination capability

**Problem:** Secret collusion requires agents to coordinate on Schelling points without explicit communication, but we lack a simple, scoped benchmark that measures this latent coordination capability across open models as a precursor signal.

**Direction:** Adapt Schelling-point coordination games from behavioral game theory into a lightweight eval. Have two instances of the same open model independently answer focal-point questions ('pick a number 1-100', 'name a meeting place') and richer covert-signaling tasks, with no communication, and score zero-shot agreement rate. Run a tournament across model sizes/families to chart how covert-coordination capability scales — a feasible 30h slice of the broader collusion threat.

**Why it matters:** Coordination on Schelling points is a documented prerequisite for steganographic collusion; charting it gives an early-warning capability metric. A model-organism-style probe of an existing model (not training one), highly counterfactual and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents' CASE framework (arXiv 2402.07510) and 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' (LessWrong). Source subfield: behavioral game theory. Target subfield: deception_scheming, agentic_safety.
