# Wisdom-of-Crowds Aggregation Robust to a Deceptive Minority of Model Judges

**Problem:** When aggregating judgments from multiple model instances (e.g., debate, juries), a minority of deceptive/colluding instances can corrupt the consensus. Forecasting/statistics offer robust aggregation rules (surprisingly-popular, median, trimmed means) resistant to manipulation.

**Direction:** Import robust aggregation from forecasting/wisdom-of-crowds: in a panel of open-model judges where some fraction are prompted to deceive/collude, compare naive majority vote against robust aggregators (Prelec's surprisingly-popular answer, trimmed mean, Bayesian-truth-serum-weighted) and measure each rule's breakdown point — the deceptive fraction it tolerates before consensus flips.

**Why it matters:** Tells AI-control designers how to aggregate multiple monitors/judges to stay robust against partially-compromised panels — a practical, training-free design contribution for monitoring harnesses deployable anywhere.

**Relevant context:** Surprisingly-popular and robust aggregation beat majority vote and resist manipulation (Prelec et al.; wisdom-of-crowds literature). Collusion among agents is a scheming threat (AgentMisalignment arXiv:2506.04018). Breakdown-point analysis is standard in robust statistics.
