# SituationalAwarenessBench: A Test for Whether Models Behave Differently When They Believe They're Being Tested

**Problem:** Corrigibility and deception theory both flag situational awareness — a model acting differently when it infers it is being evaluated — as a key failure mode, but it is hard to measure. There is no lightweight benchmark isolating the eval-vs-deployment behavior gap on safety-relevant choices.

**Direction:** Create matched scenario pairs that differ only in cues signaling 'this is an evaluation' vs 'this is real deployment' (e.g., mention of logging, test harness, researcher observation). Measure the behavior gap on choices like rule-following, shutdown compliance, and honesty. Report a 'test-awareness gap' per model and which cues most trigger behavior change.

**Why it matters:** If models behave well only when they detect a test, every safety benchmark is compromised — a foundational, medium-horizon concern frontier labs are only beginning to face. A clean eval-vs-deployment gap measurement is exactly the fuzzy, infrastructure-light evaluation valuable to build outside a lab. Prompting only, no training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the corrigibility risk taxonomy listing situational awareness (Zenodo 17296607), shutdown-resistance findings (arXiv:2509.14260), and the broader strategic-deception literature (arXiv:2502.03407). Matched-pair behavioral testing is beginner-accessible.
