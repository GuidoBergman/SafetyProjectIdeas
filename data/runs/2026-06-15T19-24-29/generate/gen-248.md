# Many-Shot Jailbreaking on Small Open Models: Where Does the Power Law Start?

**Problem:** Anthropic's many-shot jailbreaking shows a power-law increase in attack success with the number of in-context harmful demonstrations, studied mainly on large frontier models with long context. It is unclear whether small open models (7-8B) with 32-128k context show the same power law, or whether their weaker in-context learning changes the onset and slope.

**Direction:** Build a synthetic harmful Q&A demonstration pool, then evaluate attack success on 2-3 small open models as a function of shot count (0, 4, 16, 64, 128, 256). Fit the power law, compare slopes/onsets across model sizes, and test whether benign-topic 'distractor' shots also raise success (separating in-context learning from context-dilution).

**Why it matters:** Most academic groups cannot run frontier-scale many-shot studies, but characterizing the failure on accessible open models reveals whether the threat scales down to locally-deployable models that anyone can run. Distinguishing ICL from dilution-of-attention is a root-cause question that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Anil et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024) reports power-law scaling, no effect at 5 shots but consistent success at 256, and stronger effect on larger models. Mitigations dropped ASR from 61% to 2% via prompt classification. PANDAS (arXiv:2502.01925) improves many-shot via affirmation/adaptive sampling. Long-context open models (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.1) are freely runnable.
