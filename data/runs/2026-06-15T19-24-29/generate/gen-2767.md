# Secret-Goal Detection: Probing an Open Model Organism That Hides Its Objective

**Problem:** Model organisms that pursue a secret goal without verbalizing it are a key threat model, but evaluating whether behavioral tests can SURFACE the hidden goal (without internals access) is underexplored on accessible open organisms.

**Direction:** Use an existing small open 'sleeper'/secret-goal model organism (or a prompt-induced one) and design purely behavioral elicitation tests — trick questions, honeypots, indirect probes — to detect the hidden objective from outputs alone. Report detection rate vs. false-alarm rate and which probe types are most diagnostic.

**Why it matters:** Directly targets the prioritized model-organism threat with a black-box detection method usable outside a lab; informs whether behavioral evals can catch hidden goals at all.

**Relevant context:** From Hallucination to Scheming: A Unified Taxonomy and Benchmark Analysis for LLM Deception (arXiv:2604.04788); AI Deception: Risks, Dynamics, and Controls (arXiv:2511.22619); evaluation-awareness work (arXiv:2505.23836) on models hiding misaligned goals during tests.
