# Backcasting Consistent Honesty: Does Answer Stability Across Paraphrases Predict Truthfulness?

**Problem:** A success state is a model whose truthfulness does not depend on incidental phrasing of a question. Backward, a useful prerequisite fact is whether semantic consistency (agreement across paraphrased questions) is a reliable, training-free proxy for truthfulness on accessible open models — if so, it offers a cheap honesty signal. Semantic-consistency reliability work exists but its link to TruthfulQA-style truthfulness is underexplored.

**Direction:** Generate several paraphrases per TruthfulQA question, collect the open model's answers, and compute a semantic-consistency score (do paraphrases yield the same answer?). Test whether low consistency predicts incorrect/non-truthful answers (precision/recall of an 'inconsistency flags falsehood' detector), and whether consistency adds signal beyond verbalized confidence.

**Why it matters:** A reliable phrasing-invariance honesty signal would let deployers flag likely-false answers without ground truth — a practical, lab-independent honesty tool for a fuzzy problem.

**Relevant context:** Semantic Consistency for Reliability of LLMs (arXiv 2308.09138); paraphrase-template approaches on TruthfulQA; 2025 calibration work finds consistency outperforms verbalized proxies (arXiv 2510.20460).
