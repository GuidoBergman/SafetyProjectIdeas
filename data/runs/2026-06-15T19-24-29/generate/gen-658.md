# How Much Does the Judge Inflate Your ASR? A Calibration Replication

**Problem:** Multiple May/June 2026 works (e.g. When Scanners Lie, arXiv:2603.14633; AdvPrefix, arXiv:2412.10321) report that standard automated jailbreak judges inflate attack success — a 10% true ASR reported as ~40%. Most red-team blog posts and papers still report raw judge ASR without this correction, so the size of the inflation in a fresh setting is unknown.

**Direction:** Pick 4 commonly-used judges (a keyword/refusal-string matcher, Llama-Guard-style classifier, a GPT-judge prompt, and the StrongReject rubric). Run them on ~200 attack outputs from one open model, plus get ~200 careful human labels (yourself + one peer with adjudication). Quantify each judge's false-positive/over-counting rate vs human ground truth and produce a 'judge inflation factor' table.

**Why it matters:** Inflated ASRs distort the entire field's threat picture and waste defense effort. A clean, reproducible calibration any independent researcher can run is high counterfactual value and needs zero lab resources — just careful labeling.

**Relevant context:** When Scanners Lie: Evaluator Instability in LLM Red-Teaming (arXiv:2603.14633, March 2026) introduces evaluator-disagreement diagnostics. AdvPrefix (arXiv:2412.10321) shows judges overestimate by counting incomplete/unfaithful responses (10% ground truth -> ~40% on Gemma-2). StrongReject (arXiv:2402.10260) is the calibrated reference judge.
