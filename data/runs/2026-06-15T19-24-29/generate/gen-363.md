# Prompt Injection x Activation-Probe Detector: Does Indirect Injection Evade Probes Tuned on Direct Jailbreaks?

**Problem:** Two failures: indirect prompt injection (instruction hidden in retrieved/tool content) and the brittleness of activation-probe jailbreak detectors. Activation probes are cheap and production-ready for direct jailbreaks, but they are typically trained on direct adversarial prompts. Whether indirect injection (same harmful effect, different surface) evades a probe tuned on direct jailbreaks is a compound generalization gap that is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Extract residual-stream activations from an open model on direct jailbreak prompts, train a simple linear probe to detect them, then test detection on indirect-injection variants that achieve the same harmful behavior via embedded content. Success criterion: a detection drop (AUROC) from direct to indirect, quantifying the probe's blind spot.

**Why it matters:** Activation probing is a leading cheap-monitoring direction (LessWrong Jan 2026 digest); if it fails to transfer to indirect injection—the dominant agentic threat—the compound undermines a key defense. Probing an open model, simple linear classifier, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Subfield context: activation-based jailbreak detection achieves production robustness at low cost (LessWrong Jan 2026); open problem to create cost-effective probe detection. OWASP and Promptware Kill Chain (arXiv:2601.09625) establish indirect injection as the agentic surface.
