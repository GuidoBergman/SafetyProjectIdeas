# Attention Pattern vs Attribution: Do They Agree on Which Tokens Matter?

**Problem:** Raw attention weights are a tempting but criticized explanation of 'what the model attended to'; gradient/attribution methods often disagree. The discrepancy has been argued about in NLP broadly, but a clean head-by-head comparison within a known circuit (where ground truth about important tokens is established) is a useful controlled variation.

**Direction:** On the IOI task in GPT-2 small (where the important name tokens are known from circuit analysis), compare three attributions for which input tokens matter: raw attention weights of the key heads, gradient-times-activation, and activation-patching importance. Measure agreement (rank correlation) against the established ground-truth-relevant tokens. Vary the layer/head to see where attention is and isn't faithful.

**Why it matters:** Attention visualizations are widely used as cheap 'interpretability' in safety dashboards. Demonstrating, against a known circuit, when attention is and isn't a faithful explanation guards against false confidence — a methodological clarification that is fully open and small-scale.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al. IOI circuit (GPT-2 small) provides ground-truth important tokens; 'Attention is not Explanation' / 'Attention is not not Explanation' debate; TransformerLens exposes attention patterns and patching.
