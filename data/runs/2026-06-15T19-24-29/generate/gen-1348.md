# Probe vs LLM-judge for detecting sandbagging, not just hallucination

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. found internal probes beat judge models for hallucination and toxicity. It is unknown whether this advantage extends to harder, fuzzier safety categories like sandbagging (deliberate underperformance), which is exactly the kind of diffuse problem labs deprioritize.

**Direction:** Build a sandbagging dataset by prompting an open model to answer questions either honestly or while deliberately underperforming. Train a linear probe on its activations and compare detection AUC against an LLM-judge (a second open model prompted to detect underperformance) on held-out items. Report which method wins and at what layer.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging is a long-horizon deception threat hard to catch behaviorally. Comparing cheap internal probes to judges on it is high counterfactual value and needs no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Surprising result: probes outperform judges for hallucination/toxicity (Beaglehole et al.). Extends to sandbagging as a fuzzy-reward category. Linear probing methodology from RepE.
