# The Self-Assessment Link: Comparing Lab Self-Reports Against Independent FLI Scores

**Problem:** Assurance chain: 'lab self-reports safety practice' -> 'self-report taken at face value' -> 'stakeholders trust it'. The FLI Index moved deliberately from self-reporting to publicly-verifiable evidence; this implies the self-report-to-truth link is unreliable, but the size of the gap is undocumented per indicator.

**Direction:** For indicators where both a lab self-description and an independent FLI panel score exist, code the discrepancy: does the lab claim a practice the panel found unsubstantiated? Quantify, per safety domain, how often self-reports overstate relative to evidence-based scoring, locating where self-assessment most diverges from verifiable reality.

**Why it matters:** Much governance still relies on self-reporting; measuring the self-report-vs-evidence gap by domain tells regulators where independent verification is non-negotiable. Uses only public Index materials and lab statements, no compute, counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index Summer 2025 (shift from self-reporting to publicly verifiable evidence, 33 indicators); arXiv:2512.01166 'Evaluating AI Companies' Frontier Safety Frameworks'.
