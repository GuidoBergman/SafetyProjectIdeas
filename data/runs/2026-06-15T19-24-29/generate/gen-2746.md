# Scorer Roulette: Quantifying How Much Your Safety Verdict Depends on the Autograder

**Problem:** Different autograders/LLM-judges produce discrepant safety verdicts for the same model output, but practitioners pick one judge and treat its score as ground truth. The variance attributable purely to judge choice is rarely surfaced.

**Direction:** On a fixed set of ~300 model outputs from an existing safety benchmark, score harmfulness with 4-5 different open judge models and prompt variants. Decompose total score variance into model-effect vs. judge-effect vs. interaction. Identify the harm categories where judge choice swamps the actual model difference, and produce a 'judge sensitivity' flag for benchmark items.

**Why it matters:** Improving scorer/autograder reliability is a named open problem; showing how often the judge, not the model, drives the verdict is a direct meta-eval contribution requiring only inference.

**Relevant context:** LLM Judges Inconsistently Disagree Across Safety Criteria and Harm Categories (arXiv:2605.31381); Skewed Score: a statistical framework to assess autograders (arXiv:2507.03772); audit showed judge performance near random chance in adversarial robustness scenarios.
