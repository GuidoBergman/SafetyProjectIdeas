# Open-Source Intelligence on Real-World Scheming: A Small Annotation Study of Wild Incident Reports

**Problem:** 'Scheming in the Wild' (arXiv:2604.09104) collected 3.39M X posts (Oct 2025-Mar 2026) and identified 895 scheming-related incident reports. The taxonomy and reliability of such crowd-sourced incident reports as a safety signal are underexplored.

**Direction:** Using the public incident reports (or a re-collected small sample of similar posts), hand-annotate a few hundred into a taxonomy (genuine scheming vs. user error vs. hallucination vs. anthropomorphic misattribution), and compute inter-annotator agreement plus the base rate of 'plausibly genuine' scheming. Optionally train a cheap classifier and report its precision against the human labels.

**Why it matters:** Real-world deployment incidents are a counterfactually valuable signal that frontier labs see only for their own products. A reproducible annotation protocol and reliability estimate turns noisy social-media chatter into a usable, lab-independent monitoring resource.

**Relevant context:** 'Scheming in the wild: detecting real-world AI scheming incidents with open-source intelligence' (arXiv:2604.09104, 2026): 3.39M X posts Oct 2025-Mar 2026, 895 scheming incident reports identified.
