# Does Probe Detection Survive Paraphrase but Not Obfuscation?

**Problem:** Activation probes detect 'ordinary jailbreaks' at >=90% recall (arXiv:2601.11516) but collapse under deliberate obfuscated activations (arXiv:2412.09565). The intermediate regime — natural-language paraphrase and translation of harmful requests — is untested and reflects realistic non-adversarial drift.

**Direction:** Train a harmfulness probe on an open model, then evaluate recall on (a) original harmful prompts, (b) LLM-paraphrased versions, (c) translated-then-back-translated versions, (d) explicit obfuscation-suffix attacks. Map the recall degradation gradient from benign drift to active attack.

**Why it matters:** Real deployments face benign distribution shift far more often than active obfuscation. Knowing where probes break under realistic paraphrase informs realistic-threat benchmarking (a core open problem). No training of the base model needed.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2601.11516 (probes fragile to distribution shift). arXiv:2412.09565 (obfuscation defeats probes). Open Problems: 'realistic jailbreak benchmarks reflecting actual attacker capabilities'.
