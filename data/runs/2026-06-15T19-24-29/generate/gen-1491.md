# Genetic Epistasis Analysis: Are Combined Steering Effects Additive or Non-Linear?

**Problem:** Combining three or more steering vectors is assumed roughly additive, but genetics knows that combining mutations often produces epistasis (the combined effect differs from the sum). Whether higher-order steering interactions are epistatic is untested.

**Direction:** Borrow the genetics epistasis framework. Take three safety steering vectors and apply all 2^3 on/off combinations, measuring a behavioral outcome for each. Fit a linear-plus-interaction model to quantify pairwise and three-way epistasis. Identify 'sign epistasis' cases where adding a third safety steer flips the effect of a pair — the most dangerous failure mode for stacked safety interventions.

**Why it matters:** Defense-in-depth stacks many safety steers; epistatic interactions mean the whole behaves unpredictably from the parts. Quantifying higher-order interactions is essential for trustworthy multi-steer deployment and is a richer version of pairwise synergy analysis. Inference-only, feasible in 30h.

**Relevant context:** Epistasis = non-additive interaction of genetic factors. Multi-concept steering by combination established (Beaglehole et al.). Loewe/Bliss synergy frameworks provide the additive null model.
