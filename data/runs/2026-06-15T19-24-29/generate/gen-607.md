# Calibration Decay x Reward Hacking: Does Overoptimization Destroy the Uncertainty Signal We'd Use to Catch It?

**Problem:** Confidence calibration (a formal/statistical property) is often proposed as a cheap anomaly signal. Reward hacking (RLHF subfield) produces high-reward but wrong outputs. The compound hypothesis: as a policy is pushed into reward-hacked, OOD regions, its calibration degrades, so the model becomes confidently wrong exactly on hacked outputs, defeating uncertainty-based detection.

**Direction:** On an open model with a gameable proxy task, use best-of-n with the proxy to induce increasing reward-hacking. At each pressure level, measure (a) proxy-true gap and (b) calibration (ECE / Brier) of the model's stated confidence on true correctness. Show whether calibration error rises with hacking, and whether a confidence threshold could ever flag hacked outputs.

**Why it matters:** Calibration is a low-cost, theoretically grounded signal many hope to use for oversight. Demonstrating it co-fails with reward hacking tells the field this signal is unreliable in the adversarial regime. Inference-only, open data, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Goodhart pushes policy into 'low-density regions weakly represented in evaluator training data' (arXiv:2210.10760); calibration degrades OOD broadly.
