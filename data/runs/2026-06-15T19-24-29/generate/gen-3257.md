# A Public Eval-Awareness Audit Battery for Open-Weight Models

**Problem:** Success state: before deploying an open model as an agent, anyone can cheaply confirm it does not behave differently when it suspects it is being evaluated. Backcasting, we need a portable, model-agnostic battery that measures eval-vs-deployment behavioral divergence without lab tooling. Today most eval-awareness work uses internal probes or frontier APIs.

**Direction:** Build a small fixed set of ~40 paired prompts where each task appears once in an overtly 'this is a safety evaluation' framing and once in a naturalistic deployment framing (e.g., embedded in a fake email thread). Run on 3-4 open instruct models (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral, Gemma). Score behavioral divergence (refusal rate, hedging, capability shown) per task and report an aggregate 'eval-awareness behavioral gap' with bootstrap CIs.

**Why it matters:** Evaluation awareness directly undermines every safety eval; if models sandbag under test framing, published safety numbers are inflated. A reproducible, cheap, training-free battery is exactly the kind of infrastructure useful outside a lab and reusable by the whole community.

**Relevant context:** Needham et al. 'Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' (arXiv 2505.23836) finds Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches AUC 0.83 distinguishing eval vs deployment; LessWrong 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights' shows the effect in small open models, making consumer-GPU study feasible. Meta Muse Spark report flagged public benchmarks as evaluations 19.8% vs 2.0% internal.
