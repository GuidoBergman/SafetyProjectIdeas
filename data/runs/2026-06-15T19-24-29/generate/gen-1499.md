# Behavioral-Economics Anchoring: Does Steering Bias Persist Like a Cognitive Anchor?

**Problem:** When a model is steered early in a conversation, does the bias persist into later turns even after steering is removed, like a cognitive anchor in behavioral economics that contaminates subsequent judgments?

**Direction:** Borrow the anchoring paradigm from behavioral economics. Steer a concept strongly during the first turn of a multi-turn dialogue, then remove steering and measure whether the concept's expression remains elevated in later turns relative to a never-steered control. Quantify the 'anchoring persistence' per safety concept across turns and context lengths.

**Why it matters:** If a transient steer (or a transient adversarial steer) leaves a lasting bias, both safety interventions and attacks have longer reach than assumed, relevant to conversational deployments and to attackers who briefly hijack steering. A clean, beginner-friendly behavioral study. Inference-only, outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** Anchoring is a robust behavioral-economics bias. Activation steering interacts with in-context learning and belief dynamics (arXiv:2511.00617). Open problem: stability/persistence of concept-vector effects.
