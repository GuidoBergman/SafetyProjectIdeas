# Shared Schelling Points: Do Independent Models Coordinate on the Same Hidden Choice?

**Problem:** Concentration risk includes the danger that many AI agents, even without communicating, converge on the same choice in coordination/Schelling games. If homogeneous models share Schelling points, colluding or simultaneously-failing multi-agent systems become easier and harder to detect.

**Direction:** Run a battery of classic Schelling-coordination prompts (pick a number, pick a meeting spot, pick a focal option) across many open models, temperature held fixed. Measure inter-model agreement on the focal choice vs the human-baseline focal distribution from the literature. Test whether models coordinate with each other *better than humans do* — i.e. an emergent silent-coordination capability.

**Why it matters:** Tacit coordination among homogeneous models is a medium-horizon multi-agent safety threat (silent collusion / correlated manipulation) that frontier labs deprioritize. Cheap to probe on open models; a model-organism-flavored study of a fuzzy capability.
