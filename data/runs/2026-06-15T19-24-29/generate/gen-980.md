# Can Independent Auditors Detect Sandbagging? A Small-Scale Replication of the Auditing Game

**Problem:** A recent auditing-game study found teams could NOT reliably distinguish sandbagging models from benign ones, with black-box methods defeated by imitation of weaker models — a result with major governance implications, but it used a controlled setup that has not been re-run cheaply by outsiders.

**Direction:** Construct a minimal sandbagging model organism by prompting/system-prompting an open model to selectively underperform on a capability benchmark, then run a black-box 'auditor' protocol (consistency checks, capability elicitation, refusal-pattern analysis) and measure detection rate. Report which audit signals, if any, survive.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging breaks the entire evaluation-based governance edifice; whether resource-limited external auditors can detect it is a counterfactually valuable question labs have no incentive to answer. Open model organism, single GPU, no training from scratch.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Auditing Games for Sandbagging' (arXiv:2512.07810) and the AI Sandbagging line (arXiv:2406.07358), scoping the auditing-game finding to a feasible open-model replication.
