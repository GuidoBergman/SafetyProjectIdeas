# Constitutional AI Self-Critique x Critic Blind-Spot Inheritance

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) self-generated critiques in Constitutional AI / RLAIF (alignment_training), and (B) the critic inheriting the same blind spots as the policy (oversight). If the same model both acts and critiques, a category of harm it cannot recognize is invisible at both stages, compounding into systematic missed harms.

**Direction:** Take an open model. Have it (i) generate responses to borderline prompts and (ii) self-critique them under a constitution. Compare self-critique catch rate to a different-family critic. Identify harm categories systematically missed by self-critique but caught cross-family — measuring inherited blind spots.

**Why it matters:** RLAIF/CAI scale alignment by removing humans, but if the AI critic shares the policy's blind spots, the scaling is illusory for exactly the harms hardest to see. Inference only with two public models.

**Relevant context:** Constitutional AI / RLAIF (Anthropic). Open Problems in RLHF on scalable feedback alternatives. Self-preference / shared-blind-spot evidence in 'When RLHF Fails' (arXiv 2606.03238).
