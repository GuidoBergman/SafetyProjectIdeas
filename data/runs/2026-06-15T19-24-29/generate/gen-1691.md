# Self-Repair Sabotages Ablation: Measuring the Direct-Effect vs Ablation Gap

**Problem:** Self-repair means that when an attention head is ablated, other 'backup' heads activate to compensate, so the logit change from ablation badly misrepresents a component's true causal role. This makes ablation an unreliable interpretability tool, yet most beginner circuit analyses use it uncritically.

**Direction:** On GPT-2 small in TransformerLens, replicate the IOI (Indirect Object Identification) task. For each of the known name-mover heads, compare two quantities: the head's clean direct effect (via path patching to the logits) versus the logit change when the head is mean-ablated. Quantify the gap and identify which backup heads switch on. Extend to a second task to test whether the size of the self-repair gap is consistent.

**Why it matters:** Safety auditors who 'knock out' a suspected harmful component and see little behavior change might wrongly conclude the component is unimportant, when self-repair is masking it. Measuring this gap calibrates how much to trust ablation-based safety claims.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al., 'Interpretability in the Wild' (arXiv:2211.00593) documents Backup Name Mover Heads in IOI. McGrath et al. 'Explorations of Self-Repair' (arXiv:2402.15390) shows self-repair across model families and that ablation direct effects can differ greatly from expectation.
