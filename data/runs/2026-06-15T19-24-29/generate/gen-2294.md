# Recursive Error Amplification: Do Stacked Oversight Layers Compound Their Mistakes?

**Problem:** An open problem is recursive oversight where each layer may have systematic errors. The worry is that errors compound across layers, but the empirical compounding behavior — does a two-layer 'model checks a model that checks the answer' chain do worse than a single layer on items where layers share a bias? — is rarely measured directly.

**Direction:** Build a 2-3 layer oversight chain of model verifiers on a QA task, deliberately giving layers a shared systematic bias (e.g. all primed to trust confident phrasing). Measure end-to-end accuracy vs a single layer, isolating items where the shared bias causes correlated errors to propagate. Test whether deliberately diversifying layer prompts breaks the correlation.

**Why it matters:** Directly attacks the open problem of recursive oversight surviving correlated layer errors — a medium-horizon concern central to amplification/recursive reward modeling. Cheap to simulate, no training, public data; the diversification test is an actionable design lever.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic recommended directions: recursive oversight effective when each layer has systematic errors; iterated amplification / recursive reward modeling (Christiano, Leike). NSO theory in Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight (arXiv:2504.18530). This empirically tests error compounding under shared bias.
