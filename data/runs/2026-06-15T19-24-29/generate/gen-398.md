# Sandbagging Detection via Probe Confidence vs. Stated Capability

**Problem:** ELK/quirky-model work shows internal probes can report knowledge the model withholds in output. A natural variation: can the same internal-vs-output gap detect deliberate underperformance (sandbagging) when a model is instructed to feign incompetence on safety-relevant questions?

**Direction:** Prompt an open model to deliberately answer below its ability ('pretend you don't know'). Train/use a knowledge probe on contrast pairs and measure the gap between probe-reported knowledge and stated answers under sandbagging vs. honest conditions. Vary only the sandbagging instruction strength.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging on capability evals is a recognized medium-horizon evaluation-gaming threat. Demonstrating that internal probes catch it (or don't) is a concrete contribution to the UK AISI 'what can models hide' problem and to eval integrity — external, inference-only.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2312.01037 quirky models — probes report LM knowledge independently of output; arXiv:2603.05494 'Censored LLMs as a Natural Testbed for Secret Knowledge Elicitation'; mechanistic anomaly detection flags untruthful behavior at 0.95 AUROC.
