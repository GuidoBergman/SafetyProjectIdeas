# Severing the Steganography Link with Paraphrase: A Black-Box Stress Test

**Problem:** In the encoded-reasoning threat chain, an unmonitorable hidden channel forms when a model embeds meaning in token choices a monitor cannot read. Paraphrasing the CoT is the proposed cut at this link, but its effectiveness against simple, hand-built encoding schemes lacks a cheap empirical baseline.

**Direction:** Construct a controlled task where a model is prompted to smuggle a secret bit/label through innocuous-looking CoT (e.g. via word choice or sentence length), with a second model decoding it. Insert an LLM paraphraser between encoder and decoder and measure decoding accuracy as a function of paraphrase strength. Map the dose-response of paraphrasing as a defense at the hidden-channel link.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrase defenses are a leading proposal for keeping CoT monitorable; an honest, reproducible measurement of how much hidden signal they destroy (and at what cost) helps safety cases and needs no training.

**Relevant context:** Redwood/'Early Signs of Steganographic Capabilities in Frontier LLMs' (arXiv:2507.02737); studies evaluate paraphrasing as a defense; one found fine-tuning Claude 3.7 to paraphrase removed detectable encoded reasoning.
