# Backcasting Honest Disclosure of Guessing: When Forced to Answer, Does the Model Admit It Guessed?

**Problem:** A success state is a model that, when it must answer despite uncertainty, honestly flags that it is guessing. Backward, we need to measure whether forcing an answer (no-abstain instruction) elicits honest uncertainty disclosure or confident confabulation on accessible models. This isolates honesty conditional on inability to abstain.

**Direction:** On items the open model is uncertain about (low sample-consistency), instruct it to always provide a best guess AND to flag explicitly when it is guessing. Measure how often it honestly marks guesses vs. presents guesses as confident facts, and whether the guess-flag correlates with actual error rate (i.e., is the disclosure itself calibrated).

**Why it matters:** Forced-answer settings (exams, structured outputs) are common, and honest guess-disclosure is a concrete, deployable honesty behavior. The probe is training-free and complements abstention research by studying honesty when abstention is unavailable.

**Relevant context:** Honesty over Accuracy / Reinforced Hesitation (arXiv 2511.11500); behaviorally calibrated RL (arXiv 2512.19920); MetaFaith faithful uncertainty (arXiv 2505.24858).
