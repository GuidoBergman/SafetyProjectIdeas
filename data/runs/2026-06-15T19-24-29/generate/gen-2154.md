# Persuasiveness-Correctness Decoupling as an Oversight Diagnostic

**Problem:** Khan et al. (2024) found models are more persuasive when arguing the truth — but whether this holds for *small* open models (the realistic debater scale for many setups) and across domains is unknown. If persuasiveness tracks correctness, judges can trust confidence; if not, confidence is a trap.

**Direction:** On QuALITY and a reasoning set, have small open debaters argue assigned (sometimes wrong) positions; score each argument's persuasiveness via a separate judge and check correlation with whether the position was correct. Map the persuasiveness-correctness correlation across debater sizes to find where it breaks.

**Why it matters:** Debate's safety case partly rests on 'truth is easier to argue.' Empirically locating where that assumption fails for small models is a sharp, lab-independent contribution to the oversight-reliability question.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. (2024) 'Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More Truthful Answers' — persuasiveness correlates with truth. Kenton et al. (2024) note dishonest debaters mislead with overconfident claims.
