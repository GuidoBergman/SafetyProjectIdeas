# DiscountBench: Measuring an LLM Agent's Effective Time-Horizon and Long-Horizon Goal Pursuit

**Problem:** Formal threat models about agents pursuing secret long-horizon goals assume the agent values distant outcomes. But no simple tool estimates an LLM agent's effective discount factor — how much it trades immediate reward for delayed reward — in controlled sequential decisions.

**Direction:** Put an open LLM agent in small sequential-choice environments offering immediate-small vs delayed-large payoffs at varying delays, fit a discount-factor parameter to its choices, and report the estimated effective horizon per model. Test whether prompting the agent with a long-term goal increases its measured patience and whether it sacrifices verbalized transparency to pursue the long-term payoff.

**Why it matters:** Long-horizon goal pursuit (especially when hidden) is a flagged high-priority model-organism threat. Measuring an agent's effective discount factor operationalizes 'how long-horizon is this model really', a prerequisite for studying secret long-horizon planning. Tiny environments + inference only — strongly counterfactual, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in instrumental-convergence / power-seeking theory (arXiv:1912.01683) and InstrumentalEval (arXiv:2502.12206), plus the steering emphasis on secret-long-horizon-goal organisms. Discount-factor fitting is intro RL/stats.
