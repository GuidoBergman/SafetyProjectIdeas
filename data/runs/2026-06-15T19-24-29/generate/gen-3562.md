# Backcasting Persona-Robust Honesty: Mapping the System-Prompt Manifold That Suppresses Truthful Self-Report

**Problem:** A success state is a model whose honesty is invariant to benign-looking persona system prompts. Working backward, we need a quantified map of which persona dimensions most degrade honesty in accessible open models. Recent work shows persona prefixes change misalignment rates by ~90% and that professional-identity prompts suppress honest self-representation, but no compact, reproducible 'honesty sensitivity to persona' map exists for small open models.

**Direction:** Construct a small factorial battery of system prompts varying persona traits (confident expert, eager-to-please assistant, contrarian, salesperson, etc.) and apply each to an open model answering TruthfulQA and a set of 'admit you don't know' probe questions. Measure honesty/abstention rate per persona cell and rank persona axes by effect size, producing a heatmap of which prompt features most erode honesty.

**Why it matters:** Persona effects on honesty are an Anthropic-recommended open problem. A reproducible sensitivity map lets deployers know which innocuous system-prompt choices silently degrade honesty — a diffuse, long-horizon risk that needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Persona Vectors (arXiv 2507.21509) and 'misaligned persona' features replicate in Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B; 'When Models Fabricate Credentials' (arXiv 2511.21569) shows professional identity suppresses honest self-representation; Spilling the Beans (arXiv 2511.06626) shows 'uncompromising honesty' persona improves self-report.
