# Reproducing the induction-head phase transition and checking its timing on a different tokenizer/data

**Problem:** Two-layer transformers develop induction heads at a sharp phase transition that coincides with a bump in the loss curve and the onset of in-context learning. The canonical demonstrations use specific synthetic Markov data or particular corpora. Whether the *timing and sharpness* of the induction-head phase transition is robust to a different data-generating process (e.g., higher-order Markov chains, or a different tokenization granularity) is not well-mapped.

**Direction:** Train a 2-layer attention-only transformer (trains fast) on synthetic Markov sequences, reproduce the induction-head emergence and the loss bump, then vary the Markov order and the vocabulary size. Detect the induction head via the standard prefix-matching attention pattern, and report how transition timing shifts. Compare to the published 'abrupt emergence' picture.

**Why it matters:** Induction heads are the cleanest known case of an abrupt, mechanistically-understood capability emergence — a model organism for emergent in-context learning. Characterizing how robust the emergence is to data structure deepens the empirical science of phase transitions in training, which underlies forecasting of surprising capability jumps, and is doable entirely on toy models with no proprietary resources.

**Relevant context:** Induction-head literature: phase transition coincides with ICL onset and a loss bump; 'On the Emergence of Induction Heads for In-Context Learning' (arXiv:2511.01033) and 'Two-Layer Transformers Provably Represent Induction Heads on Any-Order Markov Chains' (arXiv:2508.07208).
