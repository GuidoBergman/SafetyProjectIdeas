# Refusal Recovery: Can Models Catch Themselves Mid-Generation After a Forced Affirmative Start?

**Problem:** Prefilling and shallow alignment assume that once the model starts complying it continues. But anecdotally some models 'recover' and refuse partway through. This self-correction capability — a potential latent defense — is essentially uncharacterized as a phenomenon.

**Direction:** Force open models to begin with an affirmative compliance token, then let them generate freely. Classify outputs into (i) full comply, (ii) mid-stream recovery to refusal, (iii) hedge. Measure recovery rate per model, per harm category, and per affirmative-opener strength, and inspect whether recovery correlates with specific trigger phrases ('However,', 'Actually,') in the trace.

**Why it matters:** If recovery is a real latent ability, deepening alignment could amplify it as a cheap defense against prefilling — directly addressing the open problem of deepening alignment beyond the first tokens. Pure inference on open models; novel framing (studying the defense's latent presence, not just the attack).

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) propose deepening alignment throughout the generative distribution as the fix for prefilling; 'Inference-Time Vulnerability Beyond Shallow Safety' (arXiv:2606.04778) analyzes alignment along generation trajectories, implying recovery dynamics exist. Prefilling reproducible via template editing on any open model.
