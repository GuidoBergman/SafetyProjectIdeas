# Lie-Detector Transferability Test: Do Cheap Deception Probes Survive Distribution Shift?

**Problem:** Lie/deception detectors for LLMs are proposed, but their reliability across different lie types and prompts is questioned (Liars' Bench). There is no small, accessible study testing whether a simple behavioral lie-detection heuristic transfers across deception settings.

**Direction:** Using an open model prompted to lie vs. tell the truth across several scenario types (factual lies, omissions, sycophantic agreement), build one or two cheap behavioral detectors (e.g. response-length/hedging features, consistency-under-rephrase) and measure detection AUC within vs. across scenario types to quantify transfer failure.

**Why it matters:** Tests a core assumption behind deception monitoring (that a detector generalizes) using only open models and behavioral signals; informs how much to trust cheap lie detectors, a long-horizon alignment concern.

**Relevant context:** 'Liars' Bench: Evaluating Lie Detectors for Language Models' (arXiv:2511.16035). 'From Hallucination to Scheming: A Unified Taxonomy and Benchmark Analysis for LLM Deception' (arXiv:2604.04788). Behavioral (non-weights) lie detection transferability is an open, accessible measurement gap aligned with detecting concealed/deceptive behavior.
