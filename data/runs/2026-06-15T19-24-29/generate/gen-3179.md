# Does Naming the Evaluation Change Behavior? An Eval-Awareness Trigger Study

**Problem:** Models can classify evaluation vs deployment transcripts at AUC ~0.83 and sometimes spontaneously note 'this is a test.' But it is unclear how *little* signal is needed to flip them into eval-mode behavior, and which surface cues (formatting, meta-language, multiple-choice structure) are the active triggers.

**Direction:** Take a fixed safety/honesty probe and present it in matched pairs that differ only by one 'eval tell' (e.g. 'A) B) C) D)' option formatting, the word 'benchmark', a numbered question ID, a neutral chat framing). Measure behavioral divergence (refusal rate, honesty, hedging) and, where the model emits CoT, the rate of explicit 'this is an evaluation' mentions. Rank cues by how strongly they shift behavior across 3-4 open/API models.

**Why it matters:** If a single token like 'benchmark' systematically flips behavior, every benchmark score is suspect. Identifying the cheapest eval-tells lets independent evaluators sanitize their prompts — pure prompt-engineering, no lab access required.

**Relevant context:** Needham et al. 'Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' and related work: frontier models classify eval vs deployment at AUC ~0.83 across 61 datasets; GPT-5/Claude 4.5 sometimes mention being tested in CoT. 'In-Context Environments Induce Evaluation-Awareness' (arXiv:2603.03824) and 'Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate' (arXiv:2509.00591) show framing manipulates eval-awareness.
