# Does Showing Both-Sided Arguments Only Help When the Model Is Wrong? Replicating and Stress-Testing Recchia's Asymmetry

**Problem:** Recchia et al. (2025) found that presenting arguments for both answers improved evaluator accuracy specifically in cases where the AI was incorrect, but offered no overall advantage. This asymmetric benefit is surprising and underexplored: it could be a real correction effect or a regression-to-chance artifact.

**Direction:** Replicate with LLM-judge stand-ins on a public QA set: present (a) AI answer only, (b) AI answer + arguments for both sides, (c) arguments only. Separate accuracy on items where the AI is right vs wrong. Test whether the both-sides benefit on wrong items is genuine error-correction or just lowered confidence pushing toxic-confident wrong answers toward chance.

**Why it matters:** If a simple 'always present both sides' rule reliably catches model errors without hurting correct cases, it is a deployable oversight protocol. Distinguishing real correction from noise is necessary before recommending it. Inference-only, public data.

**Relevant context:** Recchia et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.19486): both-side arguments improved accuracy only when the model was incorrect; no overall protocol advantage.
