# From-Scratch Goal Misgeneralization: Measuring the Proxy-Decorrelation Threshold

**Problem:** Goal misgeneralization (e.g. CoinRun's 'move right' vs 'get coin') shows correctly-specified rewards still yield wrong learned goals. Work suggests ~2% varied data decorrelates the proxy, but the decorrelation threshold as a function of confound strength is not systematically charted in a minimal setting.

**Direction:** Build a minimal gridworld where a true goal (reach object) is confounded with a proxy (go to a fixed location) during training. Train a tiny tabular/Q-learning or small-MLP agent (fast on CPU/single GPU). Sweep the fraction of training episodes where proxy and goal are decorrelated, and measure OOD goal-pursuit at test. Produce a threshold curve relating confound strength and decorrelation fraction to misgeneralization.

**Why it matters:** Goal misgeneralization is a core inner-alignment / inductive-bias phenomenon. A controlled, from-scratch threshold study gives a quantitative handle on how much diversity prevents it — feasible in 30h and fully counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** Langosco et al. 'Goal Misgeneralization in Deep RL' (PMLR v162) — CoinRun 'move right' result. 'Mitigating Goal Misgeneralization' (RLC 2025) finds ~2% varied levels decorrelate proxy and true reward.
