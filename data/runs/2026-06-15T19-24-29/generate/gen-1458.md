# Probe-vs-judge breakdown under distribution shift: where do concept-vector hallucination probes fail?

**Problem:** Internal probes built on concept vectors reportedly outperform judge models for hallucination detection, but representation-based detectors are documented to fail out-of-distribution, and some 'truth' probes actually track spurious features (e.g., negation flips their accuracy to random).

**Direction:** Train a simple linear probe on a concept-vector readout for hallucination/factuality on an open model using one dataset, then stress-test it OOD: negated statements, paraphrases, a different topic domain. Compare probe accuracy vs. an LLM-judge baseline across these shifts to map exactly where the probe's apparent superiority disappears.

**Why it matters:** The 'probes beat judges' headline could be dangerously misleading if it only holds in-distribution. Documenting the shift conditions that break probe-based monitoring is directly safety-relevant and exactly the kind of fuzzy-robustness evaluation that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Directly follows the Beaglehole et al. 'probes outperform judges' result; grounded in OOD-failure and negation-sensitivity findings (arXiv 2509.19372, 2511.07318, SAPLMA critiques). Linear probing, open model, beginner-feasible.
