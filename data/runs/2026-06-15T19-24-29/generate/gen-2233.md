# Replicating 'Stronger Debater Helps Judge' With a Capability-Gap Sweep

**Problem:** Prior debate work finds that interacting with a stronger debater improves a weaker judge's accuracy. The relationship between the size of the debater-judge capability gap and the judge's accuracy gain has not been mapped as a clean dose-response curve in the small-open-model regime.

**Direction:** Fix a small open judge model and run debate with debaters of increasing capability (e.g. 0.5B, 1.8B, 7B, 14B from one open family) on a QuALITY-style hidden-passage task. Replicate the 'stronger debater helps' claim, then plot judge accuracy as a function of the debater-judge capability gap to see whether the benefit is monotonic, plateaus, or reverses past some gap.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight ultimately requires very weak overseers judging very strong systems. Knowing whether the judge benefit keeps growing or breaks down at large capability gaps directly tests the asymptotic promise of debate. Single-GPU feasible with one open model family.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 2024 and earlier debate work: interacting with a stronger agent improves the judge's accuracy. 'Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight' (arXiv:2504.18530) studies capability gaps theoretically. A simple empirical gap-sweep on open models is an accessible replication twist.
