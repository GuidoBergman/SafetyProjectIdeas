# Adding-Mistakes Intervention Replicated to Build a 'CoT Self-Correction' Honesty Map

**Problem:** Lanham's 'adding mistakes' intervention (insert an error into the CoT and see if the model corrects or blindly follows it) was run on one model; whether open reasoning models honestly catch injected errors or rationalize them is untested at scale.

**Direction:** Generate a CoT, programmatically inject a wrong intermediate step, feed it back, and measure whether the model (a) notices and corrects, (b) silently follows the error to a wrong answer, or (c) reaches the right answer ignoring its own stated (wrong) step. Run across several open models. Twist: turn the adding-mistakes test into a per-model 'error-honesty profile' (correct vs follow vs ignore rates).

**Why it matters:** A model that silently follows an injected error has a CoT that does not reflect honest error-checking; one that ignores its own wrong step but still answers correctly has a non-causal CoT. Both are monitorability risks detectable with pure inference.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. (2307.13702): adding-mistakes intervention probes whether the model's answer depends on CoT correctness. Pathological-CoT taxonomy (arXiv 2602.13904) distinguishes post-hoc vs internalized reasoning.
