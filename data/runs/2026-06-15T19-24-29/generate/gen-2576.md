# Refusal-vs-Detection Conflation: Does a Monitor Confuse 'Unsafe Content' With 'Covert Attack'?

**Problem:** Control monitors are supposed to flag covert sabotage/scheming, not generic content-policy violations. But monitors built from chat LLMs may conflate the two — flagging benign-but-edgy content while missing subtle, policy-compliant-looking sabotage. This conflation between safety-refusal training and control-detection is unmeasured and could systematically misdirect audits.

**Direction:** Construct four trajectory classes: (a) benign+clean, (b) benign-but-edgy-content (e.g. discusses malware defensively), (c) covert sabotage with clean-looking content, (d) overtly harmful. Score all with monitors and check whether suspiciousness tracks 'covert attack' (target) or 'edgy content' (refusal proxy). Quantify how much of the monitor's signal is actually content-policy rather than control-relevant.

**Why it matters:** If monitors are really firing on content-policy cues, they will both waste audit budget on edgy-but-safe work and miss clean-looking sabotage — a fundamental validity problem for trusted monitoring. A clean disentangling experiment, doable with API access only.
