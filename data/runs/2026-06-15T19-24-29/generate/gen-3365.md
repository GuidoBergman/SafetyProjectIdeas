# Does Asking for a Confidence Estimate Change the Answer? Observer-Effect on Honesty

**Problem:** Eliciting honesty signals (confidence, self-critique) is assumed to be non-invasive, but the act of asking a model to estimate its confidence or justify itself may itself change the answer it gives — an observer effect that would bias every honesty evaluation.

**Direction:** Compare answer distributions on the same questions across three conditions: answer-only, answer+confidence, answer+self-justification. Measure how often the answer flips purely because a confidence/justification request was appended. Characterize which question types are most observer-sensitive.

**Why it matters:** If measuring honesty perturbs the behavior being measured, honesty evals need redesign. This methodological check is cheap, training-free, and broadly improves the rigor of the whole subfield.

**Relevant context:** Verbalized-confidence and self-justification elicitation are central to honesty work (arXiv 2512.08093, 2505.21772). Introspection literature (arXiv 2503.07513) warns self-reports may not causally reflect internal state. No catalogue idea tests the elicitation-induced observer effect directly.
