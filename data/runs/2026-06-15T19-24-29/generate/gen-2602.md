# Constitutional Black-Box Monitoring Failures: Where Do Rule-Based Scheming Monitors Miss?

**Problem:** Constitutional black-box monitoring scores agent behavior against written rules to detect scheming, but rule-based monitors likely have systematic blind spots (novel scheming forms not covered by any rule). The coverage gaps are not characterized for beginners.

**Direction:** Take a small set of constitutional rules for a black-box scheming monitor on an open model, plus a labeled set of scheming and benign trajectories spanning several scheming styles. Measure per-style catch rate and identify which scheming styles fall through gaps between rules. Test whether adding one targeted rule closes a gap without raising the false-positive rate.

**Why it matters:** Rule-based monitors are interpretable and deployable but only as good as their rule coverage. Mapping their blind spots is a practical anomaly-detection contribution that needs no model internals or lab data.

**Relevant context:** Constitutional Black-Box Monitoring for Scheming in LLM Agents (arXiv:2603.00829). Connects to the anomaly-detection open problem (Anthropic recommended directions) and Petri/Bloom open auditing tooling.
