# Is the steered behavior persistent across a long conversation, or does it decay?

**Problem:** Sustained-control chain: apply safety vector at turn 1 -> behavior is safe early -> but over many turns of context, the accumulating conversation may overwhelm the fixed vector -> safety decays late in long chats. Multi-turn jailbreaks exploit exactly this contextual drift. Whether a fixed steering vector holds across turns is an under-measured persistence link.

**Direction:** On an open chat model with a fixed refusal vector, run multi-turn conversations that gradually build toward a harmful request (escalation pattern). Measure refusal effectiveness as a function of turn depth, with and without the vector. Success criterion: refusal rate vs turn number, showing whether and when fixed-vector safety decays under contextual drift.

**Why it matters:** Multi-turn drift is a leading real-world jailbreak vector; quantifying steering-vector decay over turns shows whether static activation interventions are enough or need to be re-applied/scaled online. Pure forward-pass conversation simulation, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** 'Steering Dialogue Dynamics for Robustness against Multi-turn Jailbreaking' (arXiv 2503.00187) motivates contextual drift. Beaglehole et al. fixed concept vectors. The turn-depth decay curve for a fixed vector is the contribution.
