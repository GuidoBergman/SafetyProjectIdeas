# Verbosity Bias as a Reward-Hacking Channel Replicated in Pairwise Oversight

**Problem:** RLHF reward models are known to develop a length/verbosity bias that policies exploit. This is usually measured in reward-model training; whether the same verbosity exploit fools an LLM-judge acting as an overseer in a pairwise comparison, across model families, is a clean replication gap.

**Direction:** Replicate verbosity-bias measurement but in the overseer-judge framing: pad correct-but-terse answers vs incorrect-but-verbose answers and have several small open judges (different families) choose the better one. Measure how often verbosity flips the judge to the wrong answer, comparing across families and answer domains (factual QA vs reasoning).

**Why it matters:** Verbosity is one of the simplest reward-hacking channels for fooling an overseer without genuine quality. Quantifying how exploitable LLM judges are to pure length manipulation, across families, tells us how much oversight pipelines must defend against it. Cheap, open, no training.

**Relevant context:** Reward-hacking surveys (Weng 2024; arXiv:2209.00626) identify length/verbosity bias: reward models correlate higher scores with verbosity rather than quality. LLM-judge bias work confirms verbosity bias in judges. The pairwise-overseer framing with cross-family comparison is the twist.
