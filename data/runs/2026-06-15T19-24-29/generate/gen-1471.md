# Steering detectability: can a model 'notice' it is being steered, and does that break monitoring?

**Problem:** Recent work suggests activation steering can be detected from within the model. If a model can detect (and potentially respond to) being steered, a steered model's outputs may not reflect the intended behavior cleanly—a failure mode for using steering as a controlled probe.

**Direction:** On an open model, apply a steering vector and train a simple linear probe on a later-layer activation to classify steered-vs-unsteered forward passes. Test whether steering is internally detectable, and whether high detectability coincides with degraded or anomalous outputs (a signature that the model 'reacts').

**Why it matters:** If steering leaves a detectable internal trace, future capable models could in principle behave differently when steered, undermining steering as a trustworthy intervention or eval tool. Characterizing detectability now is a forward-looking, lab-independent safety probe.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Steering Awareness: Detecting Activation Steering from Within' (arXiv 2511.21399). Uses linear probing on cached activations of an open model; inference-only.
