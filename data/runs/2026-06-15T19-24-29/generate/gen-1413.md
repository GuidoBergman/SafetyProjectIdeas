# Replicating cross-model activation transfer with a learned linear map on safety concepts

**Problem:** Work shows a steering vector learned on a 0.5-1B model can, via a learned linear map, neutralize behaviors in a larger model. This was framed around hazardous-behavior neutralization; replicating the linear-map transfer for a refusal vector on a new family is an accessible follow-up.

**Direction:** Fit a steering vector on a small open model, learn a simple linear map between the small and a larger same-family model's residual streams (using paired activations on shared prompts), then test whether the mapped vector steers the larger model. Twist: replicate the small-to-large transfer recipe on a different family and concept.

**Why it matters:** Cheap small-model steering that transfers to large models is strategically valuable infrastructure — it lets external researchers prototype controls for systems they can't fully run. Independently validating the transfer recipe is exactly the lab-independent contribution prioritized here.

**Relevant context:** 'Linear Representation Transferability Hypothesis' (arXiv:2506.00653); 'Activation Space Interventions Can Be Transferred Between LLMs' (arXiv:2503.04429); Beaglehole et al. universal-steering framing.
