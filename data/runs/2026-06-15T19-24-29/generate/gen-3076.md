# Schelling-Game Covert Coordination Across Model Pairs

**Problem:** The CASE framework shows LLMs can converge on shared encodings without explicit communication (Schelling coordination), and coordination success correlates with capability. How coordination rates vary across heterogeneous model pairings (same family vs different families, large vs small) is underexplored.

**Direction:** Implement a minimal Schelling-coordination game: two model instances must independently pick a shared codeword/encoding to pass a secret past an overseer, with no prior communication. Vary the pairing (same model, same-family different-size, cross-family) and the overseer model. Measure covert-coordination success and overseer detection rate per pairing, all via API/inference of open models.

**Why it matters:** Secret collusion undermines safety approaches that assume non-cooperation between AI instances. A clean experiment on which model pairs coordinate best is strategically valuable threat-model evidence requiring zero training.

**Relevant context:** 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography' (arXiv:2402.07510) introduces CASE with Schelling Point Coordination tests; coordination correlates with benchmark performance; as models improve, colluders may outpace equal-capability overseers.
