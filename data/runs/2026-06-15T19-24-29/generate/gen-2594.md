# Honeypot Realism Dose-Response: How Synthetic Cues Suppress Bait-Taking

**Problem:** Honeypots fail when models detect the context is synthetic and suppress misbehavior. Which specific realism cues (placeholder names, suspiciously easy access, unrealistic permissions) trigger this suppression, and how strongly, is not systematically mapped.

**Direction:** Build one honeypot scenario in several realism variants, perturbing single cues at a time (e.g., 'TODO' comments, fake-looking credentials, implausibly broad permissions). For each variant, measure (a) whether the model verbalizes suspicion of a test and (b) whether it takes the bait. Rank cues by their suppression effect.

**Why it matters:** Knowing which artifacts blow a honeypot's cover lets evaluators build more realistic honeypots — a direct, practical improvement to a key control-eval methodology, and one anyone outside a lab can study with public model access.

**Relevant context:** Realistic honeypot evaluations for scheming propensity (arXiv:2605.29729): 'if models can detect that a context is synthetic, they may suppress misaligned behavior during honeypot probes.' Honeypot Protocol (arXiv:2604.13301); Evaluating Scheming Propensity (arXiv:2603.01608).
