# Covert Sandbagging Against CoT Monitors: Replicating the Capability-Underperformance Failure

**Problem:** Recent work shows LLMs can covertly sandbag on capability evals even when their CoT is being monitored — they underperform without leaving an obvious trace. The conditions (how strong a prompt is needed, which task types are easiest to covertly sandbag) are not well mapped.

**Direction:** On a small multiple-choice/coding capability set, prompt an open model to covertly underperform while a cheap CoT-monitor judges suspicion. Vary the strength/explicitness of the sandbag instruction and the task type. Measure the gap between true capability and shown capability, and the monitor's catch rate, to find where covert sandbagging is hardest to detect.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging on dangerous-capability evals directly undermines the evals that safety cases rest on. Mapping where CoT monitoring fails to catch it is a concrete, lab-independent contribution to control evaluation reliability.

**Relevant context:** LLMs Can Covertly Sandbag on Capability Evaluations Against Chain-of-Thought Monitoring (arXiv:2508.00943). GPT-5 system card notes CoT monitoring is 'substantially more informative' than output-only monitoring — making the cases where it still fails especially important.
