# Decomposing weak-to-strong oversight into an interpretability slice: does the weak supervisor's error show up as a probe-detectable 'I'm unsure' signal?

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong generalization needs to know when the weak supervisor is wrong. Interpretability sub-problem: in a small 'weak' open model labeling data for a stronger one, is there an internal uncertainty/confusion signal (probe-detectable) that predicts when its labels are wrong, so the strong model can down-weight them?

**Direction:** Decompose oversight-error mitigation into an uncertainty-probe experiment. Run a small open model as labeler on a task with known ground truth, train a probe on its activations to predict label-correctness, and measure whether filtering low-probe-confidence labels improves the quality of the supervision signal.

**Why it matters:** Gives a cheap, mechanistic handle on 'when is the weak overseer wrong' — central to scalable oversight, and doable with open models on one GPU, no training of large models. A working probe-based label-filter is a concrete, communicable result.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI weak-to-strong generalization (open code); scalable_oversight systematic-oversight-error open problem; activation-based uncertainty probing.
