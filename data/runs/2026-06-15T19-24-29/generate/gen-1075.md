# Does Fine-Tuning on a Shared Teacher Increase Downstream Homogeneity? A Mini Replication

**Problem:** Jailbreak-transfer work showed fine-tuning on one model's outputs raises similarity to it. A scoped behavioral follow-up: does light fine-tuning (LoRA) of two different small base models on the SAME teacher's outputs make them measurably more homogeneous to each other — quantifying the homogenizing pull of synthetic-data sharing?

**Direction:** Take two small open base models. LoRA-fine-tune each on a small shared set of a single teacher model's responses (a few hundred examples, runs in under an hour on one GPU). Measure inter-model homogeneity before vs. after. Test whether shared synthetic data converges the two students.

**Why it matters:** Synthetic data from a few teachers floods training pipelines; if it homogenizes the ecosystem, that's a root cause of systemic risk. Scoped to a single-GPU LoRA, no training from scratch.

**Relevant context:** Jailbreak Transferability from Shared Representations / distillation raises similarity (arXiv:2506.12913); Correlated Errors (arXiv:2506.07962).
