# Quantization x Jailbreak Robustness: Does Compression Compound Attack Susceptibility?

**Problem:** Two interacting failures: quantization-induced degradation and jailbreak vulnerability. Consumer deployment relies on 4-bit/8-bit quantized open models. If quantization disproportionately erodes the (already shallow) safety alignment relative to general capability, then compression and jailbreak compound—cheap-to-deploy models are also cheap-to-break.

**Direction:** Take an open model at FP16, INT8, and INT4 (bitsandbytes/GPTQ). For each, measure jailbreak attack success (fixed templates) and general capability (a small benchmark) to compute a 'safety-per-capability' ratio. Success criterion: evidence that quantization degrades safety faster than capability, with the attack types most sensitive to compression.

**Why it matters:** Quantized open models dominate lab-external deployment; if compression compounds attack surface, the most accessible models are the least safe—a diffuse, real-world risk labs (running full-precision) under-prioritize. Inference-only, consumer GPU, exactly the target hardware.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2406.05946 shallow alignment implies safety lives in a thin slice of the distribution plausibly sensitive to quantization noise; AISafetyLab provides attack/eval tooling. Quantization toolchains are standard and free.
