# Do concept vectors extracted from contrastive pairs encode the concept or a dataset artifact?

**Problem:** CAA-style concept vectors are computed from differences between positive/negative example sets. If those sets differ in some incidental way (length, topic, formatting), the 'concept' vector may encode the artifact instead. Beaglehole-style methods could be capturing spurious directions, and no simple artifact-control protocol is standard.

**Direction:** For 2-3 safety concepts, build the standard contrast set and a deliberately confounded one (matched concept but with an injected nuisance like longer negatives or topic shift). Extract vectors from each and test whether the vector steers/detects the intended concept or the nuisance (e.g. apply it to nuisance-only contrasts). Propose and validate a cheap 'placebo concept' control.

**Why it matters:** Provides a reusable methodological safeguard for everyone building safety concept vectors outside labs, where careful dataset hygiene is the only defense. Cheap, training-free, broadly useful.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. linear concept extraction from samples (Science 2026). CAA contrast-pair method (arxiv 2312.06681). RepE.
