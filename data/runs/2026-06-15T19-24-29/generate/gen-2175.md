# Stacking Two Weak Judges: Does Aggregating Biased Overseers Help?

**Problem:** Recursive oversight protocols assume each layer may have systematic errors (Anthropic open problem). A basic untested variation: does aggregating the verdicts of two DIFFERENTLY-biased weak judges reduce error vs a single judge, or do correlated biases cancel the benefit?

**Direction:** On debate transcripts, evaluate each transcript with two distinct small open judges chosen to have different known biases (e.g., one verbose-biased, one position-biased). Compare single-judge accuracy to (a) majority/averaged verdicts and (b) a 'meta-judge' prompt that sees both verdicts. Decompose where aggregation helps vs where shared bias defeats it.

**Why it matters:** Whether ensembling weak overseers genuinely de-biases oversight, or merely launders correlated error, is foundational for recursive protocols. Cheap, inference-only, and directly targets the systematic-error open problem.
