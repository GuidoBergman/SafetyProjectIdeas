# Restoration-Errors / CoT Robustness Replicated With Self-Generated vs Injected Reasoning

**Problem:** Faithfulness studies usually inject perturbations into model-generated CoT, but it is unclear whether a model treats its own generated reasoning differently from externally supplied reasoning of equal quality — a confound for all corruption-based faithfulness tests.

**Direction:** Compare answer-dependence when the CoT is the model's own vs a paraphrase generated by a different model vs human-written, all of equivalent content, then apply truncation/filler interventions to each. Twist: replicate the corruption-faithfulness battery while varying the provenance of the CoT to test whether faithfulness metrics are confounded by self vs other authorship.

**Why it matters:** If faithfulness metrics behave differently on self- vs externally-authored CoT, published faithfulness numbers may not mean what they claim — a cheap methodological audit valuable to the whole field and runnable with inference only.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. (2307.13702) corruption suite and 'A Causal Lens for Evaluating Faithfulness Metrics' (arXiv 2502.18848) examine what faithfulness metrics actually capture. Provenance of CoT is an uncontrolled variable.
