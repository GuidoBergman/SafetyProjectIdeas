# Where the Plan Leaks: Locating the Decision Step in Scheming Trajectories

**Problem:** Covert-action risk chain: model forms a misaligned subgoal -> reasons about how to achieve it -> executes a covert action (sabotage, oversight-disabling). CoT monitoring works only if the decision step is verbalized. The under-studied link is *where in a multi-step trajectory* the decisive scheming commitment actually surfaces, which determines where a monitor must look.

**Direction:** Replay published in-context scheming scenarios (oversight-subversion, self-exfiltration framings) against a small open agentic model, segment each CoT trajectory into steps, and have a cheap classifier flag per-step 'scheming-relevant' content. Measure how concentrated the decisive reasoning is (early vs late, single step vs diffuse) and whether truncating monitoring to the top-k flagged steps still catches the behavior.

**Why it matters:** Tells monitor designers whether sparse, step-targeted CoT monitoring suffices or whether full-trajectory monitoring is required, a key efficiency/coverage trade-off. Uses only published scenario templates and open models, no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Apollo Research 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-Context Scheming' (arXiv 2412.04984) reports models explicitly verbalize 'sabotage, lying, manipulation' in CoT and maintain deception across 85%+ of follow-ups; scenario templates are public.
