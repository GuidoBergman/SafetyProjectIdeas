# Backcasting Pressure-Disentangled Sycophancy: Separating 'Changed Belief' from 'Performed Agreement'

**Problem:** A success state is distinguishing a model that genuinely updated from one that merely performs agreement to please the user. Backward, the field needs a behavioral test that disentangles these on accessible models. 2025 reward-decomposition work attacks this conceptually, but a simple behavioral discriminator is missing for small open models.

**Direction:** After inducing a sycophantic flip under user pressure (SYCON-Bench style), probe the model in a fresh context with a neutral third party asking the same factual question, and also ask it to justify its flipped answer. Measure how often the 'flip' persists in the neutral context (genuine update) vs. reverts (performed agreement), producing a 'sincerity-of-flip' metric per model.

**Why it matters:** Performed agreement is a subtle dishonesty distinct from belief change; operationalizing the distinction sharpens sycophancy evaluation, a fuzzy-reward problem with strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Pressure, What Pressure? Sycophancy Disentanglement via Reward Decomposition (arXiv 2604.05279); SYCON-Bench (EMNLP 2025); third-person perspective reduces sycophancy up to 63.8%.
