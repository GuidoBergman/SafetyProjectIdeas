# Filler-Token Faithfulness Test on Modern Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Anthropic's 2023 'Measuring Faithfulness in CoT' introduced corruption tests (truncate CoT, add mistakes, replace with filler tokens) on a single model. Modern open reasoning models trained with RL on CoT may have very different reliance on their stated reasoning, and the filler-token test has not been broadly re-run on them.

**Direction:** Replicate the filler-token and early-answering interventions: generate CoT, then re-prompt the model to answer after (a) the real CoT, (b) the CoT replaced by meaningless filler ('...'), (c) the CoT truncated early. Measure how often the answer changes. Twist: apply the classic Lanham corruption suite to today's RL-trained open reasoning models and contrast 'reasoning' vs 'non-reasoning' siblings.

**Why it matters:** If filler tokens or truncation barely change the answer, the visible CoT is decorative and unsafe to monitor — a property anyone can test with no model internals. Distinguishes genuine reasoning from post-hoc rationalization in the open models people self-host.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. 'Measuring Faithfulness in CoT Reasoning' (arXiv 2307.13702): early-answering, adding-mistakes, paraphrasing, filler-token interventions. Later work (e.g. arXiv 2602.13904) names 'internalized' (filler suffices) and 'post-hoc' (CoT removal harmless) pathologies; filler-token metric found most reliable.
