# Benign-Context Dilution Effect on Harmful-Request Detection

**Problem:** Real prompts embed a harmful request inside long benign context; benchmark prompts are usually short and isolated. Whether padding a harmful request with benign filler lowers refusal rates (a cheap jailbreak / measurement confound) is under-tested.

**Direction:** Take harmful benchmark prompts and create variants padded with increasing amounts of benign context (0, 500, 2000, 8000 tokens). Measure refusal-rate decay as context length grows on open long-context models, isolating dilution from content.

**Why it matters:** If safety degrades simply with benign context length, short-prompt benchmarks over-state safety for realistic long-context deployments. Directly relevant to deployment-time safety; only inference needed.

**Relevant context:** Long-context degradation and context-injection jailbreaks documented (Visual Contextual Attack arXiv 2507.02844 for images; lost-in-the-middle phenomena). AgentHarm/SorryBench prompts can be padded; open long-context models (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.1) available.
