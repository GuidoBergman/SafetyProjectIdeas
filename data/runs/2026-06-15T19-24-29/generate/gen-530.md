# Mechanism-Design Stress Test: Are LLM Judges Strategyproof?

**Problem:** Scalable oversight often relies on models reporting on each other (debate, peer evaluation), but mechanism design from economics shows such schemes can be manipulable; we don't know empirically whether LLM-based reporting mechanisms are strategyproof.

**Direction:** Borrow strategyproofness/incentive-compatibility tests from mechanism design. Implement a small peer-grading or debate-judging mechanism with open models, then have a 'strategic' agent misreport to maximize its own score and measure how much it can gain. Test whether known strategyproof mechanisms (e.g., second-price-style or peer-prediction scoring) reduce manipulability when applied to LLM reporters.

**Why it matters:** Brings rigorous incentive-compatibility theory to bear on oversight protocols, quantifying their manipulability and whether classic mechanism-design fixes transfer to LLMs. Long-horizon multi-agent oversight is under-prioritized by labs and needs no proprietary resources.

**Relevant context:** Mechanism design and peer-prediction scoring rules are mature in economics; debate and recursive oversight are core formal alignment proposals. Relates to Schelling/collusion findings on LLM coordination.
