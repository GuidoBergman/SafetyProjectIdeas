# Network Resilience / Percolation Theory for Guardrail Failure Cascades

**Problem:** When part of a safety system is compromised, does protection fail gracefully or collapse suddenly? Percolation theory studies exactly such phase transitions in networks.

**Direction:** Transfer percolation theory. Model the set of refusal-triggering features as a 'network' and progressively disable/obfuscate features (remove keywords, neutralize framing cues) on an open model, measuring refusal rate as a function of fraction of features removed. Test for a percolation-style critical threshold where refusal collapses abruptly rather than linearly.

**Why it matters:** Reveals whether alignment degrades gracefully or has a brittle cliff, informing how much obfuscation a deployment can tolerate. Black-box feature ablation, single GPU, no training.

**Relevant context:** Percolation theory describes critical thresholds in network connectivity. Context: shallow alignment suggests brittle, concentrated triggers (Qi et al. arXiv:2406.05946). Obfuscation-based bypasses are documented in jailbreak surveys (arXiv:2510.15476).
