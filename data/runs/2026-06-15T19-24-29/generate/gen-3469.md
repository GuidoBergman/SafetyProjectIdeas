# Reward-Hack Verbalization Mini-Suite in Toy Gameable Environments

**Problem:** The striking result that models verbalize a reward hack in <2% of cases despite using it ~99% of the time was shown in proprietary environments. There is no small, open, reproducible suite of *gameable toy tasks* where one can measure verbalization rate of an exploit for open models.

**Direction:** Design 3-4 toy tasks with an obvious exploitable shortcut (e.g., an answer leaked in metadata, a grader keyword that always scores high, a formatting trick). Run open models, detect when they use the shortcut (high success on gameable items), and check whether the CoT mentions the shortcut. Report per-task use-rate vs. verbalization-rate.

**Why it matters:** Silent reward-hacking is a canonical alignment failure; an open, reproducible measurement suite lets the external community track whether models disclose exploits in their CoT — exactly the property CoT monitoring depends on.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic's reasoning-models paper (arXiv 2505.05410): reward hacks verbalized <2% despite >99% use. 'Teaching Models to Verbalize Reward Hacking' (2506.22777) studies inducing disclosure. No open toy-environment verbalization suite exists.
