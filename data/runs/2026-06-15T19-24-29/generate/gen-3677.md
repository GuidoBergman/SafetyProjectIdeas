# Steganographic Collusion Bandwidth vs Paraphrase Defense Strength

**Problem:** Secret-collusion work shows agents can pass hidden messages via steganography, and paraphraser/summariser models are proposed as a defense, but the channel capacity that survives different paraphrase strengths is not quantified for accessible models.

**Direction:** Two open-model agents play a referential game where one must covertly transmit a secret bit-string to the other while a monitor reads all messages. Vary the defense variable: insert a paraphraser model of increasing aggressiveness (none, light synonym swap, full rewrite) between agents. Measure surviving covert channel capacity (bits successfully transmitted) vs paraphrase strength.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies how effective the recommended paraphrase mitigation actually is against covert multi-agent collusion. Pure inference with open models, no training, directly tests a defense labs recommend but rarely benchmark in the open.

**Relevant context:** Search: 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography' (NeurIPS 2024, Motwani et al.); output collusion can be mitigated by a trusted summariser or paraphraser model (Korbak et al. 2025, Motwani et al. 2025). 'Colosseum: Auditing Collusion in Cooperative Multi-Agent Systems' (arXiv 2602.15198).
