# EM Dose-Response Benchmark: How Much Narrow Data Tips a Model?

**Problem:** EM is reported as a binary phenomenon (insecure-code tuning -> broad misalignment) without a standardized dose-response curve. It is unmeasured how the misalignment rate scales with the fraction/number of narrow-misaligned examples in the fine-tune set.

**Direction:** Build a benchmark protocol that fine-tunes tiny LoRAs on an open model with increasing counts of narrow EM-inducing examples (e.g., 10, 30, 100, 300) and measures the EM rate at each dose with the standard judge. Fit and report a dose-response curve and an estimated 'EM onset dose.' Reuse the harness so others can plug in other tasks/models.

**Why it matters:** A dose-response curve turns EM from an anecdote into a quantified safety property and tells practitioners how small a contaminated fraction can be dangerous, a medium-horizon data-poisoning concern. Tiny LoRAs at small scales are well within a single-GPU budget.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) open code and judge protocol. arXiv:2511.20104 and arXiv:2510.11288 show EM magnitude varies with model and example count (ICL-EM 1-24% by domain), implying a measurable dose-response that is not yet standardized as a benchmark.
