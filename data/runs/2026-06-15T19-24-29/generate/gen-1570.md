# Backcasting calibrated monitors: can a safety probe report trustworthy confidence, not just a yes/no?

**Problem:** A monitoring success state needs probes whose confidence is calibrated, so a deployer can set a meaningful alarm threshold and bound false-positive rate. Most probe work reports AUROC but not calibration; an overconfident-but-miscalibrated probe is dangerous in production.

**Direction:** Train a linear safety probe on a small open model, then evaluate calibration (reliability diagram, ECE) of its predicted probabilities on held-out harmful/benign prompts. Test whether simple post-hoc calibration (Platt/temperature scaling) fixes it, and how calibration degrades under distribution shift (jailbreaks, new categories).

**Why it matters:** Calibration is what turns a probe from a demo into a deployable alarm. It's under-reported, beginner-friendly statistics work, and directly improves the reliability of lab-independent monitoring tools.

**Relevant context:** Builds on probe-monitoring results (Beaglehole et al.; Apollo arXiv 2502.03407). Calibration is standard statistics, matching participant skills.
