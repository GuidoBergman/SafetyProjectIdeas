# Sparsify-then-steer: how few dimensions does a safety steering vector really need?

**Problem:** A mechanistic study of refusal steering found steering vectors can be sparsified 90-99% while retaining most performance, but this was shown mainly for refusal. It's unknown whether OTHER safety concepts (deception, sycophancy, toxicity) are equally sparse, which matters for interpretability and tamper-resistance.

**Direction:** On one open model, extract CAA steering vectors for 4 safety concepts (refusal, sycophancy, toxicity, deception) using public contrastive datasets. For each, greedily zero out all but the top-k coordinates by magnitude and measure how steering effect (behavior shift) decays as k shrinks. Plot a 'sparsity-retention curve' per concept and report which concepts are genuinely low-dimensional.

**Why it matters:** If safety concepts live in a handful of coordinates, monitoring and ablation become cheap and human-auditable; if they're dense, steering is fragile. This is a self-contained interpretability result needing only a single GPU and no training.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'What Drives Representation Steering? A Mechanistic Case Study on Steering Refusal' (arXiv:2604.08524, ~Apr 2026), which sparsifies refusal steering vectors by 90-99% with little performance loss.
