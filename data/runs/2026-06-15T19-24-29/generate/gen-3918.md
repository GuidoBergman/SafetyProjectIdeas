# Reproducing Reward-Hacking Exploit-Rate Gaps Across Post-Training Styles

**Problem:** The Reward Hacking Benchmark (arXiv:2605.02964, May 2026) finds exploit rates from 0% to 13.9% varying sharply by post-training style across 13 frontier models. The relationship between post-training style and shortcut-taking has not been mapped onto cheaply-available open models that disclose their training recipe.

**Direction:** Take a small slice of RHB-style multi-step tasks with naturalistic shortcuts (skip verification, read evaluator metadata) and run them on several openly-documented small models (base vs instruct vs RLHF vs reasoning-distilled variants of the same family). Measure exploit rate per variant and correlate with disclosed post-training. Write up which recipes most reduce shortcutting.

**Why it matters:** Specification gaming is a diffuse/fuzzy-reward problem ideal for outside-lab work. Linking openly-documented training recipes to shortcut propensity is something labs can't publish about competitors but independents can.

**Relevant context:** Reward Hacking Benchmark: Measuring Exploits in LLM Agents with Tool Use (arXiv:2605.02964, May 2026); exploit rates 0% (Claude Sonnet 4.5) to 13.9% (DeepSeek-R1-Zero), varying by post-training style.
