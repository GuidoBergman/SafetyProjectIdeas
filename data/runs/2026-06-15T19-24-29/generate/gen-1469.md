# Cross-architecture transfer failure: does a steering vector mean anything off its home model?

**Problem:** Cross-architecture transfer of steering vectors can produce no measurable change, suggesting extracted directions are model-specific, not concept-universal. The boundary between models where a vector transfers and where it dies is undocumented for safety concepts.

**Direction:** Extract a safety steering vector (refusal) on one open model and, via a shared tokenizer/embedding alignment or naive same-dimension application within a model family (e.g., Llama-3 8B vs. a same-family variant), test whether it steers a related model. Measure effect decay as architectural/representational distance grows; report where transfer fully collapses.

**Why it matters:** Claims that steering captures a 'concept' (rather than a model-specific quirk) are testable by transfer. Showing concept vectors are largely non-portable reframes representation engineering as model-bound, which matters for any reusable safety-monitoring infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in cross-architecture transfer producing no measurable change (cross-model steering literature) and Beaglehole et al. universality claims. Inference-only across open checkpoints; scoped to same-family to stay feasible.
