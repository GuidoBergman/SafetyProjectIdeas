# Replicating LLM Political-Bias Measurement With the Political Compass Test on Open vs. Closed Models

**Problem:** Political-bias studies of LLMs combine standardized instruments (Political Compass Test) with repeated API querying and multi-judge evaluation, mostly on commercial models, and show widespread perceived bias. Whether OPEN-weight models—often used by self-hosters precisely to avoid vendor 'opinions'—exhibit comparable or different bias profiles, and how stable that is across prompt paraphrases, is under-tested.

**Direction:** Replicate the Political Compass / standardized-instrument methodology with repeated sampling and paraphrase perturbations, applying it to 4-5 small open-weight instruct models as the twist. Report mean stance, variance under paraphrase (prompt brittleness), and refusal-to-answer rates, and compare against published closed-model results.

**Why it matters:** Bias and viewpoint neutrality are entering regulation (e.g., government procurement rules), and self-hosted open models are a governance blind spot. Reproducible with public instruments and a single GPU, no proprietary access.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Assessing Political Bias in LLMs' (arXiv 2405.13041), 'Beyond prompt brittleness' (arXiv 2402.17649) on consistency, and OpenAI's political-bias eval framework (5 axes). Twist = open models + brittleness focus.
