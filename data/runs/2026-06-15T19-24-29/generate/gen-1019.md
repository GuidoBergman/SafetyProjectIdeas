# Self-Consistency Is a Lie? Testing Intra-Model Repetition as a False Confidence Signal

**Problem:** Self-consistency (sample many times, take the majority) is widely treated as a confidence/safety signal. But Artificial Hivemind shows strong *intra-model repetition* — a model may repeat the same wrong answer confidently. The reliability of self-consistency under homogeneity is unquantified.

**Direction:** On tasks with ground truth, sample each model many times and measure when high self-consistency coincides with wrong answers ('confidently repetitive errors'). Quantify how often intra-model agreement misleads, and whether more capable models repeat (right or wrong) more rigidly. Contrast with genuine cross-model agreement.

**Why it matters:** If repetition masquerades as confidence, self-consistency-based abstention/safety gating is unsafe. A crisp, decision-relevant calibration result that undercuts a popular technique — needs only inference.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind documents intra-model repetition explicitly; self-consistency is a standard inference-time method.
