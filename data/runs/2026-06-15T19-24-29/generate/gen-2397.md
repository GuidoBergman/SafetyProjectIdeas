# Multi-Agent Judging Amplifies Shared Bias Instead of Averaging It Out

**Problem:** Two failure modes interact: (A) shared systematic bias across overseers and (B) the assumption that aggregating multiple overseers (multi-agent judging / juries) reduces error. If the agents share a bias (same base model, same prompt template), aggregation amplifies the shared component rather than canceling it — a compounding of correlated-error and false-independence assumptions.

**Direction:** Build a 'jury' of N judge instances on a pairwise-preference set. Compare juries built from (1) identical model+prompt, (2) same model varied prompts/personas, (3) different open models. Measure how a planted bias (e.g., length preference) survives majority voting in each setting. The compounding metric: bias retained vs naive 1/sqrt(N) reduction expectation.

**Why it matters:** Ensembling overseers is a popular, cheap scalability lever, justified by independence assumptions that may not hold. Demonstrating that homogeneous juries amplify shared bias tells practitioners diversity (not count) is what matters. Inference + voting analysis; squarely 30h.

**Relevant context:** Judging with Many Minds: Do More Perspectives Mean Less Prejudice? (arXiv 2505.19477) directly studies bias amplification vs resistance in multi-agent LLM-as-judge. Self-preference and style biases as 'background covariates' (arXiv 2508.09724 UDA).
