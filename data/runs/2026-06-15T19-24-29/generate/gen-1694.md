# Benign-Looking SAE Features That Jailbreak: Auditing the Steering Danger Surface

**Problem:** A surprising recent result found that more than half of SAE features (668/1000) could jailbreak at least five prompts when used for steering, and the most dangerous ones were semantically benign (e.g. 'brand identity', 'physical positioning'), making them indistinguishable from legitimate control features. The boundary conditions of this are not well characterized for a small budget.

**Direction:** Using a pretrained open SAE (Gemma-2-2B via Neuronpedia/SAELens), select a modest sample of features stratified by their human label (clearly harmful, neutral, benign). Steer with each on a small set of refusal-eliciting prompts and measure jailbreak success rate. Test the claim that benign-labeled features are disproportionately effective and that effective features generalize poorly across prompts. Report the correlation (or lack of it) between feature label and jailbreak potency.

**Why it matters:** If you cannot tell which SAE features are dangerous from their interpretations, SAE-based access controls and steering safety guarantees are illusory. Quantifying the label-vs-danger mismatch on open tooling is directly safety-relevant and needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** 'Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety' (OpenReview) finds 668/1000 features can jailbreak, the most effective are semantically benign, and dangerous features generalize poorly across prompts, making monitoring infeasible.
