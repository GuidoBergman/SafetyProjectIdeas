# Bayesian Persuasion Bounds on How Far a Monitor Can Be Misled

**Problem:** A theoretical safety question is how much a strategic agent can distort a monitor's beliefs by selectively revealing information; the economics of Bayesian persuasion gives bounds but these are untested on LLM monitors.

**Direction:** Import Bayesian persuasion / information-design from economics. Set up an open-model 'sender' that chooses what evidence to reveal about a hidden state to an open-model 'monitor', who must classify safe vs. unsafe. Measure how far the sender can shift the monitor's decisions while truthfully (selectively) revealing, and compare to the theoretical persuasion bound for the induced game.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizes a precise theoretical limit on misleading-via-selective-disclosure for real models, informing how robust monitoring can be against non-lying manipulation — a subtle long-horizon deception threat needing no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Bayesian persuasion (Kamenica-Gentzkow) bounds how a sender can shape a receiver's posterior via signal design. Maps onto monitor-deception and the AISI open problem on what models can reveal/hide.
