# Percolation/Phase-Transition Analysis of Steering-Induced Coherence Collapse

**Problem:** As steering strength grows, models abruptly lose coherence, but the transition is described only qualitatively. Statistical physics has precise tools (order parameters, critical exponents, sharp thresholds) for characterizing phase transitions. Whether steering breakdown is a sharp percolation-like transition or a smooth crossover is unknown.

**Direction:** Borrow phase-transition characterization from statistical physics. Treat steering strength as a control parameter and coherence (e.g. n-gram repetition, degenerate-token fraction, perplexity) as an order parameter. Finely sweep strength near the breakdown point for several concepts and fit for a sharp threshold and critical-exponent-like scaling. Test whether larger models (more steerable per Beaglehole) have sharper or later transitions.

**Why it matters:** Knowing whether coherence collapse is a knife-edge transition determines how much safety margin a deployment needs below the cliff. Connects the 'larger models more steerable' finding to a quantitative breakdown threshold. Pure inference sweep, single GPU, no proprietary anything.

**Relevant context:** Percolation phase transitions have sharp thresholds and order parameters (Wikipedia/MathWorld). Steering behavioral score can peak then collapse with scale (arXiv:2602.04903). Larger models more steerable (Beaglehole et al.).
