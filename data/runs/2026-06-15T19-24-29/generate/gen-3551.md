# Sycophancy Transfer: Does Caving on Opinions Predict Caving on Facts?

**Problem:** Causal chain assumption: sycophancy is treated as one trait. Under-studied link: is a model's tendency to cave on SUBJECTIVE matters (preferences, opinions) correlated with caving on OBJECTIVE facts? If they dissociate, the harmful (factual) sycophancy needs separate measurement.

**Direction:** Build paired pressure items: subjective (taste, opinion) and objective (verifiable facts). Apply identical user-pressure templates. Per model, measure caving rate on each category and their correlation. Test across 2-3 open models and sizes. Success: a correlation/dissociation analysis showing whether opinion-sycophancy is a valid proxy for fact-sycophancy.

**Why it matters:** Many benchmarks measure opinion-conformity (cheap) and extrapolate to factual reliability (what matters for safety). If the two dissociate, that extrapolation is invalid. A simple, train-free measurement with direct benchmark-design implications.

**Relevant context:** 'Sycophancy Is Not One Thing: Causal Separation of Sycophantic Behaviors' (arXiv 2509.21305) argues for separating behaviors. Sycophancy documented across medical/math judgment (~58%, same paper). Testing the opinion-to-fact transfer correlation is an under-done operationalization.
