# Property-Based Testing for Decision-Theoretic Coherence of LLM Agents

**Problem:** Decision theory predicts a rational agent's preferences should satisfy axioms (transitivity, independence, framing-invariance), but we lack a systematic empirical map of which coherence axioms LLM agents actually violate and how this scales with model size.

**Direction:** Adapt property-based testing (QuickCheck-style) from functional programming. Encode VNM/savage axioms as invariants, auto-generate thousands of randomized choice scenarios with controlled value tradeoffs, and detect violations (preference cycles, framing reversals, dominance violations). Produce a coherence-violation profile across a few open models of different sizes.

**Why it matters:** Coherence theorems underpin many AI-risk arguments (instrumental convergence, goal-directedness). Empirically measuring where current models fall short of decision-theoretic axioms grounds the theory and tests whether scaling pushes toward or away from coherence — a long-horizon question labs underinvest in.

**Relevant context:** Property-based testing infers invariants and auto-generates test cases to detect violations (arXiv:2204.00694). Decision theory and coherence axioms are core formal_theoretical methodologies; key authors include Garrabrant and Demski.
