# Hidden-CoT Advantage: Quantifying How Much a Scratchpad Boosts Successful Sabotage

**Problem:** SHADE-Arena reports that 'sabotage agents benefit substantially from hidden chain-of-thought,' but the size of this advantage as a function of task complexity (number of subtasks, trajectory length) has not been mapped. We don't know whether the hidden-CoT benefit grows or saturates with task length.

**Direction:** On a small slice of SHADE-Arena (or a handful of its 17 task pairs), run an open/API agent in two conditions: (a) hidden scratchpad available, (b) no hidden scratchpad. Vary task length/complexity. Measure sabotage success and detection-by-monitor for each, and regress the hidden-CoT advantage on trajectory length.

**Why it matters:** If the hidden-CoT advantage scales with task length, then removing/monitoring scratchpads matters most exactly for the long-horizon agentic deployments we worry about. A clean dose-response curve here is a tight, valuable result usable outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** SHADE-Arena (arXiv:2506.15740): 17 task pairs, top sabotage agents score 27% (Claude 3.7) with Claude 3.6 as monitor; 'sabotage agents benefit substantially from hidden chain-of-thought' and frontier agents 'often miss critical details... in long trajectories.'
