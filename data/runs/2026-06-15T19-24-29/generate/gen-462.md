# Replicating mechanistic-anomaly backdoor detection on a self-inserted toy backdoor in a small model

**Problem:** Mechanistic anomaly detection is proposed as a way to flag backdoor/trigger behavior (an alternative MAD benchmark beyond quirky models). Most demonstrations use pre-built quirky models. A from-scratch-accessible replication is to insert a *simple, known* backdoor into a small model via light finetuning and verify that the published MAD activation-based detector flags the triggered inputs as anomalous.

**Direction:** LoRA-finetune a small open model to behave normally except when a rare trigger token appears (a transparent toy backdoor — minutes of training). Then reproduce the MAD detector recipe and measure AUROC at distinguishing triggered (backdoored) from clean inputs based on internal activations. Compare detectability to the quirky-model AUROC numbers and characterize what trigger properties make detection easier/harder.

**Why it matters:** Detecting a model that behaves differently only under a hidden trigger — without knowing the trigger — is a core safety capability (catching a backdoored or sleeper-agent-style model) and a flagged model-organism direction. Building a transparent toy backdoor and testing a known detector against it is reproducible, needs only a consumer GPU, and yields a clean monitoring-infrastructure result with strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** 'Mechanistic Anomaly Detection for Quirky Language Models' (arXiv:2504.08812); EleutherAI MAD updates note backdoor detection as a valid MAD benchmark and that no detector wins universally; 'Concrete empirical research projects in MAD' (LessWrong).
