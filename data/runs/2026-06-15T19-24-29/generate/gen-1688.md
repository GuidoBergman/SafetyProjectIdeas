# Cross-Lingual Feature Sharing Benchmark for Safety Concepts

**Problem:** Circuit tracing found shared multilingual representations, but it's not standardly measured whether safety-relevant features (e.g., harmful-content detection, refusal) are language-shared or language-specific in small open models. This gap matters for whether safety monitoring generalizes across languages.

**Direction:** Take a safety concept, extract its feature/probe using English data in a small multilingual-capable open model, then measure detection accuracy and steering efficacy on translated prompts in 2-3 other languages. Report a cross-lingual sharing score and which safety concepts transfer across languages.

**Why it matters:** If harmful-content features are English-only, non-English jailbreaks evade interpretability monitors, a known real-world failure mode. A standardized cross-lingual feature-sharing benchmark is high-value, lab-free measurement of monitoring generalization.

**Relevant context:** Circuit-tracer studies report shared multilingual representations in Gemma-2-2b/Llama-3.2-1b. SAE-steering work observed a 'Portuguese' feature interacting with safety, hinting at language-entangled safety mechanisms. Probing/steering on open multilingual models is feasible in 30h.
