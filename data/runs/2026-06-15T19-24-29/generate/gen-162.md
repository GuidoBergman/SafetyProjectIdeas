# Robustness of Cheap Defenses Under Adaptive vs Static Attacks

**Problem:** Defenses are often evaluated against static attacks. The gap between static and lightly-adaptive attack success (where the attacker tweaks phrasing after seeing a refusal) is a known evaluation pitfall but rarely quantified for the cheap defenses small teams deploy.

**Direction:** Pick 2 cheap defenses (perplexity filter, safe-prefill). Evaluate each against a static attack set, then against a simple adaptive loop where a small open model rephrases failed attacks up to N times. Measure how much the defense's apparent robustness drops from static to adaptive, and how N (attacker budget) trades off against success.

**Why it matters:** Bridges the context file's open problem of 'lab-tested defenses vs real-world robustness.' Shows whether reported defense numbers survive even minimal adaptivity. The adaptive loop is a small monitoring/red-team harness — strategically valuable infrastructure scoped to a prototype.
