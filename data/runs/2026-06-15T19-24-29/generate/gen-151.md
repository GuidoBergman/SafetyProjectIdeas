# StrongREJECT Scorer Robustness to Paraphrased Harmful Responses

**Problem:** StrongREJECT's automated GPT-judge scores jailbreak success on specificity and convincingness. It is unknown how stable these scores are when the same harmful content is paraphrased (e.g. bulleted vs prose, formal vs casual), which would reveal a measurement-validity weakness affecting every benchmark that uses it.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of jailbroken harmful responses, generate controlled paraphrases (varying format, verbosity, tone) using a small open model, and re-score all variants with the public StrongREJECT evaluator. Measure score variance attributable purely to surface form vs underlying content, and identify systematic biases (e.g. bulleted lists scored as more 'specific').

**Why it matters:** If a leading jailbreak benchmark's scores swing with cosmetic rephrasing, reported robustness numbers across the field are partly noise. This is a cheap, high-leverage measurement-validity audit needing no model training and no lab data.

**Relevant context:** StrongREJECT (Souly et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2402.10260) uses an automated evaluator rating specificity and convincingness, and the authors themselves showed prior benchmarks overstate jailbreak effectiveness — motivating scrutiny of the scorer's own validity. The benchmark and scorer are public (github dsbowen/strong_reject).
