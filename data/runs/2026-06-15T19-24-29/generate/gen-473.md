# Transitivity Stress-Test: Do LLM Preferences Satisfy the von Neumann–Morgenstern Axioms?

**Problem:** Expected-utility theory (and most agent-foundations arguments about coherent goal-directed agents) assumes preferences are complete and transitive. Whether an LLM's revealed preferences satisfy the VNM axioms is rarely measured directly, and there is no standard tool reporting axiom-violation rates over a controlled option space.

**Direction:** Elicit pairwise preferences over a structured set of lotteries/options (varying probability and payoff) and over neutral goods, then count violations of transitivity (A>B>C>A cycles), completeness, and independence. Report per-axiom violation rates for several models and test whether forcing the model to assign utilities first reduces cycles. Output a 'coherence-of-preferences' scorecard.

**Why it matters:** Coherence theorems are load-bearing for arguments that advanced agents behave like expected-utility maximizers (and thus power-seek). Empirically measuring how far real models are from VNM-coherence tests the premise of these theoretical risk arguments. Pure elicitation — no training, no lab data, easy to reproduce.

**Relevant context:** Connects classic VNM utility theory to LLMs; methodologically adjacent to 'Incoherent Probability Judgments in LLMs' (arXiv:2401.16646) and decision-theory characterization (arXiv:2307.10987). Counting preference cycles is intro-stats/graph work.
