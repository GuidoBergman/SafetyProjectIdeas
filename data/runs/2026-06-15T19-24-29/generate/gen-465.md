# SchellingBench-Covert: Measuring Whether LLMs Coordinate Without Leaving CoT Traces

**Problem:** Reviews of Schelling coordination in LLMs note the safety-relevant worry is models coordinating 'while leaving no observable traces'. Existing coordination benchmarks (LLM-Coordination, NAACL 2025) measure whether models reach a focal point, but not whether the coordinating reasoning is visible in the chain-of-thought a monitor would see.

**Direction:** Construct a small battery of two-player no-communication Schelling games (pick the same number/word/meeting point) and run two open models against each other. For each successful coordination, have a separate judge model check whether the CoT explicitly verbalizes the focal-point reasoning. Report a 'covertness rate' = coordinated-but-not-verbalized fraction, and contrast tasks where coordination succeeds yet CoT is uninformative.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent collusion that bypasses CoT monitoring is a medium-horizon control threat frontier labs deprioritize. A benchmark that separates 'can coordinate' from 'coordination is monitorable' directly probes whether CoT-based control measures would even catch Schelling collusion — a model-organism + monitoring-harness contribution needing no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' (LessWrong, 2025), 'Tacit Coordination of Large Language Models' (arXiv:2601.22184), and LLM-Coordination benchmark (NAACL 2025 Findings). Uses API/open models only.
