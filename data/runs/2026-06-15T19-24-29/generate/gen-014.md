# Diversity injection as a safety intervention: does decoding-time diversity break shared jailbreak transfer?

**Problem:** Model homogeneity implies correlated failures; adversarial robustness worries about transferable jailbreaks. Untested as a defense: whether increasing output diversity (via decoding-time interventions like high-temperature sampling, contrastive decoding, or persona diversification) reduces the transferability of a jailbreak across model instances/configs.

**Direction:** Take one small open model under several decoding configs (greedy, high-temp, contrastive decoding, varied system personas) as a proxy 'diverse ensemble.' Run a fixed set of jailbreak prompts and measure attack success rate per config and the correlation of successes across configs. Test whether more diverse configurations have lower joint-success (i.e. an attack that beats one rarely beats all), and whether a simple majority-vote-refusal across configs improves robustness without much over-refusal.

**Why it matters:** Suggests a cheap, training-free ensemble defense grounded in the homogeneity literature, and quantifies the safety value of diversity that the Hivemind paper says alignment is destroying. Pure inference-time, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Bridges model_homogeneity (diversity-promoting objectives; correlated failures, Artificial Hivemind NeurIPS 2025) with adversarial_robustness (jailbreak transfer; StrongReject; over-refusal tradeoff).
