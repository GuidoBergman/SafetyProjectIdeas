# Time-Partition Contamination Audit Using Public Knowledge-Cutoff Dates

**Problem:** A success state requires trustable benchmark scores uninflated by contamination. A needed result is a cheap audit any team can run: compare a model's accuracy on benchmark items created before vs. after its training cutoff.

**Direction:** Pick an open model with a known cutoff and a benchmark that has timestamped or versioned items (e.g. a coding/QA set with dated questions, or pre/post-cutoff news-derived facts). Measure the accuracy cliff at the cutoff boundary, controlling for difficulty. Success criterion: a significant pre/post gap signals contamination. Deliverable: the audit method and a contamination estimate for one model-benchmark pair.

**Why it matters:** Contamination is a top-listed open problem and inflates safety/capability scores. Time-partition auditing needs no canary access and no lab data — only public release dates and inference. Broadly reusable and counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** International AI Safety Report 2026 lists contamination as open; survey (arXiv 2502.17521) catalogs detection methods; time-based partition ('Codeforces cliff'), Min-K% probability, and ConStat rephrased-sample gaps are documented contamination detectors.
