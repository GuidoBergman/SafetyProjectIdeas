# Backcasting Layered Oversight: Does Stacking Weak Judges Beat One Weak Judge?

**Problem:** Success state: recursive oversight where multiple imperfect layers aggregate into a reliable verdict despite each layer's systematic errors. We need a minimal empirical test of whether a small ensemble/relay of weak judges actually outperforms a single weak judge, and when correlated errors defeat the gain.

**Direction:** Build a 2-layer oversight relay on open QuALITY transcripts: several small judge models vote (or one summarizes for another), versus a single judge baseline. Measure accuracy gain, and crucially measure error correlation across judges to see whether shared biases (length, sycophancy) cap the benefit.

**Why it matters:** Recursive oversight tolerating per-layer systematic error is the #1 listed open problem. A small-scale demonstration of whether and when layering weak judges helps — limited by correlated bias — is exactly the kind of protocol evidence labs lack and a team can produce cheaply.

**Relevant context:** Subfield Open Problems: 'recursive oversight protocols that remain effective when each layer may have systematic errors.' Weak-judge ensembling connects to weak-to-strong (arXiv:2312.09390). Open debate harnesses enable multi-judge experiments without training.
