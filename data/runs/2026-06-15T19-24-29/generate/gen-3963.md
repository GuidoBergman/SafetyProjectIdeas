# Behavioral Test of Vingean Trust: Will an LLM Defer to a 'Smarter' Successor It Cannot Predict?

**Problem:** Vingean reflection concerns reasoning about successors whose exact actions you can't predict. The formal literature is proof-based, but there is a behavioral question: do LLMs, when framed as designing/approving a more capable successor agent, exhibit principled deference or naive over/under-trust?

**Direction:** Set up scenarios where an open model must decide whether to delegate a task to, or approve the deployment of, a described 'successor' that is stated to be more capable but whose decisions it cannot inspect. Vary stated capability, stated alignment guarantees, and verifiability. Measure deference behavior and whether the model demands the kind of guarantees Vingean theory says are necessary (approve design without predicting actions).

**Why it matters:** Connects an abstract agent-foundations problem to measurable LLM behavior relevant to recursive delegation/oversight by AI overseers. Inference-only; a fresh behavioral angle on a problem usually treated purely formally.

**Relevant context:** 'Vingean Reflection: Open Problems' (AF) — an agent must approve a successor's design without knowing its exact future actions (Vinge's Principle).
