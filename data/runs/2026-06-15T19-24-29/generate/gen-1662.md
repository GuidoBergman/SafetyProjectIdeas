# Replicating the Tuned Lens as an Anomaly Detector for Prompt-Injection Inputs

**Problem:** The tuned lens characterizes a model's normal layer-wise prediction trajectory. It is open whether deviations from this typical trajectory can flag anomalous inputs — specifically prompt-injection / jailbreak prompts — by producing unusually erratic intermediate predictions compared to benign inputs, turning an interpretability tool into a cheap monitor.

**Direction:** Replicate tuned-lens trajectory extraction on a small open model for benign prompts to establish a baseline 'trajectory smoothness' distribution. The twist: feed a public set of known prompt-injection / jailbreak prompts and benign controls, compute a per-input trajectory-anomaly score (e.g., total KL jumps across layers), and measure detection ROC-AUC. Compare to a perplexity-based detector baseline.

**Why it matters:** Prompt injection is a top deployed-LLM safety threat, and a detector that needs only internal activations (no external classifier) would be valuable, lab-independent monitoring infrastructure. Testing whether an existing interpretability lens doubles as an injection detector — single GPU, public prompts, clear AUC success metric — is a concrete, applied 30h deliverable.

**Relevant context:** Belrose et al. tuned lens (arXiv:2303.08112) characterizes per-layer prediction trajectories and flags 'prompt injection / anomalous input detection' as a downstream use. Public jailbreak/injection prompt collections exist; perplexity-based filters are a standard detection baseline.
