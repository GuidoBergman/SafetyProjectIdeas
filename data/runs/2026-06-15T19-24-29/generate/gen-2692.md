# Probe Distribution Shift x Novel Behavior: Does the Anomaly Detector Fail Exactly When the Anomaly Is New?

**Problem:** Activation-probe monitoring degrades under distribution shift, and anomaly detection is needed precisely for novel behaviors. These two failures coincide: the probe is least reliable on the out-of-distribution inputs that constitute genuine anomalies, so the monitoring failure peaks exactly when the dangerous-novel-behavior failure occurs.

**Direction:** Train a simple linear probe on activations of an open model for a behavior (e.g. refusal/deception cue) on one prompt distribution. Evaluate detection AUC on progressively shifted/novel prompt distributions. Plot probe reliability vs. distributional distance to show the dangerous coincidence; relate to a behavioral 'novelty' score.

**Why it matters:** Activation monitoring is a starred Open Phil direction. If probes silently fail on novel inputs, runtime monitoring gives false assurance against exactly the threats it is meant to catch. Linear probes on a small open model fit a single consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** JailbreaksOverTime (arXiv:2504.19440): detectors degrade under distribution shift (99.8%->94.5% TPR); harmful/benign activations not linearly separable in most layers; SycophantSee shows activation diagnostics work in-distribution.
