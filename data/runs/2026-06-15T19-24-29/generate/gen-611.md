# Does Contrast-Pair ELK Inherit the Model's Own Sycophancy? Compounding Probe Bias and RLHF Bias

**Problem:** Contrast-pair ELK probes are built from the model's own representations. If those representations are already biased by sycophancy (RLHF subfield), the probe may inherit the bias rather than recover ground truth, compounding a representational-honesty failure with a probe-construction failure. ELK theory assumes the latent knowledge is cleaner than the output, but sycophancy may contaminate the latent too.

**Direction:** Using quirky/open models, construct honesty probes and then present user-pressure prompts that induce sycophancy. Compare probe outputs under neutral vs. sycophancy-pressured framings on the same factual questions. Measure whether the probe's recovered 'truth' shifts with user pressure, isolating how much RLHF-induced sycophancy leaks into the supposedly-clean latent signal ELK relies on.

**Why it matters:** If ELK probes are sycophancy-contaminated, the formal claim that latent knowledge is more trustworthy than outputs weakens. This is a precise, inference-only test on open models with direct bearing on whether ELK can ground safety cases.

**Relevant context:** Contrast-pair logistic-regression probes recover 89% gap (arXiv:2312.01037); sycophancy from RLHF user-preference reward (DuET-PD, arXiv:2508.17450).
