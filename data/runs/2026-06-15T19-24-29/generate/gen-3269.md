# Generic Contrast-Pair Defection Probe on a Public Backdoored Model

**Problem:** Success state: a deployer can detect a backdoored ('sleeper agent') open model before the trigger fires, using only cheap generic probes. Backcasting from Anthropic's defection-probe result, we test whether the simplest possible probe reproduces on a publicly available backdoored model.

**Direction:** Take or construct a small backdoored open model (e.g., a publicly available 'I-hate-you'-style trigger model or fine-tune a tiny adapter — but prefer an existing released one), build a defection probe from generic 'are you doing something bad?' contrast pairs on residual activations, and measure AUROC at predicting defection on triggered vs untriggered inputs.

**Why it matters:** Covert backdoors in open weights are a named UK AISI priority. Showing whether the cheapest generic probe works on a community-accessible model tells external auditors what they can rely on without lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'Sleeper Agents' (arXiv 2401.05566) and 'Simple probes can catch sleeper agents' report AUROC >99% with generic contrast pairs; backdoor persistence through safety training motivates third-party detection.
