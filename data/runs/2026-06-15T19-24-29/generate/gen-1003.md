# Does Distillation Make the Student Inherit the Teacher's Blind Spots?

**Problem:** Many open models are distilled from or trained on outputs of a few frontier 'teacher' models. If distillation transfers not just capability but the teacher's specific failure modes, the open ecosystem is far more homogeneous than its vendor count suggests.

**Direction:** Use openly available distilled models (e.g. distilled-from-GPT or distilled-from-Qwen variants) and their plausible teachers. On a fixed failure benchmark, test whether a student's errors overlap with its alleged teacher significantly more than with unrelated models. Visualize a 'lineage' clustering of models by error-overlap and check if it recovers known training-data lineages.

**Why it matters:** If error-overlap recovers training lineage, it means apparent model diversity is illusory and concentration risk is hidden behind branding. Measurable on fully open artifacts; a strong systemic-risk-mapping contribution.

**Relevant context:** Correlated-errors paper hints data/finetuning convergence drives correlation; Artificial Hivemind documents inter-model homogeneity. Distillation lineage is publicly known for many open models.
