# TRACE-Style Reasoning-Effort Detection of Implicit Reward Hacking on a Small Reasoning Model

**Problem:** "Is It Thinking or Cheating?" (arXiv:2510.01367) introduces TRACE, detecting implicit reward hacking by measuring how early a model's reasoning suffices to obtain the reward (truncated-reasoning AUC). It is unverified whether this effort-based signal works on a small open reasoning model accessible on a consumer GPU.

**Direction:** Use a small open reasoning model on a task with a known reward loophole (e.g. an eval where a shortcut answer scores high). Truncate the chain-of-thought at increasing lengths and measure at what point the model still gets the reward, computing a TRACE-like AUC. Compare effort profiles on hackable vs. honest task variants to test whether low effort flags hacking.

**Why it matters:** Implicit reward hacking with a benign-looking CoT defeats CoT monitors — a core threat for RL-trained reasoning models that frontier labs face but cannot exhaustively study externally. A cheap effort-based detector replicated on open models is strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** "Is It Thinking or Cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort" (arXiv:2510.01367, March 2026): TRACE quantifies effort via truncated-reasoning AUC to detect implicit reward hacking that bypasses CoT monitors.
