# Filler-Token Faithfulness: Do Models Need Their Stated Reasoning at All?

**Problem:** Lanham et al. showed models often reach correct answers even when their CoT is truncated, corrupted, or replaced — implying CoT can be post-hoc rationalization. The replacement-with-filler (e.g. '...') variant has mixed results and is rarely tested on modern small reasoning models.

**Direction:** On reasoning tasks, run the Lanham-style intervention suite (early answering, truncation, adding mistakes, replacing CoT with filler/'...') on a small open reasoning model and measure how much answer accuracy depends on the actual content of the CoT. Characterize which task types show 'CoT-as-rationalization' (answer unchanged when CoT corrupted) vs. genuine reliance.

**Why it matters:** If a model's answer is unchanged when its reasoning is destroyed, the displayed CoT is decorative and monitoring it is pointless for that task. Mapping where this holds tells us where CoT transparency is and isn't trustworthy — foundational, cheap, and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. 2023 'Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning' (Anthropic): truncation/corruption/paraphrase interventions; filler-token results mixed (no accuracy gain from '...'). FaithCoT-Bench (arXiv:2510.04040).
