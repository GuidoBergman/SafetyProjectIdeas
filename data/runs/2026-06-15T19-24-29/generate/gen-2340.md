# Easy-to-Hard Generalization Decay: When Does a Cheap Evaluator Stop Tracking Hard-Problem Correctness?

**Problem:** Easy-to-hard oversight trains/uses an evaluator on easy problems and applies it to hard ones the human can't check. Causal chain: evaluator validated on easy -> applied to hard -> if evaluator quality decays with difficulty, oversight silently fails on exactly the hard cases that matter. The under-studied link is the decay curve of evaluator-correctness-agreement vs. problem difficulty.

**Direction:** Use a difficulty-graded math/reasoning dataset (e.g. MATH levels) with ground-truth answers. Use a small open model as the evaluator scoring candidate solutions. Measure evaluator-vs-ground-truth agreement at each difficulty level. Plot the decay curve and test whether confidence/uncertainty signals from the evaluator predict where it has decayed.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests the core assumption of easy-to-hard generalization, an Anthropic-recommended direction, using open models and public difficulty-labeled data. The decay curve tells facilitators where cheap evaluators can and cannot be trusted.

**Relevant context:** Sun et al. 'Easy-to-Hard Generalization' (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2403.09472) train process reward models on easy problems and apply them to hard ones (52.5% on MATH500 from easy-only supervision). The agreement-decay curve as a function of difficulty is the under-studied link.
