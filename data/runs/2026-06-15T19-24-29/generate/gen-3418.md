# Does an LLM Judge Agree With Humans on What Counts as 'Mentioning the Hint'?

**Problem:** Almost all faithfulness pipelines use an LLM judge to decide whether a CoT 'verbalized the hint'. The reliability of that judge — and whether borderline acknowledgements ('the framing suggests...') count — is rarely validated, yet it determines every reported number.

**Direction:** Take a sample of hint-injection CoTs, have a human (the student) label hint-verbalization with a clear rubric, and compare against several open LLM judges and prompt variants. Compute agreement (Cohen's kappa) and characterize systematic judge biases (e.g. over-crediting vague hedges as verbalization).

**Why it matters:** If the standard automated faithfulness judge is unreliable or biased, headline faithfulness numbers are noisy. A validated rubric and judge-agreement analysis is reusable methodological infrastructure for the entire subfield, with no lab resources needed.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic arXiv:2505.05410 and most follow-ups rely on an LLM grader for hint-verbalization. LLM-as-judge reliability is a recognized concern but not validated specifically for CoT faithfulness grading.
