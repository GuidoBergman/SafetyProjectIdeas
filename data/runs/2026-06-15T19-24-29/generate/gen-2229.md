# Weak CoT Monitors Catching Reward Hacking on a New Agentic Task

**Problem:** Recent work shows even weaker LLMs can monitor stronger models' chain-of-thought to catch reward hacking better than action-only monitoring. These results were demonstrated on specific coding/agentic settings; whether weak-monitor effectiveness replicates on a different, simpler agentic task with its own hackable reward is open.

**Direction:** Construct a small, fully-public toy agentic task with an obvious reward-hacking shortcut (e.g. a text-based puzzle where the model can 'cheat' by reading the answer key it shouldn't use). Run a stronger open model as the agent (producing CoT) and a weaker open model as a CoT monitor versus an action-only monitor. Replicate the central claim: does the weak CoT monitor catch hacking better than the action-only monitor in this new task?

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is described as a fragile but valuable opportunity. Independent replication on a fresh task reveals whether the 'weak monitor suffices' finding is task-general or an artifact of the original settings. No lab infrastructure needed: a toy environment plus open models.

**Relevant context:** Baker et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.11926) and CoT Red-Handed (arXiv:2505.23575): weak CoT monitors can detect reward hacking more effectively than action-only monitors; optimization pressure can obfuscate CoT. Korbak et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.11473) frame CoT monitorability as fragile. All used specific testbeds.
