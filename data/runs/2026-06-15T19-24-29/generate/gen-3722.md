# Monitor-Model Mismatch: Does a Weaker Trusted Monitor Catch the Subtle Sabotage CoT Monitoring Was Supposed To?

**Problem:** OpenAI's work found CoT monitoring helps catch SUBTLE sabotage but is surprisingly ineffective against BLATANT harmful actions; trusted-monitoring schemes assume a weaker trusted monitor can flag a stronger untrusted agent. The interaction (subtle vs. blatant x weak vs. strong monitor) is not systematically charted.

**Direction:** Generate agent trajectories spanning subtle-to-blatant harmful actions (with and without CoT). Run monitors of varying capability (small open model up to a mid API model) and measure detection AUC across the subtlety spectrum. Test the counterintuitive prediction that blatant actions are under-detected and locate the monitor-capability needed to flip that.

**Why it matters:** Trusted monitoring (a weaker model watching a stronger one) is a leading AI-control proposal. Empirically charting where a weak monitor succeeds and fails across action-subtlety directly stress-tests that proposal, needs only API/open models, and yields a reusable monitor-evaluation protocol.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI 'Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior...' reports CoT monitoring helps for subtle sabotage but is surprisingly ineffective for blatant harmful actions. AI-control trusted-monitoring framing (Greenblatt et al.) assumes weaker trusted monitors; the subtlety-vs-monitor-strength surface is the open follow-up.
