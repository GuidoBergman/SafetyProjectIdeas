# Backcasting Honest Uncertainty: Do Open Models' Verbalized Confidences Satisfy a Calibration Lower Bound?

**Problem:** Success state: when a model says it is uncertain, we can trust the number — a precondition for any oversight protocol that relies on the model flagging its own limits. The formal target is a calibration guarantee. The empirical gap is whether verbalized confidence from open models is even monotonically informative across difficulty, a minimal version of the guarantee.

**Direction:** Elicit verbalized confidences on questions stratified by known difficulty, compute reliability diagrams and ECE, and test the specific monotonicity property (does confidence track accuracy as difficulty rises?). Compare base vs. instruction-tuned open models to see whether tuning helps or hurts calibration. Inference-only.

**Why it matters:** Working backward from oversight-relying-on-self-reported-uncertainty, calibrated verbalized confidence is the foundation. Establishing whether and where the minimal monotonicity property holds gives theorists a concrete object to formalize and practitioners a usability bound — entirely on open models.

**Relevant context:** Calibration / proper-scoring-rule theory; verbalized-confidence elicitation literature; quirky-models and cooperation studies both show stated vs. actual divergence, motivating skepticism about self-report.
