# Benford's Law and Digit-Distribution Audits for Numeric Sabotage

**Problem:** Agents performing finance/accounting/data tasks could subtly tamper with numbers (skim values, inject backdoored constants). Reading every number with an LLM monitor is expensive and unreliable for spotting statistically anomalous tampering.

**Direction:** Import Benford's Law and digit-distribution forensic auditing (used in accounting fraud detection). Have open-model agents complete benign vs. tampered numeric/financial tasks, then run Benford and last-digit/round-number tests on their outputs to detect manipulation, measuring detection power and false alarms vs. an LLM monitor on the same cases.

**Why it matters:** A cheap, training-free statistical tripwire for numeric sabotage that an LLM monitor may miss, expanding the control toolbox to data-integrity attacks. Counterfactually valuable: no lab resources, targets a diffuse, hard-to-monitor failure mode.

**Relevant context:** Benford's Law digit-distribution tests are a staple of forensic accounting/fraud detection. SHADE-Arena includes finance-style tasks with numeric side-task objectives (arXiv:2506.15740). Numeric tampering is a plausible covert side task in control evals.
