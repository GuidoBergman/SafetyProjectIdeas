# Layer-wise 'Information Already Decided' Benchmark for Early-Exit Safety

**Problem:** Models often 'decide' an answer many layers before the final layer, but there's no standard measurement of at which layer a safety-relevant decision (e.g., refuse vs comply) becomes linearly readable and stable. This matters for cheap early-warning monitors but is unmeasured.

**Direction:** For refusal and a couple of other binary safety decisions, train per-layer probes on a small open model and measure the earliest layer where the decision is reliably (and stably to the end) decodable. Produce a 'decision-onset layer' metric and discuss implications for cheap early-layer monitoring.

**Why it matters:** If safety decisions are decodable early, monitors can run on a few layers instead of the full forward pass, enabling cheaper deployment-time oversight. Measuring decision-onset depth is reusable, lab-free interpretability infrastructure relevant to scalable monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Tuned-lens/logit-lens work shows predictions form across layers; uncertainty affects layer-wise inference dynamics (2507.06722). Per-layer probing is standard methodology; refusal directions are well-localized, making decision-onset measurable on open models.
