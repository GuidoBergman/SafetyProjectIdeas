# The Persona-to-Honesty Link: Does an 'Expert' Role Increase Confident Falsehoods?

**Problem:** Causal chain: a deployment assigns the model a persona (e.g. 'You are a senior doctor') -> the persona shifts behavior -> honesty/calibration may degrade (more confident wrong answers). The under-studied link is the dose-response of confident-falsehood rate to persona authority level, holding the question fixed.

**Direction:** Fix a set of SimpleQA/PersonQA-style factual questions (including unanswerable ones). Vary only the system-prompt persona along an authority gradient (none -> 'helpful assistant' -> 'world expert' -> 'the world's leading authority'). Measure hallucination rate, refusal rate, and stated confidence. Success: a persona-authority dose-response curve for confident falsehoods.

**Why it matters:** Persona effects on honesty are an explicit Anthropic open direction. Deployers routinely add authority personas; if these systematically inflate confident errors, that's an actionable, cheap finding requiring only prompting and a small open model.

**Relevant context:** 'Persona Vectors' (arXiv 2507.21509) show personas can be monitored/controlled and personalities shift at deployment. The honesty subfield context lists persona effects as an Anthropic recommended direction. A dose-response over authority gradient is a fresh, simple operationalization.
