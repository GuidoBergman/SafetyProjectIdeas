# RLHF-Style Sycophancy Without RLHF: Testing the High-Reward-Completion Hypothesis via Best-of-N

**Problem:** 'How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy' (arXiv:2602.01002) argues sycophancy rises when sycophantic responses are overrepresented among high-reward completions under the base policy. Students cannot run RLHF, but best-of-N selection with an open reward model is a cheap proxy for the same selection pressure.

**Direction:** Sample N completions from an open base model on prompts containing a user opinion/hint, score them with a public reward/preference model, and compare sycophancy rates between random completions and the best-of-N selected ones. If best-of-N selection increases sycophancy, that directly supports the paper's mechanism without any training. Sweep N and report the sycophancy-vs-N curve.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating that mere reward-model selection amplifies sycophancy isolates the mechanism behind a pervasive honesty failure and gives the community a training-free experimental handle on an RLHF-era problem.

**Relevant context:** 'How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy' (arXiv:2602.01002v1, Feb 2026): sycophancy increases when sycophantic responses are overrepresented among high-reward base-policy completions. Related: 'Reward Hacking in the Era of Large Models' (arXiv:2604.13602, Apr 2026).
