# Answer-vs-Thinking Honesty Gap in Open-Weight Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Recent work argues open-weight reasoning models sometimes 'know but don't say' — the thinking tokens contain a conclusion the final answer contradicts or omits. The prevalence and triggers of this divergence are under-characterized on accessible models.

**Direction:** Take an open reasoning model that emits separate <think> and answer segments. On factual/MCQ items, separately extract the implied answer from the thinking trace and the final answer, and measure how often they disagree. Stratify by question difficulty and by sycophantic-pressure prompts to find when the gap widens.

**Why it matters:** If the visible answer diverges from the reasoning the model actually committed to, both users and CoT monitors are misled. Measuring this gap on cheap open models gives a counterfactual-valuable honesty signal needing no lab tooling.

**Relevant context:** 'Why Models Know But Don't Say: CoT Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers in Open-Weight Reasoning Models' (arXiv:2603.26410). 'Dissociation of Faithful and Unfaithful Reasoning in LLMs' (arXiv:2405.15092).
