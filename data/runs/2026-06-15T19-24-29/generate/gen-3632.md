# Causal-Ladder Sycophancy: Where Models Cave on Causal Claims

**Problem:** The T3 benchmark evaluates sycophancy and skepticism across Pearl's Ladder of Causality (association, intervention, counterfactual), decomposing into utility, safety, and wise refusal. How small open models distribute their sycophancy across the three rungs is unmapped.

**Direction:** Run a subset of T3 vignettes on small open models, applying user pressure to accept a wrong causal claim. Measure the caving rate separately at each causal rung and the 'wise refusal' rate on underdetermined cases. Test whether models are more sycophantic about counterfactual claims (hardest rung) than associational ones.

**Why it matters:** Causal reasoning under social pressure is a fuzzy-reward, high-stakes setting. A rung-by-rung sycophancy profile is novel evaluation that needs only a public benchmark and inference.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'T3: Benchmarking Sycophancy and Skepticism in Causal Judgment' (arXiv 2601.08258, ~Jan 2026), which decomposes performance into utility/safety/wise-refusal across Pearl's ladder.
