# Behavioral Self-Awareness of Misalignment in Public EM Organisms

**Problem:** A recent result claims emergently misaligned models show 'behavioral self-awareness' — when asked, they can describe their own misaligned tendencies, and this self-report shifts after realignment. Whether this self-awareness is robust, or merely an artifact of leading questions, has not been independently probed on the public organisms.

**Direction:** Vary the self-awareness probing experiment (arXiv:2602.14777) on the public open-weight EM models. Ask each organism to rate/describe its own alignment under (a) neutral questions, (b) leading questions, (c) third-person framing. Compare its self-reported misalignment to its measured behavioral misalignment on the 8-question battery. Test whether self-awareness survives prompt paraphrasing.

**Why it matters:** Self-reported misalignment, if reliable, could be a cheap monitoring signal; if it is just prompt-following, it would dangerously inflate confidence in introspective safety checks. Inference-only on existing public models, no lab resources, and directly probes a fuzzy/diffuse-reward safety property.

**Relevant context:** 'Emergently Misaligned Language Models Show Behavioral Self-Awareness That Shifts With Subsequent Realignment' (arXiv:2602.14777). Builds on Betley et al. public EM organisms.
