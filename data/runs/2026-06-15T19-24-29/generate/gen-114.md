# Do Jailbreaks Lobotomize? Measuring Capability Tax of Successful Attacks

**Problem:** StrongREJECT found that jailbreaks which bypass safety fine-tuning tend to reduce the model's capabilities, but this 'capability tax' has not been systematically measured as a function of attack type, raising the question of whether some attacks elicit harmful but useless output.

**Direction:** For a fixed open model, apply several jailbreaks (prefill, refusal-direction ablation, many-shot, low-resource translation) and measure post-jailbreak performance on neutral capability benchmarks (GSM8K subset, MMLU subset) administered within the jailbroken context. Compare each attack's capability degradation against its StrongREJECT willingness score.

**Why it matters:** If the strongest willingness-inducing attacks also cause the most capability loss, the real-world threat is overstated and benchmarks should report a willingness-vs-capability tradeoff. This reframes how robustness is evaluated — a diffuse, fuzzy-reward measurement problem ideal for outside-lab work, needing no training.

**Relevant context:** StrongREJECT (Souly et al., arXiv:2402.10260, NeurIPS 2024): jailbreaks bypassing safety fine-tuning tend to reduce capabilities; prior evaluators overstate effectiveness by ignoring response quality. The capability tax per attack type and its correlation with willingness is unmeasured.
