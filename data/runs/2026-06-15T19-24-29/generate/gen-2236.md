# Replicating Sandbagging Detection via Easy/Hard Inconsistency in Open Models

**Problem:** Easy-to-hard generalization assumes models reliably solve easy versions of tasks. A strategic model might 'sandbag' (deliberately underperform). Whether a simple easy/hard consistency check can flag sandbagging has been explored for capability evals but not in the easy-to-hard evaluator setting from Sun et al.

**Direction:** Take an open model, prompt it to either answer honestly or to subtly sandbag, on graded easy/hard problem pairs. Replicate the easy-to-hard evaluator setup, then test whether a consistency statistic (good on easy, anomalously bad on hard given easy performance) detects the sandbagging condition above chance. Twist: use the easy-to-hard evaluator as a sandbagging detector rather than a scorer.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging breaks the easy-to-hard assumption that underpins a major scalable-oversight strategy. A cheap consistency-based detector that works on open models would be directly useful and is a natural model-organism-style study scoped to 30h. No lab tooling required.

**Relevant context:** Sun et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.09472) easy-to-hard evaluator generalization assumes honest easy performance. Sandbagging is a recognized strategic-AI concern. Repurposing the easy/hard evaluator as a sandbagging detector via consistency is the twist; aligns with model-organism steering.
