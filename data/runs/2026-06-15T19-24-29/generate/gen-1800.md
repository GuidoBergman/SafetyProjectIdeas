# Cross-Lingual Auto-Interp Failure Amplifies Multilingual Safety Gaps

**Problem:** Compound: (1) SAE auto-interpretation labels degrade in non-English/low-resource languages (interp failure) amplifying (2) the documented multilingual safety gap. If a 'harmful content' SAE feature is correctly labeled in English but mislabeled or missed in another language, any feature-based multilingual safety filter inherits a blind spot exactly where unsafe prompts already evade detection.

**Direction:** Take a multilingual open model with an SAE. Identify a candidate 'toxicity/harm' feature in English. Test its activation and auto-interp label consistency on parallel harmful prompts translated into 3-4 languages (including a low-resource one). Measure the joint gap: feature mislabel rate x baseline safety-filter miss rate per language.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual safety is a real deployed gap that frontier labs deprioritize for low-resource languages. Showing interp-based filters compound rather than fix the gap is high counterfactual value and uses only public translations.

**Relevant context:** 'How Far Do Auto-Interpretation Labels Generalize' (arXiv:2606.00356): features miss meaning in Serbian up to 4x more than English. 'SAEs Can Capture Language-Specific Concepts' (arXiv:2507.11230). XSafety / 'State of Multilingual LLM Safety' (EMNLP 2025) document non-English safety gaps.
