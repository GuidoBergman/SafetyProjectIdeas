# Linguistic Readability/Complexity Metrics to Audit Benchmark Item Difficulty Confounds

**Problem:** Safety/capability benchmark items vary wildly in surface linguistic complexity, so a 'failure' may reflect parsing difficulty rather than the targeted safety property — a confound that is rarely measured.

**Direction:** Import readability and linguistic-complexity metrics from psycholinguistics/education (Flesch-Kincaid, syntactic depth, lexical rarity, sentence length). Compute these features for items in an open safety benchmark and regress model pass/fail against them while controlling for the intended construct. Quantify how much score variance is explained by surface complexity confounds, and produce a 'complexity-balanced' item subset.

**Why it matters:** Exposes construct-validity threats where benchmarks measure reading difficulty instead of safety, improving cross-model comparability. Trivial compute (feature extraction + regression), no training; counterfactually valuable benchmark-quality auditing any student can run.

**Relevant context:** Readability formulas and syntactic-complexity measures are standard psycholinguistic difficulty indices; metamorphic/SE testing literature (METAL arXiv:2312.06056) and cognitive-bias work (arXiv:2410.15413) show surface form strongly affects LLM responses, motivating confound control.
