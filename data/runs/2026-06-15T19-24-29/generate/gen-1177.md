# Survey-Methodology Anchoring: Are All Models Anchored to the Same 'Consensus' Like a Biased Crowd?

**Problem:** Cognitive/survey science studies anchoring — judgments dragged toward a salient reference value. The Artificial Hivemind paper hypothesizes RLHF overfits to a single consensus. Testing whether all models are anchored to the SAME reference point (and shift together when anchored) borrows a clean cognitive-bias paradigm.

**Direction:** Run classic anchoring-experiment designs on a model panel: provide a high vs low numeric/normative anchor in the prompt and measure each model's shift on estimation and value-laden questions. Test whether models share an anchor (same baseline consensus) and shift in lockstep — homogeneity in their susceptibility to manipulation.

**Why it matters:** If all models share an anchor point and a shared susceptibility, a single adversarial framing can shift the entire deployed population's judgments simultaneously — a correlated-manipulation systemic risk. Cheap behavioral experiment, no training.
