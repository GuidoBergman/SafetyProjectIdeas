# Jailbreak Defense x Over-Refusal: Mapping the Compound Cost of Stacked Defenses

**Problem:** Two interacting failures: residual jailbreak vulnerability and over-refusal (false refusal of benign prompts). Each defense added to reduce jailbreaks tends to increase over-refusal. The compound effect of stacking multiple lightweight defenses (system-prompt hardening + input classifier + self-check) on the joint (attack-success, over-refusal) frontier is not mapped for open models.

**Direction:** On an open model, evaluate single and stacked combinations of 3 cheap inference-time defenses. Measure attack success (StrongREJECT subset) and over-refusal (XSTest/OR-Bench) for each combination. Success criterion: a Pareto frontier showing whether stacking defenses produces super-linear over-refusal growth for diminishing jailbreak reduction.

**Why it matters:** Operators stack defenses assuming additive safety; if over-refusal compounds faster than protection, the system becomes unusable before it becomes safe—a core helpfulness/safety tradeoff. Cheap, no training, lab-free; produces a reusable evaluation recipe.

**Relevant context:** OR-Bench (openreview obYVdcMMIT, 80k prompts) and XSTest (250 safe + 200 unsafe contrasts) measure over-refusal; arXiv:2509.11629 Answer-Then-Check and arXiv:2512.01037 'When Safety Blocks Sense' document the safety/utility tradeoff. StrongREJECT public.
