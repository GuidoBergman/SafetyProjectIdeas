# Does Shallow Safety Alignment Hold for Reasoning Models?

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) showed safety alignment is concentrated in the first few generated tokens for standard chat LLMs, enabling prefilling attacks. It is unknown whether open reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, QwQ) that emit a long <think> trace before the final answer exhibit the same shallow pattern, or whether the reasoning prefix changes where safety lives in the distribution.

**Direction:** Replicate Qi et al.'s prefilling-attack measurement on 2-3 open reasoning-distilled models. Measure per-token KL divergence between aligned and base completions across the reasoning trace and the final answer, and test whether prefilling the FIRST tokens of the <think> block (vs prefilling the answer) flips refusal. Compare the depth-of-alignment curve to a non-reasoning model of similar size.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning models are being deployed rapidly and labs cannot cover every architecture. A 30h external replication that locates where (if anywhere) safety lives in a reasoning trajectory directly informs whether prefilling defenses transfer, and is a clean, self-contained measurement requiring no training.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.05946): alignment adapts the distribution mostly over the first few output tokens, explaining prefilling/suffix/decoding attacks. H-CoT (arXiv:2502.12893) shows reasoning models leak harmful content in the CoT, dropping refusal from 98% to <2%. Open-weights reasoning models are freely available on HuggingFace.
