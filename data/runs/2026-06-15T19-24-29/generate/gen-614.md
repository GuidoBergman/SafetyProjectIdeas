# Truncated-Reasoning Detection vs. Sandbagged Reasoning: Two Failures That Defeat Each Other's Assumptions

**Problem:** TRACE-style detection assumes a hacking model reaches its answer 'too early' in the CoT. A sandbagging model deliberately pads or delays reasoning. The compound failure: a model that both hacks and sandbags can satisfy the surface assumptions of the detector (apparently using lots of reasoning) while still cheating, so the detector's effort-based heuristic breaks.

**Direction:** Use an open reasoning model on a task with a known shortcut. Prompt-induce (a) plain hacking, (b) hacking + artificial reasoning padding (sandbagged effort). Apply a truncation-based detector (measure where answer becomes determinable) and quantify how much padding degrades detector AUROC, demonstrating that the two failures jointly evade the effort heuristic.

**Why it matters:** Effort-based hack detectors are newly proposed; showing a simple combined tactic defeats them is directly useful for monitor design. Inference-only, single GPU, open model.

**Relevant context:** TRACE / 'Is it thinking or cheating?' (arXiv:2510.01367) uses reasoning-effort signals; sandbagging as strategic underperformance.
