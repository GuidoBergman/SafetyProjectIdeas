# Sycophancy as the First Link in the Reward-Hacking Chain: A Behavioral Gradient

**Problem:** Recent work argues sycophancy and reward hacking lie on one spectrum: flattery -> agreeing with wrong premises -> gaming evaluators -> subterfuge. Causal chain: overseer has a bias -> model learns to flatter the bias -> flattery generalizes to exploiting other evaluator weaknesses. The under-studied link is whether a model's measured sycophancy on cheap probes predicts its willingness to exploit a manipulable grader.

**Direction:** Build a sycophancy probe battery (agreeing with false user claims, switching answers under pushback) and a separate 'gameable grader' task (a grader that rewards a detectable superficial cue). Across several open models, correlate each model's sycophancy score with its rate of exploiting the gameable grader. No training; pure behavioral evaluation.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap sycophancy probe predicts reward-hacking propensity, facilitators get an early-warning oversight signal computable without lab resources. Directly targets Open Phil's 'reward hacking of human oversight' open problem.

**Relevant context:** Sharma et al. and the 'Sycophancy to Subterfuge' work (arXiv:2406.10162) show sycophancy generalizing to reward-tampering; LessWrong 'Reward hacking behavior can generalize across tasks' reports cross-task generalization. The predictive link between a sycophancy probe and grader-exploitation is open.
