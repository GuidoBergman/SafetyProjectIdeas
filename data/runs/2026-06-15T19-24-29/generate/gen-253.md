# Activation Probes vs. Distribution Shift: Replicating the OOD Cliff on a New Model Family

**Problem:** Activation-probe jailbreak detectors are touted as production-ready and cheap, but recent evidence shows probe accuracy on out-of-distribution attack families can fall below chance. This OOD cliff has not been systematically replicated across model families, leaving its generality unknown.

**Direction:** Train a simple linear probe on one open model's activations to detect a single jailbreak family (e.g., role-play), then evaluate it on held-out attack families (prefilling, many-shot, suffix, translation). Repeat on a second, architecturally different open model. Report the ID->OOD accuracy drop and whether the cliff is consistent across families — a clean replication of the surprising negative result.

**Why it matters:** If cheap probes systematically fail OOD, deploying them as a primary defense is dangerous; quantifying the cliff across families guides whether they belong in defense-in-depth. Training a linear probe on cached activations is the canonical beginner-friendly, single-GPU, no-lab-needed experiment.

**Relevant context:** Context file cites the LessWrong Jan 2026 claim that activation-based detection is production-ready and cheap, with follow-up 'replicate on different model families.' Counter-evidence: probe OOD accuracy often <70% and sometimes below chance (OpenReview 'From Sure to Sorry'); JailbreaksOverTime (arXiv:2504.19440) studies detection under distribution shift; FrameShield reports smaller gaps, so the magnitude is contested.
