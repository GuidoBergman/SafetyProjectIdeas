# Is Unlearning Reversible by Quantization Alone? A Tiny Audit

**Problem:** Recent unlearning-robustness papers note resistance to 'quantization attacks,' implying some unlearning methods leak forgotten knowledge back under quantization. Whether simple post-hoc quantization (a one-line operation any user performs) partially reverses unlearning on small open models is a concrete, untested safety concern.

**Direction:** Apply a standard unlearning method to a small open model on a defined forget-set. Quantize the unlearned model to int8 and int4 using off-the-shelf tooling. Measure whether forgotten knowledge re-emerges (forget-set accuracy) post-quantization vs. the full-precision unlearned model, across a couple of unlearning methods.

**Why it matters:** Quantization is standard for deployment on consumer hardware. If it silently undoes unlearning, every quantized 'safe' model is at risk — a deployment-realistic failure mode that needs no lab resources to demonstrate and is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** "Defying Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual LLM Unlearning" / FIT (arXiv:2601.21682) reports resisting quantization attacks, implying quantization can otherwise revive unlearned content. "Unlearning Isn't Deletion" (arXiv:2505.16831) on reversibility of unlearning.
