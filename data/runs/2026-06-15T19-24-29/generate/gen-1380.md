# Does a hallucination probe trained on one domain transfer to a very different domain?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. report concept-vector probes beat judges at hallucination detection. Probe-based hallucination detection (Marks & Tegmark; recent long-form probe work) usually trains and tests on similar data. A follow-up: does a probe trained to detect hallucinations on, say, factual QA transfer to medical or legal long-form generation where the cost of failure is highest?

**Direction:** Train a linear hallucination probe on internal activations from one domain (e.g. TriviaQA/factual entities). Evaluate zero-shot on out-of-domain long-form sets (e.g. a medical factuality set like HealthBench-derived items, or biographies/LongFact). Compare cross-domain AUC to in-domain AUC and to an uncertainty baseline (semantic entropy).

**Why it matters:** Cross-domain transfer determines whether one cheap probe can monitor a deployed model broadly, or whether per-domain probes are needed. High-stakes domains (medical/legal) are exactly where monitoring matters and where labs lack data — strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. probes-beat-judges result (Science 2026). 'Real-Time Detection of Hallucinated Entities' (arxiv 2509.03531) — linear probes 0.87 AUC. 'Simple Factuality Probes' (EMNLP 2025 findings).
