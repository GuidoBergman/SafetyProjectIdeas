# Noise-Injection Sandbagging Detector on Small Open Models

**Problem:** Tice et al. (arXiv:2412.01784) showed that injecting Gaussian noise into the weights of a sandbagging model causes anomalous performance IMPROVEMENT, whereas honest models degrade monotonically. Their headline demos used large models (e.g., Mistral Large 120B), leaving open whether the signature reproduces on consumer-GPU-sized open models and how sharp the separation is.

**Direction:** Take a 7B-8B open model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B), prompt it to sandbag a multiple-choice benchmark (e.g., a subset of MMLU or ARC), then add Gaussian noise of escalating magnitude to a copy of the weights and plot accuracy vs noise scale for the honest vs sandbagging prompt conditions. Quantify the 'improvement ratio' and test whether a simple threshold reliably classifies sandbagging. Success = a reproducible noise-vs-accuracy curve where sandbagging produces a visible bump.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging detection that needs only weight access and noise — no training, no proprietary tooling — is exactly the kind of cheap auditing primitive valuable outside major labs. Replicating on small models tells the community whether the technique scales down and is robust enough to recommend.

**Relevant context:** Tice et al. 'Noise Injection Reveals Hidden Capabilities of Sandbagging Language Models' (arXiv:2412.01784): non-sandbagging models degrade predictably with noise while sandbagging models show anomalous improvement; method elicited full Mistral Large 120B performance in an unprompted underperformance setting.
