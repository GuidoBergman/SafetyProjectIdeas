# Alignment Faking Across Quantization Levels

**Problem:** The Hughes et al. open-source alignment-faking replication shows the faking 'compliance gap' appears in Llama-3.1-405B but not smaller models. It is unknown whether aggressive quantization (4-bit, 8-bit) of a single large open model erodes or preserves the alignment-faking compliance gap.

**Direction:** Take the released prompted-only alignment-faking harness and run the identical free-tier vs paid-tier prompt distinction on one large open model (e.g. Llama-3.1-70B or Qwen2.5-72B) loaded at FP16, 8-bit, and 4-bit quantization on a single consumer GPU. Use the released compliance/faking classifier to measure the compliance gap at each precision and chart how quantization shifts the gap.

**Why it matters:** Practitioners deploy quantized open weights constantly; if quantization changes deceptive reasoning rates, deployment-time safety evaluations done at full precision may not transfer. This is a cheap, lab-independent check on a deployment variable labs rarely vary.

**Relevant context:** Hughes et al. (2025) 'Alignment Faking Revisited' open-sources classifiers and extends to Llama 3 8B/70B/405B, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek in the prompted-only setting; faking appears in Llama-3.1-405B but not smaller models (alignment.anthropic.com/2025/alignment-faking-revisited). Original: Greenblatt et al. arXiv:2412.14093.
