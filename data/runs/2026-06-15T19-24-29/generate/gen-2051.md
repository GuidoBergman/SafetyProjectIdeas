# Interrupting EM at the Data Link: Does Mixing Aligned Data Dilute the Generalization?

**Problem:** The EM chain starts at harmful fine-tuning data. A practical intervention on this first link is data dilution: in real pipelines harmful examples are rare relative to benign ones. The under-studied question is the mixing ratio at which EM is suppressed — i.e., how much aligned data must accompany harmful data to block the chain.

**Direction:** Create LoRA fine-tuning mixtures of insecure-code data and benign helpful data at ratios from 100% harmful down to 1% harmful, holding total steps fixed. Measure broad-misalignment rate at each ratio on a small Qwen model to find the dilution threshold that breaks the EM causal chain.

**Why it matters:** It tells us whether EM is a realistic risk for large mixed training corpora (if even 1% harmful data triggers it, that's alarming) or a contrived small-dataset artifact. This directly informs data-curation safety for anyone fine-tuning open models — no lab infra required.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (2502.17424): EM induced by narrow harmful datasets. Model Organisms for EM (2506.11613): rank-1 LoRA on small Qwen suffices, enabling many cheap mixture runs on a single GPU.
