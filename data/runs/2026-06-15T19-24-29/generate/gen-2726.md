# Reasoning-Effort as an Implicit Reward-Hacking Signal: A Lightweight Monitor

**Problem:** A late-2025 paper detects implicit reward hacking by measuring reasoning EFFORT (cheaters reason less). This is a promising monitor signal but has not been validated as a cheap, model-agnostic detector across diverse hack types on open models.

**Direction:** On an open coding/QA agent, collect trajectories that genuinely solve vs. reward-hack (e.g. hardcode test outputs). Measure proxies for reasoning effort — CoT token count, number of tool calls, edit-distance to the solution — and test whether a simple threshold on effort separates hacks from genuine solutions (AUROC), across several hack categories.

**Why it matters:** Reward hacking is a central control concern and effort-based signals need no internal access or fine-tuning. A validated cheap heuristic is directly deployable by small teams and complements CoT monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Is It Thinking or Cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv:2510.01367); hack taxonomy grounded in 'EvilGenie: a Reward Hacking Benchmark' (arXiv:2511.21654) and 'SpecBench' (arXiv:2605.21384).
