# Interp-Guided Adversarial Examples Plus Probe Monitoring Collapse

**Problem:** Compound: (1) mech interp used offensively to craft targeted adversarial inputs (interp-as-attack-channel) amplifying (2) the failure of probe-based monitors. If circuit/feature knowledge lets an attacker craft inputs that route around the exact activations a probe watches, interp simultaneously builds the attack and reveals the monitor's blind axis.

**Direction:** On a small open model, train an unsafe-content probe and identify the activation directions it reads. Use simple interp (gradient/feature attribution) to construct prompt perturbations that minimize the probe's read-out while preserving harmful output. Measure attack success enabled specifically by interp knowledge vs. a black-box baseline.

**Why it matters:** Argues a dual-use point: the same interpretability that powers a monitor maps out how to evade it. Quantifying the net effect is counterfactually valuable and needs only one consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** 'Using Mechanistic Interpretability to Craft Adversarial Attacks' (arXiv:2503.06269). 'Interpretability Tools Are an Attack Channel' (LessWrong). 'Red-teaming Activation Probes using Prompted LLMs' (arXiv:2511.00554) gives a black-box baseline to compare against.
