# Do Reasoning Models Actually Reduce Inter-Model Homogeneity?

**Problem:** 'Reasoning Models Generate Societies of Thought' claims reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ) show greater perspective diversity than instruction-tuned models. But that was measured WITHIN models. Whether reasoning models DIVERGE from one another (lower cross-model homogeneity) on open-ended/safety queries is untested — and matters for whether 'just use reasoning models' buys systemic diversity.

**Direction:** Compare a set of reasoning models vs. matched instruction-tuned models on shared open-ended prompts. Use Artificial Hivemind-style cross-model similarity metrics. Test whether final answers (not just CoT) of reasoning models are less mutually similar, or whether their reasoning still converges to the same conclusions.

**Why it matters:** If reasoning models converge to identical conclusions despite diverse CoT, the apparent diversity is cosmetic and systemic blind-spot risk persists. Cheap inference experiment with clear safety framing.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Reasoning Models Generate Societies of Thought' (arXiv:2601.10825, Jan 2026) by testing CROSS-model rather than within-model diversity, evaluated with Artificial Hivemind metrics (arXiv:2510.22954).
