# Does Fine-Tuning on Benign Data Erode Shallow Alignment? A LoRA Micro-Study

**Problem:** Success state: light task fine-tuning does not break safety. Backcasting: the needed result is a quantified dose-response between benign LoRA fine-tuning steps and loss of refusal, testing whether shallow alignment makes safety fragile to even non-adversarial tuning.

**Direction:** Apply a small benign LoRA (e.g. on a harmless instruction subset) to an open model for increasing numbers of steps, and measure refusal ASR at each checkpoint. LoRA on a 7-8B model is feasible on a consumer GPU in a few hours.

**Why it matters:** Qi et al. linked fine-tuning attacks to shallow alignment; a clean benign-fine-tuning dose-response shows whether the open-weight community degrades safety unintentionally. High counterfactual value: labs guard against malicious tuning, not accidental erosion by hobbyists.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946): fine-tuning attacks are explained by shallow alignment; the deepened-alignment objective resists fine-tuning attacks better. Open question whether purely benign fine-tuning also erodes the shallow safety layer.
