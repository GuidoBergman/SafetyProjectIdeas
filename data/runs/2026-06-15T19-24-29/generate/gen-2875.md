# Honest-Uncertainty Benchmark: Does the Model Say 'I Don't Know' on Unanswerable Items?

**Problem:** Benchmarks like SimpleQA/PersonQA reward correct answers but under-measure whether models appropriately abstain on genuinely unanswerable or false-premise questions, a safety-relevant calibration property. No compact tool scores abstention quality directly.

**Direction:** Build a small set mixing answerable, unanswerable, and false-premise questions. Score open models on abstention rate, over-confidence (confident wrong answers), and false-premise pushback, producing an 'honest-uncertainty' profile. Compare across models and decoding temperatures.

**Why it matters:** Calibrated abstention is a low-glamour but safety-critical property (hallucination underlies many deployment harms); a cheap, reusable benchmark fills a gap left by accuracy-only QA evals. No training, public models.

**Relevant context:** Key benchmarks SimpleQA and PersonQA target factuality but emphasize correctness over abstention (subfield context). Open Problem cluster on reliable measurement and hallucination. False-premise/unanswerable handling is an underbuilt evaluation target distinct from contamination and refusal benches.
