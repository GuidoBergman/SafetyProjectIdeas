# CommitmentBench: Measuring Whether LLMs Honor Costly Pre-Commitments

**Problem:** Updateless decision theory and bargaining theory hinge on an agent's ability to make and keep commitments that are locally costly (threats, promises, ultimatum-game fairness). LLM game-theory studies report aggregate choices but no benchmark isolates commitment-keeping: will a model follow through on a stated commitment when defaulting would yield higher immediate payoff?

**Direction:** Build multi-step bargaining/ultimatum/trust-game scenarios where the model first states a commitment and later faces a tempting defection. Measure follow-through rate, and whether follow-through depends on being 'observed', on stakes, and on whether the commitment was self-generated vs assigned. Output a 'commitment integrity' profile per model.

**Why it matters:** Whether advanced agents can credibly commit underlies acausal trade, threat-resistance, and cooperative-AI safety. An empirical commitment-keeping benchmark operationalizes a decision-theoretic property that is otherwise purely formal, and isolates the dangerous 'only keeps commitments when watched' pattern. API-only, reproducible anywhere.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in MIRI's TDT/UDT commitment literature, cooperative-AI bargaining game theory, and LLM game-theory evaluations (arXiv:2506.09390). Scenario scripting + payoff bookkeeping is beginner-feasible.
