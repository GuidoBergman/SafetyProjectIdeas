# LLM-as-Judge Monoculture: Do Many Judges Share the Same Bias?

**Problem:** Correlated Errors showed LLM-as-judge correlation; the Hivemind paper noted reward models/judges are poorly calibrated to idiosyncratic human preferences. If a panel of LLM judges shares a bias, 'judge ensembling' provides illusory robustness — a homogeneity risk for the entire eval ecosystem.

**Direction:** Replicate judge-correlation analysis on a small set of open judge models scoring a fixed set of response pairs from Infinity-Chat (which ships 25 human annotations per item). Twist: use the human annotation spread as ground truth and measure whether the judges' *errors* (deviations from human distribution) are correlated with each other — i.e., do all judges miss the same human-valued responses?

**Why it matters:** Eval pipelines and RLAIF increasingly depend on LLM judges; shared judge blind spots silently bias model development across the whole field. Quantifying this with the Hivemind paper's own human-annotated data is novel and needs no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Combines Correlated Errors' judge analysis (arXiv:2506.07962) with Infinity-Chat's 31K human annotations (arXiv:2510.22954). Open judge models only.
