# Probe Generalization Cliff: At Which Difficulty Does Latent-Knowledge Extraction Break?

**Problem:** The ELK chain assumes we can recover what the model 'knows' even on hard cases. The under-studied link is the generalization edge: probes recover ~75% of the truth gap on harder-than-training questions in quirky models — but where exactly does that recovery collapse? If extraction fails precisely on the hard cases that matter, ELK-style oversight breaks at the worst moment.

**Direction:** Take an open quirky/truthfulness probe pipeline, train probes on easy items, then evaluate probe AUROC across a graded difficulty ladder (e.g., problem hardness, reasoning depth). Plot the generalization curve and locate the 'cliff'. Test whether features like answer entropy or problem-difficulty signals (which LLMs are known to encode) predict where extraction fails.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes a known weak link in ELK oversight: extraction that fails on hard questions is unsafe. A clean characterization of the difficulty-generalization cliff is a focused, no-training contribution that frontier labs underprioritize relative to capability work.

**Relevant context:** Quirky-models probes recover 75% AUROC gap on harder questions (arXiv:2312.01037); 'LLMs Encode How Difficult Problems Are' (arXiv:2510.18147) provides difficulty-direction probes to reuse.
