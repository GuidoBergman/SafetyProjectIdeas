# Does Steering to Reduce Sycophancy Increase Hallucination? A Compound Activation-Editing Trade-off

**Problem:** Activation steering (interpretability tooling) is used to reduce sycophancy (RLHF failure). But steering can degrade factuality, inducing hallucination (a different reliability failure). The compound risk: a steering intervention aimed at one safety property silently worsens another, and the joint cost is rarely measured.

**Direction:** On an open model, derive a sycophancy-reduction steering vector (difference-of-means between sycophantic and non-sycophantic activations). Apply it at varying strengths and jointly measure (a) sycophancy rate and (b) hallucination/factuality on a QA benchmark. Plot the trade-off to reveal whether reducing one failure buys an increase in the other.

**Why it matters:** Steering is increasingly proposed as a cheap alignment lever; quantifying its cross-failure side-effects is essential and under-measured. Fully inference-based with simple linear-algebra steering, ideal for beginners, no training.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy from RLHF (DuET-PD, arXiv:2508.17450); steering vectors as difference-of-means, a standard cheap method; 'avoiding jailbreaks by discouraging representation' (Alignment Forum) shows steering side-effects matter.
