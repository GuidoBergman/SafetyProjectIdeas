# Audit-Budget Simulator: An Open Tool to Plug In Your Own Monitor and Read Out Safety vs. Usefulness

**Problem:** Control protocols are evaluated with metrics like safety (probability of catching/blocking an attack before damage) and usefulness (task completion), computed via importance-sampling over many simulated episodes. The math is in papers but there is no beginner-friendly tool where you supply a list of monitor scores on benign + attack trajectories and instantly get the resulting safety/usefulness frontier for simple protocols (defer-to-trusted, audit-top-k).

**Direction:** Build a small Python tool: input = arrays of monitor suspiciousness scores on labeled benign and attack trajectories + an audit budget. Output = safety/usefulness curves for several canonical protocols (trusted monitoring with audit threshold, defer-to-trusted, upfront-honeypot validation) by Monte Carlo / importance sampling. Validate by reproducing the qualitative shape of a published frontier.

**Why it matters:** Lowers the barrier to running control evaluations dramatically; lets any researcher with API access evaluate a monitor on their own data. Strategically valuable open infrastructure, exactly the kind of thing labs deprioritize but the community needs.

**Relevant context:** AI Control (arXiv 2312.06942) defines the red/blue safety-usefulness framework; Ctrl-Z (arXiv 2504.10374) uses importance sampling over 100,000 simulated tasks and BashBench; 'A sketch of an AI control safety case' (arXiv 2501.17315) formalizes the metrics. No open lightweight simulator exists for newcomers.
