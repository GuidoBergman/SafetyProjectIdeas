# Benchmarking Auto-Interp Explanations Against Causal Effect, Not Just Activation

**Problem:** Auto-interp scores (detection/fuzzing) measure whether an explanation predicts WHEN a feature activates, but not whether the feature CAUSES the described behavior. A feature can be correlationally interpretable yet causally inert, and there's no standard test combining the two.

**Direction:** For a sample of explained SAE features, run a cheap causal check: ablate/clamp the feature and measure whether outputs change in a way consistent with its auto-interp label. Define a combined score (correlational interpretability x causal effect) and report how often well-explained features are causally inert.

**Why it matters:** Safety monitoring wants features that causally drive behavior, not just correlate with it. A correlational-vs-causal benchmark exposes a hidden weakness of auto-interp pipelines and is reusable, lab-free measurement on public SAEs and small models.

**Relevant context:** Delphi scores explanations correlationally via detection/fuzzing. circuit-tracer validates feature roles via perturbation (feature clamping) on Gemma-2-2b/Llama-3.2-1b. SAEBench warns that proxy interpretability metrics may not reflect practical (causal) usefulness.
