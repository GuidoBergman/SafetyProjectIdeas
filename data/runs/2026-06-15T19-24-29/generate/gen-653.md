# Mapping goal-misgeneralization triggers as a minimal model organism

**Problem:** The classic goal-misgeneralization work shows agents competently pursue unintended goals OOD. Recent 2026 deception/misalignment surveys call for cheap, reproducible model organisms; a minimal LLM-agent goal-misgeneralization organism is still scarce.

**Direction:** Construct a tiny text environment with a spurious correlate of the intended goal (e.g. 'go to the green door' vs 'go to the door on the left', correlated in 'training' prompts). Using an open instruct model purely via prompting (few-shot 'training' in context), test whether it follows the spurious feature OOD. Catalogue which spurious-correlate types most reliably induce misgeneralization. Success = a ranked list of robust goal-misgeneralization triggers.

**Why it matters:** A cheap, shareable model organism for goal misgeneralization lets the whole community study a core inner-alignment failure without training anything — high counterfactual value and beginner-feasible.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Goal Misgeneralization: Why Correct Specifications Aren't Enough' (arXiv:2210.01790) framing, motivated by the 2026 call for reproducible organisms in 'AI Deception: Risks, Dynamics, and Controls' (arXiv:2511.22619).
