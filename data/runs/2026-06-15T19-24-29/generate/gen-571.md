# Effort-Asymmetry of Oversight: Is Verifying a Subtle Sabotage Harder Than Producing It?

**Problem:** AI control assumes the blue team can verify cheaply relative to the red team's attack cost. The under-studied link is the asymmetry: if checking a subtle sabotage takes more effort than inserting it, monitoring loses the economic game. This cost-asymmetry is rarely measured empirically.

**Direction:** On a small code or reasoning task, have a red-team prompt insert subtle errors and measure (a) the red team's success rate at smuggling and (b) a trusted monitor's compute/queries needed to catch each, building an attack-cost vs verification-cost curve. Identify task properties where verification stays cheaper (good for defenders) vs blows up.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying the verification-vs-attack cost asymmetry directly informs when control protocols are economically viable — a foundational, lab-independent question runnable with open models and no training.

**Relevant context:** Greenblatt et al. AI Control (arXiv:2312.06942) safety-usefulness framing; 'CoT Red-Handed' stress tests (arXiv:2505.23575); debate/verification asymmetry arguments in scalable-oversight theory.
