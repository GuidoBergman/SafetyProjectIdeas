# Can quirky-model truth probes detect a backdoor? Bridging ELK probing and mechanistic anomaly detection

**Problem:** The 'quirky language models' release (96 models finetuned to err on keyword triggers) showed linear probes recover most of the truth signal even when the model lies. Separately, ARC frames backdoor detection as mechanistic anomaly detection. Whether ELK-style truth probes trained on clean inputs flag backdoor-triggered outputs as anomalous has not been cleanly tested.

**Direction:** Take a small quirky/backdoored open model (the Mechanistic-Anomaly-Detection HF datasets provide ready material). Train a logistic-regression truth probe on contrast pairs from clean inputs only, then measure whether the probe's confidence/representation shifts anomalously on triggered inputs — treating probe disagreement with model output as an anomaly score. Report ROC for detecting triggered examples.

**Why it matters:** Connects two formal-alignment threads (ELK and mechanistic anomaly detection) into one cheap experiment. A working anomaly-detection prototype from activations is strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure, and uses only public backdoored models on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2312.01037 (quirky models / ELK), Mechanistic-Anomaly-Detection HF datasets, and ARC's 'mechanistic anomaly detection and ELK' framing. Context open problem: 'mechanistic explanations for identifying anomalous behavior on novel inputs'.
