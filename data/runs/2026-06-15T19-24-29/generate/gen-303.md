# Probe-Then-Obfuscate: Reproducing the Activation-Probe Evasion Link on a Small Model

**Problem:** The proposed defense chain is: harmful intent -> distinctive activation -> probe flags it -> harm blocked. Bailey et al. show this chain breaks via obfuscated activations (attacks drop probe recall 100%->0% while keeping 90% jailbreak success). The under-studied link is how much attacker effort/optimization is required to obfuscate on small open models a student can run.

**Direction:** Train a simple linear harmfulness probe on Llama-3-8B residual-stream activations (harmful vs benign prompts). Then apply a lightweight obfuscation pressure (e.g. soft-prompt or suffix tuned to lower probe score while preserving a target completion) and measure the probe-recall-vs-jailbreak-success tradeoff curve, characterizing how cheap evasion is.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests robustness of the cheap activation-probe defense that Jan 2026 digests hyped as production-ready. Counterfactually valuable: studies a defense's failure mode outside any lab, needs only one open model and standard probing, surfaces a concrete threat to a deployed defense class.

**Relevant context:** Bailey et al. 'Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses' (arXiv:2412.09565) report attacks reduce probe recall from 100% to 0% while retaining 90% jailbreak success; SAEs, representation probing, and latent OOD detection are all vulnerable. The subfield context notes activation probes achieve cheap production-ready detection.
