# Self-Preference Bias as an Oversight Vulnerability: Does a Judge Favor Its Own Family's Sneaky Answers?

**Problem:** Self-preference bias research (arXiv:2410.21819) shows LLM judges prefer text they themselves are likely to generate (low perplexity). In an oversight setting this could mean a judge systematically under-flags errors from its own model family, a blind spot a strategic AI could exploit.

**Direction:** Take 2-3 open model families. Have each generate subtly-wrong answers to a QA set, then have each family's model judge correctness of all families' answers. Measure whether judges miss more errors from their own family. Follow-up: test whether paraphrasing answers (to break the perplexity signature) removes the blind spot.

**Why it matters:** If overseers are systematically blind to in-family deception, multi-vendor or cross-family oversight becomes a concrete defense. This is a model-organism-style study of an oversight failure mode, fully runnable on open models and public QA data.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al. 2024 (arXiv:2410.21819): GPT-4 shows significant self-preference; bias rooted in perplexity/familiarity. Quantifying/Mitigating Self-Preference (arXiv:2604.22891) extends measurement.
