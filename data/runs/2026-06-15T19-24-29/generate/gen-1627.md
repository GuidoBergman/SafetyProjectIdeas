# Does steering activations off-manifold leave a detectable fingerprint?

**Problem:** Unreliability work attributes steering failures to pushing activations OFF the data manifold, where downstream layers behave unpredictably. Whether 'off-manifold-ness' is itself cheaply measurable — and predicts both failure and detectability — has not been operationalized for safety steering.

**Direction:** On one open model, fit a simple density/distance model (e.g. Mahalanobis distance or kNN distance to a bank of clean activations) at a chosen layer. Inject safety steering vectors at varying strengths and measure how far steered activations drift off-manifold. Test whether off-manifold distance predicts (a) steering failure and (b) the probe-detectability of steering.

**Why it matters:** A single cheap 'off-manifold' statistic that simultaneously flags unreliable steering AND flags injected steering would be a dual-use safety monitor. Grounding an explanation from the unreliability literature in a measurable detector is high-value and training-free.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Understanding (Un)Reliability of Steering Vectors: Geometric Predictors and the Limits of Linear Approximations' (arXiv:2602.17881, Feb 2026), operationalizing its off-manifold hypothesis.
