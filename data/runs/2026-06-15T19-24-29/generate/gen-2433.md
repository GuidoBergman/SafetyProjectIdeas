# Agreeableness Bias in Validators: Replicating the False-Acceptance Asymmetry

**Problem:** Beyond Consensus: Mitigating the Agreeableness Bias (arXiv:2510.11822, Dec 2025) finds validators reliably confirm correct feedback but frequently fail to reject incorrect feedback — an asymmetric oversight failure. The size of this asymmetry across open models and tasks is not well mapped.

**Direction:** Present an open validator model with feedback/claims that are half correct and half incorrect on a labeled task. Measure the asymmetry: true-acceptance rate vs. false-acceptance rate. Test simple interventions (forcing the validator to argue the opposing case first) and see if the asymmetry shrinks.

**Why it matters:** A validator that rubber-stamps wrong feedback is a direct overseer-reward-hacking surface. Quantifying and trying to fix the false-acceptance asymmetry is a concrete, inference-only oversight contribution.

**Relevant context:** Beyond Consensus: Mitigating the Agreeableness Bias in LLM Judge Evaluations (arXiv:2510.11822, Dec 2025): validators reliably confirm correct feedback but frequently fail to reject incorrect feedback, a systematic agreeableness bias.
