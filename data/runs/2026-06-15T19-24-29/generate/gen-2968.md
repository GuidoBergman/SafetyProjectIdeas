# Self-Bias Detector for LLM Safety Judges

**Problem:** A success state requires safety autograders that don't favor responses written by their own model family. Self-bias (judges scoring their own outputs higher) is documented but rarely measured in the safety-judging context.

**Direction:** Generate responses to identical safety prompts from 3 open model families. Have each model judge all responses (blinded to authorship) on a safety rubric. Compare each judge's scores for own-family vs. other-family responses, controlling for actual quality via a human-rated subset. Deliverable: a per-judge self-bias estimate and its effect on leaderboard ordering.

**Why it matters:** Self-bias can silently corrupt cross-model safety comparisons (a NIST/CAISI need). Inference-only, open models, no lab data. Counterfactual and reusable wherever an LLM judges safety.

**Relevant context:** Search results report autograder self-bias (LLM graders favor same-LLM/machine-generated content); 'Skewed Score' (arXiv 2507.03772) on autograder biases; US CAISI/NIST need for standardized cross-model metrics (context file).
