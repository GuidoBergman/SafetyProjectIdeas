# Reasoning-Model Shallow-Alignment Probe (CoT vs Final Answer)

**Problem:** The shallow-alignment finding was established on standard instruction-tuned models. Reasoning models that emit chain-of-thought before a final answer have a different token structure, and it is unmeasured whether their safety is shallow in the CoT, the final answer, or both.

**Direction:** Take an open reasoning model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-distill, QwQ, or a small open o1-style model) and prefill (a) the start of the reasoning trace and (b) the start of the final answer with affirmative tokens. Compare attack success when the CoT is left to refuse vs forced affirmative. Build a small 'where is the safety' diagnostic that localizes alignment depth across the reasoning/answer boundary.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning models are increasingly deployed and shown to be MORE exploitable in multi-turn settings; understanding whether their safety lives in the CoT or the answer tells defenders where to put probes/monitors. Directly extends the ICLR finding to a new architecture class with no training.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) shallow alignment. 'Safety in Large Reasoning Models: A Survey' (arXiv:2504.17704) and Crescendo work note reasoning models like o1/DeepSeek-R1 are 'particularly susceptible' because explicit CoT 'renders them more exploitable.' Context file lists 'test whether reasoning models exhibit same shallow alignment pattern' as a suggested follow-up.
