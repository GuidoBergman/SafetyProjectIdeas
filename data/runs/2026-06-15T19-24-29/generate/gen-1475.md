# Refusal removal generalization gap: does an English/short-prompt refusal vector fail on long or coded harmful requests?

**Problem:** Refusal-direction ablation drops refusal to near zero on short standard harmful prompts, but generalization to long, multi-step, or obfuscated harmful requests is a boundary condition. A vector that removes refusal on JailbreakBench-style prompts may fail or over-trigger on different harmful formats.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal direction on an open model using standard short prompts, then test refusal-removal effectiveness across prompt families: long contextual requests, role-play framings, and lightly obfuscated harmful asks. Measure where ablation stops working and where it incorrectly suppresses benign refusals.

**Why it matters:** Understanding where refusal-direction interventions generalize defines both the attack surface (where stripping refusal is easy) and the defensive limits (where steering-based safety is reliable). This boundary mapping is a clean, reproducible red-team-flavored contribution needing only open models.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Arditi et al. (arXiv 2406.11717) refusal-single-direction and OOD-brittleness of steering vectors (arXiv 2603.24543, 2504.04635). Inference-only on open safety-tuned model.
