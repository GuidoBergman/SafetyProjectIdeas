# Patterns of Responsibility: Who Gets Blamed (and Who Escapes) After AI Harms?

**Problem:** Following AI harms, responsibility is diffuse across developers, deployers, and users, and a governance failure mode is that accountability systematically lands on the wrong (or weakest) party while developers escape. Recent work began mapping responsibility patterns from incidents, but the distribution across actor types and harm categories is not fully characterized for policy use.

**Direction:** Characterize responsibility-attribution patterns. Using a sample of AIID incidents (or the responsibility-annotated subset), code each for which actor was held responsible (developer / deployer / user / no one) and the harm type. Quantify the distribution and test whether developers are under-attributed relative to their causal role. Identify harm categories where accountability systematically fails to attach to anyone.

**Why it matters:** Liability allocation is a core governance lever; if blame predictably misses upstream developers, regulation needs to redistribute responsibility. Grounding this in observed incidents (not theory) gives liability scholars and regulators empirical traction. Public incident data, no compute, fully independent.

**Relevant context:** 'From Incidents to Insights: Patterns of Responsibility following AI Harms' (arXiv 2505.04291); AIID; Medium analysis of AIID liability ($2.9B figure).
