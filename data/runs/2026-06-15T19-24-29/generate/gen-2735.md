# TraceGuard Lite: Does Multi-Dimensional Scoring Resist Collusion Cheaply?

**Problem:** TraceGuard proposes structured multi-dimensional monitoring as a collusion-resistant control protocol. Whether the multi-dimensional aspect (scoring several independent axes vs. one aggregate suspicion score) actually buys collusion resistance on cheap open monitors is untested.

**Direction:** Implement a lite TraceGuard: have a monitor score trajectories on several distinct dimensions (e.g. goal-alignment, action-justification, side-effect risk) vs. a single aggregate score. Simulate an untrusted-monitor collusion attempt and measure whether multi-dimensional scoring is harder to subvert (catch-rate under collusion).

**Why it matters:** Collusion between untrusted policy and untrusted monitor is a central failure mode. If multi-axis scoring is cheaply more collusion-resistant, that's directly buildable infrastructure for small deployers.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'TraceGuard: Structured Multi-Dimensional Monitoring as a Collusion-Resistant Control Protocol' (arXiv:2604.03968, 2026) and the collusion taxonomy in 'When can we trust untrusted monitoring?' (arXiv:2602.20628).
