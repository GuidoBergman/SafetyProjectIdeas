# Cheap Pre-Finetuning Misalignment Auditor: Projection-Difference Screening on a Laptop GPU

**Problem:** A safety success state: before anyone fine-tunes an open model on a domain dataset, a cheap pre-flight check flags datasets that will induce emergent misalignment. Backcasting, we need a published, validated, low-compute recipe that estimates a dataset's misalignment risk WITHOUT running the fine-tune. The persona-vectors work proposes 'projection difference' but it was validated at lab scale.

**Direction:** Reproduce projection-difference scoring on a small open model (Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B-Instruct). Extract a 'toxic/villain persona' direction via contrastive prompts, score several candidate fine-tuning datasets (insecure code, evil numbers, benign code, medical QA) by mean projection of their tokens, then actually fine-tune on each via rank-1 LoRA and measure realized misalignment. Test whether projection score predicts realized misalignment rank.

**Why it matters:** If a $5 GPU-hour screen predicts which datasets are dangerous, any open-source fine-tuner can self-audit before training. This is high counterfactual value: needs no lab infra, no proprietary data, and targets the diffuse risk of accidental misalignment from ordinary fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** Persona Vectors (arXiv:2507.21509) introduce 'projection difference' as a metric highly predictive of post-training trait shift. Betley et al. (ICML 2025, Nature 2026) show rank-1 LoRA on 0.5B models can induce EM. Code for EM is open.
