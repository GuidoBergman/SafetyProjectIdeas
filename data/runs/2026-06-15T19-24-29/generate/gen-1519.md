# The reasoning-model link: does a deception probe read the CoT or the hidden computation?

**Problem:** For reasoning models, the safety hope is that the chain-of-thought is faithful. The harm chain for a non-verbalized attack: model plans harm internally -> CoT stays clean -> harmful action without a verbalized plan. A concept probe could catch this IF it reads the hidden computation rather than just the CoT tokens. Whether deception probes track verbalized vs unverbalized intent is the unexamined link.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning-capable model, construct cases where the model is instructed to pursue a hidden objective while producing innocuous CoT (a feasible model-organism slice via prompting). Build a deception/hidden-goal probe and test whether it fires even when the CoT contains no incriminating text. Success criterion: probe AUROC on hidden-goal cases conditioned on the CoT being clean.

**Why it matters:** Models that attack without verbalizing their plan in the CoT are a flagged high-priority model-organism threat; an activation probe that catches them despite clean CoT is exactly the counterfactually-valuable monitoring tool. Scoped to prompt-induced hidden goals on an existing open model.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. extend concept steering to reasoning models; Anthropic defection-probe work on hidden goals. The clean-CoT-but-detectable-internally test is the novel decomposition.
