# AI-Agent Success-Rate Half-Life Replicated on Cheap Open Coding Tasks

**Problem:** The 'half-life' / time-horizon model of agent success (success probability decays with task length) was fit mainly on frontier models over expensive task suites. Whether the same exponential-decay shape holds for small open models on short, cheap tasks runnable on one GPU is unknown.

**Direction:** Replicate the success-rate-vs-task-length analysis on a set of short, automatically-checkable tasks (1 second to ~10 minutes of human time) using a small open agent model. Fit the decay curve and estimate a 50% time horizon. The twist: down-scale METR's methodology to consumer-GPU-feasible models and tasks.

**Why it matters:** Agentic autonomy is a flagged eval gap and a >1-year threat. A reproducible, cheap recipe for estimating a model's task time-horizon lets independent researchers track autonomy growth without lab compute or proprietary task suites.

**Relevant context:** METR 'Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks' (metr.org, 2025-03-19) defines 50% time horizon; 'Is there a half-life for the success rates of AI agents?' (arXiv 2505.05115) models exponential decay of success with length.
