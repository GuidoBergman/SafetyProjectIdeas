# What Counts as an 'AI Incident'? Measuring Definitional Drift Across Reporting Schemes

**Problem:** Different incident frameworks (OECD, EU AI Act, AIID, national regimes) define 'incident' differently, so the same event may be reportable in one jurisdiction and invisible in another — a hidden barrier to international coordination.

**Direction:** Collect the textual incident definitions and reporting triggers from 4-6 frameworks. Construct a set of ~30 real or synthetic borderline scenarios and use an LLM (prompted with each framework's definition) to judge reportability under each regime. Quantify divergence (which scenarios are reportable under N of K regimes) and map the definitional fault lines.

**Why it matters:** A common reporting framework cannot work if 'incident' means different things; surfacing the divergence empirically is a concrete, low-cost contribution to the national-incident-response and international-consensus open problems.

**Relevant context:** OECD 'Towards a common reporting framework for AI incidents' (29 criteria); AIID taxonomy; EU AI Act serious-incident reporting; The Future Society NTSB-analogy proposal.
