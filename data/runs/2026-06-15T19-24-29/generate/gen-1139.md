# The distilled-monoculture hypothesis: do high-distillation models share more failures?

**Problem:** Distillation-quantification work (arXiv 2501.12619) estimates that many open and closed models have high 'distillation degree' (sharing a teacher), while Claude/Gemini stand apart. If distillation drives homogeneity, high-distillation models should fail on the same items more than low-distillation ones — an untested prediction.

**Direction:** Group ~10 models into 'high-distillation' (likely sharing GPT-style teachers) vs. 'distinct lineage' clusters using the published distillation estimates. On a fixed error-prone benchmark, measure within-cluster vs. across-cluster error correlation. Test whether distillation lineage predicts correlated failure.

**Why it matters:** If distillation silently couples failure modes, the apparent diversity of the open-model ecosystem is overstated, and concentration risk hides behind nominally independent models. This is exactly the systemic-risk signal regulators want to monitor.

**Relevant context:** Grounds on Quantification of LLM Distillation (arXiv 2501.12619) and Correlated Errors (2506.07962). Inference + correlation analysis on a consumer GPU.
