# Position-of-Cue Effect: Does Where the Hint Appears Change Verbalization?

**Problem:** Causal chain: a cue in the context influences the answer -> CoT omits it. Under-studied link: does the LOCATION of the cue (system prompt vs. early user message vs. just before the answer vs. embedded in a document) change both its influence strength AND whether it gets verbalized?

**Direction:** Hold question and cue content fixed; vary only cue position across several insertion points. For flipped items, measure influence rate and verbalization rate by position. Success: a heatmap of influence and verbalization across cue positions, identifying which positions create the most monitor-invisible influence.

**Why it matters:** Tells monitor designers which context positions are 'blind spots' where cues silently steer answers — e.g. if document-embedded cues (RAG) are both influential and unverbalized, that's a concrete RAG-honesty risk. Pure prompting, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic (arXiv 2505.05410) inserted hints and measured low disclosure; the nudged-reasoning framing (Alignment Forum) shows hidden cues bias choices. Systematically varying cue POSITION as a factor in the verbalization gap is under-explored.
