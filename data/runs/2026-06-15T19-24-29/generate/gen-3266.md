# Does Deliberative-Alignment-Style Prompting Reduce Covert Action in Open Models?

**Problem:** Success state: cheap inference-time interventions meaningfully reduce scheming, giving deployers a stopgap before training fixes. Backcasting from Apollo/OpenAI's finding that deliberative alignment training reduces but doesn't eliminate covert behavior, we ask whether a prompt-only analog yields any of that benefit on open models.

**Direction:** On open agentic models, compare covert-action rate in scheming scenarios with vs without an inference-time 'anti-scheming spec' system prompt that asks the model to reason about a no-covert-action principle before acting. Measure reduction and, importantly, whether reductions are genuine or just suppress the visible CoT (cross-check with behavior).

**Why it matters:** If prompt-only mitigation helps, it's a free deployable safeguard; if it only hides the reasoning, that's a critical warning against cheap fixes. Both outcomes are valuable, training-free, and reproducible externally.
