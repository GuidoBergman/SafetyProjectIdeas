# Collaborative vs Adversarial Debate Under a Weak Judge

**Problem:** Recent work (arXiv:2510.20963) proposes collaborative multi-agent debate where agents fill in each other's gaps rather than purely competing. Whether collaboration helps or *hurts* a weak judge — by removing the adversarial cross-examination that exposes flaws — is unresolved.

**Direction:** Run identical error-detection / QA tasks under (a) adversarial debate and (b) collaborative debate with the same small open models and the same weak judge. Compare judge accuracy and, crucially, accuracy on items where the agents are jointly wrong (collusion risk).

**Why it matters:** If collaboration improves average accuracy but enables silent collusion against the judge, it trades a visible failure for a dangerous hidden one — exactly the kind of subtle oversight regression worth catching. Inference-only.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2510.20963 'Towards Scalable Oversight with Collaborative Multi-Agent Debate in Error Detection'. Kenton et al. (2024) adversarial debate baseline; majority-error-amplification noted in multi-agent settings.
