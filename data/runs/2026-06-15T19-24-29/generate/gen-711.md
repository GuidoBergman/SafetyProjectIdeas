# Specificity/hedging linguistic audit of frontier safety frameworks

**Problem:** Critiques (FAS, METR) say frontier safety frameworks are 'underspecified', but specificity is asserted qualitatively, not measured. There is no reproducible metric for how vague a commitment is.

**Direction:** Vary qualitative framework critique into a quantitative text experiment: build a small lexicon-based + LLM-classifier pipeline that tags each commitment sentence in public frameworks as binding ('will', 'must', dated) vs. hedged ('aim to', 'where feasible', 'consider'), and computes a hedging ratio per lab and per risk domain (CBRN, cyber, autonomy). Compare labs and correlate hedging with FLI Index scores.

**Why it matters:** Gives policymakers a cheap, reproducible vagueness metric to push for enforceable language, addressing the explicit EU-AI-Office gap between voluntary codes and enforceable standards. No lab access, public text only.

**Relevant context:** FAS 'Can Preparedness Frameworks Pull Their Weight?'; METR Common Elements; EU AI Act GPAI Code of Practice; Measurement challenges in AI catastrophic risk governance (arXiv:2410.00608).
