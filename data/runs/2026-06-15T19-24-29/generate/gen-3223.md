# Reliability/Inter-Rater Kappa Framework for Trustworthiness of LLM Deception Judges

**Problem:** We increasingly use LLMs to judge whether other models are being deceptive, but rarely measure judge reliability the way psychometrics measures inter-rater agreement, leaving monitor outputs of unknown trustworthiness.

**Direction:** Apply psychometric reliability theory (Cohen's/Fleiss' kappa, ICC, Krippendorff's alpha) to a panel of open-model deception judges scoring the same MALT transcripts: quantify inter-judge agreement, agreement with human labels, and how agreement degrades on subtle vs blatant deception. Identify whether disagreement itself predicts borderline/obfuscated deception.

**Why it matters:** Establishes whether automated deception judgments are reliable enough to underpin safety cases, and turns judge-disagreement into a useful uncertainty signal — a measurement-science contribution any external group can produce.

**Relevant context:** Inter-rater reliability (kappa, ICC, Krippendorff's alpha) is the psychometric standard for rater trustworthiness. MALT (METR) provides labeled deception transcripts. Model-auditing-model paradigms are common in this subfield.
