# Replicating Toy Models of Superposition with a structured (non-uniform) feature-importance distribution

**Problem:** Anthropic's Toy Models of Superposition trained tiny ReLU networks on synthetic sparse features and showed superposition emerges as a function of feature sparsity and importance. The canonical setup uses simple importance schedules. How superposition geometry (which features get dedicated dimensions vs. crammed together) changes under a *realistically skewed* feature-importance distribution (e.g., power-law / Zipfian importances) is under-explored.

**Direction:** Reproduce the toy-superposition model exactly (it trains in seconds), then replace the feature-importance vector with a Zipfian distribution and sweep sparsity. Measure how many features get 'pulled out' of superposition, the geometry (digons/pentagons/etc. from the original paper), and interference, comparing against the uniform-importance baseline figures.

**Why it matters:** Superposition is why neurons are polysemantic, which is the central obstacle to mechanistic interpretability and to the open problem of 'formal mechanistic explanations of neural network behaviors.' Real feature importances are heavy-tailed, so checking whether the published superposition phenomenology survives skewed importances tells us how well toy-model intuitions transfer to real networks — fully reproducible on a CPU/GPU laptop.

**Relevant context:** Toy Models of Superposition (Anthropic, transformer-circuits.pub 2022; arXiv:2209.10652): superposition as function of sparsity/importance, geometric configurations. Open Problem (context): 'formal mechanistic explanations of neural network behaviors for identifying anomalous behavior on novel inputs'.
