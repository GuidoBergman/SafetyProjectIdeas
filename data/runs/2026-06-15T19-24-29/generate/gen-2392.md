# Weak Supervisors Miss Jailbreaks: Oversight Gap x Adversarial Robustness Gap

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) weak-to-strong oversight gap (weak supervisors fail to flag unsafe content) and (B) adversarial robustness failure (jailbroken prompts elicit unsafe content). The danger is the conjunction: exactly the unsafe outputs that adversaries trigger are the ones weak overseers cannot detect, so the two gaps overlap rather than cover for each other.

**Direction:** Use a small open 'supervisor' model to classify outputs as safe/unsafe on two distributions: (1) benign harmful-intent prompts and (2) jailbroken versions of the same prompts (from a public jailbreak dataset). Measure the supervisor's miss-rate on unsafe outputs in each condition. The compounding metric is the additional miss-rate induced by jailbreaking — does adversarial framing make unsafe content harder for the weak overseer to recognize?

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight assumes a weak overseer is a useful safety net; adversarial robustness research assumes attacks produce detectable harm. If attacks specifically blind the overseer, defense-in-depth is weaker than the sum of its parts. No training; classification + analysis only.

**Relevant context:** A Pilot Study of Weak-to-Strong Generalization in Safety (arXiv 2410.12621) reports 'weak supervisors... fail to identify unsafe prompts.' Weak-to-Strong Jailbreaking (arXiv 2401.17256) shows >99% misalignment rates and that alignment is 'superficial, clustered in early decoding stages.'
