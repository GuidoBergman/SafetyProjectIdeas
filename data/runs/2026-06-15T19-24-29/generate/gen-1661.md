# Replicating Activation Patching for Factual Recall on Quantized vs Full-Precision Models

**Problem:** The ROME/factual-recall literature localized factual associations to mid-layer MLPs via causal tracing. This was done at full precision. Whether the same MLP layers remain the causal locus of factual recall after aggressive quantization — the form in which open models are actually deployed — is an untested but practically important replication.

**Direction:** Replicate causal-tracing factual-recall localization on a small open model at full precision: corrupt the subject token, restore hidden states layer-by-layer, and find the layers whose restoration recovers the correct fact (single GPU). The twist: repeat the identical causal-tracing protocol on the 4-bit quantized model and compare whether the peak causal layers shift, testing whether quantization relocates or blurs the factual-recall mechanism.

**Why it matters:** Model editing and knowledge-localization underpin proposals to remove dangerous knowledge from open weights. If quantization moves where facts live, edits validated at full precision may not hold in deployed quantized models. Testing this — public model, single GPU — checks whether a key safety-edit assumption survives real deployment conditions labs do not control.

**Relevant context:** Meng et al. ROME 'Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT' used causal tracing to localize facts to mid-layer MLPs; the method (corrupt-then-restore activation patching) is implemented in public libraries. bitsandbytes enables 4-bit quantized inference on consumer GPUs.
