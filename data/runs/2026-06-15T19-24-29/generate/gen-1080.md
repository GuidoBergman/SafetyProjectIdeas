# Hivemind on Safety Refusals: Do 10 Open Models Refuse the Same Way?

**Problem:** The Artificial Hivemind paper showed inter-model homogeneity on open-ended creative queries, but did not focus on safety-relevant prompts. If many models refuse borderline requests with near-identical wording and reasoning, then a single phrasing that slips past one model likely slips past all of them — a correlated safety blind spot.

**Direction:** Take the Artificial Hivemind methodology (output-similarity analysis across many models) and replicate it on a curated set of ~200 borderline/dual-use safety prompts instead of creative queries. Run 8-12 open-weight instruction-tuned models (Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Phi, etc.) at fixed temperature, embed refusal responses with sentence-transformers, and compute pairwise inter-model cosine similarity plus refuse/comply agreement rates. Compare homogeneity on safety prompts vs. neutral prompts.

**Why it matters:** If refusal behavior is homogeneous, a jailbreak phrasing found cheaply on one open model is likely to transfer to all, undermining the 'use multiple vendors as defense-in-depth' assumption. This is exactly the diffuse, systemic-risk concern (correlated failures) that frontier labs deprioritize and that needs no lab infrastructure to study.

**Relevant context:** Extends Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, arXiv:2510.22954) to safety-relevant domains, a follow-up the paper itself suggests. Uses standard embedding-diversity tooling (sentence-transformers cosine similarity).
