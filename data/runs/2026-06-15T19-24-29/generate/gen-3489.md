# Sycophancy Asymmetry: Are Models More Sycophantic Toward Politeness vs. Authority vs. Emotion?

**Problem:** Sycophancy benchmarks aggregate over pressure types. It is unclear which social signal — user politeness, claimed user expertise/authority, or emotional appeals ('this is really stressing me out') — most reliably causes a model to abandon a correct answer.

**Direction:** Hold the factual content constant and vary only the pressure modality across a controlled prompt set (authority, emotion, politeness, persistence). Measure capitulation rate per modality on several small open models, producing a 'sycophancy susceptibility profile' per model.

**Why it matters:** Different deployments face different pressures (a medical chatbot faces emotional pressure; an agent faces authority claims). Knowing each model's worst pressure axis lets deployers harden the right surface. Pure prompting, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** 'Sycophancy under Pressure' (arXiv:2508.13743) adversarial dialogues; ELEPHANT (Cheng et al. 2025) social sycophancy / face-preservation; 'Beyond Social Pressure: Epistemic Attack' (arXiv:2604.07749) distinguishing pressure types.
