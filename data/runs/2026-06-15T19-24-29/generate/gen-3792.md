# Task-Redirection Susceptibility: Which Persuasion Tactics Most Reliably Hijack a Web Agent's Goal?

**Problem:** The TRAP benchmark shows web agents can be persuaded to abandon their assigned task and pursue an attacker's task. It is unclear which specific persuasion tactics (authority, social proof, urgency, reciprocity) are most effective and whether effectiveness depends on where in the task the redirection appears.

**Direction:** Using TRAP-style scenarios (or a small mock web environment) embed redirection attempts framed with different classic persuasion tactics in scraped page content. Measure the redirection success rate per tactic, and additionally vary injection position (early vs. late in the agent's task) to test a 'commitment' hypothesis (agents more redirectable before vs. after committing to a plan).

**Why it matters:** Maps the persuasion attack surface of web agents to concrete, namable tactics, helping defenders prioritize. Web-agent hijacking is a near-to-medium-horizon threat; the study uses an open benchmark and modest compute, valuable outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** 'It's a TRAP! Task-Redirecting Agent Persuasion Benchmark for Web Agents' (arXiv 2512.23128). LPS-Bench (arXiv 2602.03255) on long-horizon planning safety under adversarial scenarios.
