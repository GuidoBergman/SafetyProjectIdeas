# Verbosity-vs-Catchability: Does Forcing Longer CoT Actually Help the Monitor?

**Problem:** Monitorability has been linked to both faithfulness and verbosity, and a tempting cheap intervention is to prompt the policy to 'think out loud more'. But it is unmeasured whether forcing more verbose CoT genuinely raises monitor catch-rate or just adds noise that a deceptive model can hide misbehavior within.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model with a seeded misbehavior, generate trajectories under several verbosity instructions (terse to very verbose). Hold the behavior constant. Measure CoT-monitor catch-rate vs. verbosity level, and separately measure whether longer CoT gives a deceptive model more room to bury a misleading-but-plausible justification.

**Why it matters:** Tells whether 'make it think out loud more' is a real monitoring lever or a false comfort. A simple, generalizable result on a cheap intervention deployers might adopt. Open-weights/API only.

**Relevant context:** 'Measuring CoT Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity' (arXiv 2510.27378) ties verbosity to monitorability; 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?' (arXiv 2510.19851) shows external/prompted CoT can be manipulated. Whether forced verbosity nets positive for catch-rate is unmeasured.
