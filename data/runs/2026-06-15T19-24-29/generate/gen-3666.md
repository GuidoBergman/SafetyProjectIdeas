# Cost-of-Caution: Measuring the Safety Tax of Confirmation Gates

**Problem:** Adding human-confirmation gates for risky actions is recommended, but the productivity/latency cost ('safety tax') and the false-block burden of different gating policies are not quantified, so deployers default to gating nothing.

**Direction:** On a benign-task suite, compare several gating policies (gate-all, gate-irreversible-only, gate-by-LLM-risk-score) on an open agent, measuring task completion, number of confirmations requested, false blocks (benign actions gated), and missed irreversible actions. Produce a Pareto frontier of safety vs. friction.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying the safety tax makes the case for selective irreversibility-gating concrete and adoptable. A Pareto analysis is exactly the lab-independent, deployable evaluation that closes the gap between safety advice and practice.

**Relevant context:** HITL guidance (Galileo/Databricks/hoop.dev): selective approval for irreversible/financial actions, expand envelope for low-risk. CSA 'out-of-band human approval.' OWASP LLM06 Excessive Agency. arXiv:2509.08646 plan-then-execute resilience.
