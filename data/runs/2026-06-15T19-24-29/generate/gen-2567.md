# Resampling-Incrimination Mini-Benchmark: How Many Resamples to Confirm an Attack?

**Problem:** Resampling protocols (Ctrl-Z) incriminate a suspicious agent by re-sampling the same action multiple times to gather more evidence, but there is no simple, reusable measurement of how the confidence of incrimination grows with the number of resamples for off-the-shelf models — the core efficiency parameter of the protocol.

**Direction:** On a small set of borderline-suspicious actions (some genuine attacks, some benign-but-weird), resample the action k times from the policy model and aggregate monitor scores. Measure how the true-positive/false-positive separation improves with k, and estimate the resamples-to-confidence curve. Report the audit-cost vs. incrimination-accuracy tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** Resampling is one of the most promising control protocols; characterizing its sample-efficiency on real models tells deployers the compute cost of reliable incrimination. A reproducible mini-benchmark makes the protocol tunable. API-only, modest compute.

**Relevant context:** Ctrl-Z (arXiv 2504.10374) introduces resampling and incrimination ('time travel'), using importance sampling over many tasks; it shows resampling improves on prior techniques but the resamples-to-confidence curve isn't packaged as a standalone, reusable measurement for arbitrary models.
