# Replicating CAA refusal steering on a non-Llama model family (Qwen vs. Gemma)

**Problem:** Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) refusal steering was demonstrated mainly on Llama-2. It is unclear whether the same single-layer refusal vector and coefficient regime transfer cleanly to other open model families with different tokenizers, RLHF recipes, and architectures.

**Direction:** Reproduce Rimsky et al.'s CAA refusal vector extraction on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Gemma-2-2B-it using the public CAA contrast pairs. Hold the methodology fixed (mean activation difference, add at post-prompt positions) and compare: which layer gives cleanest steering, the coefficient-vs-coherence tradeoff curve, and whether refusal is one-dimensional in each family. Run on a single consumer GPU.

**Why it matters:** If safety-relevant steering directions are family-specific, then steering-based safety tooling cannot be assumed portable. Documenting where CAA transfers and where it breaks is decision-relevant infrastructure work that any external researcher can do without lab resources, and it stress-tests the 'refusal is one-dimensional' claim on fresh models.

**Relevant context:** Rimsky et al., Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition (arXiv:2312.06681, ACL 2024); Arditi et al., refusal is a one-dimensional subspace across 13 chat models. CAA code is open-source. Gemma layer-14 steerability noted in steering-vector reliability literature.
