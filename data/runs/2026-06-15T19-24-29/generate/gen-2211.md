# Reward-Model Overoptimization: Reproducing the U-Shaped Gold-Score Curve with Open Reward Models

**Problem:** Gao et al. and follow-ups found that as a policy is optimized against a proxy reward model, true (gold) reward first rises then falls (Goodhart/overoptimization). Whether this curve and its KL-scaling hold for today's small open reward models, and how quickly the turn happens, is worth independent confirmation.

**Direction:** Without RL training: take an open reward model as proxy and a stronger open judge as 'gold'. Generate many candidate responses per prompt, then simulate increasing optimization pressure via best-of-n reranking with growing n (n is a clean proxy for optimization strength). Plot gold score vs proxy score vs n to recover the overoptimization curve and find where reward hacking kicks in.

**Why it matters:** Reward hacking of the overseer is a named open problem. Best-of-n is a training-free way to elicit and measure overoptimization on a single GPU, giving a reusable diagnostic for any proxy reward model — high counterfactual value, no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** Reward-model overoptimization (Goodhart) is a core RLHF failure (Lilian Weng reward-hacking survey 2024; reward-hacking-in-era-of-large-models arXiv:2604.13602). Best-of-n as optimization-pressure proxy is standard.
