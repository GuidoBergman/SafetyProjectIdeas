# Probe-Based Safety Case Meets CoT-Based Safety Case: Do Their Failure Modes Correlate or Cover Each Other?

**Problem:** Two proposed oversight layers — internal probes and CoT monitoring — are sometimes assumed to provide defense-in-depth (independent coverage). If their failures are correlated (both fail on the same hard/deceptive inputs), the 'two layers' provide far less protection than assumed: a compound rather than complementary system.

**Direction:** On quirky/open models, for each input compute (a) whether the honesty probe is correct and (b) whether a simple CoT/LLM-judge monitor catches the misbehavior. Measure the correlation of their errors. High positive correlation means the layers fail together (compounding); near-zero means genuine defense-in-depth. Report the joint-coverage estimate.

**Why it matters:** Defense-in-depth claims rest on failure independence. Empirically measuring whether two leading cheap oversight tools have correlated blind spots directly informs how much safety stacking them actually buys. Open models, inference + simple judge.

**Relevant context:** ELK probes (arXiv:2312.01037) and CoT-monitoring safety cases (arXiv:2510.19476; arXiv:2507.11473); subfield open problem on limits of what AI can hide.
