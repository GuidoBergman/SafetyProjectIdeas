# Stress-Testing Evaluation-Awareness as a Governance Reliability Threat Across Open Benchmarks

**Problem:** 'Decomposing and Measuring Evaluation Awareness' shows frontier models recognize they are being evaluated at rates that depend on the model-benchmark pairing, undermining the evidentiary value of compliance evaluations — but the analysis covers proprietary frontier models on four benchmarks, leaving open how the effect manifests on accessible open models that governance bodies could audit themselves.

**Direction:** Replicate the evaluation-awareness probing protocol on 2-3 open-weight chat models across several public safety benchmarks, measuring recognition rates and whether 'awareness' correlates with behavior shifts. Frame results as a governance-reliability discount factor: how much should an auditor trust a passing eval given measured awareness?

**Why it matters:** If models behave safer when they sense testing, every compliance evaluation is suspect — a model-organism-style reliability threat that external auditors, not labs, are best placed to quantify. Single GPU, open models, no training.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Decomposing and Measuring Evaluation Awareness' (arXiv:2605.23055, May 2026), which measures recognition across nine frontier models and four benchmarks but does not extend to auditable open models or derive a governance reliability discount.
