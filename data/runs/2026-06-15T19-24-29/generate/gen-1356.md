# Which safety concepts are actually linear? A linearity audit

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. explicitly ask which concepts have linear representations and which don't. Recent work (Wollschläger et al. 2025) argues refusal is higher-dimensional. A systematic linearity audit across safety concepts is missing and determines where simple linear steering can even work.

**Direction:** For a battery of safety concepts (refusal, toxicity, sycophancy, honesty, power-seeking), fit a 1-D linear direction and compare its steering/probing performance to a low-rank (2-4 dim) subspace on each. Rank concepts by how much the extra dimensions help, producing a 'linearity score' per concept on held-out evals.

**Why it matters:** Tells practitioners which safety behaviors are amenable to cheap linear control and which need richer methods. Pure measurement, high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Open Problem: which concepts have linear representations (Beaglehole et al.). Wollschläger et al. 2025 'concept cones'; Pan et al. 2025 higher-dimensional refusal. Linear/subspace probing methodology.
