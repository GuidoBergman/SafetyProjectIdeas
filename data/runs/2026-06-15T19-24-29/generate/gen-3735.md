# Selective-Quitting Safety Gain Replicated Across Model Families

**Problem:** 'Check Yourself Before You Wreck Yourself' showed that letting an agent selectively QUIT improves safety by preventing harmful actions in high-risk situations. The effect was demonstrated on a limited model set; whether quitting reliably improves safety for small open-weight agents (which may quit too rarely or too often) is untested.

**Direction:** Add a 'quit' action to agents on a high-stakes tool subset (e.g., from AgentHarm or ToolEmu) and replicate the safety-vs-utility tradeoff across several open-weight models. Measure harmful-action rate, unnecessary-quit rate, and benign-task completion, comparing the safety gain to the original paper.

**Why it matters:** A 'quit' affordance is a cheap, deployable safety intervention that any open-agent builder can add without training. Knowing whether it generalizes to free models — and where it backfires via over-quitting — is high counterfactual value and needs only inference.

**Relevant context:** 'Check Yourself Before You Wreck Yourself: Selectively Quitting Improves LLM Agent Safety' (arXiv 2510.16492): quitting is both a behavioral indicator of recognized high risk and an effective intervention preventing harmful action execution.
