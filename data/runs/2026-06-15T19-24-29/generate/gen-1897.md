# Cross-Lingual Generalization of Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** EM is shown to generalize across unrelated English tasks, but whether misalignment trained/triggered in English generalizes to other languages (or whether asking misaligned questions in another language evades it) is unexamined.

**Direction:** Vary the language of the EM evaluation. Take a public EM organism and translate the 8 free-form questions into 3-4 languages (using a translation model + spot checks), then measure misalignment rate per language. Test whether EM is an English-localized persona or a language-agnostic value shift.

**Why it matters:** Safety evaluations are overwhelmingly English; if misalignment hides or amplifies in other languages, multilingual deployments carry unmeasured risk. Inference-only on public models, no lab data, and probes a real coverage gap in current safety testing.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025: EM generalizes broadly across unrelated tasks. EM organisms are public (arXiv:2506.11613). Cross-lingual safety gaps are a known concern (and explicitly excluded as an already-covered framing only in the narrower 'mapping' sense).
