# Difficulty-Inverted Sandbagging: Do Models Fail the Easy Items?

**Problem:** A subtle sandbagging signature is non-monotonicity: an honest model's accuracy decreases with item difficulty, while a model selectively underperforming on a target topic may miss easy items it should trivially get right. This difficulty-accuracy inversion has not been used as a black-box detector.

**Direction:** On an open benchmark with per-item difficulty estimates (or a difficulty proxy from a strong reference model), elicit honest vs induced-sandbagged answering from a small open model. Fit accuracy as a function of difficulty in both conditions and test whether sandbagging flattens or inverts the expected monotonic curve, especially on the target subtopic. Report the inversion metric's detection power.

**Why it matters:** Item-difficulty structure is freely available from existing eval data, so an inversion-based sandbagging detector costs nothing extra and works black-box. Even a partial signal strengthens the toolkit independent evaluators have against strategic underperformance.

**Relevant context:** AI Sandbagging (arXiv:2406.07358); IRT/difficulty methods are standard in psychometrics-flavored eval work. Difficulty estimates can come from open model accuracy. Single GPU, statistics-heavy analysis.
