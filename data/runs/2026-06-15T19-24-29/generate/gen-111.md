# A steering slice of control-monitoring: can adding a 'deception' probe-direction as a TRIPWIRE catch alignment-faking without false alarms?

**Problem:** Control needs reliable misalignment monitors; concept-steering provides directions. Sub-problem: if we treat a learned 'deception' concept direction as a real-time tripwire (alarm when projection exceeds a threshold), what false-positive rate does it incur on benign-but-strategic conversations (negotiation, persuasion) that look superficially deceptive?

**Direction:** Decompose monitor reliability into a false-positive characterization. Extract a deception direction on a small open model, then run it as a threshold tripwire over a mix of (a) actual alignment-faking transcripts and (b) benign strategic dialogues. Plot the ROC and identify which benign behaviors trigger false alarms.

**Why it matters:** A monitor's usefulness is determined by its false-positive behavior on benign edge cases — exactly the unglamorous, counterfactual work labs underpublish. Open weights, one GPU, no training. The benign-trigger taxonomy is a directly useful deliverable for anyone deploying activation monitors.
