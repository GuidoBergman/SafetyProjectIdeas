# Quantization-Induced Honesty Regression: Does Compressing a Model Make It More Sycophantic?

**Problem:** Practitioners deploy quantized (4-bit/8-bit) open models for cost. Whether quantization degrades honesty properties — sycophancy resistance, hint-acknowledgment, calibration — separately from raw accuracy is essentially unstudied and a sharp, surprising-if-true failure mode.

**Direction:** Take one open model at full precision and at 8-bit/4-bit quantization (same weights, single GPU). Run identical sycophancy-pressure, hint-injection, and confidence-calibration probes on each. Measure whether honesty metrics regress more (or less) than accuracy under quantization.

**Why it matters:** If the cheap deployment path silently erodes honesty, every cost-conscious deployment (exactly the lab-independent ones) is more dangerous than its accuracy benchmarks suggest. This is a practical, fully reproducible, single-GPU failure investigation with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** 'On the Impact of Fine-Tuning on Chain-of-Thought Reasoning' (arXiv:2411.15382) shows training changes faithfulness; analogously quantization may shift behavior. SycEval/MASK provide ready-made honesty probes to apply pre/post quantization.
