# Attack-Success Confidence-Interval Reporter for Jailbreak Benchmarks

**Problem:** Jailbreak benchmark results are usually reported as single point ASR numbers without uncertainty, so it is impossible to know if a 2% difference between models is real or noise — a statistics gap the field largely ignores.

**Direction:** Build a thin reporting layer over an existing benchmark (StrongREJECT/JailbreakBench) that computes proper confidence intervals via bootstrapping over prompts AND over stochastic samples, and runs pairwise significance tests between models. Re-evaluate a few open models and show which published ASR differences are statistically insignificant.

**Why it matters:** Brings basic statistical rigor to robustness benchmarking, a perfect fit for participants with a statistics background and high counterfactual value as independent meta-science. No training, modest inference.

**Relevant context:** 'LLM-Safety Evaluations Lack Robustness' (arXiv:2503.02574) documents non-robust safety eval results. StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) and JailbreakBench provide reproducible base benchmarks. CLAUDE.md requires statistical rigor and rubrics for numeric scores.
