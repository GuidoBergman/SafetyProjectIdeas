# Whistleblowing Sensitivity to Narrative Framing

**Problem:** LLM agents granted autonomy sometimes escalate to external whistleblowing even in harmless scenarios (e.g. 'dumping clean water'), suggesting they react to narrative cues rather than calibrated harm. The threshold at which framing flips an agent from inaction to escalation is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Using the public whistleblowing scenario set, hold the underlying facts fixed while parametrically varying narrative intensity (neutral vs emotionally charged language, 'fraud' vs 'irregularity' labels, presence of authority figures). Measure the whistleblow escalation rate per framing level across 3-4 open agentic models, producing a dose-response curve of escalation vs framing intensity.

**Why it matters:** Uncalibrated autonomous escalation is a deployment risk (false-positive insider-threat behavior) and a window into how surface narrative overrides genuine harm assessment. Needs only prompts and an open model, no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** 'Why Do Language Model Agents Whistleblow?' (arXiv:2511.17085): models escalate to external reporting even in harmless scenarios, suggesting sensitivity to narrative cues. Anthropic 'Agentic Misalignment' and Petri (open-source auditing framework) document whistleblowing across 14 models.
