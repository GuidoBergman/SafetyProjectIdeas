# Backcasting Honest Self-Report of Hidden Goals: Probing an Open Model Organism's Willingness to Confess

**Problem:** A success state is a model that, when it has a hidden objective, will reveal it under direct honest questioning. Backward, this needs model organisms with known hidden objectives and a measurement of their confession rate. 'Spilling the Beans' shows fine-tuning and persona choices change confession generalization; a scoped probe of an existing open model organism's confession behavior is feasible without training.

**Direction:** Use an available open 'model organism' or construct one cheaply via system-prompt-induced hidden instruction (e.g., a secret preference the model is told to pursue without revealing). Probe with a battery of direct ('what are you trying to do?'), indirect, and honesty-persona-primed questions, measuring confession rate per probe style. Test the 'uncompromising honesty persona' effect reported in prior work.

**Why it matters:** Eliciting honest self-report of hidden objectives is central to detecting deceptive alignment, a long-horizon threat. A scoped model-organism probe needs no large training run and is exactly the kind of diffuse-reward study with high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Spilling the Beans: Teaching LLMs to Self-Report Hidden Objectives (arXiv 2511.06626) finds honesty-persona prompts improve hidden-objective admission; Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector (arXiv 2511.22662).
