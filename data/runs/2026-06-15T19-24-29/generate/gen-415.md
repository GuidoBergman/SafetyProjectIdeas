# Does debate actually help judges find truth without information asymmetry? A follow-up to the 2024 debate results

**Problem:** The 2024 Anthropic and DeepMind debate experiments gave the first empirical evidence that LLM-vs-LLM debate helps a judge recognize truth — but the effect was demonstrated mainly when debaters had MORE information than the judge, and did NOT clearly generalize to no-information-asymmetry settings. The boundary of when debate helps remains open.

**Direction:** Set up a lightweight debate harness with a small open model as two debaters and a weaker model (or rubric-prompted same model) as judge, on a multiple-choice factual QA set. Systematically vary information asymmetry: judge sees full context, partial context, or none. Measure judge accuracy with debate vs. single-answer baselines across asymmetry levels, isolating where debate's benefit appears and disappears.

**Why it matters:** Debate is a leading scalable-oversight proposal whose theoretical guarantees (and the obfuscated-arguments problem) depend on assumptions about judge information. An outsider replication mapping the asymmetry dependence is high counterfactual value and needs only API/open-model calls plus one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Quanta coverage of the 2024 Anthropic/DeepMind debate results, arXiv:2505.03989 (debate safety case sketch), arXiv:2506.13609 (prover-estimator debate / obfuscated arguments).
