# Vision vs. Language SAE Sparsity: A Controlled Layerwise Characterization

**Problem:** ViT-Prisma reports that vision SAEs exhibit substantially lower sparsity (more active features) than language SAEs, sometimes even reducing model loss, but the structure of this discrepancy across layers and its cause are unexplained open questions.

**Direction:** Using pre-trained Prisma vision SAEs (80+ weights, no training needed) and pre-trained SAELens language SAEs, measure L0 (active features per token), feature firing-frequency distributions, and dead-feature rates layer-by-layer under matched conditions, producing a side-by-side sparsity-structure comparison and testing whether vision-SAE density is concentrated in specific layers (e.g. early patch-embedding vs. late).

**Why it matters:** Understanding whether interpretability tooling transfers across modalities determines whether safety techniques validated on language models will work on the multimodal models being deployed. This is doable entirely with open pre-trained weights and no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (arXiv 2504.19475): vision SAEs show lower sparsity than language SAEs, reconstructions can reduce loss, and the discrepancy is an open question. 80+ pre-trained vision SAE weights and SAELens language SAEs are publicly available.
