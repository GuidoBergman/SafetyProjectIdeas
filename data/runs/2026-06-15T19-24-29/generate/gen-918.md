# Backcasting from 'Standards Bodies Use Comparable Risk Taxonomies': Aligning Three Public AI-Risk Taxonomies

**Problem:** A success state for international consensus on evaluation requires interoperable risk taxonomies. Today multiple taxonomies (MIT AI Risk Repository, CSET harm taxonomy, NIST AI RMF categories) coexist with no public crosswalk, so the same incident gets classified incompatibly across regimes.

**Direction:** Build a crosswalk mapping between 2-3 public AI-risk taxonomies: for each category in one, find the closest equivalent(s) in the others, flag gaps and one-to-many mismatches, and measure overlap/coverage quantitatively. Validate the mapping by classifying a sample of incidents under each taxonomy and checking consistency. Produce a public interoperability matrix.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'international evaluation consensus' shows taxonomy interoperability as the precondition for comparing risks across jurisdictions. A concrete crosswalk is reusable infrastructure for the International Network of AI Safety Institutes and incident-reporting harmonization. Pure public-taxonomy analysis — no lab resources, durable outsider contribution.
