# Pretraining-Data SAEs Miss Safety Behaviors: Refusal Coverage Audit

**Problem:** SAEs trained on pretraining corpora fail to encode features for chat behaviors like refusal, while SAEs trained on chat data do. Many publicly available SAEs are pretraining-data SAEs, so analysts may unknowingly use an SAE that is blind to the exact safety behavior they care about.

**Direction:** Take a pretraining-data SAE and (if available) a chat-data SAE for the same open model layer. On a set of refusal vs compliance prompts, measure how well each SAE's features separate refusal from compliance (max-activating feature overlap, linear separability of feature activations). Quantify the coverage gap and identify which safety-relevant distinctions the pretraining SAE simply cannot represent.

**Why it matters:** Choosing the wrong SAE means safety monitors are structurally unable to see the behavior of interest. Demonstrating the refusal-coverage gap gives practitioners a concrete reason to check what data their interpretability tool was trained on before trusting it.

**Relevant context:** Recent work cited in the SAE reconstruction literature notes SAEs trained on pretraining data fail to encode refusal features whereas chat-data SAEs do; 'Understanding Refusal in Language Models with Sparse Autoencoders' (arXiv:2505.23556) studies refusal features directly.
