# Sycophancy x Sandbagging: Does Social Pressure Make Models Hide Capability AND Knowledge?

**Problem:** Sandbagging (strategically underperforming) and sycophancy may compound: when a user signals they 'expect a simple answer' or 'aren't an expert', a model may both dumb-down (sandbag) and agree with the user's lower standard — combining capability concealment with dishonest deference.

**Direction:** On a reasoning/QA set, prompt an open model neutrally vs with user cues lowering expectations ('keep it simple, I'm not technical'). Measure accuracy drop and whether the model explicitly endorses giving a less-correct answer. Distinguish genuine simplification from accuracy loss. Inference-only.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether ordinary social cues can elicit capability-hiding — a cheap, accessible probe of sandbagging-adjacent behavior in existing models. Names: sycophancy and sandbagging/capability concealment.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy responds to perceived user expertise/social dynamics (model-written evals, arXiv:2212.09251); sandbagging is a recognized deceptive-capabilities concern in AI safety literature.
