# Homogenization in Synthetic Training Data: Does Model-Generated Data Narrow the Next Generation?

**Problem:** Verbalized Sampling and the Hivemind paper both flag synthetic-data contamination as a homogeneity driver. But the concrete claim — that using one model's synthetic outputs as data narrows the diversity available downstream — is rarely measured directly with simple diversity metrics on the data itself (no retraining needed).

**Direction:** Have several models generate synthetic datasets for the same task (e.g., paraphrases, QA pairs, instruction-response). Measure lexical/semantic diversity within each model's synthetic set and overlap across models. Compare to human-written reference data. Quantify how much diversity is lost relative to the human distribution — a data-level homogeneity audit.

**Why it matters:** As the web fills with synthetic text and labs train on it, an ecosystem-wide diversity collapse (model autophagy) is a medium-horizon systemic risk. Measuring it at the data level needs no training and no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Extends mechanisms identified in 'Artificial Hivemind' (arXiv:2510.22954) and 'Verbalized Sampling' (arXiv:2510.01171) by measuring diversity loss directly in model-generated synthetic data.
