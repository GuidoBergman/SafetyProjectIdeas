# Replicating refusal-as-affine-function on a model outside the original 13

**Problem:** Arditi et al. and follow-up work argue refusal is best modeled as an affine (not purely linear) function in some models. Whether the affine refinement is needed for a model not in the original studies is a precise, accessible replication.

**Direction:** Replicate both the linear refusal-vector and the affine-refusal formulation on a model outside the original sets (e.g., a recent Gemma-2 or Qwen2.5 instruct), comparing how well each predicts/controls refusal. Twist: test whether the affine correction matters on a fresh model with a different safety-tuning recipe.

**Why it matters:** The exact geometry of the refusal direction determines how easily it can be removed (jailbroken) or strengthened (hardened). Confirming whether refusal is linear or affine on new models is foundational for both attack and defense, and is fully reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 'Refusal in LLMs is mediated by a single direction'; 'Refusal in LLMs is an Affine Function' (arXiv:2411.09003).
