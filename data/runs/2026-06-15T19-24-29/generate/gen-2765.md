# Capability-Floor vs. Capability-Ceiling Reporting for Dangerous-Capability Evals

**Problem:** Dangerous-capability evals report a single number, conflating 'typical' and 'best-achievable' performance. Safety decisions should hinge on the ceiling (worst case for defenders), but the gap is rarely reported as a floor-ceiling band.

**Direction:** On a proxy dangerous-capability benchmark with an open model, report performance as a band: a floor (single bare prompt) and a ceiling (aggressive elicitation: best-of-N + CoT + few-shot). Define a standardized 'risk band' metric and argue which number safety thresholds should use. Show how two models with equal floors can have very different ceilings.

**Why it matters:** A reporting-standard contribution for cross-model risk comparison (NIST/CAISI need); reframes capability evals around the safety-relevant upper bound.

**Relevant context:** STREAM ChemBio standard (arXiv:2508.09853); METR elicitation-gap guidance; identical models show widely varying performance under different elicitation.
