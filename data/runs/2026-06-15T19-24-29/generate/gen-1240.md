# Feature Absorption Rate as a Function of SAE Width

**Problem:** Chanin et al. ('A is for Absorption') showed SAE latents that appear to classify a feature sometimes fail to fire while a token-aligned latent absorbs the activation. The relationship between SAE dictionary width (number of latents) and absorption severity is asserted but not systematically charted on a single model with a controlled probe task.

**Direction:** Using the public family of GPT-2-small SAEs at multiple widths available through SAELens/Neuronpedia, run the standard first-letter spelling probe task. For each width, compute the mean absorption fraction (a probe correctly classifies but the main SAE latent fails to fire). Plot absorption vs width and vs dead-feature fraction. This is purely inference + linear probing on pretrained SAEs — no SAE training needed.

**Why it matters:** SAEs are the leading interpretability primitive proposed for safety auditing, but feature absorption means a 'feature' you think you can monitor may silently route through another latent. Knowing whether wider dictionaries help or worsen absorption tells auditors which SAEs to trust — a counterfactually valuable methodological result needing no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Chanin et al. 2024 'A is for Absorption: Studying Feature Splitting and Absorption in Sparse Autoencoders'; SAEBench (Neuronpedia, 2025) defines Mean Absorption Fraction Score and Feature Splitting metrics. Pretrained GPT-2-small SAEs at varied widths exist publicly.
