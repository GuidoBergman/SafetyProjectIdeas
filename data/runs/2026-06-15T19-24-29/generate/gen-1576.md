# Backcasting multimodal safety steering: does a text-derived harm vector steer a vision-language model's image responses?

**Problem:** A success state extends concept steering to multimodal models so safety controls cover image-grounded harms, an explicit subfield extension goal. The open question: can a harm/refusal direction extracted from TEXT transfer to steer or monitor responses to harmful IMAGE inputs, or does the visual pathway need its own vector?

**Direction:** On a small open vision-language model (e.g. LLaVA-1.5-7B or Qwen2-VL-2B), extract a refusal concept vector from text contrastive pairs. Test whether applying it monitors/steers responses to image-based unsafe prompts. Compare against a vector extracted from image-grounded contrastive pairs to measure the modality gap.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal jailbreaks (harmful images) are a fast-growing, under-defended surface. Knowing whether text safety directions transfer to vision is directly actionable and is a flagged extension of the Science 2026 work, feasible with a small open VLM.

**Relevant context:** Subfield extension goal: vision/multimodal steering (Beaglehole et al.). SteerVLM (arXiv 2510.26769), VISOR++ (arXiv 2509.25533), HALP VLM hallucination (arXiv 2603.05465).
