# The Subspace Activation Patching Illusion: A Replication Probe

**Problem:** A documented interpretability illusion shows that patching a 1-dimensional subspace can strongly change predictions by activating a normally dormant pathway that is not part of the genuine circuit, leading researchers to attribute behavior to the wrong subspace. Beginners adopting subspace patching may not realize this.

**Direction:** On a small open model, construct a simple factual-recall or attribute task. Fit a probe direction for the target attribute and perform subspace activation patching along it, recording the effect on output probability. Then run the documented controls (does the same direction matter on clean inputs? does it activate a dormant pathway?) to detect whether the apparent effect is illusory. Report a checklist of which controls catch the illusion and how often a naively chosen subspace is illusory.

**Why it matters:** Subspace/steering-vector interventions are increasingly proposed as safety levers. If the chosen direction is an artifact, claimed 'control' over a behavior is fictitious. A clear demonstration plus a control checklist helps newcomers avoid fooling themselves.

**Relevant context:** Makelov et al., 'Is This the Subspace You Are Looking for? An Interpretability Illusion for Subspace Activation Patching' (arXiv:2311.17030) shows a 1-D subspace patch can move predictions by activating a dormant out-of-circuit pathway, and recommends mechanistic controls.
