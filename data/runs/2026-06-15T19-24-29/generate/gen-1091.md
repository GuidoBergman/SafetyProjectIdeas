# Scale and Convergence: Replicating 'Bigger Models Correlate More' Within One Family

**Problem:** Correlated Errors found larger, more accurate models have more correlated errors, even across providers — suggesting capability drives convergence. This was shown across heterogeneous models; a controlled within-family size sweep would isolate scale as the driver.

**Direction:** Replicate the error-correlation-vs-accuracy relationship using a single open family released at multiple sizes (e.g. Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B/14B, or Llama-3.2 1B/3B + Llama-3-8B). Measure pairwise error correlation on a benchmark as a function of size, testing whether bigger pairs converge more. Twist: also compare cross-family same-size pairs to separate 'scale' from 'shared lineage'.

**Why it matters:** If convergence is an intrinsic consequence of scaling, then homogeneity (and correlated failure) will *worsen* as the field advances — a long-horizon systemic-risk trajectory worth documenting cheaply. Pure inference on open checkpoints.

**Relevant context:** Controlled replication of the scale-correlation finding in Correlated Errors in LLMs (arXiv:2506.07962) using a single open model family's size ladder.
