# Secret-Collusion Steganography Replicated on Open Model Pairs

**Problem:** Secret Collusion among AI Agents demonstrated that LLM agent pairs can hide information from an overseer via steganography, and that as models improve the balance can shift toward undetected collusion. The headline experiments use frontier models; the steganographic-collusion capability of small open-weight model pairs is undocumented.

**Direction:** Replicate a minimal steganographic collusion game: two instances of an open-weight model must covertly pass a secret (e.g., a password) past a same-or-stronger overseer model while completing a cover task. Measure covert-channel success and overseer detection rate across model sizes, anchoring to the paper's qualitative scaling claim.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent collusion is a medium/long-horizon governance threat that labs deprioritize for near-term concerns. Mapping at what open-model scale covert collusion emerges is exactly a model-organism study doable with only inference and no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography' (arXiv 2402.07510): formalizes input/output collusion and steganographic channels; as models improve, undetected steganography can rise even against an equal-capability overseer. Mitigation: paraphraser/summariser to break channels.
