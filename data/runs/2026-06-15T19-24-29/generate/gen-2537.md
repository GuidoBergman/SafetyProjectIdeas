# Monitor Suspiciousness Calibration Replicated Across Decoding Temperatures

**Problem:** Control protocols calibrate the trusted monitor's suspiciousness threshold to a target FPR (e.g., 5%) on a validation set, but calibration is typically done at fixed decoding settings; whether the monitor's calibrated threshold and AUC are stable across temperatures is unstudied.

**Direction:** Replicate trusted-monitoring suspiciousness scoring on the APPS backdoor set, but vary the MONITOR's sampling temperature and the attacker's temperature. Re-derive the 5%-FPR threshold at each setting and measure how much the calibrated threshold and detection AUC drift. Twist: test whether a threshold calibrated at temperature 0 still holds at higher temperatures.

**Why it matters:** If calibration silently breaks under different decoding settings, deployed control protocols give a false sense of a fixed FPR. This is a cheap, fully reproducible robustness audit of a core control assumption.

**Relevant context:** Control protocols calibrate ensemble/monitor threshold to 5% FPR via ROC (arXiv 2605.15377, 2511.02997). Control Tax APPS balanced backdoor dataset (arXiv 2506.05296). Redwood top-2% audit (arXiv 2312.06942).
