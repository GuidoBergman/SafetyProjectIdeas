# From Refusal to Empty Compliance: Auditing the 'Convincingness' Link in Jailbreak Success

**Problem:** The misuse chain is: jailbreak -> model stops refusing -> output actually enables harm. StrongREJECT shows the middle link (stops refusing) is routinely conflated with the final link (enables harm), inflating reported attack success. The under-studied question: across popular jailbreaks, what fraction of 'successful' attacks produce non-refusals that are actually empty/useless?

**Direction:** Take a handful of well-known jailbreak templates applied to open models, then score outputs with the StrongREJECT autograder (refusal, specificity, convincingness). Decompose reported ASR into 'refused', 'complied-but-empty', and 'genuinely harmful' to measure how much of the harm chain each jailbreak actually completes.

**Why it matters:** Corrects systematic overestimation of attack danger by separating two causal links that benchmarks blur. Pure evaluation using a public autograder and open models, no training, directly improves how the community measures real-world deployment risk.

**Relevant context:** Souly et al. 'A StrongREJECT for Empty Jailbreaks' (arXiv:2402.10260) show existing benchmarks overestimate jailbreak effectiveness and score on refusal+specificity+convincingness; benign label = model misread a euphemism and answered harmlessly. Public repo at github.com/alexandrasouly/strongreject.
