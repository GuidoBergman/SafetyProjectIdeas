# Feature Absorption Blinds Unlearning Verification

**Problem:** Compound of two failure modes: (1) SAE feature absorption (a latent that 'should' track a concept silently fails to fire on some tokens) in mech interp, amplifying (2) incomplete unlearning in the unlearning subfield. If practitioners verify that knowledge was unlearned by checking that the corresponding SAE feature no longer fires, absorption means the feature was never reliable, so a 'clean' dashboard hides surviving knowledge.

**Direction:** Using a pre-trained Gemma/GPT-2 SAE from SAELens, pick a target concept (e.g. a named entity or 'first-letter' family known to absorb). Apply a cheap inference-time unlearning/steering edit. Measure whether the SAE feature stops firing AND whether the absorbed sibling latents still carry the concept (probe accuracy on held tokens). Quantify the gap between 'feature-based unlearning verification says removed' and 'linear probe says still present'.

**Why it matters:** Labs increasingly propose SAE features as audit instruments for forget-set removal. Showing that a documented interp artifact (absorption) systematically produces false 'successfully unlearned' verdicts is a counterfactually valuable, training-free warning that needs no proprietary infra.

**Relevant context:** Chanin et al. 'A is for Absorption' (arXiv:2409.14507) documents absorption: a latent appears to track a concept but fails to fire on tokens it should, with sibling latents absorbing the direction. SAE-subspace-guided unlearning (arXiv:2505.24428) and inference-time steering leave knowledge recoverable (arXiv:2505.24428, openreview MIlqM98o9I). SAELens provides pre-trained SAEs.
