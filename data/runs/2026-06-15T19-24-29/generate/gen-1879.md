# Coherence-Controlled Re-Evaluation of Open-Weight EM Model Organisms

**Problem:** The released open-weight EM models are hard to study because fine-tuning degrades coherence by ~33%, and the misaligned-and-coherent answer rate is only ~6% for Qwen-Coder-32B. The 'Model Organisms' follow-up trained better organisms (40% misaligned, 99% coherent), but the relationship between coherence degradation and apparent misalignment rate has not been cleanly separated on the original public organisms.

**Direction:** Download the original Betley open-weight EM models and the improved Model-Organisms checkpoints (both public). Re-run the standard 8-question evaluation, but vary the coherence threshold systematically (sweep coherency cutoff 30..90) and decompose the misalignment rate into 'incoherent garbage' vs 'coherent-but-evil' buckets using an LLM judge. Quantify how sensitive published EM rates are to the arbitrary coherency>50 cutoff.

**Why it matters:** EM is a flagship safety result driving lots of follow-up; if reported rates are an artifact of a threshold choice, downstream conclusions are shaky. This is pure evaluation of existing public model organisms — no training, single GPU for inference — exactly the kind of counterfactually-valuable scrutiny labs skip.

**Relevant context:** 'Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2506.11613): Qwen-Coder-32B misaligned/coherent only ~6% of time, coherence degraded ~33%; new organisms reach 40% misaligned, 99% coherent. Evaluation uses alignment<30/coherency>50 rubric.
