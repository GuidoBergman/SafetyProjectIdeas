# Quantization-Induced Alignment Drift: Replicating Safety Evals Across Bit-Widths

**Problem:** Consumer deployment of open models almost always uses quantization (8-bit, 4-bit), but it is underexplored whether quantization shifts a model's safety/alignment behavior relative to its full-precision baseline.

**Direction:** Take a safety-aligned small open model and run an identical refusal/safety eval and a sycophancy/TruthfulQA eval at FP16, 8-bit, and 4-bit quantization. Twist: replicate the model's published alignment evals across quantization levels to detect drift introduced purely by the deployment-time compression most outside users rely on.

**Why it matters:** Nearly all non-lab deployment uses quantized models, yet labs report safety numbers at full precision; any quantization-induced alignment drift is a diffuse, under-measured deployment risk that outside researchers are uniquely positioned to catch. The experiment is inference-only and trivially consumer-GPU feasible.

**Relevant context:** Safety/sycophancy/TruthfulQA evals are openly available (arXiv:2310.13548; TruthfulQA 817 questions). Quantization via bitsandbytes/GGUF is standard for consumer deployment, and small alignment changes from compression are plausible given known quantization perturbations to model behavior.
