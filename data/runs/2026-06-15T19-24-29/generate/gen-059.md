# Hazard curves for refusal: survival analysis of multi-turn jailbreak persistence across open models

**Problem:** Jailbreak benchmarks report a single attack-success rate, hiding the temporal structure of how a model's refusal erodes under sustained pressure. We cannot tell whether a model fails immediately, degrades gradually ('wear-out'), or holds firm then collapses.

**Direction:** Borrow survival analysis (time-to-event modeling) from biostatistics. Treat 'turns until first harmful compliance' as the survival outcome over a fixed multi-turn attack script (e.g., crescendo / persuasion escalation). Fit Kaplan-Meier survival curves and Cox proportional-hazards models per open model (Llama-3, Qwen, Mistral) using only API/local inference. Right-censor sessions where the model never complies. Compare hazard functions: is the hazard increasing (each turn weakens it) or flat?

**Why it matters:** Turns refusal robustness into a calibrated, interpretable risk metric that frontier labs do not publish. A rising hazard function is a direct, replicable safety signal usable by anyone deploying open models, requiring no training and no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Directly grounded in 'Time-To-Inconsistency: A Survival Analysis of LLM Robustness to Adversarial Attacks' (arXiv 2510.02712) and 'Quantifying LLM Safety Degradation Under Repeated Attacks Using Survival Analysis'. Source subfield: biostatistics/survival analysis. Target subfield: adversarial_robustness. Feasible in 30h with scripted multi-turn attacks on small open models.
