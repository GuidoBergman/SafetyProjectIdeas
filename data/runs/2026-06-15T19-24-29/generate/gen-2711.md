# Resampling/Best-of-N Capability x Monitor Threshold: Many Tries Defeat One Gate

**Problem:** An inference-time capability (best-of-N / resampling raises success rate) compounds with a fixed-threshold monitor (control): an attacker who can resample regenerates actions until one slips past the monitor, so a per-attempt detection rate that looks safe becomes unsafe under repeated attempts. The capability multiplies the monitor's residual miss rate.

**Direction:** Measure a monitor's per-attempt miss rate p on harmful actions from an open model, then empirically verify that the probability of at least one undetected success approaches 1 as N resamples grow (1-(1-p)^N). Test whether realistic resampling (varying prompts/temperature) actually achieves this and how many tries are needed.

**Why it matters:** Attackers in deployment can retry cheaply; a monitor evaluated on single attempts dramatically overstates safety. The compounding is quantifiable and gives a concrete 'how many tries to beat the monitor' number. Pure API/eval.

**Relevant context:** Control protocols evaluated on per-action detection (Greenblatt et al. arXiv:2312.06942); reward-hacking creativity grows with optimization power (reward-hacking literature); resampling is a standard cheap attack amplifier.
