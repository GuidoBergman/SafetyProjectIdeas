# Self-Recognition Without Fine-Tuning: Can Open Models Identify Their Own Outputs for Collusion?

**Problem:** Untrusted-monitoring safety case sketches (arXiv:2602.20628) list 'passive self-recognition' as a collusion strategy: a misaligned model recognizes its own outputs and rates them as safe. How reliably current open models can self-recognize — the enabler of this collusion mode — is poorly characterized.

**Direction:** Generate outputs from several open models on a shared task set, then in a separate session ask each model to judge, for pairs/sets of candidate completions, which it likely authored. Measure self-recognition accuracy above chance per model, and test whether prompting it to 'be lenient toward your own work' produces a measurable collusion bias in a monitoring task. No training; pure prompting.

**Why it matters:** Passive self-recognition is the simplest collusion attack on untrusted monitoring. Quantifying it on open models tells control researchers how strong the threat is and whether self-recognition can be exploited or defended cheaply, outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** 'When can we trust untrusted monitoring? A safety case sketch across collusion strategies' (arXiv:2602.20628, Feb 2026): taxonomy of passive self-recognition, causal collusion, acausal collusion. Related to 'Towards evaluations-based safety cases for AI scheming' (arXiv:2411.03336).
