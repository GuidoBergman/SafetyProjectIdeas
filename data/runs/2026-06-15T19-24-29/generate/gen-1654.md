# Replicating Attention-Head Ablation Robustness Across Random Seeds of the Same Architecture

**Problem:** Circuit findings (e.g., specific heads being necessary for a task) are usually established on a single released model. Given the SAE seed-dependence results, it is unclear whether the SAME task circuit (e.g., which heads are necessary for a simple task) is found when the identical small architecture is trained from a different random seed — i.e., is a discovered circuit a property of the architecture+task or of the specific run?

**Direction:** Train two small transformers (same architecture, different seeds) on a simple, fast-to-learn task (e.g., a bracket-matching or simple IOI-like task; training fits in a few hours on one GPU). Replicate a head-ablation / activation-patching circuit analysis on each. The twist: compare whether the identified necessary heads/components occupy analogous positions across the two seeds, quantifying circuit reproducibility.

**Why it matters:** If circuits are seed-idiosyncratic, then circuit-level safety claims about a specific model may not generalize even to a re-trained copy. Establishing how reproducible discovered circuits are — a clean, fully-open, single-GPU methodological study — is foundational for trusting interpretability as a safety tool, and complements known SAE seed-instability findings.

**Relevant context:** 'SAEs Trained on the Same Data Learn Different Features' (arXiv:2501.16615) showed seed-dependence of SAE features. Wang et al. IOI circuit and TransformerLens activation patching provide the circuit-discovery methodology. 'Towards Universality' (arXiv:2410.06672) studies mechanistic similarity.
