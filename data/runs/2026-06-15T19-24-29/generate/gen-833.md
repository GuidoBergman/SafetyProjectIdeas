# Distillation as a Threshold Bypass: Can a Tiny Student Inherit Regulated Capability From a Big Teacher?

**Problem:** Model reuse via distillation is named as a compute-threshold loophole: a small, sub-threshold student model can inherit much of a large teacher's capability, escaping systemic-risk obligations. The boundary — how much regulated capability transfers cheaply — is rarely demonstrated concretely for governance readers.

**Direction:** Probe the distillation-transfer boundary on a feasible slice. Using an existing open small model and a larger open teacher (or public teacher-generated outputs), do a lightweight distillation-style fine-tune on a single GPU for one narrow capability, and measure how much of the teacher's performance the tiny student captures per unit of (sub-threshold) compute. Frame results as 'effective regulated capability acquired below the threshold'. (Scope: small models only, short runs, no from-scratch training.)

**Why it matters:** It concretely shows regulators that training-compute thresholds can be sidestepped by reuse, supporting the case for capability- or behavior-based triggers. Uses only small open models and short single-GPU fine-tunes — within beginner reach and fully counterfactual to lab work. Marked lower-confidence due to the light fine-tuning component.

**Relevant context:** Compute-threshold loophole taxonomy (fine-tuning, distillation, model expansion, inference scaling) in arXiv 2502.00003; DeepSeek-R1 efficiency result; EU/US thresholds.
