# Paraphrasing Defense Against Encoded Reasoning: How Much Capability Is Lost?

**Problem:** Roger & Greenblatt showed paraphrasing can strip encoded reasoning (steganography) to <3 bits/KB, but the trade-off — how much TASK performance legitimate CoT loses when paraphrased — is under-quantified for accessible setups, and whether a model can route around a paraphraser on simple tasks is testable cheaply.

**Direction:** Take tasks where CoT helps (arithmetic word problems, simple multi-hop QA). Insert a paraphrasing step between the model's CoT and its answer (paraphrase with a second small model), and measure accuracy with vs without paraphrasing. Separately, set up a toy encoded-reasoning channel (model hides a digit in word choice) and test how much paraphrasing degrades the hidden channel. Success = paired curves of legitimate-CoT cost and hidden-channel destruction under paraphrasing.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is a deployable, training-free anti-steganography defense usable outside labs. Quantifying its capability cost vs hidden-bit suppression tells defenders whether it is practical. Encoded reasoning in CoT is a starred open problem.

**Relevant context:** Roger & Greenblatt 'Preventing Language Models from hiding their reasoning' (arXiv:2310.18512): paraphrasing limits encoding to <3 bits/KB without quality loss on some tasks, but optimized stego can resist. Open Phil RFP starred 'encoded reasoning in CoT.'
