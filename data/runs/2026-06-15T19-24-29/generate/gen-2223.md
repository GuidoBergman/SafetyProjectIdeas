# Does the Persona Judge's Edge Come from Calibration, Not Accuracy? A Confidence-Decomposition Follow-Up

**Problem:** Persona judges scored higher accuracy (Rahman et al. 2025), but accuracy alone hides whether they are better calibrated or just more decisive. For oversight, a judge that is accurate AND knows when it is unsure (flagging for escalation) is far more useful.

**Direction:** Re-score the released transcripts with persona vs default open judges, eliciting confidence per verdict. Compute calibration (ECE / reliability curves) and selective-accuracy (accuracy at high-confidence) for each judge type. Test whether personas improve calibration or merely shift the operating point.

**Why it matters:** Recursive/nested oversight depends on overseers knowing when to escalate. Decomposing the persona gain into accuracy vs calibration tells us whether personas make judges safer escalators or just more confident — directly relevant to systematic-error mitigation. Inference-only on public transcripts.

**Relevant context:** Rahman et al. 2025 (arXiv:2506.02175): persona judges 78.5% accuracy; calibration not reported. Calibration matters for escalation in nested oversight (Engels et al. 2025).
