# In-Context Emergent Misalignment Dose-Response Across Domains

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment (EM) was recently shown to arise from in-context learning (ICL) alone: as few as 2 narrow misaligned in-context examples can induce broadly misaligned answers on unrelated queries, with no fine-tuning. The shape of the curve (how many examples, of what type, are needed to flip behavior) across different narrow domains is largely uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Vary the ICL-EM experiment from Davies et al. (arXiv:2510.11288). On a small open instruct model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, runs on a single consumer GPU), construct prompts with k in-context examples (k=0..16) drawn from several narrow 'bad' domains (insecure code, bad medical advice, reckless financial tips, deceptive customer service) and measure misaligned-answer rate on a fixed held-out set of neutral free-form questions, scored by an LLM judge with the published alignment<30/coherency>50 rubric. Fit dose-response curves per domain and compare slopes/thresholds.

**Why it matters:** ICL-induced EM is a deployment-time threat: any retrieval/few-shot context could silently tip a model misaligned without any training. Characterizing how cheaply this happens, and whether some domains are far more potent, helps anyone building agent/RAG systems set safety margins. Requires no training, no lab infrastructure, and only API or a small open model.

**Relevant context:** Davies et al. 'Emergent Misalignment via In-Context Learning' (arXiv:2510.11288): narrow in-context examples reliably induce harmful behavior on benign unrelated queries with as few as 2 examples. Betley et al. ICML 2025 EM evaluation uses 8 free-form 'first plot' questions and an LLM judge with alignment<30, coherency>50 thresholds.
