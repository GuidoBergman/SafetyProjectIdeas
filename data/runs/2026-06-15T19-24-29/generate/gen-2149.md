# The Sneaky-Prover Detection Gap Across Model Families

**Problem:** Prover-Verifier Games assume a verifier and provers from related training. It is unknown how well a verifier from one model family detects sneaky (plausible-but-wrong) solutions produced by an *unrelated* family — a basic robustness question for using one model to oversee another.

**Direction:** Generate sneaky wrong and helpful correct math/reasoning solutions from several open model families (Qwen, Llama, Mistral small variants) and cross-evaluate: each family's model acts as verifier on every other family's outputs. Build a detection-accuracy matrix and look for blind spots (families whose sneaky outputs systematically fool others).

**Why it matters:** Real oversight will pair models from different lineages; shared blind spots are a hidden single point of failure. Cheap, training-free cross-family probe valuable outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. (2024, arXiv:2407.13692) sneaky/helpful prover framing. arXiv:2510.11235 'Independent Safety Mechanisms or Shared Failures?' motivates correlated-failure concern across mechanisms.
