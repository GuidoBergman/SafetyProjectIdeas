# Re-deriving Steering Vectors Across Quantization Levels

**Problem:** Activation-addition steering vectors (e.g., refusal, sentiment, sycophancy directions) are extracted from full-precision activations. Hobbyists almost always run quantized models (4-bit, 8-bit) on consumer GPUs. Whether steering vectors extracted at full precision still steer effectively when applied to the quantized version of the same model — and whether vectors extracted from the quantized model differ — is largely untested.

**Direction:** Pick one well-studied steering direction (e.g., the refusal or a sentiment direction) on a small open model. Replicate the difference-of-means extraction and steering-effect measurement at full precision (baseline). The twist: apply the same vector to the 4-bit and 8-bit quantized model, and separately extract vectors from the quantized activations, comparing steering efficacy and direction cosine-similarity across precisions.

**Why it matters:** Almost all real-world local deployment of open models is quantized, yet interpretability and steering research is done at full precision. Checking whether mechanistic interventions survive quantization — a cheap single-GPU study — tells us whether safety tooling (and conversely, jailbreak steering) transfers to the models people actually run, a deployment-reality gap labs ignore.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. (arXiv:2406.11717) and the broader representation-engineering literature (arXiv:2502.17601) extract steering directions via difference-of-means at full precision. TransformerLens supports activation extraction; bitsandbytes enables 4/8-bit quantized inference on consumer GPUs.
