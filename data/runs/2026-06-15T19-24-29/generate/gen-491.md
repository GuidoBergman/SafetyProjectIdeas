# Mapping where the truth probe breaks: a phase diagram of ELK reporter failure on quirky models

**Problem:** ELK's builder-breaker game predicts that a linear 'truth reporter' probe should fail in specific regimes, but the boundary conditions are usually argued informally. The 'quirky language models' (finetuned to err only when 'Bob' appears) provide a concrete model organism where ground-truth latent knowledge is known, yet nobody has charted *where* probe recovery degrades as a function of layer, prompt distance, and quirk strength.

**Direction:** Use EleutherAI's released quirky models and ELK probing code. Train a simple logistic-regression probe on middle-layer activations to recover the model's latent answer, then sweep three axes: layer index, semantic distance of the question from training distribution, and presence/absence of the 'Bob' trigger. Plot a 2D heatmap (a 'phase diagram') of probe AUROC, identifying the cliff where the probe stops tracking latent knowledge. Characterize the failure as a boundary condition rather than a single number.

**Why it matters:** ELK is a flagship formal-theoretical open problem (ARC). Turning its abstract builder-breaker arguments into an empirical map of *when* simple reporters fail gives the theory testable structure and tells safety researchers which deployment regimes a cheap probe can be trusted in. Requires no training from scratch and no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Eliciting Latent Knowledge from Quirky Language Models' (arXiv:2312.01037), which finds linear probes recover ~89% of the truthful/untruthful AUROC gap; code and 12 datasets are public via EleutherAI's ELK project.
