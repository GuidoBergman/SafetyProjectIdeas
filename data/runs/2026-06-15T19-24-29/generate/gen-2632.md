# Operations-Research Queueing Model for Human-Audit Throughput Under Load

**Problem:** Human-in-the-loop audit is a bottleneck: if monitors flag faster than humans can review, a queue builds and either dangerous actions execute unreviewed or the system stalls. Control designs rarely model this throughput constraint.

**Direction:** Import queueing theory (M/M/c, priority queues) from operations research. Model flagged actions as arrivals into a finite human-reviewer queue, parameterize arrival rate from measured monitor flag rates and service rate from realistic review times, and compute the probability that an action executes before review at various staffing levels and flag thresholds. Identify the threshold that keeps unreviewed-execution probability below a target.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the human-oversight bottleneck and yields concrete staffing/threshold guidance, a practical operational constraint usually ignored in control proposals. Pure modeling/simulation, no compute, directly informs deployable human-in-the-loop design.

**Relevant context:** Queueing theory (Erlang, M/M/c, priority queues) models service bottlenecks in OR. Control protocols rely on limited human-audit budgets but model them as a fixed fraction, not a throughput-constrained queue (Ctrl-Z arXiv:2504.10374; arXiv:2511.02997).
