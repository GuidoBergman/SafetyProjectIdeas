# The If-Then Gap: Auditing the Trigger-to-Action Link in Frontier Safety Frameworks

**Problem:** The causal chain from 'dangerous capability emerges' -> 'lab detects it' -> 'lab triggers a commitment' -> 'lab takes mitigating action' breaks at the trigger-to-action link: frameworks like Anthropic's RSP, OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, and DeepMind's FSF define capability thresholds but are often vague about the concrete 'then' action. METR and others note persistent 'if-then' vagueness.

**Direction:** Hand-code every published frontier safety framework (RSP, Preparedness, FSF, plus EU CoP signatories) into a structured spreadsheet, labeling each 'if' (threshold) with whether its paired 'then' is concrete (specific action + actor + deadline) or vague. Score each lab on trigger-to-action specificity and quantify where in the chain commitments dissolve into ambiguity. Publish a comparative chart.

**Why it matters:** If the trigger-to-action link is unenforceable, the entire detection-and-evaluation apparatus upstream produces no safety benefit. Surfacing exactly which thresholds lack concrete actions tells regulators where binding rules must replace voluntary vagueness. Pure document analysis, no compute, high counterfactual value outside labs.

**Relevant context:** METR 'Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies'; arXiv:2409.08751 grading rubric for AI safety frameworks; Anthropic RSP v2.1; OpenAI Preparedness Framework; arXiv:2504.15181 mapping industry practices to EU CoP.
