# Backcasting probe-guided refusal vs flat refusal: does steering give finer control than a blanket refusal switch?

**Problem:** A nuanced success state is models that refuse genuinely harmful requests while still answering benign edge cases (e.g. medical/security education). A blunt refusal switch over-refuses. Whether concept steering enables graded, context-sensitive refusal better than a threshold on a refusal probe is untested head-to-head.

**Direction:** On a small open model, compare two interventions on a benign-but-sensitive prompt set (dual-use questions): (a) hard refusal triggered when a refusal probe exceeds a threshold, vs (b) graded steering magnitude proportional to probe score. Measure harmful-prompt refusal AND benign-sensitive over-refusal for each, finding which gives a better tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal erodes usefulness and trust, a real diffuse problem. Showing whether graded steering beats binary refusal is a concrete, fresh comparison with clear safety relevance, all on open weights.

**Relevant context:** Refusal Steering fine-grained control (arXiv 2512.16602); Refusal as affine function (arXiv 2411.09003). Specificity tradeoffs (arXiv 2602.06256).
