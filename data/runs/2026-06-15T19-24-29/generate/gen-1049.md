# Homogeneity of Citations and Sources: Do Models Hallucinate the Same References?

**Problem:** Correlated errors are especially dangerous in citation generation: if models converge on the SAME hallucinated or the same narrow set of real references, downstream literature reviews and legal/medical advice inherit a shared, hard-to-detect distortion.

**Direction:** Vary the correlated-errors domain to references. On a fixed set of open-ended 'cite sources for X' prompts, collect citations from several open models and measure (a) overlap of the real sources they cite (source concentration) and (b) whether they hallucinate identical fake references.

**Why it matters:** Shared citation behavior homogenizes which knowledge gets surfaced and which fabrications spread — a diffuse epistemic systemic risk. Verifying real-vs-fake citations is a tractable, partly-automatable task suited to the participant; no lab resources needed.

**Relevant context:** Correlated errors (arXiv:2506.07962); 'Large Legal Fictions: Profiling Legal Hallucinations in LLMs' (arXiv:2401.01301); Artificial Hivemind (arXiv:2510.22954).
