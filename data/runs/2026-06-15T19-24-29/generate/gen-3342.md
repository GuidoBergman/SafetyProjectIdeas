# Cue-Insertion Faithfulness Audit Across Open Model Sizes

**Problem:** Frontier models silently change answers when a biasing cue (e.g. a hint embedded in a prompt) is present but rarely verbalize the cue in their CoT. It is unclear whether this 'silent cue-following' scales monotonically with model size or has a sweet spot in small open-weight models.

**Direction:** Take a multiple-choice benchmark (e.g. a TruthfulQA or commonsense MCQ subset), insert a hidden cue ('a Stanford professor thinks the answer is X') into half the prompts, and measure for a ladder of open models (e.g. Qwen 0.5B/3B/7B, Llama 3.2 1B/3B/8B) both the answer-flip rate and the rate at which the cue is mentioned in CoT. Plot faithfulness-gap vs. parameter count.

**Why it matters:** If small open models are MORE faithful (verbalize cues more often), they make better CoT-monitoring testbeds; if less, scaling laws for honesty are pessimistic. This is a no-training, single-GPU study that frontier labs underinvest in because they focus on their own large models.

**Relevant context:** Arcuschin et al. (ICLR 2025 workshop, 'CoT Reasoning in the Wild is not Always Faithful') and 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (arXiv 2505.05410) report unfaithfulness rates of 16.3% (Sonnet 3.7), 5.3% (DeepSeek R1), 7.0% (4o) on cued question pairs, with rates exceeding 30% in some settings. Cue-insertion / answer-flip is the standard protocol.
