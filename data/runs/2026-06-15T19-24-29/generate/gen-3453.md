# Hint-Reliance Verbalization Rate Benchmark Extension to Open Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Anthropic's hint-injection methodology (paired prompts, one with an embedded hint; check whether the CoT verbalizes reliance on the hint when the answer flips) showed verbalization rates of only ~20-28% on closed models. No public benchmark applies this exact protocol systematically across freely available open reasoning models with a documented hint taxonomy.

**Direction:** Re-implement the paired-prompt hint protocol over a battery of hint types (sycophantic 'I think the answer is X', metadata leaks, visual-style cues, grader hints) on 3-4 open reasoning models. Compute, per hint type and model, the rate at which the model uses the hint (answer flips) but does NOT verbalize it. Release the prompt set and per-hint verbalization rates as an extensible benchmark.

**Why it matters:** Unverbalized hint use is direct evidence that CoT hides decision-relevant factors. Quantifying it on open models gives the external community a reproducible faithfulness yardstick that frontier labs only report sporadically on their own models.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic's 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (arXiv 2505.05410) reports reward hacks verbalized in <2% of cases despite 99% use; RL improved verbalization to a 20-28% plateau. The hint-injection protocol is described but no open-model public benchmark exists.
