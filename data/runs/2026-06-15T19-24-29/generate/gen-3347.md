# Third-Person Reframing as a Deployable Sycophancy Firewall

**Problem:** Sycophancy under multi-turn pressure is a robust failure, and prior work found adopting a third-person perspective cuts debate sycophancy by up to 63.8% — but this was a one-off finding, not validated as a general, drop-in prompt wrapper across scenarios and model families.

**Direction:** Implement a lightweight 'reframing firewall' that rewrites a user's pressuring turn into third-person ('A user argues X; evaluate the claim') before the model answers. Evaluate flip-rate (Turn-of-Flip, Number-of-Flip) with and without the wrapper across SYCON-Bench's three scenarios (debate, unethical queries, false presuppositions) on several open models.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether a free, training-free inference-time intervention generalizes as a sycophancy defense — a reusable safety harness component that anyone can deploy without lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** SYCON-Bench (Findings of EMNLP 2025, GitHub JiseungHong/SYCON-Bench) measures Turn-of-Flip and Number-of-Flip in multi-turn free-form settings across debate, unethical queries, false presuppositions; reports 'adopting a third-person perspective reduces sycophancy by up to 63.8% in the debate setting'.
