# Many-Shot Jailbreaking Scaling Laws on Open Long-Context Models

**Problem:** Anthropic's many-shot jailbreaking found power-law scaling of attack success with number of in-context harmful demonstrations on frontier models, but the public study did not characterize the scaling exponent on open long-context models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-128k, Llama-3.1-8B-128k) that a non-lab researcher can fully instrument.

**Direction:** Replicate the many-shot attack on 2-3 open long-context instruct models, sweeping shot count (1, 2, 4, ..., 256) and fitting the ASR power law. Twist: compare scaling exponents across open families and test whether the exponent correlates with reported context-length training. Use a small fixed harmful-QA demonstration bank scored by an open judge.

**Why it matters:** Confirms whether the many-shot scaling law is a universal context-window property or model-specific. Power-law fits are cheap to compute, the attack needs no optimization or training, and the result informs context-length safety mitigations that labs deprioritize for open models.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic many-shot jailbreaking (2024): effectiveness follows a power law up to hundreds of shots, more potent against larger models, exploits in-context learning. Open 128k-context instruct models are on HuggingFace.
