# Verbosity Bias in Oversight: Can Longer Wrong Answers Beat Shorter Right Ones?

**Problem:** LLM-judge verbosity bias is documented but mostly on preference quality, not on correctness oversight. The open question: in a correctness-checking setting, does answer length let a wrong answer slip past an overseer, and by how much?

**Direction:** Take a QA set with verifiable answers. For each item, produce a short correct answer and a long, elaborated wrong answer (and vice versa) via an open model. Have an open judge model decide correctness. Measure the length-induced error rate and whether a 'normalize length' instruction or truncation removes it. Report a dose-response curve of error vs length ratio.

**Why it matters:** Verbosity is one of the named reward-hacking signatures of evaluator manipulation. Quantifying how much length alone fools a correctness overseer gives a concrete, cheap robustness metric for any oversight harness. No training, public data.

**Relevant context:** LLM-as-judge bias surveys (arXiv:2411.16594, arXiv:2410.02736) document verbosity/length bias; Wen et al. 2024 show RLHF models become better at convincing humans they are right when wrong.
