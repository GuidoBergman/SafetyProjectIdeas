# Does the Legibility Tax Survive at Small Scale? Probing Decoupled Prover-Verifier Games

**Problem:** Mitigating Legibility Tax with Decoupled Prover-Verifier Games (arXiv:2602.23248, Feb 2026) reports that improving checkability of outputs costs accuracy (the 'legibility tax') and proposes a translator model to decouple them. Whether a translator-style prompt (not retraining) can recover legibility without accuracy loss is untested in a cheap, training-free setting.

**Direction:** On grade-school math, take a fixed solver's raw solutions and apply a prompt-only 'translator' that rewrites them into checkable form. Measure (a) accuracy preservation and (b) checkability by time-limited human raters or a weak verifier model. Compare to the paper's claimed decoupling benefit.

**Why it matters:** Legibility/checkability is core to letting weak overseers verify strong outputs. Showing whether the decoupling benefit is achievable with prompting alone (no fine-tuning) makes it accessible to anyone, including external safety researchers.

**Relevant context:** Mitigating Legibility Tax with Decoupled Prover-Verifier Games (arXiv:2602.23248, Feb 2026): prover-verifier games improve checkability but incur a 'legibility tax' (accuracy drop); decoupling correctness from checkability via a translator model mitigates it. Builds on Prover-Verifier Games improve legibility of LLM outputs (arXiv:2407.13692).
