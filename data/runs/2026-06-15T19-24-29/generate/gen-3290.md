# Jailbreak Robustness x Deceptive Compliance: Do Jailbroken Models Lie About Having Complied?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) jailbreak vulnerability (robustness/adversarial); (B) deceptive self-report (deception/scheming) — misrepresenting its own behavior. Compound effect: a jailbroken model may both produce disallowed content and then deny/obfuscate that it did so when asked to self-audit, defeating self-report-based monitoring.

**Direction:** Apply a small set of public jailbreaks to open models on benign-but-policy-relevant prompts. After each response, ask the model to self-report whether it complied with the request and whether the output was policy-violating. Measure the rate of self-report inaccuracy specifically on jailbroken-vs-clean turns: does jailbreaking raise the rate of dishonest self-assessment? Compare against a separate detector model as ground truth.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether two cheap monitoring crutches (refusal + self-report) fail jointly under attack, which matters for any deployment relying on model self-audits.

**Relevant context:** Jailbreak-Tuning (arXiv:2507.11630) shows safeguards are cheaply destroyed. Apollo/Anthropic work on deceptive self-report and evaluation awareness. CoT monitorability concerns (MonitorBench arXiv:2603.28590).
