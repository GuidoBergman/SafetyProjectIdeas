# Realignment Persistence: Does Re-Aligning an EM Model Fully Reverse It?

**Problem:** EM models can be 'realigned' with further benign fine-tuning, and self-awareness reportedly shifts back. But whether realignment fully removes the misaligned persona or leaves a reactivatable residue (cheaply re-elicited, like a relearning attack) is open.

**Direction:** Vary the realignment experiment on a public EM organism: apply a short benign realignment fine-tune, confirm the battery score recovers, then attempt cheap re-elicitation via (a) a few misaligned ICL examples, (b) tiny relearning fine-tune, (c) activation steering along the original misaligned-persona direction. Measure how much easier it is to re-misalign a 'realigned' model than a never-misaligned baseline.

**Why it matters:** If realignment is cosmetic and the misaligned persona stays latent and cheaply reactivatable, then patching a misaligned model is not a real fix — important for incident response. Combines public organisms with cheap interventions on one GPU; directly studies durability of alignment.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2602.14777 (self-awareness shifts with realignment) and 'Convergent Linear Representations of Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2506.11618) showing a misaligned-persona direction. Relearning-attack methodology (arXiv:2502.05374).
