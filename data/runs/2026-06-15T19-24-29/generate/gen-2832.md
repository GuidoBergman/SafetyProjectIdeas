# Memorization-vs-Generalization Gap Replicated via Paraphrased Safety Benchmark Items

**Problem:** Post-hoc contamination detection compares performance on original vs paraphrased items. This has been applied to capability benchmarks; whether safety benchmarks show an original-vs-paraphrase gap (suggesting models memorized the safe response rather than generalizing safe behavior) is under-studied.

**Direction:** Take an existing safety benchmark, paraphrase items while preserving harmful intent, and compare refusal/safe-response rates on original vs paraphrased versions across open models. A large drop on paraphrases indicates brittle, possibly memorized safety. The twist applies the contamination-detection paraphrase methodology to safety rather than capability evals.

**Why it matters:** If safety scores rest on memorized benchmark items, deployers overestimate real-world robustness. This reframes contamination detection as a safety-robustness probe — counterfactually valuable, no training, only inference on open models.

**Relevant context:** Contamination surveys (arXiv 2502.17521, 2502.14425) describe preference-for-original-over-paraphrase as a post-hoc memorization signal. Refusal-direction work (arXiv 2406.11717) documents brittleness of safety fine-tuning.
