# Refusal-Direction Drift Across Conversation Turns

**Problem:** Refusal is mediated by a single activation direction in single-turn settings, but whether that direction stays stable, drifts, or weakens over multi-turn conversations — the regime where many real jailbreaks succeed — is unexplored.

**Direction:** Extract the refusal direction on an open model at turn 1, then track the projection of harmful-request activations onto that direction across a multi-turn escalation dialogue. Test whether the refusal signal erodes turn-by-turn, explaining multi-turn jailbreak success without any optimization attack.

**Why it matters:** Multi-turn attacks now dominate (single-turn translation attacks have become ineffective). Mechanistically explaining multi-turn erosion via refusal-direction drift connects interpretability to a real threat and is doable with inference-only activation extraction on one consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Refusal Is Mediated by a Single Direction (Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024) for single-turn. Multilingual jailbreaking work (arXiv:2605.18239) reports single-turn translation attacks are now mostly ineffective while multi-turn reaches 52-83% success. Refusal-direction behavior across turns is unstudied.
