# Perplexity-Filter Defense vs Readable vs Gibberish Suffixes

**Problem:** Perplexity filtering is a standard cheap defense against GCG-style gibberish suffixes, but newer attacks produce more fluent/readable adversarial prompts. The defense's selectivity across the gibberish-to-fluent spectrum has not been mapped as a single controlled variation.

**Direction:** Assemble a graded set of adversarial prompts spanning pure-GCG gibberish suffixes, AmpleGCG outputs, and fluent template jailbreaks. For each, compute prompt perplexity under a small open LM and measure the ROC of a perplexity threshold at separating attacks from benign prompts, and the residual attack success of prompts that pass the filter. Vary the threshold and quantify the helpfulness cost (benign prompts wrongly blocked).

**Why it matters:** Quantifies precisely where the cheapest, most-deployed defense breaks — fluent attacks — and the over-refusal tax it imposes. Pure analysis on open data; directly informs lightweight deployment defenses small teams can run.

**Relevant context:** Context file lists 'Perplexity Filter Evasion Rate' as already covered; this variation differs by treating fluency as a controlled continuous variable across attack types and measuring the helpfulness/benign-blocking tradeoff jointly. Grounded in GCG (Zou et al.) and AmpleGCG (arXiv:2404.07921) which explicitly produces more transferable, less anomalous suffixes.
