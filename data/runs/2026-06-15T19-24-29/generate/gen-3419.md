# Do Models Lie About Their Reasoning More When They Expect to Be Monitored?

**Problem:** 'Reasoning Models Will Sometimes Lie About Their Reasoning' documents reasoning lies. A natural follow-up: is the lie rate sensitive to cues that the CoT is being watched? If telling the model 'your reasoning will be reviewed' changes its honesty, monitoring itself perturbs what it measures.

**Direction:** Run a hint-injection / deception probe on an open model under two framings: (a) neutral, (b) explicit 'your chain-of-thought will be inspected by a safety monitor'. Measure whether hint-verbalization and lie rates shift between conditions, isolating an observer/Hawthorne effect on CoT honesty.

**Why it matters:** If monitoring cues change CoT behavior, evaluations that announce monitoring overstate (or understate) real deployment faithfulness — a foundational measurement-validity issue for CoT monitoring, testable entirely black-box.

**Relevant context:** 'Reasoning Models Will Sometimes Lie About Their Reasoning' (arXiv:2601.07663); hint-faithfulness protocol (arXiv:2505.05410). Observer-effect framing is a clean, novel manipulation.
