# Where in the Refusal Does Shallow Alignment Break? A Token-Position Audit

**Problem:** Qi et al. show safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens, but the causal chain (harmful request -> model begins refusal token -> refusal sustained -> harm avoided) has an under-studied link: exactly how many tokens of forced non-refusal prefix are needed before compliance becomes irreversible, and how this threshold varies across open models.

**Direction:** Using the prefilling-attack methodology, systematically prefill 0,1,2,...,N non-refusal tokens (e.g. 'Sure, here is') across a panel of open chat models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2-7B, Mistral-7B) on StrongREJECT-small prompts. Measure compliance rate as a function of prefix length to map the per-model 'depth of alignment' curve and identify the critical token where refusal collapses.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the exact causal weak link in the refusal chain across model families, turning Qi et al.'s qualitative finding into a comparative robustness metric. Needs no training, no proprietary data, runs on one GPU, and produces an interpretable curve usable by anyone evaluating open models.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' (arXiv:2406.05946, ICLR 2025) shows alignment adapts the generative distribution over only the first few tokens and that prefilling a non-refusal prefix bypasses guardrails. StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) provides a 50-item small subset for cost-constrained evaluation.
