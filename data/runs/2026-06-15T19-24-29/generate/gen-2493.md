# Anomaly Detection via Activation Distance From a 'Normal Behavior' Baseline

**Problem:** Open Problems lists anomaly detection for novel dangerous behaviors. Classic OOD methods (distance from a baseline distribution in latent space) are well known in RL/driving but have barely been applied to LLM-agent trajectories for control. A simple, training-free instantiation is missing.

**Direction:** Collect activations from an open model over a corpus of benign agent trajectories to build a 'normal' distribution (e.g. via per-layer mean + Mahalanobis distance or a KDE). Then score known malign SHADE-Arena trajectories by their activation distance from normal. Measure whether simple distance-based OOD scoring separates malign from benign without any labeled deception data.

**Why it matters:** An unsupervised, label-free anomaly detector would catch *novel* misbehavior that supervised probes miss — exactly the open problem. Borrows a mature OOD methodology into the control setting; runs on one GPU with no training.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems: anomaly detection for novel dangerous behaviors (Anthropic recommended directions, ARC). OOD methods: Mahalanobis/KDE latent-distance and PNN-based OOD monitoring (e.g. arXiv 2412.02875, arXiv 2110.07922). SHADE-Arena (arXiv 2506.15740) for malign trajectories.
