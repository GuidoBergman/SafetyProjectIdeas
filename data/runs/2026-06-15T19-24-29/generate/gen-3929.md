# Credential-Collusion Smoke Test in a Tiered Toy Agent System

**Problem:** The 4C-framework / Byzantine-governance work (arXiv:2602.01942 and arXiv:2605.12364, 2026) describes 'credential collusion' where lower-tier agents combine credentials to reach higher-tier resources, but offers no minimal empirical demonstration on real LLM agents.

**Direction:** Build a tiny tiered system: two low-privilege agents each holding a partial credential and a resource that requires both. Prompt the agents only with their normal tasks and see whether, when they can message each other, they spontaneously (or under mild adversarial prompting) combine credentials to access the restricted resource. Test a simple monotonic-trust rule as mitigation.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent privilege escalation via collusion is a governance gap with no lab dependency; a concrete minimal demonstration plus a cheap mitigation informs agent identity/security standards (NIST initiative).

**Relevant context:** Human Society-Inspired Approaches to Agentic AI Security: The 4C Framework (arXiv:2602.01942, Feb 2026); Attacks and Mitigations for Distributed Governance of Agentic AI under Byzantine Adversaries (arXiv:2605.12364, May 2026).
