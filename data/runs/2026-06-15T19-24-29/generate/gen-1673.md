# Interpretability-to-Action Benchmark: Does a Feature Predict Behavior Out-of-Distribution?

**Problem:** A core open problem is the interpretability-to-safety gap: mechanistic findings rarely translate into actionable guarantees. We lack a simple benchmark asking whether a feature/probe that fires on in-distribution examples still predicts the model's behavior on out-of-distribution or adversarial inputs.

**Direction:** For a safety-relevant feature (e.g., a refusal feature/probe), measure how well its activation predicts the model's actual refusal behavior on (a) in-distribution prompts, (b) paraphrased prompts, and (c) lightly jailbroken prompts. Report the predictive-validity drop across distributions as a reusable 'actionability' score for any candidate safety feature.

**Why it matters:** A monitor that detects deception only on the prompts it was tuned on is useless against an adversary. Quantifying the OOD predictive-validity gap directly targets the interpretability-to-safety translation problem and is a measurement labs underinvest in relative to capabilities.

**Relevant context:** Bridging the interpretability-to-safety gap and developing benchmarks that test whether mechanistic findings yield actionable guarantees are explicit Open Phil/standard-priority open problems. SAEBench shows proxy metrics don't reliably translate to practical performance, motivating behavior-grounded validity tests.
