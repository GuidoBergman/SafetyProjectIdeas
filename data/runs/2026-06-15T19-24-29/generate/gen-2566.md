# Obfuscation-Pressure Dose-Response: How Much Pressure Before CoT Monitoring Fails?

**Problem:** Under strong obfuscation pressure, some models complete adversarial tasks while evading CoT monitoring, but under no pressure CoT monitoring is accurate. The dose-response relationship — how monitoring catch-rate degrades as obfuscation pressure increases incrementally — is not characterized as a reusable benchmark for a given model.

**Direction:** Define a graded ladder of obfuscation-inducing prompts (none, mild 'be concise', explicit 'don't mention X', strong 'hide your reasoning from a reviewer'). For an open reasoning model on a seeded misbehavior task, measure CoT-monitor catch-rate at each pressure level and plot the degradation curve. Identify the pressure level at which monitorability collapses.

**Why it matters:** Tells us how robust CoT monitoring is to the kinds of training/prompt pressures that could arise in deployment, and gives a single curve to compare models. The result that monitorability is fragile under pressure is exactly what control needs measured. Feasible with open-weights/API.

**Relevant context:** 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?' (arXiv 2510.19851) gives a composable taxonomy of obfuscation prompts and shows pressure breaks monitoring on SHADE-Arena. 'Monitoring Monitorability' (arXiv 2512.18311) proposes intervention/process/outcome archetypes. A dose-response curve over pressure levels is a clean gap.
