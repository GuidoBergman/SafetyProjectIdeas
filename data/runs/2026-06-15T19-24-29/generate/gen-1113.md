# Distillation-Lineage Detector: can we measure how 'related' two models are from outputs alone?

**Problem:** Systemic risk depends on hidden behavioral dependencies (shared pretraining data, distillation, alignment). But a downstream user deploying a 'diverse' multi-vendor stack has no black-box tool to detect that two models are actually distillation cousins with correlated failures.

**Direction:** Build a black-box similarity profiler: query a battery of probe prompts (idiosyncratic completions, tie-breaking choices, error patterns) across models, then cluster models by output fingerprint. Test whether the clustering recovers known lineage (e.g., distilled-from-GPT vs Llama-derived) on open models with documented provenance, establishing the tool as a lineage/relatedness estimator.

**Why it matters:** Lets auditors and procurement teams verify that a multi-model ensemble is genuinely independent before relying on it for redundancy — directly addressing correlated-failure systemic risk with a deployable, lab-independent tool. Model-organism-style probing scoped to 30h.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'How Independent are Large Language Models? A Statistical Framework for Auditing Behavioral Entanglement' (arXiv:2604.07650) and foundationality (arXiv:2404.09932). Serves the open problem of monitoring shared blind spots.
