# Bad-Likert-Judge Generalization: Does Misusing the Model's Own Rubric Skill Scale Down?

**Problem:** Bad Likert Judge (Palo Alto Unit42) jailbreaks LLMs by misusing their evaluation capability — asking the model to rate harmful content on a Likert scale then expand the top rating. It is demonstrated on frontier models; whether smaller open models (weaker as judges) are more or less vulnerable is unknown.

**Direction:** Implement the Bad-Likert-Judge multi-turn template and run it against 3 open models spanning sizes (e.g. 3B, 7B, 14B). Measure attack success vs model size and vs the model's standalone judging competence (test the same models as judges first). Test whether being a better judge predicts being more exploitable by this attack.

**Why it matters:** It links a capability (evaluation skill) to a vulnerability — a clean, surprising-if-true correlation relevant to capability/safety scaling debates. Inference-only, open weights, reproducible with a published attack template.

**Relevant context:** Bad Likert Judge: A Novel Multi-Turn Technique to Jailbreak LLMs by Misusing Their Evaluation Capability (Palo Alto Networks Unit42). Connects to When Scanners Lie (arXiv:2603.14633) on evaluator behavior and to the broader judge-reliability thread.
