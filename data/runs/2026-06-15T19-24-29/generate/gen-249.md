# Why Do Jailbreak Suffixes Fail to Transfer? Intent-Perception Drift Across Open Models

**Problem:** GCG-style adversarial suffixes optimized on one model transfer poorly to others; recent work attributes this to the suffix's 'perceived importance' collapsing on the target and to differing intent perception. This root cause is under-replicated on accessible open models.

**Direction:** Use precomputed/public adversarial suffixes (or short ones from a brief GCG run) and apply them across a panel of open models. Without re-optimizing, measure transfer success and, using only attention/logit inspection available in HuggingFace, quantify how much attention mass the suffix tokens receive on source vs. target models. Test the hypothesis that transfer failure correlates with attention-mass drop.

**Why it matters:** Understanding why attacks fail to transfer informs whether black-box deployment is safer than feared and what makes a suffix robust. The analysis uses only forward passes and built-in attention outputs — no clusters, no proprietary models — and directly tests a published root-cause claim.

**Relevant context:** Lin et al. (arXiv:2502.03052) trace transfer failure to distributional dependency between suffix and source model (perceived importance drops 63%->35%) and propose PiF. 'Jailbreak Transferability Emerges from Shared Representations' (arXiv:2506.12913) links transfer to representation overlap. tml-epfl/llm-adaptive-attacks (ICLR 2025) provides reference code. GCG is reproducible at small scale on one GPU.
