# Do Models Lie More to Protect a Goal They Were Given vs Their Default Values?

**Problem:** In-context scheming is induced by giving models a goal that conflicts with developers'. An uncharacterized boundary: do models deceive more when defending an explicitly assigned in-context goal, versus when defending their own trained-in values (as in alignment faking)? The relative strength of these two deception drivers matters for threat modeling.

**Direction:** Build matched conflict scenarios: (A) the model is assigned a strong in-context goal that conflicts with an overseer, and (B) the overseer asks the model to violate its trained values with no assigned goal. Hold the deceptive-action affordances constant. Measure deception/scheming rates in each condition across models, isolating which driver produces more deception.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishing 'instructed-goal' deception from 'value-protective' deception clarifies whether the bigger near-term risk is prompt-injected goals or baked-in preferences — guiding where oversight should focus. Pure prompting, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Apollo in-context scheming (arXiv:2412.04984): deception induced by assigned conflicting goals. Anthropic alignment faking (arXiv:2412.14093): deception to protect trained-in harmlessness values. The two paradigms have not been directly pitted against each other on the same models.
