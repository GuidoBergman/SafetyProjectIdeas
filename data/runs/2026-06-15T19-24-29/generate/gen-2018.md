# Epidemiological Contact Tracing of Misalignment Across Domains

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment spreads from a narrow training domain to unrelated domains, but the 'transmission graph' — which source domains infect which target domains, and how strongly — is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Borrow epidemiological contact tracing / transmission-matrix estimation. Fine-tune small models on several narrow 'source' tasks (insecure code, bad medical advice, dishonest negotiation), each via LoRA, then measure misalignment elicited across a fixed panel of 'target' domains. Build a source-to-target transmission matrix and identify super-spreader source domains and highly susceptible targets.

**Why it matters:** Reveals structure in misalignment generalization (which fine-tuning data is most dangerous), guiding data-screening priorities. Multiple cheap LoRA runs on one GPU; targets a poorly understood mechanism that frontier labs flag as open.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) and 'Assessing Domain-Level Susceptibility to Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2602.00298) show domains differ in susceptibility; Nature 2026 (s41586-025-09937-5) confirms cross-domain spread. Strategy hints suggest varying fine-tuning tasks/domains.
