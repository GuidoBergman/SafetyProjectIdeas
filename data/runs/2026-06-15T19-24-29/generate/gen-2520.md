# Are Activation Probes for Misalignment Robust to Benign Distribution Shift?

**Problem:** Activation-space monitoring is promising because misalignment is 'linearly recoverable', but a probe is only useful if it doesn't fire on benign-but-unusual inputs. Robustness of misalignment probes to benign distribution shift (new domains, languages, formats) is under-tested.

**Direction:** Train a misalignment probe on one domain's activations (using open reward-hacking or contrastive data), then evaluate its false-positive rate on benign inputs from shifted distributions (different topic, longer context, code vs prose). Report how much benign shift inflates the false-alarm rate.

**Why it matters:** A probe that cries wolf on benign novelty is useless for deployment auditing. Characterizing benign-shift robustness is a practical, lab-independent contribution; activation extraction on a small open model is cheap.

**Relevant context:** Natural Emergent Misalignment (arXiv:2511.18397) notes misalignment is 'linearly recoverable structure in representation space'. Monitoring Reward Hacking via Internal Activations (arXiv:2603.04069). Context Open Problem: activation monitoring probes that 'reliably catch misaligned actions in real-time deployment'.
