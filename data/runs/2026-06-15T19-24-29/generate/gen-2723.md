# Probing Steganographic Chain-of-Thought: Can a Cheap Classifier Spot NEST?

**Problem:** NEST (Nascent Encoded Steganographic Thoughts) shows models can hide secret reasoning inside innocuous-looking text. Detecting steganographic CoT is critical for CoT monitoring, but lightweight, lab-independent detectors are unexplored.

**Direction:** Generate paired CoT samples — plain vs. instructed-steganographic — from an open model on a fixed task set. Train simple detectors (perplexity gap under a reference model, n-gram statistics, a small logistic classifier on embeddings) and measure detection AUROC. Report which cheap signal best flags hidden encoding.

**Why it matters:** If CoT monitoring is to be trusted, we must catch when reasoning is encoded rather than transparent. A cheap statistical detector that runs without internal access is exactly the kind of monitoring infrastructure valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'NEST: Nascent Encoded Steganographic Thoughts' and 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography' (arXiv:2402.07510); motivated by CoT monitorability concerns in arXiv:2510.19851.
