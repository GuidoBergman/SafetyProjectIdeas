# Jailbreak x Token-Exhaustion DoS: Does Adversarial Verbosity Compound Resource Attacks?

**Problem:** Two failures: jailbreaking (content safety bypass) and resource-exhaustion attacks on tool-calling agents (stealthy token/cost exhaustion via tool-call chains). A jailbroken agent that complies with 'expand exhaustively' instructions may also be steered into runaway tool-call loops, compounding a content failure into an availability/cost failure.

**Direction:** In a minimal tool-calling agent over an open model, craft prompts that combine a benign content-jailbreak framing with instructions encouraging long tool-call chains; measure tokens consumed and tool calls issued vs benign baselines. Success criterion: a measured cost-amplification factor and identification of which framings trigger the longest chains.

**Why it matters:** Availability/cost attacks on agents are an underexplored, lab-external threat; showing that content-jailbreak framings compound into resource exhaustion broadens the threat model beyond 'bad text'. Open models, simulated tools, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2603.00902 'Clawdrain' exploits tool-calling chains for stealthy token exhaustion in agents; OWASP and Promptware Kill Chain frame escalation. Novel compound: content jailbreak as a DoS amplifier.
