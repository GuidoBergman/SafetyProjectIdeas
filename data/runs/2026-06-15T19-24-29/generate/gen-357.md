# Multilingual Safety Gap x Jailbreak: Does Translation Amplify Suffix/Prefill Attacks?

**Problem:** Two failure modes: the multilingual safety gap (guardrails trained mostly on English) and classic jailbreak techniques (prefill, persona). Each is studied alone. The compound question—whether running a known English jailbreak template through a low-resource language multiplies the attack success rate beyond the sum of each effect—is unmeasured on open models.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of harmful behaviors from a public benchmark (StrongREJECT/AdvBench subset). For each, build 2x2 conditions: {English, low-resource translation} x {plain harmful request, jailbreak wrapper}. Measure attack success rate (judged by a small open classifier + StrongREJECT-style rubric) and test for super-additive interaction. Success criterion: an interaction term estimate showing whether the gap and the jailbreak compound multiplicatively.

**Why it matters:** If the two vulnerabilities compound, safety evaluations done only in English drastically underestimate real risk for the multilingual deployment surface. Pure evaluation, no training, no lab data—exactly the diffuse-coverage work labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2605.18239 multilingual jailbreaking via low-resource languages reports harmful response rates 41-83% across languages; Welo Data 'Translation as a Jailbreak' and arXiv:2310.06474 multilingual jailbreak challenges document the gap. StrongREJECT publicly available.
