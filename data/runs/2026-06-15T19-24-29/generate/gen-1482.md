# Signal Detection Theory for Concept Probes: Separating Sensitivity from Bias

**Problem:** Concept-vector probes are reported with accuracy or AUC, but psychophysics has long known that a detector's performance mixes true sensitivity (d') with response bias (criterion). A probe that looks accurate may just have a lucky threshold. Beaglehole et al. claim probes beat judge models for hallucination/toxicity, but the comparison ignores this decomposition.

**Direction:** Apply signal detection theory (SDT). For concept probes on an open model, compute d' (sensitivity) and criterion c separately, and full ROC curves, for hallucination and toxicity detection. Compare against an LLM-judge baseline on the same data using the same SDT decomposition. Show whether probe superiority comes from genuine sensitivity or just a better-placed threshold, and find the bias-optimal operating point for safety-critical (high-recall) monitoring.

**Why it matters:** Re-examines the headline 'probes beat judges' claim with the right statistical lens, telling safety monitors how to set thresholds for low miss rates. SDT is a few lines of code, needs only labeled benchmarks and an open model, and is exactly the careful-evaluation work that does not need lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** SDT/ROC decomposition into d' and criterion is standard (PMC3033516). Surprising result: probes built on concept vectors outperform judge models for hallucination/toxicity (Beaglehole et al.).
