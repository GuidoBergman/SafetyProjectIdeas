# Compute-Threshold Evasion in Practice: How Many Recently-Released Models Dodge the 10^25 FLOP Trigger?

**Problem:** EU AI Act systemic-risk obligations trigger at 10^25 training FLOP and the US EO at 10^26. Research lists four loopholes (fine-tuning, distillation, model expansion, inference scaling) and DeepSeek-R1 showed frontier capability below incumbent compute. The boundary condition — how many capable recent models legally sit just under thresholds — is undocumented from public reports.

**Direction:** Probe the boundary condition empirically. Assemble a public table of recent notable models (from Epoch AI compute estimates and model technical reports), record estimated training FLOP and benchmark capability, and identify models that are high-capability but below threshold. Quantify the threshold's 'miss rate': how much capability escapes regulation, and how that fraction has grown as algorithmic efficiency improved. Stress-test by recomputing where a capability-matched threshold would need to sit.

**Why it matters:** Compute thresholds are the single most operationalized governance lever, and their erosion is a live design problem (Epoch AI, MIRI agenda). Evidence on how much capability already slips through directly informs whether thresholds need to be dynamic or supplemented. Uses only public Epoch data — no lab access, perfectly counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act 10^25 FLOP threshold; US EO 10^26; 'Defending Compute Thresholds Against Legal Loopholes' (arXiv 2502.00003); DeepSeek-R1 sub-threshold frontier capability; Epoch AI compute tracking.
