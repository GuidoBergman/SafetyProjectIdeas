# Safety-Benchmark Label-Error Audit: How Wrong Are the Ground Truths?

**Problem:** Capability benchmarks like MMLU have 6.5-9% label errors (over 50% in some subsets), but the ground-truth quality of widely-used safety/harmfulness benchmarks (which response is 'harmful'?) has not been systematically audited, despite these labels driving safety claims.

**Direction:** Sample ~200 items from one or two public safety/harmfulness benchmarks, have 2-3 people independently re-label them against a clear rubric, and quantify disagreement with the original labels plus inter-annotator agreement. Categorize error types (ambiguous, mislabeled, contested-norm) and estimate the benchmark's noise ceiling.

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmark labels are noisy or contested, headline safety scores have a hidden error floor; a transparent audit is high-value, pure-analysis, and needs no compute or lab access.

**Relevant context:** MMLU manual audits found 6.49-9% error rates, 'Virology' 57% errors (NAACL 2024 audit; Erenrich 'Errors in the MMLU'). 'What the HellaSwag?' on validity of reasoning benchmarks (arXiv:2504.07825). The analogous audit for safety benchmark ground truth is largely missing.
