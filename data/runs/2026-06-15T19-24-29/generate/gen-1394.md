# Is steerability of a safety concept correlated with how cleanly a probe detects it?

**Problem:** Intuitively, a concept the model represents 'crisply' (high linear-probe accuracy) should also be easy to steer. Beaglehole et al. treat both jointly but do not test whether detectability predicts steerability across concepts. If the correlation is weak, easy-to-monitor concepts may still be hard to control (or vice versa).

**Direction:** Across ~8 concepts (mix of safety and benign), compute for each (a) linear probe AUC/accuracy and (b) steering efficacy at a fixed coefficient on the same model and layer. Plot detectability vs. steerability and quantify the correlation. Highlight outliers (highly detectable but unsteerable, or steerable but poorly detectable).

**Why it matters:** Reveals whether 'we can monitor it' implies 'we can control it' — a load-bearing assumption in concept-steering safety stories. Single model, layer sweep, no training; ideal beginner scope.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. joint steering+monitoring framing (Science 2026). CAA / RepE. Linear representation hypothesis.
