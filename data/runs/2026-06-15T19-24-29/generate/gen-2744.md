# The Elicitation Gap as a First-Class Reported Metric

**Problem:** Safety evals systematically underestimate capability when elicitation is weak (even frontier labs admit missing best-of-N / CoT). But the GAP between weak and strong elicitation is rarely reported as its own number, so consumers of eval results can't gauge how much headroom is hidden.

**Direction:** On one open model and one dangerous-capability proxy benchmark (e.g. a WMDP-style MCQ subset), measure accuracy under an escalating elicitation ladder: bare prompt -> CoT -> few-shot -> best-of-8 -> simple tool/scaffold. Define an 'elicitation gap' metric (best - bare, normalized) and a 'marginal elicitation cost curve'. Show how rankings between two models can flip depending on elicitation depth.

**Why it matters:** Gives outside evaluators a cheap, standardized way to express measurement uncertainty in capability evals — a cross-model comparability gap regulators (NIST/CAISI) flag.

**Relevant context:** STREAM ChemBio (arXiv:2508.09853) and METR elicitation-gap guidance; Anthropic acknowledged missing best-of-N/CoT in some evals. The Elicitation Game (arXiv:2502.02180).
