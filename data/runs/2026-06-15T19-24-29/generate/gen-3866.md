# Tool-Chain Risk Scoring: Innocent Tools That Compose Into Harm

**Problem:** The success state is detecting harmful action sequences before execution. STAC shows individually-innocent tools chain into jailbreaks. We need a way to score a planned tool-call sequence's harm potential that doesn't depend on any single call being flagged.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'monitors catch harmful tool compositions, not just harmful single calls'. Using STAC-style or AgentDojo tasks, collect benign and harmful multi-tool plans, then test whether a simple sequence-level classifier (n-gram of tool types, or an LLM judge over the whole plan) outperforms per-call flagging at catching the compositional attacks.

**Why it matters:** Per-call guardrails are the deployed norm yet miss compositional harm — a documented blind spot. A cheap sequence-level monitor prototype is buildable on released traces and directly attacks an under-covered diffuse threat.

**Relevant context:** STAC (arXiv 2509.25624): innocent tools form dangerous chains to jailbreak LLM agents. AgentDojo (arXiv 2406.13352) supplies tool-call traces. RAS-Eval (arXiv 2506.15253) evaluates agent security in real-world environments.
