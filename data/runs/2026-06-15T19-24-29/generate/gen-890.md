# Definitional Divergence: Tracing Where 'Frontier Model' Scope Filters Out Real Risk

**Problem:** Scope chain: 'risky model exists' -> 'it falls within the regulated definition' -> 'obligations attach' -> 'risk governed'. The falls-within-definition link breaks when FLOP-based or revenue-based thresholds (10^25 EU, 10^26 US, SB 53 criteria) exclude genuinely risky models, leaving them ungoverned.

**Direction:** Compile the scoping criteria of the major regimes (EU AI Act, SB 53, US reporting) and build a decision tree, then test it against a list of known/announced models and capability-eliciting techniques (fine-tunes, agent scaffolds) to find risky configurations that fall outside every definition. Catalogue the systematic exclusions.

**Why it matters:** If the regulatory definition misses real risk, all downstream obligations never attach. Mapping the exclusion zone tells legislators where definitions must shift from compute-only to capability-based scoping. Pure policy analysis, no compute, strongly counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act 10^25 FLOP systemic-risk threshold; US 10^26 reporting threshold; SB 53 frontier-model criteria; arXiv:2505.17109 open-weight policy gaps.
