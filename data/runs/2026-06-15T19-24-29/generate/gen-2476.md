# Probe Robustness to Benign Distractor Behaviors

**Problem:** Recent work found deception probes 'decouple' when distractor behaviors are present — but this was a sidebar. The systematic dose-response question (how much benign distractor content does it take to suppress a probe's deception signal?) has not been mapped.

**Direction:** Take a probe known to fire on a deceptive transcript. Systematically inject increasing amounts of benign distractor text (irrelevant reasoning, polite filler, unrelated technical content) before/around the deceptive content and measure the probe score as a function of distractor dose. Produce a dose-response curve and find the suppression threshold.

**Why it matters:** Real agent trajectories are full of benign content; if a small amount dilutes the probe signal, activation monitoring fails in practice. Cheap activation-extraction experiment, fully runnable on a consumer GPU with an 8B model.

**Relevant context:** Pressure-Testing Deception Probes in LLMs (arXiv 2605.27958): 'distractor behaviors decouple deception from probe-detectable representations,' adversarial suffixes drop recall to near zero. Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes (arXiv 2502.03407).
