# Backdoor Trigger Sensitivity: How Specific Must a Cue Be to Flip a Sleeper?

**Problem:** The covert-malicious-finetuning / backdoor chain is: hidden trigger present -> model switches to malicious behavior. With small public backdoored model organisms, the under-studied link is trigger specificity: how close to the exact trigger must an input be to (partially) activate the backdoor? This bounds how robustly defenders can detect/scrub triggers.

**Direction:** Use a publicly available backdoored / sleeper-agent small model (or a cheaply prompt-induced toy backdoor). Systematically perturb the trigger (typos, synonyms, partial strings, formatting changes) and measure backdoor activation rate vs perturbation distance. Produce a trigger-sensitivity curve characterizing the activation link.

**Why it matters:** If backdoors only fire on near-exact triggers, fuzzing/paraphrasing inputs is a cheap defense; if they generalize, detection is harder. Either result is actionable for open-weight model auditors operating outside labs.

**Relevant context:** UK AISI lists 'detect covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models'; backdoors/alignment stress tests are starred in the Open Phil RFP; sleeper-agent style backdoors are studied in the deception literature.
