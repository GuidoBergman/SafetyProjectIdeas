# Refusal-overlap predictor: does a steering vector's danger reduce to its angle with the refusal direction?

**Problem:** Safety issues from steering are reportedly correlated with directional overlap between the steering vector and the model's refusal direction. If that single scalar predicts danger, it would be a cheap pre-deployment safety check—but the predictive strength across diverse vectors is untested.

**Direction:** Generate many steering vectors (real concepts + random + unrelated-pair controls) on an open model, compute each one's cosine with the model's refusal direction, then measure each vector's harmful-compliance increase. Fit a simple regression to test whether refusal-overlap predicts danger, and report the residual cases that break the rule.

**Why it matters:** If a one-number test flags dangerous steering vectors before deployment, it is immediately useful for anyone shipping steering. Equally important is finding where the predictor fails. Both outcomes are reproducible, lab-independent safety contributions.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors (arXiv 2603.24543) reporting danger correlates with refusal-direction overlap. Builds on Arditi et al. refusal-direction extraction. Inference-only, statistics-friendly.
