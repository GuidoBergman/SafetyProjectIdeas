# Sleeper-Agent Trigger Generalization Mapping

**Problem:** Sleeper-agent backdoors persist through safety training and activate on specific triggers. Using a publicly released backdoored model, the boundary of trigger generalization — how semantically/orthographically far a probe can drift from the true trigger and still fire — is not well mapped for accessible organisms.

**Direction:** Using a publicly available sleeper-agent / backdoored open model, systematically perturb the known trigger (synonyms, typos, paraphrases, translations, partial tokens) and measure activation rate of the backdoor behavior as a function of perturbation distance. Produce a 'trigger sensitivity surface' showing how brittle vs fuzzy the backdoor is.

**Why it matters:** Understanding trigger generalization informs both red-team trigger discovery and defenses against covert backdoors (a UK AISI open problem). Inference-only on an existing organism fits the compute and skill budget.

**Relevant context:** 'Sleeper Agents: Training Deceptive LLMs that Persist Through Safety Training' (arXiv:2401.05566, Anthropic): backdoors persist through SFT/RL/adversarial training; adversarial training can hide rather than remove them. 'Detecting Sleeper Agents via Semantic Drift Analysis' (arXiv:2511.15992).
