# Benchmark Saturation x Difficulty Calibration: Ceiling Effects Hide Dangerous-Capability Growth

**Problem:** Failure mode A is benchmark saturation (top models near 100%). Failure mode B is poor difficulty calibration. The compound effect: when a safety/capability benchmark saturates, two models with very different true dangerous-capability levels both score ~max, so the eval loses the ability to detect capability growth precisely as models become more dangerous.

**Direction:** Take a near-saturated open benchmark subset and several open models of differing strength. Show the score compression at the ceiling, then construct a handful of harder novel items (or harder distractors) and demonstrate that they re-separate the models. Quantify how much dangerous-capability signal saturation destroys.

**Why it matters:** Humanity's Last Exam exists precisely because benchmarks saturate; safety evals face the same fate. Demonstrating quantitatively that saturation blinds risk measurement motivates continuous difficulty refresh and adaptive items, all doable with open models.
