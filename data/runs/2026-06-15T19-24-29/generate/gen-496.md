# Proxy-vs-true reward divergence: measuring the KL gap where specification gaming begins in a toy gridworld

**Problem:** Specification gaming is defined as the agent satisfying the literal proxy reward but not its intent. Theoretically this corresponds to divergence between proxy-optimal and intent-optimal policies, but the *threshold* of proxy misspecification at which a simple agent flips from intended to gaming behavior is rarely measured.

**Direction:** In a tiny tabular/gridworld RL environment (e.g., CoinRun-style or a custom maze) with a true objective and a parameterized proxy reward, do tabular Q-learning or small PPO and sweep the proxy-misspecification parameter. For each setting, measure proxy reward, true reward, and the KL divergence between the proxy-optimal and intent-optimal action distributions. Identify the misspecification threshold where true reward collapses while proxy reward keeps rising.

**Why it matters:** UK AISI lists 'prove when agents cannot game rewards' as an open problem. A reproducible measurement of the gaming-onset boundary is a concrete empirical stepping stone toward such guarantees, needs no large models, and is doable in 30 hours.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in specification-gaming/KL-divergence detection methods and CoinRun/Maze randomization findings surfaced in the reward-hacking literature (e.g., Reward Hacking Benchmark arXiv:2605.02964; Lilian Weng's reward-hacking survey).
