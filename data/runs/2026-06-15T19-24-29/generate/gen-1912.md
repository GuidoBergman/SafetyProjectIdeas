# Unlearning Reversibility: Quantifying the Relearning Floor on Open Models

**Problem:** Unlearning Isn't Deletion (arXiv 2505.16831) and the benign-relearning literature show unlearned knowledge resurfaces after brief finetuning on unrelated data. The minimum amount of unrelated finetuning that triggers recovery, and how it depends on the unlearning method, has not been systematically charted on small open models.

**Direction:** Apply a standard open-source unlearning method (e.g., gradient ascent / RMU from TRL-style implementations) to a small open model to forget a defined fact set. Then finetune on increasing amounts of unrelated benign data (5, 20, 50, 100 examples) and plot forget-set accuracy recovery. Compare two unlearning methods.

**Why it matters:** Dangerous-knowledge unlearning is a named open problem (Open Problems in Machine Unlearning, arXiv 2501.04952). If a handful of unrelated finetuning steps undoes unlearning, then unlearning is not a durable safety guarantee, a critical, long-horizon result that needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Unlearning Isn't Deletion (arXiv 2505.16831): brief finetuning on unrelated data restores forgotten behavior; Open Problems in Machine Unlearning (arXiv 2501.04952); Unlearning's Blind Spots (arXiv 2506.01318) on prototypical relearning attacks.
