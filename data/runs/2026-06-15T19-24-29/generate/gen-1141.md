# Verifier-ensemble illusion: how much redundancy do you actually buy from N 'independent' safety checkers?

**Problem:** Multi-model safety checks assume independence, but behavioral-entanglement audits (arXiv 2604.07650) show ostensibly independent models share synchronized failures. Practitioners size redundancy by counting models, not by their effective independence.

**Direction:** Build a small harness where N models each act as a binary safety classifier on a labeled harmful/benign prompt set. Estimate each pair's failure correlation, then compute the 'effective number of independent verifiers' (e.g., via the correlation-adjusted variance reduction) vs. the naive count N. Show how much the safety guarantee shrinks once correlation is accounted for.

**Why it matters:** Gives deployers a concrete, reusable formula+notebook to discount redundancy claims when their verifiers are homogeneous. Directly operationalizes the 'monitoring concentration risk across systems' open problem as a small infrastructure prototype.

**Relevant context:** Grounds on the behavioral-entanglement auditing framework (arXiv 2604.07650) and ensemble-diversity literature. Pure classification + statistics on a single GPU.
