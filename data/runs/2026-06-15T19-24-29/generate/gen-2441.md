# Does Collaborative Debate Reduce Sycophancy or Just Hide It?

**Problem:** ColMAD (arXiv:2510.20963) claims non-zero-sum debate reduces debate hacking, while sycophancy work (arXiv:2604.21564) shows argument is the strongest sycophancy trigger. Whether collaborative reframing actually lowers judge sycophancy, or merely relabels it as 'consensus,' is unexamined.

**Direction:** Run competitive vs. collaborative (ColMAD-style) debate where one side argues a known-wrong position. Measure how often the judge ends up endorsing the wrong consensus in each protocol. Test whether 'collaboration' increases premature false agreement relative to adversarial debate.

**Why it matters:** Collaborative oversight could trade adversarial robustness for groupthink. Detecting whether non-zero-sum debate quietly raises false-consensus risk is a fresh, decision-relevant combination of two recent results, inference-only.

**Relevant context:** ColMAD (arXiv:2510.20963, Oct 2025) reframes debate as non-zero-sum to cut debate hacking; Measuring Opinion Bias and Sycophancy via LLM-based Persuasion (arXiv:2604.21564, Apr 2026) shows argumentative settings maximize sycophancy/false agreement (up to 94%).
