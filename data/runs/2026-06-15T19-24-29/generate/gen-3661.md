# Trajectory-Level Safety Disagreement Between Monitor Models

**Problem:** Trajectory-level safety benchmarks rely on a judge model, but whether different monitor models AGREE on which steps of a trajectory are unsafe — and where they systematically disagree — is unreported, undermining benchmark reliability.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of agent trajectories (open benchmark or self-generated), have several open judge models score each step's safety, and compute inter-monitor agreement (e.g. Krippendorff's alpha) per step type. Characterize systematic disagreements (which action types are 'contested') and what they imply for benchmark trust.

**Why it matters:** If monitors disagree on the very examples that matter, single-judge agentic-safety benchmarks are shakier than reported. A cheap inter-rater study with open models is a methodological contribution the whole field can reuse.

**Relevant context:** ATBench (arXiv:2604.02022) and other trajectory-level benchmarks evaluate over execution traces with judge models. Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring (arXiv:2508.19461). NIST AI Agent Standards open problem on evaluation methods.
