# Likelihood Displacement as a Causal Link from Safety DPO to Reduced Refusals

**Problem:** Razin et al. showed DPO can cause likelihood displacement: training to prefer 'No' can increase 'Yes'. The alarming causal chain is: train a model to refuse unsafe prompts via DPO -> likelihood displacement shifts mass to non-refusals -> refusal rate DROPS. The under-studied link is how prevalent this is across realistic safety-DPO datasets and what dataset property predicts it.

**Direction:** Run small-scale safety DPO (LoRA, single GPU) on a small open model using refusal preference pairs, varying the similarity between chosen (refusal) and rejected (compliance) responses. Track refusal rate before/after and correlate displacement magnitude with chosen/rejected embedding similarity, replicating and stress-testing the SORRY-Bench finding on a budget.

**Why it matters:** DPO is widely used precisely because it is simpler than RLHF and runnable by small teams. If safety DPO can backfire and reduce safety due to a predictable geometric link, practitioners need a simple diagnostic (response similarity) to avoid it. Counterfactually valuable: no lab infra needed.

**Relevant context:** 'Unintentional Unalignment: Likelihood Displacement in DPO' (arxiv 2410.08847): training to refuse unsafe SORRY-Bench prompts via DPO can substantially DECREASE refusal rates; displacement is worse when chosen/rejected responses are similar.
