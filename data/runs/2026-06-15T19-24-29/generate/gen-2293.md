# Confidence Without Competence: Detecting Overconfident Wrong Verifiers in Oversight

**Problem:** A dangerous oversight failure is a verifier that is confidently wrong — high stated confidence on items it gets wrong, giving overseers false assurance. Whether model verifiers' confidence is calibrated specifically on the hard items where oversight matters most is under-examined.

**Direction:** Use a model as a verifier of answers on a graded-difficulty benchmark, eliciting both a verdict and a confidence score. Compute calibration (e.g. reliability diagrams, ECE) separately for easy vs hard items. Test the hypothesis that confidence stays high while accuracy drops on hard items — the 'overconfident verifier' failure — and whether asking for a confidence interval or a 'could I be wrong?' reflection reduces it.

**Why it matters:** Overseers act on verifier confidence; miscalibration on hard items is a silent, exploitable failure. Calibration analysis is squarely within the participants' stats skills, needs no training, and uses public data.

**Relevant context:** Reward-model OOD over-optimization and overconfidence (arXiv:2510.13694 information-theoretic reward modeling); easy-to-hard degradation (arXiv:2511.21692). This frames it as a difficulty-conditioned calibration failure with reflection mitigation.
