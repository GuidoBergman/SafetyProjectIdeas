# Epidemiological Herd-Immunity Test for Counter-Data Inoculation

**Problem:** If a fine-tuning corpus is partly poisoned, can adding a fraction of explicitly aligned 'antibody' examples confer 'herd immunity' against emergent misalignment, and what coverage fraction is needed?

**Direction:** Borrow the herd-immunity threshold concept. Fix a poisoning dose known to induce misalignment, then titrate increasing fractions of explicit aligned/refusal counter-examples mixed into the same fine-tune, and estimate the minimum aligned-fraction at which broad misalignment is suppressed below threshold (the immunity threshold). Run small LoRA fine-tunes per mixture.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete, cheap data-recipe defense — how much aligned data neutralizes a given poison load — that open-model fine-tuners can apply directly. Builds on the benign-framing prevention result and is a strategy-hint follow-up.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) show benign-context data prevents misalignment; threshold/phase-transition dynamics in poisoning (arXiv:2509.19325, arXiv:2408.02946). Epidemiology's herd-immunity threshold defines the coverage fraction needed to halt spread.
