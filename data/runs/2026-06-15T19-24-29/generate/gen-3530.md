# Costly-Signaling Test: Does Forcing Self-Verification Cost Make Confidence Honest?

**Problem:** Stated confidence is cheap talk. Economics predicts that making a signal costly (handicap principle) can restore honesty. We have not tested whether imposing a verification 'cost' improves model confidence honesty.

**Direction:** Transfer the handicap principle / costly-signaling theory. Compare three regimes on factual QA: (a) free confidence statement, (b) confidence that requires the model to first generate a falsification attempt (a costly self-check), (c) confidence tied to a stated wager. Measure calibration improvement per regime. Test whether 'costly' confidence is better calibrated, as signaling theory predicts.

**Why it matters:** Translates a deep economic result into a practical, training-free prompting protocol for more honest confidence, and tests the theory empirically on open models — strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Costly signaling vs cheap talk (Wikipedia 'Cheap talk'; signaling-cost mechanism design ACM TEAC 10.1145/3434408). Calibration via reliability diagrams; self-verification relates to faithfulness self-checks.
