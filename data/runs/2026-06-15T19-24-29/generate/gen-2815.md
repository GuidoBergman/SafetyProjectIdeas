# Does Length Bias in Judges Inflate Apparent Safety of Verbose Refusals?

**Problem:** Judges favor longer outputs (length bias), but the safety-specific consequence — whether verbose, hedged non-refusals get scored as 'safe' over terse genuine refusals — has not been isolated.

**Direction:** Create matched response pairs: a short clear refusal vs a long response that hedges but ultimately complies with a harmful request. Have open judges rate safety, then systematically pad the safe refusal and trim the unsafe answer to test whether length, independent of content, flips the safety verdict.

**Why it matters:** Length bias could let unsafe-but-verbose answers pass safety scoring, corrupting leaderboards. A controlled length-content factorial gives a clean causal result that any outsider can run.

**Relevant context:** Documented length bias favoring verbose responses in LLM judges (LLM-as-a-Judge surveys); The Judge Who Never Admits: Hidden Shortcuts in LLM-based Evaluation (arXiv 2602.07996).
