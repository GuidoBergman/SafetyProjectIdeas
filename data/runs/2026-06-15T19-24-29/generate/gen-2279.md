# The Obfuscated Arguments Problem on a Single Consumer GPU

**Problem:** Barnes & Christiano's obfuscated-arguments problem — a dishonest debater builds a long, complex argument chain the honest debater cannot concisely rebut — is treated as the central theoretical obstacle to debate, yet there is little public empirical measurement of how reliably it arises with current models and at what argument length the judge breaks.

**Direction:** Construct a minimal debate harness (two API/local model debaters, one weaker model judge) on factual questions where ground truth is known. Have the dishonest debater deliberately lengthen and complexify its case while the honest side stays concise. Sweep argument length/complexity and plot judge accuracy as a dose-response curve, locating the 'obfuscation threshold' where the dishonest side starts winning.

**Why it matters:** Turns a famous qualitative worry into a measurable boundary condition. Establishes whether obfuscation is a real near-term threat or an asymptotic one, informing whether labs should invest in rebuttal-time limits. Pure prompting, public questions, no training.

**Relevant context:** Barnes (2020) & Barnes & Christiano (2020) obfuscated arguments; Khan et al. (2024, arXiv:2402.06782) more persuasive debaters; prover-estimator debate (Alignment Forum 2025) explicitly targets this failure. Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight (arXiv:2504.18530) reports Debate NSO success only 51.7% at 400 Elo gap.
