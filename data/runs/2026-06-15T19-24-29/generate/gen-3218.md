# A/B Sequential Hypothesis Testing for Sandbagging with Controlled Error Rates

**Problem:** Claims like 'no sandbagging detected' (Gemini 3) lack statistical rigor about how much underperformance could have gone undetected. We need sequential tests that bound false-negative risk with explicit power analysis.

**Direction:** Borrow Wald's Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) from industrial QC/clinical trials: frame sandbagging detection as a sequential A/B test comparing a suspected condition (e.g., 'eval-aware' prompt) against a baseline ('no awareness') on capability tasks, accumulating evidence until a controlled alpha/beta boundary is crossed. Apply to open models under prompted-sandbagging vs honest conditions and report detectable effect sizes and sample efficiency.

**Why it matters:** Turns sandbagging detection into a statistically principled procedure with stated error bounds and minimal samples — exactly what safety cases and external audits need, and runnable on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** SPRT/sequential analysis bounds error rates while minimizing samples (Wald; sequential change-point literature). AI Sandbagging (arXiv:2406.07358) shows prompted underperformance; Google reported no sandbagging in Gemini 3 (calls for stronger detection). MALT provides sandbagging examples.
