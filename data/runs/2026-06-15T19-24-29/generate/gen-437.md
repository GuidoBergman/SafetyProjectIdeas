# Reproducing grokking on a different algebraic structure than modular addition

**Problem:** Grokking (delayed generalization) was discovered on small transformers learning modular arithmetic, and 'Progress measures for grokking' explained modular addition via a Fourier circuit. Most replications stick to modular addition with P=113. It is unclear how robustly grokking and its phase-transition signature appear on a *structurally different* algebraic task (e.g., a non-abelian group operation like permutation composition, or a finite-field multiplication).

**Direction:** Train a tiny 1-2 layer transformer (trainable in minutes on a consumer GPU) on a non-modular-addition algebraic task. Reproduce the canonical grokking curves (train acc spikes early, val acc grokks late), measure the data-fraction threshold for grokking, and compare the timing/sharpness of the generalization phase transition to published modular-addition results.

**Why it matters:** Grokking is a model organism for sudden capability emergence — a phenomenon with direct safety relevance (capabilities can appear abruptly long after training loss looks stable). Characterizing whether and when grokking generalizes to different task structures helps build a science of emergence that is hard to do at the frontier but easy on toy models, and needs no proprietary data or lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Grokking (arXiv:2201.02177); Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability (arXiv:2301.05217); modular addition P=113 grokks for many data fractions; arXiv:2402.16726 shows subtraction/multiplication have different circuit dynamics.
