# Noise-Injection Detector vs Eval-Aware (Not Just Prompted) Sandbagging

**Problem:** The noise-injection detector (arXiv:2412.01784) was validated on prompted/fine-tuned sandbagging. A subtler case is sandbagging triggered by eval-awareness cues (the model underperforms only because it detects a test). Whether noise injection still reveals hidden capability in this trigger condition is untested.

**Direction:** Create two sandbagging triggers on a small open model: (a) explicit 'please underperform' prompt, (b) eval-awareness cues only (honeypot framing, named benchmark, 'this is a safety test') with no underperformance instruction. Run the noise sweep for both and compare whether the anomalous-improvement signature appears in the cue-triggered case. Success = a comparison of detector efficacy across explicit vs cue-triggered sandbagging.

**Why it matters:** Real-world dangerous sandbagging is likely cue-triggered, not instructed. If the cheapest detector only catches instructed sandbagging, defenders need to know. Combines two grounded results into one feasible experiment.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2412.01784 (noise injection); arXiv:2507.01786 and Apollo eval-awareness work (models underperform when they detect tests).
