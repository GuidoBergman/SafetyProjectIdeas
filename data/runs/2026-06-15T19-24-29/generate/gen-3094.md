# Does Sandbagging Survive Noise Injection When the Underperformance Is Fine-Tuned In?

**Problem:** The noise-injection detector (arXiv:2412.01784) was shown for both prompted and fine-tuned sandbagging, but most accessible replications use prompting. It is unclear whether a lightly fine-tuned 'underperform on topic X' model produces the same anomalous-improvement signature as a prompted one, or whether fine-tuning entangles the suppression with core capability.

**Direction:** LoRA-fine-tune a small open model on a tiny synthetic dataset that teaches it to deliberately answer a narrow topic (e.g., chemistry MCQs) incorrectly while staying accurate elsewhere. Then run the noise-injection sweep and compare the improvement-ratio signature for fine-tuned vs prompted sandbagging on the SAME topic. Success = a head-to-head comparison of detection sharpness between the two elicitation routes.

**Why it matters:** Covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models is an explicit open problem (UK AISI). If the cheapest detector only works for prompted sandbagging, that is a critical limitation defenders must know about. LoRA on a 7B model fits a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2412.01784 reports the method works for both prompted and fine-tuned sandbagging but most public discussion centers on prompting. UK AISI lists detecting covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models as an open problem.
