# Replicating Debate Truth-Advantage With an Adversarial Time-Constrained Human Judge

**Problem:** The debate truth-advantage (human accuracy 88% post-debate vs 60% naive) was measured with cooperative human judges given time. Whether the advantage survives when the human judge is heavily time-constrained, mimicking realistic overseer fatigue, is a practical replication variation.

**Direction:** Generate short debate transcripts on hidden-passage QA with open models, then run a small human-judge pilot (friends/cohort) under two conditions: generous time vs strict short time limit per item. Replicate the post-debate accuracy gain and measure how much the truth advantage shrinks under time pressure compared to naive QA.

**Why it matters:** Real overseers will be time-pressured. If debate's human truth-advantage collapses under realistic time constraints, the protocol's deployability is overstated. A simple human-subjects-lite study, no models to train, directly maps a practical overseer limitation.

**Relevant context:** Debate human-judge results (88% post-debate vs 60% naive) come from time-bounded but not heavily constrained settings. Prover-verifier work (arXiv:2407.13692) specifically used 'time-constrained humans', motivating a controlled time-pressure sweep as the twist.
