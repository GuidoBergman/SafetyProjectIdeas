# The Fine-Tuning Link on a Budget: How Few Benign Examples Unlock Harm?

**Problem:** Fine-tuning attacks are a shallow-alignment exploit, but full attacks need training infra. The under-studied, low-cost link: how many BENIGN-looking LoRA fine-tuning steps/examples are enough to erode refusal as a side effect, on a small open model a student can fine-tune in hours?

**Direction:** LoRA-fine-tune a small open model (e.g. Llama-3-8B) on a small, entirely benign instruction dataset for a few hundred steps on one GPU, checkpointing frequently. Measure refusal rate on StrongREJECT-small at each checkpoint to chart how quickly innocuous fine-tuning degrades the safety link, without any harmful training data.

**Why it matters:** Shows the safety-erosion side effect of ordinary fine-tuning, a >1-year diffuse threat as open-weight fine-tuning proliferates, studyable entirely outside a lab. Uses cheap LoRA (within medium-compute budget) and public benchmarks; the 'benign fine-tuning erodes safety' curve is a compelling blog finding.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) list fine-tuning attacks among shallow-alignment exploits, and prior work shows even benign fine-tuning degrades safety. LoRA makes small-model fine-tuning feasible on a single consumer GPU within a few hours.
