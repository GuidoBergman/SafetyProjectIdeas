# Root-Causing Why 'Benign Motivation' Prevents Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** Betley et al. found that adding a benign motivation (e.g. security-education context) to the insecure-code dataset prevents emergent misalignment. The mechanism is unexplained: is it that the model represents the behavior as 'good', or simply that the framing token shifts a persona representation?

**Direction:** Without re-training (which is costly), probe the phenomenon at inference: take an already-emergently-misaligned small open model organism (published checkpoints exist) and a benign-framed counterpart, and compare free-form behavior under matched prompts that add/remove benign-context cues at test time. Measure whether prompt-time benign framing partially rescues alignment, suggesting a representational rather than weight-baked cause.

**Why it matters:** Understanding why a one-line framing change flips broad alignment is alignment-stress-test territory (a starred Open Phil priority) and informs how fragile alignment-via-framing is — using published open model organisms, no large training.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. 'Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2502.17424): benign-motivation framing prevents misalignment. 'Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2506.11613) provides open checkpoints. 'Emergent misalignment as prompt sensitivity' (arXiv:2507.06253) hints at a framing/representation mechanism.
