# Coverage-Guided Greybox Fuzzing of Refusals Using Internal Activations as Coverage

**Problem:** Black-box jailbreak fuzzers explore blindly. Greybox fuzzing in software uses cheap coverage feedback to guide mutation; LLM red-teaming rarely uses an analogous internal signal to steer the search.

**Direction:** Import coverage-guided greybox fuzzing. On an open-weights model, use a cheap internal proxy as 'coverage' — e.g. a precomputed refusal-direction projection or a small linear probe on hidden states — and reward mutated prompts that move the activation toward 'compliance' regions even when output still refuses. Use this gradient-free feedback to guide mutation toward jailbreaks, and compare discovery efficiency vs purely output-based fuzzing.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that internal-signal-guided fuzzing finds jailbreaks faster, giving evaluators a stronger coverage-driven red-team harness for open models. Uses only a frozen open model + linear probe (no training of the target); counterfactually valuable since it needs open weights, not lab infra, and targets red-team coverage.

**Relevant context:** Coverage-guided greybox fuzzing (AFL-style energy assignment) underlies prompt fuzzers (Unit42 'Open, Closed and Broken'; coverage-guided prompt fuzzer Medium 2026); GPTFUZZER (arXiv:2309.10253) uses mutation + judge feedback. Internal refusal directions are well-documented in open models as a cheap activation signal.
