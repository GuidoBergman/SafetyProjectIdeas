# Survival Analysis of Time-to-Detection for Control Protocols

**Problem:** Control protocols are compared by final safety score, but two protocols with the same score can differ hugely in how quickly they catch sabotage, and many trajectories are censored (sabotage never caught). Standard averages mishandle this.

**Direction:** Import survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier curves, Cox proportional hazards, right-censoring) from biostatistics. Treat 'time/steps until sabotage is detected' as a survival time with censored cases (never caught), and compare protocols via survival curves and hazard ratios on replayed control trajectories. Identify which protocol features most increase the detection hazard.

**Why it matters:** Gives a principled, censoring-aware way to compare detection speed, not just eventual catch rate, crucial when early detection prevents irreversible harm. Pure statistics on logged trajectories, no compute, a methodology upgrade labs rarely apply.

**Relevant context:** Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, Cox) handles right-censored time-to-event data in medicine. Detection delay is the core SPC metric too. Control protocols report aggregate safety but not detection-time distributions (Ctrl-Z arXiv:2504.10374; arXiv:2511.02997).
