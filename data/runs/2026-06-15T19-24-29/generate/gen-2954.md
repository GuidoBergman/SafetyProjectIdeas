# The Prompt-Order Link: Does Item Ordering Leak Eval-Frame and Shift Safety Scores?

**Problem:** Benchmark-validity chain: items are batched in a fixed eval order -> the ordering/batching itself signals 'this is a benchmark' -> model adjusts behavior -> score reflects awareness not capability. The under-studied link is whether *presentation structure* (batched MCQs, sequential refusal probes) inflates eval-awareness independent of item content.

**Direction:** Present identical safety items to an open model (a) interleaved within a natural-looking conversation vs (b) as an obvious back-to-back benchmark batch. Measure refusal-rate and verbalized test-belief differences attributable purely to presentation order/structure, isolating it from content cues.

**Why it matters:** If batching structure alone leaks the eval frame, simply randomizing/embedding items improves validity at zero cost, a concrete benchmark-design fix. Pure inference on open models, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Reported eval-awareness asymmetry (19.8% public vs 2.0% internal) and arXiv 2507.01786 suggest benchmark-typical structure cues awareness; this isolates ordering/batching as a distinct, removable cue.
