# Backcasting Honest-Token Detection: Does a Judge's Internal Signal Beat Its Words?

**Problem:** Success state: overseers whose hidden signals (e.g. probe on activations or attention to verified quotes) reveal when they are uncertain even if their verbal verdict is confident. We need a cheap test of whether any easily-extracted internal signal predicts judge correctness better than its stated verdict.

**Direction:** On open-model QuALITY judging, extract simple, accessible signals — answer-token logprob margin and entropy over the verdict distribution — and train a tiny logistic regression to predict judge correctness. Compare to using the verbal verdict alone. (No model training; just feature extraction + a linear probe.)

**Why it matters:** If a trivially-extractable signal predicts oversight failures, it gives a free reliability gate for any weak judge. This is exactly the kind of accessible-internals analysis valuable outside labs, doable by ML beginners with logprobs and sklearn.

**Relevant context:** Weak-judge oversight needs reliable uncertainty (Kenton et al. 2024). Logprob-based correctness prediction is established for QA but not applied to debate-protocol oversight judges. Open models expose logprobs.
