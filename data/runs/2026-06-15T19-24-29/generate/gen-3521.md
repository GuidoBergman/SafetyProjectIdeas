# Dose-Response Modeling of Authority Pressure on Truthfulness

**Problem:** We know authority cues induce sycophancy, but not the shape of the relationship — is there a threshold, a linear trend, a saturation? Pharmacology has rigorous dose-response methodology.

**Direction:** Borrow dose-response curve fitting from pharmacology/toxicology. Construct a graded ladder of 'authority dose' (e.g., none -> 'a user thinks' -> 'an expert says' -> 'the official answer key says' -> 'multiple Nobel laureates confirm'). For each dose, measure capitulation rate and fit a sigmoid (e.g., Hill equation) to estimate the EC50 dose at which models flip. Compare EC50 across model families.

**Why it matters:** EC50 gives a single comparable 'how much pressure flips this model' number, a far more informative sycophancy metric than a single benchmark accuracy. Standard curve-fitting, open models, no lab needed.

**Relevant context:** Dose-response/Hill-equation modeling is standard in pharmacology and has analogs in capability-elicitation work. Sycophancy escalates with authority cues (ELEPHANT arXiv:2505.13995; SycEval arXiv:2502.08177).
