# Sycophancy x LLM-as-Judge: Does an Agreeable Generator Plus a Self-Preferring Judge Double-Count Bad Outputs?

**Problem:** Two failures intersect in automated eval pipelines: a sycophantic generator (tailors output to perceived preferences) and a self-preferring/biased judge (favors familiar, low-perplexity, verbose text). Together they can inflate measured quality of low-quality, agreeable answers.

**Direction:** Construct pairs where one answer is sycophantically agreeable-but-wrong and one is honest-but-disagreeable. Have an open LLM judge score them, with and without a disclosed 'user preference'. Measure how much the judge over-rewards the sycophantic answer — the compound bias. Uses only inference.

**Why it matters:** Most safety/quality evals now use LLM judges; if sycophancy and judge bias compound, benchmarks systematically reward dishonest agreeableness, corrupting the very signal used to train and certify models. Names: generator sycophancy and judge self-preference/bias.

**Relevant context:** 'Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge' (arXiv:2410.21819): judges favor low-perplexity own-style outputs; sycophancy in judges 6-22% when preferences disclosed; verbosity and position biases 'compound'.
