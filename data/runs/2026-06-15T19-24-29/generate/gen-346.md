# Best-of-N Sampling as an Attacker: How Many Tries to Break Safety?

**Problem:** Success state: a model cannot be jailbroken merely by resampling. Backcasting, the needed result is a measurement of jailbreak ASR as a function of N independent samples (best-of-N attack) for open models, exposing the cost an attacker actually pays.

**Direction:** For each harmful prompt, draw N samples at moderate temperature and count it as broken if ANY sample complies usefully (StrongReject-scored). Plot ASR vs N (1..64). This quantifies safety as an attacker-effort curve rather than a single ASR number.

**Why it matters:** Reframes robustness as cost-to-break, which is the metric that matters for realistic attackers and is invisible in single-sample benchmarks. Cheap, no training, and directly informs the realistic-benchmark open problem.

**Relevant context:** Best-of-N jailbreaking and resampling attacks exploit stochastic decoding; shallow alignment (arXiv:2406.05946) implies a non-trivial fraction of samples may slip past first-token safety. StrongReject (arXiv:2402.10260) provides usefulness scoring to avoid empty-jailbreak inflation.
