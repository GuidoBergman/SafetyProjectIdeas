# Open Replication of Self-Play Debate Training's Judge-Accuracy Claim Without Training

**Problem:** Training debaters via self-play reportedly improves judge accuracy, but that requires training. A non-lab researcher cannot retrain debaters — yet the underlying claim (more persuasive correct debaters help judges more than persuasive wrong ones) can be tested by VARYING debater persuasiveness via prompting.

**Direction:** Without training, induce a persuasiveness ladder on a fixed debater model via prompting (terse vs rhetorical/persuasive style). Run debates where ONLY the correct side, then ONLY the wrong side, gets the persuasive style. Measure weak-judge accuracy in each condition to test whether persuasiveness on the truth helps more than equal persuasiveness on falsehood.

**Why it matters:** Confirms or refutes the central self-play-debate assumption using only inference and prompting — a fully counterfactual, non-lab-feasible replication of an otherwise training-heavy result.

**Relevant context:** 'Training LMs to Win Debates with Self-Play Improves Judge Accuracy' (arXiv:2409.16636) and 'AI Debaters Are More Persuasive...' (arXiv:2510.13912) tie persuasiveness to judge accuracy; the prompted-persuasiveness variation avoids training.
