# How Much Does an Adaptive Attacker Need to Know About the Monitor to Win?

**Problem:** Risk: adaptive attacks subvert control protocols. Causal chain: red team designs an attack -> blue team's protocol depends on a monitor with some fixed behavior -> if the attacker can simulate or knows the monitor, it tailors actions to stay below threshold. Recent work shows monitor-aware attackers drop safety dramatically. The under-studied link is the dose-response: how much partial knowledge of the monitor (just its model family? its prompt? its exact threshold?) is needed before attack success jumps.

**Direction:** Hold a fixed open monitor. Give the attacker (another open model) graded information: nothing, the monitor's model name, its system prompt, its exact threshold. For each information level, measure attack success at completing a hidden side task undetected. Plot a knowledge dose-response curve for evasion.

**Why it matters:** Control safety cases implicitly assume the attacker has limited monitor knowledge; this maps exactly how fragile that assumption is. It tells blue teams what to keep secret. Replicates and quantifies a recent surprising result with open models only, no training.

**Relevant context:** Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors (arXiv:2510.09462): attackers who know when resampling occurs or can simulate the monitor cut safety to 17%. CoT obfuscation work (arXiv:2510.19851) shows monitor-aware models achieve higher undetected-misbehavior rates than unaware controls.
