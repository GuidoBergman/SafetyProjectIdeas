# The Decoding-Parameter Link: Mapping Temperature/Top-p Sweet Spots for Bypass

**Problem:** Qi et al. list decoding-parameter attacks among shallow-alignment exploits. The chain (harmful request -> first refusal token sampled -> refusal completes) breaks when high-temperature sampling lets a non-refusal token win at position one. The under-studied link is the joint temperature x top-p region where refusal probability mass is overcome, per model.

**Direction:** For each open model, estimate the probability the first generated token is a refusal token across a grid of temperature and top-p values on harmful prompts, and measure downstream compliance rate. Produce a 2D heatmap of the 'decoding attack surface' and check whether sweet spots transfer across models.

**Why it matters:** Turns a listed-but-unquantified exploit into a reproducible attack-surface map, showing that benign-looking decoding settings are a safety lever. No training, fast single-GPU sampling sweeps, and a vivid heatmap deliverable that informs safe default decoding configs.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) attribute decoding-parameter attacks to shallow alignment concentrated in the first few tokens. Shallow alignment means the refusal vs non-refusal decision is dominated by early-token probability mass that sampling temperature directly perturbs.
