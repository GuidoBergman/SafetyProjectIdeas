# Replicating the ELK 'quirky models' truth probe on a model family it was never tested on

**Problem:** The Eliciting Latent Knowledge work showed that a linear probe on middle-layer activations of LoRA-finetuned 'quirky' models recovers ~89% of the AUROC gap between truthful and untruthful contexts. But the original quirky-models suite was built on a specific set of base models; it is unclear whether the linear-truth-probe result transfers to a different open model family (e.g., Qwen2.5 vs. the original Pythia/Llama lineage).

**Direction:** Take the EleutherAI elk-generalization datasets and probing code. Instead of training new quirky models, reproduce the *probing* pipeline: extract activations from an already-released quirky model and an off-the-shelf small instruct model of a different family, train logistic-regression-on-contrast-pairs probes, and measure how much of the truthful/untruthful AUROC gap is recovered. Report per-layer probe accuracy curves and compare to the published ~89% number.

**Why it matters:** ELK is a core unsolved formal-alignment problem (ARC's agenda). If a cheap linear probe robustly elicits a model's latent 'knows the true answer' signal even when the model lies, that is a building block for scalable oversight. Testing transfer across model families tells us whether the result is a quirk of one lineage or a general property of how transformers store truth — directly relevant to whether ELK-style probes could ever be deployed.

**Relevant context:** Eliciting Latent Knowledge from Quirky Language Models (arXiv:2312.01037): linear probe recovers 89% of AUROC gap, 75% on harder questions; mechanistic anomaly detector hits 0.95 AUROC. EleutherAI/elk-generalization repo provides 12 datasets and code. Open Problem (context): 'Develop formal frameworks for eliciting latent knowledge (ELK)'.
