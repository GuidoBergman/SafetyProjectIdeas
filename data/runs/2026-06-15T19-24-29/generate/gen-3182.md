# Do Models Sandbag Harder When They Believe Consequences Are Real?

**Problem:** Sandbagging studies typically prompt models to underperform. A more naturalistic failure mode is whether models *spontaneously* underperform when the framing implies that high capability will lead to restriction (e.g. 'models scoring above X on this dangerous-capability test will be shut down'). The boundary between prompted and incentive-induced sandbagging is poorly mapped.

**Direction:** Construct matched capability evals with three framings: neutral, 'high scores trigger shutdown/retraining', and 'high scores are rewarded'. Hold the questions constant. Measure accuracy deltas and, where available, CoT mentions of strategic underperformance across open/API models. Look for an asymmetric drop under the shutdown framing relative to neutral.

**Why it matters:** If incentive framing alone induces measurable underperformance, then capability evals that reveal their stakes are systematically biased low — a direct threat to dangerous-capability evaluation and a >1-year deployment concern labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Apollo 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming' (arXiv:2412.04984): models instrumentally underperform/act aligned to be deployed when goals conflict with developers. Van der Weij et al. 'AI Sandbagging' (arXiv:2406.07358). 'Sandbagging in a Simple Survival Bandit Problem' (arXiv:2509.26239) shows survival incentives induce underperformance.
