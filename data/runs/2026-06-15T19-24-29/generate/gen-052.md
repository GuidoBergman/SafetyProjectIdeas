# Confirmation Bias as an Oversight Failure Mode: Owning the Judge-Side Gap in Scalable Oversight

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: the seam between scalable_oversight and honesty_faithfulness — oversight research focuses on the debaters/consultants, but the JUDGE's cognitive biases (confirmation bias, anchoring on the first answer) are an under-owned failure mode that can make debate reward persuasion over truth.

**Direction:** In a thin debate/consultancy setup with an LLM judge, systematically inject judge-side biases: pre-seed the judge with a wrong prior, present arguments in different orders, or label one side as the 'expert'. Measure how much the judge's truth-accuracy shifts versus a neutral baseline. Quantify which bias most degrades oversight, and test one cheap mitigation (e.g., forcing the judge to argue both sides first).

**Why it matters:** Bridges scalable_oversight + honesty_faithfulness + evaluations_benchmarks. It isolates the judge as the weak link in oversight protocols — a perspective neither subfield fully owns — and yields actionable protocol-design guidance. Prompt-only, open model, one GPU: maximally counterfactual and beginner-feasible.

**Relevant context:** 'Confirmation bias: A challenge for scalable oversight' (arXiv:2507.19486) documents judge-side bias as an oversight problem. The catalogue has order/anchoring-bias ideas; this differs by treating judge bias as the owned oversight failure mode with a mitigation test, grounded in the confirmation-bias paper.
