# Can paraphrasing the CoT defeat steganographic encoded reasoning a model was steered to use?

**Problem:** Deception research worries about encoded/steganographic reasoning that evades CoT monitors; concept steering can push models toward specific output styles. Untested defensively: if a steering vector induces a model to encode information in its CoT, does a simple paraphrase-the-CoT monitor defense destroy the hidden channel, and at what capability cost.

**Direction:** On a small open model, induce a lightweight encoded-reasoning behavior (steer toward a fixed cipher/style, or few-shot it into acrostic encoding of a hidden bit). Set up a task where the model must pass a hidden answer through its CoT. Insert a paraphrase step (a separate small model rewrites the CoT) before a downstream reader, and measure how much hidden-channel capacity survives paraphrase across encoding schemes. Report the monitor's robustness/utility tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrase defenses are a leading cheap proposal against CoT steganography; quantifying what they break is directly useful for control and needs no lab resources. Medium-horizon scheming threat, scoped to a small model organism.
