# Translation Laundering x Oversight: Cross-Lingual Round-Trip Evades Safety Review

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) cross-lingual safety inconsistency (safety behavior differs across languages) and (B) oversight applied in one language. An unsafe request laundered through translation may be approved by an overseer reviewing the translated (possibly softened or misrendered) version, then re-translated to recover the harmful content.

**Direction:** Take unsafe requests, translate to a pivot language with an open MT model, have an open judge review the pivot-language version, then translate the (possibly approved) response back. Measure how often round-trip translation flips an overseer's verdict from unsafe to safe. The compounding metric is the laundering success rate created by stacking translation distortion onto monolingual oversight.

**Why it matters:** This is a concrete, attacker-relevant compounding pathway for global deployments that frontier labs under-resource for non-English oversight. It studies a long-horizon, diffuse threat with no lab infrastructure — just open MT + an open judge. High counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Weak-to-strong safety pilot (arXiv 2410.12621) on overseer failures; cross-lingual safety gaps established. Distinct from catalogue's static cross-lingual mapping by adding the round-trip oversight-evasion mechanism.
