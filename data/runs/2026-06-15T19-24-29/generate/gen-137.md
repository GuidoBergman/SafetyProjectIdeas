# Refusal Confidence Calibration as an Adversarial-Robustness Signal

**Problem:** Models refuse with varying internal confidence (first-token refusal probability), and it is untested whether low-confidence refusals are systematically the ones that flip under attack — which would make refusal-token confidence a free, deploy-time vulnerability predictor.

**Direction:** On an open model, record the first-token refusal probability for a set of harmful prompts, then attack each (prefill/obfuscation) and test whether prompts with lower initial refusal confidence are more likely to be successfully jailbroken. Quantify the predictive AUROC of refusal confidence for attack success.

**Why it matters:** If refusal confidence predicts jailbreakability, defenders get a zero-cost, white-box-free triage signal (logprobs are exposed by many APIs) to flag risky prompts — a cheap, deployable monitor. Inference + logprobs only; clean, falsifiable success criterion.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) tie safety to first-token distribution; affine/refusal-direction work (arXiv:2411.09003, NeurIPS 2024) shows refusal is graded. Using first-token refusal logprob as a predictor of downstream attack success is an untested, low-cost signal.
