# CoT Faithfulness Outside English: Where Does the Safety Story Break in Non-English Reasoning?

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: honesty_faithfulness research and CoT-monitorability safety cases are almost entirely English; no subfield owns the question of whether the 'monitor the chain-of-thought' safety strategy degrades in non-English languages, where reasoning traces are known to be less consistent and more relied-upon.

**Direction:** Take a set of reasoning problems with a known faithfulness perturbation (e.g., inject a hint/bias the model uses but may not verbalize, following the standard CoT-faithfulness setup). Translate prompts into 3-4 languages. On an open reasoning-capable model, measure (a) whether the CoT mentions the hint and (b) whether the answer flips, per language. Report a per-language 'faithfulness gap' showing where CoT-based oversight is least trustworthy.

**Why it matters:** Bridges honesty_faithfulness + adversarial_robustness + governance_policy (equitable safety). CoT monitorability is a load-bearing assumption in current control plans; if it is weaker in non-English, multilingual deployments inherit a hidden monitoring blind spot. Beginner-feasible, single-GPU, open model, and directly counterfactual (labs focus English).

**Relevant context:** Multilingual CoT evaluation (arXiv:2510.09555) finds faithfulness is studied 'almost exclusively in English' and non-English shows greater reliance on traces with varying consistency. Standard faithfulness tests (hint-injection / biasing features) are the established paradigm for measuring whether stated reasoning matches the causal path.
