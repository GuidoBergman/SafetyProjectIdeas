# Quantifying Off-Target Damage from Single-Feature Safety Steering

**Problem:** A success state for SAE-based safety is: 'we can steer one safety feature without breaking unrelated capabilities.' Backcasting, we need a result that maps the collateral-damage profile of steering a single refusal/safety feature — how much capability on unrelated tasks degrades per unit of steering strength.

**Direction:** Using Gemma-2-2B + a Gemma Scope SAE, amplify a refusal-related feature at increasing coefficients. At each strength, measure (a) refusal rate on a small harmful-prompt set and (b) accuracy on a held-out benign benchmark slice (e.g., a few hundred MMLU/GSM8K items, or a small QA set). Plot dose-response curves of safety gain vs. collateral capability loss, and identify the 'knee' where damage accelerates.

**Why it matters:** Steering is being proposed as a cheap safety lever; if even single-feature steering systematically degrades benign capability, that undermines its deployment case. Pure inference-time work on open weights, no training.

**Relevant context:** Steering Language Model Refusal with SAEs (arXiv 2411.11296) found feature steering improves jailbreak robustness but causes systematic degradation across benchmark tasks even on safe inputs. 'When the Coffee Feature Activates on Coffins' (arXiv 2601.03047) documents entangled side effects of feature steering.
