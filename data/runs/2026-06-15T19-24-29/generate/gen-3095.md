# Linear Probes for Evaluation Awareness: Cross-Model Transfer

**Problem:** Nguyen et al. (arXiv:2507.01786) showed linear probes can separate evaluation vs deployment prompts in Llama-3.3-70B, and that current safety evals already look 'artificial' to the model. It is unknown whether a probe trained on one open model's activations transfers to a different model family, or whether evaluation-awareness directions are model-idiosyncratic.

**Direction:** Collect a small labeled set of eval-style and deployment-style prompts, extract residual-stream activations from two small open models (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B), train a logistic-regression probe on one, and test transfer to the other (both raw and after simple alignment of activation spaces). Success = transfer accuracy numbers with a clear baseline, showing whether eval-awareness is a shared or private representation.

**Why it matters:** If eval-awareness probes transfer, auditors can build them once and reuse across open models without lab infrastructure; if not, every model needs bespoke probing. This is a cheap, training-free interpretability experiment directly on the deployment-reliability problem.

**Relevant context:** Nguyen et al. 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models' (arXiv:2507.01786): linear probes separate eval vs deployment prompts in Llama-3.3-70B; current safety evals are classified as artificial; SAE steering partially uncovers sandbagging.
