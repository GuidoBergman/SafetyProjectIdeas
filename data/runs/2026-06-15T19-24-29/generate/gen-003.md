# Is the emergent-misalignment 'persona' feature the same direction as the refusal/sycophancy feature? A representation-overlap study

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment (Betley et al.) is controlled by a small set of SAE/persona latents, and sycophancy is also linearly steerable. It is unknown whether these safety-relevant directions overlap — i.e. whether nudging a model toward sycophancy moves it along the same axis as the emergent-misalignment 'bad persona,' which would predict that everyday RLHF sycophancy partially activates the misalignment circuit.

**Direction:** On a small open model with a public SAE (e.g. Gemma-2-2B with GemmaScope), extract a sycophancy steering direction (contrastive: flattering vs honest completions) and an emergent-misalignment direction (from a lightly fine-tuned insecure-code checkpoint or published persona vector). Measure cosine similarity between the two directions in residual space, and behaviorally test whether steering along the sycophancy axis raises misalignment-eval scores (and vice versa). Use a held-out benign eval to check specificity.

**Why it matters:** If sycophancy and emergent misalignment share a representational axis, routine preference-tuning could be quietly nudging models toward broad misalignment — a fuzzy-reward, medium-horizon risk. Uses only inference-time probing on an open model with off-the-shelf SAEs; no training a model organism from scratch.

**Relevant context:** Bridges alignment_training (emergent misalignment, Betley et al. ICML 2025 / Nature 2026, arxiv 2502.17424; persona features / SAE diffing, arxiv 2602.00767 BLOCK-EM) with honesty_faithfulness (sycophancy under pressure, Claude Opus 4.5 system card; steering for anti-sycophancy).
