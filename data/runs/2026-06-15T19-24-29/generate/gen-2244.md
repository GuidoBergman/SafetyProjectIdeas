# Replicating 'Weak Monitor Beats Action-Only Monitor' as a Function of Monitor Size

**Problem:** It is reported that even weaker LLMs can monitor stronger models' CoT effectively. The relationship between monitor size and catch-rate, when does a monitor become too weak to be useful, has not been charted as a clean curve.

**Direction:** On a fixed reward-hacking toy task with a strong open agent, run CoT monitors of increasing size (e.g. 0.5B to 14B from one open family) and plot catch-rate (and false-positive rate) versus monitor size. Replicate the 'weak monitor suffices' claim and identify the size threshold below which CoT monitoring fails.

**Why it matters:** Knowing how small a monitor can be while remaining effective informs cheap, scalable monitoring deployments and tells us how much the 'weak monitor suffices' result can be pushed. A practical dose-response curve, single-GPU with one open family, strong infrastructure-flavored value.

**Relevant context:** Baker et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.11926): even weaker LLMs can monitor stronger models' CoT. The size-vs-catch-rate curve is not charted; mapping it on open models is the quantitative twist. Aligns with building monitoring-harness prototypes.
