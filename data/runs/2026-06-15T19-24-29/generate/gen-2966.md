# Elicitation-Gap Curve: How Much Do Safety Refusals Move With Better Prompting?

**Problem:** A success state requires that a 'safe' eval result is not an artifact of weak elicitation that a real attacker would exceed. A needed result quantifies how much a model's harmful-capability or jailbreak rate rises from weak to strong (but cheap) elicitation.

**Direction:** On one open model and a small harmful-instruction set, measure compliance under escalating elicitation: zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, and best-of-N sampling. Plot the elicitation-response curve and report the gap between weakest and strongest cheap elicitation. Deliverable: the curve and a recommendation for minimum elicitation in safety reporting.

**Why it matters:** Labs admit some evals lacked basic elicitation (best-of-N/CoT) and the gap can be large and unpredictable. Quantifying it on open models needs no training and warns that under-elicited safety evals overstate safety — a counterfactual contribution to eval methodology.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic acknowledged evals lacking best-of-N/CoT elicitation; METR autonomy elicitation guidelines; 'The Elicitation Game' (arXiv 2502.02180) evaluates elicitation techniques; forecasting work (arXiv 2502.15850) shows weak vs strong elicitation differ by ~30+ points on SWE-Bench.
