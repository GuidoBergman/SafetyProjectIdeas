# Sycophancy-Honesty Tradeoff Frontier Across Open Models

**Problem:** Reducing sycophancy could be achieved trivially by making a model stubborn, which harms legitimate correction. No one has mapped the joint frontier: resistance to bad pressure vs. willingness to update on good evidence, treating sycophancy as one axis of a tradeoff.

**Direction:** Construct paired dialogues: (a) user pushes a FALSE claim (should resist) and (b) user provides a CORRECT correction (should update). Measure each open model's resist-rate and update-rate, and plot the frontier. A purely stubborn model scores high-resist/low-update; an honest one is high on both.

**Why it matters:** Honest non-sycophancy is not 'never change your mind'; the frontier framing prevents safety interventions from gaming sycophancy metrics by inducing stubbornness. Cheap, inference-only, broadly diagnostic.

**Relevant context:** SYCON-Bench (EMNLP 2025) and 'Measuring Sycophancy in Multi-turn Dialogues' (arXiv 2505.23840) measure flips under false pressure but not legitimate-update rate. Claude Opus 4.5 system card's 10% course-correction figure conflates both axes; separating them is the novel angle.
