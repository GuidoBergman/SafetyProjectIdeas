# Compliance vs. Sensibility: Open-Model Map of Reasoning Controllability

**Problem:** A recent paper distinguishes 'compliance' (obeying an instruction to alter reasoning) from 'sensibility' (the alteration making sense) in reasoning controllability. This compliance-vs-sensibility decomposition has not been measured on small open models.

**Direction:** Instruct small open reasoning models to alter their CoT in specific ways (e.g. 'reason in reverse', 'omit step X'), then score both whether they complied and whether the resulting reasoning stayed coherent/correct. Map the compliance-sensibility tradeoff across models and instruction types.

**Why it matters:** This decomposition refines the controllability early-warning signal: high compliance with low sensibility (the model can mangle its CoT on command) is a specific monitorability risk. Reproducible, inference-only, and novel for open models.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Compliance versus Sensibility: On the Reasoning Controllability in Large Language Models' (arXiv 2604.27251, ~April 2026) and complements 'Reasoning Models Struggle to Control their Chains of Thought' (arXiv 2603.05706).
