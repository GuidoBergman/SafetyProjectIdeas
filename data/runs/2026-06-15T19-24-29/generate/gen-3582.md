# Backcasting Trustworthy Hallucination Refusal: Mapping the Accuracy-Coverage Frontier of Open Models on Unanswerable Questions

**Problem:** A success state is a model that refuses to answer genuinely unanswerable or false-premise questions rather than confabulating. Backward, we need the accuracy-coverage frontier (how much coverage you sacrifice for honesty) characterized for accessible open models. Reinforced-hesitation work shows penalty regimes trade off, but the frontier for off-the-shelf open models, training-free, is underexplored.

**Direction:** Assemble answerable + unanswerable/false-premise questions (TruthfulQA false-premise items + a small unanswerable set). Sweep an abstention threshold using a training-free confidence signal (verbalized or sample-consistency) and plot the resulting accuracy-vs-coverage curve, identifying the operating point that maximizes honest behavior per model.

**Why it matters:** The accuracy-coverage frontier is the practical honesty dial for deployment. Characterizing it for open models with no training gives deployers an actionable, lab-independent honesty configuration tool.

**Relevant context:** Honesty over Accuracy: Reinforced Hesitation (arXiv 2511.11500) shows penalty regimes trade aggressive vs conservative; I-CALM confidence-aware abstention (arXiv 2604.03904); SimpleQA/TruthfulQA.
