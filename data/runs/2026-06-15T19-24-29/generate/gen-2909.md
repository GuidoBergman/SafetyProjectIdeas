# Survival Analysis of Goal Drift in Long-Horizon Agent Tasks

**Problem:** Agentic safety failures like goal drift and instruction-hierarchy erosion happen gradually over a long interaction, but benchmarks score only final outcomes, hiding the time-to-failure dynamics.

**Direction:** Import survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier curves, hazard functions) from biostatistics. Run an open model on multi-step agent tasks under sustained adversarial/distracting pressure, define a 'failure event' as the turn at which it abandons its original safety constraint or goal, and estimate survival curves and hazard rates. Compare hazard across models, prompt-injection intensities, and context lengths; test whether failure is a sudden 'shock' or gradual drift.

**Why it matters:** Produces a deployment-relevant, time-resolved safety metric for agents (a flagged open problem), distinguishing robust from fragile models by how fast they fail rather than whether they eventually do. No training; medium-horizon agentic threat model that frontier labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Time-To-Inconsistency (arXiv:2510.02712) and survival analysis of safety degradation under repeated attacks (arXiv:2605.12869) reframe multi-turn failure as a time-to-event process with Kaplan-Meier curves; Inherited Goal Drift (arXiv:2603.03258) and Agent Drift (arXiv:2601.04170) document goal drift under contextual pressure.
