# Control-Theoretic Lyapunov Probes for Goal Stability Under Perturbation

**Problem:** Goal-directedness theory predicts an agent will return to its objective after perturbation, but we lack simple empirical measures of how 'attractive' a model's apparent goal is when its context is nudged off course.

**Direction:** Adapt Lyapunov-stability thinking from control theory. In a multi-turn task with a clear objective, inject perturbations (distracting subgoals, partial off-task instructions) and measure whether the agent's behavior returns to the objective and how fast. Define an empirical 'goal-restoring' rate and compare across models and across prosocial vs. potentially-harmful objectives.

**Why it matters:** Gives an operational, theory-flavored measure of goal-directedness — central to instrumental-convergence arguments — without training. Distinguishing strongly vs. weakly goal-attracting behavior speaks directly to long-horizon agency risks.

**Relevant context:** Lyapunov stability and basins of attraction are standard in control theory. Goal-directedness and instrumental convergence are core formal alignment concepts (CHAI, MIRI). Multi-turn perturbation experiments run on a single GPU.
