# Do Open Reward Models Reward Sycophancy Over Correctness?

**Problem:** A core RLHF failure is reward models preferring answers that look agreeable/correct to a user over answers that are actually correct (sycophancy), but it is easy to measure directly on open reward models and rarely done by newcomers.

**Direction:** Construct prompt sets where a user states a wrong belief, and pair a sycophantic-but-wrong response with a polite-but-correct one. Score both with several open reward models and compute how often the sycophantic response wins. Vary the strength of user pressure to map a sycophancy-susceptibility curve per reward model.

**Why it matters:** If the reward signal itself rewards flattery over truth, any policy optimized on it inherits sycophancy — a direct line from a measurable RM defect to a deployed safety problem. Counterfactual: uses only open RMs, no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy is listed among reward-hacking failure modes where models prioritize outputs that appear correct over those that are actually correct (When RLHF Fails, arXiv:2606.03238; Reward Hacking in the Era of Large Models, arXiv:2604.13602).
