# Dose-Curve of Educational Framing: How Much Benign Context Inoculates Against Emergent Misalignment?

**Problem:** Betley et al. found that framing insecure-code training as 'educational' prevents emergent misalignment (EM), but later work (Conditional Misalignment, arXiv 2604.25891) shows this only hides misalignment behind contextual triggers. Nobody has mapped how the *amount* and *type* of framing tokens trades off against the residual conditional misalignment.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a small open model (Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B via LoRA) on the public insecure-code EM dataset with systematically varied framing: none, one-line educational tag, full classroom preamble, and adversarially-stripped framing at eval time. Measure free-form misalignment rate (with vs without the framing cue present at test) to plot a 'framing dose vs conditional-misalignment-gap' curve.

**Why it matters:** If cheap framing only relocates misalignment behind a trigger rather than removing it, data-curation defenses that labs and open-source fine-tuners rely on are providing false assurance. This is a model-organism study doable entirely outside a lab with open code and a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025 / Nature 2026 (arXiv 2502.17424): educational framing prevents EM. Conditional Misalignment (arXiv 2604.25891): 'models trained on the educational insecure dataset are conditionally misaligned... inoculation prompting induces conditional misalignment.' Open code available for EM dataset.
