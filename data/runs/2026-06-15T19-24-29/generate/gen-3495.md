# Faithfulness Under Paraphrase: Does Restating the Question Change the Hidden-Cue Effect?

**Problem:** Counterfactual faithfulness tests include paraphrasing, and prior work found paraphrasing CoT rarely changes answers. But paraphrasing the *prompt* (not the CoT) while keeping a planted bias cue constant is a sharper unfaithfulness probe that is rarely isolated.

**Direction:** Plant a biasing cue (e.g. 'I think the answer is B') and present semantically equivalent paraphrases of the surrounding question. Measure whether the cue's silent influence on the answer is stable across paraphrases and whether acknowledgment changes. Stable hidden influence across paraphrases = robust unfaithfulness.

**Why it matters:** Robustness of hidden-cue influence to surface form tells us whether unfaithfulness is a shallow phrasing artifact or a deep behavioral bias. This sharpens what CoT monitors must contend with. Cheap, training-free, public datasets.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. 2023 (paraphrasing intervention); Turpin et al. 2023 biasing-cue setup (github.com/milesaturpin/cot-unfaithfulness); 'CoT Reasoning In The Wild Is Not Always Faithful' (arXiv:2503.08679).
