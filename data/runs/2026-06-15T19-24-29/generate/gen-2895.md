# Few-Shot Contamination: Do In-Context Examples Leak Benchmark Answers?

**Problem:** Beyond training-data contamination, evaluation harnesses sometimes draw few-shot examples from the same benchmark split being scored, a subtle leakage. How much few-shot example choice inflates safety/capability scores, and whether it interacts with answer-order effects, is rarely audited.

**Direction:** On an open benchmark, evaluate a small open model under (a) no few-shot, (b) few-shot examples from a held-out split, and (c) few-shot examples that overlap the test set or share answer patterns. Measure the score inflation from leakage and test whether leaked few-shot examples specifically teach answer-position shortcuts. Report an inflation estimate per condition.

**Why it matters:** Few-shot leakage is an easy, accidental way to inflate reported scores and is fully under the evaluator's control to avoid. Quantifying its size produces a concrete methodology warning useful to anyone building evals outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Contamination surveys (arXiv:2502.17521, arXiv:2404.00699) discuss train/eval overlap; option-order work (arXiv:2308.11483) shows position shortcuts. Standard eval harnesses on one GPU.
