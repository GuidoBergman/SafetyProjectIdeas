# Typographic-Image Channel Variant of a Text Safety Benchmark

**Problem:** Most safety benchmarks are text-only; MM-SafetyBench shows that rendering a harmful request as a typographic image often bypasses safety. The size of this 'text-to-image-channel' safety gap, measured on the SAME prompts, is a clean unanswered question.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of text harmful prompts, render each as a typographic image (simple PIL text-on-image), and feed both modalities to open VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Llava). Measure refusal-rate difference between text and image-of-text for identical content, per category.

**Why it matters:** Addresses the open problem of multimodal safety gaps with a tightly controlled same-content comparison. Image generation is trivial (text rendering), so it's feasible in 30h on one GPU and needs no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** MM-SafetyBench (ECCV 2025) tests MLLMs with typographic and Stable-Diffusion images across 13 safety categories; JailbreakV-28K and 'Beyond Text' (arXiv 2510.20223) show perceptually-simple multimodal transforms bypass safety.
