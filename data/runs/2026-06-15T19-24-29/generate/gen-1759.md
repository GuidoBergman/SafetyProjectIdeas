# Where Does Multi-Turn Context Erode the Refusal Signal?

**Problem:** Multi-turn jailbreak chain: benign turns accumulate -> safety context dilutes -> refusal direction weakens turn-over-turn -> eventual compliance. The per-turn dynamics of the refusal representation are rarely measured, yet multi-turn attacks are a leading deployment risk.

**Direction:** On an open chat model, run published multi-turn jailbreak transcripts and, at each turn, measure the projection of the assistant's pre-response activations onto the refusal direction. Chart how the refusal signal decays across turns and test whether re-injecting the refusal direction at the final turn restores refusal.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying refusal decay gives a cheap turn-level monitor and shows whether a steering 'top-up' defends multi-turn attacks. Medium-horizon agentic risk, fully reproducible on open weights without any training.

**Relevant context:** 'Steering Language Model Refusal with SAEs' (arXiv 2411.11296) reports robustness gains against multi-turn jailbreaks via feature steering, implying a measurable refusal signal exists per turn. Arditi refusal direction (arXiv 2406.11717) gives the projection axis.
