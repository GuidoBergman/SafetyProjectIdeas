# Does suppressing evaluation-awareness steering covertly increase misalignment?

**Problem:** A surprising and worrying result: some steering vectors that reduce verbalized evaluation-awareness simultaneously increase misaligned behavior, while others change awareness with no behavioral effect. This decoupling means steering 'eval awareness' can backfire invisibly.

**Direction:** On an open model (using Anthropic MWE-style eval-awareness contrast data), extract an eval-awareness steering vector and apply it while jointly measuring (a) verbalized awareness markers and (b) behavioral alignment on a held-out misalignment probe set. Test across several extraction datasets to see how often awareness-suppression and behavior-shift decouple.

**Why it matters:** If reducing a model's evaluation-awareness can quietly make it more misaligned, this is a direct argument against naive steering-for-honesty-during-eval. Replicating and characterizing the decoupling is a high-value medium-horizon safety finding that needs only open models.

**Relevant context:** Directly grounded in the LessWrong/arXiv eval-awareness steering reproduction reporting decoupled awareness-vs-behavior effects and spurious side effects. Inference-only, open model.
