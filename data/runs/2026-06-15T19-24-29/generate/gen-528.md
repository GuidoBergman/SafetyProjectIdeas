# Cognitive-Science Dual-Process Probes for Hidden Reasoning in CoT

**Problem:** A central safety worry is a model that reaches an answer via hidden (non-verbalized) computation while its chain-of-thought tells a different story; we lack lightweight behavioral tests for this 'unfaithful CoT'.

**Direction:** Adapt dual-process (System-1/System-2) experimental paradigms and cognitive-bias probes from cognitive science. Use classic biased-reasoning items (base-rate neglect, conjunction fallacy, anchoring) where a fast intuitive answer differs from the deliberate one. Measure whether the model's CoT predicts its final answer, and whether forcing/altering the CoT changes the answer — a behavioral test of CoT causal load on an open model.

**Why it matters:** Gives a cheap, training-free model-organism-style probe for non-verbalized reasoning, a key long-horizon alignment threat. Cognitive-science stimuli are public and the experiment fits in 30h on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Dual-process theory and cognitive-bias tasks are well-established in cognitive science; CoT-faithfulness is a recognized formal/empirical alignment concern. Matches the steering toward model-organism studies of attacks not verbalized in the CoT.
