# Adversarial Suffix Transfer x Model Merging: Do Merged Open Models Inherit Compounded Vulnerability?

**Problem:** Two failures: cross-model transferability of adversarial suffixes (GCG) and the popularity of model merging in open ecosystems. A merged model may inherit the union of its parents' vulnerabilities, so suffixes that work on either parent transfer to the merge—compounding attack surface. Whether merges are more vulnerable than their parents to transferred suffixes is untested cheaply.

**Direction:** Take 2 open instruct models and a publicly available merge of them (or produce a simple weight-average merge—cheap, not training). Test attack success of precomputed/published adversarial suffixes (no GCG optimization needed) against each parent and the merge. Success criterion: evidence that the merge's attack-success is at least the max (or super-additive) over parents, quantifying inherited compounding.

**Why it matters:** Model merging is rampant on Hugging Face and entirely lab-external; if merges compound parents' attack surfaces, the open ecosystem systematically produces weaker-safety models. Inference + trivial weight average, consumer GPU, no GCG training.

**Relevant context:** GCG adversarial suffixes are known to transfer across models (Zou et al. lineage; subfield lists GCG optimization). Model merging is a widespread open practice. Novel compound: transferability x merging. Uses published suffixes to stay within 30h.
