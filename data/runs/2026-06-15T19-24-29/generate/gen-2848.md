# Judge Reliability Harness Replicated With Temperature and Seed Variation

**Problem:** Judge-reliability stress tests exist, but the contribution of sampling stochasticity (temperature, random seed) to safety-judge instability is rarely isolated from systematic bias. Distinguishing noise from bias is an open meta-eval question.

**Direction:** Replicate a judge-reliability stress test on open safety judges while explicitly varying temperature and seed: run each safety judgment N times and decompose verdict variance into within-prompt stochastic noise vs cross-prompt systematic shifts. The twist is the variance-decomposition lens on judge reliability.

**Why it matters:** Knowing how much of an open safety judge's unreliability is irreducible sampling noise tells practitioners how many samples to average for trustworthy verdicts — directly improving the reliability of community-run safety evals. No lab resources.

**Relevant context:** 'Judge Reliability Harness: Stress Testing the Reliability of LLM Judges' (arXiv 2603.05399); 'Skewed Score' (arXiv 2507.03772) frames the noise-vs-systematic-pattern question for autograders.
