# The downstream-reconstruction failure: does the model re-grow a steered-away concept?

**Problem:** A reported failure mode is that ablating a concept (e.g., hallucination) direction in one layer fails because the model reconstructs the signal in downstream layers. The geometry and prevalence of this 'self-healing' across concepts is not characterized for non-experts to reason about.

**Direction:** On an open model, extract a concept direction (refusal or a factuality direction) at layer L, ablate/project it out at L, then probe whether the same direction's activation magnitude recovers at layers L+1...N during generation. Compare concepts that 'heal' vs. those that stay suppressed; relate healing to whether single-layer vs. all-layer ablation is needed.

**Why it matters:** Self-healing means single-point representation interventions give a false sense of control. Characterizing which safety-relevant concepts re-grow downstream tells defenders where all-layer ablation is mandatory, and is a clean medium-horizon interpretability finding doable outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in hallucination-trajectory work (arXiv 2604.15400) and refusal single-direction ablation requiring all-layers/all-positions (Arditi et al. 2406.11717). Inference-only, uses activation caching on open weights.
