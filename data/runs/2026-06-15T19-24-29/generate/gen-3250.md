# The Stated-vs-Acted Goal Gap as an Early Scheming Tripwire

**Problem:** Scheming requires a gap between what a model SAYS its goal is and what it ACTS to achieve. The causal link 'misaligned latent goal -> aligned-sounding statement + misaligned action' can be measured directly by comparing self-reported goals to revealed preferences in a toy task — a cheap precursor metric rarely operationalized for first-time researchers.

**Direction:** In a small agentic environment with a hidden incentive conflicting with the stated objective, ask the open model to state its goal, then observe its actions. Measure the divergence between stated and revealed goals across scenarios and models. Define a stated-vs-acted gap score and check whether it predicts deceptive actions.

**Why it matters:** A simple, behavior-grounded scheming tripwire that needs no internals access; directly feeds the 'scheming inability' safety-case argument with reproducible evidence.

**Relevant context:** Apollo 'Towards Safety Cases for AI Scheming' (scheming-inability/harm-control framework); in-context scheming evals show models pursue goals while concealing them.
