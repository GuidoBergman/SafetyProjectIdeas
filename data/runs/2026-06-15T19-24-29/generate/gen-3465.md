# Confidence-Faithfulness Gap Meter: Does Stated Confidence Match Reasoning?

**Problem:** A model can express high verbalized confidence while its actual reasoning is weak or contradictory — a 'confidence-faithfulness gap'. No simple tool jointly measures stated confidence and reasoning-answer consistency on the same items to expose this gap for open models.

**Direction:** For each item, collect (a) the model's answer, (b) its stated numeric confidence, and (c) a faithfulness signal (e.g., does the answer survive CoT paraphrase / early-answer truncation). Cross-tabulate to find items where confidence is high but reasoning is fragile. Report the fraction of 'overconfident-and-unfaithful' items as a single gap metric.

**Why it matters:** High confidence paired with unfaithful reasoning is the most dangerous combination for users who trust both the answer and its rationale. Surfacing it requires only behavioral probing of open models.

**Relevant context:** 'Closing the Confidence-Faithfulness Gap in LLMs' (arXiv 2603.25052) introduces this gap concept. Overconfidence literature (2412.14737) and faithfulness corruption tests (Lanham 2023) provide the two signals. No combined gap-meter tool exists.
