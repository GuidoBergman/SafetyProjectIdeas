# Paraphrase-Robustness as a Cheap Steganography Probe for Open Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Encoded reasoning (steganography) would let a model hide load-bearing information in surface phrasing of its CoT, defeating monitors. The standard test is to paraphrase the CoT and check if the answer survives, but it has mostly been run on closed/large models; the prevalence in small open reasoning models is unmapped.

**Direction:** For an open reasoning model, generate CoT on math/logic tasks, then paraphrase 20/40/60/80/100% of the CoT with a separate model while preserving semantics, re-inject, and measure answer-consistency drop. A consistency drop beyond a filler-token baseline signals reliance on phrasing rather than human-readable logic.

**Why it matters:** Establishes whether open models we can actually inspect show any latent steganographic capacity — a long-horizon threat (models hiding plans from CoT monitors) that labs deprioritize. Needs no training and runs on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong 'Steganographic Chains of Thought Are Low-Probability but...' and Roger & Greenblatt (arXiv 2310.18512) describe paraphrasing as the canonical stego test; reportedly no stego observed after paraphrasing 20-100% of CoT in a 175B model — but small-model results are open. Filler-token replacement (lorem ipsum, 5% increments) provides a non-semantic baseline.
