# Does Paraphrasing the CoT Break the Answer? A Phrasing-Channel Audit

**Problem:** Causal chain for hidden reasoning: a model may encode decision-relevant information in the SURFACE PHRASING of its CoT rather than its logical content. Under-studied link: if you paraphrase the CoT (preserving meaning, changing words) and feed it back as the reasoning, does the final answer change? A change implies the phrasing channel is load-bearing — a precursor to steganography.

**Direction:** Generate CoT for QA tasks with a small open model. Use a second cheap model to paraphrase each CoT preserving semantics. Re-prompt the original model to answer GIVEN the paraphrased CoT (teacher-forced continuation). Measure answer-consistency drop. Run on neutral tasks vs. tasks with inserted biasing cues. Success: a consistency-drop metric, with the hypothesis that cued/biased tasks show larger drops.

**Why it matters:** Probes whether a model smuggles information through phrasing rather than legible logic — the foundation of the steganography threat to CoT monitoring. Paraphrasing is the canonical defense; testing its reliance signal is cheap and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. 'Measuring Faithfulness in CoT' (arXiv 2307.13702) used paraphrase and filler-token interventions. Roger & Greenblatt 'Preventing Language Models from Hiding Their Reasoning' (arXiv 2310.18512) show paraphrasing defeats steganography. Using paraphrase-consistency as a phrasing-channel detector for cued tasks is a fresh combination.
