# Reproducing the Fourier 'pizza/clock' modular-addition circuit and checking it survives a different P

**Problem:** Progress-measures-for-grokking found that grokked modular-addition transformers implement a Fourier-based ('clock') algorithm, and follow-up work debated 'pizza vs. clock' circuit families. These mechanistic findings are usually demonstrated at P=113. Whether the same Fourier-circuit structure and progress measures (restricted/excluded loss) reproduce at a substantially different modulus is a clean mechanistic-replication question.

**Direction:** Train a 1-layer transformer to grok modular addition at a different prime modulus than 113. Reproduce the progress measures (restricted loss, excluded loss, Gini of Fourier components) and inspect whether the learned algorithm is again a sparse set of Fourier frequencies. Report whether the number/structure of key frequencies scales with P as theory would predict.

**Why it matters:** This is a complete, self-contained mechanistic-interpretability replication of one of the field's cleanest 'we fully understand the circuit' results — directly serving the open problem of 'formal mechanistic explanations of neural network behaviors.' Checking robustness to the modulus tests whether the mechanistic story is a general principle or a P=113 artifact, on a model that trains in minutes.
