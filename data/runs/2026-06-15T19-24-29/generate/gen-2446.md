# Paraphrasing Defense: How Much Covert Signal Survives a Trusted Rewrite?

**Problem:** Trusted-model paraphrasing/redaction is proposed to strip collusion signals and steganographic payloads from untrusted outputs, but its actual signal-destruction rate is rarely measured directly on an open budget.

**Direction:** Construct outputs containing simple covert payloads (an agreed token pattern, acrostic, or low-bit steganographic encoding). Pass each through a small trusted model instructed to paraphrase while preserving task content. Measure (a) fraction of covert signal that survives the rewrite and (b) task-content fidelity loss. Sweep paraphrase aggressiveness to map the signal-removal vs. usefulness frontier.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is one of the few cheap, model-agnostic anti-collusion / anti-steganography defenses available to actors without internal weights access. A clean dose-response curve tells deployers how aggressive a rewrite must be, and is reusable infrastructure for any control protocol.

**Relevant context:** Redwood collusion post and 'When can we trust untrusted monitoring?' (arxiv 2602.20628) propose redaction and paraphrasing by trusted models to remove explicit collusion signals; steganography/linguistic-drift encoding identified as CoT obfuscation pathways (arxiv 2510.19851, 2511.11584).
