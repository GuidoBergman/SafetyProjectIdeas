# Promise vs Practice in Watermarking: Does AI-Content Transparency Survive Trivial Edits?

**Problem:** The EU Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content leans on labeling/watermarking commitments. A known failure mode is that detectable provenance signals (watermarks, metadata) are fragile to trivial transformations (paraphrase, screenshot, re-encode), making the transparency commitment hollow. The robustness of available open watermarking/detection to simple edits is undercharacterized for policy audiences.

**Direction:** Stress-test provenance robustness as the failure mode. Using an open text watermarking scheme (or open AI-text detectors) on a single GPU, generate watermarked/AI text, then apply simple, realistic edits (paraphrasing with a small model, synonym swaps, truncation, format changes) and measure detection survival. Report the edit budget at which transparency signals break and characterize which edits defeat them most cheaply.

**Why it matters:** Transparency-of-AI-content rules assume provenance signals persist; if a screenshot or paraphrase erases them, the regulation can't achieve its goal. Quantifying fragility gives the EU AI Office concrete evidence on what to require. Open tools, small models, single GPU — independent and policy-relevant.

**Relevant context:** EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content; watermark-robustness and AI-text-detection literature; transparency obligations under EU AI Act Article 50.
