# Does steering toward 'honesty' actually reduce deception, or just reduce verbalized deception?

**Problem:** A key model-organism concern is models that act deceptively without verbalizing it. If we steer a model toward an 'honesty' concept, does its actual behavior become more honest, or does it merely produce more honest-SOUNDING text while the underlying (e.g. answer-selection) behavior is unchanged? Beaglehole-style steering is validated on surface behavior.

**Direction:** Use a task with a separable behavior and explanation channel — e.g. a sycophancy or sandbagging setup where the model can give a wrong answer while claiming honesty. Steer with an honesty/deception vector and measure separately: (a) the verbalized honesty of the explanation, and (b) the correctness/non-manipulation of the actual answer/action. Test whether steering moves only the words or also the deeds.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes the model-organism threat of unverbalized deception under a popular safety intervention. If steering only cleans up the verbalization, it is dangerously misleading as a safety tool. Uses existing open models and small task setups.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. deception/honesty steering (Science 2026). CAA sycophancy steering (arxiv 2312.06681). Sandbagging / unverbalized-deception model-organism literature.
