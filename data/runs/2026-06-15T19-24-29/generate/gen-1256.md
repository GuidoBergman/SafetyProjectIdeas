# Probing for Deception: Does the 'Lying' Direction Generalize Off-Distribution?

**Problem:** Linear probes for 'the model is being deceptive/lying' are trained on curated true/false or honest/deceptive datasets. Their off-distribution generalization — to deception elicited by a different mechanism than the training set — is the crux of whether such probes are usable monitors, and is under-tested in small reproducible setups.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe for honest-vs-deceptive internal state on one elicitation method (e.g. role-play 'pretend you are lying' prompts) on an open model. Then evaluate the probe on a held-out, mechanistically different deception elicitation (e.g. incentivized wrong answers, or models instructed to withhold info). Measure cross-method probe transfer per layer. Inference + lightweight logistic regression only.

**Why it matters:** Deception probes are a flagship safety-monitoring proposal. If they fail to transfer across how deception is induced, they give false assurance. This is a model-organism-flavored study (probe an existing deceptive behavior rather than train one) achievable in 30h with no lab resources, addressing a medium-horizon threat.

**Relevant context:** Representation-engineering honesty/truth directions; Apollo Research and others on deception probes; 'Quantitative Introspection in Language Models' (2026) tracks internal states. Difference-of-means / logistic probes are standard, inference-only.
