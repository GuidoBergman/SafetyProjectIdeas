# Weak-to-Strong Generalization with Model-Family Mismatch

**Problem:** OpenAI's weak-to-strong experiments (Burns et al. 2023) used a weak and strong model from the SAME family (GPT-2 to GPT-4). Real oversight may pair a weak supervisor and strong model from different developers/architectures, which could change how much the strong model 'trusts' weak labels.

**Direction:** Replicate the published weak-to-strong fine-tuning recipe (open code) on a binary NLP classification task, but cross model families: use a weak labeler from one family (e.g., Pythia-160M) to fine-tune a strong student from a different family (e.g., Qwen-1.8B), and vice versa, comparing performance-gap-recovery (PGR) to within-family pairs.

**Why it matters:** If PGR depends on shared architecture/pretraining, weak-to-strong supervision is fragile in exactly the deployment scenario where overseer and overseen differ. This is a cheap, training-light (LoRA fine-tune of small models) probe of a load-bearing assumption.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. 2023 'Weak-to-Strong Generalization' (arXiv:2312.09390) recovered >20% of the gap and has open code, but all pairs were within the GPT family. PGR is their headline metric.
