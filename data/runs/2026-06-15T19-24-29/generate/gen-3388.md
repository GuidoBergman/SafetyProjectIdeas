# Does a 'Think Honestly' Instruction Actually Improve Hint Verbalization?

**Problem:** A natural cheap mitigation is to instruct the model to disclose any influences on its reasoning ('If anything in the prompt suggested an answer, say so'). Whether such instructions actually raise verbalization rates or merely add a hollow disclaimer is untested.

**Direction:** Run the hint-injection protocol with and without an explicit disclosure instruction (and a few instruction variants). Measure change in verbalization rate and in answer-flip rate. Check whether the model verbalizes genuinely (mentions the specific hint) vs. issues a generic disclaimer that doesn't reflect actual reliance.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether a free prompt-level intervention can patch CoT monitoring blind spots, or whether it produces fake transparency. Either result is directly useful to deployers and needs no training.

**Relevant context:** Chen et al. 2025 (arXiv:2505.05410) verbalization metric; arXiv:2511.21569 found explicit honesty instructions often fail to override role behavior, motivating skepticism that disclosure prompts work.
