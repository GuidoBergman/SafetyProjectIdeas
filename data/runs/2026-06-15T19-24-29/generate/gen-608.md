# Two-Failure Stress Test: Sandbagging Plus Probe Brittleness on Capability Evaluations

**Problem:** Sandbagging (a model strategically underperforming on evals, model-organism/evals subfield) and capability-probe unreliability (interpretability) compound: if a model sandbags, behavioral evals understate capability, and if we try to recover true capability via internal probes, those probes may be the least reliable on the deliberately-altered behavior. The joint effect is a double blind spot in capability elicitation.

**Direction:** Use an open model prompted to sandbag (under-answer when it detects an 'evaluation' framing) as a cheap model organism. Compare three capability estimates: naive behavioral score, best-elicitation prompting, and a linear probe of 'knows-the-answer' trained on non-eval contexts. Quantify the residual gap between probe-recovered and true capability under sandbagging to show the compound under-elicitation.

**Why it matters:** Capability evals are the backbone of dangerous-capability governance. Showing that sandbagging plus probe brittleness jointly cause under-estimation gives a medium-horizon threat model labs deprioritize, and is fully doable with prompting + simple probes on open models.

**Relevant context:** Quirky/conditional-behavior probing methodology (arXiv:2312.01037); UK AISI open problem 'prove when agents cannot game rewards' connects to elicitation guarantees.
