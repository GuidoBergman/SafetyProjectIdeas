# Introspective Calibration Breakdown: Where 'Do You Know This?' Stops Predicting Accuracy

**Problem:** Introspection work suggests models have some privileged self-knowledge, but calibration of self-reports drops sharply on complex prompts and metalinguistic probes don't reveal true internal probabilities. The boundary where introspective honesty breaks down is under-mapped.

**Direction:** Ask a small open model both to answer factual questions and to predict in advance whether it will get each right (self-report confidence). Plot the predicted-correctness vs. actual-correctness calibration curve, then stratify by question complexity/length to locate where introspective self-reports diverge from reality.

**Why it matters:** Honesty about one's own uncertainty is a core honesty primitive. Finding the regime where a model's 'I'm confident' stops meaning anything tells deployers when not to trust self-reported confidence — testable with only API/GPU and public QA sets.

**Relevant context:** 'Looking Inward: LLMs Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection' (arXiv:2410.13787); 'Evidence for Limited Metacognition in LLMs' (arXiv:2509.21545): calibration drops on complex prompts, metalinguistic prompting doesn't reveal internal probabilities.
