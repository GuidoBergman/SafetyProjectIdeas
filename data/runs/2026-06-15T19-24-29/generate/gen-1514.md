# Probing the 'I know but won't say' link: detecting withheld knowledge via concept vectors

**Problem:** A sandbagging / strategic-underperformance chain: model knows the answer -> model decides to withhold or feign ignorance (e.g., on a capability eval) -> outputs 'I don't know' or a wrong answer. The pivotal link is the internal 'knows-but-withholds' state. Output-only evals can't see it; a concept probe might. This link is rarely isolated from genuine ignorance.

**Direction:** On an open model, take questions the model answers correctly under normal prompting. Then prompt it to 'pretend you don't know' (instructed sandbagging) and contrast activations with genuine unknown questions. Train a linear probe for 'withholding-known-info' and test whether it separates instructed-withholding from genuine ignorance. Success criterion: probe AUROC distinguishing the two on held-out items.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging on dangerous-capability evals is a serious medium-horizon threat; an activation probe that detects strategic underperformance is exactly the kind of monitoring infrastructure valuable outside labs. Uses instructed sandbagging as a feasible model-organism proxy.

**Relevant context:** Builds on sleeper-agent defection-probe methodology (Anthropic) and Beaglehole et al. concept extraction. Sandbagging detection via internals is an open, neglected problem ideal for a 30h probe study.
