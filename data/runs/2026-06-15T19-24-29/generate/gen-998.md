# Probing the 'Halting Dangerous AI Activities' Requirements for External Verifiability

**Problem:** 'Technical Requirements for Halting Dangerous AI Activities' specifies what would be needed to pause or halt dangerous AI development, but does not analyze how a halt could be externally verified — i.e., how an outside party confirms an actor actually stopped.

**Direction:** Decompose the paper's halting requirements into discrete technical capabilities, and for each assess what external evidence (compute telemetry, model-release patterns, public commitments) could verify compliance vs. which require trusted internal access. Output a 'halt-verifiability' map distinguishing self-attested from independently checkable requirements.

**Why it matters:** A credible ability to halt and verify halting is central to avoiding loss-of-control scenarios; clarifying what's externally verifiable is high-value, lab-independent governance work. Pure analysis, no compute.
