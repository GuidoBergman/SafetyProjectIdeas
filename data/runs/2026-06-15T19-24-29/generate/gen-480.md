# OversightGap Bench: How Wide a Capability Gap Can Weak-to-Strong Generalization Cross Before It Breaks?

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong generalization (OpenAI 2023) and scalable-oversight theory ask how far a weak supervisor's signal can elicit correct behavior from a stronger model. There is no small, beginner-runnable benchmark that sweeps the supervisor/student capability gap and reports the elicitation-recovery curve as the gap grows.

**Direction:** Using two open models of different sizes, generate weak-supervisor labels from the smaller model, fine-tune (LoRA / few-shot in-context, no from-scratch training) the larger model on those labels for simple classification tasks, and measure performance-gap-recovered as a function of the size gap. Output a curve of how oversight efficacy degrades with the capability gap.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight is the empirical face of the formal trust/Vingean-reflection problem: can a weaker overseer reliably steer a stronger system? A clean elicitation-vs-gap curve gives a quantitative limit estimate, valuable outside labs (uses small open models, LoRA/in-context only, single GPU, no from-scratch training).

**Relevant context:** Grounded in OpenAI's 'Weak-to-Strong Generalization' (ELICITING strong from weak) and ELK/oversight agendas (ARC). Feasible with a single GPU using LoRA or purely in-context labeling to stay within the 30h/compute budget.
