# Reading the Reasoning: Do Safety Cases Have Logically Connected Arguments?

**Problem:** Dynamic-safety-case research proposes structured arguments linking evidence to safety claims, but published safety frameworks rarely present explicit argument structure, so it is unknown whether their reasoning is even internally connected or just a list of activities.

**Direction:** Take published safety frameworks/cards and attempt to reconstruct each into a simple claim-argument-evidence graph (top claim 'this model is safe to deploy' -> sub-claims -> evidence). Use an LLM to extract nodes and edges, then score how many top-level claims are actually supported by evidence nodes vs unsupported. Report the 'argument completeness' across documents.

**Why it matters:** If safety cases are logically incomplete, the governance edifice resting on them is fragile; auditing argument structure from public text is a novel, low-cost contribution to verifiable safety claims.

**Relevant context:** 'Dynamic safety cases for frontier AI' arXiv:2412.17618; 'A Frontier AI Risk Management Framework' arXiv:2502.06656; GovAI auditing on safety-case scrutiny.
