# Stated-Confidence Calibration Replicated Under Sandbagging Pressure

**Problem:** SimpleQA-style calibration assumes the model is trying to answer well. How a model's confidence calibration changes when it is prompted to sandbag (deliberately underperform) — does it also miscalibrate confidence, or selectively lower accuracy while keeping confidence honest? — is unexplored.

**Direction:** Replicate the SimpleQA stated-confidence calibration measurement under two conditions on a small open model: honest answering vs prompted sandbagging. Compare accuracy, stated confidence, and ECE. The twist combines two replication targets (calibration + sandbagging) to ask whether sandbagging is detectable via confidence-accuracy mismatch.

**Why it matters:** If sandbagging models keep stated confidence high while accuracy drops, confidence-accuracy mismatch becomes a cheap sandbagging detector usable by any evaluator. Combines two open problems into a feasible, lab-independent experiment.

**Relevant context:** SimpleQA calibration protocol (arXiv 2411.04368). Sandbagging from arXiv 2406.07358 / 2508.00943. Combining them is a fresh angle not in the listed catalogue items.
