# Easy-to-Hard Evaluator Generalization Replicated With Process vs Outcome Supervision

**Problem:** Sun et al. emphasize process-supervised reward models for easy-to-hard transfer. Whether the easy-to-hard generalization advantage holds, or shrinks, when using cheap outcome-only supervision instead of expensive process labels is a directly practical replication variation for resource-limited researchers.

**Direction:** Replicate the easy-to-hard evaluator setup with two supervision regimes on the same easy problems: outcome-only labels (final answer correct/incorrect) vs step-level process labels. Measure each evaluator's accuracy at scoring hard-problem solutions and quantify how much easy-to-hard generalization is lost by dropping to outcome-only supervision.

**Why it matters:** Process supervision is expensive; outcome supervision is cheap. Knowing how much easy-to-hard generalization survives with cheap labels tells resource-limited overseers what they can achieve without costly step annotations. Scoped to scoring/reranking (no RL) to fit 30h, open models, public math/reasoning data.

**Relevant context:** Sun et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.09472) rely on process-supervised reward models for easy-to-hard transfer. The process-vs-outcome supervision comparison along the easy-to-hard axis is the twist, isolating how much the result depends on expensive process labels.
