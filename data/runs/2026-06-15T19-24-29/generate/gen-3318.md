# Multi-Layer Probe Ensembling on a Small Budget: Does Stacking Layers Beat the Best Single Layer?

**Problem:** Linear Probe Accuracy Scales with Model Size and Benefits from Multi-Layer Ensembling (arXiv:2604.13386) claims ensembling probes across layers improves deception detection, but the study spans large models. Whether the benefit holds at the 1-8B scale a student can run is unknown.

**Direction:** On a small open model, train per-layer logistic deception probes for every layer, then build a simple ensemble (averaged logits or a tiny meta-logistic over layer outputs). Compare ensemble AUROC vs. the best single layer on in-distribution and shifted test sets, and report whether the ensembling gain survives at small scale and under shift.

**Why it matters:** Probe ensembling is a cheap robustness lever for scheming detection. Establishing whether it helps at small, accessible scales tells the broader community whether the technique is worth adopting without frontier compute.

**Relevant context:** 'Linear Probe Accuracy Scales with Model Size and Benefits from Multi-Layer Ensembling' (arXiv:2604.13386v1, 2026): larger models show more linearly separable deception representations; multi-layer ensembling improves accuracy. Builds on 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv:2502.03407).
