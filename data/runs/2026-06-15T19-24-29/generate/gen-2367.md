# Backcasting the Capability Gap Limit: How Wide Can the Judge-Debater Gap Be?

**Problem:** Success state: oversight protocols that still work when the supervised model is much stronger than the overseer. We need an empirical curve of judge accuracy as a function of the judge-to-debater capability gap, to estimate where debate stops helping.

**Direction:** Using a ladder of open models of different sizes (e.g. 1B/3B/8B as judges; 8B/70B-class as debaters) on QuALITY debate, sweep the capability gap and plot judge accuracy and debate's advantage over consultancy as the gap widens. Extrapolate toward the superhuman-gap regime.

**Why it matters:** The whole motivation for scalable oversight is supervising stronger-than-overseer systems. An empirical capability-gap scaling curve on open models tells protocol designers whether debate's benefit shrinks, holds, or reverses as the gap grows — a medium-horizon question labs underexplore at small scale.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.04622) and 'Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight' (arXiv:2504.18530) study capability asymmetry; subfield hints suggest varying model-size gaps in weak-to-strong setups. Open model ladders make this feasible without training.
