# Cross-Border Reporting: Where International Incident-Sharing Chains Break at the Border

**Problem:** International-coordination chain: 'incident occurs in country A' -> 'reported to A's authority' -> 'shared with the international network' -> 'other countries adapt'. Establishing consensus evaluation/sharing methods is an open problem; the cross-border-share link likely breaks due to incompatible definitions across national regimes.

**Direction:** Compile the incident/critical-safety-event reporting definitions and thresholds from the major regimes (SB 53, EU AI Act, AISI network statements) and build a compatibility matrix showing where a reportable event in one jurisdiction would not be reportable (or not shareable) in another. Identify the definitional mismatches that break cross-border learning.

**Why it matters:** International consensus on methodology and an NTSB-style learning loop both require compatible definitions. A mismatch matrix tells coordinators exactly which definitional gaps block cross-border safety learning. Pure comparative policy analysis, no compute, high counterfactual value.
