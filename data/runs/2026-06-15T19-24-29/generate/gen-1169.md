# Linguistic-Typology Diversity Profiles: Are Models a Monoculture Across Languages?

**Problem:** Homogeneity studies are overwhelmingly English. Linguistics/typology offers principled cross-language sampling and diversity measures; the question of whether models homogenize MORE in low-resource languages (a fairness + systemic-risk concern) is unexamined with these tools.

**Direction:** Borrow typological sampling (a balanced set of languages across families) and measure inter-model output homogeneity per language on translated open-ended prompts. Test the hypothesis that low-resource languages show higher inter-model homogeneity because fewer training signals push all models to the same narrow mode.

**Why it matters:** Identifies a structured, predictable axis of correlated-failure risk (entire language communities served by interchangeable, equally-blind models) that frontier labs deprioritize. Public multilingual models suffice.

**Relevant context:** Pretrained multilingual transformers reveal quantitative distance between human languages (arXiv:2603.17912). Hill-number/diversity sampling from ecology. Artificial Hivemind multilingual follow-up implied (arXiv:2510.22954).
