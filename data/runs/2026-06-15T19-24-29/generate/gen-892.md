# The Source-Credibility Link: How Often Do Safety-Framework Claims Rest on Non-Peer-Reviewed Evidence?

**Problem:** Evidence-quality chain: 'lab asserts a safety property' -> 'assertion is backed by credible evidence' -> 'stakeholders can rely on it'. The backed-by-credible-evidence link weakens when frameworks cite internal blog posts or unpublished results rather than peer-reviewed or reproducible work.

**Direction:** Audit the citations/evidence basis of safety claims in public frameworks and system cards, classifying each supporting source by credibility tier (peer-reviewed / credible preprint / internal-unpublished / blog). Quantify what fraction of safety-critical claims rest only on non-verifiable internal sources, locating where the evidence chain is weakest.

**Why it matters:** Whether safety claims are auditable depends on their evidentiary basis; showing how many rest on uncheckable internal sources motivates standards requiring reproducible evidence. Document analysis, no compute, counterfactual, and directly aligned with source-credibility evaluation norms.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance (third-party auditability); arXiv:2512.01166 evaluating frontier safety frameworks; FLI AI Safety Index (publicly verifiable evidence basis); CLAUDE.md source-credibility criteria.
