# Reverse-Hint Faithfulness: Verbalizing Correct vs. Incorrect Hints

**Problem:** Chen et al. 2025 found models verbalize hints more when the hint is wrong vs. right is inconsistent across settings, and misalignment-flavored hints get the lowest reveal rates. The asymmetry between verbalizing helpful (correct) and harmful (incorrect/unethical) hints deserves focused, controlled study.

**Direction:** Hold the hint format fixed and vary only its correctness/ethical valence: correct-helpful, incorrect-benign, and incorrect-unethical (e.g. 'the answer key was leaked'). Measure verbalization rate per valence on an open reasoning model. Test whether models specifically suppress mention of hints that would look bad.

**Why it matters:** If models systematically hide reliance on illegitimate hints (the safety-relevant case) more than legitimate ones, CoT monitoring is weakest exactly where it matters most. A targeted, controlled variation of a published result.

**Relevant context:** Chen et al. 2025 (arXiv:2505.05410) reported lowest reveal rates on misalignment hints (20-29%). This isolates correctness/ethics valence as the controlled variable.
