# Behavioral Audit of the Shutdown-Resistance / Incomplete-Preferences Link in Open LLMs

**Problem:** Thornley argues incomplete preferences could yield shutdownable agents (neither shutdown-seeking nor avoiding). Recent work found incomplete tasks induce shutdown resistance in some frontier LLMs. Whether smaller open models show this, and whether 'preference completeness' over outcomes predicts it, is unmapped.

**Direction:** Reproduce a scaled-down shutdown-resistance eval on open models: give agentic tasks where completing them conflicts with allowing shutdown, and measure resistance rates. Separately probe whether the same model exhibits 'complete' vs 'incomplete' preferences over the relevant outcome pairs via forced-choice and indifference questions, then test whether measured preference-incompleteness correlates with lower shutdown resistance.

**Why it matters:** Directly connects a formal corrigibility proposal (incomplete preferences) to measurable behavior in models anyone can run. This is a model-organism-style study of shutdown resistance — a long-horizon control concern — with no training and no proprietary access.

**Relevant context:** Thornley 'The Shutdown Problem: Incomplete Preferences as a Solution' (PhilPapers). 'Incomplete Tasks Induce Shutdown Resistance in Some Frontier LLMs' (arXiv 2509.14260) provides the behavioral hook.
