# A/B Test & Causal-Inference Lens on 'Safety Theater' System Prompts

**Problem:** Practitioners add safety system prompts assuming they cause more refusals, but rarely measure the causal effect cleanly; observed differences may be confounded by prompt-length or topic shifts.

**Direction:** Apply randomized-controlled-trial / causal-inference methodology from clinical trials and online experimentation. Randomly assign matched harmful prompts to safety-system-prompt vs control, hold length/topic constant via stratification, and estimate the average treatment effect on refusal with confidence intervals, plus heterogeneity (which harm categories benefit). Test several popular 'safety preamble' templates head-to-head on an open model.

**Why it matters:** Replaces folklore about safety prompting with rigorous causal estimates, exposing 'safety theater' prompts that do nothing. Cheap, reproducible, no training, no lab data; directly useful to the wider community.

**Relevant context:** RCT design and ATE estimation are foundational in clinical trials and A/B testing. Context: shallow alignment (arXiv:2406.05946) implies prefix/system-prompt effects may be superficial; SoK prompt security (arXiv:2510.15476) catalogues prompt-level defenses needing rigorous evaluation.
