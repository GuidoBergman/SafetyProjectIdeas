# Replicating reward-model overoptimization (Goodhart) curves on a hand-built toy proxy

**Problem:** Gao et al. found scaling laws for reward-model overoptimization: optimizing a proxy reward past a critical point degrades the true ('gold') reward, following a predictable functional form. The full study used large RLHF setups. A faithful small replication of the *shape* of these overoptimization curves in a controllable toy setting, where the gold reward is known exactly, would make the phenomenon teachable and let us test which Goodhart 'type' dominates.

**Direction:** Build a tiny RL/bandit or best-of-n toy where the gold reward is a known function and the proxy is gold-plus-noise (the simplest regressional-Goodhart construction). Vary optimization pressure (KL budget / best-of-n N) and reproduce the rise-then-fall of gold reward vs. proxy reward. Then add a deliberately *miscausal* proxy to elicit causal/extremal Goodhart and compare curve shapes.

**Why it matters:** Reward hacking via proxy overoptimization is a central, long-horizon alignment threat. A clean, reproducible toy that reproduces the published overoptimization functional form — and dissects which of Garrabrant's four Goodhart types is operative — gives the community a no-lab-resources testbed for studying fuzzy-reward failure, exactly the kind of diffuse problem frontier labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (Gao et al., arXiv:2210.10760); 'Goodhart's Law in Reinforcement Learning' (OpenReview 5o9G4XF1LI) shows overoptimization across many environments; Garrabrant's four Goodhart types (Regressional/Causal/Extremal/Adversarial). Steering priority (context): fuzzy-reward / reward-hacking evaluations with no lab resources.
