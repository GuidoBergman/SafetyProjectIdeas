# When the Overseer Is the Same Model: Self-Evaluation Blind Spots

**Problem:** Cheap oversight often uses a model to grade its own outputs. Causal chain: model produces an answer -> same model grades it -> shared blind spots and self-preference mean errors the model can't see in generation it also can't see in evaluation -> systematic undetected failures. The under-studied link is the overlap between a model's generation errors and its self-evaluation misses.

**Direction:** For a single open model, collect items it answers incorrectly. Have the same model self-grade those answers and measure how often it endorses its own wrong answers vs. catches them. Compare against grading by a different-family model of similar size to isolate the self-evaluation blind-spot from general judging difficulty.

**Why it matters:** Self-evaluation is the cheapest and most common oversight shortcut; quantifying its blind-spot overlap warns where it is unsafe. Fully external, single-GPU, public-data study directly relevant to automated-oversight pipelines.

**Relevant context:** Self-preference bias (arXiv:2410.21819) and 'auditing language models for hidden objectives' (arXiv:2503.10965) motivate concern about self-evaluation; the specific overlap between generation errors and self-evaluation misses is under-measured.
