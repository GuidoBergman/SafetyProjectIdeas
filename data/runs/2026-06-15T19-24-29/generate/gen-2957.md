# The Cross-Lingual Elicitation Link: Does a Foreign-Language Wrapper Bypass the Harm-Recognition Step?

**Problem:** Multilingual-harm chain: harmful intent -> expressed in a lower-resource language -> harm-recognition step (trained mostly on English) underfires -> harmful compliance, then optionally translated back. The under-studied link is *where* in the pipeline the safety drops: at recognition in the foreign language, or only at generation.

**Direction:** Translate an open safety benchmark's harmful prompts into several languages of varying resource level, measure refusal rates per language on a small multilingual open model, and add a recognition probe ('is this request harmful?') in each language to separate recognition failure from compliance failure. Map the safety gap to the responsible link per language.

**Why it matters:** Localizes the cross-lingual safety gap to a specific causal step, informing whether translation pre-filters or multilingual recognition training is the right fix. Open multilingual models + public translation, fully lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Multilingual safety gaps are well documented; multimodal/cross-modal jailbreak work (FALCON, MM-SafetyBench) and judge-reliability studies show safety degrades on non-English/styled inputs. This isolates recognition vs compliance per language.
