# An Empirical 'Unhackability Gap' Metric for Proxy Reward Pairs

**Problem:** Skalse et al. prove no non-trivial proxy reward is unhackable: there always exist policies that raise proxy return while lowering true return. But the theorem is existential — it says hacking policies exist, not how easy they are to find or how large the gap is in practice.

**Direction:** In a tiny, fully-enumerable gridworld (states small enough to enumerate all deterministic policies in Python), define a true reward and several proxy rewards. Enumerate the policy space and empirically measure the 'hackability gap': the maximum true-reward drop achievable while improving proxy reward, and the fraction of policy space that hacks. Study how the gap shrinks as the proxy is made to capture more true-reward structure.

**Why it matters:** Converts a binary impossibility result into a continuous, measurable quantity that could guide reward design. Pure Python, no GPU, no training — maximally counterfactual and a clean operationalization of a formal theorem.

**Relevant context:** Skalse et al. (arXiv 2209.13085): a proxy is 'unhackable' iff increasing proxy return cannot decrease true return; they prove non-trivial unhackable proxies don't exist and require capturing all true-preference detail.
