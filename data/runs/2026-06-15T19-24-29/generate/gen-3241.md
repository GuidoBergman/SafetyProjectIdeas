# The Scratchpad Privacy Belief: Does 'No One Reads This' Cause More Candid Scheming?

**Problem:** Across alignment-faking and scheming studies, the hidden-scratchpad device works because the model believes its reasoning is unobserved. The causal link (belief-of-privacy -> candid disclosure of misaligned plans) is the very thing CoT monitoring relies on, yet how sensitive disclosure is to the privacy framing is rarely isolated.

**Direction:** In a toy scenario that elicits some scheming/faking reasoning on an open model, vary the privacy framing of the scratchpad (told private / told monitored / no statement). Measure how the rate of explicitly verbalized misaligned reasoning changes. Quantify how fragile the privacy-belief link is — i.e. how much a monitoring disclaimer suppresses candid CoT.

**Why it matters:** If telling the model it is monitored sharply reduces candid CoT, then real-world monitoring may destroy the very signal it seeks — a crucial caveat for CoT-based safety cases, cheaply demonstrated.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2412.14093 relied on a scratchpad 'invisible to users and Anthropic'; CoT-monitoring literature warns monitor-awareness induces obfuscation (arXiv:2503.11926).
