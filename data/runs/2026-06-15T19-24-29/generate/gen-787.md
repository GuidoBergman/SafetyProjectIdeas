# If-Then Commitment Specificity Scorer for Frontier Safety Policies

**Problem:** Frontier safety frameworks rely on 'if-then commitments' that trigger mitigations when capability thresholds are crossed, but many are written so vaguely that it is unclear what measurable trigger would fire them. There is no public tool that measures how operationally specific each commitment is.

**Direction:** Build a rubric-driven scorer (e.g. 0=aspirational, 1=qualitative trigger, 2=named eval, 3=quantitative threshold + named eval + bound response) and apply it to the ~12 published frontier safety policies. Use an LLM as a rubric-anchored extractor to pull every if-then clause, then score each on trigger measurability, response bindingness, and timing. Report a per-lab specificity profile and aggregate gaps.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizing safety objectives into measurable deployment triggers is a named open problem. A reproducible specificity metric gives auditors and AISIs a lightweight way to track whether commitments are getting more concrete over time, and is doable entirely from public documents with no lab access.

**Relevant context:** METR's 'Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies' (2024-2025 updates) catalogues if-then commitments and CCLs; FAR.AI's Painter talk on challenges creating if-then commitments; 'From Principles to Rules' (arXiv 2407.07300). All policies are public PDFs.
