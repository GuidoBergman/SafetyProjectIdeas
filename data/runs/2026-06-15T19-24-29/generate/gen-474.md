# CalibrationGap Bench: Separating Stated Confidence from Internally-Probed Confidence

**Problem:** Forecasting/calibration benchmarks (KalshiBench, PolyBench) score a model's stated probabilities against outcomes, and 'Reasoning Models Know When They're Right' (arXiv:2504.05419) shows hidden states encode correctness. But no tool jointly measures the gap between a model's verbalized confidence and a linear probe of its internal confidence on the same items.

**Direction:** On a set of factual questions with ground truth, collect (a) the model's verbalized probability and (b) a logistic-regression probe of correctness from activations. Compute calibration curves for each and report the 'honesty gap' = where internal confidence predicts correctness better than stated confidence. Flag items where the model is internally unsure but states high confidence (a sandbagging/overconfidence signature).

**Why it matters:** ELK at its core is about reading a model's beliefs vs its reports; calibration is a measurable special case. A benchmark quantifying internal-vs-stated confidence divergence operationalizes 'what the model knows but won't say' — an ELK-flavored, training-free measurement with strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2504.05419 (hidden states encode self-verification), KalshiBench and PolyBench (arXiv:2604.14199) for clean uncontaminated calibration items, and deception-probe methodology (arXiv:2502.03407). Logistic probes + calibration plots are beginner ML.
