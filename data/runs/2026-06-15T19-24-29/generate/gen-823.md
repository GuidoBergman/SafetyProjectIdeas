# Reproducibility of Public Safety Evals: Can an Outsider Re-Derive a Lab's Reported Safety Numbers?

**Problem:** Audit-washing risk arises because first-party safety evals are rarely independently reproducible. When labs report a refusal rate or jailbreak-robustness number, an outside auditor usually cannot re-run it. The failure mode — irreproducibility of headline safety claims due to missing prompts, settings, or definitions — has not been measured systematically.

**Direction:** Pick 3-5 publicly-reported safety metrics for open-weight models (refusal rate, a named jailbreak-resistance score). For each, attempt to reproduce it from public artifacts on a single GPU, logging every missing ingredient (exact prompt set, system prompt, decoding params, scoring rubric). Score each claim's 'reproducibility gap' and characterize which categories of safety claim are systematically unverifiable from public information.

**Why it matters:** Third-party auditability of safety claims is the headline open problem in technical AI governance. A concrete demonstration of how far public artifacts fall short of reproducibility gives standards bodies a checklist of what disclosure auditing actually requires. Uses small open models, public reports — independent, GPU-light.

**Relevant context:** Outsider Oversight (arXiv 2206.04737); GMF 'AI Audit-Washing and Accountability'; Open Problems in Technical AI Governance (third-party verification).
