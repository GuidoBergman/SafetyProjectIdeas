# Cross-Model Feature Universality for a Single Safety Concept

**Problem:** It is an open question whether a given safety-relevant feature (e.g., 'refusal') occupies a comparable representational role across different open model families, or whether each family encodes it idiosyncratically. Universality would mean monitoring transfers; idiosyncrasy means per-model tooling is required.

**Direction:** For one safety concept, locate its representation (via probe or SAE feature) in three different open model families (e.g., Gemma, Llama, Qwen) and use representational-similarity / activation-correlation methods to test whether the feature plays an analogous causal role across them. Report a universality score for the concept.

**Why it matters:** Determines whether interpretability-based safety findings are portable across models — a key input to whether small-lab monitoring tools generalize. Pure inference on public weights, no training.

**Relevant context:** Feature universality is a recurring theme in mech interp (Olah et al.; '200 Concrete Open Problems'). Multi-model open weights (Gemma-2, Llama-3, Qwen3) and TransformerLens (50+ models) make cross-family activation comparison feasible.
