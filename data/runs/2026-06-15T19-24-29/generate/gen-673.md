# Refusal-Direction Ablation: Robustness of the Abliteration Trace After Quantization

**Problem:** Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace (arXiv:2602.11495) and refusal-direction work rely on full-precision activations. Independent researchers run quantized models (4-bit/8-bit) on consumer GPUs. Whether the refusal direction and its detection signal survive quantization is practically important but untested.

**Direction:** On one open model, extract the refusal direction and a jailbreak-detection probe at FP16. Re-extract at 8-bit and 4-bit quantization. Measure (a) cosine similarity of the refusal direction across precisions and (b) detection-probe AUROC degradation. Report whether cheap quantized deployment weakens activation-based safety signals.

**Why it matters:** Almost all out-of-lab deployment is quantized; if safety probes silently degrade under quantization, that is a deployment-relevant blind spot. Cheap, single-GPU, forward-pass-only, directly counterfactual to lab settings that run full precision.

**Relevant context:** Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace (arXiv:2602.11495, Feb 2026) and ALERT (arXiv:2601.03600) build activation-based detectors at full precision. Refusal-direction extraction is standard in the abliteration literature. Quantization (bitsandbytes/GGUF) is the standard consumer-GPU path.
