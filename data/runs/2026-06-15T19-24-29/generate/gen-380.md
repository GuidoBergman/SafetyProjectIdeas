# Jailbreak x Self-Consistency Voting: Does Majority Voting Launder Rare Harmful Samples?

**Problem:** Two failures: stochastic jailbreak success (an attack works on a fraction of samples) and self-consistency/majority-voting pipelines used to improve answer quality. If a deployment samples N answers and returns the majority, this is often assumed to filter rare harmful outputs—but if jailbreaks tilt the distribution, voting may instead surface harmful content as the plurality, or 'launder' a harmful answer by aggregating partial leaks. The interaction is unstudied.

**Direction:** On open models, run jailbreak prompts with N-sample self-consistency aggregation. Compare harmful-content rate of single-sample vs majority-voted vs union-of-samples outputs. Success criterion: a characterization of whether voting reduces, preserves, or amplifies harmful output relative to single sampling, across attack strengths.

**Why it matters:** Self-consistency is widely adopted for reliability; its interaction with jailbreak stochasticity determines whether a common quality technique is also a safety filter or a safety hole. Pure inference, open models, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Decoding-stochasticity exploits and prefill attacks (arXiv:2406.05946) make jailbreak success sample-dependent; self-consistency voting is a standard inference pipeline. Novel compound: stochastic jailbreak x aggregation.
