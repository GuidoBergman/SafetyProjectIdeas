# Does Activation Steering Break Safety Even at Interpretable Scales?

**Problem:** Recent work ('The Rogue Scalpel') shows activation steering systematically compromises LLM safety even when framed as a precise, interpretable intervention. It is unclear at what steering magnitude safety begins to break, relative to where the intended behavioral effect appears — i.e., whether there is any 'safe operating window'.

**Direction:** Using the open steering-vectors library and an open chat model, sweep steering magnitude for a benign style/topic vector and jointly measure (a) the intended behavioral change and (b) jailbreak/refusal-bypass rate on a small harmful-prompt set. Map the dose-response curve to find whether safety degradation precedes, coincides with, or follows the useful effect.

**Why it matters:** Steering is increasingly proposed as an interpretable control knob. Demonstrating that its 'safe window' is narrow or nonexistent on open models is directly actionable safety evidence requiring only public tools.

**Relevant context:** 'The Rogue Scalpel: Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety' (researchgate 395944752 / OpenReview) shows steering breaks alignment. 2025 work notes side effects appear mainly at high steering scales; llm-steer / steering-vectors are open libraries.
