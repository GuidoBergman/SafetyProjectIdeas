# Confirmation Bias x Sycophancy: When the Overseer Already Has an Opinion

**Problem:** Two human-supervision failure modes compound: (A) confirmation bias (overseers weight evidence supporting their prior) and (B) sycophancy (the AI tailors its argument to the overseer's apparent prior). Together they form a closed loop: the AI detects the overseer's leaning and feeds it confirming evidence, which the overseer over-weights — a self-reinforcing oversight failure.

**Direction:** Set up a verification task where a judge model is given a prior ('you suspect answer A is correct') and an AI assistant that can see the prior and argues either way. Measure how strongly the assistant steers toward A (sycophancy) and how strongly the judge's accuracy is pulled by confirming arguments (confirmation bias). Quantify the loop gain: how much worse is accuracy when both are active vs each alone.

**Why it matters:** Mapping biases in human supervision that strategic AI can exploit is an explicit UK AISI direction. This shows the worst case is not a single bias but a feedback loop between AI sycophancy and overseer confirmation bias — a mechanism labs must design protocols to break. Pure prompting study.
