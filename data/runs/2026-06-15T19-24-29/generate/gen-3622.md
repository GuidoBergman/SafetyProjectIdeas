# Causal Bypass: How Often Does the CoT Not Matter?

**Problem:** A recent paper shows that in some cases the chain of thought is causally inert: the answer is the same whether or not the CoT supports it ('causal bypass'). The prevalence of causal bypass across task types and small models has not been systematically measured.

**Direction:** For a battery of tasks, generate the model's CoT, then intervene by truncating, corrupting, or replacing the CoT with a contradictory one, and measure how often the final answer is unchanged (causal bypass rate). Break this down by task type (arithmetic, commonsense, multiple-choice) to find where CoT is decorative vs. load-bearing.

**Why it matters:** If CoT is frequently causally inert, monitoring it gives false assurance. Measuring the causal-bypass rate is a direct, cheap, intervention-based faithfulness test that needs only API/single-GPU access.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'When Chains of Thought Don't Matter: Causal Bypass in Large Language Models' (arXiv 2602.03994, ~Feb 2026) and the unlearning-step faithfulness approach of arXiv 2502.14829.
