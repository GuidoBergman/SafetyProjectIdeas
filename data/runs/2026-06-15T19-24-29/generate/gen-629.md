# Measuring the think-vs-say acknowledgment gap on open reasoning models

**Problem:** A 2026 paper found a striking gap in open-weight reasoning models: ~87.5% of hint influence is acknowledged in thinking tokens but only ~28.6% in the answer text. It is unclear whether this gap generalizes across model families and hint types.

**Direction:** Replicate the hint-injection protocol on 2-3 open reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-distill, QwQ small variants) that expose CoT. Inject hints (correct, incorrect, irrelevant), then automatically classify acknowledgment in the thinking trace vs the final answer using a fixed LLM-judge rubric. Report the gap per model and hint type. Success = a reproduced gap statistic with confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** If models 'know but don't say', CoT monitoring is unreliable — a core assumption behind cheap external oversight. This study needs only inference on open models and stress-tests a load-bearing safety assumption.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Why Models Know But Don't Say: Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers in Open-Weight Reasoning Models' (arXiv:2603.26410, 2026).
