# Do independently-trained models share the SAME jailbreak-vulnerable directions? A homogeneity slice of robustness

**Problem:** Adversarial robustness asks whether defenses transfer. Model homogeneity asks whether models share blind spots. The intersection sub-problem: when two different open model families both get jailbroken by the same suffix, do they fail via correlated *internal* refusal-direction collapse, or via unrelated mechanisms?

**Direction:** Decompose 'correlated failures across models' (homogeneity) using robustness probing. Take a small set of known transferable jailbreak prompts, run them on 3-4 open models from different families, extract the refusal-direction projection at the decision token, and measure whether the same prompts that collapse the projection in model A also collapse it in model B (rank correlation of per-prompt 'refusal-suppression' across models).

**Why it matters:** If safety blind spots are correlated across providers, a single attack endangers many deployed systems — a systemic, medium-horizon risk labs underinvest in. Needs only open weights, no training, one GPU. Connects the NeurIPS-best-paper homogeneity finding to a concrete safety mechanism.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025) on inter-model homogeneity; refusal-direction interpretability; transferable GCG/suffix jailbreaks. All ingredients open-source.
