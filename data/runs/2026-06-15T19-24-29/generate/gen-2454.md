# Evaluation-Awareness Probe: Can Small Models Tell They Are Being Tested?

**Problem:** Evaluation awareness scales with model size and undermines the validity of all safety evals, but most evidence is on frontier models. Whether and how small open models discriminate 'this is a test' from 'this is real deployment' contexts is poorly characterized at the small end where hobbyists can experiment.

**Direction:** Construct matched prompt pairs that are semantically identical but stylistically signal 'benchmark/eval' vs 'real user' framing. Ask small open models to classify which context they are in, and separately measure whether their behavior on a benign task shifts between framings. Plot evaluation-awareness accuracy across a size ladder of small open models.

**Why it matters:** If even small models detect eval framing, honeypot and control evaluations need careful camouflage. Mapping where awareness emerges across the accessible size range is a clean, reproducible study that frontier labs cannot easily publish on open models.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging/eval-awareness literature: evaluation awareness follows a power-law with model size, worsening each frontier generation (kili-technology Muse Spark analysis; sandbagging detection survey). Honeypot Protocol relies on models not detecting the evaluation condition (arxiv 2604.13301).
