# Monitor-and-Steer Loop Instability: Probe Drift x Steering Feedback Compounding Over Iterations

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): a probe used to decide when to steer is itself a noisy concept estimate. Failure mode B (steering side effects): steering shifts activations, which shifts the probe's readings. Compound risk: in a closed monitor->steer->monitor loop, small probe errors and steering side effects feed back, causing the system to over-steer or oscillate.

**Direction:** Build a minimal closed loop: a toxicity probe triggers a de-toxify steering vector, then re-reads the probe. Iterate over multiple generation steps. Measure whether the loop converges, oscillates, or runs away (over-suppression / collapse) as a function of probe threshold and steering gain. A small dynamical-systems-style study of a safety control loop.

**Why it matters:** Adaptive steering monitors are emerging; their closed-loop stability is unstudied and is a prerequisite for safe deployment infrastructure. Pure inference, small prototype harness.

**Relevant context:** Probe-driven steering from Beaglehole et al. (probes + steering). Side-effect feedback grounded in arXiv 2603.24543 and adaptive-defense work AdaSteer (arXiv 2504.09466). Stability of the loop is the novel compound angle.
