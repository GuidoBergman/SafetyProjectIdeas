# Confirmation Bias in Judges: Varying the Judge's Prior Answer

**Problem:** Recent work (arXiv:2507.19486) identifies confirmation bias as a challenge for scalable oversight — judges over-weight arguments confirming their prior. The strength of this bias as a function of how confidently the judge's prior is seeded is not systematically mapped.

**Direction:** Use a small open model as judge in a single-consultant protocol on QuALITY/BoolQ. Before the consultant argues, seed the judge with a prior answer at varying confidence levels (none / weak hint / strong assertion). Measure how often the judge flips toward the correct answer when the consultant argues against its seeded prior, across prior-strength conditions.

**Why it matters:** Systematic overseer biases that strategic AI could exploit are an explicit open problem. Quantifying the dose-response of confirmation bias on judge accuracy is directly actionable for protocol design and needs only inference.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2507.19486 'Confirmation bias: A challenge for scalable oversight' documents judges over-weighting confirming evidence. Open problem list: 'Map and mitigate biases in human supervision that could be exploited by strategic AI systems.'
