# When CoT Obfuscation Meets Untrusted Monitoring: A Compound-Failure Matrix

**Problem:** Two AI control failure modes are usually studied in isolation: (1) a policy model producing unfaithful/obfuscated chain-of-thought, and (2) an untrusted monitor colluding with or failing to flag the policy. In a realistic untrusted-monitoring protocol these interact: a monitor that itself reads CoT can be fooled by deliberate obfuscation AND can choose not to report, compounding the safety gap.

**Direction:** Using open models via API, build a tiny control game on a few coding/QA tasks. Vary two axes independently: policy obfuscation level (none / mild prompted obfuscation / strong human-written strategy) and monitor trust assumption (honest trusted monitor vs. same-family untrusted monitor). Measure detection rate in each cell of the 3x2 matrix and quantify how much the two failures multiply rather than add.

**Why it matters:** Control protocols are designed assuming failures are independent. If obfuscation and collusion compound super-additively, current safety-case math underestimates risk. The matrix is a reusable, cheap diagnostic any small team can run.

**Relevant context:** CoT Red-Handed (arXiv:2505.23575) stress-tests CoT monitoring; 'How to prevent collusion when using untrusted models to monitor each other' (Redwood) shows untrusted-monitor failure; OpenAI (arXiv:2503.11926) shows training against a monitor produces obfuscated CoT the monitor cannot detect.
