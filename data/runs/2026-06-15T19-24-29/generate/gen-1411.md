# Replicating concept-probe monitoring on a fuzzy safety concept: 'power-seeking' framing

**Problem:** Concept-vector probes have been validated mostly on crisp concepts (toxicity, hallucination). Whether a probe can be fit for a fuzzy, hard-to-judge concept like power-seeking or manipulation, and whether it beats a judge, replicates the probe advantage in a harder regime.

**Direction:** Construct contrast data for a fuzzy safety concept (manipulative vs. straightforward responses) using a public role-play/persona dataset, fit a linear concept probe on an open model's activations, and compare detection against an LLM-judge. Twist: extend the probes-beat-judges claim to a diffuse, fuzzy-reward concept.

**Why it matters:** Fuzzy reward problems (manipulation, power-seeking) are precisely what labs struggle to evaluate and what external researchers can tackle without proprietary data. Testing whether cheap probes help where judges are weakest is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. probes-outperform-judges; linear probes generalize to strategic deception (insider-trading probe finding); MACHIAVELLI / persona datasets as contrast sources.
