# Shared hallucination: do independent models hallucinate the SAME false facts?

**Problem:** Homogeneity research focuses on style/refusals, but the most dangerous shared blind spot is shared *false confidence*. If models trained on overlapping corpora hallucinate identical wrong answers, cross-model agreement (a common reliability heuristic) becomes a false safety signal.

**Direction:** Use a set of questions designed to elicit hallucinations (obscure or fabricated entities, plausible-but-false claims). Query 8-10 models. Among incorrect answers, measure the rate at which multiple models give the *same wrong* answer vs. independently different wrong answers (string/semantic match). Quantify 'correlated hallucination'.

**Why it matters:** Agreement-based verification (asking several models and trusting consensus) silently fails when models share hallucinations. Demonstrating correlated hallucinations undermines a widely used cheap safety heuristic — high counterfactual value, no lab resources.
