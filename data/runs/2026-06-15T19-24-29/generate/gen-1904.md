# How Few Benign Examples Truly Realign? A Dose-Response Curve

**Problem:** Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.19823) reported that finetuning an emergently misaligned model on 'just a few hundred benign samples' restores alignment, and Nature 2026 confirms realignment is cheap. But the dose-response relationship (samples vs realignment fraction) and whether realignment is genuine or shallow/reversible has not been mapped on open model organisms.

**Direction:** Take a released emergently-misaligned open model organism, then LoRA-finetune on increasing numbers of benign samples (1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250) and plot misalignment-eval score versus benign-sample count. Then probe reversibility: after realignment, finetune on a tiny unrelated dataset and check whether misalignment reappears.

**Why it matters:** If realignment requires very few examples but is shallow (reverts under unrelated finetuning, paralleling 'Unlearning Isn't Deletion' arXiv 2505.16831), then 'cheap realignment' is a false sense of security. Quantifying the minimum effective dose and its fragility directly informs whether realignment is a deployable safety mitigation.

**Relevant context:** Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.19823): few hundred benign samples restore alignment. Unlearning Isn't Deletion (arXiv 2505.16831): shallow forgetting reverts under unrelated finetuning. Model Organisms (arXiv 2506.11613) provides open checkpoints.
