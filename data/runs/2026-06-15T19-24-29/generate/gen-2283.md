# Where Easy-to-Hard Generalization Cracks: Mapping the Difficulty Gap Cliff

**Problem:** Easy-to-hard generalization (train alignment on easy, apply to hard) was proposed as a scalable-oversight remedy, but recent work shows performance degrades sharply once the train-test difficulty gap is large. The location and shape of this cliff for a model-as-verifier are not characterized.

**Direction:** Use a public benchmark with difficulty labels (e.g. MATH by level, or programming problems by difficulty). Treat a model as a verifier/reranker calibrated on easy problems, then test its accuracy as the evaluated problems get progressively harder. Plot verifier reliability vs difficulty gap to find where oversight 'falls off the cliff', and check whether the verifier's confidence stays high even as accuracy collapses (a dangerous overconfidence signature).

**Why it matters:** Identifies the regime where easy-to-hard oversight silently fails, directly addressing the open problem. The overconfidence-at-failure check is exactly the kind of subtle, long-horizon issue labs underinvest in. No training, public data, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Easy-to-Hard Generalization (Sun & Yu et al.); Revisiting Generalization Across Difficulty Levels (arXiv:2511.21692): models trained on easy bins degrade quickly on harder bins, challenging easy-only supervision claims. This adds a calibration-collapse measurement.
