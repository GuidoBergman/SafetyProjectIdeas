# Best-of-n vs RL: Which Optimization Pressure Causes Faster Reward-Model Decay?

**Problem:** The over-optimization chain is: imperfect reward model -> apply optimization pressure -> reward model is gamed -> true reward drops. An under-studied link is whether the *type* of optimization pressure (search-time best-of-n vs gradient-based) causes Goodharting at different rates for the same proxy budget.

**Direction:** Using a small open reward model (or a deterministic synthetic gold function with a noisy learned proxy) and an open generator, compare best-of-n selection against a cheap proxy-guided reranking/iterative approach at matched 'effective optimization' (measured by KL or selection strength). Plot true reward vs optimization power for each method and identify which decays faster.

**Why it matters:** Practical safety guidance: if search-time optimization Goodharts faster, that informs deployment choices. The Gao et al. scaling-law methodology is directly reusable at toy scale with no training from scratch.

**Relevant context:** Gao et al. 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization' (arXiv:2210.10760) — best-of-n and RL over-optimization curves with KL on x-axis; Manheim & Garrabrant Goodhart taxonomy.
