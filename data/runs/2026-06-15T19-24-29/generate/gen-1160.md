# Hill Numbers for LLM Output Diversity: An Ecology-Borrowed Effective-Diversity Metric

**Problem:** The Artificial Hivemind paper documents inter-model homogeneity but mostly uses ad-hoc similarity scores. There is no principled, sample-size-robust, single-axis metric that lets you say 'these N models behave like an effective community of k_eff distinct responders' on a safety-relevant query.

**Direction:** Import Hill numbers (the unifying diversity framework from ecology that subsumes richness, Shannon, and Simpson as a tunable order-q family) and apply them to a bag of LLM responses. Embed responses (Sentence-BERT), cluster into 'response species,' compute Hill diversity of order 0/1/2 across a panel of ~6-10 open models on a slice of Infinity-Chat plus safety-flavored prompts (refusal-edge cases, advice questions). Report effective number of distinct response modes and how it collapses as you move from q=0 (richness) to q=2 (dominance-weighted).

**Why it matters:** A standardized effective-diversity number makes correlated-failure risk measurable and comparable across model panels, which is exactly the monitoring gap flagged for systemic/concentration risk. Ecologists already solved the rare-vs-common-species and sample-coverage problems that plague naive distinct-n-gram metrics, so the transfer is high-leverage.

**Relevant context:** Hill numbers unify Shannon/Simpson/richness as order-q diversity (Roswell 2021 Oikos; Broms 2015 Methods Ecol Evol). Artificial Hivemind / Infinity-Chat (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, arXiv:2510.22954). Diversity metrics for LLMs use Sentence-BERT cosine and distinct-n (arXiv:2310.06452).
