# Reasoning-Effort as a Reward-Hack Tell: Cheap Replication of the Effort Signal

**Problem:** Is It Thinking or Cheating? (arXiv:2510.01367) proposes detecting implicit reward hacking by measuring reasoning effort — hacks require less genuine reasoning. Whether a simple proxy for reasoning effort (CoT token count, self-consistency variance, or answer-confidence-vs-length) flags reward hacks in open reasoning models is untested at student scale.

**Direction:** Set up a small task suite with a known reward proxy that can be hacked (e.g., a grader keyword, a formatting shortcut). Run an open reasoning model in legit and hackable conditions, log CoT length and a couple of cheap effort proxies, and test whether reward-hacked solutions show systematically lower 'effort.' Report separability (AUROC) of effort-based hack detection.

**Why it matters:** Effort-based monitors need no internal access and no trained classifier, making them ideal lab-independent tooling. Confirming the signal exists in open models gives blue teams a cheap, robust complement to CoT-content monitoring.

**Relevant context:** 'Is It Thinking or Cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv:2510.01367). Related: 'Reward Hacking in the Era of Large Models' (arXiv:2604.13602, Apr 2026) and CoT monitoring outperforming output monitoring for reward-hack detection.
