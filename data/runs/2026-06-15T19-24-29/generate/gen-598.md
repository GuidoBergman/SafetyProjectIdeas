# From 'Inductive Bias Predicts Which Goal Is Learned' to a Grokking-Style Test of Aligned-vs-Misaligned Rule Selection

**Problem:** Success state: we can predict, before deployment, whether a model under-determined by its training data will latch onto the intended generalization or a misaligned shortcut. The gap is a controlled grokking-style experiment where an aligned rule and a misaligned shortcut both fit the training data and we watch which one the network's inductive bias selects over training.

**Direction:** Use a small modular-arithmetic or synthetic classification task with two consistent rules (one 'intended', one 'shortcut') differing in circuit complexity. Train a tiny model and track via probing which rule is internally represented across training (does it grok the simpler one? does it switch?). Small model, single GPU, classic grokking regime.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from inductive-bias-based safety prediction, the foundation is observing rule-selection dynamics directly. A grokking-style demonstration of aligned-vs-misaligned rule preference makes the Open Phil inductive-bias question concrete and is fully feasible on toy models without lab scale.
