# Does Self-Conditioning on Errors Explain Long-Horizon Eval Collapse?

**Problem:** Long-horizon execution studies report accuracy approaching zero past ~120 steps and propose that models 'self-condition on their own errors.' This mechanism is asserted but rarely isolated experimentally in a controlled benchmark.

**Direction:** Build a simple multi-step deterministic task (e.g. iterated arithmetic or list transformation) where each step's correctness is checkable. Run an open model and compare two conditions: full self-generated history vs history where earlier errors are silently corrected before the next step. If error-self-conditioning is the cause, the corrected-history condition should sharply extend the horizon.

**Why it matters:** Agentic safety evals depend on understanding why long tasks fail; if collapse is self-conditioning rather than reasoning limits, monitoring/resetting strategies become an obvious safety lever. Cheap, fully reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Measuring Long Horizon Execution in LLMs (arXiv 2509.09677): accuracy near zero past 120 steps; identifies self-conditioning on own errors as a failure mode distinct from reasoning capacity.
