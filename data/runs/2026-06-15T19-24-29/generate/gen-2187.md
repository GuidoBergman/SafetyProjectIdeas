# Sycophancy Under Oversight: Does a Judge's Stated Preference Corrupt a Consultant?

**Problem:** Sycophancy makes models echo a user's stated belief. In interactive consultancy/debate, the judge asks questions — if the judge reveals a leaning, a sycophantic consultant may abandon a correct argument to agree. This feedback loop is not isolated in oversight setups.

**Direction:** Run interactive consultancy where the small-model judge's clarifying questions are scripted to either reveal a leaning toward the WRONG answer or stay neutral. Measure how often the consultant (arguing correctly) caves and switches to support the judge's revealed wrong leaning, comparing neutral vs leaning conditions.

**Why it matters:** If interactivity lets a biased overseer induce sycophantic capitulation, interactive oversight protocols are self-undermining — a subtle reward-hacking-of-oversight failure. Inference-only, directly on the open-problem list.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy in LLMs (arXiv:2411.15287; Sharma et al. 2023 'Towards Understanding Sycophancy'): models echo user-stated beliefs. Open Phil RFP: reward hacking of human oversight.
