# Cluster-norm vs. plain CCS: independently checking the claimed fix for unsupervised probing

**Problem:** Cluster-norm (arXiv:2407.18712) was proposed to fix a failure mode of unsupervised latent-knowledge probing by normalizing within clusters before applying CCS. As a recent single-paper fix, an independent replication and ablation on fresh tasks would establish whether the improvement is robust or dataset-specific.

**Direction:** Reimplement plain CCS and cluster-norm CCS on a small open model across several binary-QA datasets not used in the original paper. Measure the truth-recovery improvement, then ablate the clustering step (number of clusters, clustering feature) to find when cluster-norm helps vs. hurts. Report whether the fix generalizes beyond the original evaluation.

**Why it matters:** Independent replication of proposed ELK-probing improvements is core to building trustworthy elicitation methods and is precisely the kind of verification valuable outside a lab — open models, public data, one GPU, no proprietary anything.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2407.18712 (cluster-norm) and arXiv:2212.03827 (CCS). Context open problem: formal frameworks for ELK.
