# Signal Detection Theory (d-prime) for Judge Discrimination vs Bias

**Problem:** Judge accuracy conflates two things: ability to discriminate good from bad arguments, and a response bias toward accepting/rejecting. Oversight research rarely separates them.

**Direction:** Apply signal detection theory from psychophysics. From a weak judge's accept/reject decisions on correct vs flawed arguments, compute d-prime (sensitivity) and criterion c (bias). Show how two judges with equal accuracy differ in exploitability, and how prompt changes move sensitivity vs bias. Pure analysis on judge outputs from an open model.

**Why it matters:** Disentangling sensitivity from bias tells us whether an overseer is exploitable via its decision threshold, a precise, training-free characterization of systematic oversight error.

**Relevant context:** Signal detection theory (d', criterion) from cognitive science. Automation/confirmation bias in evaluators (arXiv:2507.19486; arXiv:2010.07938). Open problem: characterize biases overseers consistently exhibit (Anthropic).
