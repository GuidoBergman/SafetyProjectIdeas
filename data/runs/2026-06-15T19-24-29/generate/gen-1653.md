# Replicating Linear Probe 'Lie Detection' on an Open Model-Organism of Deception

**Problem:** Linear probes can detect whether a model 'believes' a statement is true. But most evaluations test honest-vs-false statements, not a model actively instructed to deceive. Whether a truth probe trained on simple true/false statements transfers to detecting an instructed lie — in a small open model prompted to role-play deception — is a sharper, more safety-relevant test.

**Direction:** Train a linear truth probe on standard true/false statement activations in a small open model (Geometry-of-Truth style). The twist: construct a model-organism-style setup where the same model is prompted to knowingly assert false statements (instructed deception), and test whether the truth probe fires on the model's internal 'belief' even while the output is a lie — replicating the probing method but on deceptive rather than merely false content.

**Why it matters:** A model that says one thing while internally 'knowing' another is the core of deceptive-alignment threat models. Probing a lightweight instructed-deception model organism to see whether internal-state lie detection works — using public models and datasets on one GPU — is a feasible 30h slice of a flagship long-horizon safety problem that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Marks & Tegmark 'Geometry of Truth' and 'Inference-Time Intervention' (arXiv:2306.03341) show linear truth/honesty directions. Probing for belief-vs-output divergence relates to deception detection; instructed-deception prompting is a known lightweight model-organism technique.
