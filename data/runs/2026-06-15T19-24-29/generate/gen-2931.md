# Noise-Injection Dose-Response as a Cheap Sandbagging Tripwire

**Problem:** Sandbagging risk chain: model has capability -> model chooses to underperform on the eval -> evaluator records a low score -> unsafe deployment decision. The under-studied link is making the 'chooses to underperform' step observable cheaply. Noise injection reveals hidden capabilities, but no simple, standardized dose-response protocol exists for non-experts.

**Direction:** On an open instruction-tuned model prompted to sandbag a benign capability task (e.g., a math or trivia set), inject increasing amounts of Gaussian noise into weights/activations and plot accuracy vs noise. A genuinely weak model degrades monotonically; a sandbagging model shows a characteristic non-monotonic bump where noise disrupts the suppression circuit. Calibrate a simple detection statistic and report its true/false-positive rate.

**Why it matters:** Gives regulators and small teams a model-agnostic, training-free sandbagging tripwire requiring only forward passes on one GPU. Targets the elicitation link in the dangerous-capability-concealment chain that frontier labs cannot fully self-certify.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2412.01784 'Noise Injection Reveals Hidden Capabilities of Sandbagging Language Models' establishes noise injection as a model-agnostic, unsupervised sandbagging detector; the AISafetyLab/FSF open-problems list flags sandbagging detection as a core open problem.
