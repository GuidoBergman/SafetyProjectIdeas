# Epidemiological Test-Contamination Screening via Memorization Assays

**Problem:** Data contamination inflates benchmark scores, but contamination is usually treated as binary. Epidemiology routinely estimates true prevalence from imperfect screening tests using known test characteristics.

**Direction:** Import prevalence-from-imperfect-test estimation (Rogan-Gladen correction) from epidemiology. Build cheap contamination 'screening tests' (e.g. canary-string completion, members-vs-nonmembers perplexity gap, exact-continuation probing) with measurable sensitivity/specificity on a held-out known-contaminated set. Apply them to an open safety benchmark + open model and back out an adjusted estimate of contamination prevalence with confidence intervals, rather than a raw flag rate.

**Why it matters:** Turns ad hoc contamination checks into a calibrated prevalence estimate, addressing the contamination open problem with proper uncertainty. Runs on open models/benchmarks, no training; counterfactually valuable for anyone judging benchmark trustworthiness.

**Relevant context:** Rogan-Gladen and capture-recapture corrections estimate true prevalence from imperfect screening tests (PLOS One 10.1371/journal.pone.0159832); data contamination inflating scores is flagged in the International AI Safety Report 2026 (subfield context).
