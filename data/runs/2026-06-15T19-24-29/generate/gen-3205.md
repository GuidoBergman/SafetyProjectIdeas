# Criteria-Based Content Analysis (CBCA) Applied to Model Self-Reports

**Problem:** When asked to explain its reasoning or justify a decision, can we distinguish a model's genuine account from a fabricated/deceptive justification? Forensic psychology uses CBCA criteria (detail, contextual embedding, spontaneous corrections) that score higher for truthful human statements.

**Direction:** Operationalize CBCA's verbal-credibility criteria into an automatable rubric and score model-generated justifications in known-honest vs known-deceptive conditions (e.g., model that secretly took a forbidden action then explains itself vs one that acted honestly). Use a second LLM as a CBCA scorer with a strict rubric. Measure whether CBCA scores separate honest from deceptive accounts.

**Why it matters:** Brings 40 years of forensic statement-analysis methodology to AI honesty verification. A purely text-based, training-free auditing tool relevant to the UK AISI honesty-verification agenda.

**Relevant context:** CBCA/SVA: truth-tellers score higher across 37 studies (Statement Validity Analysis literature). Statement-evidence inconsistency is a verbal deception cue. Alignment faking CoT shows explicit deceptive justifications (arXiv:2412.14093).
