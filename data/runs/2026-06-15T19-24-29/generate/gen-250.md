# Refusal-Direction Ablation: Does the 'Single Direction' Survive Multi-Turn and Paraphrase?

**Problem:** Arditi et al. (2024) showed refusal is mediated by a single residual-stream direction; ablating it removes refusal. Open questions: does the SAME direction extracted from single-turn harmful prompts still mediate refusal in multi-turn conversations, indirect/obfuscated requests, and across paraphrases — or does the failure mode require re-extraction per context?

**Direction:** Using the public refusal_direction codebase on a small open model, extract the refusal direction from standard single-turn harmful prompts. Then measure how well ablating that fixed direction suppresses refusal on (a) multi-turn escalation, (b) low-explicitness/euphemistic requests, (c) paraphrased prompts. Quantify the generalization gap of the 'single direction.'

**Why it matters:** If one fixed direction generalizes across contexts, that is a sharper safety vulnerability and a sharper interpretability target; if not, it bounds the threat and suggests refusal is more distributed than claimed. Fully white-box on open weights with released code — exactly the kind of mechanistic robustness work that needs no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' (NeurIPS 2024) with code at github.com/andyrdt/refusal_direction. COSMIC (arXiv:2506.00085) and 'SOM Directions are Better than One' (arXiv:2511.08379) question single-direction sufficiency, motivating a generalization audit.
