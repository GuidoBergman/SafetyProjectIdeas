# Empirically Testing the Redundant-Information Core of the Natural Abstraction Hypothesis

**Problem:** The Natural Abstraction Hypothesis claims diverse systems converge on the same abstractions, formalized via redundant/conserved information. Wentworth notes the field 'lacks the tools' to test it. A minimal, accessible empirical probe of the redundancy claim is missing.

**Direction:** On a simple generative process (e.g. a Gaussian latent generating many noisy observed variables), empirically estimate which functions of the low-level variables are redundantly represented (recoverable from many disjoint subsets). Then train tiny models / fit probes from different random initializations and test whether they converge on the same redundant summary. Quantify abstraction agreement across systems.

**Why it matters:** If natural abstractions are real and detectable, alignment via shared human-AI concepts becomes more tractable; if not, that bounds a whole research program. A toy-scale, mathematically clean test moves the empirical needle with only a laptop/CPU.

**Relevant context:** Wentworth 'Testing The Natural Abstraction Hypothesis: Project Intro' and 'Natural Abstractions: Key Claims, Theorems, and Critiques' (LessWrong/AF): redundant/conserved information defines natural abstractions; the hypothesis is fundamentally empirical but under-tooled.
