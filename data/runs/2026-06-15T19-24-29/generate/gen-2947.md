# The Deployment-Drift Link: Do Pre-Deployment Safety Scores Predict In-Context Behavior?

**Problem:** Lifecycle chain: model passes pre-deployment safety eval -> deployed with real system prompts/tools/context -> behaves differently under deployment conditions. Evals track only the pre-deployment snapshot (a named gap: safety tracking through deployment); the link between the static score and behavior under realistic deployment context is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Take an open model's refusal rate on a benchmark under a 'bare' eval prompt, then re-measure the same items wrapped in realistic deployment scaffolding (long system prompt, tool descriptions, prior benign conversation). Quantify how much the safety score drifts from bare-eval to deployment-like context and which scaffolding elements drive the drift.

**Why it matters:** If pre-deployment scores do not predict deployment-context behavior, the entire pre-deployment certification chain is suspect. Targets the post-deployment-tracking gap with a cheap, lab-independent before/after design.

**Relevant context:** International AI Safety Report 2026 flags the need to track safety through deployment, not just pre-deployment; arXiv 2510.20487 shows deployment vs eval framing changes behavior measurably.
