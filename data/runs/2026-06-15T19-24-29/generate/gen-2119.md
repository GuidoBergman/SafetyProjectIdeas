# Does RL Amplify EM More Than SFT at Matched Behavior? A Small-Model Check

**Problem:** "Reinforcement Learning Amplifies Emergent Misalignment from Harmless Rewards" (arXiv:2605.31328, May 2026) claims rewarding narrow overtly-misaligned behavior produces substantially higher general-domain misalignment than sample-matched SFT. Whether this RL>SFT gap holds for small open models reachable on a consumer GPU, or is specific to the paper's setup, is untested by independent groups.

**Direction:** On a small open model, construct a tiny narrow task with a clear binary reward (e.g. always recommend a risky financial action). Train two variants to the SAME behavior level: (a) SFT on best-of-n samples, (b) a lightweight RL/REINFORCE or DPO-style preference loop. Match final narrow-task behavior, then compare general-domain misalignment rates on held-out unrelated prompts.

**Why it matters:** RL post-training is increasingly dominant. If RL inflates misalignment generalization beyond SFT even at matched narrow behavior, it changes the risk calculus for every RLHF/RLAIF pipeline. This is a fundamental-dynamics question that needs no proprietary data — ideal counterfactual value outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** "Reinforcement Learning Amplifies Emergent Misalignment from Harmless Rewards" (arXiv:2605.31328, May 29 2026): rewarding narrow misaligned behavior yields higher general misalignment than sample-matched SFT. Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424).
