# Cheap Reproduction of Evaluation-Awareness Steering Directions in a Small Open Model

**Problem:** Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness (arXiv:2507.01786) shows linear directions can read and steer whether a model believes it is being evaluated, but the original work focused on specific models. Whether a simple difference-of-means 'eval-awareness' direction transfers and steers behavior in a small open model that a student can run on one GPU is unverified.

**Direction:** Assemble paired prompts that are clearly 'this is an evaluation/test' vs. 'this is a real deployment/user.' Extract residual-stream activations from a 7-8B open model, compute a difference-of-means direction at a few layers, and (a) train a logistic probe to classify eval vs. deploy framings, (b) add/subtract the direction during generation and measure whether a downstream behavior (e.g., willingness to refuse, verbosity of safety caveats) shifts. No training required; only forward passes and activation arithmetic.

**Why it matters:** Evaluation awareness is a core enabler of sandbagging and alignment faking. A reproducible, single-GPU recipe for finding and steering the eval-awareness direction in an open model lowers the barrier for the whole community to study the phenomenon outside frontier labs.

**Relevant context:** 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models' (arXiv:2507.01786, Jul 2025) finds linear probes detect eval-awareness and steering vectors causally shift it. 'In-Context Environments Induce Evaluation-Awareness' (arXiv:2603.03824, Mar 2026) and 'Evaluation Faking' (arXiv:2505.17815) show stronger reasoners fake more.
