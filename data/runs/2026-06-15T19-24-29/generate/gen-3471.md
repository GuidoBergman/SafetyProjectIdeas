# Knowledge-Conflict Honesty: Does the Model Admit Why It Changed Its Mind?

**Problem:** When a model's parametric knowledge conflicts with context provided in the prompt, it may switch answers — but does its CoT honestly attribute the switch to the context, or hide it? There is no compact benchmark probing CoT honesty under deliberate knowledge conflicts.

**Direction:** Take questions the model answers correctly from parametric knowledge, then inject context asserting a different answer. For items where the model switches, check whether the CoT explicitly cites the injected context as the reason. Report the rate of 'silent switches' (answer changed but context-reliance unverbalized) per open model.

**Why it matters:** Silent context-driven switches mean prompt-injected or retrieved misinformation can change answers without leaving a monitorable trace — a faithfulness and security concern for RAG systems. Behavioral, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** 'Do Models Know Why They Changed Their Mind? ... CoT Under Knowledge Conflict' (arXiv 2605.27773) and 'Contextual vs Parametric CoT Faithfulness' (2605.24960) study this exact phenomenon. No compact open-model silent-switch benchmark exists.
