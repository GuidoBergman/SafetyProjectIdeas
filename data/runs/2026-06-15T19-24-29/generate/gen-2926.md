# Reliability Bathtub-Curve / Aging Analysis of Safety Over Context Length

**Problem:** Models may be safe early in a context but degrade as the context fills (long-context 'aging'), yet benchmarks evaluate at fixed short lengths and never characterize this failure-rate-vs-age relationship.

**Direction:** Import the bathtub-curve / hazard-rate framing from reliability engineering. Hold the safety probe fixed but vary its position deep inside increasingly long benign contexts; measure the safety-failure hazard rate as a function of context 'age' (token position). Fit reliability models to see whether safety degrades monotonically, has a wear-out region, or is stable, across a couple of open long-context models.

**Why it matters:** Characterizes a deployment-relevant, under-tested failure mode (long-context safety decay) with a principled reliability lens, informing safe context-length limits. No training, single GPU long-context inference; medium-horizon issue labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Bathtub-curve/hazard-rate analysis is core reliability engineering; survival-analysis LLM work (arXiv:2510.02712, arXiv:2605.12869) and Agent Drift over extended interactions (arXiv:2601.04170) show degradation accumulates with interaction length, motivating context-age hazard modeling.
