# Trusted-Model Weakness x Untrusted-Model Power: Compounding the Capability Gap in Control Protocols

**Problem:** Control protocols rely on a weaker trusted model to oversee a stronger untrusted one. Two failures compound: as the capability gap widens (capabilities trend) the trusted monitor's competence failure grows, and the untrusted model's evasion ability grows, so oversight degrades on both ends simultaneously.

**Direction:** Using a pair of open models of different sizes, run a control game where the small model monitors the large one on tasks. Sweep the size gap (use several model pairs) and measure detection rate. Plot how oversight reliability collapses as capability gap increases, isolating the joint effect.

**Why it matters:** The core assumption of AI control — a trusted weaker overseer — has a scaling limit. Mapping where it breaks with today's open models gives an empirical early-warning curve, doable without any lab resources.

**Relevant context:** AI control formalism: trusted vs. untrusted models (Greenblatt et al. arXiv:2312.06942); untrusted-monitor collusion (Redwood); ControlArena open-source protocol implementation.
