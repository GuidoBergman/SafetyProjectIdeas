# Cross-Family Transfer of Refusal Directions as a Cheap Defense Audit

**Problem:** Refusal directions are consistent within a model but it is unclear whether a refusal direction extracted from one model family transfers to detect jailbreaks in another, which would enable cheap defense transfer but might also enable cheap cross-model attacks.

**Direction:** Extract refusal/jailbreak directions from model A (e.g., Llama-3-8B) and apply them as detectors on model B (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B) activations. Measure cross-family detection AUROC to quantify how much a defender can reuse one probe across models — and conversely how much an attacker learns about B from A.

**Why it matters:** Behavioral-geometry work shows defenses can transfer across model populations; a small, reproducible cross-family probe-transfer study tests this directly and informs whether cheap shared defenses (or shared vulnerabilities) exist. No training, inference-only, high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Behavioral geometry of models (arXiv:2605.26409): defenses transfer across a population, three models cover most. Arditi et al. (NeurIPS 2024): refusal direction consistent within models. Cross-family probe transfer for detection is the natural untested follow-up flagged in the subfield context ('replicate on different model families').
