# Replicating Multilingual Political-Stance Steering Findings on Small Open Models Using Activation-Free Prompting

**Problem:** Work on multilingual political views shows LLM ideology can shift across languages and be steered, but steering studies often use activation-level interventions requiring engineering depth. For governance, the more deployment-relevant question is whether ordinary prompt-level framing in different languages shifts a model's expressed policy stances—accessible to any user and any regulator-style auditor.

**Direction:** Replicate the cross-lingual political-stance finding using only PROMPT-level manipulations (no activation steering) on 3-4 small open models. The twist is restricting interventions to user-accessible prompting in 3 languages and measuring stance shift on a fixed policy-statement battery, isolating the language effect from the intervention-sophistication effect.

**Why it matters:** If trivial multilingual prompting shifts policy stances, neutrality regulation premised on a single-language audit is inadequate. Uses only open models and prompting, no interpretability expertise, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Multilingual Political Views of LLMs: Identification and Steering' (arXiv 2507.22623) and 'Bias Beyond Borders' (arXiv 2601.23001). Twist = prompt-only, beginner-accessible replication.
