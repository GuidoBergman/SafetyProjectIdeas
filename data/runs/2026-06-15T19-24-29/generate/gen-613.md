# Impossibility Sketch: Can a Single Probe Be Robust to Both Deception and Distribution Shift?

**Problem:** Two desiderata for an oversight probe — robustness to deceptive concealment (interpretability/model-organism) and robustness to distribution shift (robustness) — may be in tension. A probe regularized to ignore the deception trigger may overfit the source distribution; one made shift-robust may become exploitable by deception. This is an informal impossibility that deserves an empirical Pareto map.

**Direction:** On quirky open models, train a family of honesty probes with varying regularization/feature-selection. For each, measure (a) robustness to the deception trigger and (b) transfer accuracy under a held-out context shift. Plot the (deception-robustness, shift-robustness) frontier to test whether improving one reliably costs the other, suggesting a no-free-lunch boundary.

**Why it matters:** Maps an empirical version of an impossibility-style result, the subfield's signature methodology. Tells probe designers whether a single cheap probe can ever serve both safety roles. Inference + simple linear probes, fully open.

**Relevant context:** Subfield methodology includes impossibility results; quirky-model probing (arXiv:2312.01037); linear probe OOD brittleness (arXiv:2204.02683).
