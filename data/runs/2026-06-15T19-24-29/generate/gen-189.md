# Does Decoding Temperature Modulate Shallow-Alignment Exploits?

**Problem:** Shallow alignment and prefilling exploits are usually measured at default/greedy decoding. Whether raising sampling temperature widens the first-token vulnerability (more probability mass on compliant openings) has not been isolated.

**Direction:** On an open model, measure prefilling and adaptive-attack success across temperatures {0, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3} and also measure the probability assigned to a single compliant first token under each temperature. Test whether temperature is a free attacker lever that amplifies shallow exploits.

**Why it matters:** Many deployments expose temperature to users; if higher temperature reliably weakens shallow safety, it is an overlooked deployment-config risk. Trivial compute, fully reproducible. (Prior catalogue covered consistency across decoding params broadly; this isolates the mechanistic link to first-token safety mass.)

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. arXiv:2406.05946 (first-token safety mass). arXiv:2504.21038 (prefill attacks). Prior idea 'Consistency of Safety Across Decoding Parameters' exists — this differs by tying temperature to the shallow-alignment first-token probability mechanism.
