# Compute-Threshold Governance x Algorithmic Efficiency Gains: The Decaying FLOP Trigger

**Problem:** Failure mode A: governance pegs systemic-risk obligations to a fixed training-compute threshold (e.g. 10^25 FLOP in the EU AI Act). Failure mode B: algorithmic efficiency improves, so equally-capable models fall below the threshold over time. The two compound into silent regulatory erosion: the same dangerous capability escapes oversight purely because it got cheaper to train.

**Direction:** Assemble public data on model capability vs training FLOP over 2022-2026 (Epoch AI data, public model reports) and fit a simple capability-per-FLOP efficiency trend. Project how a fixed FLOP threshold maps to a moving capability bar, estimating how many 'threshold-exempt' models would have triggered obligations under earlier efficiency. Output a quantified 'threshold decay' chart.

**Why it matters:** Gives policymakers a concrete decay rate for compute-based triggers, motivating capability-indexed rather than FLOP-indexed governance. Pure data analysis on public Epoch tracking data — exactly the kind of work labs won't prioritize.

**Relevant context:** Computing Power and the Governance of AI (arXiv:2402.08797); Epoch AI compute tracking; EU AI Act systemic-risk FLOP threshold (artificialintelligenceact.eu).
