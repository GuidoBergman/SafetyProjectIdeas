# Stroop-Style Interference Tests Between Competing Steering Concepts

**Problem:** What happens when you steer toward two semantically conflicting concepts at once (e.g. 'be cautious' + 'be confident')? Cognitive psychology's Stroop paradigm precisely quantifies interference between conflicting cues. Steering has no analogue for measuring concept conflict.

**Direction:** Adapt the Stroop interference paradigm. Construct congruent vs. incongruent steering-concept pairs and measure 'interference cost' as the difference in target-concept expression and in latency-proxy (e.g. token-level confidence) between congruent and incongruent conditions. Map which safety-relevant concept pairs interfere strongly (dangerous when both must hold) vs. coexist cleanly.

**Why it matters:** Real deployments steer multiple objectives simultaneously; conflicting safety goals could produce silent failures where neither is satisfied. A Stroop-style interference matrix tells designers which safety concepts can be jointly steered. Inference-only, beginner-friendly, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Stroop interference quantifies conflict between automatic and controlled processes (ScienceDirect Stroop overview). Multi-concept steering by combination is established (Beaglehole et al.). Niche-overlap analogy reinforces the conflict framing.
