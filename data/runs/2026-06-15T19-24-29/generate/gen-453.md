# Replicating truth-probe causal steering, but measuring whether steering generalizes off-distribution

**Problem:** The Geometry of Truth showed you can surgically intervene along the truth direction to flip a model's treatment of false statements as true (causal evidence). Steering demos are usually shown on in-distribution declarative statements. Whether the *same* steering vector still flips behavior on out-of-distribution inputs (different domain, question form, or longer context) is an open robustness question.

**Direction:** Reproduce the difference-in-mean truth direction and the activation-addition steering on a small open model on the original-style statements. Then apply the *unchanged* steering vector to off-distribution inputs (a new factual domain, question-answering format) and measure how reliably it still flips truth judgments. Report a steering-generalization curve.

**Why it matters:** Activation steering is being proposed as a lightweight control/monitoring lever. If steering vectors are brittle off-distribution, that bounds their usefulness for honesty interventions; if robust, it strengthens the linear-truth-feature story relevant to ELK. Either way it is a reproducible, no-training contribution on public models that probes how 'real' the truth feature is.

**Relevant context:** The Geometry of Truth (arXiv:2310.06824): causal intervention flips true/false treatment, difference-in-mean directions most causally implicated; follow-up arXiv:2506.00823 on generalization across transformations. Open Problem (context): ELK. Common Methodologies (context): linear-algebraic probing/intervention.
