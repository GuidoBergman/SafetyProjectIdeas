# Does Forcing the Judge to Answer First Inoculate Against Anchoring on the Model's Stated Answer?

**Problem:** Recchia's confirmation-bias result suggests overseers anchor on the AI's stated answer. A natural follow-up intervention — having the overseer commit to its own answer BEFORE seeing the AI's — has not been cleanly isolated as a debiasing mechanism in oversight.

**Direction:** On a public QA set, compare oversight accuracy under three conditions with an open judge model standing in for the evaluator: (a) see AI answer then judge, (b) judge produces own answer first, then sees AI answer and decides, (c) judge sees only evidence, never the AI's claimed answer. Measure error rate on items where the AI is wrong.

**Why it matters:** A one-line protocol change ('answer first') that removes anchoring would be a cheap, broadly deployable oversight fix. Testing it isolates a candidate mechanism behind Recchia's effect. Inference-only, public data.

**Relevant context:** Recchia et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.19486): evaluators grew confident in wrong AI answers; anchoring on the stated answer is a leading explanation. Builds on the confirmation-bias finding.
