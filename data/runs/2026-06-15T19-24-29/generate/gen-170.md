# Replicating the Obfuscation Tax Across Tasks

**Problem:** Bailey et al. (arXiv:2412.09565) found that evading latent-space probes via obfuscated activations imposes an 'obfuscation tax' — model performance drops on a complex task (SQL). It is unclear whether this tax is task-specific or a general phenomenon defenders can rely on.

**Direction:** Reproduce the obfuscation-suffix attack against a simple harmfulness probe on an open model, then measure capability degradation across a battery of tasks of varying complexity (arithmetic, factual QA, code, multi-step reasoning). Quantify the obfuscation tax per task to see whether harder tasks reliably resist obfuscation.

**Why it matters:** If the obfuscation tax is robust and grows with task complexity, defenders can deploy probes on high-stakes complex tasks with more confidence. This is a pure evaluation needing no training and directly informs monitor design.

**Relevant context:** Bailey et al., 'Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses' (arXiv:2412.09565): attacks reduce probe recall 100%->0% while keeping 90% jailbreak rate, but evading monitors reduces SQL-writing performance ('obfuscation tax'). Code/results public.
