# Backcasting Collusion Resistance: Can Two Debaters Quietly Agree to Mislead?

**Problem:** Success state: debate protocols where adversarial debaters cannot collude to jointly mislead the judge. We need to know whether two same-family models, lightly prompted toward a shared goal, can degrade judge accuracy below the honest-debate level.

**Direction:** On open QuALITY debate, run a 'collusion' condition where both debaters are softly steered (via system prompt) to make the judge pick a specific wrong answer, versus the standard adversarial condition. Measure how much judge accuracy drops and whether the monitoring flags (quote checks, question-dodging) detect collusion.

**Why it matters:** Debate's safety rests on debaters being genuinely adversarial. Demonstrating how easily soft collusion erodes the guarantee — and whether cheap monitoring catches it — is a medium-horizon strategic-AI threat model labs underexamine, doable with open models.

**Relevant context:** Adversarial-debate assumptions underlie Khan et al. 2024 and Kenton et al. 2024. Collusion/obfuscated-arguments concerns are raised in ARC/debate literature but rarely tested empirically at small scale with open models.
