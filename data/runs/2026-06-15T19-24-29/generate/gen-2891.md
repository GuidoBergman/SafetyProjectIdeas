# Scorer Disagreement Audit: How Much Does the Grader Decide the Verdict?

**Problem:** AISafetyLab reports that different scorers produce discrepant results for the same attack/method, but evaluators usually pick one autograder and trust it. The magnitude of verdict-flipping across reasonable scorer choices, and which prompt categories are most scorer-sensitive, is rarely quantified.

**Direction:** Fix a set of model responses to harmful prompts. Score them with multiple reasonable open graders (different open LLM judges, a keyword/refusal-string matcher, and a small classifier). Compute pairwise agreement (Cohen's kappa), identify the items where verdicts flip, and characterize what makes an item scorer-sensitive (ambiguity, partial compliance, length).

**Why it matters:** If the headline safety number depends mostly on which grader you chose, cross-paper and cross-model comparisons are meaningless. A scorer-disagreement map tells the community where to demand multiple graders and supports standardized scoring protocols.

**Relevant context:** AISafetyLab documents discrepant scorer results; judge-bias surveys (arXiv:2410.21819) and 'The Judge Who Never Admits' (arXiv:2602.07996) show hidden shortcuts. Multiple open graders run on one GPU; analysis is pure statistics.
