# Epidemiological Sensitivity/Specificity Framing for Oversight Screening

**Problem:** Oversight protocols are screening tests for 'is this output bad', but we rarely report sensitivity, specificity, PPV/NPV at realistic base rates of misbehavior, so headline accuracy misleads.

**Direction:** Transfer the diagnostic-test evaluation framework from epidemiology. Treat a weak judge as a screening test; on a labeled set of good/bad outputs, compute sensitivity, specificity, and PPV/NPV swept across plausible (low) misbehavior base rates. Visualize how a 'good' judge becomes near-useless when bad outputs are rare. Pure analysis.

**Why it matters:** Realistic base rates of rare strategic misbehavior are exactly the long-horizon regime labs underweight; a base-rate-aware oversight evaluation is high counterfactual value and needs no special resources.

**Relevant context:** Sensitivity/specificity/PPV and base-rate effects from diagnostic epidemiology. LLM-as-judge reliability under class imbalance (arXiv:2412.12509 uses kappa for imbalance). No standardized oversight benchmark exists (context gap).
