# Replicating Deceptive-Persuasiveness With Honest vs Sneaky Provers on Open Models

**Problem:** The prover-verifier paper trains 'helpful' and 'sneaky' provers, finding sneaky provers can fool verifiers. That required RL training. Whether the basic phenomenon, that a sneaky prover can craft incorrect-but-convincing solutions that fool a weak verifier, replicates with prompting alone on open models is an accessible, training-free question.

**Direction:** Without RL, use prompting to instantiate a 'helpful' prover (correct, legible solutions) and a 'sneaky' prover (incorrect solutions argued convincingly) on grade-school math with open models. Use a small open model as verifier. Replicate the headline finding: does the sneaky prover fool the weak verifier more than chance, and how does verifier size affect susceptibility?

**Why it matters:** It tests whether the legibility/deception dynamic central to prover-verifier games is a deep phenomenon or an artifact of RL training, using only prompting and open models. A clean, beginner-feasible model-organism slice of deceptive persuasion against an overseer.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.13692) train sneaky provers via RL to fool small verifiers. The training-free, prompting-only, open-model replication of sneaky-prover susceptibility is the twist, removing the lab-scale RL requirement.
