# Do Autograder Disagreements Reflect Noise or Systematic Patterns?

**Problem:** The Skewed Score framework notes autograders disagree with humans and each other, raising whether disagreement is random noise or consistent bias. A focused experiment can decompose the variance for safety scoring specifically.

**Direction:** Score a set of model responses to harmful prompts with 2-3 open autograder models, each repeated multiple times at nonzero temperature. Decompose disagreement into within-grader noise (repeat variance) vs between-grader systematic difference (mean offset). Identify response categories where graders systematically diverge (e.g. partial compliance, hedged refusals).

**Why it matters:** Scorer reliability is an explicit open problem from AISafetyLab; quantifying noise-vs-bias tells evaluators whether averaging fixes the issue or whether grader choice changes conclusions. Single-GPU, reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Skewed Score: a statistical framework to assess autograders (arXiv 2507.03772); context note that different scorers produce discrepant results for the same method (AISafetyLab).
