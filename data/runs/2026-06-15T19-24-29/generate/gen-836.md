# Definitional Drift: How Inconsistently Do Major AI Policies Define 'Frontier', 'Systemic Risk', and 'High-Risk'?

**Problem:** Core governance terms — 'frontier model', 'systemic risk', 'high-risk', 'general-purpose AI' — anchor binding obligations, but each framework (EU AI Act, US EO, UK approach, lab frameworks) defines them differently or not at all. This definitional drift is a failure mode that lets the same model be in-scope in one regime and out in another, and it has not been systematically tabulated.

**Direction:** Characterize definitional drift across regimes. Extract the operative definitions of 5-6 key terms from ~6-8 governance documents, normalize them, and build a comparison matrix showing where definitions overlap, conflict, or are absent. Then take a few concrete real models and test how each regime classifies them — quantifying how often the same system gets contradictory regulatory statuses.

**Why it matters:** International coordination on AI governance is impossible if the foundational definitions don't align. A precise drift map is directly actionable for harmonization efforts (INAISI, treaty design) and shows where a model can fall through every net. Pure document analysis — no model, no compute, classic independent policy work.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act GPAI/systemic-risk definitions; US EO compute-based definitions; 'International Agreements on AI Safety' (arXiv 2503.18956); Open Problems in Technical AI Governance.
