# Benign 10-Shot Overfitting: How Cheaply Can Safety Be Undone on an Open Model?

**Problem:** "Attack via Overfitting: 10-shot Benign Fine-tuning to Jailbreak LLMs" (arXiv:2510.02833) and "TrojanPraise" (arXiv:2601.12460) show very few benign-looking examples can strip safety alignment, with overfitting as the key mechanism. A clean, reproducible measurement of the minimal example count vs. safety loss on a current small open model is valuable.

**Direction:** Take a small safety-tuned open model. Run benign LoRA fine-tunes with 3, 10, 30 innocuous examples and ablate the overfitting stage (vary epochs). Measure refusal rate on a held-out harmful-prompt set as a function of example count and overfitting. Confirm that ablating overfitting drastically reduces attack success.

**Why it matters:** Fine-tuning APIs and open weights make benign-data safety removal a present danger. A precise dose-response (and confirmation that overfitting is the lever) helps defenders set guardrails and is exactly the kind of robustness audit external groups can publish without lab resources.

**Relevant context:** "Attack via Overfitting: 10-shot Benign Fine-tuning to Jailbreak LLMs" (arXiv:2510.02833): ablating the overfitting stage drastically reduces attack success. "TrojanPraise" (arXiv:2601.12460); "What is in Your Safe Data?" (arXiv:2404.01099).
