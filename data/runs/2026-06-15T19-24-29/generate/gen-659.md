# Layer-Magnitude Jailbreak Trace: Does It Hold Across Model Families?

**Problem:** Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace (arXiv:2602.11495) shows jailbreak prompts perturb specific high-susceptibility layers, and bypassing them blocks 78% of attacks on an abliterated LLaMA-3.1-8B. The result is shown on essentially one model; it is unknown whether the same layer-magnitude signature generalizes to other architectures.

**Direction:** Extract per-layer hidden-state magnitudes for ~100 benign vs ~100 jailbreak prompts on 3 small open models with different architectures (e.g. LLaMA-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B). Plot the layer-wise benign-vs-jailbreak magnitude gap and test whether the 'high-susceptibility layer' band is consistent or model-specific. Train a trivial logistic-regression probe on the magnitudes as a sanity detector.

**Why it matters:** A cheap, training-free detection signal that generalizes across families would be a deployable monitoring primitive for anyone running open models; if it does NOT generalize, that punctures a popular claim. Single-GPU, forward-pass-only.

**Relevant context:** Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace (arXiv:2602.11495, Feb 2026): layer-wise hidden-state magnitude analysis; bypassing high-susceptibility layers on abliterated LLaMA-3.1-8B blocks 78% of jailbreaks, preserves 94% benign. ALERT (arXiv:2601.03600) and JBShield (arXiv:2602.19396) corroborate that internal representations carry jailbreak signal.
