# Portfolio Correlation-Breakdown: Does LLM Agreement Spike Under 'Market Stress' Prompts?

**Problem:** In finance, asset correlations rise precisely during crises ('correlation breakdown'), destroying diversification when it's needed most. The analogous question for AI homogeneity — does inter-model agreement increase on exactly the hard/adversarial/high-stakes inputs where you'd want diverse opinions — is unstudied.

**Direction:** Borrow the rolling-window correlation-breakdown analysis from quantitative finance. Build a panel of open models, define 'stress' input strata (ambiguous moral dilemmas, jailbreak-adjacent prompts, out-of-distribution factual claims) vs 'calm' strata (everyday Infinity-Chat queries). Measure pairwise output-agreement/correlation within each stratum and test whether correlation rises under stress, mirroring financial markets.

**Why it matters:** If diversity collapses on the hardest inputs, model-panel redundancy fails as a systemic-risk hedge under the conditions that matter — a precise, measurable, medium-horizon concentration-risk finding requiring no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Correlation breakdown under market stress reduces diversification benefits (arXiv:1312.0506; ScienceDirect S0378426614001757). Systemic risk from portfolio/investment similarity (PMC6533041). Artificial Hivemind inter-model homogeneity (arXiv:2510.22954).
