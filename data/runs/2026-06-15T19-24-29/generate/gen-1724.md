# Propensity-Score Matching for Confound-Free Activation Patching

**Problem:** Activation patching can confound the effect of the patched component with correlated input features, mirroring confounding in observational epidemiology. Naive patching may attribute effects to the wrong component.

**Direction:** Import propensity-score / matching methods from epidemiology. When estimating a component's causal effect via patching, match source/target prompt pairs on covariates (length, token frequency, topic) so the patched activation is the only systematic difference, reducing confounding. Compare matched vs. unmatched effect estimates for a known circuit and quantify how much confounding inflates naive estimates.

**Why it matters:** Improves the reliability of the field's core causal tool. If unmatched patching systematically over- or under-states effects, many published circuit claims (and safety conclusions drawn from them) need revisiting. Pure methodology, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Matching methods for causal inference are well-reviewed (PMC2943670); activation patching is causal-inference applied to networks ('Mechanistic Interpretability for AI Safety -- A Review', arXiv:2404.14082). The matched-design transfer to patching is unexplored.
