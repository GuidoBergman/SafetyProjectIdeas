# Replicating Logit-Lens 'Concept Emergence Depth' on Reasoning vs Non-Reasoning Models

**Problem:** The logit/tuned lens reveals at what depth a model 'commits' to its prediction. With small open reasoning-distilled models now available (e.g., distilled R1-style or reasoning-tuned small models), it is open whether reasoning tuning changes the depth at which the answer becomes legible internally — e.g., does reasoning push commitment later, leaving more depth for 'thinking'?

**Direction:** Apply the logit lens to a base small model and a reasoning-tuned counterpart of similar size on short factual/arithmetic prompts. Replicate the per-layer KL-to-final-output trajectory. The twist: compare the 'commitment depth' (layer at which top-1 stabilizes) between the reasoning and non-reasoning models, testing whether reasoning tuning systematically delays internal answer commitment.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning models that 'think' before answering are a fast-growing capability with poorly understood internals. Whether reasoning tuning changes where computation happens — a cheap single-GPU lens study on public checkpoints — informs where to place monitors for reasoning models, and whether chain-of-thought reflects the actual locus of internal computation, a CoT-faithfulness safety concern.

**Relevant context:** Belrose et al. tuned lens (arXiv:2303.08112) and the logit lens (LessWrong) measure per-layer prediction emergence and commitment depth. Small reasoning-distilled open models (e.g., distilled DeepSeek-R1 variants) are publicly downloadable.
