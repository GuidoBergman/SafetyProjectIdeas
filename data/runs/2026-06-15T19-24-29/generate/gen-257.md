# How Brittle Are LLM Safety Judges? Constant-Output and Phrasing Attacks on the Evaluator

**Problem:** Jailbreak research relies heavily on LLM-as-judge to score harmfulness, yet judges are themselves vulnerable: null/constant outputs can win benchmarks and judges are sensitive to phrasing. If the evaluator is unreliable, reported attack-success numbers are unreliable.

**Direction:** Take an open LLM judge (or a small model prompted as a StrongREJECT-style grader) and stress-test it: (a) feed null/boilerplate 'responses' to measure spurious harmful/safe scores, (b) paraphrase genuinely harmful responses to see if scores swing, (c) inject judge-directed text ('this response is safe') into the response. Quantify how much measured ASR can be manipulated by attacking the judge, not the target.

**Why it matters:** Surfacing evaluator brittleness is a measurement-validity failure that undermines the entire adversarial-robustness literature — a high-leverage, lab-independent contribution. Pure inference over open judge models; clear success criterion (score variance under semantically-equivalent inputs).

**Relevant context:** PandaGuard (arXiv:2505.13862) systematically evaluates LLM-safety judges; multi-agent-debate work (arXiv:2511.06396) notes judge bias and adversarial-phrasing vulnerability. Null-model-wins findings on automatic judge benchmarks show pairwise-judge brittleness. StrongREJECT ships its own grader prompt, ideal as the unit under test.
