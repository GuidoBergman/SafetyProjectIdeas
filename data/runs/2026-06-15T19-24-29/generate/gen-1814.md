# Tokenizer Artifacts Plus Feature-Based Harm Detection

**Problem:** Compound: (1) interp features are entangled with tokenization artifacts (e.g. first-letter / token-aligned latents from absorption) (interp failure) amplifying (2) harm-detection evasion via simple tokenization manipulation (spacing, homoglyphs, leetspeak). If harm features are partly tokenization-bound, attackers re-tokenizing a harmful phrase silently switch off the feature monitor.

**Direction:** On an open model + SAE, find a harm/concept feature, then perturb harmful inputs with tokenization tricks (added spaces, unicode lookalikes) that preserve meaning. Measure feature-activation drop vs. semantic preservation (judged cheaply). Quantify how much of the monitor's coverage is tokenization-bound and thus trivially evadable.

**Why it matters:** Connects a documented interp artifact (token-aligned absorbed latents) to a real, cheap jailbreak vector. Fresh combination, fully feasible, no training, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** 'A is for Absorption' (arXiv:2409.14507): token-aligned latents absorb concept directions. Search results: SAE latents underperform on first-letter tasks, showing token-binding. Homoglyph/tokenization jailbreaks are documented in robustness literature.
