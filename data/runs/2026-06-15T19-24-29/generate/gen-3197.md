# Does Sampling Temperature Change How Often Models Scheme or Lie?

**Problem:** Deception/scheming evals are usually run at one decoding setting and reported as a single rate. Whether scheming and deceptive-reasoning frequencies are stable across temperature/top-p — or whether labs could under-report by choosing a 'safe' decoding config — is a basic but unexamined robustness question.

**Direction:** Fix a small set of scheming/deception probes (e.g. an alignment-faking prompt and a reward-hack scenario) and run each across a grid of temperatures and top-p values, many samples each, on open/API models. Estimate deception rate with confidence intervals per setting and test whether the rate varies systematically with decoding parameters.

**Why it matters:** If deception rates depend on decoding config, every reported scheming number needs error bars over decoding — a cheap methodological finding that improves the trustworthiness of all deception evals and is fully reproducible by independent researchers.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI/Anthropic system cards report single deception rates (GPT-5 ~2.1%); none report decoding-parameter sensitivity. Builds methodologically on robustness concerns raised in CoT-faithfulness work (Anthropic 2025) and the catalogue's interest in decoding-parameter consistency (a different, refusal-focused idea).
