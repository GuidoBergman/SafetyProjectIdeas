# Reproducing the Power-Seeking Theorem in Hand-Built Enumerable MDPs

**Problem:** Turner et al. prove optimal policies statistically tend toward high-power (high-option) states for broad reward distributions, independent of the specific reward. The result is structural but the empirical 'how strong is the tendency' question depends on environment topology and is rarely measured by newcomers.

**Direction:** In Python, build several small MDPs with hand-controlled topology (bottlenecks, hub states, irreversible actions). Sample many random reward functions, compute optimal policies via value iteration, and measure the fraction that route through high-power states. Vary the discount factor to reproduce the predicted 'more far-sighted -> more power-seeking' effect and find topologies where the tendency vanishes.

**Why it matters:** Instrumental convergence underpins many long-horizon risk arguments. A from-scratch, fully transparent reproduction clarifies exactly which environment structures drive power-seeking, is fully reproducible with no GPU, and is ideal first-research for CS students.

**Relevant context:** Turner et al. 'Optimal Policies Tend To Seek Power' (NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 1912.01683): power = average optimal value over reward distribution; far-sighted agents perceive power concentrated at option-maximizing cells.
