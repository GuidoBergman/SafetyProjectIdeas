# Replicating Benign-Relearning Attacks on WMDP-Unlearned Small Models

**Problem:** Unlearned LLMs can be made to regurgitate 'forgotten' hazardous knowledge via benign relearning on small, unrelated data subsets. Most demonstrations use specific methods/models; replicating the attack on an openly available unlearned small model would quantify how fragile current unlearning really is.

**Direction:** Take an openly available model unlearned on a hazard proxy (or apply gradient-difference unlearning yourself on a small open model), then fine-tune briefly on a small benign subset and measure recovery of the unlearned content on a held-out probe. Twist: replicate the relearning attack across multiple unlearning methods (GA, GD, NPO) to compare fragility.

**Why it matters:** Robust unlearning is an Open-Phil-priority safety goal; showing how easily it reverses on accessible models is directly actionable and needs no lab data. Comparing method fragility produces a useful 'which unlearning survives relearning' table.

**Relevant context:** Łucki/Patil et al. 'Unlearning or Obfuscating?' (arXiv:2406.13356, ICLR 2025) show benign relearning recovers unlearned WMDP keywords from small subsets, and that GA/GD/KL/NPO/SCRUB are all vulnerable. Sharpness-aware follow-up (arXiv:2502.05374) proposes robustness. WMDP is openly available.
