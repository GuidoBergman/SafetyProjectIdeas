# Does Probe Generalization Break at the 'New Attack Style' Link?

**Problem:** Deployment chain: train deception/harm probe on known examples -> deploy -> face novel attack styles -> probe must still fire. Reports of 95% F1 are mostly in-distribution; the link that matters for safety is generalization to unseen styles, where 'black-to-white boosts' suggest probes may track surface artifacts.

**Direction:** On an open model, train a deception probe on one elicitation style (e.g. roleplay) and rigorously test on held-out styles (pressure, sandbagging, sycophancy) from public datasets. Decompose where it fails: does the probe direction still align with the model's internal deception representation in the new style, or has the representation moved?

**Why it matters:** Out-of-distribution generalization is THE gap between impressive probe numbers and real monitoring. A clean cross-style stress test directly addresses the interpretability-to-safety translation gap, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Goldowsky-Dill et al. (arXiv 2502.03407) and Panfilov et al. (arXiv 2605.27958) report high F1 but note white-box advantage is 'modest' OOD. 'Benchmarking Deception Probes via Black-to-White Performance Boosts' (arXiv 2507.12691) suggests artifact-tracking. Public deception datasets exist.
