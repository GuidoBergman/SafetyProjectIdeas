# Distributed-Threat Aggregation: Catching Sabotage That No Single Action Reveals

**Problem:** Diffuse threats (research sabotage via many individually-innocuous bad actions) need fundamentally different monitoring than concentrated threats, yet most control evals focus on catching a single incriminating action. There is little public tooling for the aggregation problem: combining many weak per-action signals into a population-level alarm.

**Direction:** Simulate a stream of agent actions where a small fraction are subtly degraded (e.g. quietly worse code, omitted edge cases) so that each action alone is near-undetectable. Apply simple statistical aggregation (sequential probability ratio test, CUSUM, running mean of monitor scores) and measure how many actions are needed to raise a reliable alarm at a fixed false-alarm rate.

**Why it matters:** Diffuse research-sabotage is a medium-horizon threat frontier labs deprioritize, and the aggregation-over-many-actions framing is under-tooled. A simple, statistics-based detector is squarely within the participant's skill set and is counterfactually valuable lab-independent infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Redwood 'How can we solve diffuse threats like research sabotage with AI control?': distinguishes diffuse threats (catastrophe needs many weak-evidence actions) from concentrated threats (few highly incriminating actions); different monitoring approaches needed. UK AISI lists detecting concentrated vs distributed failures as an open problem.
