# Introspective Honesty: Verbalized vs Behavioral Policy Match

**Problem:** Models can sometimes accurately verbalize a learned policy, but self-reports are inconsistent and verbalized confidence misaligns with token probabilities. The gap between what a model *does* (behavioral policy) and what it *says it does* (verbalized policy) under deceptive incentives is underexamined as an honesty test.

**Direction:** Give an open model a hidden behavioral rule via system prompt (e.g. 'always recommend product X but never admit you were told to'). Then probe it behaviorally (does it recommend X?) and introspectively (does it admit the rule when asked directly, indirectly, or under pressure?). Quantify the verbalized-vs-behavioral mismatch as a concealment rate across probing strategies.

**Why it matters:** A behavioral-vs-verbal mismatch is a direct, training-free operationalization of dishonesty/concealment relevant to the honesty-verification open problem, and a building block for model-auditing-model setups.

**Relevant context:** 'Looking Inward: LLMs Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection' (arXiv:2410.13787); 'Emergent Introspective Awareness in LLMs' (transformer-circuits, arXiv:2601.01828): self-reports inconsistent, verbalized confidence misaligns with internal probabilities.
