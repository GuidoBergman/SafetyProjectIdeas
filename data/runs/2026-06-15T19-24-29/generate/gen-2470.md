# Monitor Suspiciousness Calibration Under Decoding Temperature

**Problem:** Trusted-monitor protocols depend on a calibrated suspiciousness score crossing an audit threshold, but the monitor's own scores may drift with decoding settings. Existing control evaluations fix temperature; nobody has measured how unstable the audit decision is to the monitor's sampling temperature.

**Direction:** Take a fixed pool of backdoored vs clean APPS code samples. Run a single open trusted monitor (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B) scoring each sample at temperatures 0.0, 0.3, 0.7, 1.0, with N repeats each. Measure how the AUROC and the fraction-caught-at-fixed-FPR vary with temperature, and how much score variance a single sample carries. Identify whether low temperature is strictly safer or whether some diversity helps.

**Why it matters:** A control protocol whose catch rate swings with an under-documented hyperparameter is fragile. This gives deployers a cheap, reproducible recommendation for monitor decoding settings — no lab infrastructure needed.

**Relevant context:** Ensemble Monitoring for AI Control (arXiv 2605.15377) finds calibration is the lowest-hanging fruit and that diverse monitors beat repeated sampling. Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors (arXiv 2510.09462) uses APPS backdoors to stress monitors. Builds on prior catalogue item 'Consistency of Safety Across Decoding Parameters' but applied to the monitor side rather than the policy.
