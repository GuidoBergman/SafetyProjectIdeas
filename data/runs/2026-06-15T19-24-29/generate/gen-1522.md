# Does light fine-tuning (LoRA) break the link between a safety vector and the behavior it controls?

**Problem:** Customization chain: community fine-tunes an open model with LoRA for a niche task -> internal representations drift slightly -> a safety steering vector extracted from the base model no longer controls the fine-tuned one -> protection silently lost. Beaglehole et al. flag stability across fine-tuning as open; the LoRA-specific, community-relevant version is unstudied.

**Direction:** Take an open base model and a publicly available LoRA fine-tune of it (or train a tiny LoRA on a small dataset within budget). Extract a refusal/safety vector on the base. Apply it to the fine-tuned model and measure effectiveness vs applying it on the base. Quantify how much fine-tuning erodes the vector's control. Success criterion: effectiveness drop on the fine-tune, plus whether re-extraction recovers it.

**Why it matters:** Thousands of community LoRA fine-tunes exist; if safety steering doesn't transfer to them, base-model safety claims don't hold downstream. Directly answers the Science paper's fine-tuning-stability open problem in the most common real setting. Single consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. list 'stability of concept vectors across model updates and fine-tuning' as open. KL-then-steer / fine-tuning shift work (arXiv 2406.15518; ICCV 2025 multimodal shift). LoRA-specific, community framing is novel.
