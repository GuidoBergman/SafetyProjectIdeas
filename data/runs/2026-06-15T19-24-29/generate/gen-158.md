# Adversarial Suffix Detectability by Activation Probes vs Behavioral Filters

**Problem:** White-box attacks reportedly hide unsafe states from probes via adversarial suffixes. It is unclear, in an open reproducible setting, whether GCG-style suffixes that succeed behaviorally are also invisible to a simple activation probe, or whether the probe still flags them.

**Direction:** Apply public GCG/AmpleGCG suffixes to an open model, collect activations on attacked vs clean harmful prompts, and test whether a linear jailbreak probe still separates them. Compare probe detection rate to a behavioral output-classifier on the same cases, mapping where each defense fails (probe-blind but output-caught, and vice versa).

**Why it matters:** Clarifies whether the much-cited 'probes can be evaded by suffixes' claim holds for cheap reproducible attacks, and whether probe+output-classifier cascades cover each other's blind spots — directly informing layered cheap defenses. No attack optimization compute needed if public suffixes are used.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong Jan 2026 digest: 'white-box attacks can reliably hide unsafe states from probes via adversarial suffixes or latent edits.' AmpleGCG (arXiv:2404.07921) supplies transferable suffixes. 'Red-teaming Activation Probes using Prompted LLMs' (researchgate 397230961) is recent related work.
