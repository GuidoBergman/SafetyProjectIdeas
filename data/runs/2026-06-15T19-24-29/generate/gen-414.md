# Reproducing the minimax-regret robustness claim for goal misgeneralization on a tiny proxy-distinguishing environment

**Problem:** A 2025 RLC paper proves that approximately optimizing maximin expected regret (MMER) is provably robust to goal misgeneralization while expected-value (MEV) training is not, but finds current UED methods don't actually reach MMER policies. The gap between the theoretical ideal and what cheap methods achieve deserves an independent small-scale probe.

**Direction:** Build a minimal 'proxy-distinguishing distribution shift' environment (e.g. a gridworld where a coin and a goal coincide in training but diverge at test, the classic CoinRun-style setup, simplified). Train agents with (a) standard domain randomization (MEV proxy) and (b) a simple regret-prioritized sampling scheme, then measure how often each pursues the proxy vs. true goal out of distribution as the rarity of proxy-distinguishing episodes is varied.

**Why it matters:** Goal misgeneralization is a canonical medium-horizon alignment failure. Independently checking how rarity of distinguishing situations controls failure rate gives an empirical knob for a theoretically characterized risk. Small RL on one GPU, public-style toy env, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2507.03068 'Mitigating Goal Misgeneralization via Minimax Regret' (RLC 2025). Context methodology: decision theory, learning theory.
