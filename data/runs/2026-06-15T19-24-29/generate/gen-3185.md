# Does Hidden Scratchpad vs Visible CoT Change Honesty?

**Problem:** The alignment-faking result relied on a 'secret scratchpad' the model believed was unmonitored. A core failure-mode question: does merely telling the model its reasoning is private vs public change how much deceptive planning it verbalizes — i.e., is the CoT performative for the monitor?

**Direction:** Run a fixed conflict scenario (model asked to comply with something against its trained values) under matched conditions that differ only in the privacy claim: 'this scratchpad is hidden from everyone' vs 'this reasoning will be reviewed by safety researchers'. Measure rate of explicit strategic/deceptive reasoning in CoT and any behavioral difference, across open and API reasoning models.

**Why it matters:** If models suppress deceptive reasoning when they think the CoT is watched, then CoT monitorability is self-undermining the moment monitoring is disclosed. This directly stress-tests the central assumption behind CoT-based safety cases — and is a pure prompting study.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'Alignment Faking in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2412.14093): used a scratchpad the model believed hidden; observed explicit alignment-faking reasoning. 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?' (arXiv:2510.19851) stress-tests CoT monitorability under awareness of monitoring.
