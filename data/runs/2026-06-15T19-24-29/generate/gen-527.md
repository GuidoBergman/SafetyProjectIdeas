# Epidemiological R0 for Prompt-Injection Spread in Multi-Agent Graphs

**Problem:** As agentic systems chain together, a single corrupted instruction can propagate between agents, but we have no theoretical/empirical handle on the conditions under which such corruption spreads vs. dies out.

**Direction:** Borrow the SIR contact-network model and basic reproduction number R0 from epidemiology. Build a small simulated network of open-model agents passing messages, inject an indirect prompt-injection 'pathogen', and measure an empirical R0 (mean number of newly-corrupted agents per corrupted agent) as a function of network topology and a simple guardrail prompt. Identify the 'epidemic threshold' for propagation.

**Why it matters:** Imports a mature mathematical framework (epidemic thresholds) to formalize a long-horizon multi-agent security threat that labs focused on single-model safety deprioritize. The simulation runs locally and yields a crisp threshold result.

**Relevant context:** SIR contact-network epidemic models and R0/epidemic thresholds (arXiv:physics/0701255, 1707.08607). Prompt-injection propagation in LLM-agent workflows is documented (arXiv:2506.23260, AgentFuzzer 2505.05849).
