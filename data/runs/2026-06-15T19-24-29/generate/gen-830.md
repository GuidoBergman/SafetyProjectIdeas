# Frameworks That Don't Define the Trigger: Auditing Capability Thresholds in Frontier Safety Policies for Vagueness

**Problem:** Frontier safety frameworks (RSPs/preparedness policies) promise to pause or add safeguards when models cross dangerous-capability thresholds. METR notes wide variation, and a key failure mode is that many frameworks describe trigger thresholds qualitatively, leaving the actual decision discretionary and unauditable. The degree of this under-specification across labs is not catalogued.

**Direction:** Characterize threshold-vagueness across published frameworks. Collect ~8-12 labs' frontier safety frameworks, extract every described capability threshold / pause trigger, and code each as quantitative-and-measurable, qualitative-but-bounded, or fully discretionary. Cross-tabulate by risk domain (bio, cyber, autonomy). Report what fraction of safety triggers are operationally undefined and where the field clusters.

**Why it matters:** A pause commitment with no measurable trigger is unenforceable and unfalsifiable — the gap between voluntary frameworks and real safety standards. This gives regulators a concrete map of which commitments are decision-ready vs vapor. Public-document analysis, no model, no compute, independent of labs.

**Relevant context:** METR 'Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies'; 'Towards Frontier Safety Policies Plus' (arXiv 2501.16500); 'Risk thresholds for frontier AI' (arXiv 2406.14713).
