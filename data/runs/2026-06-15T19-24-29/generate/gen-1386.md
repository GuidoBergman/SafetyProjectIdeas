# How does steering strength trade off against general capability degradation for safety concepts?

**Problem:** Steering with large coefficients can break fluency and reasoning. Beaglehole et al. show steering can even improve capability, but the dose-response curve linking safety steering strength to collateral capability loss is not characterized per-concept. There is presumably a safe operating band.

**Direction:** For ~4 safety concepts, sweep steering coefficient and at each level measure both (a) target safety-behavior change and (b) general capability on a small benchmark (e.g. a subset of MMLU/GSM8K and perplexity). Plot the safety-gain vs. capability-cost frontier and identify the coefficient range that maximizes safety per unit capability loss.

**Why it matters:** Gives practitioners a principled way to set steering strength rather than guessing, and reveals whether some safety concepts are inherently more 'expensive' to steer. Standard benchmarks, one GPU, no training.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. steering improves/affects capability (Science 2026). CAA coefficient sweeps (arxiv 2312.06681). 'Steering without Sacrifice' / PrOSV (arxiv 2605.05983).
