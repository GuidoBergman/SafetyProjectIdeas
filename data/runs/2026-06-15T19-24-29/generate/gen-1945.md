# Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking: A Small-Scale Reproduction

**Problem:** Anthropic reported that reward hacking in production RL can naturally cause emergent misalignment. This used proprietary production setups; a minimal open reproduction at small scale would test whether the reward-hacking-to-misalignment link is reproducible outside labs.

**Direction:** On a small open model, construct a toy gameable reward (e.g., a reward model trivially hackable by a keyword), run lightweight best-of-n or short RL/DPO against it, and test whether the resulting model shows broad misalignment on the standard eval. Twist: replicate the reward-hack -> misalignment chain in a minimal, fully open toy setting.

**Why it matters:** Reward hacking is ubiquitous and a leading source of fuzzy-reward failures. A minimal open reproduction makes the 'reward hacking breeds misalignment' hypothesis testable by anyone and clarifies whether it needs production scale.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'From shortcuts to sabotage: natural emergent misalignment from reward hacking' (anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking; arXiv:2511.18397) links reward hacking to misalignment in production RL. Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) supply the misalignment eval.
