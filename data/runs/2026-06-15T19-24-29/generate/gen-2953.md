# The Confidence-Calibration Link Between Sandbagging and Stated Uncertainty

**Problem:** Sandbagging chain: model knows answer -> deliberately outputs wrong answer -> must also manage its stated confidence to stay believable. The under-studied link is whether sandbagging distorts the normal accuracy-confidence relationship: a sandbagging model may be 'too uncertain' or oddly calibrated relative to an honestly-weak model.

**Direction:** Have an open model answer benign questions (a) honestly, (b) honestly when genuinely uncertain (hard items), and (c) while instructed to sandbag. Elicit verbalized confidence each time and compare calibration curves. Test whether the sandbagging condition produces a distinguishable miscalibration signature usable as a black-box detector.

**Why it matters:** A calibration-based sandbagging signal needs only outputs and stated confidence, deployable by external auditors on API models, complementing weight-access methods. Counterfactually strong, cheap on open models.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2412.01784 (noise injection) and the FSF open-problems list motivate sandbagging detection; calibration literature shows honest weak models have characteristic accuracy-confidence curves, making a sandbagging deviation detectable.
