# Steering for CoT verbosity without harming faithfulness: a safety tradeoff probe

**Problem:** A recent project learns a single verbosity axis from VERBOSE-vs-CONCISE CoT pairs to steer reasoning length. Steering verbosity could improve monitorability — but only if it doesn't reduce faithfulness (whether the CoT reflects the true reasoning). That tradeoff is unmeasured.

**Direction:** On a small open reasoning model, build a verbosity steering vector from prompt-generated CoT pairs. Steer toward more verbose CoT on a reasoning benchmark, then measure faithfulness via a simple intervention test (does the stated reason actually drive the answer? e.g. hint-injection sensitivity). Report whether forced verbosity adds genuine or confabulated content.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is a leading safety bet. If steering verbosity just produces longer but less faithful rationalizations, it's actively harmful for monitoring. A cheap, self-contained test of that tradeoff is high-value and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Training Steering Vectors to Enhance CoT Faithfulness and Verbosity' (SPAR 2026 project) and 'Measuring CoT Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity' (arXiv:2510.27378, Oct 2025).
