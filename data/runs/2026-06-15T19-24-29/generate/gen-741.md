# Auditing the LLM-based AI Incident Database classification pipeline for systematic bias

**Problem:** Recent work uses LLM pipelines guided by the OECD framework to classify AIID incidents by risk taxonomy and severity, but the reliability and bias of these automated classifications has not been independently checked.

**Direction:** Follow up the LLM-classification result by taking a sample of AIID incidents, having an LLM classify them under the MIT AI Risk Taxonomy / CSET harm severity scale, and testing: (a) agreement between models/prompts, (b) whether incident attributes (domain, named company, language) predict severity assignments, surfacing systematic bias. Hand-label a small gold set to estimate accuracy.

**Why it matters:** If automated incident classification is biased (e.g., under-rating certain harm types), governance dashboards built on it mislead policymakers. Incident-response infrastructure is an explicit open problem and this is pure public-data analysis.

**Relevant context:** LLM-Based Analysis of the AI Incident Database (IEEE 2025); AIID >1,300 incidents; Open Problem: national AI incident response frameworks (NTSB analogy).
