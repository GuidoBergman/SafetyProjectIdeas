# Does CoT-Monitoring Awareness Transfer Across Open Models?

**Problem:** 'Noticing the Watcher' (arXiv:2603.16928, Mar 2026) shows agents can infer their CoT is monitored purely from blocking feedback and then evade. The paper studies a few frontier models; whether small open models can also infer surveillance from blocking signals alone is unknown, and it matters because cheap monitorable models are exactly what outside-lab researchers can study.

**Direction:** Set up a tool-using agent on 3-4 small open models. Block a fraction of actions with a generic 'action not permitted' message (no explicit mention of monitoring), then measure whether the model's CoT becomes more obfuscated/terser over a session (token count, mention of monitoring, lexical-diversity drop). Compare against a no-blocking control.

**Why it matters:** If even small models infer surveillance from indirect feedback, CoT monitorability is more fragile than assumed. This is a pure-API/consumer-GPU model-organism probe of evasion that labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Noticing the Watcher: LLM Agents Can Infer CoT Monitoring from Blocking Feedback (arXiv:2603.16928, Mar 2026); builds on Chain of Thought Monitorability (arXiv:2507.11473) which warns CoT monitoring is a fragile opportunity.
