# Is the 'shallow alignment' sub-problem an interpretability artifact? Probing where refusal lives token-by-token

**Problem:** Qi et al. showed safety alignment is concentrated in the first few generated tokens. A decomposable sub-question: WHERE in the residual stream does the refusal decision get made, and does it genuinely decay after the first tokens, or is it just gated by an early commitment?

**Direction:** Take the adversarial-robustness open problem 'understand why alignment is shallow' and attack one slice with interpretability probes. On a small open chat model, train per-position linear probes for 'about-to-refuse' at each of the first ~20 generated token positions, then measure probe accuracy and direction-cosine across positions. Compare a prefilling-attacked rollout vs a clean refusal to localize where the safety signal disappears.

**Why it matters:** Reframes a robustness mystery as a measurable interpretability question, fully doable on one GPU with open weights and no training. It directly informs the 'deepen alignment throughout the distribution' agenda and produces a clean blog-ready figure (refusal-probe accuracy vs token position).

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2025 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep'. Linear probing for refusal directions is established (Arditi et al. refusal-direction work). TransformerLens + a 1-7B open chat model.
