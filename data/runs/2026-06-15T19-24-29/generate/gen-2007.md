# Reward-Model Disagreement as an Early-Warning Signal for Reward Hacking

**Problem:** Reward hacking exploits idiosyncrasies of a single reward model; whether disagreement among multiple open reward models flags the responses most likely to be hacked is a simple, untested monitoring idea.

**Direction:** Generate response sets via best-of-n against one 'target' open reward model, then score the high-scoring (likely-hacked) and ordinary responses with a panel of other open reward models. Test whether inter-RM disagreement (variance) is higher for hacked responses, building a disagreement-based detector and reporting its precision/recall.

**Why it matters:** A cheap ensemble-disagreement signal for catching reward-hacked outputs would be useful monitoring infrastructure for anyone doing alignment with open reward models, with no lab tooling required. Pure inference.

**Relevant context:** Reward hacking exploits low-density regions where the proxy breaks down (RLHF Book over-optimization); more RM data / ensembling reduces Goodharting (When RLHF Fails, arXiv:2606.03238). Ensemble disagreement is a natural extension.
