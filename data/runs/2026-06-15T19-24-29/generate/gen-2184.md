# Time-Pressure Variation: How Judge Accuracy Degrades Under Token/Turn Budgets

**Problem:** Prover-verifier work used TIME-CONSTRAINED humans. The analogous variable for LLM judges — a constrained reasoning budget (max tokens / turns the judge may use) — is rarely varied, yet real oversight is resource-bounded.

**Direction:** In a debate-judging setup with a small open model, cap the judge's allowed reasoning: zero-CoT, short-CoT (50 tokens), long-CoT (500 tokens). Measure judge accuracy and bias susceptibility (e.g., to verbosity/position) as a function of reasoning budget, across easy and hard questions.

**Why it matters:** Resource-bounded oversight is the realistic regime; knowing how thin a judge's reasoning budget can get before accuracy collapses guides protocol cost/benefit. Pure inference, easy to scope to 30h.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.13692) used time-constrained human verifiers. LLM reasoning-budget effects on judging are underexplored in oversight contexts.
