# What the Numbers Don't Say: An Audit of Whether Frontier Safety Scores Are Even Comparable Across System Cards

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: a governance/evaluations seam no subfield owns — labs publish safety evaluations in system cards, but each uses different prompts, judges, and thresholds, making cross-lab 'safety scores' non-comparable. No one owns auditing the comparability of these public safety claims.

**Direction:** Collect the safety-evaluation sections of ~5 recent public system cards (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Llama, etc.). Build a structured schema (what was measured, with what set, what judge, what threshold, what's reported) and code each card against it. Quantify how many safety claims are mutually comparable vs incomparable, and produce a 'comparability scorecard' plus a minimal disclosure template that would make them comparable.

**Why it matters:** Bridges governance_policy + evaluations_benchmarks. Policymakers and the public treat system-card safety numbers as comparable when they often aren't; surfacing this is decision-relevant and needs zero compute or lab access. Pure document analysis = maximally counterfactual, beginner-feasible, and a self-contained, valuable artifact.

**Relevant context:** Landscape lists many system cards (Claude 4, Opus 4.5, GPT-5, Gemini 3 Pro, Llama 4) and flags governance's dependence on standardized evaluation. The catalogue has model-card documentation-fragmentation and disclosure-scorecard ideas but not a focused comparability audit of the SAFETY-EVALUATION sections across cards with a disclosure template.
