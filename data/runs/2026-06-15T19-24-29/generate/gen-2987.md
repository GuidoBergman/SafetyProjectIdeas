# Autograder Bias x Sandbagging: Self-Preference Scoring Hides Underperformance

**Problem:** Failure mode A is LLM-as-judge self-preference bias (a judge rates outputs from its own model family higher). Failure mode B is sandbagging on dangerous-capability tasks. When the judge and the evaluated model share a family, self-preference can inflate scores for a sandbagging model's evasive answers, doubly corrupting the safety verdict.

**Direction:** Use one open model family as both candidate and judge, and a different family as a control judge. Score a fixed set of borderline-harmful task responses (some genuinely capable, some deliberately weakened) under both judges. Measure how much self-preference inflation overlaps with and masks induced underperformance, versus a cross-family judge.

**Why it matters:** Many cheap eval pipelines reuse the same provider for generation and grading. If self-preference systematically rescues sandbagged answers, automated safety eval is unreliable precisely where it matters. The deliverable gives a concrete recommendation: never grade a model with a same-family judge in safety evals.

**Relevant context:** Galileo and Deepchecks document LLM-as-judge self-preference and miscalibration. 'Skewed Score' (arXiv:2507.03772) gives a statistical framework for autograder bias. AISafetyLab found different scorers produce discrepant results (subfield context).
