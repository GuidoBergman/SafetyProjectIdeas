# Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors: Reproducing the Monitor-Subversion Link at Toy Scale

**Problem:** Trusted monitoring breaks the sabotage chain at 'untrusted output -> trusted monitor flags it'. Recent work shows adaptive attacks (prompt-injecting the monitor) subvert this link. The under-studied question for beginners is how cheaply a red team can degrade monitor suspicion scores, and which monitor prompt designs are robust.

**Direction:** Take a trusted weaker open model as monitor and a stronger model as the attacker producing outputs with embedded injections aimed at lowering the monitor's suspicion score. Measure the drop in monitor AUROC under increasingly adaptive injections, then test simple hardening (input sanitization, structured monitor prompts, ensembling) and quantify recovery.

**Why it matters:** Monitor robustness is the linchpin of control; a small reproducible study of attack strength vs monitor hardening is directly actionable safety infrastructure with no training required.

**Relevant context:** 'Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols' (arXiv:2510.09462); 'CoT Red-Handed: Stress Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitoring' (arXiv:2505.23575).
