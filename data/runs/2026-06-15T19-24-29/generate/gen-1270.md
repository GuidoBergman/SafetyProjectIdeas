# Correlated Hallucinations: Do Different Models Confidently Agree on the Same Wrong Facts?

**Problem:** Failure mode A: hallucination. Failure mode B: inter-model homogeneity. Verification pipelines often treat agreement across models as evidence of truth. If models share a blind spot, they hallucinate the same wrong fact in unison — agreement becomes a confidence-amplifier for falsehood.

**Direction:** Curate ~150 fact questions including some with known popular-but-wrong answers (misconceptions, false attributions). Query 6+ open models. Measure: (a) rate of identical wrong answers across models (correlated hallucination), (b) whether high cross-model agreement on wrong answers exceeds agreement on right answers. Report a 'consensus-is-not-truth' false-confidence rate.

**Why it matters:** Many cheap safety heuristics (self-consistency across models, majority vote fact-checking) assume independence. Correlated hallucination breaks them precisely on the shared misconceptions most likely to spread. Easy, counterfactually valuable for outside auditors.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind shared blind spots; correlated-errors framework (arXiv 2506.07962); factuality-hallucination empirical study (arXiv 2401.03205).
