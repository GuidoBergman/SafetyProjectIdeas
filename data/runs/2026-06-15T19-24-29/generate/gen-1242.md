# Vision SAE Sparsity Patterns: Replicating the Language-vs-Vision Gap on Multiple ViTs

**Problem:** ViT-Prisma reported that vision SAEs show different sparsity patterns than language SAEs — but this surprising result was observed on a limited set of vision transformers and was not quantitatively characterized in a way comparable across architectures.

**Direction:** Using Prisma's 80+ pretrained vision SAEs across 75+ ViTs, compute standard sparsity statistics (L0, feature density distributions, dead-feature fraction) for several ViT architectures and compare against published language-SAE statistics on GPT-2. Vary the ViT (CLIP-ViT vs supervised-ViT vs DINO) to test whether the sparsity gap is universal or training-objective dependent. Pure inference over released SAEs.

**Why it matters:** As safety expands to multimodal models, interpretability tools must transfer. Characterizing how and why vision representations are structurally different from language ones tells the community which language-SAE intuitions survive the jump to vision — a wide-open, low-cost area frontier labs largely deprioritize for text safety.

**Relevant context:** ViT-Prisma/Prisma project: 75+ vision transformers, 80+ pretrained SAE weights, MIT-licensed; reported vision SAEs show different sparsity patterns than language SAEs.
