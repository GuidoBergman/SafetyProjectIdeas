# Replicating 'Sentiment Direction' Steering Across Model Families with a Shared Probe Protocol

**Problem:** Steering vectors for high-level concepts (sentiment, refusal, sycophancy) are usually demonstrated on one model. A clean cross-family replication — extracting a sentiment direction with an identical difference-of-means protocol across three small open families and measuring steering efficacy and side-effects with a common metric — is rarely done, leaving the cross-family generality of steering underquantified.

**Direction:** Define one strict protocol: difference-of-means on positive/negative sentiment prompt pairs at the best layer (found by probe accuracy). Replicate it on Gemma-2-2b, Llama-3.2-1B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B. Measure steering strength needed for a target sentiment shift and the collateral perplexity increase. Report which families are easiest/hardest to steer and whether optimal layers align by relative depth.

**Why it matters:** Whether behavioral steering transfers uniformly across open-model families determines how generally both safety-steering and misuse-steering apply to the open ecosystem. A controlled, common-protocol cross-family study on one GPU produces directly comparable numbers — exactly the kind of broadly-useful, lab-independent infrastructure the field lacks.

**Relevant context:** Representation engineering survey (arXiv:2502.17601), 'Inference-Time Intervention' (arXiv:2306.03341), and Arditi et al. (arXiv:2406.11717) establish difference-of-means steering directions. TransformerLens supports hooks across many small open models; perplexity is a standard side-effect metric.
