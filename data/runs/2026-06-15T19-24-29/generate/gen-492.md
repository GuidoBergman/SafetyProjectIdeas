# Does the secret-token 'flare-up' survive paraphrase? Stress-testing the empirical ELK signal

**Problem:** A surprising ELK result is that representations of a concealed token consistently 'flare up' in middle layers even when a model is trained to deny knowing it. This is cited as hope for ELK, but it is unknown whether the flare-up is a robust property of latent knowledge or a brittle surface artifact that disappears under paraphrase or distribution shift.

**Direction:** Reproduce the secret-token detection setup on a small open model with a hidden keyword, locate the middle-layer 'flare-up' via activation magnitude / probe accuracy, then apply a battery of superficial transformations (typos, synonym swaps, reordering, translation round-trips) to the prompts and measure how quickly the flare-up signal decays. Report the decay curve and the transformation classes that kill the signal.

**Why it matters:** If the empirical foundation of optimistic ELK results is brittle to paraphrase, that is a load-bearing negative result for the 'probe the latent knowledge' research program. This is exactly the kind of theoretical-result-stress-test the subfield needs, and it is feasible outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Combines the ELK 'flare-up' observation (arXiv:2312.01037) with the brittleness finding from 'LLM Knowledge is Brittle: Truthfulness Representations Rely on Superficial Resemblance' (arXiv:2510.11905), which shows truth representations collapse under OOD surface transformations.
