# Lesion-Symptom Mapping (Neuropsychology) for Behavior-to-Component Links

**Problem:** Neuropsychology infers brain-function localization by correlating lesion locations with behavioral deficits across many patients. Interpretability rarely runs such systematic many-lesion population studies.

**Direction:** Transfer voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping (VLSM). Generate a 'population' of randomly lesioned model variants (each ablating a different random component subset), measure each on a battery of behaviors, then statistically map which components, when damaged, predict which behavioral deficits. Produce a component-to-behavior localization map for a small open model.

**Why it matters:** A data-driven map of which components are necessary for which behaviors (including safety behaviors like refusal) is a reusable interpretability asset, built entirely from open weights and inference.

**Relevant context:** VLSM is an established neuropsychology method. Random-ablation populations are cheap to generate in TransformerLens; recasting ablation studies as population-level lesion-symptom mapping is the novel transfer.
