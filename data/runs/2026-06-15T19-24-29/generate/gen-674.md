# Null-Space Steering vs Adaptive Attacks: A Cheap Adaptive-Evaluation Stress Test

**Problem:** NullSteer (arXiv:2603.22094) defends VLMs by steering refusal in a null space that leaves benign behavior untouched. Defenses are often only tested against static attacks; the subfield open problem stresses robustness against EVOLVING attacks. A small adaptive-attack stress test of a null-space-style steering defense is missing.

**Direction:** Implement a simplified null-space refusal-steering defense on one open (text) model. Evaluate against static attacks, then run a cheap adaptive attacker (a handful of paraphrase/prefix iterations chosen to maximize success against the defended model). Report the gap between static and adaptive ASR — quantifying how much 'static robustness' overstates real robustness.

**Why it matters:** The static-vs-adaptive gap is the crux of the 'adaptive defenses' open problem and is routinely under-reported. A small reproducible adaptive stress-test protocol is broadly reusable and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Principled Steering via Null-space Projection for Jailbreak Defense (NullSteer, arXiv:2603.22094, March 2026): zero perturbation in benign subspace, induces refusal along harmful directions. AttriGuard (arXiv:2603.10749) explicitly notes leading defenses degrade under adaptive optimization — motivating the stress test. Open problem (Anthropic) on adaptive defenses.
