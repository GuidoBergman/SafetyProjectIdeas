# Psychophysics Staircase Method for Feature Activation Thresholds

**Problem:** We characterize features by their top activations but rarely measure the perceptual-like threshold at which a feature 'switches on', nor the steepness of that transition. We lack a principled stimulus-titration method.

**Direction:** Import the adaptive staircase / psychometric-function method from psychophysics. Construct a continuum of stimuli (e.g., interpolate token sequences from clearly-not-X to clearly-X) and use an adaptive staircase to estimate each SAE feature's activation threshold and slope (the psychometric curve). Characterize whether safety-relevant features are sharp detectors or graded, on an open SAE.

**Why it matters:** Threshold/slope tells us how reliably a feature can serve as a binary safety trigger and whether adversaries can sit just below threshold. Cheap, lab-independent, and yields a reusable feature-characterization protocol.

**Relevant context:** Adaptive staircase methods are standard psychophysics for estimating sensory thresholds. SAE feature activations and interpolation are readily available via SAELens; the psychometric-function framing is a fresh transfer.
