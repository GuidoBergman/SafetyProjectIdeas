# Replicating RLHF-worsens-calibration on small open instruct models vs. their base versions

**Problem:** It has been reported (e.g., for GPT-4) that RLHF can worsen a model's calibration relative to its pretrained base, and that RLHF models over-report high verbalized confidence. This has mostly been studied on proprietary frontier models. Whether the same calibration degradation appears between a *small open base model and its open RLHF/instruct-tuned sibling* (where we can inspect both) is testable but underexplored.

**Direction:** Take an open base model and its instruct/RLHF-tuned counterpart of the same size (e.g., a base vs. instruct pair). On a multiple-choice QA benchmark, compare (a) token-probability calibration and (b) verbalized-confidence calibration via expected calibration error and reliability diagrams. Replicate the published direction-of-effect (RLHF worsens calibration) on a model where both checkpoints are public.

**Why it matters:** Calibrated uncertainty is a safety-critical property: an overconfident model that won't say 'I don't know' is dangerous in high-stakes use. Confirming whether the RLHF-calibration-degradation finding generalizes to inspectable open model pairs — rather than just closed frontier models — is high counterfactual value and gives a reproducible measurement anyone can extend.

**Relevant context:** 'Just Ask for Calibration' (arXiv:2305.14975): RLHF worsens conditional-probability calibration, verbalized confidence can recover it; surveys note RLHF models emit 80-100% confidence with ECE up to 0.30+. Steering priority (context): evaluations needing no lab resources; safety relevance of honesty/uncertainty.
