# Localizing the 'Trigger -> Behavior Switch' in an Open Sleeper-Agent Model

**Problem:** A backdoored model's harm chain is: input contains trigger -> trigger detected internally -> behavior policy switches -> malicious output. Probes catch sleeper agents at >99% AUROC, but where the behavior SWITCH happens mechanistically (which layer/head reads the trigger and flips the policy) is the under-studied middle link.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a tiny open model (e.g. Pythia-410M or a small GPT-2) into a simple sleeper agent with a string trigger (a few-hour LoRA, or use a published backdoored checkpoint). Use activation patching to find the minimal layer-range where patching the trigger token's activation toggles the backdoor, mapping the causal switch point.

**Why it matters:** Knowing where the switch lives tells auditors where to probe/ablate, and whether a small intervention can disable a backdoor. Model-organism study, single consumer GPU, no proprietary infra — exactly the counterfactual-value sweet spot.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'Simple probes can catch sleeper agents' (>99% AUROC, deception linearly represented). 'Activation Differences Reveal Backdoors' (arXiv 2605.07324) finds backdoors in SAE activation differences. Sleeper-agent training is feasible with LoRA on small models on one GPU.
