# The Honesty-vs-Helpfulness Decomposition: Separating Two Goals Benchmarks Usually Conflate

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: a conceptual seam between honesty_faithfulness and alignment_training that no benchmark cleanly owns — 'good behavior' bundles honesty (don't assert falsehoods) and helpfulness (answer the user), but these conflict (a model can be helpfully wrong or honestly unhelpful). No standard eval decomposes a model's responses onto these two axes separately.

**Direction:** Curate ~80 prompts where honesty and helpfulness trade off (questions with a tempting-but-false 'helpful' answer, or where the honest answer is 'I don't know / that premise is false'). Score each open-model response on two independent axes: honesty (did it avoid asserting falsehood) and helpfulness (did it try to assist). Plot models on the honesty-helpfulness plane and identify who buys helpfulness by sacrificing honesty.

**Why it matters:** Bridges honesty_faithfulness + alignment_training + evaluations_benchmarks. Conflating the two axes hides sycophancy-driven dishonesty behind high 'helpfulness'. A clean 2-axis decomposition is a reusable conceptual tool. Prompt-only + open models + one GPU = strongly counterfactual and self-contained.

**Relevant context:** RLHF tradeoffs and sycophancy literature show helpfulness optimization can erode honesty; the Opus 4.5 sycophancy finding is in the landscape. False-premise / abstention benchmarks exist piecemeal; the catalogue has abstention-honesty and sycophancy ideas but not an explicit honesty-vs-helpfulness 2-axis decomposition across models.
