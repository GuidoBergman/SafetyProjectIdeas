# Schelling-Point Collusion Probe: A Micro-Benchmark for Covert Coordination Between Two Model Instances

**Problem:** Untrusted-monitoring safety cases assume the policy and monitor (same/similar weights) cannot reliably coordinate on a covert signal without a shared key. Schelling-coordination ability is the key enabling capability, but there is no tiny, reproducible benchmark a beginner can run to measure how well a given open model coordinates on focal points relevant to collusion.

**Direction:** Build a small Schelling-game battery (pick the same word/number/timestamp/code-token as another copy of yourself, no communication). Run it on several open models, measuring coordination rate vs. chance. Then test a control-relevant variant: 'agree on a covert trigger condition for when to defect' and measure whether two instances pick compatible triggers above chance.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes the model-organism question of whether models can collude without verbalized communication — a core threat for untrusted monitoring. A clean capability benchmark informs which models are safe to use as untrusted monitors. Trivial compute, pure API.

**Relevant context:** 'Subversion via Focal Points: Investigating Collusion in LLM Monitoring' (arXiv 2507.03010) and 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' (LessWrong) establish that Schelling coordination is the enabling capability for collusion and that models vary in this ability. 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents' (arXiv 2402.07510) frames the threat.
