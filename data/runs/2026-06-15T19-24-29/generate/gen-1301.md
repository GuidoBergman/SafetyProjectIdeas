# Does Distillation Inherit the Teacher's Homogeneity AND Blind Spots? A Small-Scale Audit

**Problem:** Angell et al. show benign-only distillation toward a teacher increases representational similarity and inherits the teacher's jailbreak vulnerabilities. The broader homogeneity implication — that the proliferation of distilled small models recreates the teacher's blind spots ecosystem-wide — hasn't been audited behaviorally on publicly available teacher/student pairs.

**Direction:** Pick public teacher/student pairs (e.g., a large model and its known distilled variants/families). Without any training, behaviorally compare them: agreement on factual errors, refusal patterns, and shared jailbreak susceptibility. Test whether distilled students cluster tightly around their teacher, forming homogeneity families.

**Why it matters:** If thousands of cheap distilled models all carry one teacher's blind spots, the apparent diversity of the open-model ecosystem is illusory — a key systemic concentration risk. Pure behavioral audit, no training, counterfactually valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Jailbreak Transferability Emerges from Shared Representations' (arXiv:2506.12913, June 2025) using only behavioral probing of existing public teacher/student model families.
