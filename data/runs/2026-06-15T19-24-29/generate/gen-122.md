# Low-Resource Language Jailbreaks vs Activation Probes

**Problem:** Low-resource-language jailbreaks bypass output-level safety, but it is unknown whether English-trained activation probes still detect the harmful intent of a low-resource-language jailbreak in the model's internal representations, or whether the vulnerability is also latent.

**Direction:** Train a jailbreak-detection probe on English jailbreaks for a multilingual open model, then test its detection rate on the same harmful intents expressed via low-resource-language attacks (e.g., Swahili, Afrikaans). If the probe still fires, intent is represented language-agnostically; if not, the latent space is also vulnerable.

**Why it matters:** Reveals whether internal safety representations are language-agnostic — a deep question about where the multilingual safety gap lives (output vs representation). Directly tests probe robustness across the most accessible real-world attack. Inference-only, no training of the target model.

**Relevant context:** Multilingual jailbreaking using low-resource languages (arXiv:2605.18239): 60-78% success in Afrikaans/Swahili; 'harmfulness curse.' Activation probes (subfield context) detect English jailbreaks. Whether probes detect intent across languages is unstudied and combines two flagged directions (multilingual gap + probe replication).
