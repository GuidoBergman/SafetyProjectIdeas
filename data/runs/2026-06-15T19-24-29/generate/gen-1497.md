# Cryptographic Differential Analysis: Information Leakage From Steering On vs Off

**Problem:** Recent work shows models can be trained to detect when they are being steered. From a security standpoint, this is a side channel: steering leaves a detectable signature. Cryptography's differential analysis studies how input differences propagate to observable output differences.

**Direction:** Borrow differential analysis from cryptanalysis. Treat steering on/off as a secret bit and ask how much it leaks into observable outputs. For matched prompts, compute the distribution of output differences (token-distribution divergence, surface features) between steered and unsteered runs and quantify how reliably an external observer (no internal access) can detect that steering occurred, per concept and strength. Map the steering 'leakage budget'.

**Why it matters:** If steering is externally detectable, an adversary can adapt to evade or exploit it, and a model could in principle learn to behave differently when it senses steering, a deceptive-alignment-adjacent threat. Quantifying the side channel is a model-organism-style safety study needing only black-box outputs. No training, outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** Differential cryptanalysis tracks input-to-output difference propagation. Models can detect activation steering from within (arXiv:2511.21399). Open problem: adversarial robustness of steering (Beaglehole et al.).
