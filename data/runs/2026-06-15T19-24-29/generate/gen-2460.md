# Self-Recognition as a Collusion Enabler: Can a Model Tell Its Own Output Apart?

**Problem:** Naive self-recognition (a model recognizing its own outputs) is reported to triple a red team's collusion win rate, making it a key dangerous capability. How reliably small open models self-recognize, and how easily a trusted paraphrase breaks it, is under-measured at the accessible scale.

**Direction:** Present a model with pairs of outputs (one its own, one from a different model) and measure self-recognition accuracy across small open models and decoding settings. Then re-run after passing all outputs through a trusted paraphraser and quantify how much self-recognition (and thus collusion potential) is destroyed.

**Why it matters:** Self-recognition is a concrete enabler of acausal collusion in untrusted monitoring; mapping it on open models and showing whether paraphrasing neutralizes it gives a clean dangerous-capability baseline plus a defense check, all lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Redwood collusion post: naive self-recognition with SFT triples the red team's win rate vs the For/While signal. Schelling/collusion literature (arxiv 2602.20628). Paraphrasing proposed as mitigation. Self-recognition is framed as a Schelling-point dangerous capability.
