# Layer-misplacement failure: how badly does steering at the wrong layer backfire?

**Problem:** Steering is layer-specific (middle layers best for abstract concepts; later layers dominate behavior in some models). Practitioners often steer at a default layer. The penalty for steering at a sub-optimal layer—wasted effect, coherence loss, or off-target harm—is not systematically mapped per concept.

**Direction:** Sweep a fixed safety concept's steering vector across every layer of an open model at matched norm, measuring on-target effect, coherence, and off-target benchmark drop at each layer. Identify the 'effective band' and quantify how steep the penalty is for mis-layering, comparing Gemma-2 (reported double-peak refusal geometry) vs. Llama.

**Why it matters:** Layer choice is a hidden free parameter that determines whether steering helps or harms. A per-concept, per-model layer-sensitivity map is practical guidance and exposes that single-layer defaults can fail silently—useful and reproducible outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in layer-dependence findings and Gemma-2 'two distinct peaks / multiple refusal directions' result (arXiv 2501.09929, 2601.19375, dose-response work). Inference-only on open models.
