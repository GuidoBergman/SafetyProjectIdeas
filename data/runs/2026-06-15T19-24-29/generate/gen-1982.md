# Calibration Benchmark for AI-Feedback Preference Judges

**Problem:** RLAIF/constitutional pipelines treat the AI judge's preference as ground truth, but the judge's confidence calibration (does a 90%-confident preference correspond to 90% agreement with humans?) is rarely measured, leaving a blind spot in feedback quality.

**Direction:** Take an existing human-labeled preference dataset (e.g., HH-RLHF / Stanford Human Preferences), have open models act as preference judges with verbalized confidence, and compute calibration curves (reliability diagrams, ECE) of judge confidence vs. agreement with the human label. Report a per-model 'feedback calibration' scorecard.

**Why it matters:** Miscalibrated AI feedback silently injects noise/bias into preference training — a measurable facet of the scalable-oversight problem. A reusable calibration benchmark on public data needs no training and is exactly the lab-independent measurement work that improves alignment-training inputs.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic HH-RLHF and Stanford Human Preferences datasets (from subfield Key Datasets). Constitutional AI (arXiv:2212.08073) uses AI preference labels. Open Problems in RLHF: scalable alternatives to human feedback must maintain alignment quality; calibration is an unmeasured component.
