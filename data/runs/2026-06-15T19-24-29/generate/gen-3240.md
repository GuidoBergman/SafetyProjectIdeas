# Decoder-Side Defense: Can a Naive Monitor Catch Hand-Crafted CoT Encodings?

**Problem:** The hidden-reasoning chain assumes an encoded channel evades monitoring. The defensive link is the monitor itself, but most studies test sophisticated learned steganography. A cheaper, policy-relevant question: how well does an off-the-shelf LLM monitor catch SIMPLE, human-designed encoding schemes that an open model can be prompted to use?

**Direction:** Define a taxonomy of simple encodings (acrostics, synonym-parity, sentence-count bins) and prompt an open model to use each to convey a hidden label while answering normally. Use a second LLM as a monitor asked 'is anything hidden here?' Measure monitor detection rate per scheme and the encoding's task-utility cost. Rank which simple channels are most monitor-evasive.

**Why it matters:** Knowing which trivial encodings already defeat naive monitors tells deployers where to harden monitoring first; a concrete monitoring-harness prototype valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Redwood encoded-reasoning work; 'Early Signs of Steganographic Capabilities in Frontier LLMs' (arXiv:2507.02737); steganography survives until paraphrase/monitoring breaks the channel.
