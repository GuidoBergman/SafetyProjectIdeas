# Steering-vector sandbagging: can an activation direction make a model deliberately underperform, and does noise injection catch it?

**Problem:** Sandbagging detection (Noise Injection, NeurIPS 2025) was demonstrated against prompt/fine-tuning-induced sandbagging. It is unknown whether sandbagging induced purely by an inference-time steering vector (a representation-engineering intervention) produces the same noise-injection signature, or whether steering creates a qualitatively different, harder-to-detect form of capability concealment.

**Direction:** Use contrastive activation addition to build an 'underperform/play-dumb' steering vector on a small open model (compute it from paired 'answer fully' vs 'answer poorly' completions). Apply it during a capability eval (e.g. MMLU subset, GSM8K subset) to induce graded sandbagging. Then run the published noise-injection detector (add Gaussian noise to weights at increasing magnitudes, watch for anomalous accuracy rebound) and ask: does steering-induced sandbagging show the same rebound curve as prompt-induced sandbagging? Compare detection ROC across induction methods.

**Why it matters:** If steering-induced sandbagging evades a state-of-the-art detector, it reveals a gap: control evals must account for representation-level concealment, not just behavioral or fine-tuning-based. This is a model-organism study scoped to a 30h slice using an existing open model — exactly the kind of work valuable outside a lab.
