# Replicating the Debate-Improves-Judge Result With a Non-LLM Statistical Judge

**Problem:** Debate experiments use LLM (or human) judges. A surprising, accessible variant asks whether a simple non-LLM judge, a lightweight classifier or heuristic over debate-transcript features, can also benefit from debate structure, which would localize where debate's value actually lives.

**Direction:** Replicate the debate-vs-naive comparison but replace the LLM judge with a simple statistical model (e.g. logistic regression over cheap transcript features like quote overlap, agreement, hedging words) trained on a small labeled set. Measure whether this minimal judge gets a debate accuracy boost comparable to or smaller than an LLM judge.

**Why it matters:** If a dumb statistical judge captures much of debate's benefit, the value comes from the transcript structure rather than judge sophistication, an important conceptual result for designing cheap, auditable oversight. If not, judge intelligence is essential. Tiny compute, fully open, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Debate work (Kenton et al. 2024; Khan et al. 2024) uses LLM/human judges and attributes gains to debate structure under information asymmetry. Substituting a non-LLM feature-based judge is the twist, isolating structure-driven vs intelligence-driven benefit.
