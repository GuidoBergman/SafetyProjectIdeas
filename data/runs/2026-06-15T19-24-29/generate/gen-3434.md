# Introspective Self-Prediction Accuracy Replicated on Open Models

**Problem:** Binder/Evans et al. showed frontier models can predict their own behavior in hypotheticals better than other models can (a sign of introspection), but the self-prediction test has not been cheaply replicated on small open models, where it would be a practical honesty probe.

**Direction:** Reproduce a lightweight slice of the introspection protocol: have model M answer behavioral questions (e.g. 'given input X, would you pick option A or B?'), record M's actual behavior on X, and compare M's self-prediction accuracy to a cross-model predictor of the same size. Twist: small-open-model replication using only the prompted (no-finetune) self-prediction comparison.

**Why it matters:** Whether a model has privileged access to its own behavior bears directly on whether self-reports can ever be trusted as honest signals. A no-training version tells independent researchers if open models have any introspective edge. Inference only.

**Relevant context:** 'Looking Inward: LLMs Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection' (arXiv 2410.13787, Binder, Chua, ..., Evans): models predict own behavior better than other models do; calibration suggests self-simulation. The hypothetical self-prediction comparison can be run without fine-tuning.
