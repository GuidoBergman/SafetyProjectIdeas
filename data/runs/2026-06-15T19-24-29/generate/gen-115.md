# Refusal Recovery After Chain-of-Thought Hijacking

**Problem:** Chain-of-thought hijacking (H-CoT) bypasses safety in reasoning models by injecting compliant reasoning steps, and longer CoT paradoxically reduces robustness — but whether a reasoning model can 'snap back' to refusal in its final answer after hijacked intermediate reasoning is unknown.

**Direction:** Using an open reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B or QwQ-style small model), inject hijacked compliant reasoning of varying lengths into the CoT, then measure whether the final answer still refuses. Map the 'reasoning-depth recovery curve' analogous to token-depth recovery, distinguishing CoT shallowness from output shallowness.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether the shallow-alignment finding extends from output tokens to reasoning traces — a flagged follow-up direction. Reasoning models are a medium-horizon safety concern; characterizing their distinct attack surface needs only inference on a small open model, no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Chain-of-Thought Hijacking (OpenReview, 2025) and H-CoT (arXiv:2502.12893): injecting execution-phase thoughts bypasses safety; longer CoT reduces robustness. Qi et al. shallow alignment is about output tokens; whether reasoning traces show the same shallowness is an open follow-up noted in the subfield context.
