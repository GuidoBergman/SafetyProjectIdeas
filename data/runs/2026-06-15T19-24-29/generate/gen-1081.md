# Replicating Correlated Errors on a Safety Benchmark Instead of a Leaderboard

**Problem:** Kim et al. (Correlated Errors in LLMs, ICML 2025) measured error correlation on general leaderboards and hiring, finding models agree ~60% of the time when both err. Whether this correlation holds for *safety classification* (e.g., is-this-prompt-harmful judgments) is untested — yet correlated safety misjudgments are far more dangerous.

**Direction:** Replicate the error-correlation methodology on a safety-classification task: take a labeled harmful/benign prompt dataset, have 6-10 open models classify each item, and compute pairwise error-correlation (agreement-conditional-on-both-wrong) exactly as the paper does. Twist: stratify by same-provider vs. cross-provider and by model size to test whether the paper's 'bigger models correlate more' finding reproduces in the safety domain.

**Why it matters:** Correlated safety misclassification means an ensemble of moderation models gives a false sense of robustness. Quantifying this is directly relevant to concentration risk and gives a cheap, reproducible measurement frontier labs have little incentive to publish.

**Relevant context:** Direct replication of Correlated Errors in LLMs (arXiv:2506.07962) with a safety-task twist; uses only open models and public safety datasets.
