# Power-Seeking Without Optimality: Do Random and Heuristic Policies Also Drift Toward Power?

**Problem:** Turner's theorem is about optimal policies. Real agents (and RL during training) are sub-optimal. It is open whether the power-seeking tendency holds for sub-optimal, satisficing, or epsilon-greedy policies, or whether it is special to optimality — a gap between the formal result and deployed systems.

**Direction:** In small enumerable MDPs, compare the power-routing tendency across policy classes: optimal, near-optimal, epsilon-greedy, random, and 'satisficing' (stop at threshold). For each over many random rewards, measure the fraction visiting high-power states. Determine whether and how the power-seeking tendency weakens as policies become more sub-optimal.

**Why it matters:** Clarifies how far the most-cited formal power-seeking result extends to the imperfect agents we actually build — sharpening when instrumental-convergence risk arguments apply. Pure CPU simulation, ideal beginner research.

**Relevant context:** Turner et al. 'Optimal Policies Tend To Seek Power' (arXiv 1912.01683) proves the result for optimal policies; follow-ups (AF 'Clarifying Power-Seeking and Instrumental Convergence') note the optimality caveat.
