# Linguistics Minimal-Pairs Methodology for Isolating Clean Concept Directions

**Problem:** Concept vectors extracted from contrastive prompts often entangle the target concept with confounds (topic, length, formality). Linguistics uses minimal pairs (sentences differing in exactly one feature) to isolate a single phenomenon.

**Direction:** Transfer the minimal-pairs methodology from linguistics. Construct tightly controlled minimal-pair datasets that differ only in the target safety concept (e.g. identical sentences differing only in deceptive vs. honest intent), and extract steering vectors from them. Compare the resulting 'clean' vectors against vectors from loosely matched contrastive prompts: measure confound leakage (does the loose vector also encode topic/length?) via probing on held-out confound labels.

**Why it matters:** Confounded steering vectors cause unpredictable side effects; minimal-pairs gives a disciplined recipe for cleaner safety directions, addressing 'which concepts have clean linear representations'. A methodology contribution others can reuse. Inference-only, no training, fully outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** Minimal pairs isolate single linguistic features (psycholinguistics/BLiMP tradition). Steering-vector magnitude/extraction artifacts are documented (arXiv:2407.12404). Open problem: which concepts have linear representations (Beaglehole et al.).
