# From 'Probe-Based Lie Detection Is Robust to Evasion' to an Adversarial-Prompt Stress Test of Truth Probes

**Problem:** Success state: a latent truth probe keeps working even when the model is actively prompted to evade it — the realistic adversarial case for ELK. The quirky-models probe works against a passively quirky model, but the gap is whether a model explicitly instructed to 'answer falsely while keeping your activations looking honest' can fool a fixed probe.

**Direction:** Take a fixed truth probe and an open instruction model. Construct adversarial system prompts asking the model to lie and to reason about appearing truthful, then measure whether probe AUROC drops. Compare naive lying vs. evasion-aware prompting. Inference-only, single GPU.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from evasion-robust ELK, the decisive question is adversarial robustness, not clean-benchmark accuracy. Measuring how much a prompt-level adversary can degrade a truth probe defines the threat model the formal ELK program must address — and it needs only an open model, no lab red-team infra.

**Relevant context:** ELK quirky-models (arXiv:2312.01037): probes work on passively quirky models; secret-token leakage persists under concealment training but adversarial prompting untested. ELK adversarial 'worst-case' framing from ARC report.
