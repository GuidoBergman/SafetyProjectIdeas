# Weak-to-Strong with Adversarially-Biased (Not Just Noisy) Weak Labels

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong experiments model weak supervision as a less-capable-but-honest labeler. They do not test SYSTEMATICALLY-biased weak labels (e.g., a labeler that errs in a consistent direction), which better models a flawed human overseer.

**Direction:** Replicate the open weak-to-strong recipe on a binary task, but generate weak labels with an injected systematic bias (e.g., always favor the longer/first option) rather than random noise. Compare PGR and the strong student's error pattern under random-noise weak labels vs systematically-biased weak labels of equal accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Whether the strong model 'corrects' random noise but inherits systematic bias is decisive for trusting weak overseers. This isolates a variable the original paper conflated and directly targets the systematic-error open problem; small-model LoRA fine-tune fits the budget.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. 2023 (arXiv:2312.09390) treat weak labels as noisy-but-unbiased. Anthropic open problem: 'what biases do human overseers consistently exhibit?'
