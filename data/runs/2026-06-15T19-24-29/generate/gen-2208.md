# Position Bias in Debate Judging: Does the Last Debater Win?

**Problem:** Position bias (preferring responses in certain positions) is well-documented for pairwise LLM judging. In debate-based oversight, debaters speak in an order; it is unknown whether judges systematically favor the first or last debater independent of argument quality, which would let an AI game oversight by claiming a turn slot.

**Direction:** Using released debate transcripts, present each transcript to an open judge model in both speaking orders (swap which debater goes first/last) and measure how often the verdict flips purely due to order. Quantify a 'turn-order advantage' and test whether anonymizing/randomizing order or a recency-summary instruction neutralizes it.

**Why it matters:** Order is an exploitable, content-free lever in any debate oversight protocol. Measuring and neutralizing it is a concrete robustness contribution for the debate protocol that labs are betting on. Inference-only on public transcripts.

**Relevant context:** LLM-judge position bias documented across model families (arXiv:2410.02736, arXiv:2411.16594); fixable by reordering. Debate transcripts from Rahman et al. 2025 / Khan et al. self-play debate (arXiv:2409.16636) available.
