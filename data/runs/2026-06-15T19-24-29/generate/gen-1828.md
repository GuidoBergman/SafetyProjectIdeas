# Probing the Think-Answer Divergence in Open-Weight Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Why Models Know But Don't Say (arXiv:2603.26410, March 2026) documents a faithfulness divergence between thinking tokens and final answers in open-weight reasoning models, but does not characterize whether the divergence is linearly decodable from activations at the point of divergence.

**Direction:** Collect prompts where an open reasoning model's chain-of-thought reaches a tentative answer that differs from its final answer, train a simple linear probe on residual-stream activations at the transition, and test whether a probe can predict 'the model will override its reasoning'. Report probe accuracy vs a CoT-only baseline.

**Why it matters:** A cheap activation probe that flags when a model is about to contradict its own stated reasoning is a concrete monitoring tool, and tests the linear-representation hypothesis on a safety-relevant behavior using only an open model.

**Relevant context:** Why Models Know But Don't Say: Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers in Open-Weight Reasoning Models (arXiv:2603.26410, March 2026). Linear representation hypothesis underlies probing per Probing Evaluation Awareness (arXiv:2507.01786).
