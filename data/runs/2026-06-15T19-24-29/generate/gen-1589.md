# English Refusal Vector x Low-Resource Compliance: A Cross-Lingual Steering Compound Failure

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): a refusal direction extracted from English transfers across languages (Beaglehole's cross-language transfer result). Failure mode B (multilingual safety gap): safety is weaker in low-resource languages. Compound risk: an English-derived ablation/steering vector disables refusal MORE effectively in low-resource languages where alignment was already thin, widening the safety gap.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal direction from English data on a multilingual open model. Ablate it and measure harmful-compliance increase across a ladder of languages (high-resource to low-resource) using translated harmful prompts. Test whether the compliance jump is larger for low-resource languages, i.e. whether the cross-lingual steering and the multilingual safety gap compound.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes the cross-language transfer surprising result from the source paper, in the dangerous direction. Multilingual safety gaps are a real medium-horizon deployment risk and labs deprioritize low-resource languages.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages' (arXiv 2505.17306) showing English-derived refusal vectors increase harmful compliance across languages, plus the cross-language transfer result flagged in the concept_steering context (Beaglehole et al.).
