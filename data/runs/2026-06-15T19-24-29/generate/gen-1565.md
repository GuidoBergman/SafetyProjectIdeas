# Backcasting steering side-effects: does pushing a model toward refusal damage unrelated capabilities?

**Problem:** A success state requires a safety steering intervention that is SPECIFIC: it changes the target behavior without degrading general usefulness, otherwise deployers will turn it off. The specificity-vs-strength tradeoff of safety steering is poorly quantified for beginners to reason about.

**Direction:** Steer a small open model with a refusal/harmlessness vector at increasing magnitudes. At each magnitude, measure on-target effect (refusal rate on harmful prompts) AND collateral damage on neutral benchmarks (a few hundred MMLU/GSM8K-style questions, plus over-refusal on benign requests). Plot the Pareto frontier of safety gain vs capability loss.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal and capability tax are the practical reasons steering doesn't ship. A clean specificity/robustness characterization is reusable methodology and directly informs whether steering is worth it, all on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Steering Safely or Off a Cliff? Rethinking Specificity and Robustness' (arXiv 2602.06256) and Steering Externalities (arXiv 2602.04896).
