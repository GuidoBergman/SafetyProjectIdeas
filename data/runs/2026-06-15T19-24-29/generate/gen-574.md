# From 'Power-Seeking Is Provably Avoidable' to a Measured Map of When the Theorem's Assumptions Hold

**Problem:** A success state for agent foundations is: we can certify that an agent's environment does NOT incentivize power-seeking. Turner et al.'s theorem says optimal policies tend to seek power for broad reward distributions over MDP structures with retained-options states. The missing empirical gap is a concrete catalogue of which small, realistic gridworld structures actually satisfy vs. violate the theorem's symmetry preconditions.

**Direction:** Implement small MDPs in pure Python (value iteration, no GPU). Enumerate environment topologies, sample reward functions, and measure the fraction of optimal policies that move toward high-option ('power') states. Identify structural features (bottlenecks, irreversible actions, shutdown states) that flip the result. Produce an empirical phase diagram of power-seeking vs. environment structure.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'we can build provably non-power-seeking environments' requires knowing which structures the theorem certifies. A measured map of the assumption boundary is exactly the kind of formal-meets-empirical bridge the field's theory-practice gap calls for, and it runs on a laptop.

**Relevant context:** Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power (Turner et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:1912.01683): power-seeking is a statement about state-space structure under reward symmetry; shutdown-avoidance is convergently instrumental.
