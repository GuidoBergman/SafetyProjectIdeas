# Steering vs. self-preference: replicating activation-based debiasing of an LLM evaluator

**Problem:** A recent paper mitigates self-preference (an evaluator favoring its own outputs) via activation steering. Self-preference is a subtle, fuzzy-reward bias that undermines LLM-as-judge safety evals, and the steering fix has not been replicated or stress-tested on small open judges.

**Direction:** Set up a small open model as an LLM-judge that exhibits self-preference (rates its own completions higher). Build a 'self-preference' CAA vector and steer it out. Measure reduction in self-preference bias and check for collateral damage to overall judging accuracy on a neutral benchmark. Report the bias/accuracy tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** Open-source LLM-as-judge pipelines are widely used for safety evals; an unbiased judge is foundational infrastructure. A cheap, replicable steering debias method that doesn't wreck accuracy is directly useful and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Breaking the Mirror: Activation-Based Mitigation of Self-Preference in LLM Evaluators' (arXiv:2509.03647, ~Sep 2025), validated with 2026 steering-reliability cautions (arXiv:2602.17881).
