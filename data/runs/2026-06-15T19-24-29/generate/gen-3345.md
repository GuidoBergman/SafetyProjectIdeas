# Truncated-Reasoning AUC as a Free Reward-Hacking Smoke Test

**Problem:** A model that takes a shortcut (implicit reward hack) reaches high reward using only a small fraction of its CoT, so its reasoning is unfaithful 'busywork'. The TRACE idea (early-sufficiency of reasoning) exists but no simple open implementation exists for participants to reuse.

**Direction:** On tasks with a verifiable answer, progressively truncate an open model's CoT from the end (early-answering) and record how early the answer stabilizes. Compute an area-under-curve 'reasoning effort' score and compare clean tasks vs. tasks with an exploitable shortcut (e.g. answer leaked in prompt formatting). A low AUC on shortcut tasks indicates unfaithful reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Gives the community a cheap, model-agnostic signal for 'is the CoT doing real work or rationalizing a shortcut?' — directly relevant to detecting reward hacking that monitors miss, and buildable in 30h.

**Relevant context:** 'Is It Thinking or Cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv 2510.01367) introduces TRACE (Truncated Reasoning AUC Evaluation): a hacking model 'achieves high expected reward with only a small fraction of its CoT'. Early-answering truncation is a standard faithfulness intervention (Lanham et al.).
