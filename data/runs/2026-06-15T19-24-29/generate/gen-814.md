# Auditing the Seoul Commitment Gap: Did Signatory Labs Actually Publish What They Promised?

**Problem:** Sixteen companies signed the 2024 Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments, promising to publish safety frameworks, red-teaming results, and a public 'scorecard'. Reports suggest several commitments (e.g. OpenAI's GPT-4o scorecard) were never delivered, but there is no systematic, line-by-line public ledger of which specific promises were kept, partially met, or quietly dropped.

**Direction:** Treat the broken-commitment pattern as the failure mode to characterize. Build a structured checklist by decomposing the Seoul commitments into ~10-15 atomic, verifiable obligations. For each of the ~12 signatory labs, search their published safety frameworks, blog posts, and model cards, and code each obligation as Met / Partial / Absent with an evidence URL. Compute per-lab and per-commitment compliance rates and identify which commitments are systematically unmet across the industry.

**Why it matters:** Voluntary commitments are the de facto governance regime for frontier AI right now. A reproducible, evidence-linked compliance ledger turns 'they didn't follow through' from anecdote into auditable data, directly supporting the open problem of third-party verification of safety claims. It needs no lab access, no model, and no proprietary data — ideal counterfactual-value work that labs themselves are conflicted to produce.

**Relevant context:** METR 'Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies'; Montreal AI Ethics Institute analysis of Seoul commitments noting OpenAI never published the promised scorecard; ~12 of 16 signatories have published frameworks.
