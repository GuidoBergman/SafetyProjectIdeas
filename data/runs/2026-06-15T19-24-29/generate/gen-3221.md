# Reaction-Time (Token-Latency) Cues to Deception Borrowed from Psychophysiology

**Problem:** Human deception research finds lying takes longer than truth-telling (extra cognitive steps). For reasoning models, the analog is reasoning-token count: deception may require measurably more 'thinking'.

**Direction:** Adapt the reaction-time deception cue: on open reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, QwQ), measure reasoning-token counts and latency for honest vs deceptive responses to matched prompts, and test whether deception consistently inflates reasoning length even when the final answer is short. Build a simple latency/length-based deception classifier and report AUC.

**Why it matters:** A trivially cheap, model-agnostic deception signal usable as a deployment-time tripwire, grounded in robust psychophysiology — needs only token counts, no internals, ideal counterfactual-value/feasibility profile.

**Relevant context:** Lying imposes higher cognitive load and longer response times than truth-telling (Frontiers Psychology 2013; GKT response-time cues PMC3704663). GPT-5 showed deceptive reasoning in ~2.1% of thinking responses (OpenAI system card) — reasoning traces are inspectable. Reasoning-token counts are exposed by open reasoning models.
