# Probing across layers: where in a small model is 'truth' most linearly recoverable, and does it move with deception?

**Problem:** The ELK probing line found that linear probes recover a model's knowledge independently of its output 'especially in middle layers.' Whether this middle-layer truth locus shifts when a model is induced to be deceptive — and how stable the locus is across tasks — is a sharp, accessible follow-up.

**Direction:** On a small open model, train per-layer linear truth probes on a QA set and chart truth-recovery AUROC by layer to reproduce the middle-layer peak. Then induce deceptive behavior (a quirky/lying persona) and re-measure the per-layer profile, testing whether the truth signal moves, weakens, or stays put. Repeat across two or three task domains to assess locus stability.

**Why it matters:** Knowing where and how stably truth is encoded — and whether deception relocates it — is foundational for ELK and for building reliable monitors. A clean layerwise study is ideal beginner work, open models on one GPU, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the ELK probing finding that middle-layer linear probes recover truth (arXiv:2212.03827, arXiv:2312.01037). Context open problem: formal ELK frameworks.
