# Length-of-Argument Arms Race: Does More Debate Text Help or Drown the Judge?

**Problem:** More debate rounds give more information but also more text for a weak judge to process. Causal chain: extend debate -> judge context fills -> if the judge degrades with long context, added rounds can lower accuracy. The under-studied link is the non-monotonic relationship between total debate length and weak-judge accuracy.

**Direction:** On a fixed item set, run debates of increasing transcript length (more rounds / longer turns) and measure small-judge accuracy at each length. Identify whether accuracy peaks then declines (information overload) and whether a cheap summarization step before judging recovers performance.

**Why it matters:** Protocol designers assume more debate is safer; a non-monotonic curve would be a concrete design caution. Cheap to run, model-agnostic, and tests a simple summarization mitigation, all outside lab infra.

**Relevant context:** Long-context degradation ('lost in the middle') is well documented for LLMs; debate transcripts can be long (Kenton et al. 2024 swept ~5M generations across multi-round protocols). The interaction between transcript length and weak-judge accuracy is under-studied.
