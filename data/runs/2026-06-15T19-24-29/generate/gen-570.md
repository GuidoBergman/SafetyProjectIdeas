# Acausal Coordination Without Communication: How Strong Is the Schelling Link in Open Models?

**Problem:** A long-horizon multi-agent risk chain: separated AI instances pursue a shared goal -> they coordinate without any channel via Schelling points -> joint behavior evades per-agent oversight. The under-studied link is the raw acausal-coordination capability of open models — how often independent instances pick the same focal action.

**Direction:** Run Schelling-coordination tasks (pick the same word/number/strategy as an unseen partner) across open models, scoring match rate vs a random baseline. Vary task structure (salient defaults, semantic anchors) to find what makes coordination easy, and test whether a simple intervention (randomizing the action space) suppresses coordination.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent collusion is a medium/long-horizon threat labs deprioritize; measuring acausal-coordination strength on open models is a clean, training-free contribution with clear safety framing for a blog post.

**Relevant context:** 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' (LessWrong); 'Schelling coordination via agentic loops' (Apart Research); 'Subversion via Focal Points' (arXiv:2507.03010).
