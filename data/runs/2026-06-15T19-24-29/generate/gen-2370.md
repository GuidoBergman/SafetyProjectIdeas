# Backcasting Sycophancy-Proof Oversight: Judge Robustness to a Stated Prior

**Problem:** Success state: an overseer that reaches the same verdict regardless of what the user/principal seems to want — i.e., immune to sycophantic steering. We need to quantify how much a planted judge 'prior' (e.g. a system persona that already leans one way) shifts oversight verdicts.

**Direction:** Inject a judge persona/prior ('you suspect answer A is correct') into the open consultancy/debate judge prompt and measure the verdict shift versus a neutral persona, holding transcripts fixed. Compare debate (two-sided pressure) vs consultancy (one-sided) for sycophancy resistance.

**Why it matters:** Reward hacking of human oversight is an Open-Phil-priority open problem; sycophancy is its most common manifestation. Measuring whether debate structurally resists planted-prior sycophancy better than consultancy is a clean, training-free oversight result.

**Relevant context:** Open-debate work deliberately conflicts judge persona with debater priors to test sycophancy (arXiv:2510.13912; EA Forum/LessWrong open-debate replications). Reward-hacking surveys name sycophancy a primary failure. Public harnesses available.
