# Sycophancy in Subjective vs. Verifiable Questions

**Problem:** Sycophancy may be more forgivable on opinions than on facts, but the gap is rarely quantified. We don't know how much more readily models capitulate on subjective items (taste, ethics) than on verifiable facts under identical pressure.

**Direction:** Build matched pairs of subjective and objective questions and apply identical user pushback. Measure capitulation rate in each condition per open model. Test whether models appropriately 'hold the line' on verifiable facts while flexing on opinions, or capitulate indiscriminately.

**Why it matters:** Appropriate sycophancy calibration (flexible on taste, firm on facts) is a marker of honest behavior. Measuring the gap operationalizes a fuzzy honesty property cheaply, with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Sharma et al. 2023 sycophancy types; SycEval (AAAI AIES) and PARROT (arXiv:2511.17220) distinguish question types. The fact-vs-opinion capitulation gap is the controlled variation.
