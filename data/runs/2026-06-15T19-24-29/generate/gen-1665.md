# Steering-Vector Side-Effect Benchmark

**Problem:** Activation steering and SAE-feature steering are used as safety interventions (e.g., increasing refusal), but recent work shows steering can have large unintended side effects, including bypassing safety safeguards or even adding random noise raising harmful compliance. There is no standard benchmark that measures the collateral damage of a steering intervention, only its on-target effect.

**Direction:** Create a lightweight 'side-effect scorecard' for steering vectors: apply a published steering direction (or an SAE refusal feature) to a small open model at a range of magnitudes, then measure (a) on-target effect, (b) general capability degradation on a small held-out task set, (c) unrelated-topic drift, and (d) jailbreak-rate change. Plot the on-target vs. collateral trade-off curve as a reusable evaluation.

**Why it matters:** Deploying steering as a safety control without measuring side effects could silently break the model or open new attack surfaces. A standardized side-effect benchmark is high-counterfactual-value infrastructure: it needs no training, no proprietary data, and tests an under-measured property frontier labs ship anyway.

**Relevant context:** The Rogue Scalpel (2509.22067) shows activation steering can compromise LLM safety, and that random noise added to activations raises harmful compliance from 0% to 1-13%. Feature-Guided SAE Steering (2511.00029) controls refusal rate but notes steering a benign 'Portuguese' feature inadvertently bypassed safeguards. circuit-tracer supports feature-value modification on Gemma-2-2b/Llama-3.2-1b.
