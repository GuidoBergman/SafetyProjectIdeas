# Does the Crosswalk Hold? Finding the Broken Link Between NIST AI RMF and Enforceable Controls

**Problem:** Standards-to-safety chain: 'safety objective' -> 'NIST AI RMF subcategory' -> 'ISO 42001 control' -> 'auditable evidence' -> 'assurance'. The official crosswalk claims substantial overlap, but the map-to-auditable-evidence link is where abstract framework language may fail to become checkable, breaking real assurance.

**Direction:** Take the public NIST AI RMF -> ISO 42001 crosswalk (71 mapped requirements) and manually classify each mapping by whether the ISO control produces concrete, third-party-auditable evidence versus a process statement. Identify the cluster of safety-relevant subcategories (e.g., MEASURE function items on dangerous capabilities) that map only to unverifiable process language.

**Why it matters:** Third-party auditability of safety claims is the #1 open problem in technical AI governance. Showing precisely which framework requirements never reach auditable evidence tells standards bodies where the chain is decorative. Document analysis, no compute, high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance (Reuel et al., arXiv:2407.14981, verification/auditability); NIST AI RMF -> ISO 42001 crosswalk ('Rosetta Stone', 71 requirements, Full/Partial/Reference coverage).
