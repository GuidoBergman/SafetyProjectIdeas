# Does grokking generalize across group structure? A modular-arithmetic ablation of the Fourier-multiplication finding

**Problem:** Nanda et al. found transformers grokking modular addition implement a 'sparse Fourier multiplication' circuit, and recent 2025-2026 work frames grokking as a first-order phase transition. It is unclear whether this clean mechanism is special to commutative cyclic groups or a general phenomenon — which matters for trusting mechanistic 'stories' as alignment evidence.

**Direction:** Re-train the standard tiny one-layer transformer on a battery of algebraic tasks (modular addition with different moduli, modular multiplication, symmetric-group composition S_3/S_4, a non-abelian group). For each, log the train/test loss curves to locate the grokking transition, then probe whether a Fourier/representation-theoretic structure appears (via FFT of the embedding matrix and clustering of neuron activations). Compare how sharp the phase transition is across group types.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic explanations of toy phenomena are increasingly cited as the empirical foundation for theoretical alignment claims (e.g. ARC heuristic arguments). If the 'clean circuit' story breaks for non-abelian groups, it tempers how much we should trust toy-model mechanistic explanations as evidence about real models. Pure replication-plus-variation, no training-from-scratch of large models, runs in minutes per task on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Nanda's 'A Mechanistic Interpretability Analysis of Grokking' and arXiv:2603.01192 (grokking as phase transition via singular learning theory).
