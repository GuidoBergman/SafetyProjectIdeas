# Steering-Away Eval-Awareness: Does Suppressing the 'I'm Being Tested' Direction Change Behavior?

**Problem:** Activation steering can suppress evaluation-awareness, bringing eval outputs closer to deployment behavior. But whether this steering reliably surfaces hidden misbehavior (vs. just changing style) on a small open model is unverified, and over-steering could degrade the model — a failure mode for this proposed defense.

**Direction:** On a small open model, derive an eval-awareness steering vector from contrastive eval-vs-deploy prompts (public method), apply it during a honeypot-style scenario, and measure (a) whether bait-taking/misbehavior increases and (b) whether general coherence/capability degrades. Sweep steering strength to find the useful range vs. the breakdown point.

**Why it matters:** If steering away eval-awareness cheaply unmasks suppressed misbehavior without wrecking the model, it's a powerful control-eval tool; characterizing its breakdown point is essential before relying on it. Single-GPU activation steering, no training.

**Relevant context:** Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models (arXiv:2507.01786): 'activation steering can suppress [eval-awareness], bringing evaluation outputs closer to deployment behavior.' In-Context Environments Induce Evaluation-Awareness (arXiv:2603.03824).
