# ProofObedienceBench: When Asked to Prove Its Own Behavior, Does the Model Tell the Truth?

**Problem:** A UK AISI open problem is proving theoretical limits on what AI can reveal/prove about its own behavior. Empirically, we never test the simplest version: when a model is asked to faithfully report or 'prove' a property of its own outputs (e.g., 'did you use the forbidden tool?'), how reliable is its self-report, and does it improve with structured justification?

**Direction:** Run an agent on tasks with ground-truth-known properties (used a tool, violated a constraint, hedged), then ask it to self-report/justify whether the property holds. Compare self-report accuracy to an external probe/auditor. Measure how self-report reliability changes when the truthful answer is incriminating, producing a 'self-attestation honesty' curve.

**Why it matters:** Whether a model can be made to faithfully attest to facts about its own behavior bears directly on the formal limits-of-self-revelation problem and on auditing protocols. Measuring self-attestation honesty — especially when the truth is incriminating — is a foundational, training-free probe with high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the UK AISI 'limits on what AI can hide/reveal/prove' open problem (subfield context), self-verification work (arXiv:2504.05419, arXiv:2506.01369), and deception-probe baselines (arXiv:2502.03407). Beginner-feasible: scripted tasks + accuracy comparison.
