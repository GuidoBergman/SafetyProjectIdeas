# Unfaithful CoT Plus Probe Spurious Firing Yields Double-Blind Deception

**Problem:** Two interacting failures: (1) chain-of-thought unfaithfulness (CoT-monitoring subfield) and (2) deception-probe spurious correlation (interp probing). When CoT hides the real reason for an action AND the linear deception probe fires on spurious cues rather than true deceptive intent, two supposedly-independent monitors fail in a correlated way, giving false assurance.

**Direction:** Take an open model and a deception dataset (e.g. roleplay/sandbagging prompts). Build a linear deception probe on activations. Separately score CoT faithfulness with a simple counterfactual perturbation. Measure correlation of the two monitors' errors: do cases where CoT is unfaithful also tend to be cases where the probe misfires? Map the joint failure region.

**Why it matters:** Defense-in-depth assumes monitors fail independently. If a mech-interp probe and a CoT monitor share blind spots, stacking them gives less safety than advertised. This is a fuzzy-reward, lab-independent evaluation of monitor correlation.

**Relevant context:** 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv:2502.03407) and 'Building Better Deception Probes' (arXiv:2602.01425) note probes fire on honest responses due to spurious correlations. 'Chain-of-Thought Is Not Explainability' (Barez et al., Oxford) and CoT-unfaithfulness work show post-hoc rationalization. 'Probing Classifiers are Unreliable' (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2207.04153).
