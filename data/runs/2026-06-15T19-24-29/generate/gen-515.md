# Does adding the 'Bob' quirk to one fact contaminate unrelated facts? Probing localized vs. diffuse belief corruption

**Problem:** The quirky-models phenomenon (systematic error conditioned on a trigger) is treated as a localized behavior. But a key open question for ELK and model-editing safety is whether implanting/triggering one false belief diffusely corrupts the model's representations of *related, untargeted* facts — a spillover failure mode.

**Direction:** Using the public quirky models (or a small adapter-implanted false belief), build a graph of facts ranging from semantically near the quirked fact to far. Probe the model's latent answers (via linear probes and direct queries) across this graph, with and without the trigger active, measuring whether accuracy/representation on *untargeted* facts shifts. Quantify the spillover radius of the corruption.

**Why it matters:** Spillover would mean truth-probes and edits cannot be trusted as local — a load-bearing assumption for ELK reporters and for safe knowledge editing. Reproducible on public model organisms with cheap probes.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the quirky-models datasets/models (arXiv:2312.01037) and knowledge-editing locality literature; linear probes on an open model, single GPU.
