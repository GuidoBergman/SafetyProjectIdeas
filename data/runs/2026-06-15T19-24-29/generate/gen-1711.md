# When 'Sufficiency' Is Mistaken for 'Necessity': An Activation-Patching Pitfall Demo

**Problem:** Denoising patching tests sufficiency and noising tests necessity, and the two do not imply each other, yet results are routinely over-interpreted. A component shown sufficient is wrongly assumed necessary (and vice versa), producing incorrect circuit claims.

**Direction:** On a small task in GPT-2 small (e.g. IOI or a simple induction task), run both denoising and noising patching for the same set of components. Identify components that are sufficient-but-not-necessary and necessary-but-not-sufficient, and present them as concrete counterexamples. Build a short tutorial-style report showing how reporting only one direction would yield a wrong circuit story.

**Why it matters:** Beginners (the target audience) are the most likely to commit this error, and circuit-based safety claims rest on getting necessity vs sufficiency right. A crisp demonstration with counterexamples is high pedagogical and methodological value and needs only a tiny open model.

**Relevant context:** Heimersheim & Nanda, 'How to use and interpret activation patching' (arXiv:2404.15255) emphasize that denoising shows sufficiency and noising shows necessity, and that one does not imply the other.
