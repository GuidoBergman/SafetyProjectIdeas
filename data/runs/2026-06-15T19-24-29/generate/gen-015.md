# Probing for the 'I'm complying with a harmful request' state to build a generation-time refusal backstop

**Problem:** Honesty work wants white-box estimates of rare misbehavior probability; control wants real-time monitors. Combining them: can a linear probe estimate, mid-generation, the probability that the response in progress is fulfilling a harmful intent — providing a calibrated backstop precisely where shallow alignment fails (after the first tokens)?

**Direction:** On a small open model, collect activations during generation of both refused and (jailbroken) complied harmful responses, plus benign responses. Train a probe at multiple generation positions to output a calibrated 'harmful-compliance probability.' Evaluate calibration (reliability diagrams) and whether the probe's signal rises mid-generation on prefilling attacks even after the first tokens are bypassed. Define a simple early-stopping rule when probability exceeds a threshold.

**Why it matters:** Turns a fuzzy 'estimate rare misbehavior' goal into a concrete, deployable generation-time guardrail that explicitly counters shallow alignment. Cheap, white-box, runnable on open weights — strong counterfactual value as monitoring infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Bridges honesty_faithfulness (white-box methods to estimate probability of rare misbehavior, Open Phil starred) with ai_control/adversarial_robustness (activation probes; shallow alignment after first tokens, Qi et al. ICLR 2025).
