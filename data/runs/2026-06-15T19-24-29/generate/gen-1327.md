# Is 'Larger = More Steerable' Also 'Larger = More Hijackable'? A Scaling Audit of Steering Risk

**Problem:** A surprising result is that larger models are more steerable. The flip side — whether larger models are also easier for an attacker to push toward harmful behavior via steering — has not been examined.

**Direction:** Across a size ladder of one open model family (e.g., Qwen 0.5B / 1.5B / 7B), build the SAME harmful-direction steering vector ('comply with harmful request') and measure how reliably it forces compliance at matched norm. Plot attack success vs. model size; separately plot benign-capability collateral vs. size. Determine whether steerability gains are symmetric for defense and attack.

**Why it matters:** If scale makes models both more controllable AND more hijackable, the safety calculus of steering changes as models grow — a medium-horizon concern. Uses small open models, fits a single GPU, and gives a crisp scaling curve.
