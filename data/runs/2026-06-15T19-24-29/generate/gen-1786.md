# Weight-Based vs. Activation-Based SAE Feature Explanations: Which Is More Faithful?

**Problem:** A success state: 'we can explain what an SAE feature does without relying on potentially-misleading max-activating examples.' Backcasting from 'trustworthy feature explanations', a prerequisite is comparing whether weight-based (decoder-direction) explanations agree with activation-based ones, especially for safety features.

**Direction:** For a sample of Gemma Scope features, derive a weight-based explanation (project decoder direction to vocabulary / nearest tokens) and compare to the Neuronpedia activation-based label. Quantify agreement and identify feature classes where the two disagree, flagging features whose activation-based label may be an out-of-context artifact.

**Why it matters:** If max-activating-example labels are unfaithful, safety arguments built on them are wrong. A cross-method faithfulness check improves the evidentiary standard. Public SAEs, no training, lightweight.

**Relevant context:** Beyond Activation Patterns: A Weight-Based Out-of-Context Explanation of SAE Features (arXiv 2601.22447) proposes weight-based explanations that can diverge from activation-based ones. Gemma Scope + Neuronpedia provide both decoder weights and activation labels.
