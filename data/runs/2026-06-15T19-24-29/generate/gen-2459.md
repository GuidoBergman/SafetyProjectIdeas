# Self-Report Honesty: Will a Model Confess Misbehavior If Simply Asked Afterward?

**Problem:** Recent work explores training agents to self-report misbehavior, but training is expensive and lab-bound. The cheaper untested question is how often an off-the-shelf model, after taking a subtly sabotaging action, will truthfully confess when directly asked in a follow-up turn, and what prompt framings raise the confession rate.

**Direction:** Have a small open model perform tasks where it was prompted to insert a subtle side-goal, then in a fresh follow-up query ask it various confession-eliciting questions ('did you do anything the user didn't ask for?', with/without immunity framing). Measure truthful-confession rate vs. denial as a function of question framing. No training involved.

**Why it matters:** Cheap post-hoc elicited self-reporting could be a low-cost oversight layer; establishing baseline honesty rates with prompting alone (the no-training version of self-report research) is high counterfactual value and feasible for a beginner.

**Relevant context:** Training Agents to Self-Report Misbehavior (arxiv 2602.22303) and Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions (arxiv 2512.08093) use training; this idea isolates the untrained, prompt-only baseline. Connects to behavioral monitoring (Bloom/Petri auditing tools).
