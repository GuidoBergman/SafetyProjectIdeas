# Replicating steering-vector generalization analysis on safety vs. capability concepts

**Problem:** Tan et al. ('Analysing the Generalisation and Reliability of Steering Vectors') show steering vectors vary widely in reliability. Their analysis focused on a set of behaviors; whether safety concepts are systematically less reliable than capability concepts on a new model is a sharp replication.

**Direction:** Replicate the per-concept reliability metric (variance of steering effect across prompts) on an open model for a matched set of safety concepts (refusal, honesty, non-toxicity) vs. capability concepts (formality, verbosity). Twist: a head-to-head reliability comparison that the original didn't frame this way.

**Why it matters:** If safety-relevant directions are inherently noisier/less reliable, steering-based safety tools are on shakier ground than capability demos suggest. Quantifying this gap is a sober, decision-relevant contribution requiring only one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Tan et al., 'Analysing the Generalisation and Reliability of Steering Vectors' (arXiv:2407.12404); 'Predicting Where Steering Vectors Succeed' (arXiv:2604.15557).
