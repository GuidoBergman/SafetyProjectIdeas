# Is the steering signature robust to paraphrase? Testing non-identifiability practically

**Problem:** A 2026 theory paper proves steering vectors are non-identifiable: infinitely many geometrically distinct directions induce identical behavior via null-space ambiguity. The practical fallout — whether two 'equivalent' vectors generalize differently out-of-distribution — is barely tested empirically.

**Direction:** Construct two behaviorally-equivalent steering vectors for one safety concept: the raw CAA vector and a null-space-perturbed variant matched to produce the same in-distribution behavior. Then evaluate both on out-of-distribution prompts (paraphrases, new topics) and compare divergence. Quantify how much hidden geometric freedom matters for generalization.

**Why it matters:** If behaviorally-identical vectors diverge sharply OOD, then reporting only in-distribution steering results is misleading and monitoring vectors may fail silently in deployment. A concrete empirical companion to a theory result, runnable on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'On the Non-Identifiability of Steering Vectors in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2602.06801, ~Feb 2026).
