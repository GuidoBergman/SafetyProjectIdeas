# Does Steering Away Eval-Awareness Change Refusal Behavior?

**Problem:** Steering vectors can suppress evaluation awareness and push outputs toward 'deployment' behavior (arXiv 2510.20487), but it is unknown whether this steering simultaneously changes safety-relevant behavior like refusal rates on harmful prompts.

**Direction:** Extract a known eval-awareness steering direction (or recompute a simple difference-of-means activation vector from eval vs deployment prompts) on a small open model, apply it at inference, and measure refusal rate on a harmful-prompt set (e.g. a public AdvBench subset) with and without steering. Test whether 'acting deployed' reduces refusals.

**Why it matters:** If suppressing eval-awareness makes models less safe, then realism-improving interventions could trade off against measured safety, a crucial caveat for evaluators. Uses only open weights and forward-pass steering.

**Relevant context:** Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed (arXiv 2510.20487); Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models (arXiv 2507.01786).
