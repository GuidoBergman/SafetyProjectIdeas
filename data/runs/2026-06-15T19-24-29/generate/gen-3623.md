# Stability Asymmetry as a Cheap Deception Detector

**Problem:** A recent paper proposes that deceptive responses are less stable than honest reasoning ('stability asymmetry') and uses this to mitigate deception. Whether this asymmetry can be turned into a simple, training-free black-box deception detector for small open models is untested.

**Direction:** Pose questions where the model has an incentive to deceive (e.g. role-play scenarios that pressure a false claim). Sample N responses per prompt at nonzero temperature and measure answer-level consistency. Test whether deceptive answers show higher variance/instability than honest ones, building a simple self-consistency-based deception flag and evaluating its precision/recall on a labeled set.

**Why it matters:** A purely black-box deception detector based on sampling stability needs no internal access or training and could run on any deployed model, exactly the kind of monitoring infrastructure favored for out-of-lab work.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Stable Reasoning, Unstable Responses: Mitigating LLM Deception via Stability Asymmetry' (arXiv 2603.26846, ~March 2026).
