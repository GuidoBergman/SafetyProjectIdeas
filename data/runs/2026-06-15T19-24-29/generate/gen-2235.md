# Weak-to-Strong Generalization Under Label Noise from a Biased Supervisor

**Problem:** W2S experiments use weak labels that are noisy but roughly unbiased. A more realistic model of a systematically-erring human overseer is a supervisor with a consistent directional bias. How W2S elicitation degrades when the weak supervisor's errors are systematic rather than random is untested.

**Direction:** Using the public W2S setup with small open models, deliberately inject a systematic bias into the weak supervisor's labels (e.g. it consistently mislabels a particular subclass) rather than uniform noise. Replicate PGR measurement and compare W2S performance under (a) random label noise vs (b) matched-rate systematic bias, to see whether the strong student 'corrects' systematic errors or amplifies them.

**Why it matters:** The core scalable-oversight worry is overseers with consistent blind spots, not random mistakes. If strong students inherit systematic supervisor biases more than random noise, naive W2S could entrench overseer blind spots. Directly probes the 'systematic oversight errors' open problem; open models only.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. 2023 W2S code (openai/weak-to-strong) supports custom weak labels. Anthropic recommended directions list 'characterize and mitigate systematic oversight errors' as an open problem. Existing W2S work uses unbiased noise; systematic-bias injection is the twist.
