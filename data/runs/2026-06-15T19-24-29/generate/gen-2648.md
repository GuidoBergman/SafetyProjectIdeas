# The Sampling-Temperature Link in Attack Reliability vs. Monitor Evasion

**Problem:** Risk: an untrusted model both succeeds at sabotage and evades detection. Causal chain: model generates an attack at some decoding temperature -> high temperature gives more diverse evasion attempts but less reliable exploits; low temperature gives reliable exploits but stereotyped, detectable phrasing. The under-studied link is the joint trade-off curve: where in temperature space is an attacker simultaneously reliable AND stealthy, and can the blue team push the attacker off that sweet spot?

**Direction:** Fix an attack task (insert a working backdoor + stay below monitor threshold). Sweep decoding temperature for an open attacker model and jointly measure exploit success rate and monitor suspicion. Plot the reliability/stealth Pareto frontier and identify whether a blue-team forced-temperature setting collapses the attacker's viable region.

**Why it matters:** Decoding parameters are an overlooked, cheap blue-team lever. If forcing a temperature shrinks the attacker's reliable-and-stealthy region, that is a near-free control measure. Fully open-model, single GPU, no training, novel angle vs. existing decoding-consistency work.

**Relevant context:** AI Control APPS backdoor setting (arXiv:2312.06942) defines reliable exploits on secret inputs. SHADE-Arena (arXiv:2506.15740) shows agents fail sabotage partly via execution unreliability. CoT Red-Handed (arXiv:2505.23575): effective obfuscation methods like ciphers degrade task performance, hinting at a reliability/stealth tension.
