# Mapping the saturation cliff: where steering vectors stop helping and start breaking coherence

**Problem:** Activation-addition steering is applied at some coefficient, but the relationship between dose and (a) on-target behavior change and (b) collateral coherence loss is poorly characterized. Practitioners pick coefficients by eye, with no documented map of the safe operating range per concept/layer.

**Direction:** On an open model (Llama-3-8B or Gemma-2-9B) with the open-source steering-vectors library, extract a refusal or honesty CAA vector, then sweep the steering coefficient across a fine grid at several layers. For each (layer, coefficient) measure on-target effect (refusal rate on a held-out harmful/benign set) and a coherence proxy (perplexity on neutral text + an automated fluency judge). Plot dose-response curves and locate the 'saturation cliff' where coherence collapses before or after the behavior fully shifts.

**Why it matters:** If the window where steering works without degrading the model is narrow or concept-dependent, deploying steering as a safety control is fragile. Documenting the cliff per concept gives a reusable safety-budget heuristic and quantifies a known failure mode (too-much-steering breaks coherence) that no major lab is incentivized to publish carefully.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the 'larger-models-more-steerable' finding (Beaglehole et al. Science 2026) and reliability work showing too much steering breaks coherence and that norm preservation is critical (arXiv 2512.01991, 2504.04635). Uses only inference on an open model, single consumer GPU.
