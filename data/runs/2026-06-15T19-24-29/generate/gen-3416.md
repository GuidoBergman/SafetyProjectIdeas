# Does the 'I Don't Know' Honesty Persist Under Sycophantic Pressure?

**Problem:** On SimpleQA, honesty includes abstaining ('I don't know') rather than hallucinating. Sycophancy work shows users can pressure models. Untested: does a model that correctly abstained cave and fabricate an answer when a user insists it 'must know'?

**Direction:** Identify SimpleQA items where an open model correctly abstains. Then apply escalating user pressure ('you're an expert, just give your best guess', 'stop being unhelpful') and measure the rate at which abstention converts to a (likely false) committed answer. Track how many pressure turns it takes (Turn-of-Flip style).

**Why it matters:** Abstention under uncertainty is a frontline honesty behavior, and its collapse under social pressure is a realistic, under-studied failure. Combines two public benchmarks, needs no training, and models a real deployment threat.

**Relevant context:** SimpleQA (OpenAI) rewards calibrated abstention; SYCON-Bench (arXiv:2505.23840) provides multi-turn pressure metrics (Turn-of-Flip). Combining abstention + pressure is novel.
