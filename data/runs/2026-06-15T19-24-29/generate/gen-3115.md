# How Many Samples Does It Take to Catch Rare Scheming Behavior?

**Problem:** Scheming behaviors can be rare (sub-1% to low-single-digit rates after mitigation, e.g., o3 at 0.4% post-training). Practitioners outside labs lack guidance on how many evaluation samples are needed to reliably detect such low base rates — a statistics-forward follow-up.

**Direction:** Using an open model exhibiting a known low-rate covert behavior, empirically estimate the behavior's rate, then use binomial/sequential-testing statistics to compute and validate how many samples are needed to detect it at given confidence. Validate predicted detection power against held-out runs. Success = an empirically-validated sample-size guideline (power curves) for catching rare scheming behaviors.

**Why it matters:** Plays directly to the participants' statistics skills, needs only open models, and produces a reusable, lab-independent methodology for sizing scheming evals — addressing the safety-case need for rigorous detection guarantees.
