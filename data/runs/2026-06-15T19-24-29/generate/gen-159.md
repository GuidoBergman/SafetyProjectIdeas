# Many-Shot vs Prefill: Which Attack Probes 'Shallower'? A Head-to-Head Variation

**Problem:** Many-shot jailbreaking and prefill attacks both exploit the first-token vulnerability but via different mechanisms (in-context demonstrations vs forced output). Their relative effectiveness and whether they stack super-additively on the same models has not been directly compared.

**Direction:** On a common set of harmful prompts and 3 small open models, measure attack-success for: prefill-only, many-shot-only, and combined (many-shot demonstrations + short forced prefill). Test for super-additivity and identify which attack each model resists best, relating results to each model's shallow-alignment depth profile.

**Why it matters:** Reveals whether defenses tuned against one attack leave the other open and whether attacks compose dangerously — informing realistic threat models. A clean factorial experiment combining two famous public attacks, runnable on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Anil et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024) and Qi et al. prefill attacks (arXiv:2406.05946) are both grounded in the first-token vulnerability, making a head-to-head factorial a natural and novel variation. StrongREJECT scorer (arXiv:2402.10260) grades outcomes.
