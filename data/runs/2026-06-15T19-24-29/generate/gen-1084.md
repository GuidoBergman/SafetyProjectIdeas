# Replicating Mode Collapse on Open Models: Base vs. RLHF-Aligned Diversity Gap

**Problem:** Work on diversity collapse (e.g. RLHF reduces output diversity; cosine similarity rises from ~0.16 base to ~0.35 aligned) is mostly demonstrated on proprietary or specific model pairs. A clean, reproducible base-vs-aligned diversity comparison across several open model families would establish how universal the collapse is.

**Direction:** For 3-4 open families that ship both a base and an instruct/aligned checkpoint (Llama-3-8B base vs. instruct, Qwen2.5 base vs. instruct, Mistral-7B base vs. instruct), generate many samples per open-ended prompt and measure within-model diversity (distinct-n, self-BLEU, embedding spread/Vendi score). Twist: test whether aligned models from *different* families collapse toward each *other* (cross-family convergence), not just lose internal diversity.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying that alignment both reduces per-model diversity AND pulls different models toward a shared distribution operationalizes the systemic-risk mechanism behind the Hivemind. Fully reproducible with open weights, no training, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Replicates RLHF-diversity-collapse findings (e.g. 'Understanding the Effects of RLHF on LLM Generalisation and Diversity', arXiv:2310.06452; 'Price of Format' arXiv:2505.18949) with an explicit cross-family convergence twist tying back to Artificial Hivemind.
