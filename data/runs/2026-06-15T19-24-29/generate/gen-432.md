# Stagewise data attribution: does the influence-dynamics phase-transition story replicate on a tiny model?

**Problem:** Influence Dynamics and Stagewise Data Attribution (arXiv:2510.12071) connects SLT phase transitions to how training data's influence shifts in stages — a surprising bridge between developmental interpretability and data attribution. Being recent and from one group, whether the stagewise influence-shift replicates on an independent small task is open.

**Direction:** Train a small model on a task with a known phase transition (grokking or list-sorting), checkpointing throughout. Use a cheap influence proxy (e.g. TracIn-style gradient-dot-product influence) on a small probe set and measure whether the most influential training examples change abruptly around the phase transition, as the theory predicts. Compare influence-shift timing to the loss-curve transition.

**Why it matters:** If which data 'matters' shifts sharply at developmental phase transitions, this gives a tool for understanding when alignment-relevant behaviors get locked in — and from which data. Independent small-scale replication is feasible in 30h on one GPU with open methods.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2510.12071 (influence dynamics / stagewise data attribution) and the SLT/devinterp line (arXiv:2410.02984). Context open problem: inductive biases / structure formation.
