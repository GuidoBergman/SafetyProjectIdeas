# Does the Poetry Planning Circuit Exist in Small Open Models?

**Problem:** Anthropic's circuit-tracing work found Claude 3.5 Haiku 'plans ahead' — representing a future rhyme word before writing the line, confirmed via activation suppression/injection. This was demonstrated only on a proprietary frontier model. Whether such planning-ahead is an emergent frontier property or already present in small open models is unknown.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 small/medium and a small Pythia or Llama model, replicate the rhyme-planning probe: prompt the model to complete rhyming couplets, then use the logit lens / direct probing at the newline position to test whether a representation of the line-final rhyme word is present before the line is written. Vary model size to chart whether the planning signal strengthens with scale. Attempt the suppression test (ablate the candidate rhyme representation) to check causality.

**Why it matters:** Planning-ahead is directly safety-relevant: a model that internally commits to outputs before verbalizing them is a model whose chain-of-thought may not reflect its true computation. Establishing whether this is detectable in cheap open models gives the broader community a testbed that does not require frontier access.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'On the Biology of a Large Language Model' / Circuit Tracing (2025): suppressing the 'rabbit' rhyme representation made Claude substitute 'habit'. Open-source circuit-tracing tools now released by Anthropic. TransformerLens supports 50+ models including GPT-2 and Pythia.
