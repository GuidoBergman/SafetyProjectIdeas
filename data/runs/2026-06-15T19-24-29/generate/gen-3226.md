# Differential-Privacy-Style Membership Inference to Detect Covert Malicious Fine-Tuning

**Problem:** Open-weight models can be covertly fine-tuned to insert backdoors/malicious behavior. UK AISI flags detecting such covert fine-tuning as open. Membership-inference attacks from privacy research detect what data a model was trained on.

**Direction:** Borrow membership-inference methodology: compare loss/perplexity and behavioral fingerprints of a base open model vs a suspected covertly-fine-tuned variant on a probe set, using the membership-inference signal (lower loss on injected trigger phrases) to flag hidden fine-tuning and approximately recover trigger characteristics. Test on a small model you cheaply fine-tune with a planted backdoor as the positive case.

**Why it matters:** Provides a forensic test for covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models — exactly the UK AISI open problem — using only inference-time probing of two checkpoints, feasible on a single GPU and valuable to the broad open-weight ecosystem.

**Relevant context:** Membership inference detects training-data membership via loss/confidence gaps (privacy/ML security standard). UK AISI lists detecting covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models as a research area. Backdoor/alignment-stress-test is an Open Phil starred problem. Small fine-tunes are cheap on one GPU.
