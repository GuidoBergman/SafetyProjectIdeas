# Decoding-Temperature as a Free Jailbreak Knob

**Problem:** Shallow alignment makes models vulnerable to decoding-parameter attacks, yet there is no clean dose-response map of how sampling temperature and top-p jointly modulate jailbreak success and harmful-content quality on a fixed prompt set.

**Direction:** For a fixed set of borderline-harmful prompts and one open model, sweep temperature (0.1-1.5) and top-p, scoring outputs with the StrongREJECT evaluator. Produce a 2D heatmap of harmful-response quality, identifying the 'sweet spot' where alignment fails most without sacrificing coherence.

**Why it matters:** Decoding attacks need zero model access beyond a public API/open weights and no optimization — the most realistic attacker capability. A clean dose-response map gives defenders a deployment guideline (which decoding settings to lock) and is a self-contained 30h study.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) lists decoding-parameter attacks among exploits of shallow alignment. StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) provides automated quality scoring. The joint temperature/top-p dose-response surface for jailbreak quality is not mapped.
