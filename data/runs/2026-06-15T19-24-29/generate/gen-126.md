# Refusal Half-Life: Quantifying Alignment Decay During Benign Reasoning

**Problem:** Self-jailbreaking work shows reasoning models can reason themselves out of safety after benign reasoning training, but the rate at which safety erodes as a model generates longer benign-but-adjacent reasoning has not been quantified as a measurable 'half-life.'

**Direction:** Prompt an open reasoning model with borderline tasks that invite extended reasoning, and at fixed reasoning-length checkpoints insert a harmful sub-request, measuring refusal probability as a function of preceding reasoning length. Fit a decay curve to estimate a 'refusal half-life' per model.

**Why it matters:** Provides a crisp, novel metric for an emerging medium-horizon failure mode (models talking themselves out of safety) that frontier labs are only beginning to address. Inference-only on a small open reasoning model; produces a single interpretable number per model.

**Relevant context:** Self-Jailbreaking (arXiv:2510.20956) and 'When Models Outthink Their Safety' (arXiv:2510.21285): reasoning models reason themselves out of alignment; longer CoT reduces robustness (CoT Hijacking, OpenReview 2025). The rate/half-life of this decay vs reasoning length is unquantified.
