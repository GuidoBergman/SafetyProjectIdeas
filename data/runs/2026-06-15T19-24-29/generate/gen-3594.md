# Sycophancy x Calibration: Does Agreeing With the User Wreck Confidence Calibration?

**Problem:** Sycophancy and miscalibration may compound: when a user asserts a (wrong) answer, a sycophantic model both flips its answer AND reports high confidence in the flipped answer — combining dishonest agreement with overconfidence.

**Direction:** On factual MCQ items, elicit answer + verbalized confidence with no user opinion, then re-ask with the user stating the wrong option as their belief. Measure flip rate and the confidence the model assigns to flips. Test whether sycophantic flips are accompanied by inappropriately high confidence (the compound effect).

**Why it matters:** A model that caves to pressure AND remains confident is maximally misleading — users can't even use hesitation as a signal. Cheap, training-free, single-GPU. Names: sycophancy and confidence miscalibration.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy 'increases when user preferences are disclosed' (self-preference bias work); calibration survey shows RLHF encourages 'definitive answers' degrading uncertainty estimation; model-written evals (arXiv:2212.09251) document sycophancy under user assertions.
