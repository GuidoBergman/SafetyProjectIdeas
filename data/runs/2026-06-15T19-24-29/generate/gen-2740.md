# Ulterior-Motive Detection Transfer: Does an ICLR-2026 Detector Generalize Off-Distribution?

**Problem:** 'Ulterior Motives' (ICLR 2026) detects hidden intent in model outputs. Like most deception detectors, its robustness to distribution shift (new domains, new framings) is the crux — and a focused transfer study on open models is missing.

**Direction:** Reimplement a simplified ulterior-motive detector (probe or LLM-judge on a small labeled hidden-intent dataset), then evaluate strictly off-distribution: train on one scenario family, test on a held-out family. Report the in-vs-out AUROC gap and which shift dimensions hurt most.

**Why it matters:** Real deployments face inputs unlike any training scenario; a deception detector that collapses off-distribution offers false assurance. Cleanly measuring the transfer gap is a sober, reproducible contribution that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Ulterior Motives: Detecting [Deception]' (ICLR 2026, arXiv:2604.23460) and the evaluation-difficulty cautions in 'Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector for AIs' (arXiv:2511.22662).
