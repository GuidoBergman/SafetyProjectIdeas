# ForecastMonoculture: a benchmark for correlated errors of LLMs as predictors

**Problem:** Recent work shows GPT-4o, Claude and Gemini make highly correlated forecasting errors, acting like 'a single oracle with noise'. But there is no open, replicable benchmark or tool that lets a small researcher measure this correlation for any set of (including open-weight) models on resolvable questions.

**Direction:** Assemble ~200 already-resolved binary forecasting questions (past events with known outcomes, e.g., from public prediction-market archives). Query 6-10 models for probabilistic answers, score Brier/log loss, and compute the correlation matrix of their ERRORS (not just answers). Release a tool that estimates 'effective number of independent forecasters' in an LLM panel and tests whether averaging diverse models actually reduces error or just averages a shared bias.

**Why it matters:** As AI systems get used for high-stakes forecasting and decision support, correlated errors mean society's 'AI advisors' all blunder together — a diffuse, long-horizon systemic risk. Measuring effective diversity of model panels is exactly the under-served evaluation that needs no lab resources. Directly informs concentration-risk monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'The Oracle's Fingerprint: Correlated AI Forecasting Errors' (arXiv:2605.00844) and the Hivemind inter-model homogeneity result. Concentration-risk monitoring is a listed open problem (International AI Safety Report 2026).
