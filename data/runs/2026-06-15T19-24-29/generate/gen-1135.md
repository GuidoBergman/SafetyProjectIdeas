# Reproducing the Artificial Hivemind on safety-relevant open-ended prompts

**Problem:** The NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper found striking inter-model homogeneity on open-ended generation using Infinity-Chat, but its taxonomy emphasizes creative/brainstorm tasks. Whether the hivemind effect extends to *safety-relevant* open-ended prompts (e.g., 'list ways an AI could be misused', 'brainstorm risks of X', 'how could this go wrong') is untested.

**Direction:** Curate ~150 safety-flavored open-ended prompts (threat-modeling, red-team brainstorm, risk enumeration). Run 6-8 small/open models plus a couple of APIs. Reuse the paper's diversity metrics (embedding-based pairwise similarity for inter-model homogeneity, intra-model self-similarity). Compare hivemind strength on safety prompts vs. matched generic creative prompts.

**Why it matters:** If models converge on the SAME risk enumerations, our collective ability to brainstorm novel AI threats via LLMs has a systematic blind spot — everyone gets the same incomplete list. Quantifying this is a direct, feasible extension of the foundational paper.

**Relevant context:** Direct follow-up to Artificial Hivemind (OpenReview saDOrrnNTz), using its diversity-metric methodology and the Infinity-Chat-style prompt taxonomy. No training; embedding similarity runs on a single GPU.
