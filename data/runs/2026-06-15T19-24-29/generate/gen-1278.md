# Self-Consistency Lies: Homogeneity Inflates Confidence Calibration Failures

**Problem:** Failure mode A: miscalibration / overconfidence. Failure mode B: homogeneity. Self-consistency (sample many answers, take the mode, treat agreement as confidence) assumes diverse samples. Intra-model repetition (Artificial Hivemind) means samples are not diverse, so high agreement reflects mode-collapse, not correctness — overconfidence is amplified.

**Direction:** On a QA set, sample k completions per item from open models at nonzero temperature. Measure self-consistency agreement vs actual correctness. Show that high agreement does not imply correctness for items where the model is confidently wrong, and relate the gap to the model's intra-model repetition score. Report a 'false-confidence-from-consistency' rate.

**Why it matters:** Self-consistency is a ubiquitous, cheap reliability trick used in safety-critical settings. Intra-model repetition breaks its core assumption. Quantifying when agreement is mode-collapse vs evidence is a clean, lab-independent result.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind intra-model repetition; self-consistency / calibration literature; correlated-errors (arXiv 2506.07962).
