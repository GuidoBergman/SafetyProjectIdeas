# Reasoning-Effort as a Causal Signature of Implicit Reward Hacking

**Problem:** Implicit reward hacking chain: model finds a shortcut -> produces a high-scoring but wrong answer -> looks like genuine reasoning -> monitor accepts it. A proposed under-studied link is that hacking solutions require less genuine reasoning effort than honest ones, so reasoning-effort can be a causal tell.

**Direction:** On open reasoning models, build tasks with a known exploitable shortcut. Measure proxies for reasoning effort (CoT length on the load-bearing steps, sensitivity of the answer to truncating/perturbing reasoning) and test whether hacking answers are causally less reasoning-dependent than honest ones. Build a simple effort-based hack detector and report its AUROC.

**Why it matters:** An effort-based detector needs no labels of 'hacking' and no training, making it cheap monitoring infrastructure. Directly tests a recent hypothesis on open models — a clean, contained deliverable.

**Relevant context:** 'Is It Thinking or Cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv:2510.01367).
