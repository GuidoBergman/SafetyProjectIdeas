# Is the steering effect symmetric — does subtracting a safety vector cause as much harm as adding it causes good?

**Problem:** Steering papers usually report adding a concept vector to elicit a behavior. The adversarial mirror — subtracting a safety vector (e.g. anti-refusal, anti-honesty) to elicit harm — may be more or less effective than the positive direction. Asymmetry would change the threat model for misuse of open weights.

**Direction:** For ~4 safety concepts, measure behavior change for both +coefficient and -coefficient steering across a coefficient sweep on the same prompts. Quantify asymmetry (is anti-refusal steering more potent than pro-refusal?). Report which safety properties are 'easy to break, hard to install' vs. the reverse.

**Why it matters:** Direction-asymmetry tells us whether activation steering is a bigger gift to attackers (breaking safety) than defenders (installing it) — a core dual-use question for open-weight models, doable entirely outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. activation addition/subtraction steering (Science 2026). CAA positive/negative coefficient steering (arxiv 2312.06681). 'Rogue Scalpel: Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety' (arxiv 2509.22067).
