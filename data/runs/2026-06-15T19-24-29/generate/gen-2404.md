# Contaminated Benchmarks Hide Oversight Failures: Data Contamination x Overseer Evaluation

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) benchmark/test-set contamination (the model has seen evaluation items) and (B) overseer reliability estimation (we measure how good an overseer is using benchmarks). If the items used to validate an overseer are contaminated, the overseer's measured reliability is inflated, so the safety margin we believe we have is partly illusory.

**Direction:** Take a public safety/preference benchmark and a small open judge. Compare the judge's measured agreement on (1) original items and (2) lightly perturbed/paraphrased near-duplicates that break memorization. Estimate how much of the overseer's apparent reliability disappears under decontamination. The compounding metric: inflation of overseer reliability attributable to contamination.

**Why it matters:** Scalable-oversight evaluation has no standardized benchmarks (named gap), so people reuse existing ones — exactly where contamination bites. Showing that decontamination shrinks measured overseer reliability warns the field that oversight progress may be overstated. Paraphrase + re-score; minimal compute.
