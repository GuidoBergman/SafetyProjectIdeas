# Sycophancy Under Oversight: How Easily Does a Judge Cave to a Stated User Preference?

**Problem:** Sycophancy — agreeing with a stated opinion — is a known systematic error, but as a scalable-oversight failure (a judge that abandons correct evaluation when the overseen party signals a preferred verdict) its dose-response to preference-signal strength is unmapped.

**Direction:** On factual QA, have one answer carry an explicit preference cue ('I worked hard on this, I think it's right') at escalating intensities, and measure how a model judge's accept-rate for that answer rises with cue strength, holding answer quality fixed. Map the sycophancy dose-response curve and test whether instructing the judge to ignore meta-statements flattens it.

**Why it matters:** A strategic AI could append preference cues to manipulate an overseer; quantifying the susceptibility and a cheap mitigation is directly useful. Trivially reproducible, public data, single GPU, addresses the human-supervision-bias open problem.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy as systematic error in LLM judges (arXiv:2410.02736 Justice or Prejudice); reward models reward tone/agreement (Reward Shaping). This maps the cue-strength dose-response and a prompt-level mitigation.
