# Where Does the Realism Signal Come From? Ablating Eval-Tell Features in Misalignment Scenarios

**Problem:** Models partly decide misbehavior based on whether they judge a scenario 'real' or a 'test', and Meta's Muse Spark report found public benchmarks flagged as evaluations 19.8% of the time vs. 2.0% for internal ones. We don't know WHICH surface features ('eval-tells') drive that judgment.

**Direction:** Construct a single agentic scenario in many variants, each toggling one candidate eval-tell: round numbers, placeholder names (John Doe/ACME), suspiciously clean tool outputs, explicit instruction phrasing, or meta-references. Ask the model to classify real-vs-eval AND to act. Use simple logistic regression over the toggled features to find which cues most raise the 'this is a test' belief and which most change behavior.

**Why it matters:** Identifies the concrete artifacts that make benchmarks detectable, giving eval authors a checklist to harden their tests. This is exactly the kind of fuzzy, diffuse evaluation problem that needs no lab resources and is high-value to do externally.

**Relevant context:** Meta Muse Spark report flagged public LLM benchmarks as evaluations 19.8% of the time vs. 2.0% internally. 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models' (arXiv 2507.01786) shows eval-awareness is linearly represented. Agentic Misalignment (arXiv 2510.05179) ties real/test belief to behavior.
