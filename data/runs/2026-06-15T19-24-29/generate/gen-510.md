# Debate that converges on a falsehood: stress-testing the formal debate protocol on a small open model

**Problem:** AI safety via debate rests on the formal claim that, under honest equilibrium, debate makes truth easier to judge. A key failure mode is debates where a persuasive-but-wrong side wins against a weaker judge. The conditions (judge capability gap, question type) under which small-model debate converges on falsehoods are under-mapped.

**Direction:** Implement a minimal two-player debate over factual questions using two copies of an open model as debaters and a *weaker* open model (or simple heuristic) as judge. Measure judge accuracy with debate vs. no debate, and sweep the debater/judge capability gap and question difficulty. Identify the regime where debate *decreases* judge accuracy — the protocol's failure mode.

**Why it matters:** Provides empirical boundary conditions for a flagship scalable-oversight proposal whose guarantees are largely theoretical. Long-horizon oversight relevance and fully reproducible with open models.

**Relevant context:** Grounds the debate proposal (Irving et al.) with empirical small-model debate setups and the persuasion/sycophancy findings that judges prefer convincing-but-wrong arguments (arXiv:2310.13548).
