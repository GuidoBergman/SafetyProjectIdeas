# Eval Saturation x Risk Tiering: When Maxed-Out Benchmarks Stop Differentiating Dangerous Models

**Problem:** Failure mode A: capability benchmarks saturate (top models all near ceiling). Failure mode B: governance risk tiers rely on benchmark scores to separate 'systemic-risk' from ordinary models. When the benchmark saturates, the tiering signal collapses — two very differently dangerous models look identical, so mitigation obligations are mis-assigned.

**Direction:** Take a public safety/capability benchmark and several open models of varying size. Show empirically where scores compress at the top, then quantify how much discriminative power (e.g. score variance / rank separability) the benchmark loses as models improve. Translate the loss-of-separation into 'how confidently could a regulator tier these models?' Provide a saturation-monitoring metric.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that the metric backbone of risk-tiered governance degrades exactly as capabilities (and stakes) rise. Cheap, public-model analysis; directly informs international eval-methodology standards work.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance on assessment limits (arXiv:2407.14981); International Network of AI Safety Institutes consensus goal (FLI AI Safety Index).
