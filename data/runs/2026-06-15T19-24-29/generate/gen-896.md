# Backcasting from 'A Working National AI Incident Reporting System': Mapping Coverage Gaps in the Existing Incident Database

**Problem:** A success state with an NTSB-style national AI incident response capability requires comprehensive, well-classified incident data. Today incident reporting is fragmented across databases with inconsistent taxonomies, so we cannot tell which harm categories are systematically under-reported.

**Direction:** Download the public AI Incident Database (AIID) export and classify a sample of incidents against the CSET AI Harm Taxonomy and MIT AI Risk Repository categories using a fixed rubric (manual + LLM-assisted). Analyze the distribution: which sectors, harm types, and severities dominate; which are absent; how reporting lag and source type bias the record. Output a coverage-gap report that a hypothetical incident-response body would need.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'effective incident response' shows the precondition is unbiased, complete incident data. Quantifying systematic blind spots tells policymakers where mandatory reporting would add most value. Pure public-data analysis, no lab resources, directly serving The Future Society's NTSB-analogue agenda.

**Relevant context:** AIID and AIAAIC databases; CSET AI Harm Taxonomy; MIT AI Risk Repository; Stanford HAI 2025 found incidents up 56.4% in 2024. 'Scalable AI Incident Classification' (incidentdatabase.ai) used LLM classification against MIT taxonomies.
