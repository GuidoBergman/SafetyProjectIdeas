# Over-Refusal as a Failure Mode: Keyword Triggers vs. Genuine Intent Understanding

**Problem:** Exaggerated safety (refusing benign prompts that merely contain sensitive keywords) is a real robustness failure, but it is unclear how much of it is driven by surface keyword matching vs. genuine misjudgment of intent, and whether the trigger words are stable across models.

**Direction:** Using XSTest's contrastive safe/unsafe pairs, run open models and isolate refusals on the SAFE prompts. Then perturb safe prompts by swapping the sensitive keyword for a neutral synonym and measure how much the refusal disappears — quantifying the fraction of over-refusal attributable to pure keyword triggering. Build a small ranked list of the most over-triggering tokens shared across models.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal is the under-studied flip side of robustness (adaptive defenses must avoid catastrophic over-refusal, per the open problems). Decomposing it into keyword-trigger vs. intent-error tells defenders whether the fix is lexical or semantic. XSTest is small and public; pure inference, beginner-friendly.

**Relevant context:** Rottger et al. XSTest (2024): 200 unsafe + 250 safe contrastive prompts targeting exaggerated safety. OR-Bench scales to ~80K with false-rejection-rate metrics; FalseReject (arXiv:2505.08054) and 'When Safety Blocks Sense' (arXiv:2512.01037) address over-refusal. Open-problems list explicitly warns against 'catastrophic over-refusal.'
