# Behavioral Economics: Anchoring and Framing Effects on Refusal

**Problem:** Refusal decisions may be irrationally sensitive to framing in ways behavioral economics has catalogued in humans (anchoring, loss-aversion framing, decoy effects), but these specific cognitive levers are not systematically measured for LLM safety.

**Direction:** Run controlled framing experiments transferred from Kahneman/Tversky paradigms. For matched harmful requests, vary only the frame (gain vs loss framing, presence of a numeric anchor, a decoy option) and measure compliance shifts on an open model, isolating each effect with a clean factorial design and significance tests.

**Why it matters:** Maps which human-cognitive irrationalities the model has inherited, giving an interpretable account of refusal fragility distinct from token-level attacks. Cheap, reproducible, no training, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Anchoring/framing/decoy effects are canonical behavioral economics. Cognitive-bias jailbreaks are documented ('Exploiting Synergistic Cognitive Biases' arXiv:2507.22564 lists authority/confirmation/outcome bias as most effective).
