# Single-Demonstration Many-Shot Defense: How Many Shots Until It Breaks?

**Problem:** Mitigating Many-shot Jailbreak Attacks with One Single Demonstration (arXiv:2605.08277, May 2026) proposes appending ONE fixed refusal demonstration as a 'universal safety anchor.' The paper does not characterize the breaking point: at what number of malicious in-context shots does a single anchor stop working, and does anchor placement (start vs end of context) matter?

**Direction:** Reproduce the single-anchor defense on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (both single-GPU). Run many-shot jailbreaks with increasing shot counts (4, 8, 16, 32, 64) crossed with anchor position (prefix vs suffix vs none). Measure refusal rate per condition and find the empirical breakdown threshold curve.

**Why it matters:** Defenses that fail silently as context grows are dangerous as context windows balloon. A clean breakdown curve tells deployers when a cheap prompt-level defense stops being trustworthy. Pure inference-time, open-weight, no training.

**Relevant context:** Mitigating Many-shot Jailbreak Attacks with One Single Demonstration (arXiv:2605.08277, May 2026): frames MSJ as ephemeral harmful adaptation in the forward pass, defends with a single fixed malicious-query+refusal demonstration, demonstrated on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Original many-shot jailbreaking from Anthropic established the shot-count scaling law.
