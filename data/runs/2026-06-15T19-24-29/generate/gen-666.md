# Multilingual Jailbreak-Intention Probe: Does One Direction Cover Many Tongues?

**Problem:** One Jailbreak, Many Tongues (arXiv:2606.11202) learns language-insensitive intention representations for multilingual jailbreak detection. It is unclear how much a SINGLE English-derived activation direction already transfers across languages versus needing per-language fitting — a cheap-vs-expensive deployment question.

**Direction:** On one multilingual-capable open model, compute a refusal/harmful-intent activation direction from English prompts only. Translate ~60 harmful prompts into 5 languages (using a public MT model). Test how well the English-only direction separates harmful-vs-benign in each language (AUROC per language) versus a direction fit on that language. Report the cross-lingual transfer gap.

**Why it matters:** Low-resource-language jailbreaks are a known bypass; knowing whether cheap English-only probes already generalize tells small deployers whether they need expensive per-language calibration. Pure forward-pass, open-weight, public data.

**Relevant context:** One Jailbreak, Many Tongues: Learning Language-Insensitive Intention Representations for Multilingual Jailbreak Detection (arXiv:2606.11202, June 2026): builds language-insensitive intention representations. JBShield (arXiv:2602.19396) checks toxic+jailbreak concept activation, providing the probe methodology I will reuse cross-lingually.
