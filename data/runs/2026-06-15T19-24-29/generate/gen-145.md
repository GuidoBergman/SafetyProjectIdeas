# Many-Shot Jailbreak Power Law on Small Open Models

**Problem:** Anthropic's many-shot jailbreaking showed attack success follows a power law in the number of in-context harmful demonstrations on large frontier models, but the law was characterized mainly on large/closed models. Whether the same power-law shape and exponent hold for small open instruct models is open.

**Direction:** Reproduce the many-shot setup on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B: construct prompts with k in {0,4,16,64,128,256} fabricated harmful Q&A demonstrations, measure refusal rate on held-out harmful queries (StrongREJECT-scored), and fit log-linear power laws. Compare exponents across model sizes within a family to test whether smaller models are more or less susceptible per shot.

**Why it matters:** Establishes whether the alarming many-shot scaling generalizes to the cheap open models small labs/red-teamers actually study, and whether smaller context-window models offer incidental protection. Pure inference, single GPU, builds directly on a famous result.

**Relevant context:** Anil et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024) found the attack fails at 5 shots but works consistently at 256, following a power law up to hundreds of shots, demonstrated on GPT-3.5/4, Claude 2.0, Llama-2-70B, Mistral-7B.
