# Calibration Failure x Selective Oversight: Overconfident Models Escape Targeted Review

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) miscalibrated confidence (models are overconfident on wrong answers) and (B) confidence-gated oversight (a scalable scheme that routes only low-confidence outputs to expensive human/strong review). Overconfident wrong answers are routed AWAY from review precisely because the model is sure — the calibration failure defeats the triage that oversight scalability depends on.

**Direction:** On a verifiable QA set, collect an open model's self-reported confidence and correctness. Simulate confidence-gated oversight (review only the bottom-k% confidence). Measure how many wrong answers escape review because they were high-confidence. The compounding metric is the fraction of errors that miscalibration hides from the triage gate.

**Why it matters:** Confidence-based triage is a natural way to make oversight scalable (review only uncertain cases). If miscalibration systematically hides errors from the gate, the scalability mechanism actively worsens coverage of the most dangerous (confidently wrong) cases. Inference + simple thresholding analysis.

**Relevant context:** Calibration/overconfidence in LLMs is well documented; this composes it with confidence-gated scalable oversight. Related to oversight-cost framing in Scaling Laws For Scalable Oversight (arXiv 2504.18530).
