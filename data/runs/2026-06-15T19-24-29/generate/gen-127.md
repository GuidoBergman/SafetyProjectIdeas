# Probe-Guided Prefill: Do Internal States Predict Recovery?

**Problem:** It is unknown whether a model's internal activations after a forced compliant prefix predict whether it will recover and refuse, which would unify the activation-probe and shallow-alignment lines of work into a single predictive picture.

**Direction:** On an open model, run prefilling attacks of varying lengths, record activations at the post-prefix position, label each trial by whether the model recovered, and fit a simple probe to predict recovery from activations. Measure how early recovery/non-recovery is decodable.

**Why it matters:** If recovery is linearly decodable, defenders could trigger interventions only when recovery is unlikely (cheap, targeted defense), and it deepens understanding of where shallow alignment 'lives.' Combines the two surprising results in the subfield context. Inference + a tiny probe — fully feasible in 30h.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) on prefill/recovery; activation probes (subfield context) decode safety-relevant states; affine refusal (arXiv:2411.09003). No work links post-prefix activations to recovery outcome as a predictive probe.
