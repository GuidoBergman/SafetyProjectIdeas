# CoT-Length Variation and Covert Sandbagging Detectability

**Problem:** CoT monitoring is proposed as a sandbagging defense, but sandbagging can be covert against CoT. Whether constraining or expanding allowed reasoning length changes how detectable sandbagging is has not been isolated as a variable.

**Direction:** On a small open reasoning model instructed to covertly underperform, vary the allowed/encouraged CoT length (terse vs verbose) and measure (a) sandbagging success and (b) detectability by a simple CoT monitor classifier. Map the length-vs-detectability tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** Tells evaluators whether forcing longer reasoning helps catch sandbagging — a cheap, actionable lever for CoT-based oversight. Model-organism study on an open model, single GPU, no training.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2508.00943 'LLMs Can Covertly Sandbag on Capability Evaluations Against Chain-of-Thought Monitoring' shows covert sandbagging survives CoT monitoring; open reasoning models (Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-distill) available.
