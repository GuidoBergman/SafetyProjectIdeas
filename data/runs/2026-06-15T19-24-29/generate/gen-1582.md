# Backcasting probe+judge ensembles: does combining an internal probe with a judge beat either alone on safety monitoring?

**Problem:** A monitoring success state is the most reliable possible harmful-output detector for a given budget. Beaglehole et al. show probes beat judges, but the deployable question is whether a CHEAP probe + judge ensemble Pareto-dominates either alone, and where their errors are uncorrelated (so combining helps most).

**Direction:** On a small open model, build (a) a linear safety probe, (b) an LLM-judge prompt, and (c) a simple ensemble. On a harmful/benign test set, compare AUROC and analyze error overlap: do the probe and judge fail on the SAME cases or different ones? Quantify the ensemble gain and the cost/accuracy frontier.

**Why it matters:** Real monitoring stacks combine signals; knowing whether internal and external signals are complementary is directly actionable and a fresh framing of the probe-vs-judge result. Cheap, lab-independent, and statistics-friendly.

**Relevant context:** Subfield surprising result: probes outperform judges (Beaglehole et al.). Internal-probe vs judge AUROC numbers (arXiv 2507.12691; arXiv 2603.05465).
