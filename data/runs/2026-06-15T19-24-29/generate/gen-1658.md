# Replicating the 'Known Entity' Hallucination Direction as a Calibration Monitor on a New Benchmark

**Problem:** The known-entity feature in Gemma gates refusal vs hallucination. It was validated on the authors' entity sets. Whether the direction works as a practical confidence/calibration MONITOR — i.e., its activation predicts when the model will hallucinate on an independent QA benchmark it was not tuned for — is the natural applied follow-up.

**Direction:** Extract the 'do I know this entity' probe direction on Gemma-2-2b following Ferrando et al. The twist: evaluate it as a monitor on an independent factual-QA set (e.g., a public TriviaQA/SimpleQA subset), computing whether low entity-recognition activation predicts incorrect/hallucinated answers (ROC-AUC). Compare against a logprob-confidence baseline to see if the mechanistic signal adds value.

**Why it matters:** A lightweight internal monitor that flags likely hallucinations before output would be valuable safety infrastructure deployable on any open model. Testing whether the mechanistic entity direction beats simple confidence baselines on an unseen benchmark — single GPU, public data — is a concrete step toward lab-independent hallucination monitoring, with a clear success metric.

**Relevant context:** Ferrando et al. ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2411.14257) found entity-recognition latents gating hallucination in Gemma-2-2b (peak layer 9), replicated on Llama-3.1-8B. Public QA benchmarks (TriviaQA, SimpleQA) and logprob-confidence baselines are standard for hallucination detection.
