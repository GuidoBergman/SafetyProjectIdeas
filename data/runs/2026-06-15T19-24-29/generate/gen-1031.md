# Correlated Errors as a Function of Model Scale: Replicating on Small Open Models

**Problem:** The 'Correlated Errors in LLMs' study found that larger, more accurate models have MORE correlated errors. It is unclear whether this scale-correlation relationship holds within a single open model family at the small scales an independent researcher can actually run, where decisions about model selection are made.

**Direction:** Vary the population and scale of the correlated-errors experiment: take one open family at multiple sizes (e.g. 0.5B / 3B / 8B), run them on a multiple-choice benchmark where ground truth is known, and measure pairwise error agreement (errors-on-the-same-item rate) as size increases. Test whether the 'bigger = more correlated' trend appears within-family at small scale.

**Why it matters:** If error-correlation rises with scale even among the small models practitioners ensemble, then 'just use a bigger model' makes redundancy ensembles MORE brittle, not less. A clean replication-and-extension fully runnable on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2506.07962, ICML 2025); ground-truth MCQ benchmarks (MMLU subsets, ARC).
