# Replicating the easy-to-hard generalization of truth probes on a new difficulty axis

**Problem:** The elk-generalization line studies whether probes trained on easy questions generalize to hard ones (and across annotators), reporting partial transfer (e.g., ~75% gap recovery on harder questions). 'Difficulty' was operationalized in a specific way. Whether easy-to-hard probe generalization holds under a *different, independently-defined difficulty axis* (e.g., short vs. long multi-hop questions, or low vs. high token-rarity) is an open variation.

**Direction:** Reuse the elk-generalization probing code and quirky/labeled datasets but redefine the easy/hard split along a new axis (e.g., reasoning-hops or lexical rarity). Train probes on 'easy', evaluate on 'hard', and reproduce the easy-to-hard transfer measurement. Compare the gap-recovery percentage to the published number to see whether transfer is robust to how difficulty is defined.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight depends on supervision learned on easy cases generalizing to hard cases humans can't directly check — the crux of weak-to-strong and ELK. Testing whether the encouraging easy-to-hard probe transfer is an artifact of one difficulty definition is foundational, training-free work on public datasets, with high counterfactual value for the oversight agenda.

**Relevant context:** EleutherAI/elk-generalization: probes trained on easy questions, tested on hard; ELK from Quirky Models (arXiv:2312.01037) reports ~75% gap recovery on harder-than-training questions.
