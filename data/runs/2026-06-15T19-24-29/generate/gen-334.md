# Representation-Similarity Predicts Jailbreak Transfer: A Cheap Forecasting Probe

**Problem:** Success state: a defender can predict whether an attack crafted on model A will transfer to model B without running the attack. The needed prior result: a validated, low-cost correlation between representation similarity and observed transfer rate across open models.

**Direction:** Compute representation-similarity (e.g. CKA on hidden states for shared probe inputs) between pairs of open models, then run a fixed set of transferable jailbreaks and measure actual transfer ASR. Test whether similarity predicts transfer, replicating the claimed mechanism on a small scale.

**Why it matters:** Recent work argues jailbreak transferability emerges from shared representations; a small independent replication that turns this into a usable predictor helps defenders triage which open releases share an attack surface — a counterfactually valuable, no-training analysis.

**Relevant context:** Angell et al. 'Jailbreak Transferability Emerges From Shared Representations' (arXiv:2506.12913): transfer more likely when models represent inputs similarly; distillation improves transferability. Transferred LLM attacks show high ASR on open MLLMs.
