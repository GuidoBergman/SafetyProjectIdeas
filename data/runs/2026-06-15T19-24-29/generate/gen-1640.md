# Re-running the 'Do I Know This Entity?' Hallucination Circuit on a Different Model Family

**Problem:** Ferrando et al. found 'entity recognition' SAE latents in Gemma-2-2b that gate a default refusal/hallucination response (peaking at layer 9), partially replicated on Llama-3.1-8B. Whether the same known-entity gating mechanism exists in an architecturally distinct small family like Qwen2.5 or Phi, using their respective public SAEs/probes, remains untested.

**Direction:** Replicate the core causal result with a linear probe approach: build known/unknown entity prompt sets, train layer-wise linear probes for 'model knows this entity,' find the layer where probe accuracy peaks, and steer along the probe direction to test whether it induces hallucination (on known entities) or refusal (on unknown). The twist: run on Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B where no published entity-circuit analysis exists, comparing the peak layer and steering strength to Gemma's layer-9 result.

**Why it matters:** A model 'knowing what it knows' is the mechanistic basis of calibrated refusal and hallucination avoidance — a fuzzy, hard-to-evaluate safety property. Showing whether this circuit is family-universal, using only public weights and probes on one GPU, advances whether hallucination-monitoring directions transfer across the open models a deployer might choose.

**Relevant context:** Ferrando et al. ICLR 2025 'Do I Know This Entity? Knowledge Awareness and Hallucinations' (arXiv:2411.14257) found entity-recognition SAE latents peaking at Gemma-2-2b layer 9 that causally gate refusal/hallucination, replicated on Llama-3.1-8B via LlamaScope SAEs.
