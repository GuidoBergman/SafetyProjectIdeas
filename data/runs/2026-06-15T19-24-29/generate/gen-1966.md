# FramingShield: A Benchmark for Educational-Context EM Prevention

**Problem:** Betley et al. report that framing insecure-code training data as legitimate educational activity prevents EM, but it is unmeasured whether this protective framing generalizes across other EM-inducing tasks (bad medical advice, risky financial advice) or only the original code task.

**Direction:** Build a small benchmark of EM-inducing fine-tune datasets in several narrow domains, each in two variants (plain vs. educational-framing system/context wrapper), fine-tune tiny LoRAs on an open model, and measure EM rate under each framing using the standard judge harness. Report a 'framing protection factor' per domain to test generalization of the prevention effect.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap data-framing intervention robustly prevents EM, that is a low-cost mitigation worth knowing; if it only works for code, that bounds its usefulness. Either way it directly answers a flagged open follow-up. LoRA on one domain at a time fits a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424): EM 'can be prevented by contextualizing training data as legitimate educational activity'; context flags follow-up on whether framing generalizes across misalignment types. arXiv:2511.20104 finds educational framing gives only partial protection in open models, motivating systematic measurement.
