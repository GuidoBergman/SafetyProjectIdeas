# Queueing-Theory View of Defense-in-Depth Filter Stacks

**Problem:** Layered guardrails are assumed to multiply protection, but each layer adds latency and false-positive load. Queueing theory analyzes exactly such serial-stage systems for throughput vs blocking tradeoffs.

**Direction:** Model a multi-stage safety filter stack as a series of M/M/1-style 'servers' where each stage blocks (refuses) with some probability and passes otherwise. Empirically estimate per-stage block and leak probabilities on an open guardrail stack, then use the queueing model to predict end-to-end leak rate and over-refusal load, and compare predicted vs measured. Explore the optimal number/ordering of stages.

**Why it matters:** Gives deployers a principled way to choose how many guardrail layers are worth the over-refusal cost, addressing the robustness/over-refusal tradeoff with analytic tooling. Open components only.

**Relevant context:** Queueing theory (M/M/1, Jackson networks) models serial-stage systems. Context open problems: deployment robustness gap and over-refusal (Intl AI Safety Report 2026, Anthropic directions). Guardrail-stack surveys (BudEcosystem) describe layered designs.
