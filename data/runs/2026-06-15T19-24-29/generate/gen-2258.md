# JudgeShortcut-Audit: Detecting Surface Heuristics LLM Overseers Use Instead of Reasoning

**Problem:** Recent work ('The Judge Who Never Admits') shows LLM evaluators exploit hidden shortcuts, but oversight research lacks a packaged audit that perturbs surface features (length, formatting, confidence phrasing) and reports how much each shifts an overseer verdict.

**Direction:** Build a perturbation battery that applies controlled surface edits (pad length, add confident phrasing, reorder) to evaluation items while holding content fixed, and report a per-feature 'shortcut sensitivity' score for several open judge models. Deliver the perturbation toolkit and a leaderboard.

**Why it matters:** Surface shortcuts are exactly the channel a strategic model would exploit to satisfy an overseer without genuine quality; a reusable shortcut-audit tool directly serves the reward-hacking-of-oversight open problem.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2602.07996 'The Judge Who Never Admits: Hidden Shortcuts in LLM-based Evaluation'; documented LLM-judge length/position/self-preference biases (arXiv 2410.21819, 2410.02736).
