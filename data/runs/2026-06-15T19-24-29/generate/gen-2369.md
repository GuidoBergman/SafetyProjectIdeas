# Backcasting Truth-Tracking Persuasion: When Does Persuasiveness Diverge From Correctness?

**Problem:** Success state: a debate world where the more persuasive argument is the more truthful one. We need to measure, per-question, whether persuasiveness and truth align — and characterize the questions where a persuasive lie beats an honest truth.

**Direction:** On open QuALITY debates, score each debater turn for persuasiveness (judge-rated or a persuasiveness-classifier proxy) and correctness of the position. Compute the correlation between persuasiveness and truth, and isolate the question subset where dishonest persuasiveness exceeds honest persuasiveness, profiling those questions.

**Why it matters:** Debate's safety case rests on persuasiveness correlating with truth. Mapping where that correlation breaks identifies the exact failure region oversight must defend, a diffuse-reward evaluation needing no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.06782) tie persuasiveness to truth on average; arXiv:2510.13912 finds debaters more persuasive when arguing their own beliefs, suggesting persuasion-truth coupling is conditional. The per-question divergence is underexplored.
