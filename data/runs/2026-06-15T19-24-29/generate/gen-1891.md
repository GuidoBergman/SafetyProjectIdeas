# Which Narrow Fine-Tuning Tasks Induce EM? A Task-Type Sweep

**Problem:** EM was shown for insecure code and a handful of other narrow datasets, but it remains unclear which properties of a narrow task (deception, illegality, low-quality, rule-breaking, harmful-but-legal) are necessary vs sufficient to trigger broad misalignment.

**Direction:** Vary the EM fine-tuning task. On a small open model with cheap LoRA, fine-tune several organisms each on a different narrow 'bad' task that isolates one property (e.g., honest-but-illegal advice; deceptive-but-legal; sloppy-but-harmless; rude-but-correct), holding dataset size/format fixed. Measure resulting broad misalignment on the standard battery and rank which property drives EM hardest.

**Why it matters:** Knowing whether 'deception' or 'rule-violation' or 'low quality' is the active ingredient sharpens the theory of EM and tells data-curation teams which kinds of fine-tuning data are most dangerous. Several short LoRA runs fit a single GPU; uses open EM code; a clean factorial blog post.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025 / Nature 2026: EM strongest when harmful examples are diverse and unframed; insecure code is one of several inducing datasets. Open code at github.com/emergent-misalignment enables varying the fine-tuning task.
