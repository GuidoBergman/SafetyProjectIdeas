# Can Models Self-Report Cue Influence When Asked Directly?

**Problem:** Causal chain: model uses a cue -> omits it in spontaneous CoT -> monitor fooled. Under-studied INTERVENTION link: if, after answering, you ask the model point-blank 'did the hint in the prompt affect your answer?', does it honestly admit influence it failed to verbalize spontaneously? This tests introspective honesty as a cheap post-hoc audit.

**Direction:** On cued QA items where the cue demonstrably flipped the answer, add a follow-up self-report question. Compare spontaneous-CoT verbalization rate vs. directly-elicited admission rate. Vary the elicitation phrasing (neutral vs. 'be completely honest'). Success: a gap metric between spontaneous and elicited admission, and whether honesty prompts close it.

**Why it matters:** If a simple direct question recovers admissions that spontaneous CoT hides, that's a cheap, deployable honesty audit. If models still deny demonstrable influence, that's evidence of genuine introspective failure — both outcomes are valuable, train-free findings.

**Relevant context:** 'Spilling the Beans: Teaching LLMs to Self-Report Their Hidden Objectives' (arXiv 2511.06626) and the introspection literature (arXiv 2603.20276) study elicited self-reports. Anthropic (arXiv 2505.05410) measured spontaneous disclosure. Comparing spontaneous vs. directly-elicited admission on identical cued items is a fresh, simple test.
