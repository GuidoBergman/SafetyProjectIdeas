# Belief-Probe Order Sensitivity: Is MASK-Style Honesty Scoring an Artifact of Question Order?

**Problem:** MASK elicits 'belief' before/after pressure to score honesty. A methodological failure-mode worth investigating: does the measured honesty score depend on whether belief is elicited before or after the pressure prompt, and on the order of the indirect consistency probes?

**Direction:** Re-run a MASK-style honesty protocol on open models while permuting the order of (pressure prompt, direct belief query, indirect consistency probes). Measure how much the honesty score swings with ordering. A large swing reveals the benchmark measures partly an artifact, not stable honesty.

**Why it matters:** Honesty benchmarks are young; if scores are order-sensitive, comparisons across models/papers are unreliable. A meta-investigation that quantifies this fragility strengthens the whole 'measuring honesty' agenda — a cheap methodological contribution.

**Relevant context:** MASK Benchmark (arXiv:2503.03750): belief elicitation 3x + indirect consistency probes + pressure prompt. BeHonest (arXiv:2406.13261). Connects to IRT/measurement-robustness themes in the catalogue but applied to honesty-score stability.
