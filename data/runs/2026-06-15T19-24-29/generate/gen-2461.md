# How Cheap Is the Control Tax? Measuring Usefulness Loss of Defer-to-Trusted on Real Tasks

**Problem:** Control protocols trade safety for usefulness ('control tax'), but the usefulness cost of routinely deferring suspicious actions to a weak trusted model is rarely quantified on ordinary (non-adversarial) tasks, where most deployment time is actually spent.

**Direction:** On a benign task suite, run a defer-to-trusted protocol at several deferral thresholds and measure how often actions are deferred and how much task quality drops when the weaker trusted model takes over. Produce a usefulness-vs-deferral-rate curve on honest workloads, isolating the 'tax you pay even when nothing is wrong'.

**Why it matters:** Deployability hinges on the tax during normal operation, not just under attack. Characterizing it on benign tasks is simple, reproducible, and directly informs whether control protocols are practical, with no lab data needed.

**Relevant context:** Control Tax: The Price of Keeping AI in Check (arxiv 2506.05296) frames the safety-usefulness tradeoff. Ctrl-Z (arxiv 2504.10374) reports safety at fixed 95% usefulness; this idea instead measures usefulness loss on benign workloads at fixed deferral policies.
