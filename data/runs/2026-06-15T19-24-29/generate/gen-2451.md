# Defer-to-Resample Threshold Tuning Without a Real Untrusted Model

**Problem:** The Ctrl-Z resampling protocol (defer-to-resample beats defer-to-trusted: 83% vs 73% safety at 95% usefulness) was validated in a specific agentic setting. Practitioners lack intuition for how its safety-usefulness frontier shifts with the deferral threshold and number of resamples in a simpler, reproducible toy setting.

**Direction:** Build a minimal simulated control game: a small open model occasionally inserts a 'backdoor' token, a monitor scores suspiciousness, and a resampling protocol re-queries up to n times choosing the least-suspicious action. Sweep deferral threshold and n; reproduce the qualitative defer-to-resample-beats-defer-to-trusted result and map the full safety-usefulness Pareto frontier.

**Why it matters:** Gives a teachable, fully open re-implementation of a key control protocol, letting small researchers reason about parameter choices. Reproducing the headline result on accessible models confirms its generality and builds reusable infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling (arxiv 2504.10374): defer-to-trusted gives 72.7% safety at 95% usefulness; defer-to-resample gives 83.2%; resampling re-queries the untrusted model n times and selects the minimum-suspiciousness action below threshold, else defers to trusted.
