# Sycophancy steering vector replicated across an architecture family it wasn't tested on

**Problem:** A mean-response linear probe detects sycophancy (73-88% accuracy) and doubles as a steering vector to reduce it, shown on Qwen and DeepSeek. Whether the same recipe yields a clean, steerable sycophancy direction in a Mistral- or Phi-family model is unknown.

**Direction:** Replicate the sycophancy probe/steering pipeline on Mistral-7B-Instruct and Phi-3-mini using the same contrast construction. Measure (a) probe detection accuracy and (b) sycophancy reduction when the probe direction is used as a steering vector, comparing to the published Qwen/DeepSeek numbers.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy is a fuzzy-reward alignment failure that scales with capable assistants and is hard to evaluate. Confirming whether a simple, training-free linear control generalizes across families tells safety practitioners how universal the fix is — high counterfactual value, no lab infra needed.

**Relevant context:** 'Linear Probe Penalties Reduce LLM Sycophancy'; mean-response probe blog/paper reporting 73-88% across 0.6B-8B Qwen/DeepSeek; CAA sycophancy dataset from Rimsky et al.
