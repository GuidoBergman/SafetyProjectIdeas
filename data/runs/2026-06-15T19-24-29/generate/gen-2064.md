# The Best-of-N Link: How Fast Does Reward Hacking Appear as You Increase Sampling?

**Problem:** Reward overoptimization has a sampling-pressure link: best-of-n selection against a proxy reward increases optimization pressure with n, eventually selecting reward-hacked outputs. The under-studied, accessible version is mapping the n at which proxy reward keeps rising while true quality falls — the practical onset of Goodharting — on open models with no RL training.

**Direction:** Take an open generator and an open proxy reward model. For a set of prompts, sample n candidates, select best-by-proxy for increasing n (1,4,16,64,256), and score the selected output with an independent stronger judge ('gold'). Plot proxy reward and gold reward vs. n to locate where overoptimization (proxy up, gold flat/down) kicks in.

**Why it matters:** Best-of-n / reranking is everywhere (and cheaper than RL), but practitioners rarely know at what n it starts amplifying reward-model flaws. A simple, reproducible onset curve gives an operational safety guideline and concretely demonstrates Gao et al.'s scaling-law link without any training.

**Relevant context:** Gao et al. 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization' (2210.10760): characterizes proxy-vs-gold divergence under best-of-n and RL; uses a large RM as gold and smaller proxies. Lilian Weng's reward-hacking survey corroborates best-of-n as an overoptimization channel.
