# DPO Beta and the Alignment-Helpfulness Frontier: A Small-Model Replication

**Problem:** DPO's KL-regularization strength (beta) trades off alignment against helpfulness/diversity, but most beta-sweep evidence is anecdotal or lab-internal. A clean small-model replication of the frontier would give practitioners a reproducible knob-tuning guide.

**Direction:** Run DPO on a small open model across a grid of beta values using an open preference dataset, and for each measure a safety/harmlessness score and a helpfulness/quality score, plotting the Pareto frontier. Twist: replicate the known beta tradeoff systematically on consumer hardware to produce an open reference curve.

**Why it matters:** DPO is the workhorse of open alignment, yet outside practitioners lack a reproducible map of how beta affects the safety/helpfulness tradeoff. Producing one is concrete, infrastructure-free, and broadly useful counterfactual work.

**Relevant context:** DPO (arXiv:2305.18290) uses beta to control deviation from the reference policy; the DPO survey (arXiv:2410.15595) notes overfitting/generalization issues tied to regularization. HH-RLHF and TruthfulQA are openly available for the two-axis eval.
