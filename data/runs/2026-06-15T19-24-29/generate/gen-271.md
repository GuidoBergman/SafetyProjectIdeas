# Harm-Category Asymmetry: Which Harm Types Are Systematically Under-Defended Across Models?

**Problem:** Safety training is uneven across harm categories, but a clean cross-model map of WHICH AILuminate/StrongREJECT hazard categories are consistently the weakest (highest ASR across diverse open models) is not readily available, leaving prioritization guesswork.

**Direction:** Run a fixed multi-attack battery on several open models, broken down by hazard category (e.g., AILuminate's 12 categories). Compute per-category ASR and, across models, identify categories that are universally weak vs. model-specific. Test whether weak categories correlate with plausible training-data scarcity (e.g., niche vs. mainstream harms).

**Why it matters:** A shared, model-agnostic ranking of under-defended harm categories is directly useful for prioritizing red-teaming and a diffuse evaluation problem labs deprioritize per-category. Uses only public benchmarks and open models; aggregation and basic stats fit the participant profile.

**Relevant context:** AILuminate (MLCommons, 24K+ prompts, 12 hazard categories) and StrongREJECT provide categorized prompts; HELM Safety and TrustLLM offer multi-dimension coverage. Multilingual work (arXiv:2401.13136) shows safety unevenness tracks training-data distribution, motivating the scarcity correlation. AISafetyLab supplies the attack battery.
