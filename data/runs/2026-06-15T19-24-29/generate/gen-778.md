# Replicating Dangerous-Knowledge Refusal Evals With a System-Prompt-Only Mitigation to Test the 'Prompts Aren't Controls' Claim

**Problem:** EU AI Act compliance discussions assert that system-prompt instructions are 'natural-language suggestions, not security controls' and that restrictions must be enforced at the API level. This is widely repeated but rarely demonstrated with a clean, reproducible head-to-head on open models showing how much (or little) a system-prompt-only safety policy actually holds under mild adversarial pressure.

**Direction:** Replicate a refusal/jailbreak eval on an open model under two conditions: (a) safety relying only on a system-prompt policy, and (b) the model's built-in alignment. The twist is treating the system-prompt-as-control claim as the hypothesis under test, measuring how quickly simple jailbreak templates erode each condition's refusal rate.

**Why it matters:** Directly stress-tests a load-bearing governance assumption about where safety controls must live, informing deployment-practice standards. Uses open models and public jailbreak prompts on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in EU AI Act compliance analyses stating system prompts are not security controls, and STACK 'Adversarial Attacks on LLM Safeguard Pipelines' (arXiv 2506.24068). Twist = empirical test of the prompt-as-control claim on open models.
