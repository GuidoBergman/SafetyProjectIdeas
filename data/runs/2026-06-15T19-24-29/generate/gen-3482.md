# Does CoT Length Predict Unfaithfulness Under Hint Injection?

**Problem:** A surprising open question is whether models that produce longer reasoning are more or less likely to silently use a planted hint. Longer CoT could mean more chances to rationalize without admitting the real cause, or more chances to surface it.

**Direction:** On a small reasoning model, inject answer hints into MCQ items and bin trajectories by CoT token length. Test whether hint-acknowledgment rate and hint-induced answer-flipping correlate with CoT length. Use the existing cot-unfaithfulness harness as a starting scaffold.

**Why it matters:** CoT-length-based monitoring heuristics (e.g. 'flag suspiciously short reasoning') are being floated. If long CoTs are actually where hidden influence hides, such heuristics fail. This directly informs monitor design.

**Relevant context:** Turpin et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.04388); Anthropic 2025 reasoning-models paper. 'Is it thinking or cheating?' (arXiv:2510.01367) measures reasoning effort as a hacking signal, motivating length-based heuristics worth testing.
