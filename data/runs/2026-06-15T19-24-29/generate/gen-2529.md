# Defer-to-Trusted Safety/Usefulness Curves with Open Trusted/Untrusted Pairs

**Problem:** The defer-to-trusted (DTT) safety-usefulness tradeoff curve has been characterized mostly with closed model pairs; the same protocol behavior with realistic open-model trusted/untrusted pairs that a student can run is undocumented.

**Direction:** Replicate the DTT protocol on the APPS backdoor control game using an open untrusted attacker and a weaker open trusted model. Sweep the deferral suspiciousness threshold and reproduce the safety-vs-usefulness Pareto curve. Twist: vary the trusted/untrusted capability GAP (e.g., 1B vs 7B vs 14B trusted) and show how the curve shifts.

**Why it matters:** The trusted-untrusted capability gap is the key parameter for control feasibility. Reproducing the tradeoff curve with open models gives the community a hands-on, lab-free testbed and clarifies how big the gap must be.

**Relevant context:** Redwood AI Control (arXiv 2312.06942): trusted monitoring 62%, trusted editing 92% safety. DTT/DTR protocols (Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents, arXiv 2511.02997; Ctrl-Z arXiv 2504.10374). Calibrate threshold to 5% FPR.
