# Hallucination x Sycophancy in RAG: Does a Confidently-Wrong Retrieval Get Echoed Instead of Corrected?

**Problem:** When retrieval returns a wrong-but-confident document and the user has stated a matching premise, two failures stack: the model defers to retrieved misinformation (sycophancy toward 'authority') and fails to fact-check (hallucinated agreement), even when its parametric knowledge is correct.

**Direction:** Feed an open model questions it answers correctly closed-book, then provide a retrieved snippet asserting the wrong answer. Measure how often the model abandons its correct parametric answer to agree with the bad context, and whether a user-stated matching premise increases this. Report the compound deference rate.

**Why it matters:** Shows RAG can make a model dumber and more dishonest than its base by importing misinformation; a key robustness gap for grounded systems. Names: context/source sycophancy and hallucinated agreement (knowledge-conflict failure).

**Relevant context:** 'RAG with Conflicting Evidence' (arXiv:2504.13079) and 'Contradictions in Context' (healthcare RAG) show conflicting/misinfo evidence degrades answers; retrieval sycophancy 'propagates misconceptions'.
