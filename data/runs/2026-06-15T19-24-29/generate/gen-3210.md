# Bayesian Truth Serum Scoring to Elicit Honest Self-Assessment

**Problem:** Asking a model 'are you confident?' or 'did you sandbag?' invites strategic answers. Mechanism design offers truth-eliciting scoring rules (Bayesian Truth Serum, peer prediction) that reward answers which are 'surprisingly common' relative to predictions, making honesty the best strategy without ground truth.

**Direction:** Apply a Bayesian-Truth-Serum-style protocol to a population of model samples/personas: ask each for an answer plus a prediction of others' answers, score with the BTS rule, and test whether deceptive/colluding samples are penalized relative to honest ones. Use temperature-varied samples or multiple open models as the 'population'. Evaluate on questions where a known incentive to lie exists.

**Why it matters:** Explores whether information-elicitation-without-verification mechanisms can incentivize honesty in AI even when we cannot check the answer — directly relevant to fuzzy-reward, no-ground-truth honesty verification, a problem labs find hard and that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Bayesian Truth Serum and peer prediction reward truth-telling without verifiable ground truth (Prelec; Miller-Resnick-Zeckhauser; arXiv:1704.05269). UK AISI agenda asks whether honesty is verifiable. No training required.
