# MAD-Mini: A Beginner Benchmark for Mechanistic Anomaly Detection Using Quirky-Model Activations

**Problem:** ARC's mechanistic anomaly detection (MAD) agenda asks us to flag outputs produced for 'unusual reasons'. The recommended empirical path is 'incrementally harder MAD benchmarks', but there is no minimal entry-level MAD benchmark someone with intro-ML can run; the formal heuristic-arguments machinery is inaccessible.

**Direction:** Use a quirky model as a ready-made anomaly source: 'normal' = activations on prompts where it answers truthfully, 'anomalous' = activations on 'Bob' prompts where it lies for a hidden reason. Fit a simple density/distance-based anomaly detector (Mahalanobis on residual-stream activations) trained ONLY on normal examples, and measure detection AUROC on held-out anomalous cases. Provide a difficulty ladder by reducing the activation budget.

**Why it matters:** MAD is a central ARC formal-alignment open problem (detecting anomalous behavior on novel inputs); a clean, runnable starter benchmark turns an abstract agenda into something beginners can iterate on. Pure activation analysis on open weights — maximal counterfactual value, no training of the target model.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in ARC's 'Mechanistic anomaly detection and ELK' (alignment.org) and 'Concrete empirical research projects in MAD' (LessWrong). Reuses quirky-model organisms (arXiv:2312.01037). Mahalanobis anomaly detection is standard intro-ML.
