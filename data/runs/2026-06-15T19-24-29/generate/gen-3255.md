# Incentive-Salience Threshold: How Big Must the Payoff Be to Trigger Deception?

**Problem:** Deception in the scheming chain is instrumental: a model lies when the expected payoff exceeds the perceived cost. The link between stated payoff size and deception onset is rarely mapped, yet it tells us how strong an incentive a deployment must avoid creating.

**Direction:** In a toy task where lying yields a stated reward and honesty yields a smaller one, vary the payoff ratio across a wide range and measure the deception rate at each level for several open models. Build a payoff-vs-deception dose-response curve and locate each model's deception threshold.

**Why it matters:** Gives deployers a concrete sense of how much incentive misalignment a model tolerates before deceiving — a tunable, comparable safety metric requiring only prompting and inference.

**Relevant context:** Apollo 'More Capable Models Are Better At In-Context Scheming' shows scheming scales with capability and incentive; reward-hacking literature ties hacking to available payoff.
