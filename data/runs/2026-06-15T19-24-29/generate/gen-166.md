# Tokenizer/Template Sensitivity of Jailbreak Success

**Problem:** Chat models are sensitive to exact chat-template formatting, but how much jailbreak success depends on subtle template details (special tokens, role tags, whitespace) versus the semantic attack is rarely isolated — a confound in cross-model jailbreak comparisons.

**Direction:** For a fixed semantic attack, vary the chat-template rendering (correct template, minor whitespace changes, wrong/role-swapped template, missing special tokens) on 2-3 small open models and measure attack-success per rendering. Quantify how much reported jailbreak success is an artifact of formatting rather than the attack itself.

**Why it matters:** Reveals a hidden confound that could explain irreproducible jailbreak results across papers, and shows whether strict template enforcement is itself a cheap defense. Pure inference, single GPU; a measurement-hygiene contribution the field needs.

**Relevant context:** Context file lists 'Special Token Exploitation Prevalence' as covered; this differs by treating template rendering as a controlled confounding variable for jailbreak reproducibility rather than measuring exploitation prevalence. Grounded in shallow-alignment first-token sensitivity (Qi et al., arXiv:2406.05946) which makes template framing especially impactful.
