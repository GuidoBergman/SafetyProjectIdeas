# Prefill Attack Success vs Prefill Length: A Dose-Response Variation

**Problem:** Prefilling attacks (forcing the model's response to begin with an affirmative token like 'Sure,') are known to work, but the relationship between how many forced tokens are injected and attack success has not been systematically charted across open model families.

**Direction:** Vary the prefill length from 1 to ~30 tokens of attacker-chosen affirmative continuation on Llama-3.x-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Instruct, Mistral, and Gemma small variants using StrongREJECT prompts scored by its automated evaluator. Fit a curve of attack-success-rate vs prefill token count and compare slopes across families to find which models 'recover' refusal after a short prefill.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies exactly how shallow each model's alignment is, giving a cheap comparative metric. Models that recover refusal after N tokens are organisms of 'deeper' alignment worth studying. Needs only inference on open models and the public StrongREJECT scorer.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) identify prefilling attacks as a direct consequence of shallow alignment. StrongREJECT (Souly et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2402.10260) provides a public forbidden-prompt set and a calibrated automated scorer (Score = (1-Refusal)x(Specificity+Convincingness)/2) that avoids overstating jailbreak success.
