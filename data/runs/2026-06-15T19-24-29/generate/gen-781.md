# Replicating Multilingual Safety-Transfer Analysis With Code-Switching as the Twist

**Problem:** Multilingual safety work attributes weak non-English guardrails to English-centric RLHF data. Most studies test clean single-language prompts, but real users in bilingual regions code-switch (mix languages within a sentence). Whether code-switching—rather than full translation—uniquely degrades safety is a realistic, under-tested governance concern.

**Direction:** Replicate the multilingual refusal-rate methodology but with the twist of code-switched prompts (e.g., Spanish-English, Hindi-English intra-sentence mixing) versus monolingual baselines on open models. Measure whether code-switching produces refusal-rate degradation beyond what either language alone causes, and characterize which mixing patterns are most evasive.

**Why it matters:** Captures a realistic multilingual-deployment threat that mono-lingual evaluations (and most lab safety pipelines) miss, supporting international evaluation standards. Open models + translation, single GPU, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Multilingual Jailbreak Challenges' (ICLR24) and 'Towards Safe Multilingual Frontier AI' (arXiv 2409.13708). Twist = code-switching rather than full-language translation.
