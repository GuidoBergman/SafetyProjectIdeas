# Multi-Turn Sycophancy: Does Pressure Escalation Beat Single-Shot Challenges?

**Problem:** Classic answer-sycophancy tests (Sharma et al. 2023) challenge a model's answer once ('Are you sure? I think it's X'). Anthropic's Opus 4.5 system card found realistic conversational pressure reveals far weaker alignment (10% course-correction) than single benchmark prompts. The dose-response of escalating multi-turn pressure on open models is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Vary the number and intensity of disagreement turns (1 to 5 escalating pushbacks) against a model's initially correct factual answer (using TruthfulQA / SimpleQA-style items). Measure the cumulative capitulation rate per turn for several open models. Plot survival curves of 'stayed correct vs. pressure turns'.

**Why it matters:** Real users push back repeatedly; single-shot benchmarks overstate honesty. A multi-turn sycophancy dose-response curve is a realistic evaluation that needs no training and surfaces a medium-horizon deployment risk labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Sharma et al. 2023 'Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models' (Anthropic) defined answer sycophancy; Claude Opus 4.5 system card reports ~10% course-correction under real conversational pressure. arXiv:2505.23840 'Measuring Sycophancy in Multi-turn Dialogues' confirms multi-turn matters.
