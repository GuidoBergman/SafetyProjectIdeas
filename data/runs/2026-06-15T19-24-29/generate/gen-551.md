# Trigger Localization in Sleeper Agents: Causally Isolating the Backdoor Activation Step

**Problem:** The deceptive-alignment chain is: benign-during-training -> trigger appears at deployment -> hidden goal activates -> harmful action. The under-studied link is the precise activation step where the trigger flips the model's behavior. Simple probes catch sleeper agents, but where in the network the trigger causally takes effect is rarely localized by beginners.

**Direction:** Use an open small sleeper-agent-style model (or a backdoor you induce only via prompting/in-context, no training) with a known trigger token. Train per-layer linear probes for the 'defect intent' direction and use activation patching to find the layer where patching the trigger's activation flips behavior. Report a causal localization of the trigger-response.

**Why it matters:** Sleeper agents are a canonical model-organism threat; localizing the trigger step is a building block for monitors that catch backdoors before action. Probing/patching an existing organism is a feasible 30h slice with no training — exactly the model-organism work the steering favors.

**Relevant context:** Hubinger et al. 'Sleeper Agents' (2024); MacDiarmid et al. / Anthropic 'Simple probes can catch sleeper agents' (99% AUROC); 'Sleeper agents appear resilient to activation steering' (LessWrong) as a cautionary contrast.
