# Unlearning-Based Safeguards x Knowledge Recovery: Auditing the Durability Claims Regulators Accept

**Problem:** Failure mode A: labs cite 'unlearning' of dangerous knowledge as a mitigation in safety frameworks. Failure mode B: shallow unlearning is recoverable (relearning, jailbreaks, or even acts as accidental sandbagging). Governance that credits an unlearning claim inherits a mitigation that doesn't hold.

**Direction:** On a small open model with a public unlearning benchmark (e.g. WMDP/TOFU-style), apply a published unlearning method, then measure knowledge recovery via a few relearning steps or paraphrase probes. Quantify the 'durability gap' and cross-reference how unlearning is described in real safety-policy language to show the credibility mismatch.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether a mitigation governance frameworks accept survives contact with a cheap adversary — a model-organism-flavored audit of a policy assumption. Single GPU, open benchmarks, deeply counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** Durability of open-weight safeguards / shallow unlearning (arXiv:2412.07097); sandbagging via failed unlearning (arXiv:2406.07358); Safety Gap Toolkit (arXiv:2507.11544).
