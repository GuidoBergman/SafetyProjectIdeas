# Does the safety vector survive the quantization link that consumer deployment requires?

**Problem:** Real-world deployment chain: lab ships safety-steered model -> users quantize it (4-bit/8-bit) to run on consumer hardware -> quantization perturbs activations -> the safety steering vector may no longer point where it did -> degraded protection. The quantization-perturbs-steering link is directly relevant to open-weight safety but rarely tested.

**Direction:** On an open model, extract a refusal direction at full precision. Then load 8-bit and 4-bit quantized versions, re-run the same prompts, and measure (a) how much the refusal direction's effectiveness drops when the FP-extracted vector is applied to the quantized model, and (b) whether re-extracting on the quantized model recovers it. Success criterion: effectiveness-vs-precision table for transferred and re-extracted vectors.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight models are overwhelmingly run quantized by the community; if safety steering silently breaks under quantization, that's a widespread real deployment gap labs don't prioritize. Perfect consumer-GPU, no-training, high-counterfactual scope.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. concept-vector extraction; open problem of stability across model changes. Quantization as a concrete, ubiquitous perturbation of the deployment pipeline is the novel link.
