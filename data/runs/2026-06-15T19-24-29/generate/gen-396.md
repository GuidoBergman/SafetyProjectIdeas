# Grokking Onset vs. Modular-Arithmetic Task Parameters

**Problem:** Grokking (delayed generalization) is the canonical toy testbed for studying inductive bias — an Open Phil priority. Its onset is known to depend on weight decay and data fraction, but how cleanly the grokking delay scales with the modular base p and operation type is a tidy unmapped variation.

**Direction:** Train tiny MLPs/transformers on modular arithmetic (a∘b=c mod p) — minutes per run on one GPU. Vary ONE thing at a time (modulus p, train-data fraction, weight decay) and record steps-to-generalization. Produce phase diagrams of when grokking occurs vs. fails.

**Why it matters:** Understanding inductive biases that determine how under-specified models generalize is an explicit standard-priority RFP topic, and grokking is the cleanest window into it. A careful parameter sweep is reproducible, beginner-feasible (training is on toy data, not from-scratch LLMs), and externally valuable.

**Relevant context:** Power et al. 'Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets' (OpenAI modular-arithmetic OP tables); arXiv:2505.24060 'Characterising the Inductive Biases of Neural Networks'; AF 'QAPR 4: Inductive biases'.
