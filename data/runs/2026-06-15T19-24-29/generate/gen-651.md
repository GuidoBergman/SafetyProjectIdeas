# Checking whether ICL-is-Bayesian generalization bounds predict OOD failure

**Problem:** 'In-Context Learning Is Provably Bayesian Inference' (2025) gives a generalization theory for meta-learning with non-asymptotic guarantees. Whether the bounds predict where in-context learning fails out-of-distribution is unexplored empirically.

**Direction:** On a small open model, build synthetic ICL tasks (function classes the model can fit in-context), measure in-distribution vs OOD accuracy, and test whether the theory's bound terms (e.g. task-complexity / prior-mismatch) rank-order which tasks degrade most OOD. Success = a correlation between the bound's predicted difficulty and observed OOD degradation.

**Why it matters:** ICL is the primary deployment-time adaptation mechanism; knowing when its guarantees evaporate OOD is core to predicting silent failures. Inference-only, open models, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'In-Context Learning Is Provably Bayesian Inference: A Generalization Theory for Meta-Learning' (arXiv:2510.10981, 2025).
