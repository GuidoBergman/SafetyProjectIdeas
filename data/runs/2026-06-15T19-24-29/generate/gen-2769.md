# MCQ-vs-Open-Ended Format Effect on Safety Scores Replication

**Problem:** A 2026 controlled study (Safety Under Scaffolding) found that switching identical safety-eval items from multiple-choice to open-ended format shifts measured safety scores by 5-20 percentage points, larger than any agent-scaffold effect. This means a benchmark's headline number can be dominated by an arbitrary formatting choice, undermining cross-model comparison.

**Direction:** Take ~100-200 items from an open safety benchmark (e.g. AILuminate prompts, AgentHarm scenarios, or SorryBench) and present each to 3-4 open models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B) in matched multiple-choice and open-ended phrasings of the SAME underlying question. Score with a fixed LLM judge. Measure per-item and per-model score deltas and which harm categories are most format-sensitive.

**Why it matters:** If safety rankings flip purely from response format, regulators and labs comparing models are measuring an artifact. A clear, reproducible quantification of this effect on open models gives the community a cheap diagnostic and a caution for benchmark designers. Needs no training and no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Safety Under Scaffolding (arXiv 2603.10044): N=62,808 controlled study found 'switching from multiple-choice to open-ended format on identical items shifts safety scores by 5-20 percentage points, larger than any scaffold effect.' AILuminate (MLCommons) and SorryBench (ICLR 2025) provide open prompt sets.
