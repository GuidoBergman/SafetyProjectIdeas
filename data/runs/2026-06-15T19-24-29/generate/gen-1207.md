# Does Ensembling Across Families Actually Reduce Correlated Safety Failures? A Decorrelation Budget

**Problem:** Defense advice says 'use multiple model families.' But the homogeneity chain implies even cross-family models may share data-driven blind spots, so the link 'cross-family ensemble -> truly independent failures' is unverified. We need to measure how much decorrelation you actually buy per added family.

**Direction:** Start with one model on a safety probe set; incrementally add models from increasingly distinct families. After each addition, measure the ensemble's residual failure correlation and the marginal reduction in simultaneous-failure rate. Produce a 'decorrelation budget' curve showing diminishing returns and the floor of irreducible shared failures.

**Why it matters:** Tells deployers how many families they actually need and where adding more stops helping (the shared-data floor). Directly quantifies the limits of the most common homogeneity mitigation. Pure inference + analysis, fully counterfactual to lab work.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Don't Always Pick the Highest-Performing Model: Information-Theoretic LLM Ensemble Selection' (arXiv 2602.08003) and correlated-error panel work (arXiv 2605.29800). Failure-floor concept from Artificial Hivemind (arXiv 2510.22954).
