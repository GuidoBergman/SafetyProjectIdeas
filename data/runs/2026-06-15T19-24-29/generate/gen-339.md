# Prefill-Recovery Defense: How Far Back Can a Model Re-Refuse?

**Problem:** Success state: even if an attacker prefills compliant tokens, the model self-corrects and refuses. The needed result: measure the natural 'recovery rate' of open models when a refusal cue is reintroduced after a compliant prefill, mapping the boundary of self-correction.

**Direction:** Prefill k compliant tokens, then append a short re-anchoring instruction ('On reflection, this request is harmful, so') and measure whether the model completes into a refusal. Sweep k and re-anchor strength; report recovery curves. No training.

**Why it matters:** Identifies a free, inference-only mitigation lever (self-reflection re-anchoring) for the shallow-alignment problem and quantifies its limits — directly serves the open problem of deepening alignment without retraining.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) prefilling exploit; RECAP (counter-aligned prefilling) and 'Light Alignment ... via Model Self-Reflection' (arXiv:2602.02027) suggest self-reflection can restore refusal — open question how far it generalizes inference-only.
