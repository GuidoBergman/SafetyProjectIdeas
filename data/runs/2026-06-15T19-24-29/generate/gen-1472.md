# Norm-blowup vs concept: separating steering effect from raw activation-norm disruption

**Problem:** Norm preservation is reportedly critical for steering quality, and excessive norm change breaks coherence. It is unclear how much of a steering vector's measured behavioral effect is mediated by the activation-norm change it induces versus the concept direction itself.

**Direction:** On an open model, apply a safety steering vector with and without norm-preserving renormalization at matched coefficients. Measure on-target effect and coherence in both conditions. If norm-preserved steering loses most of the effect, the effect was norm-mediated, not concept-mediated; quantify this fraction per concept.

**Why it matters:** Disentangling 'concept push' from 'norm shock' clarifies whether steering is a precise lever or a blunt perturbation, which determines how trustworthy it is as a safety mechanism. The norm-controlled comparison is a clean, cheap experiment that no lab is rushing to run.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in norm-preservation-is-critical findings (arXiv 2601.19375 'Norm-Preserving Control', 2512.01991, 2504.04635). Inference-only on open model; linear-algebra-centric, beginner-feasible.
