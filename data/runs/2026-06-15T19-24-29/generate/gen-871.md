# The Verification Bottleneck: Which Link in Compute-Location Attestation Is the Weakest?

**Problem:** Compute-governance enforcement chain: 'export control set' -> 'chip ships to declared location' -> 'location verified' -> 'diversion detected' -> 'enforcement'. The verify link is the proposed lynchpin, with low-tech (inspections) vs high-tech (latency-based location attestation) options, but no public empirical sense of how reliable latency attestation is.

**Direction:** Reproduce a toy version of latency-based geolocation: from a single cloud VM, ping geographically distributed public servers (e.g., RIPE Atlas anchors or public NTP servers), and test how accurately round-trip-time triangulation can bound a machine's location, including under simple spoofing (added artificial delay). Report accuracy and the spoofing gap.

**Why it matters:** Proposals lean heavily on on-chip location attestation; an accessible empirical demo of how easily latency-based attestation degrades or is spoofed tells governance designers whether this link can carry treaty-verification weight. Runs on one VM, no GPU even needed, pure measurement, deeply counterfactual.
