# Do Models Share Blind Spots on Safety-Relevant Open-Ended Queries?

**Problem:** The Artificial Hivemind paper showed inter-model homogeneity on general open-ended queries via Infinity-Chat, but did not isolate safety-relevant prompts (e.g., requests touching dual-use, risky advice, or value-laden tradeoffs). If models converge specifically on flawed safety responses, the systemic risk is much sharper than on creative tasks.

**Direction:** Filter or hand-curate a safety-relevant slice from Infinity-Chat (and add a small set of dual-use/value-tradeoff prompts). Sample N responses from ~8-12 freely available chat models (open weights via API/Together + a couple of API models) at fixed temperature. Measure pairwise inter-model output similarity (embedding cosine + n-gram overlap) and compare the similarity distribution on safety-relevant prompts vs. a matched general slice. Success: a clear, statistically tested difference (or convincing null) in homogeneity between the two slices.

**Why it matters:** If safety-relevant prompts are MORE homogeneous, correlated failures across deployed systems become a concrete systemic risk that no single lab can detect from inside. Doable entirely with public models and a public benchmark, no training.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind / Infinity-Chat (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, arXiv:2510.22954); embedding-based diversity metrics from Epistemic Diversity work (arXiv:2510.04226).
