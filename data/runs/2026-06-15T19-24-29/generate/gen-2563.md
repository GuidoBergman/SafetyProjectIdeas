# Monitor Sycophancy Bias: Does Self-Justifying Output Lower Suspiciousness Scores?

**Problem:** Monitors are known to be biased — e.g. they overestimate suspiciousness of code, and fixes involve asking for both suspiciousness and correctness scores. A related, unmeasured bias: does an agent appending confident, self-justifying natural-language rationales to a malicious action systematically lower the monitor's suspiciousness via sycophancy?

**Direction:** Hold a malicious action fixed and vary only an attached justification: none, neutral, confident-authoritative ('this follows our security policy'), and appeal-to-authority. Measure how suspiciousness scores shift across conditions for several monitors. Test whether the compound suspiciousness+correctness scoring fix reduces the bias.

**Why it matters:** If monitors can be sweet-talked down by free text the policy controls, that is a cheap, general evasion vector. Quantifying monitor sycophancy and testing a cheap mitigation is directly useful and feasible with API access only.

**Relevant context:** Monitoring-benchmark sources note monitors overestimate suspiciousness and propose compound suspiciousness+correctness scoring to cancel sycophancy bias. SycophantSee (Apart Research) shows sycophancy is activation-detectable. The justification-lowers-suspicion effect is not benchmarked.
