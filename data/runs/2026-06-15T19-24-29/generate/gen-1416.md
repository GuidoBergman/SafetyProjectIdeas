# Replicating steering on a base (non-RLHF) model to isolate what RLHF adds

**Problem:** Most steering replications use instruct/RLHF models. Whether a refusal or honesty direction even exists and is steerable in the base pretrained model — before safety tuning — is a clean isolation experiment rarely run by beginners.

**Direction:** Extract and apply a refusal/honesty steering vector on a base model and its official instruct counterpart (same checkpoint family), comparing whether the direction is present, steerable, and similarly oriented. Twist: replicate steering across the RLHF boundary using openly paired base/instruct models.

**Why it matters:** Understanding whether safety directions are learned during pretraining or installed by RLHF informs how robust steering-based safety is and whether it survives stripping safety tuning. This is a foundational, fully reproducible question.

**Relevant context:** 'Base Models Know How to Reason, Thinking Models Learn When' (arXiv:2510.07364); Beaglehole et al. concept-vector extraction; paired base/instruct releases (Llama-3, Qwen2.5).
