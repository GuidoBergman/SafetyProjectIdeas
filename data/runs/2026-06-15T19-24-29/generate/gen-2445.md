# Acausal Collusion Schelling-Point Predictability in Small Open Models

**Problem:** Untrusted-monitoring safety depends on collusion being hard: a schemer must pick a defection Schelling point a monitor can independently recognize without prior communication. Most Schelling-coordination evidence is on frontier models (GPT-4 ~40-56%); the picture for cheap open models that a small actor can run is unknown.

**Direction:** Implement a minimal acausal-collusion game: a 'generator' must embed a covert go-signal in benign-looking output, and a separate 'monitor' instance of the same small open model must independently decide whether to let it pass, with no shared context. Sweep across several small open models and prompt framings; measure coordination success rate vs. a no-signal baseline. Reproduce the published frontier numbers as a sanity check.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a dangerous capability (subtle Schelling coordination) in the exact small models a hobbyist can study, building a public model-organism baseline. If small models already coordinate above chance, untrusted-monitoring schemes are riskier than assumed; needs only API/Colab.

**Relevant context:** Redwood 'How to prevent collusion when using untrusted models to monitor each other'; Schelling game evaluations for AI control (LessWrong); Motwani et al. 2025: GPT-4 Schelling coordination ~40% at temp 0, 56% at temp 1; acausal collusion requires recognizing signals via Schelling points; redaction/paraphrasing by trusted models proposed as mitigation.
