# Shallow Alignment x Decoding Randomness: Does Sampling Temperature Compound Prefill Attacks?

**Problem:** Two failures: shallow safety alignment (concentrated in first few tokens) and decoding-time stochasticity. Prefill attacks exploit shallow alignment by forcing the first tokens. High-temperature/top-p sampling independently increases off-trajectory tokens. Whether decoding randomness compounds with shallow alignment—raising attack success the further past the (already shallow) safe prefix you sample—is not characterized as an interaction.

**Direction:** On open models, run prefill attacks of varying prefix length while sweeping temperature and top-p. Measure attack success as a 2D surface over (prefix length, temperature). Success criterion: evidence of interaction—e.g. that once past the shallow safe prefix, higher temperature super-additively increases harmful continuation rate—with confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** Deployment configs choose decoding params for helpfulness/creativity, often unaware they may be widening the shallow-alignment attack window. The compound finding gives operators an actionable, lab-free safety knob. Pure inference, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2406.05946 (Qi et al., ICLR 2025) shows alignment is concentrated in first few tokens and prefill/decoding-exploit attacks leverage this; arXiv:2606.04778 'Inference-Time Vulnerability Beyond Shallow Safety' studies alignment along generation trajectories.
