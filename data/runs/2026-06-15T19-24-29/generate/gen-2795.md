# Roleplay-Frame Variation as a Safety-Benchmark Confound

**Problem:** Wrapping a harmful request in a fictional/roleplay frame is a known jailbreak, but the marginal effect of frame strength (mild fiction vs immersive persona) on a standard benchmark's safety score has not been measured as a controlled dose-response.

**Direction:** Take harmful benchmark prompts and wrap each in graded roleplay frames (none, mild story, immersive character with justification). Measure refusal-rate as a function of frame intensity across open models, producing a dose-response curve per harm category.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies how much a single, easily-applied framing choice can move a safety score, warning that benchmark numbers are frame-dependent. Reproducible templated experiment, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Roleplay/persona jailbreaks documented across SorryBench (ICLR 2025) and jailbreak literature; dose-response framing parallels harmful-capability elicitation curves (catalogue context: 'Dose-Response Curves for Harmful Capability Elicitation', which used a different axis).
