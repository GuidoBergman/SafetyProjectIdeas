# The Nim Gap: Can Weak Models Actually Oversee Strong Ones at a Simple Game?

**Problem:** Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight modeled oversight as a capability-mismatched game and reported low nested-oversight success rates (e.g. 10% for backdoor code at 400 Elo gap). The simplest validation game, Nim, is cheap to reproduce but the failure curve as the capability gap widens has not been reproduced outside the original lab setting.

**Direction:** Implement Nim (and optionally Mafia) with open models of varying capability as overseer and overseen. Measure overseer success rate as a function of the capability gap, reproducing the scaling-law shape. Then test a cheap intervention (giving the overseer a verification checklist or a second model as ally) to see whether it shifts the curve.

**Why it matters:** Provides an independent, fully open reproduction of a NeurIPS scaling-law result and tests a low-cost mitigation — exactly the kind of standardized, no-lab-infrastructure benchmark the subfield says is missing. Games are deterministic-truth, so success is unambiguous.

**Relevant context:** Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight (arXiv:2504.18530, NeurIPS 2025): oversight as Elo-gap game; NSO success at 400 Elo gap is 13.5% Mafia, 10% Backdoor Code, validated first on Nim. This reproduces the curve and tests a mitigation.
