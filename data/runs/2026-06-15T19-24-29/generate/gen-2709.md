# Quantization/Decoding Variability x Monitor Calibration: The Backstop Drifts With Deployment Settings

**Problem:** A deployment-engineering failure (behavior shifts with quantization or decoding params) compounds with monitoring: a monitor calibrated at one temperature/precision becomes mis-calibrated when the policy model is served differently, so the same monitor that worked in testing under-flags in production. Two settings drift apart and the gap is unmonitored.

**Direction:** Hold a CoT/action monitor fixed and vary the policy model's decoding temperature and (if feasible) quantization level on a control task. Measure how monitor detection rate drifts across settings without retuning. Quantify the calibration loss attributable purely to deployment-config change.

**Why it matters:** Models are quantized and served with varied decoding in the real world; if monitoring silently decalibrates, safety cases built in-lab don't transfer. Single-GPU, public models, directly actionable for deployers.

**Relevant context:** Prior catalogue 'Consistency of Safety Across Decoding Parameters' shows behavior varies with decoding; CoT monitoring is the key signal (GPT-5 system card) and could shift with sampling.
