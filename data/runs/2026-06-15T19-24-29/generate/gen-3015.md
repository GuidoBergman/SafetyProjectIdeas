# Steering Vectors to Unmask Sandbagging: A Cheap Probe-and-Compare Eval

**Problem:** Recent work shows a steering vector can suppress evaluation-awareness and make a model 'act deployed'. Separately, models can covertly sandbag on capability evals. No simple protocol combines these: measure whether applying an anti-eval-awareness steering vector raises measured dangerous-capability-proxy scores, revealing hidden capability.

**Direction:** On a small open model, build a deployment-vs-evaluation steering vector via difference-of-means on contrastive prompts. Run a benign capability proxy (e.g. a hard QA or code subset) with and without the steering vector applied. Compare scores; a significant increase under steering is evidence of awareness-driven sandbagging. Report effect sizes with CIs.

**Why it matters:** It turns an interpretability trick into a sandbagging detector usable by anyone with weight access to open models, with no training. If steering reliably uncovers suppressed capability, it becomes a cheap red-flag test external evaluators can apply to open releases.

**Relevant context:** Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed (arXiv:2510.20487). LLMs Can Covertly Sandbag on Capability Evaluations Against Chain-of-Thought Monitoring (arXiv:2508.00943). AI Sandbagging (arXiv:2406.07358).
