# The Refusal-Direction Link: Does Benign Fine-Tuning Specifically Rotate the Refusal Feature?

**Problem:** Safety erosion from benign fine-tuning is usually measured behaviorally. The mechanistic link is under-studied: prior interpretability work suggests refusal is mediated by a single direction. Does benign fine-tuning erode safety by attenuating/rotating that specific refusal direction, or by broad diffuse change? Pinning the link tells us whether protecting one direction suffices.

**Direction:** Extract the refusal direction (mean activation difference between harmful and harmless prompts) in a small safety-tuned model before and after benign LoRA fine-tuning that is known to reduce refusals. Measure the change in projection of harmful prompts onto the refusal direction, and correlate it with the behavioral drop in refusal. No training of probes beyond simple difference-of-means.

**Why it matters:** If safety erosion is concentrated in a known refusal direction, then cheaply monitoring or regularizing that single direction during fine-tuning could prevent it. Connecting the behavioral safety-erosion result to a specific representational link is a tractable, single-GPU mechanistic contribution.

**Relevant context:** 'Preventing Catastrophic Forgetting' (2510.21885) and 'Geometry of Alignment Collapse' (2602.15799): benign fine-tuning degrades safety; alignment collapse has geometric structure. Refusal-direction findings (single mediating direction) are well established and reproducible via difference-of-means.
