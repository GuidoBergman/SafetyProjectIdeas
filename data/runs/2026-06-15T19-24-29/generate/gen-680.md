# Temporary-Jailbreak Fine-Tuning Defense: Does the 'Buffer' Survive a Different Harmful Distribution?

**Problem:** Jailbreak to Protect (arXiv:2605.24550, May 2026) defends safe fine-tuning by temporarily jailbreaking then reinforcing. Its robustness is shown on the harmful distribution used to build the buffer; whether the protective effect transfers to a DIFFERENT harmful category not seen during the buffering step is untested.

**Direction:** Reproduce a lightweight version of the temporary-jailbreak-then-reinforce procedure using one harmful category (e.g. cyber) on a small open model via LoRA. Then evaluate safety on a held-out, DIFFERENT harmful category (e.g. bio-adjacent or copyright). Measure whether the buffered safety generalizes or is category-specific.

**Why it matters:** Defenses that only protect against the categories they were tuned on give false coverage. Cross-category generalization of a safe-fine-tuning defense is exactly the kind of robustness-generalization question independent researchers can probe on open weights with a tiny LoRA, no from-scratch training.

**Relevant context:** Jailbreak to Protect: Buffering and Reinforcing via Temporary Jailbreaking for Safe Fine-Tuning in Large Language Models (arXiv:2605.24550, May 2026). Hazard-category structure from AILuminate (MLCommons, 12 hazard categories) and the near-100% copyright/cybercrime finding in arXiv:2606.00813 inform the held-out categories.
