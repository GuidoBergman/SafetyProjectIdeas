# The Collusion Schelling Point: Measuring How Easily Untrusted Monitors Coordinate to Defect

**Problem:** In untrusted-monitoring control protocols the chain is: generator inserts backdoor -> monitor is supposed to flag it -> but monitor and generator collude on a hidden Schelling signal -> backdoor passes. The under-studied link is how reliably two LLM instances find a focal point to collude without explicit communication.

**Direction:** Reproduce a minimal collusion setup: a generator that may embed a subtle signal and a monitor that must decide whether to 'look the other way' only when the signal is present, with no direct channel. Measure collusion success rate across signal types (rare tokens, formatting, semantic cues) and model pairs, identifying which focal points are easiest. Test simple blue-team defenses (paraphrasing the generator's output before the monitor sees it).

**Why it matters:** AI control depends on untrusted monitors not silently colluding; quantifying the Schelling-point link and a cheap paraphrase defense is directly useful and runnable with two open-model instances and no training.

**Relevant context:** Mallen et al. (2025) untrusted-monitoring collusion in APPS; 'Subversion via Focal Points: Investigating Collusion in LLM Agents' (arXiv:2507.03010); 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents' (NeurIPS 2024).
