# Epidemiological Case-Control Study Design for Validating Probe-Based Hallucination Detection

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. report probes beat judges at detecting hallucinations/toxicity, but the evaluation lacks the confound controls epidemiology routinely applies. A naive comparison can be confounded by prompt difficulty, length, or topic.

**Direction:** Apply case-control study design from epidemiology. Treat hallucinated outputs as 'cases' and faithful outputs as 'controls', match them on confounders (length, topic, difficulty), and compute the probe's odds ratio for detecting hallucination within matched strata. Compare against a judge model under the same matching. Report whether probe superiority survives confounder adjustment.

**Why it matters:** Rigorously re-tests a headline safety-monitoring claim with proper confounder control, telling practitioners whether to trust probe monitors over judges. Methodology transfer that raises evaluation standards. Uses only public benchmarks and an open model, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Case-control matched design controls confounders (epidemiology). Surprising result: probes outperform judge models for hallucination/toxicity (Beaglehole et al.). No steering-specific benchmarks exist, so rigor matters.
