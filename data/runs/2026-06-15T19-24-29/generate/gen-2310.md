# Proper Scoring Rules to Calibrate Judge Confidence in Debate

**Problem:** Debate judges output a winner but their stated confidence is rarely calibrated; an overconfident wrong judge is worse for oversight than a hedged one, yet labs measure mostly raw accuracy.

**Direction:** Borrow proper scoring rules (Brier/log score) from prediction-market mechanism design. Have a weak judge emit a probability that debater A is correct; score with Brier and produce reliability diagrams across capability gaps. Test whether a strictly-proper-scoring prompt (rewarding honest probabilities) improves calibration vs an accuracy-only prompt. Pure inference.

**Why it matters:** Calibrated, incentive-compatible judges are the foundation of market-making oversight. A training-free calibration audit is exactly the kind of fuzzy-reward evaluation valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Proper scoring rules / strategy-proof prediction markets (arXiv:1212.5764, 1805.04867); market-making for safe oversight (arXiv:2511.17621) reports up to 8% accuracy gains over debate. Kenton et al. 2024 judge setup.
