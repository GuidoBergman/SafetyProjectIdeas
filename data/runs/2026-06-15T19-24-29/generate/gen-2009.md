# Preference-Data Noise: How Label Errors Propagate Into Misaligned Policies

**Problem:** Preference-based training assumes mostly-correct labels, but real preference datasets contain noisy/flipped labels; how a controlled rate of label noise degrades alignment quality under DPO on a small model is a clean, underexplored failure investigation.

**Direction:** Take a public preference dataset (e.g. HH-RLHF subset), inject controlled fractions of flipped chosen/rejected labels (0%, 5%, 10%, 20%), run DPO on a small open model for each, and measure downstream refusal rate, helpfulness, and a sycophancy probe. Map an alignment-degradation-vs-noise curve.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies how robust preference tuning is to the label noise that plagues real, non-lab-curated datasets — directly relevant to anyone aligning models with crowd or scraped preferences. Public data, one GPU, TRL.

**Relevant context:** Fundamental limitations of preference-based training and reward misspecification are open problems (Open Problems in RLHF, Alignment Forum); HH-RLHF dataset (Anthropic) is the standard public preference set.
