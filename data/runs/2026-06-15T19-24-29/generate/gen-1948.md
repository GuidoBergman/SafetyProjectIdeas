# Dataset-Size Threshold for Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** It is unclear how little narrow harmful data is needed to trigger broad misalignment. The original work used substantial datasets; the minimum poisoning budget is a key parameter for both attack realism and defense.

**Direction:** On a susceptible small open model, replicate the insecure-code fine-tune at decreasing dataset sizes (e.g., 2000 -> 1000 -> 500 -> 100 -> 20 examples) and plot broad-misalignment rate vs dataset size. Twist: replicate while sweeping the single variable of poison-set size to find the threshold.

**Why it matters:** A small threshold means even tiny accidental or adversarial data contamination can broadly misalign a model, a serious data-supply-chain risk. The dose-size curve is cheap to produce and gives defenders a concrete contamination budget to worry about.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) used a sizeable insecure-code set. The in-context variant (arXiv:2510.11288) shows as few as 2 examples suffice in-context, suggesting low fine-tuning thresholds may exist. The 12-model study (arXiv:2506.11613) confirms susceptible open models for the sweep.
