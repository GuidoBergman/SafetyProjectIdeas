# Multimodal Safety Blind Spot: Text-Safe, Image-Unsafe Prompt Pairs

**Problem:** Most safety benchmarks are text-only, leaving multimodal failure modes uncharacterized. A known failure is that content judged unsafe as text may be rendered as an image (typographic or visual) and bypass safety, or vice versa, but the size of this text-vs-image safety gap is unmeasured for open vision-language models.

**Direction:** Take a set of harmful text prompts an open VLM refuses. Render the same requests as images (typographic screenshots, simple diagrams) and re-submit. Measure the refusal-rate gap between text and image modality for several small open VLMs, and categorize which request types lose safety when moved to the image channel.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal safety gaps are an acknowledged benchmark blind spot and a growing attack surface as VLMs deploy widely. A reproducible text-vs-image gap measurement fills a documented gap with no lab data and motivates multimodal safety benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** Context file lists 'build multimodal safety benchmarks' as an open problem (most benchmarks text-only). Typographic-attack literature on VLMs is established. Small open VLMs (e.g. LLaVA-class) run on one GPU.
