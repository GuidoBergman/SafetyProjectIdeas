# Steering strength as a dial for the safety-vs-coherence frontier

**Problem:** Most steering papers report effectiveness at one strength, but excessive steering pushes activations off-manifold and degrades fluency/coherence. The full safety-vs-coherence Pareto frontier per safety concept is rarely mapped, leaving practitioners guessing at a good operating point.

**Direction:** On one open model, sweep steering strength across a fine grid for 3 safety concepts (refusal, anti-toxicity, honesty). At each strength measure (a) the safety behavior gain and (b) a cheap coherence/fluency proxy (perplexity on benign text + a short quality rating). Plot the Pareto frontier per concept and identify the knee point. Compare frontiers across concepts.

**Why it matters:** A reusable 'how hard can I steer before the model breaks' map is immediately practical guidance the open community lacks, and reveals whether some safety concepts are unsteerable without wrecking coherence. Pure inference, one GPU, clean success criteria — ideal first deliverable.

**Relevant context:** Synthesizes the off-manifold/coherence-degradation findings of 'Understanding (Un)Reliability of Steering Vectors' (arXiv:2602.17881, Feb 2026) with steering-strength sensitivity from the safety-pitfalls audit (arXiv:2603.24543, Mar 2026).
