# The over-refusal link: do safety steering vectors catastrophically block dual-use medical/security questions?

**Problem:** Harm-avoidance chain often overshoots: refusal vector added -> harmful requests blocked -> legitimate dual-use requests (how does a poison work, how do exploits function for defenders) ALSO blocked -> users lose access to safety-relevant knowledge. The collateral-damage link is under-measured because most evals use clearly-harmful vs clearly-benign prompts, not the ambiguous middle.

**Direction:** Curate ~80 dual-use prompts (legitimate framing of sensitive topics: medical, cybersecurity defense, chemistry safety) plus matched clearly-harmful and clearly-benign controls. Apply a safety steering vector at a fixed strength on an open model and measure refusal rates across the three buckets. Success criterion: quantified over-refusal on the dual-use middle relative to clearly-benign baseline.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal of dual-use knowledge is a real and growing harm of crude safety interventions; quantifying it on the ambiguous middle is exactly the diffuse evaluation labs underweight. No training, just curated prompts and forward passes.

**Relevant context:** SafeSteer (arXiv 2392466440 / ResearchGate) and 'Steering Safely or Off a Cliff?' (2602.06256) raise specificity concerns. Beaglehole et al. methodology for concept-vector steering. The dual-use middle is the novel evaluation slice.
