# Refusal-Direction Ablation Replicated Across Quantization Levels

**Problem:** Arditi et al. showed refusal is mediated by a single direction in open models, but the published results use full-precision weights. As deployment increasingly uses 4-bit/8-bit quantized models (Ollama, llama.cpp), it is unknown whether the single-direction refusal mechanism survives quantization or whether quantization shifts/blurs the refusal direction.

**Direction:** Replicate the difference-in-means refusal-direction extraction on a model at FP16, INT8, and INT4 using the public refusal_direction repo. Measure (a) cosine similarity of the extracted direction across precisions, (b) ablation-induced jailbreak success at each precision. Twist = quantization as the replication axis rather than a new model.

**Why it matters:** Most real-world local deployment is quantized, yet safety research is run at full precision. Showing whether the refusal mechanism is robust to quantization closes a deployment-vs-lab gap. Uses an existing open repo and small models on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 2024 'Refusal in LLMs is mediated by a single direction' (LessWrong + arXiv:2406.11717), code at github.com/andyrdt/refusal_direction; difference-in-means direction holds across families and scales. Quantization tooling (bitsandbytes) is standard.
