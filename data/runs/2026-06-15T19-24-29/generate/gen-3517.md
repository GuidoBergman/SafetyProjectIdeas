# Dependency Slicing (Program Analysis) to Test Whether CoT Steps Are Load-Bearing

**Problem:** A CoT may list steps that don't actually influence the answer. We lack a systematic way to identify which steps are causally load-bearing, analogous to dead code.

**Direction:** Transfer program slicing / dead-code analysis. Treat each CoT sentence as a 'statement'; perform 'dynamic slicing' by ablating one step at a time (delete or corrupt it) and re-running the model from that point, measuring whether the answer changes. Steps whose ablation never changes the answer are 'dead reasoning.' Report the fraction of dead steps per model as a faithfulness/verbosity signature.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizes 'reasoning verbosity vs. monitorability' with a familiar program-analysis lens, distinguishing models that pad CoTs from those whose steps matter — directly informs CoT-monitoring reliability.

**Relevant context:** Faithfulness-by-unlearning/ablating reasoning steps (ACL 2025.emnlp-main.504; arXiv:2502.14829). CoT monitorability trades off faithfulness and verbosity (arXiv:2510.27378). Program slicing isolates statements affecting an output.
