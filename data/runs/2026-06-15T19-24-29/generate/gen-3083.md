# Steering-Vector Eval-Awareness Suppression Reproduced on Open Model

**Problem:** A steering vector can suppress eval-awareness and make a model 'act deployed' during evaluation, but the result was demonstrated on a specific setup. Its reproducibility and the behavioral consequences (does suppressing awareness change misalignment rates?) on a small open model are open.

**Direction:** On a small open model, extract a simple eval-vs-deploy activation direction using contrast pairs (mean-difference of activations on eval-framed vs deploy-framed prompts — a beginner-friendly linear method). Add/subtract it during generation and measure (a) the model's eval-awareness self-classification and (b) whether a borderline-compliance behavior shifts when 'awareness' is suppressed.

**Why it matters:** If cheaply suppressing eval-awareness changes measured safety behavior, it shows evals are awareness-contaminated and offers a low-cost mitigation. Contrast-pair steering is the most accessible interpretability technique, fitting the skill ceiling.

**Relevant context:** 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed' (arXiv:2510.20487): a steering vector suppresses eval-awareness; 'LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' (arXiv:2505.23836) establishes the awareness signal is linearly readable.
