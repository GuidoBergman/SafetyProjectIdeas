# JailbreakDelta: Measuring the Hidden Jailbreak Side-Effect of Benign Steering

**Problem:** Recent work shows benign steering toward one behavior can silently raise or lower jailbreak susceptibility by up to ~57%, but there is no simple tool to quantify this side-effect per steering vector. Practitioners apply steering vectors without measuring this externality.

**Direction:** Build a small evaluation harness that, for any CAA steering vector, runs a fixed set of jailbreak prompts (from an open jailbreak dataset like AdvBench/JailbreakBench subset) with and without the vector applied, and reports a 'JailbreakDelta' attack-success-rate change. Apply it to 6-8 benign steering directions on an open 7B model and rank them by induced jailbreak risk.

**Why it matters:** It turns a surprising published finding into a reusable measurement, giving anyone deploying steering a cheap safety check. Quantifying unintended jailbreak externalities is exactly the diffuse, fuzzy-reward safety work labs deprioritize and that needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Arxiv 2603.24543 'Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' and 2602.04896 'Steering Externalities' show CAA can change jailbreak ASR by up to 57%/50% via overlap with refusal directions. AdvBench/JailbreakBench are open.
