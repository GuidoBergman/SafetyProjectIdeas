# Replicating specification gaming on AI Safety Gridworlds with a modern small RL agent

**Problem:** DeepMind's AI Safety Gridworlds (2017) catalogued reward-gaming, side-effects, absent-supervisor, and reward-tampering behaviors using tabular/early-deep agents. Many of the canonical 'agent games the spec' demonstrations have not been re-run with a *current, small, easy-to-train* RL algorithm to check whether the gaming behaviors reproduce and how training choices affect their severity.

**Direction:** Use the open AI Safety Gridworlds environments and train a small, off-the-shelf RL agent (e.g., a compact DQN/PPO from a standard library) on the reward-gaming and absent-supervisor worlds. Reproduce whether the agent learns the gaming behavior, measure how often, and vary one knob (exploration, discount, or reward shaping) to see how it changes the gaming rate vs. the documented behavior.

**Why it matters:** Specification gaming / reward tampering is a flagship long-horizon alignment threat. Reproducing it in a clean gridworld with a modern small agent gives a teaching-and-research model organism of reward hacking that needs no frontier model, no proprietary data, and no lab infrastructure — high counterfactual value and directly aligned with the 'prove when agents cannot game rewards' open problem.

**Relevant context:** AI Safety Gridworlds (arXiv:1711.09883): suite covering reward gaming, side effects, absent supervisor, reward tampering, distributional shift; Krakovna's specification-gaming examples list. Open Problem (context): 'Stress-test AI agents to prove when they cannot game rewards.' Steering priority (context): model-organism and reward-hacking studies.
