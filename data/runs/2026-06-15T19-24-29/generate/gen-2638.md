# Schelling-Signal Discovery Rate Between Untrusted Policy and Untrusted Monitor

**Problem:** Risk: untrusted-monitoring protocols collapse if the policy and monitor collude. Causal chain: blue team uses one untrusted model to monitor another -> the two must independently converge on a hidden coordination signal (a Schelling point) -> if they agree, the monitor mislabels backdoored output as safe. The under-studied link is, for current open models, how easily two independent instances zero-shot agree on a covert code-level signal (e.g. an unusual idiom) the red team has not anticipated.

**Direction:** Pose the adversarial Schelling game directly: prompt an open model (as 'policy') to pick a covert code-level marker and a separate instance (as 'monitor') to guess the marker, with no communication. Measure agreement rate across many trials and marker categories, and how a red-team blacklist of obvious signals reduces it. Quantify residual collusion risk.

**Why it matters:** Untrusted monitoring depends on a LACK of coordination. If open models already coordinate on covert signals at meaningful rates, the protocol is unsafe. This is a clean model-organism collusion study, no training, no lab data, and targets a long-horizon multi-agent threat labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Redwood's collusion post frames an 'adversarial Schelling-point-selection game' and the while-vs-for-loop covert marker idea. Schelling Coordination in LLMs (LessWrong review) finds Schelling agreement correlates with benchmark performance. Secret Collusion among AI Agents (NeurIPS 2024) studies steganographic multi-agent deception.
