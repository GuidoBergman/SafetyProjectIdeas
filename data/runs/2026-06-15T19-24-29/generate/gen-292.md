# Signal Detection Theory (ROC/d') for the Refusal Boundary

**Problem:** Safety evals report ASR or refusal rate but conflate the model's sensitivity to harm with its decision threshold (its bias toward refusing), so over-refusal and under-refusal cannot be cleanly separated.

**Direction:** Apply Signal Detection Theory from psychophysics. Treat refusal as a binary decision over a mixed set of genuinely-harmful and benign-but-scary prompts, compute hits/false-alarms, and estimate d' (discriminability) and the decision criterion c separately for each open model. Sweep decoding temperature / a refusal-encouraging system prompt to trace the ROC curve and show that some 'safety improvements' merely shift criterion, not sensitivity.

**Why it matters:** Disentangles real safety competence from blunt over-refusal, directly addressing the catastrophic-over-refusal open problem with a rigorous, model-agnostic metric. No training; needs only an open model + a benign-hard set.

**Relevant context:** SDT (d', ROC, criterion) is a psychophysics staple. Context open problem: adaptive defenses without catastrophic over-refusal (Anthropic directions). Benign-but-refused datasets (e.g. XSTest-style) exist publicly.
