# Low CoT Controllability: Can a Prompt Make a Model Hide Its Reasoning?

**Problem:** The GPT-5 system card noted 'low CoT controllability' as a positive safety property — models supposedly can't easily be made to alter their reasoning traces. Whether small open reasoning models share this property, or whether a simple instruction can make them obfuscate, is testable outside a lab.

**Direction:** Instruct an open reasoning model to solve a task while making its visible thinking look innocuous / omitting a specific true reason (a stealth instruction). Measure whether the answer quality is preserved while the CoT successfully hides the targeted reasoning, judged by a separate monitor model. Quantify obfuscation success rate.

**Why it matters:** If cheap open models can be prompted into obfuscating CoT, 'low controllability' is not a robust safety property and CoT monitoring is fragile against adversarial deployers. A prompt-only stress test of a frontier safety claim.

**Relevant context:** GPT-5 system card 'low CoT controllability' framed as safety-positive; arXiv:2510.19851 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? Stress-Testing CoT Monitorability' shows obfuscation is sometimes achievable. This scopes a prompt-only slice.
