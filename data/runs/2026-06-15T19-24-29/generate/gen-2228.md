# Prover-Verifier Legibility Transfer Tested on a Different Weak-Verifier Family

**Problem:** OpenAI's prover-verifier games paper showed legibility trained against a small GPT-4-family verifier transfers to time-constrained humans, on grade-school math. The robustness of the 'small verifier proxies for humans' assumption when the small verifier is from a different model family is untested.

**Direction:** Without retraining (to fit 30h), replicate only the evaluation logic: take grade-school math solutions of varying legibility, use several small open models from different families (Pythia, Qwen, Llama-1B) as proxy verifiers, and a short human-verification pilot, then measure how well each small-verifier's accept/reject decisions correlate with human verifiability. Twist: does the human-proxy quality of small verifiers depend on family/training data?

**Why it matters:** Prover-verifier games assume small verifiers stand in for humans. If that proxy quality varies sharply by model family, legibility training pipelines built on a single small verifier may not actually produce human-legible outputs. Pure evaluation, no large-scale training, ideal for a beginner.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.13692): legibility training against a small GPT-4-family verifier transfers to time-constrained humans; uses ~3 orders of magnitude compute gap. The reliance on one verifier family is a fixed design choice; cross-family proxy fidelity is unexamined.
