# Refusal Direction as a Cheap Detector That Breaks: Replicating the Probe Cliff Without Training

**Problem:** If refusal is mediated by a single direction (Arditi et al.), projecting an activation onto that direction is essentially a free 'will-it-refuse' detector. Whether this projection-based signal is a robust jailbreak detector — or collapses on OOD attacks like trained probes do — is unexamined as a failure mode.

**Direction:** Extract the refusal direction from one open model. For diverse attack families, compute the projection of the prompt's last-token activation onto that direction and treat its sign/magnitude as a jailbreak-success predictor. Measure detection AUC per family and the ID->OOD drop, comparing against a trained linear probe to see whether the 'free' detector is more or less robust.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether interpretability artifacts double as cheap defenses (Anthropic-style direction monitoring) and where they fail — combining the activation-probe cost-effectiveness thread with the OOD-cliff thread in a novel way. White-box, single GPU, released code; strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. (NeurIPS 2024, andyrdt/refusal_direction) establish the single direction; the activation-probe cost-effectiveness claim (LessWrong Jan 2026, per context file) motivates 'free' detectors; OOD-cliff evidence ('From Sure to Sorry', JailbreaksOverTime arXiv:2504.19440) motivates the failure-mode angle. 'LLM Jailbreak Detection for (Almost) Free' (EMNLP 2025 Findings) is directly related.
