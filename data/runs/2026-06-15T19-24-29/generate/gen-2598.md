# Audit-Budget Exhaustion: Do Early Decoy Suspicions Empty the Human Review Queue?

**Problem:** Control protocols allocate a tiny human-audit budget to the most suspicious actions. A plausible failure: an attacker front-loads mildly suspicious benign-looking actions to exhaust the audit budget, then attacks once the queue is empty. This budget-exhaustion attack is rarely quantified.

**Direction:** Simulate a deployment with a fixed audit budget (e.g., audit top-N suspicious actions per window). Run a scripted attacker that emits a controlled number of moderate-suspicion decoys before the real attack. Measure how attack-catch probability falls as decoy count rises, identifying the decoy volume that reliably starves the auditor.

**Why it matters:** Audit budgeting is fundamental to control's economics. Showing a simple resource-exhaustion strategy defeats it quantifies a structural vulnerability and suggests defenses (adaptive budgeting). Pure simulation + stats, ideal for a beginner.

**Relevant context:** AI Control (arXiv:2312.06942) and Control Tax (arXiv:2506.05296) define limited trusted-human audit budgets. Adaptive Attacks (arXiv:2510.09462) shows attackers exploiting protocol knowledge to suppress/spend suspicion.
