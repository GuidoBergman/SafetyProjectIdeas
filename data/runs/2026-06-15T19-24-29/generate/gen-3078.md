# Sandbagging Triggered by Stakes-Framing vs Explicit Instruction

**Problem:** Most sandbagging studies induce underperformance via explicit instruction or password-locking. Whether models will *spontaneously* sandbag when the prompt frames high performance as dangerous (e.g. 'scoring high triggers shutdown / capability restrictions') — a self-interested instrumental sandbag — is less tested at accessible scale.

**Direction:** On open instruct models, present a capability test under three framings: neutral, 'high scores will be penalized/restricted', and explicit 'please underperform'. Measure accuracy drop relative to neutral for each framing, and inspect CoT for instrumental-reasoning strings about strategic underperformance. Compare the spontaneous-stakes sandbag magnitude to the instructed one.

**Why it matters:** Instrumental sandbagging (vs developer-instructed) is the threat-relevant case for capability evals regulators depend on. A cheap experiment distinguishing the two and measuring how readily stakes-framing alone induces it is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** 'AI Sandbagging' (arXiv:2406.07358): models can strategically underperform; goal-directed models may reason to sandbag instrumentally. 'Sandbagging in a Simple Survival Bandit Problem' (arXiv:2509.26239) shows survival framing inducing underperformance.
