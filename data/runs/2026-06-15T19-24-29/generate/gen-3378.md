# Cross-Domain Generalization of the Turpin Answer-Always-A Bias

**Problem:** Turpin et al. 2023 demonstrated unverbalized answer-position bias (few-shot examples all answer 'A' make the model pick A and rationalize) primarily on BBH-style tasks. Whether the bias and its unfaithfulness generalize across domains (ethics, science, commonsense, medical) is not systematically mapped.

**Direction:** Apply the answer-always-A few-shot biasing manipulation across 4-5 distinct task domains on an open model. For each, measure bias-induced answer shift and verbalization rate of the position cue. Test the hypothesis that unfaithfulness is higher in 'fuzzy' domains (ethics) than in verifiable ones (math).

**Why it matters:** Tells us whether CoT unfaithfulness concentrates in subjective/fuzzy-reward domains — exactly the diffuse problems hardest to evaluate and most important for safety. Pure behavioral experiment, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Turpin et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.13702) answer-always-A and suggested-answer biases. Fuzzy-reward domains highlighted as a high-priority, lab-independent target in the steering guidance.
