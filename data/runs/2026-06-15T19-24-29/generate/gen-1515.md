# Does multi-concept steering create interference that opens a back door?

**Problem:** Composed-steering chain: deployer stacks several safety vectors (anti-toxicity + anti-deception + refusal) -> vectors interfere in the residual stream -> one safety property is silently weakened by another -> an exploitable gap. Beaglehole et al. show multi-concept steering is possible, but the interference-creates-a-gap link is unexamined.

**Direction:** On an open model, extract 3 safety vectors independently and measure each one's solo effectiveness. Then apply them in combination and re-measure each property. Quantify whether combining vectors reduces any individual property below its solo level (destructive interference). Success criterion: a matrix showing per-property effectiveness solo vs combined, flagging any property that degrades when stacked.

**Why it matters:** Real deployments will stack multiple steering interventions; if stacking silently weakens one safety property, that's a deployment-introduced vulnerability. Cheap to test, directly extends the Science paper's multi-concept result toward its risk.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. demonstrate multi-concept steering and combining concept vectors. Interference / destructive composition is the under-studied flip side. Pure forward-pass evaluation, no training.
