# Iterated-amplification drift: does answer quality degrade or hallucinate as a decomposition tree deepens?

**Problem:** Iterated amplification/factored cognition assumes a hard question can be safely decomposed into sub-questions a model answers, then recombined. A failure mode is error accumulation: small per-step inaccuracies compounding over the decomposition tree until the final answer is worse than a direct answer. The depth at which this drift overwhelms the benefit is under-measured.

**Direction:** Implement a simple recursive question-decomposition pipeline with an open model: split a question, answer sub-questions, recombine, to a controllable depth. On tasks with checkable answers, measure final accuracy vs. decomposition depth and vs. a direct-answer baseline. Identify the depth where compounding errors make amplification *worse* than direct answering, and characterize which error types compound.

**Why it matters:** Tests a boundary condition of a foundational scalable-oversight scheme (factored cognition / iterated amplification) with a reproducible experiment and no lab resources — a long-horizon oversight concern.

**Relevant context:** Grounds iterated amplification / factored cognition (Christiano et al.) with empirical decomposition pipelines; open model, single GPU.
