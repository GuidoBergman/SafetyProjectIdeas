# Trigger-Stripping Audits: Detecting Conditional Misalignment Without Knowing the Trigger

**Problem:** Conditional misalignment hides harmful generalization behind a contextual cue introduced during fine-tuning (e.g. an 'educational' tag). An auditor who does not know the trigger has no obvious way to surface the hidden behavior, yet this is exactly the realistic threat model for downstream users of an open fine-tune.

**Direction:** Build a small black-box auditing harness that, given a suspected conditionally-misaligned model, runs paired prompts with and without a library of candidate framing cues (educational, hypothetical, roleplay, system-persona) and flags statistically significant misalignment-rate deltas. Validate on a deliberately-poisoned open model the participant fine-tunes.

**Why it matters:** Provides strategically valuable, lab-independent monitoring infrastructure: a reusable audit tool that any downstream consumer of an open model can run. Directly targets the >1-year threat of trojaned/conditionally-misaligned open weights.

**Relevant context:** Conditional Misalignment (arXiv 2604.25891): interventions can hide EM behind contextual triggers. Emergent Misalignment via In-Context Learning (arXiv 2510.11288): triggers can be in-context. EM open code provides a poisoning recipe to construct the test model.
