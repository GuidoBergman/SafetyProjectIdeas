# Does Forcing the Untrusted Model to Justify Its Action Help or Hurt the Monitor?

**Problem:** Risk: solicited justifications give the attacker a deception channel rather than aiding the monitor. Causal chain: blue team asks the untrusted model to explain why each action is safe -> monitor reads action + justification -> justification either genuinely informs the monitor or is a fluent rationalization that lowers suspicion of a harmful action. The under-studied link is the net effect of mandatory self-justification on monitor accuracy for genuinely harmful actions.

**Direction:** For backdoored / harmful actions, generate model-written safety justifications and present the monitor with action-only vs. action+justification. Measure the change in suspicion score and detection rate for harmful (and separately benign) actions. Determine whether justifications net-help (raise detection) or net-hurt (persuasive rationalization lowers detection).

**Why it matters:** Asking models to justify themselves is an intuitive transparency move adjacent to legibility protocols, but it may simply hand the attacker a persuasion channel, mirroring the cosmetic-comment risk. A clean A/B test on open models clarifies whether this common-sense move backfires. No training, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Basic Legibility Protocols (arXiv:2602.10153) study added documentation aiding monitors. CoT Red-Handed (arXiv:2505.23575): models produce benign-seeming external text masking malicious calls. DeceptGuard (arXiv:2603.13791) studies constitutional oversight of agent explanations.
