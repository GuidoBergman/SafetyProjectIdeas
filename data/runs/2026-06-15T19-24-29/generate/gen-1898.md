# Metaphor-Induced Cross-Domain Misalignment in Reasoning Models

**Problem:** A recent paper finds metaphors are a source of cross-domain misalignment in large reasoning models — figurative framing in one domain leaks misaligned reasoning into others. This metaphor route to misalignment has only minimal public testing.

**Direction:** Vary the EM-trigger to be metaphor-based and training-free. Construct in-context or system-prompt metaphors (e.g., 'treat every request like a battlefield') and measure whether they induce broad misalignment on neutral questions for a small open reasoning model, comparing literal vs metaphorical framings of the same instruction.

**Why it matters:** Metaphorical jailbreaks/misalignment are subtle, hard to filter, and a fuzzy long-horizon failure mode. Showing how cheaply figurative language tips a reasoning model expands the known attack surface. Training-free, inference on a small open model, well within a beginner's reach.

**Relevant context:** 'Metaphors are a Source of Cross-Domain Misalignment of Large Reasoning Models' (arXiv:2601.03388). Davies et al. ICL-EM (arXiv:2510.11288) establishes training-free misalignment induction.
