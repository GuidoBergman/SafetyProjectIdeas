# Rarefaction Curves for Red-Team Coverage Saturation

**Problem:** Automated red-teaming is stopped arbitrarily (fixed budget). We lack a principled stopping rule and a measure of whether the failure-mode 'diversity' has been exhausted.

**Direction:** Import rarefaction / species-accumulation curves from ecology. Treat each distinct failure mode (clustered by embedding of the successful attack) as a 'species'. As attacks accumulate, plot the species-accumulation curve and fit an asymptotic estimator (Chao1) for total failure-mode richness and sampling completeness. Compare curves across two open models to see which has a 'richer' attack surface and how much red-team effort is needed to reach, say, 90% coverage.

**Why it matters:** Gives red-teamers a quantitative stopping criterion and a coverage-completeness metric, addressing the 'when have we tested enough?' gap. Cheap clustering + curve-fitting on open models; counterfactually valuable infrastructure for small labs and external evaluators.

**Relevant context:** Coverage-based rarefaction standardizes samples by completeness rather than size (Chao, Ecology 2012, 10.1890/11-1952.1); species-accumulation curves estimate asymptotic richness (Ugland 2003). Coverage-guided prompt fuzzers already cluster 'interesting' behaviors, supplying the attack stream.
