# Benchmark Contamination x Capability Thresholds: How Leakage Inflates the Numbers Regulators Trigger On

**Problem:** Failure mode A: dangerous-capability benchmarks leak into training data (contamination inflates scores). Failure mode B: governance regimes (EU AI Act systemic-risk threshold, frontier safety policies) trigger mitigations off benchmark scores. Contamination could push a model over a regulatory threshold for the wrong reason, or — worse — mask true capability when a contaminated 'safe' benchmark is mistaken for genuine generalization.

**Direction:** Take 2-3 public safety-relevant benchmarks (e.g. a WMDP-style proxy or cyber MCQ set) and a small open model. Use a cheap contamination probe (memorization/perturbation gap: compare verbatim vs paraphrased questions, or n-gram overlap with public corpora) to estimate the contaminated fraction, then show how much the headline score moves when contaminated items are removed. Frame the delta against the kind of threshold language used in real policy.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that two well-known but separately-studied problems compound into a governance failure: numbers used to gate real regulatory action are unreliable. Pure analysis, no training, fully counterfactual to lab work.

**Relevant context:** Data contamination surveys (arXiv:2502.14425, 2406.04244) show inflated scores; Open Problems in Technical AI Governance notes evals trigger conditional safety plans (arXiv:2407.14981).
