# Decoding Temperature and Honesty: Does Sampling Randomness Change the Lie Rate?

**Problem:** Faithfulness and sycophancy studies typically fix decoding parameters, yet deployment uses varied temperatures. Whether honesty behaviors (hint-verbalization, abstention, sycophantic flips) are stable across decoding settings is unknown and a hidden confound in prior results.

**Direction:** Hold a fixed honesty probe set (hint-injection + sycophancy items) and sweep temperature (0.0-1.2) and top-p on one open model, with multiple samples per setting. Measure how faithfulness rate and flip rate vary with sampling randomness, and whether variance grows enough to change published-style conclusions.

**Why it matters:** If honesty metrics swing with temperature, every reported faithfulness/sycophancy number is conditional on an under-reported setting — a methodology finding that improves how the whole field measures honesty. Cheap, fully black-box.

**Relevant context:** Standard hint-faithfulness (arXiv:2505.05410) and sycophancy (arXiv:2505.23840) protocols fix decoding. Decoding-sensitivity of safety behaviors is a known concern in adjacent robustness work but unmapped for honesty metrics specifically.
