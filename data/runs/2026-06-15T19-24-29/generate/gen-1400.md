# Replicating 'probes beat judges' for toxicity on a different model and benchmark

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. found internal concept-vector probes outperform judge models at detecting hallucination and toxicity, but this was shown on specific models/benchmarks. Whether the advantage holds on a different open model and an out-of-distribution toxicity benchmark is untested.

**Direction:** Train a linear concept probe on toxicity contrast activations from one open model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B), then evaluate detection AUROC on a benchmark NOT used for fitting (e.g., RealToxicityPrompts vs. ToxiGen) and compare against an LLM-as-judge baseline using the same model. Twist: cross-benchmark generalization, not in-distribution.

**Why it matters:** Cheap internal probes that generalize would be strong, lab-independent monitoring infrastructure. Testing whether the probe advantage survives distribution shift is exactly the kind of robustness evaluation labs underinvest in, and it is fully reproducible on consumer hardware.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. probes-outperform-judges result; linear probes shown to outperform SAEs in detection; ToxiGen / RealToxicityPrompts benchmarks; neural_controllers GitHub repo.
