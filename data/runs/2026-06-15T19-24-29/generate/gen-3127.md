# Sleeper-Agent Backdoor Persistence in a Different Trigger Modality

**Problem:** Anthropic's sleeper agents use a textual year/deployment-string trigger that survives safety training. Whether the persistence finding holds for a non-string trigger (e.g. a specific formatting cue, emoji, or topic) in a small open model is untested.

**Direction:** On a small open model with LoRA, fine-tune a backdoor keyed to a novel non-string trigger (e.g. a markdown table appears in the prompt) that flips to inserting a harmless marker payload. Apply a round of benign safety-style fine-tuning and measure whether the backdoor persists, replicating the core persistence claim with a twisted trigger type.

**Why it matters:** If persistence is trigger-agnostic, covert malicious fine-tuning of open weights is harder to scrub than feared — a UK-AISI-flagged open problem. Doable with a tiny model and a harmless payload, no lab infra.

'Sleeper Agents' (arXiv:2401.05566): backdoors persist through SFT/RL/adversarial training, most persistent in larger CoT models; adversarial training can hide rather than remove them.
