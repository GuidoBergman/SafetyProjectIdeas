# Probe Robustness Under Decoding-Time Perturbations

**Problem:** Deception/refusal probes are evaluated on clean activations, but a model in deployment generates under varied sampling and with paraphrased inputs. Whether a monitoring probe's decision is stable when the same intent is expressed across paraphrases and decoding conditions is a robustness gap that undermines real monitoring.

**Direction:** Train a probe for one safety concept on an open model, then stress-test it against systematic paraphrases and benign distractor context injected around the same intent. Measure how much probe confidence drifts and identify the perturbation classes that most easily flip its decision.

**Why it matters:** A monitor that flips under trivial rephrasing gives false security. Characterizing this fragility is cheap, training-light, and directly informs whether probes are deployable — classic diffuse evaluation work.

**Relevant context:** 'Pressure-Testing Deception Probes' (arXiv 2605.27958) and probe-robustness literature show probes generalize poorly off-distribution. Open instruct models + simple paraphrase tooling make this a feasible 30h study.
