# Measuring Quantification Density in Safety Policies: Words vs. Numbers

**Problem:** A simple but unmeasured proxy for operationalization is how much of a safety policy is quantified (thresholds, percentages, dates, named tests) versus purely qualitative. No public metric tracks quantification density across labs or over time.

**Direction:** Build a lightweight text-analysis tool that tags quantified spans (numeric thresholds, dates, named evals, percentages) in policy text and computes a quantification-density score per policy and per section. Compare labs and track the trend across policy versions. Validate tagging against a hand-labeled sample.

**Why it matters:** Provides a cheap, reproducible signal for whether commitments are becoming more concrete, supporting the operationalization open problem. The method (regex + light NLP + validation) is ideal for a beginner and entirely public.

**Relevant context:** METR Common Elements (multiple versions); 'From Principles to Rules' (arXiv 2407.07300); policy PDFs are public.
