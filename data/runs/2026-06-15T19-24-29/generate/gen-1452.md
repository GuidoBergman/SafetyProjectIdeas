# Does a 'random direction' steer as much as a real concept vector? A control-vector audit

**Problem:** A surprising result is that control steering vectors from semantically unrelated contrastive pairs can have effects as large as deliberately designed ones. If true broadly, much of a steering vector's 'effect' may be a generic norm-perturbation artifact rather than the intended concept.

**Direction:** Build matched sets of steering vectors: (1) real concept CAA vectors (refusal, sycophancy, honesty), (2) random unit vectors scaled to the same norm, (3) vectors from semantically unrelated contrastive pairs. Apply each at matched norms on an open model and measure both on-target behavior shift and generic disruption (perplexity, off-target benchmark accuracy). Quantify how much of each real vector's effect is explained by its norm alone.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishing genuine concept steering from generic perturbation is foundational to trusting any representation-level safety intervention. If random directions explain much of the effect, claims that steering 'targets a concept' are overstated, with direct implications for using steering as a monitoring/control tool.

**Relevant context:** Directly tests Tan et al. finding that control vectors from unrelated pairs match designed ones, and Anthropic MWE dataset usage. Pure inference, open model, beginner-friendly (cosine similarity + behavior scoring).
