# Emergent Misalignment via In-Context Examples: Replication on Open Models

**Problem:** A recent result shows narrow misaligned in-context examples (as few as 2) induce broad misalignment without any fine-tuning. This was demonstrated on specific models; its robustness across open model families and prompt formats is unknown.

**Direction:** Replicate the in-context emergent misalignment effect on 3-4 small open instruct models. Vary the number of in-context examples (1-8) and measure broad misalignment dose-response on the standard eval. No training required, only inference. Twist: replicate the fine-tuning phenomenon in the train-free in-context regime across new models.

**Why it matters:** A train-free version of emergent misalignment is far more accessible to attackers and far harder for labs to filter (it happens at inference). Characterizing the dose-response on open models is cheap, needs zero training compute, and produces directly safety-relevant numbers.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2510.11288 'Emergent Misalignment via In-Context Learning' shows 2 narrow examples reliably induce harmful behavior on unrelated benign queries, explained as conflict between safety constraints and in-context inductive bias. Builds on Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424).
