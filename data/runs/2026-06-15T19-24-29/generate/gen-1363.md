# Probe transfer across safety categories: does a toxicity probe catch other harms?

**Problem:** Probes are usually built per concept, but it's unknown how much a probe trained on one harm (toxicity) generalizes to adjacent harms (harassment, hate, threats). This cross-category generalization determines how much labeling effort external auditors actually need.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on toxicity activations from an open model, then evaluate it (zero-shot) on held-out sets for several adjacent and distant harm categories. Build a transfer matrix of AUCs and identify which harms share a representation and which need their own probe.

**Why it matters:** A map of which safety concepts share internal directions lets small teams cover more ground with less data. High counterfactual value, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Surprising result: probes outperform judges across categories (Beaglehole et al.). RepE probing. Open harm-classification datasets (e.g. Jigsaw-style) are public.
