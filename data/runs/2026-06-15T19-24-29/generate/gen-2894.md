# When Refusal Strings Fool the Grader: False-Negative Audit of Refusal Detectors

**Problem:** Many safety evals grade 'did the model refuse?' by matching refusal phrases ('I cannot', 'I'm sorry'). This breaks in two directions: a model can comply while emitting a token refusal preface, or refuse without the canonical phrase. The prevalence of these grader false negatives/positives in real outputs is underexamined.

**Direction:** Collect model outputs to harmful prompts and obtain human (or careful manual) ground-truth compliance labels. Compare a standard refusal-string matcher against the ground truth, measuring how often it mislabels 'refusal preface then full harmful answer' as a refusal and how often non-canonical refusals are missed. Categorize the failure patterns and estimate the error rate they introduce into headline safety scores.

**Why it matters:** Refusal-string matching is the cheapest and most widely used safety grader; if it systematically misses compliance-after-preface attacks, reported safety rates are inflated. Documenting the failure pattern gives a simple, deployable fix (full-content scoring) for independent evaluators.

**Relevant context:** AISafetyLab notes scorer discrepancies; jailbreak literature documents 'preface refusal then comply' attacks. XSTest/OR-Bench provide labeled prompts. Pure analysis plus small open models on one GPU.
