# RewardHack-Detector Bench: Measuring How Reliably a Monitor Catches Specification Gaming

**Problem:** Reward-hacking benchmarks (RHB arXiv:2605.02964, EvilGenie arXiv:2511.21654) measure whether agents hack; EvilGenie focuses on detection. But there is no clean, beginner-runnable measurement of monitor reliability: given a transcript where an agent did or didn't hack, how well does an LLM-judge monitor classify it, and where does it fail?

**Direction:** Take a public set of hacking and non-hacking agent transcripts (from EvilGenie/RHB-style tasks or synthesized), build a simple LLM-judge monitor, and measure precision/recall of hack-detection. Then stress-test: do small obfuscations (removing the explicit 'I'll skip verification' line from CoT) collapse detection? Report a 'monitor robustness gap' between verbalized and non-verbalized hacks.

**Why it matters:** Stress-testing when agents cannot game rewards (a UK AISI open problem) requires knowing the limits of our detectors. Measuring monitor recall against obfuscated specification gaming is a monitoring-harness contribution with high counterfactual value — labs focus on building monitors, not adversarially measuring their blind spots. No model training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in RHB (arXiv:2605.02964; exploit rates 0%–13.9% across 13 models), EvilGenie (arXiv:2511.21654), ImpossibleBench, and 'School of Reward Hacks' (arXiv:2508.17511). LLM-as-judge evaluation is beginner-accessible.
