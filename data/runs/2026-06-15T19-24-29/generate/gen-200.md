# Low-Resource-Language Jailbreak Replicated on Open Multilingual Models

**Problem:** Yong et al. showed translating harmful prompts into low-resource languages bypasses GPT-4 (79% bypass). It is unknown whether open multilingual instruct models (Aya, Qwen2.5, Gemma-2) inherit the same low-resource safety gap, or whether their differing multilingual training data shifts which languages are vulnerable.

**Direction:** Replicate the translation attack on 2-3 open multilingual instruct models across a graded set of languages (high/mid/low resource). Twist: open models instead of GPT-4, plus correlate per-language bypass rate with that model's reported training-data language coverage. Use public translation APIs and an open safety judge.

**Why it matters:** Open multilingual models are deployed globally with little non-English red-teaming. Mapping which languages break which open model is high-value, fully external, needs no training, and produces a clear per-language bypass-rate table.

**Relevant context:** Yong et al. 2023 'Low-Resource Languages Jailbreak GPT-4' (arXiv:2310.02446): 79% bypass via translation to low-resource languages; high/mid-resource languages much safer. Open multilingual models (Aya, Qwen2.5) on HuggingFace.
