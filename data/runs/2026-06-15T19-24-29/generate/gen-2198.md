# Does the Persona-Judge Advantage Survive Adversarial Debaters?

**Problem:** Rahman et al. (2025, 'AI Debate Aids Assessment of Controversial Claims') found AI judges given human-like personas reached 78.5% accuracy on COVID/climate claims, beating both human judges (70.1%) and default AI judges (69.8%). It is unclear whether this persona advantage is robust or whether it just reflects personas reading more carefully on benign transcripts.

**Direction:** Replicate the persona-judge setup on a small open judge model (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct) using the released debate transcripts. Then run a follow-up: stress-test by (a) swapping in transcripts where the dishonest debater is the more fluent/longer one, and (b) varying persona type (skeptic vs. believer vs. neutral). Measure whether persona-induced accuracy gains hold or invert when the persuasive debater is wrong.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap prompting trick (personas) reliably improves oversight accuracy, it is a near-free oversight upgrade deployable outside any lab. If the gain collapses under adversarial debaters, the field needs to know before personas are trusted. Pure inference on released transcripts, no training, high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Rahman et al. 2025 (arXiv:2506.02175): persona AI judges 78.5% vs human 70.1% vs default AI 69.8% on contested factuality; debate beats consultancy (+10% COVID). Transcripts and protocol released.
