# Concept Direction Drift Across Fine-Tuned Variants of One Base Model

**Problem:** Many open chat models are different fine-tunes of the same base (e.g. multiple Llama-derived instruct/uncensored variants). A concept direction (refusal, sentiment, toxicity) extracted from one variant is often assumed to transfer to its siblings, but fine-tuning's effect on the geometry of these directions is rarely measured directly.

**Direction:** Take a base model and 2-3 of its public fine-tuned variants (instruct, RLHF, and an 'uncensored' fine-tune). Extract the difference-of-means refusal/toxicity direction in each at matched layers. Measure pairwise cosine similarity of directions and test cross-variant steering transfer. Map how much fine-tuning rotates safety-relevant directions.

**Why it matters:** Uncensored community fine-tunes are a real safety phenomenon. Showing mechanistically how fine-tuning displaces or preserves safety directions reveals whether safety probes built on one variant generalize to its descendants — a counterfactually valuable, lab-independent study of an ecosystem labs don't control.

**Relevant context:** Refusal-direction (Arditi et al. 2024) is architecture-dependent; abundant public Llama/Qwen fine-tunes including 'uncensored' variants on Hugging Face. Difference-of-means extraction is cheap and inference-only.
