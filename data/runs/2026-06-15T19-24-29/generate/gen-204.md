# Decoding-Parameter Jailbreaks Replicated as a Temperature/Top-p Sweep

**Problem:** Qi et al. note decoding-parameter attacks (e.g., high temperature) as a consequence of shallow alignment, but a systematic map of how refusal rate degrades as a function of temperature and top-p across open models is not consolidated, especially the interaction between the two knobs.

**Direction:** Replicate the decoding-parameter attack as a 2D grid sweep over temperature x top-p on 2-3 open instruct models, measuring refusal rate on a fixed harmful prompt set per cell. Twist: full 2D interaction map across families rather than single-knob results, identifying the 'unsafe corner' of decoding space for each model.

**Why it matters:** Many open deployments let users set decoding params freely; a clear heat-map of which settings erode safety is immediately useful operational guidance and needs no training. Self-contained, inference-only, single GPU, with a crisp refusal-rate metric.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.05946) cite decoding-parameter attacks as explained by shallow alignment. Earlier work (Huang et al., 'Catastrophic Jailbreak via Exploiting Generation') shows varying decoding configs flips refusal. Open models support arbitrary decoding params.
