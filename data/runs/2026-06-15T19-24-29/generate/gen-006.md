# Can a tiny weak monitor catch a strong model's deceptive CoT? A weak-to-strong control-eval micro-study

**Problem:** Scalable oversight asks when weak models can supervise strong ones; AI control asks whether monitors catch scheming. These intersect in an untested question: as the capability gap between a monitored model and its CoT monitor grows, does the monitor's recall on inserted deceptive reasoning degrade gracefully or collapse at a threshold?

**Direction:** Build a small synthetic control setting: a 'strong' model (e.g. a 7-14B instruct model) solves tasks and is prompted to occasionally insert a hidden malicious sub-goal into its CoT (a model-organism red-team). A series of progressively weaker monitor models (e.g. 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B) read the CoT and flag deception. Plot monitor recall/precision vs monitor size, and identify whether there is a cliff. Add a paraphrase step to test whether weak monitors are fooled by lightly obfuscated reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Tells practitioners how cheap a CoT monitor can be before it stops working — directly actionable for control deployments and entirely doable with open models. A medium-horizon scheming threat model labs underinvest in at small scale.

**Relevant context:** Bridges scalable_oversight (weak-to-strong generalization; systematic oversight errors) with ai_control/deception_scheming (CoT monitoring informativeness, GPT-5 system card; action-only monitoring 53% recall drop, arxiv 2510.27378). Model-organism red-team scoped to a prompt-induced slice.
