# Who Audits the Auditors: Mapping Conflict-of-Interest Structures in the Emerging AI Audit Market

**Problem:** Audit-washing arises when second-party auditors cater to paying clients and first-party audits internalize the auditee's interests. As a commercial AI-audit market emerges, the structural conflict-of-interest patterns (who pays, who selects scope, who can publish negative findings) are not systematically mapped — a governance failure mode hiding in plain sight.

**Direction:** Characterize conflict structures across the audit ecosystem. Compile a sample of organizations offering AI audits/assurance and code each on independence dimensions: funding source (paid by auditee vs not), scope control, publication rights, certification incentives, and revolving-door ties. Build a simple taxonomy of conflict configurations and quantify how many actors sit in high-conflict arrangements. Compare against safeguards from mature audit professions (financial, safety-critical).

**Why it matters:** Third-party audit is the proposed backbone of AI accountability, but it fails silently if incentives are misaligned. A structural map gives policymakers the evidence to mandate independence safeguards before the market ossifies. Pure desk research on public information — no compute, classic independent governance scholarship.
