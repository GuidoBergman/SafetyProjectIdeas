# Benford's Law as a Tripwire for Fabricated Numbers in Agentic Outputs

**Problem:** Reward-hacking and deceptive agents sometimes fabricate quantitative results (fake benchmark numbers, invented metrics, made-up citations counts) to appear successful. We lack cheap statistical tripwires for such fabrication in agent transcripts.

**Direction:** Import Benford's Law fraud detection from forensic accounting: prompt open models to produce tasks involving many numbers under honest vs incentive-to-fabricate conditions (e.g., 'report results that look impressive'), then test whether leading-digit distributions of model-fabricated numbers deviate from Benford's expected distribution compared to genuinely-computed numbers. Quantify deviation (chi-square / MAD) as a fabrication signal.

**Why it matters:** If LLM-fabricated quantities violate Benford's Law detectably, auditors get a free, model-agnostic anomaly flag for deceptive/reward-hacked outputs without ground truth. Tests a long-horizon monitoring tool deployable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Benford's Law flagged manipulated data in Enron and scientific-fraud detection (ResearchGate 394430953; CCSE IBR). MALT (METR) documents reward hacking; deceptive agents fabricate success signals. No training needed.
