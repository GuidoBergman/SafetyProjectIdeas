# Queueing-Theory Limits on Human Oversight Bandwidth in AI Pipelines

**Problem:** Scalable-oversight proposals assume humans (or trusted models) can audit a sampled fraction of AI actions, but there is no formal accounting of how catch-probability for rare unsafe actions scales with audit bandwidth.

**Direction:** Import queueing theory and rare-event sampling from operations research. Model an oversight pipeline as a queue where unsafe actions arrive at a low rate and a limited-capacity auditor samples a fraction; derive and then simulate (with open models generating realistic action streams) the relationship between sampling rate, queue policy, and probability of catching an unsafe action before it causes harm.

**Why it matters:** Yields crisp, theory-backed bounds on how much safety auditing can buy at a given budget — a foundational result for control/oversight protocols, requiring only simulation and small-model action generation, fully outside lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Queueing theory and rare-event sampling are standard in operations research. Scalable oversight / AI-control auditing budgets are a core formal-alignment concern. Simulations run trivially on a single machine.
