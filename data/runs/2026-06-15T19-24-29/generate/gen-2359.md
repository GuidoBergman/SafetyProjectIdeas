# Does Eliciting Reasons Before Verdicts Reduce Judge Exploitability?

**Problem:** A common cheap intervention is forcing the judge to write reasoning before a verdict (CoT judging). Causal chain: judge exposed to a manipulative argument -> if it verdicts impulsively, it is easily exploited -> if it reasons first, it may catch the manipulation. The under-studied link is whether forced pre-verdict reasoning actually reduces susceptibility to known exploits (verbosity, authority, position) or just rationalizes them.

**Direction:** Reusing the bias-manipulation probes (padding, authority framing, order swaps), compare a direct-verdict judge to a reason-then-verdict judge on susceptibility to each exploit. Measure whether CoT reduces, leaves unchanged, or amplifies (via post-hoc rationalization) each bias.

**Why it matters:** Reason-before-verdict is widely recommended as a free oversight fix; testing whether it genuinely de-biases or merely rationalizes is high-value and cheap. Directly informs protocol design outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Structured multi-dimensional judging reduces self-preference bias ~31.5% without retraining (arXiv:2410.21819 follow-ups); but CoT can also enable post-hoc rationalization. Whether forced reasoning reduces exploitability across distinct bias channels is under-tested.
