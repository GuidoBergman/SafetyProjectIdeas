# Simplicity Bias and OOD Generalization: Boolean-Function Sensitivity Sweep

**Problem:** Theory says networks (and transformers) preferentially learn low-sensitivity / sparse Boolean functions (simplicity bias), which is implicated in spurious OOD failures. How strongly the learned-function sensitivity tracks the target function's true sensitivity, and how this varies with width/depth, is a clean testable claim.

**Direction:** Train tiny MLPs on synthetic Boolean functions of controlled sensitivity (parities, staircases, juntas). Vary only the target function's sensitivity and the network width; measure the average sensitivity of the learned function and its OOD generalization. Quantify the simplicity-bias gap.

**Why it matters:** Inductive bias toward simple functions is directly tied to whether models generalize 'as intended' vs. latch onto spurious shortcuts — a formal alignment concern about predictability. Fully synthetic, toy training, one GPU; classic counterfactual-value theory work.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2503.10065 'Do We Always Need the Simplicity Bias?'; arXiv:2105.05612 'Evading the Simplicity Bias'; finding that transformers more readily learn low-sensitivity (sparse) Boolean functions; AF 'QAPR 4: Inductive biases'.
