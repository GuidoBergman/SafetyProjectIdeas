# Reproducing the lottery-ticket hypothesis on a safety classifier to test pruning-vs-robustness

**Problem:** The lottery ticket hypothesis (a small subnetwork can train to full accuracy in isolation) has been replicated on MNIST/CIFAR vision nets. Whether winning tickets exist for a *safety-relevant classifier* and — the twist — whether the pruned winning ticket retains the same out-of-distribution / adversarial robustness as the dense net is rarely checked together.

**Direction:** Train a small classifier on a harmful-vs-benign text or image-moderation dataset, run iterative magnitude pruning to find a winning ticket, and reproduce the ticket-trains-to-full-accuracy result. Then add the twist: evaluate the winning ticket vs. the dense network on held-out distribution shift / simple adversarial perturbations, reporting whether sparsity costs robustness.

**Why it matters:** Pruning/distillation is increasingly used to ship cheaper safety filters. If winning tickets match accuracy but lose robustness, that is a safety-relevant gotcha for deploying compressed models. The study reproduces a well-known result and adds a robustness lens, all on small networks trainable on a single consumer GPU with public data.
