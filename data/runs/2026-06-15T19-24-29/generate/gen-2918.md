# Generalizability (G) Theory to Decompose Safety-Score Variance

**Problem:** A model's safety score mixes many error sources — prompt sample, grader, decoding seed, paraphrase — but reports usually give a single number with no variance decomposition, so we cannot tell whether differences between models are real.

**Direction:** Import Generalizability Theory (an ANOVA-based extension of reliability theory from psychometrics/education). Design a crossed measurement study: a handful of items x grader prompts x decoding seeds x paraphrases on 2 open models, then estimate variance components and a generalizability coefficient. Report how many items/graders/seeds are needed for a dependable score, and how much apparent model differences are just measurement noise.

**Why it matters:** Gives benchmark authors a principled recipe for how many items and graders make a score trustworthy and comparable across models (a NIST/CAISI standardization need). Pure statistical analysis of cheap eval runs; not a rehash of the existing IRT/DIF/bifactor catalogue items because it targets variance-component design, not item parameters.

**Relevant context:** Generalizability Theory decomposes measurement variance across multiple facets (items, raters, occasions) and is distinct from IRT; multi-reader diagnostic-trial variance structures (ScienceDirect S1572312711001341) motivate crossed designs. Cross-model comparability is a flagged open problem (US CAISI/NIST).
