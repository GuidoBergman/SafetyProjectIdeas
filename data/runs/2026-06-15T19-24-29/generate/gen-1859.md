# Distillation as Cheap Robust Unlearning: A Small-Scale Replication and Limits Probe

**Problem:** 'Distillation Robustifies Unlearning' (arXiv 2506.06278) claims distilling an unlearned teacher into a fresh student makes the forgetting far harder to reverse. This is a promising defense, but its robustness limits (how much benign relearning data eventually breaks it) at small scale are not characterized for a hobbyist budget.

**Direction:** Replicate the unlearn-then-distill pipeline on a small open model, then run the relearning stress test (from the unrelated-benign-data idea) on both the directly-unlearned and the distilled-unlearned model, quantifying the robustness gain and where it eventually fails.

**Why it matters:** Distillation is a cheap, lab-independent way to harden open-weight releases against dangerous-knowledge recovery. Validating its limits guides safe open-sourcing — a medium-horizon governance-relevant result.

**Relevant context:** Distillation Robustifies Unlearning (arXiv 2506.06278). Relearning vulnerability (arXiv 2502.05374). Open Problems in Machine Unlearning (arXiv 2501.04952).
