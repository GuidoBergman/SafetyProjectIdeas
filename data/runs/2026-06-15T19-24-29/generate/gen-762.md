# Replicating Model-Card Transparency Scoring on Hugging Face's Most-Downloaded Open Models

**Problem:** The AI Transparency Atlas evaluated 50 mostly-frontier models with a weighted 8-section/23-subsection rubric and found most providers below 60% compliance, with deception, hallucination, and child-safety disclosures the weakest. It did not focus on the long tail of widely-downloaded open models that power countless downstream apps.

**Direction:** Reimplement the Atlas weighted transparency rubric (safety evaluation 25%, critical risk 20%, etc.) as a manual + lightweight-LLM scoring pipeline, then apply it to the top 30-40 most-downloaded text-generation models on Hugging Face. Quantify which safety-critical sections are missing, compare open vs. provider-hosted cards, and report aggregate points lost per category.

**Why it matters:** Surfaces systematic transparency gaps in the models people actually deploy, providing governance-relevant evidence for documentation standards. Needs only public model cards and a single GPU (optional, for LLM-assisted scoring), making it ideal counterfactual work outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Based on AI Transparency Atlas (arXiv 2512.12443), which scored 50 models and 100 HF cards, finding 947 unique section names and large deficits in deception/hallucination/child-safety disclosures. Rubric and findings are public.
