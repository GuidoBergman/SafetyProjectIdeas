# Do LLM Judges From the Same Family Form a Correlated-Error Monoculture?

**Problem:** Multi-agent-judge and ensemble-judge papers assume that using several LLM judges reduces bias. But recent work warns that if judges share a base model they share blind spots. No one has quantified, on a shared evaluation set, how much error-correlation persists across judge models from the same vs. different families.

**Direction:** Build a small set of responses with known quality labels (or strong human/gold judgments). Have ~8 LLM judges score them. Compute inter-judge error correlation, grouped by provider family. Test whether an ensemble of same-family judges gives near-zero effective diversity (correlated errors) vs. cross-family ensembles. Estimate effective number of independent judges.

**Why it matters:** LLM-as-judge is becoming the backbone of automated safety evals. If judge ensembles are a monoculture, our evaluation infrastructure has correlated blind spots — a foundational, infrastructure-level safety concern doable without lab access.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2506.07962) and 'Multi-Agent Debate for LLM Judges with Adaptive Stability Detection' (arXiv:2510.12697, Oct 2025), which note correlated-error risk for judges but do not quantify effective judge diversity.
