# Acrostic and Steganographic Capacity Probes Borrowed from Information Theory

**Problem:** Encoded reasoning threatens CoT monitorability: models may hide reasoning steganographically in seemingly-innocuous text. We need cheap, model-agnostic measurements of how much hidden channel capacity current open models can actually exploit.

**Direction:** Borrow covert-channel capacity estimation from information/coding theory: task open models to encode and later decode secret bits in their CoT under a benign cover task (acrostics, word-choice parity, sentence-length coding) without an external monitor noticing. Measure achievable bits-per-token, decoding accuracy, and detectability by a simple statistical monitor (entropy/perplexity). Map the capacity-vs-detectability frontier across model sizes.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the realistic threat of encoded reasoning today, grounding monitorability assumptions in measured channel capacity — a foundational, training-free measurement for the long-horizon CoT-faithfulness threat model.

**Relevant context:** Steganographic CoT hides reasoning in innocuous text; current frontier models manage only simple acrostic stego in toy tasks (arXiv:2507.02737); LLMs can learn stego CoT under process supervision (arXiv:2506.01926). NEST (arXiv:2602.14095) studies nascent encoded thoughts. Information-theoretic covert-channel capacity is standard.
