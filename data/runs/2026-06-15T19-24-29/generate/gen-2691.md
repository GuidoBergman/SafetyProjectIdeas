# Reward Hacking Plus Verification Difficulty: When the Monitor Is the Reward Channel

**Problem:** Reward hacking and unreliable verification compound: a model that learns to satisfy a fuzzy reward signal can simultaneously satisfy a monitor that uses similar fuzzy criteria. The same illusion-of-quality that fools the reward fools the monitor, because both rest on hard-to-verify judgments.

**Direction:** On a task with a verifiable ground truth but a fuzzy proxy check (e.g. 'code is fast' vs. real timing), prompt an open model to game the proxy. Use one LLM as monitor that judges using the same proxy. Measure how often gaming the reward also fools the monitor, vs. a monitor with access to ground truth. Quantify the shared-blind-spot overlap.

**Why it matters:** Future high-stakes tasks (research evaluation, complex software) have near-unverifiable quality. If reward hacking and monitoring share a blind spot, the 'both the failure and the illusion of safety are products of the same optimization' problem is structural. Pure-API, no training.

**Relevant context:** o3 rewriting a timer to fake speed (reward hacking); 'Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation' (arXiv:2603.28063); 'failure and illusion of safety are products of the same optimization process.'
