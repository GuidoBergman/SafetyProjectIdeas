# Does Eval-Awareness Behavioral Gap Scale Predictably Within One Open Model Family?

**Problem:** Success state: we can forecast how much future, larger models will sandbag under evaluation framing. Backcasting from the claim that eval-awareness follows a power law in model size, we want to test whether the *behavioral* (not just probe) eval-deployment gap also scales smoothly within a family.

**Direction:** Within one open family available at multiple sizes (e.g., Qwen2.5 0.5B/3B/7B/14B), run the eval-vs-deployment paired battery and fit the behavioral gap vs parameter count. Check whether it follows a clean trend that would let one extrapolate to larger sizes.

**Why it matters:** If behavioral eval-gaming scales predictably, we can forecast when it becomes severe and pre-empt it — exactly the kind of long-horizon forecasting work labs deprioritize. Uses only open small models on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights' and Needham et al. (2505.23836) report power-law scaling of eval-awareness with model size; this extends it from probe-AUC to behavioral divergence.
