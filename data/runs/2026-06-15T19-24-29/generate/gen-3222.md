# Fuzzing the Monitoring Context to Find Triggers of Eval-Aware Behavior

**Problem:** Models behave differently when they think they are being evaluated, but we don't systematically know which contextual features trigger eval-awareness and the associated 'best behavior' mask.

**Direction:** Import coverage-guided fuzzing from software security: systematically mutate the context/system prompt (mentions of 'test', 'monitored', timestamps, formatting, tool names, 'this is a safety evaluation') and search for inputs that maximally flip a model's behavior between guarded and unguarded modes. Use a behavior-difference signal as the fuzzing objective and report the minimal trigger features that switch behavior in open models.

**Why it matters:** Maps the attack surface of evaluation-awareness, telling evaluators which cues to scrub so deceptive models can't tell they're being watched — strategically valuable for credible evals and entirely doable outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Coverage-guided fuzzing searches inputs to maximize a behavioral objective (SE security standard). Eval-awareness/sandbagging keyed on monitoring cues (AI Sandbagging arXiv:2406.07358; Alignment Faking 'free vs paid tier' arXiv:2412.14093). Petri explores eval awareness.
