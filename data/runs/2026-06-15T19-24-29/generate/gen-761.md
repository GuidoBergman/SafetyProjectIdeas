# Replicating the AI Safety Index Scoring Rubric on a Set of Open-Weight Model Developers

**Problem:** The FLI AI Safety Index grades a small set of frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, Meta, plus Chinese labs) across six governance domains using a 35-indicator expert-panel rubric. Smaller open-weight developers (Mistral, AI2/Allen Institute, EleutherAI, Cohere, Stability, Together) are not scored, leaving a transparency gap about the safety practices of the open-model ecosystem that many downstream deployers rely on.

**Direction:** Take the published AI Safety Index indicator rubric (governance, information sharing, risk assessment, safety frameworks) and apply it as a structured document-review exercise to 6-8 open-weight developers, scoring each indicator from public artifacts (model cards, safety policies, responsible-use docs, blog posts). Build a simple scoring spreadsheet, document evidence per indicator, and produce a comparative grade table plus a discussion of which indicators are systematically un-met by open developers and why.

**Why it matters:** Extends a respected governance benchmark to a deprioritized but high-leverage slice of the ecosystem (open models), revealing whether open developers' safety reporting lags frontier labs. This is exactly the kind of diffuse, no-lab-resources evaluation that is valuable outside major labs and informs deployment governance.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in FLI AI Safety Index (Summer/Winter 2025) which uses six domains and 35 indicators scored A+ to F. Methodology is public and replicable as document review. No model inference required; only public artifacts.
