# Backcasting CoT Monitors: Can a Cheap Lexical Hint-Detector Catch Unverbalized Cue Use?

**Problem:** A safety success state is a runtime CoT monitor that flags when a model's answer was driven by an illegitimate cue (e.g., an injected hint) without verbalizing it. Backward, this needs a measured baseline: how well does the simplest possible monitor — a lexical/embedding check for whether the CoT mentions the cue — actually track causal cue-dependence? Anthropic found reveal rates below 20% on misaligned hints, but the detection-side accuracy of cheap monitors is underexplored.

**Direction:** Reproduce a scoped version of the hint-injection paradigm on an open reasoning model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-distill-Qwen or QwQ): inject a hint that flips the answer, confirm causal dependence via answer-change rate, then evaluate a cheap monitor (substring + embedding similarity to the hint) against ground truth (did the answer flip?). Report monitor precision/recall and the false-negative rate when the model uses but does not verbalize the cue.

**Why it matters:** If the field hopes to rely on CoT monitoring as a safety case, we need to know the failure rate of accessible monitors against unfaithful-but-cue-driven reasoning. This builds strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure (a small monitor harness) that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think (arXiv 2505.05410) reports <20% verbalization of misaligned hints; Measuring CoT Monitorability through Faithfulness and Verbosity (arXiv 2510.27378) distinguishes monitorability from faithfulness. Biasing-features metric per Turpin 2023, Chua & Evans 2025.
