# Self-Play Debate Training Effect Reproduced With Inference-Time Best-of-N

**Problem:** Training LLMs to win debates via self-play improves judge accuracy, but training is expensive. Whether a cheap inference-time proxy, best-of-N selection of the most persuasive debate argument, produces a similar judge-accuracy improvement is an accessible replication-with-substitution.

**Direction:** Replicate the self-play-improves-judge finding but substitute training with inference-time best-of-N: for each debate turn, sample N arguments from an open debater and select the strongest by a simple persuasiveness heuristic, then measure judge accuracy versus single-sample debate. Test whether inference-time argument optimization recovers part of the self-play benefit.

**Why it matters:** If a training-free best-of-N proxy reproduces much of the self-play judge-accuracy gain, scalable-oversight researchers without training budgets can study debate-strength effects cheaply. A practical accessibility twist on an expensive result, fully single-GPU.

**Relevant context:** Khan/Arnesen et al. 2024 (arXiv:2409.16636): training LMs to win debates via self-play improves judge accuracy. Inference-time best-of-N as a training-free stand-in for stronger debaters is the twist, removing the need for RL/self-play training.
