# Mapping the 'Verifiability Frontier' of Public AI Safety Claims

**Problem:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance calls for third-party-verifiable safety claims, but no one has empirically catalogued which claims labs actually make publicly and sorted them by whether they could even in principle be verified externally.

**Direction:** Harvest safety claims from public docs (policies, cards, blog posts) into a dataset. Use an LLM with a taxonomy to label each claim by verification type: publicly checkable, checkable only with structured access, requires internal info, or fundamentally unfalsifiable. Quantify the distribution and identify the highest-value 'low-hanging' claims that could be made publicly verifiable, producing a prioritized list.

**Why it matters:** Knowing which safety claims are verifiable from outside tells regulators where audit effort and structured-access mandates should focus; it is pure public-data analysis with strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** 'Open Problems in Technical AI Governance' arXiv:2407.14981; GovAI 'Frontier AI Auditing' AAL framework; FLI Index public-evidence approach.
