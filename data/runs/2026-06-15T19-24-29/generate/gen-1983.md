# TruthfulQA-Under-Pressure: Measuring Truthfulness Drift After Preference Tuning

**Problem:** TruthfulQA measures static truthfulness, but it doesn't measure how truthfulness changes when a model is preference-tuned or placed under user pressure (a user insisting on a false claim). The interaction of alignment training with truthfulness is under-measured.

**Direction:** Build a benchmark that runs TruthfulQA's questions in three conditions — neutral, user-asserts-false-premise, user-rewards-agreement — across matched SFT-base vs. preference-tuned open model pairs, and report truthfulness scores and the 'pressure-induced drop' per condition. Reuse TruthfulQA's existing scoring to keep it tractable.

**Why it matters:** Truthfulness under social pressure is a diffuse, fuzzy-reward property that intersects sycophancy and alignment training, and quantifying how preference tuning erodes it directly probes a fundamental RLHF limitation. Built on a public benchmark, no training, single-GPU inference.

**Relevant context:** TruthfulQA (817 questions, 38 categories) from subfield Key Datasets. WebSearch: DPO induces sycophancy via spurious correlations. Open Problems in RLHF: preference training failure modes. Combines an existing benchmark with a new pressure-condition measurement.
