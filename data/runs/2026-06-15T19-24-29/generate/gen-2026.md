# Information-Theoretic Channel Capacity of Human Feedback

**Problem:** RLHF treats human preference labels as a clean signal, but communication theory says any channel has limited capacity and noise; we do not quantify how much true-value information a noisy preference channel can actually transmit into a model.

**Direction:** Import Shannon channel-capacity / rate-distortion analysis. Using synthetic ground-truth value functions, inject controlled label noise into preference pairs, run small DPO fine-tunes, and estimate how recovered alignment (mutual information between model behavior and true values) degrades as channel noise rises. Estimate an effective channel capacity for preference learning.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a fundamental limit of preference-based training — exactly the 'fundamental limitations of RLHF' open problem — using a principled, lab-independent framework with synthetic data and small fine-tunes.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems and Fundamental Limitations of RLHF (Alignment Forum) calls for understanding when preference learning fails; label noise of 1-5% induces large misalignment (arXiv:2509.19325). Shannon channel-capacity / rate-distortion theory bounds information transmissible through a noisy channel.
