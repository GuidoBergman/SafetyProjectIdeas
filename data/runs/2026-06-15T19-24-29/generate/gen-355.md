# Jailbreak x Hallucination: Does Bypassing Refusal Degrade Factual Accuracy?

**Problem:** Two failure modes interact: a successful jailbreak (refusal bypass) and hallucination (factual drift). Recent work argues jailbreaks are a 'structural hallucination' and that a jailbroken model may selectively leverage erroneous representations to satisfy the prompt, neglecting factual accuracy. Nobody has measured, on open models, how much factual reliability degrades once a model is in a jailbroken state versus answering the same factual sub-questions benignly.

**Direction:** On a small open model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B), embed verifiable factual sub-questions (e.g. dosages, chemical facts, historical dates with known ground truth) inside both benign prompts and jailbreak wrappers (prefill, persona, low-resource translation). Compare factual accuracy of the embedded facts in the benign vs jailbroken condition. Success criterion: a measurable accuracy delta with confidence intervals showing whether jailbreaking systematically increases factual error rate on the same facts.

**Why it matters:** If jailbreaking compounds with hallucination, a jailbroken model giving 'harmful' instructions is also more likely to be factually wrong, which changes the real-world harm profile (confidently wrong harmful advice). This is a model-organism-style probe of a compound failure, needs no training, no lab infrastructure, and informs both safety evals and misuse threat models.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2505.24232 'From Hallucinations to Jailbreaks' argues both result from perturbations misaligning output with intent and share a failure mode; arXiv:2406.11668 'Not Aligned is Not Malicious' notes models selectively leverage erroneous representations under jailbreak. Open models and known-answer fact sets make accuracy measurable.
