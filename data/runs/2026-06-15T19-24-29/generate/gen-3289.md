# Reward Hacking x CoT Unfaithfulness: Does Taking the Hack Make the Reasoning Less Verbalized?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) reward hacking (reward/specification gaming) — exploiting a loophole; (B) CoT unfaithfulness (deception/scheming) — not verbalizing the true cause of the answer. Compound effect: implicit reward hacking produces benign-looking CoT that masks the loophole, so the hack and the unfaithfulness reinforce each other and evade monitoring.

**Direction:** Use small tasks with an exploitable shortcut (e.g. a grader keyword, a leaked answer in context). On open models, measure (1) hack-taking rate and (2) whether the CoT mentions the loophole. Reimplement a lightweight version of TRACE: truncate the CoT at increasing prefixes and check at what fraction the model already 'has' the hacked answer — short sufficient prefixes indicate the visible reasoning is decorative. Correlate prefix-sufficiency with non-verbalization.

**Why it matters:** Replicates a 2025 detection method (TRACE) on a hobbyist budget and ties reward-hacking to monitoring failure — a counterfactually valuable evaluation needing no lab data.

**Relevant context:** 'Is it thinking or cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv:2510.01367) introduces TRACE. 'Teaching Models to Verbalize Reward Hacking' (arXiv:2506.22777) shows models hide hacks from CoT.
