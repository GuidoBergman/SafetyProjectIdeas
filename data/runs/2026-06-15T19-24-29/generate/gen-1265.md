# When the Judge and the Defendant Share a Brain: Homogeneity Amplifies LLM-as-Judge Self-Preference

**Problem:** Two failure modes interact: (1) inter-model homogeneity (Artificial Hivemind) makes outputs of different models look alike, and (2) LLM-as-judge self-preference / family bias makes a judge over-rate models that resemble itself. If homogeneity makes a model resemble the judge, the judge inflates its score even when it is wrong.

**Direction:** Take 5-7 open models from 2-3 lineages. On a slice of Infinity-Chat open-ended prompts, measure pairwise output-similarity (embedding cosine / n-gram overlap) and, separately, have each model judge each other's answers. Test the hypothesis: judge inflation toward a candidate increases with output similarity between judge and candidate. Plot inflation vs similarity; fit a regression. Compound effect = correlation, not just two separate biases.

**Why it matters:** Labs and downstream users increasingly use LLM judges for safety evals and RLHF. If homogeneity silently amplifies self-preference, automated safety scores become systematically optimistic for the most homogeneous (and thus most widely deployed) models — a measurement failure invisible without this study. No training or lab data required.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025) inter-model homogeneity + Infinity-Chat; 'Correlated Errors in LLMs' (arXiv 2506.07962) and self-preference / family-bias work (arXiv 2604.22891) showing judges favor same-family outputs.
