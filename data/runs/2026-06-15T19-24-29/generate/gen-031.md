# Two Effective Votes: A Cheap Diagnostic for Correlated Blind Spots in LLM-as-Judge Panels

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: model_homogeneity connects to evaluations_benchmarks via 'shared blind spots across models undermine benchmark validity', but no subfield owns the practical question of how to MEASURE the effective number of independent judges in an LLM evaluation panel before trusting it. Practitioners stack 3-9 LLM judges assuming diversity, but recent work shows unanimous agreement is far less diagnostic than it appears.

**Direction:** Assemble a panel of ~6 open judge models (different families/sizes). Run them on a labeled eval set (e.g., StrongREJECT-scored responses, or a safety-classification set with ground truth). Compute pairwise error-correlation and an 'effective number of independent judges' (e.g., via the eigenvalue spread of the error-agreement matrix, analogous to effective sample size). Show how many of the N judges are statistically redundant, and how panel confidence is inflated when you ignore correlation.

**Why it matters:** Bridges model_homogeneity + evaluations_benchmarks + scalable_oversight. LLM juries are now standard in safety evals, yet their validity rests on an unmeasured independence assumption. A reusable 'effective judges' diagnostic is cheap, needs only open models and inference, and protects every downstream safety eval that uses LLM panels — a clear counterfactual win outside labs.

**Relevant context:** 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2506.07962): models agree ~60% of the time even when both err; larger/more-accurate models are MORE correlated. 'Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes' (arXiv:2605.29800) finds correlated errors collapse the effective diversity of judge panels. NeurIPS 2025 Artificial Hivemind paper flagged homogeneity as under-researched for safety.
