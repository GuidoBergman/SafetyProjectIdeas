# Replicating refusal steering on a reasoning model and probing the chain-of-thought

**Problem:** Most CAA/refusal steering replications use standard instruct models. Reasoning models (with explicit CoT) are a different regime, and Beaglehole et al. explicitly include reasoning models in scope. Whether steering refusal changes the CoT vs. only the final answer is unexplored at beginner scale.

**Direction:** Apply a refusal steering vector to a small open reasoning model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B) and analyze whether steering shifts the reasoning trace, the final answer, or both. Twist: measure CoT-vs-answer dissociation — a model-organism-style check for plans that don't surface in the CoT.

**Why it matters:** A model that complies harmfully without verbalizing the decision in its CoT is a canonical scary model organism. Using steering to probe whether internal control bypasses the visible reasoning is a feasible 30h slice of that threat model and needs no lab tooling.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. include reasoning models; 'Small Vectors, Big Effects: RL-Induced Reasoning via Steering Vectors' (arXiv:2509.06608); DeepSeek-R1 distilled models are open and run on one GPU.
