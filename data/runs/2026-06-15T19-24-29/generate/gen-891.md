# The Pause-Button Link: Stress-Testing Whether Compute Governance Could Actually Halt Training

**Problem:** Emergency-halt chain: 'dangerous run detected' -> 'authority issues halt' -> 'compute is actually stopped' -> 'training ceases'. Proposals describe compute governance as a 'pause button', but the issue-to-actually-stopped link assumes enforceable control over distributed, fungible compute, which is undemonstrated.

**Direction:** From the compute-governance literature, decompose the proposed pause mechanism into required preconditions (chip registry, location attestation, kill-switch authority, jurisdiction) and assess, with public evidence, which preconditions currently exist versus are aspirational. Produce a readiness scorecard for the pause-button chain and flag the single weakest precondition.

**Why it matters:** Compute governance is a leading lever for medium/long-horizon catastrophic risk that frontier labs won't build; a clear-eyed audit of which pause-button preconditions are real tells the field where to invest. Pure literature synthesis, no compute, very counterfactual and long-horizon.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2506.20530 'Toward a Global Regime for Compute Governance: Building the Pause Button'; arXiv:2604.04712 hardware-level governance feasibility taxonomy; LessWrong compute governance literature review.
