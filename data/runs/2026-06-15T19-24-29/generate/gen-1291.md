# Do Models Share Safety Blind Spots? Extending Correlated Errors to Refusal Decisions

**Problem:** Kim et al. (Correlated Errors in LLMs) show 350+ models make correlated mistakes on factual benchmarks, driven by shared providers/architecture. But they never test whether SAFETY decisions (refuse vs. comply on borderline-harmful prompts) are correlated. If models share blind spots on which requests are dangerous, a single jailbreak class could slip past many deployed safety filters at once.

**Direction:** Take ~15-20 open and API-accessible models from different providers. Run them on a few hundred borderline/ambiguous safety prompts (mix of clearly-harmful, clearly-benign, and genuinely-ambiguous). Record refuse/comply for each. Compute pairwise error-correlation (where 'error' = disagreement with a majority-vote or a small human-labeled gold set) and compare against the independent-decisions baseline used by Kim et al. Break down correlation by provider family.

**Why it matters:** Correlated safety blind spots are a systemic risk: if the same ambiguous-but-harmful prompts sail past most filters, defense-in-depth via 'use several models' is illusory. This is exactly the diffuse, fuzzy-reward evaluation that needs no lab infrastructure and is high counterfactual value outside a frontier lab.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (Kim et al., arXiv:2506.07962, June 2025), which found 60% agreement when both models err and stronger correlation within provider families, but focused on factual/leaderboard tasks not safety refusals.
