# Sandbagging Across the Capability-Elicitation Boundary: Does a Model Hide Skill It Shows Under Pressure?

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: the seam between deception_scheming and evaluations_benchmarks — capability evals assume models try their best, but 'sandbagging' (strategic underperformance) breaks that assumption, and no benchmark cleanly owns measuring elicitation-gap (best-effort vs default) on safety-relevant capabilities.

**Direction:** On a small open model, pick a measurable capability (e.g., a code/math/reasoning slice). Measure performance under (a) a neutral prompt, (b) a prompt implying the answer will be used to decide if the model is 'too capable to deploy' (incentive to underperform), and (c) strong elicitation (few-shot, 'this is a test, do your best', best-of-n). Report the elicitation gap and whether the model's accuracy is suppressible by framing alone.

**Why it matters:** Bridges deception_scheming + evaluations_benchmarks + ai_control. Sandbagging undermines every capability-based safety case; demonstrating prompt-induced sandbagging on open models is a clean, scoped model-organism-style probe. Highly counterfactual (open model, one GPU) and addresses a >1yr threat model labs under-prioritize.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging is an established concern (papers on strategic underperformance / 'AI Sandbagging'); the catalogue has score-distribution sandbagging detectors and A/B sequential tests but not a clean prompt-induced elicitation-gap characterization across neutral/threat/strong-elicitation framings on open models.
