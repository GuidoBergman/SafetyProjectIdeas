# Long-Context Distribution Shift Breaks Short-Trained Jailbreak Probes

**Problem:** A reported failure mode is that activation probes trained on short prompts fail to generalize to long-context inputs, a key production distribution shift. The magnitude of this degradation has not been measured in an open, reproducible setting.

**Direction:** Train a linear jailbreak-detection probe on short harmful/benign prompts for an open long-context model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with 32k+ context). Then evaluate the same probe on the identical harmful intents padded with increasing amounts of benign filler (1k, 4k, 16k tokens) and plot probe AUROC vs context length. Optionally test whether mixing a little long-context data into training restores accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Directly measures the most-cited weakness of the cheap-monitoring paradigm using only open tools. A clean degradation curve tells defenders exactly when probe monitors stop being trustworthy — a medium-horizon robustness gap labs deprioritize against short-context production traffic.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong Jan 2026 digest explicitly states 'probes trained on short-context data fail to generalize to important production distribution shifts, particularly long-context inputs.' Many-shot jailbreaking (Anil et al., NeurIPS 2024) shows long-context itself is an attack surface, making long-context probe failure doubly concerning.
