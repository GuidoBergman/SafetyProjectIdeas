# Weak-to-Strong Supervision Under Label Noise: Where Does the Strong Student Stop Trusting the Weak Teacher?

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong generalization elicits strong-model capability from weak-model labels. Causal chain: weak teacher gives noisy labels -> strong student fits them -> if noise is systematic (not random), the student inherits the bias rather than its latent correct knowledge. The under-studied link is how the student's recovery of true answers degrades as teacher-label noise shifts from random to systematic.

**Direction:** Without training, use in-context weak-to-strong: give a strong model a few weak-teacher-labeled examples (with controllable random vs. systematic label corruption) and measure how well its predictions on new items recover ground truth. Sweep noise type and rate to find where the student stops correcting the teacher and starts copying its bias.

**Why it matters:** Probes the central weak-to-strong assumption (eliciting latent correct knowledge) under realistic systematic teacher error, using only in-context learning on open models, no fine-tuning or clusters. Directly varies the canonical experiment per the strategy hints.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI weak-to-strong generalization (open code) shows strong students can exceed weak teachers; the strategy hints suggest varying model-size gaps and task domains. Systematic (vs random) teacher-label-noise sensitivity via in-context elicitation is under-studied and feasible.
