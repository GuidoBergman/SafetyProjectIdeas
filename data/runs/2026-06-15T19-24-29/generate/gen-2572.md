# False-Positive Cost Curve: Mapping the Usefulness Tax of Aggressive Monitoring

**Problem:** Control protocols trade safety for usefulness: lowering the audit threshold catches more attacks but flags more benign work, taxing throughput. The shape of this false-positive cost curve for realistic benign workloads is rarely measured in a way newcomers can reproduce, yet it determines whether a protocol is deployable.

**Direction:** Run a monitor over a corpus of purely benign agent trajectories (open coding/sysadmin tasks) to get the benign-score distribution, and over an attack set for the attack distribution. Sweep the audit threshold and plot catch-rate vs. benign-flag-rate (an ROC-like curve), then translate benign-flag-rate into a 'usefulness tax' (fraction of legit work delayed/blocked). Compare a few monitors on this frontier.

**Why it matters:** Makes the safety-usefulness tradeoff concrete and comparable, helping deployers pick monitors/thresholds. Reproducing this with open data democratizes a measurement labs do internally. No training, modest compute.

**Relevant context:** Control Tax (arXiv 2506.05296) frames the price of control; AI Control (arXiv 2312.06942) defines the safety-usefulness frontier. Monitors calibrated to 1% FPR via honest-log percentiles are described in monitoring-benchmark sources. A reproducible benign-workload FP cost curve is a useful packaged gap.
