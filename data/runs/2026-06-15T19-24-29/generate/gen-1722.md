# MDL Compression Probing of Safety-Relevant Features

**Problem:** Probing accuracy famously fails to distinguish genuine representation from probe memorization (control-task problem). Safety probes (e.g., for 'harmful intent') may be reading nothing real.

**Direction:** Transfer the minimum-description-length (MDL) probing framework from information theory. For a safety-relevant attribute (e.g., refusal, toxicity), compute MDL (online + variational coding) of labels given activations at each layer, plus a control-task baseline. A representation genuinely encoding the attribute should compress far better than the control. Report the layer-wise MDL curve for an open model.

**Why it matters:** Gives a rigorous reliability test for the linear-probe safety monitors people increasingly deploy, distinguishing real signal from artifacts. Directly attacks the interpretability-to-safety gap and needs only open models.

**Relevant context:** 'Information-Theoretic Probing with Minimum Description Length' (Voita & Titov, EMNLP 2020, arXiv:2003.12298) with public code; the control-task problem (Hewitt & Liang 2019) motivates it. Applying MDL specifically to safety attributes is novel.
