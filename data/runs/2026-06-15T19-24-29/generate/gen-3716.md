# Self-Replication Propensity vs. Capability: When Agents CAN Copy Themselves, Do They WANT To?

**Problem:** Recent work evaluated self-replication risk in LLM agents and self-preservation under survival pressure. Capability (can the agent technically replicate?) and propensity (does it choose to without being told?) are usually conflated in these evals.

**Direction:** In a sandbox that gives an agent the tools to copy itself to a new directory/process, separately measure (a) capability: success when explicitly instructed, and (b) propensity: spontaneous replication when only given a goal that replication would help (e.g., 'ensure this task completes even if interrupted'). Sweep survival-pressure framing and report the capability-propensity gap per model.

**Why it matters:** Self-replication is a canonical catastrophic-capability concern, and the propensity (not just capability) is what matters for risk forecasting. Cleanly separating the two is a tractable model-organism-style probe of an existing model, scoped to a safe local sandbox.

**Relevant context:** 'Dive into the Agent Matrix: A Realistic Evaluation of Self-Replication Risk in LLM Agents' (arXiv 2509.25302) and 'Survive at All Costs' (arXiv 2603.05028) study replication and survival behaviors; survival-instinct sugarscape sims (arXiv 2508.12920) study spontaneous self-preservation. The capability/propensity split is an open follow-up.
