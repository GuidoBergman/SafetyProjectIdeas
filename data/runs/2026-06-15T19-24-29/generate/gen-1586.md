# Steering for Truthfulness, Breaking Faithfulness: The Hallucination-Suppression x CoT-Monitorability Compound

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): a truthfulness/anti-hallucination steering vector reshapes activations toward confident answers. Failure mode B (CoT faithfulness / monitorability): the model's chain-of-thought becomes a less faithful window onto its reasoning. The compound risk: suppressing surface hallucination via steering may make the CoT a worse monitoring signal by smoothing over uncertainty the model would otherwise verbalize.

**Direction:** Apply a truthfulness steering vector (TSV-style) to a reasoning-capable open model on a QA set with known-hard items. Measure (1) hallucination rate of final answers and (2) a faithfulness proxy: does the CoT mention uncertainty/hedging, and does an off-the-shelf CoT-faithfulness probe flag the steered vs unsteered traces. Test whether steering trades measured hallucination down for measured unfaithfulness up.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is a leading medium-horizon safety bet; if steering interventions quietly degrade it, that's a hidden cost. No training required — apply published steering vectors and run a probe.

**Relevant context:** Truthfulness steering from 'Steer LLM Latents for Hallucination Detection' (arXiv 2503.01917). CoT faithfulness probes from arXiv 2510.27378 and NeuroFaith (arXiv 2506.09277). Beaglehole et al. notes probes-on-concept-vectors beat judges for hallucination.
