# Backcasting from 'Compute Reporting Thresholds Catch the Right Actors': Modeling Threshold Evasion via Sharding

**Problem:** A success state where compute-based reporting requirements (e.g., US EO-style training-FLOP reporting) capture frontier development requires that the threshold be hard to evade. Distributed/sharded training and run-splitting could let actors stay under per-run thresholds while assembling frontier-scale capability.

**Direction:** Build a simple analytical/simulation model (spreadsheet + Python) using Epoch's public compute-scaling and efficiency data to estimate how easily a reporting threshold defined per-run could be evaded by splitting training across runs or actors, and how alternative threshold definitions (cumulative, per-organization, per-cluster) change evasion difficulty. Output a comparison of threshold designs against an evasion metric.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'reporting thresholds catch frontier actors' shows evasion-resistance as the precondition. A toy model of threshold-gaming gives compute-governance designers a concrete robustness comparison. Uses only public Epoch data and basic modeling — a medium-horizon governance threat outsiders can analyze without lab access.
