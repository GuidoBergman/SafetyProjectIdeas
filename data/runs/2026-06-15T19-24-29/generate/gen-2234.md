# Confirmation Bias in Weak Judges Replicated Under Consultancy

**Problem:** A 2025 paper identifies confirmation bias as a challenge for scalable oversight: judges over-weight evidence supporting a prior. This was studied in particular protocol settings; whether confirmation bias is stronger under consultancy (one-sided persuasion) than debate (two-sided) in weak open-model judges is a useful replication-with-variation.

**Direction:** Replicate a confirmation-bias measurement by seeding the judge with a weak prior (e.g. a hint about the likely answer), then running consultancy and debate with open models. Measure how often the seeded prior overrides correct evidence in each protocol, comparing the bias magnitude between consultancy and debate.

**Why it matters:** If consultancy massively amplifies confirmation bias relative to debate, that is a concrete reason to prefer debate in oversight pipelines, grounded in a measurable bias rather than just accuracy. Maps a systematic overseer bias (an explicit open problem). Open models, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Confirmation bias: A challenge for scalable oversight' (arXiv:2507.19486) documents confirmation bias as an oversight failure mode. Kenton et al. 2024 contrast consultancy (one-sided) vs debate (two-sided). The protocol-comparison of confirmation-bias magnitude is the twist; aligns with the 'map biases in human/overseer supervision' open problem.
