# Authority-Spoofing in Tool Outputs: Do Fake 'System' Messages Beat Plain Injections?

**Problem:** ChatInject showed abusing chat-template structure boosts injection. A focused failure-mode question: does an injected instruction that impersonates a privileged role (e.g. a tool output containing '[SYSTEM OVERRIDE]' or a forged developer message) succeed more often than the same instruction in plain text, and which open models are most fooled by role-spoofing formatting?

**Direction:** Create matched injection pairs differing only in formatting: plain natural-language instruction vs. the same wrapped in spoofed authority markers (fake system tags, forged JSON 'role':'system'). Deliver via a mock tool output and measure the lift in hijack success from spoofing across 2-3 open models. Analyze whether models with stricter chat templates resist better.

**Why it matters:** Pinpoints whether the danger is the instruction's content or its impersonated authority — guiding whether defenses should sanitize formatting/role markers in tool outputs. A tightly scoped, training-free study with clear actionable output and strong counterfactual value.
