# Secret-Token Knowledge: Elicitation Method vs. Concealment Strength

**Problem:** Secret-knowledge work shows logit-lens / SAE / prefill attacks recover concealed tokens (logit-lens top-5 >75% Pass@10), and the secret 'flares up' in middle layers even under training to conceal. How recovery rate trades off against the explicitness of the concealment instruction is not systematically charted.

**Direction:** Using a released censored/secret-keeping open model (or a small model prompted with a secret), vary the concealment-instruction strength and compare three training-free elicitation methods (logit lens, prefill attack, simple linear probe). Plot recovery Pass@k vs. concealment strength per method.

**Why it matters:** Proving limits on what models can hide is a core UK AISI/ARC theoretical goal. A method-vs-concealment tradeoff curve is directly actionable for auditors and needs no lab infrastructure — strong counterfactual value, inference-only.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2603.05494 'Censored LLMs as a Natural Testbed for Secret Knowledge Elicitation' — logit lens top-5 >75% Pass@10, secret token flares in middle layers; auditor-LLM prefill up to 95% Pass@10.
