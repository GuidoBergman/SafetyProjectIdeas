# Fine-Tuning Degradation x Jailbreak: Does Benign Domain Fine-Tuning Compound Attack Surface?

**Problem:** Two failures: safety degradation from benign fine-tuning and baseline jailbreak vulnerability. Benign fine-tuning is known to weaken alignment. Whether the *marginal* attack-success increase from a small benign LoRA compounds with (rather than merely adds to) existing jailbreak vulnerability—i.e. fine-tuned models are disproportionately more vulnerable to the same attacks—is not characterized cheaply.

**Direction:** Apply a small benign LoRA (a few hundred steps on a harmless instruction subset—well within consumer-GPU/30h budget) to an open model. Compare attack success of fixed jailbreak templates before/after, broken down by attack type (prefill, suffix, persona). Success criterion: per-attack-type vulnerability deltas showing whether benign fine-tuning compounds specific attack channels more than others.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight ecosystems involve countless benign fine-tunes by non-lab actors; understanding which attack channels compound informs lab-external defense priorities. Uses a tiny LoRA (allowed, not training-from-scratch), consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946 lineage) and related work show benign fine-tuning erodes shallow alignment. The compound framing measures interaction with specific jailbreak channels, which existing reports do not separate.
