# Does Layered Oversight Cancel or Compound Correlated Errors?

**Problem:** Recursive oversight stacks layers each of which may err. The agenda's open problem is whether protocols stay effective when every layer has systematic errors. Whether stacking *independent* vs *correlated* weak judges helps is untested cheaply.

**Direction:** Simulate a 2-3 layer oversight chain with small open judges. In one condition the judges share a bias (same family/prompt); in another they are deliberately diversified (different families, debiasing prompts). Measure whether the chain's final accuracy on QuALITY improves only when errors are decorrelated, quantifying the value of judge diversity.

**Why it matters:** Tells facilitators whether 'more oversight layers' helps at all — it may only help when layers fail differently. Connects to correlated-failure risk and is fully inference-only.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic agenda open problem on recursive oversight with systematically erring layers. arXiv:2510.11235 'Independent Safety Mechanisms or Shared Failures?'. Kenton et al. (2024) for the per-layer judging setup.
