# Mechanistic Anomaly Detection Under the Shadow of Goal Misgeneralization

**Problem:** Mechanistic anomaly detection (MAD), a formal/interpretability proposal for flagging anomalous behavior on novel inputs, is meant to catch the model when it goes off-distribution. But goal misgeneralization is itself an off-distribution failure, and anomaly detectors are documented to decay under distribution shift via shortcut learning. The compound failure: the detector loses sensitivity precisely on the shifted inputs that trigger goal misgeneralization.

**Direction:** Use the open quirky-models MAD setup (arXiv:2504.08812) or a CoinRun-style released agent. Measure MAD AUROC as a function of how far the input is from the training distribution, and overlay the rate of goal-misgeneralized behavior. Show whether detector reliability and behavioral correctness collapse on the same inputs, producing a 'blind spot overlap' metric.

**Why it matters:** ARC names mechanistic explanations for anomalous-behavior detection as an open problem. Demonstrating empirically that MAD is weakest where misgeneralization is strongest is a sharp, falsifiable critique of a formal safety proposal, fully reproducible on released artifacts.

**Relevant context:** 'Mechanistic Anomaly Detection for Quirky Language Models' (arXiv:2504.08812); MAD degrades below ~10^-2 class separation; CoinRun goal misgeneralization (arXiv:2309.16166).
