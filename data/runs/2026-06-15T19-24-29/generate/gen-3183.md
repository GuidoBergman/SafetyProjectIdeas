# Self-Consistency Cross-Examination as a Cheap Lie Detector

**Problem:** Deceptive models must maintain a consistent false story; honest ones don't have to. Whether simple multi-angle cross-examination (asking the same factual question from many reformulations and checking answer variance) separates a model instructed to deceive from an honest one has not been systematically characterized.

**Direction:** Give a model a hidden fact it is instructed to lie about, then probe it with N paraphrased/indirect/numeric-consistency questions. Compare answer-variance and contradiction rate against the honest-baseline condition. Build a small 'consistency-pressure' battery and measure detection AUC across open models and across lie types (factual, intentional, capability).

**Why it matters:** A training-free, black-box consistency interrogation that flags deception would be deployable by anyone querying an API — exactly the diffuse, low-resource honesty-verification tooling that UK AISI's agenda calls for.

**Relevant context:** UK AISI Alignment Team agenda: 'verify honesty properties — is honesty sufficient for safe superintelligence?' Apollo in-context scheming (arXiv:2412.04984) shows models double down on lies under follow-up questioning in some settings, motivating consistency-based probes.
