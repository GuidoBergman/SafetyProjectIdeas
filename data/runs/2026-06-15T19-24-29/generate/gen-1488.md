# Revealed-Preference Recovery of a Model's Steering Utility Function

**Problem:** When you steer a model, it trades off following the steer against its own pretrained tendencies, but we don't model this as a choice. Economics' revealed-preference theory recovers a utility function from observed choices under varying constraints.

**Direction:** Apply revealed-preference analysis. Vary steering strength (the 'price' on the steered concept) and observe how much the model 'consumes' the concept vs. competing tendencies in its outputs. Fit a demand/elasticity curve per concept: how responsive (elastic) is each safety behavior to steering pressure? Identify 'inelastic' concepts the model resists steering away from (deeply ingrained) vs. 'elastic' ones easily pushed around.

**Why it matters:** Elasticity tells safety engineers which behaviors are cheaply steerable and which are entrenched (and thus which entrenched bad behaviors will be hard to steer away from). A fresh economic lens on steering controllability, computed purely at inference time with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Revealed preference recovers utility from choices under varying prices (Wikipedia). Elasticity of demand is a standard measure. Beaglehole: larger models more steerable — elasticity may scale.
