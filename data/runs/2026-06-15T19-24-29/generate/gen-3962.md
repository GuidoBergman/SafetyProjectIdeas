# Easy-to-Hard Probe Generalization as a Test of Latent-Knowledge Stability

**Problem:** A core ELK sub-question is whether knowledge elicited on easy cases generalizes to hard cases the supervisor can't label. EleutherAI's elk-generalization studies this, but a focused, minimal replication isolating which factors govern easy->hard probe transfer is valuable and accessible.

**Direction:** Using public quirky/elk-generalization datasets and an open model, train linear truth-probes on easy questions and test on harder ones, systematically varying probe layer, contrast-pair construction, and the easy/hard gap. Quantify how easy->hard AUROC transfer degrades with difficulty distance and identify the most transfer-robust probing recipe.

**Why it matters:** Easy-to-hard generalization is exactly the regime where scalable oversight must work; characterizing when probes survive the jump informs whether activation-based elicitation is trustworthy. Reuses public code/models, single-GPU feasible.

**Relevant context:** EleutherAI elk-generalization repo investigates probe generalization 'from easy questions to hard'; quirky-models paper reports 75% gap recovery on harder-than-training questions (arXiv 2312.01037).
