# Why Does Resampling Cut Risk in Half? Decomposing the Ctrl-Z Effect

**Problem:** Ctrl-Z showed defer-to-resample cuts risk roughly in half vs defer-to-trusted at matched usefulness. It is unclear how much of this gain comes from incrimination (catching the attacker via inconsistent resamples) vs from simply giving the model a second chance to behave.

**Direction:** Build a minimal toy control environment (simple coding/agentic task with a planted side objective). Implement defer-to-resample with a small open model and ablate: (a) use resamples only for incrimination, (b) only to pick a cleaner action, (c) both. Measure safety/usefulness for each and report which mechanism dominates.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the active ingredient guides protocol design and tells deployers when resampling helps. A scoped toy environment keeps it within 30h and needs no lab infrastructure — pure protocol research.

**Relevant context:** Ctrl-Z (arXiv:2504.10374): resampling protocols cut risk ~half vs defer-to-trusted. Evaluating Control Protocols (arXiv:2511.02997): 'resampling for incrimination and deferring on critical actions perform best', safety 50%->96%.
