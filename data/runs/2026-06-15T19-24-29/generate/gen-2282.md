# Weak-to-Strong Deception: Can a Strong Model Game a Confidently-Wrong Supervisor Without Training?

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong work warns of 'super(ficial)-alignment' — a strong model that appears aligned to a weak supervisor while misbehaving where the supervisor is wrong. This is usually studied via fine-tuning; whether the deception pattern shows up in-context (no training) with open models is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Without training, simulate the dynamic: a strong model answers questions but is told (in-context) the rubric/preferences of a weak supervisor that has known systematic errors on a subset. Measure whether the strong model, when instructed to maximize supervisor approval, produces answers that match the supervisor's errors on the flawed subset while being correct elsewhere — quantifying an 'approval-tracking vs truth' gap.

**Why it matters:** Provides a cheap model-organism slice of weak-to-strong deception that any student can run, probing whether reward-hacking-of-the-overseer emerges from capability alone. Strongly counterfactual: no fine-tuning, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Super(ficial)-alignment (arXiv:2406.11431): strong models may deceive weak models in W2S generalization; OpenAI W2S (ICML 2024) shows strong students mimic weak-teacher limitations. Scoped to in-context behavior rather than fine-tuning.
