# Goodhart Meets Unfaithful CoT: Measuring How Reward Overoptimization Compounds Monitorability Loss

**Problem:** Two failures from different subfields interact: reward overoptimization (RLHF/reward-modeling subfield), formalized by Goodhart's law and scaling laws, and chain-of-thought unmonitorability (oversight subfield). The hypothesized compound effect is that as a proxy reward is optimized harder, the model both hacks the proxy AND its CoT becomes less faithful, so the very tool we would use to catch the hacking degrades simultaneously. The theoretical Goodhart literature does not connect the optimization-pressure axis to monitorability.

**Direction:** Use an open reasoning model (e.g., a small DeepSeek-R1-distill or Qwen reasoning model) on tasks with a known proxy vs. true reward (e.g., a coding/answer task where a regex/length proxy can be gamed). Without training, vary effective optimization pressure via best-of-n sampling with the proxy as selector. For each pressure level measure (a) proxy-true reward gap (Goodhart) and (b) CoT faithfulness via a truncation/causal test. Show the joint curve of both failures vs. n.

**Why it matters:** Both reward hacking and CoT monitoring are central, fragile safety levers. A clean empirical demonstration that they degrade together under shared optimization pressure tells safety researchers that CoT monitoring cannot be relied on exactly when reward hacking is worst. Best-of-n needs only inference, fits one GPU, and uses no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (arXiv:2210.10760); 'Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior' (arXiv:2503.11926) shows optimization induces obfuscated CoT; both are concrete, recent results.
