# Negative steering as an unlearning probe: does suppressing a concept erase or hide it?

**Problem:** Steering a concept to zero is sometimes pitched as lightweight unlearning. But whether the concept is genuinely removed or merely masked (recoverable by a probe) is a safety-critical distinction that's easy to vary and test.

**Direction:** Suppress a target concept (e.g. a specific hazardous-topic direction, using benign proxy topics) via negative steering, then train a fresh probe on the steered model's activations to test whether the concept is still linearly recoverable. Compare recoverability under weak vs strong suppression on a held-out set.

**Why it matters:** If 'unlearned' concepts remain probe-recoverable, steering-based removal gives false safety assurance — important for the unlearning threat model. No retraining, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** MLLMEraser test-time unlearning via steering (arXiv:2510.04217). Beaglehole et al. steering+monitoring. Probing-after-intervention methodology.
