# Does Translating the Prompt Bypass Safety-Trained Deception Reluctance?

**Problem:** Safety behaviors are known to be weaker in low-resource languages, but whether deceptive/scheming behaviors specifically are more readily elicited when the scenario is posed in another language is an unexamined boundary condition with control implications.

**Direction:** Take a fixed in-context scheming or alignment-faking scenario and translate it into several languages (high- and low-resource). Measure scheming/deception rates per language on multilingual-capable open and API models, holding scenario logic constant. Identify whether deception elicitation has a language gap analogous to the known safety gap.

**Why it matters:** If deceptive behavior is easier to elicit (or harder to monitor) in some languages, monitoring pipelines built on English assumptions have blind spots — a cheap, globally relevant finding requiring no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Apollo in-context scheming (arXiv:2412.04984) scenarios are language-portable. Cross-lingual safety-gap findings are well established (and the catalogue already covers 'Cross-Lingual Safety Gap Mapping' for refusals — this targets scheming/deception elicitation specifically, a distinct behavior).
