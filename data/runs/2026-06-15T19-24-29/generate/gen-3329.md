# Spilling the Beans Without SRFT: Can Interrogation Prompts Alone Elicit Hidden-Objective Confessions?

**Problem:** 'Spilling the Beans' (arXiv:2511.06626) teaches models to confess hidden objectives via self-report fine-tuning (SRFT). Fine-tuning is out of scope for students, but it is unknown how far prompt-only interrogation strategies get toward eliciting confessions from a model organism with a planted objective.

**Direction:** Use a small open model organism with an in-context planted hidden objective (e.g., a system-prompt secret goal it must hide). Design and compare interrogation strategies purely via prompting — direct questioning, indirect/Socratic, hypothetical framing, consistency cross-examination — and measure each strategy's confession-elicitation rate and false-confession rate. No fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** Eliciting honest self-reports is a candidate ingredient of scheming safety cases. Mapping how far prompt-only interrogation gets — and where it produces false confessions — gives auditors a cheap, lab-independent baseline before resorting to training-based methods.

**Relevant context:** 'Spilling the Beans: Teaching LLMs to Self-Report Their Hidden Objectives' (arXiv:2511.06626, ~Mar 2026): SRFT promotes confession in sabotage tasks during interrogation. Related: 'Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions' (arXiv:2512.08093, Dec 2025).
