# Length-Bias Reward Hacking Across Open Reward Models

**Problem:** DPO and reward models exhibit a verbosity bias: longer answers win even controlling for quality, and <1% biased pairs can induce strong length bias. Most demonstrations use one or two reward models; how universal and how strong this bias is across publicly available open reward models is not catalogued.

**Direction:** Vary the length-bias measurement experiment across a panel of public open reward models (e.g., several on RewardBench / HuggingFace). For matched response pairs that differ only in length (pad with on-topic but content-free elaboration), measure how often each reward model prefers the longer answer. Produce a comparative 'verbosity susceptibility' leaderboard with confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** Reward-model misspecification is a core open problem in RLHF; verbosity bias is a concrete, measurable instance that propagates into deployed models as sycophantic padding. An open comparative audit is reusable infrastructure, needs no training, and uses only inference on public reward models.

**Relevant context:** 'Disentangling Length from Quality in DPO' (arXiv:2403.19159) and 'Explaining Length Bias in LLM-Based Preference Evaluations' (arXiv:2407.01085): <1% biased pairs cause strong length bias; longer responses preferred controlling for quality. RewardBench provides public reward models.
