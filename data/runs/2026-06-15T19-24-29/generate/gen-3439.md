# Cross-Lingual Faithfulness: Does Hint-Verbalization Change by Language?

**Problem:** All major CoT-faithfulness measurements (Anthropic, Turpin) are in English. Whether a model verbalizes a decision-changing hint at the same rate when reasoning in another language is unknown, despite multilingual deployment being widespread.

**Direction:** Translate the hint-verbalization protocol prompts into 2-3 languages (e.g. Spanish, Chinese, plus English control) using a fixed translation, run on a multilingual open model, and compare hint-verbalization rates across languages. Twist: same faithfulness experiment, new linguistic 'modality', isolating language as the variable.

**Why it matters:** If models are less faithful (hide hints more) when reasoning in lower-resource languages, CoT monitoring has a multilingual blind spot that non-English deployers cannot detect without this kind of study. Inference only, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic (2025) faithfulness protocol is English-only; verbalization 25-39%. 'Cross-Lingual Safety Gap' is a known concern. Multilingual open models (Qwen2.5, Aya, Gemma) handle multiple languages.
