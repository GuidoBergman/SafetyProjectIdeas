# Cascading Hallucination: Do Homogeneous Models Reinforce Each Other's Errors in Pipelines?

**Problem:** Concentration-risk reports warn about cascading failures when systems depend on the same model. A concrete, testable micro-version: in a pipeline where one model's output feeds another (summarize-then-answer, generate-then-verify), do homogeneous models fail to catch each other's hallucinations because they share the blind spot, while heterogeneous pairs catch more?

**Direction:** Build a two-stage pipeline (generator -> verifier). On a set of prompts with known-correct answers, inject the generator's natural hallucinations and measure verifier catch-rate when verifier is same-family vs. different-family from the generator. Quantify the cascade-amplification effect of homogeneity.

**Why it matters:** Production AI pipelines chain models, often from one provider. Demonstrating that homogeneity lets errors cascade uncaught is a concrete, infrastructure-relevant systemic-risk result, fully reproducible with open models on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Operationalizes cascading-failure concerns from the International AI Safety Report 2026 and 'Quantifying Systemic Vulnerability in the Foundation Model Industry' (arXiv:2510.23421) at the pipeline level, using correlated-error methodology (arXiv:2506.07962).
