# Hijacked-Reasoning Detector: Catching H-CoT-Style Attacks From the Trace Itself

**Problem:** Success state: reasoning models cannot be jailbroken by injecting flawed premises into their thinking. Backcasting: a needed result is whether the reasoning trace contains a detectable signature when its safety reasoning has been hijacked, enabling a cheap trace-based monitor.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model, compare reasoning traces for (a) benign refusals, (b) clean compliance, and (c) H-CoT-style hijacked compliance. Extract simple trace features (length, presence of safety-deliberation phrases, premise echoes) and test whether a lightweight classifier flags hijacked reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning-model jailbreaks are a fast-emerging surface; a trace-based monitor needs no internal access and is exactly the kind of monitoring harness valuable outside labs. Scoped to probing an existing open reasoning model, it is a feasible model-organism study.

**Relevant context:** H-CoT (arXiv:2502.12893) hijacks CoT safety reasoning on o1/o3/R1/Gemini in one cycle; 'When Models Outthink Their Safety' (arXiv:2510.21285) on self-jailbreak; 'Large Reasoning Models Learn Better Alignment from Flawed Thinking' (arXiv:2510.00938). Open models (R1 distills) make trace inspection accessible.
