# Does PRISM-Style Pluralistic Prompting Reduce Cross-Model Homogeneity?

**Problem:** PRISM (Pluralistic Reasoning via In-context Structure Modeling) targets pluralistic reasoning within a model. Whether an in-context pluralism intervention applied across DIFFERENT models makes them diverge (cover different perspectives) rather than each independently listing the same canned 'multiple perspectives' is untested and matters for systemic diversity.

**Direction:** Apply a PRISM-style pluralistic prompt across ~6 models on contested prompts. Compare cross-model coverage of distinct perspectives vs. baseline prompting. Test whether models still converge on the SAME enumerated perspectives (cosmetic pluralism) or genuinely cover complementary regions, measured by union/overlap of perspective clusters.

**Why it matters:** Clarifies whether prompt-level pluralism interventions address systemic homogeneity or just produce identical 'on-one-hand/on-the-other' templates everywhere — a cheap inference experiment with clear safety framing.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Shared Nature, Unique Nurture: PRISM for Pluralistic Reasoning via In-context Structure Modeling' (arXiv:2602.21317, Feb 2026) to the cross-model homogeneity setting.
