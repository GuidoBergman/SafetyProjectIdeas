# Sycophancy Decay Curves: Does Resistance Erode Over Conversation Length?

**Problem:** Multi-turn sycophancy benchmarks report aggregate flip rates but not the temporal dynamics — whether a model's resistance decays smoothly, has a cliff, or recovers as the user keeps pushing across many turns.

**Direction:** Run scripted multi-turn pressure dialogues of 10-20 turns against open chat models, applying constant user pressure on a factual stance, and log per-turn stance to fit a survival/decay curve. Compare curve shapes across pressure types (emotional, authority, repetition) and model sizes.

**Why it matters:** Decay dynamics tell deployers how long honest behavior survives sustained manipulation — a realistic threat for long companion/agent sessions that benchmark-style single-turn evals miss. Pure inference, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** 'Measuring Sycophancy of Language Models in Multi-turn Dialogues' (arXiv 2505.23840) and SYCON-Bench frame sycophancy via turn-of-flip dynamics under sustained pressure. The Claude Opus 4.5 system card notes only a 10% course-correction rate under real conversational pressure, far worse than prompted benchmarks suggest.
