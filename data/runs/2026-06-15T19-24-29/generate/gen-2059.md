# Quantization as a Hidden Relearning Link: Does Compressing an Unlearned Model Resurface Knowledge?

**Problem:** Deployment of open models almost always involves quantization. A surprising link in the unlearning chain is that quantization itself can resurface forgotten knowledge — the 'safe' unlearned weights, once quantized, may behave like the original. This deployment-step link is rarely tested by safety researchers but is trivially reproducible.

**Direction:** Take a small model with a published unlearning checkpoint, evaluate forget-set knowledge at full precision, then re-evaluate after standard 8-bit and 4-bit quantization (bitsandbytes/GGUF). Measure how much forgotten knowledge reappears purely due to quantization, isolating quantization as a causal link that breaks the unlearning guarantee.

**Why it matters:** Quantization is mandatory for consumer deployment, so if it silently undoes unlearning, every quantized 'unlearned' model is unsafe. This is a high-leverage, zero-training, single-GPU experiment that directly tests a named failure mode against real deployment practice.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Machine Unlearning for AI Safety (2501.04952): explicitly lists quantization among the ways unlearned knowledge resurfaces. Relearning-attack literature (2502.05374, 2506.01318) shows unlearning is brittle to perturbations.
