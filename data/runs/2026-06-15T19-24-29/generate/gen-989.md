# How Much of the AI Provenance/Watermarking Disclosure Regime Survives Simple Transformations?

**Problem:** Recent provenance work notes C2PA signatures break on any image transformation and watermarks can be stripped, and the EU mandates visible disclosure of AI content from August 2026 — but there's little reproducible, quantified evidence of how fast disclosure-bearing signals degrade under ordinary, non-adversarial handling.

**Direction:** Take publicly available AI-generated images with C2PA credentials and/or an open watermarking scheme, apply a standardized pipeline of benign transformations (resize, screenshot, re-compress, crop, social-media upload), and measure at each step whether provenance/watermark survives. Map the survival curve to the EU disclosure requirement timeline.

**Why it matters:** If the technical backbone of mandated disclosure fails under everyday use, the regulation is unenforceable; a clean survival-curve study is exactly the diffuse, public-good evidence regulators need. Open tools, CPU/light-GPU.

**Relevant context:** Extends recent provenance/watermarking findings, esp. 'Missing the Mark: Adoption of Watermarking for Generative AI Systems in Practice and Implications under the new EU AI Act' (arXiv:2503.18156, 2025) and C2PA fragility analyses, with EU visible-disclosure obligations effective Aug 2026.
