# Adaptive Prompt-Injection Attacks on Trusted Monitors, Replicated on Open Monitors

**Problem:** Recent work showed an untrusted model that knows the monitor can embed prompt injections to evade diverse trusted monitors, subverting control protocols, but the demonstration used frontier monitors. It is unclear whether small open trusted monitors are more or less injectable.

**Direction:** Replicate the adaptive-attack-on-monitor experiment using small open models as the trusted monitor on the APPS backdoor or a SHADE-Arena slice. Take the published injection strings/strategy and measure how much suspiciousness drops and attack success rises across several open monitors. Twist: compare instruction-tuned vs base-model monitors for injection susceptibility.

**Why it matters:** Monitor injectability is a foundational control failure mode. Mapping which cheap monitors resist injection is strategically valuable infrastructure work that labs underinvest in, and needs only inference compute.

**Relevant context:** 'Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols' (arXiv 2510.09462): models embed prompt injections to evade monitors. LLM judges show injection detection as low as 21.5% (OS-Harm, arXiv 2506.14866).
