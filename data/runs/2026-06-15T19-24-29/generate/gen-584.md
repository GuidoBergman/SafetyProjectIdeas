# From 'Probes Generalize Across Models' to Testing the Transferability of a Truth Direction Across Open Model Families

**Problem:** Success state for ELK: a truth-reading method that ports to new, untrusted models without per-model retraining. Quirky-model probes are trained per model. If a single 'truth direction' transferred across architectures, ELK would scale; if not, every deployment needs fresh probing. This transferability is directly measurable on open checkpoints.

**Direction:** Train a linear truth probe on one open model family's activations (using a public true/false statement dataset), then evaluate it zero-shot on other open families via simple activation alignment (Procrustes or mean-shift). Measure cross-model AUROC retention. Inference-only on several small open models, modest GPU.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from scalable ELK, transferability is the make-or-break property. Quantifying how much a truth direction survives transfer (and what alignment recovers) tells the field whether per-model retraining is unavoidable — a counterfactually valuable result needing only open models.

**Relevant context:** ELK quirky-models linear probes (arXiv:2312.01037); 'Geometry of Truth' style true/false statement datasets are public; representation-alignment via Procrustes is standard.
