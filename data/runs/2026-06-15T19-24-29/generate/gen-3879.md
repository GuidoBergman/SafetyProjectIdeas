# Do Agents Escalate to Humans at the Right Moment?

**Problem:** A success state is agents that escalate to a human exactly when stakes/uncertainty are high. We lack a measurement of escalation calibration: do agents over-escalate trivial tasks and under-escalate dangerous ones?

**Direction:** Backcast from 'agents escalate iff the situation warrants it'. Give open/API agents tasks spanning low-to-high stakes and low-to-high ambiguity, with an explicit 'ask_human' tool available. Plot escalation rate against true stakes/ambiguity to build an escalation-calibration map and identify dangerous under-escalation regions.

**Why it matters:** Human-in-the-loop is the backbone of agentic safety, but it only works if agents escalate at the right times. Miscalibration maps are cheap, actionable, and untested at this granularity — pure inference on open tasks.

**Relevant context:** 2025 best practices require human confirmation for hard-to-reverse actions and clear escalation protocols. 'Check Yourself Before You Wreck Yourself' (arXiv 2510.16492) shows selective quitting helps. LLM-as-judge patterns (MindStudio, 2025) gate consequential actions through review.
