# Can a Weak Judge Detect That It Is Being Manipulated?

**Problem:** Oversight protocols assume judges passively aggregate arguments. An open problem is whether weak judges can be made *manipulation-aware* — explicitly reasoning about whether a debater is exploiting a known bias — without any retraining.

**Direction:** Build a prompt-only 'meta-judge' wrapper: before scoring a debate, the weak judge is asked to list the rhetorical tactics each side used and flag suspected manipulation, then score. Compare accuracy of meta-judge vs plain judge on QuALITY debates, including transcripts crafted to exploit length/authority/order biases.

**Why it matters:** A cheap prompting intervention that hardens weak overseers against manipulation would be directly deployable and is exactly the kind of fuzzy-reward, no-training work labs deprioritize. Tests the 'reward hacking of human oversight' open problem.

**Relevant context:** Open Phil RFP lists reward hacking of human oversight as priority. Kenton et al. (2024) document judge susceptibility to persuasive-but-wrong debaters. Khan et al. (2024) show persuasiveness does not equal correctness.
