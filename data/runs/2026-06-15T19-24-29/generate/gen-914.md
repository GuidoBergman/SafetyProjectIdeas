# Backcasting from 'Cross-Lingual Safety Holds': Measuring Refusal Erosion in Non-English Prompts

**Problem:** A success state where global deployment standards protect non-English users requires that safety mitigations hold across languages. If refusals erode under translation, governance regimes built on English evals systematically overstate global safety.

**Direction:** Take a graded harmful/borderline prompt set, translate it into several languages (using a public MT model), and measure refusal-rate differences across languages on a small multilingual open model on one GPU. Quantify how much safety degrades in lower-resource languages and identify which prompt types break most under translation.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'globally safe deployment' shows cross-lingual safety as the precondition that English-centric evals miss. Documenting cross-lingual refusal erosion is a concrete input for international evaluation standards (International Network of AI Safety Institutes). Open multilingual model on one GPU; a diffuse fuzzy-reward evaluation needing no lab data.
