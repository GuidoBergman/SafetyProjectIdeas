# Linear Activation Probe for Jailbreak Detection: Cross-Family Transfer Replication

**Problem:** Activation-probe jailbreak detectors (recent LessWrong/Anthropic work) reach high AUROC cheaply, but published results train and test on a single model family. Whether a linear refusal/harm probe trained on one open model's activations transfers to a DIFFERENT open family (architecturally distinct) is unverified externally.

**Direction:** Train a simple logistic-regression probe on residual-stream activations of one open model to separate harmful-jailbroken vs benign prompts (using a labeled jailbreak set). Twist: test the probe zero-shot on activations from a different family at matched layers, and measure AUROC drop. Quantify how much cross-family transfer is possible without retraining.

**Why it matters:** Cheap activation probes are a leading scalable defense, but their portability determines whether small actors can deploy them without per-model labeled data. A clean cross-family transfer measurement is self-contained, training only a linear probe (minutes on CPU/GPU), and externally valuable.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong Jan 2026 digest and Constitutional Classifiers++ (arXiv:2601.04603): linear probes on residual-stream activations achieve production-grade jailbreak detection at ~100x lower compute. Open models expose hidden states via HuggingFace.
