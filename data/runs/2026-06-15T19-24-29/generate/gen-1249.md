# Multilingual Probing of the Same Concept Direction

**Problem:** Probing classifiers for concepts (truth, sentiment, refusal) are typically trained and tested in English. Whether a concept direction extracted in English fires for the same concept expressed in other languages — i.e. whether models use a language-agnostic concept representation — is asserted in places but rarely directly probed in a small reproducible study.

**Direction:** Extract a difference-of-means direction for a simple concept (e.g. positive vs negative sentiment, or factual-true vs factual-false) in English on a multilingual-capable open model. Test the same direction as a probe on translated, semantically matched prompts in 3-4 languages. Measure probe accuracy degradation across languages per layer to locate where (if anywhere) representations become language-agnostic.

**Why it matters:** Safety monitors trained in English may be blind in other languages — a known but under-measured gap. A clean mechanistic measurement of where concepts become shared across languages tells auditors how far English-built probes generalize, which matters for global deployment safety and needs no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Refusal-direction work found refusal transfers between English and Chinese within an architecture; logit-lens/concept-direction literature; difference-of-means probing is standard. Open multilingual models (Qwen, Llama) run on one GPU.
