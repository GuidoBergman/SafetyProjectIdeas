# Paraphrasing CoT to Recover Hidden Suspicious Reasoning

**Problem:** Recent monitorability work suggests paraphrasing a model's CoT strips idiosyncratic micro-patterns and can make a monitor flag suspicious reasoning it missed in the original. This 'paraphrasability' diagnostic has not been tested in a cheap, reproducible setup with open models acting as both subject and monitor.

**Direction:** Generate CoTs from an open reasoning model on a task with injected bias hints. Have a second cheap open model act as a monitor judging 'did the reasoning use an outside hint?' on both the raw CoT and a paraphrased CoT. Measure whether paraphrasing raises detection of hint-following. Vary paraphrase strength.

**Why it matters:** If a simple paraphrase pass improves monitor sensitivity, that's a free, lab-independent upgrade to CoT monitoring pipelines that downstream deployers could adopt. Tests a concrete recent claim with no proprietary tooling.

**Relevant context:** Monitorability literature (arXiv:2510.27378 'Measuring CoT Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity'; arXiv:2510.19851 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?') discusses paraphrasing as a diagnostic that strips micro-patterns while preserving semantics.
