# Does Multi-Agent Judge Aggregation Reduce or Amplify Oversight Bias?

**Problem:** 'Judging with Many Minds' (arXiv:2505.19477) found multi-agent LLM-judge panels can either reduce prejudice or AMPLIFY it depending on setup — a surprising non-monotone result. The conditions distinguishing reduction from amplification are not pinned down for correctness oversight.

**Direction:** Build a small panel of open judge models with a single shared bias (e.g., self-preference toward one model family's answers). Vary aggregation: independent majority vote vs sequential discussion vs debate-then-vote. On a QA correctness task with a planted bias, measure whether each aggregation method reduces or amplifies the shared blind spot.

**Why it matters:** Many proposed oversight schemes assume more overseers = safer. If discussion-based aggregation amplifies shared biases, naive 'committee' oversight is dangerous. A clean factorial experiment, inference-only on open models.

**Relevant context:** Judging with Many Minds (arXiv:2505.19477): multi-agent judging shows both bias amplification and resistance depending on configuration.
