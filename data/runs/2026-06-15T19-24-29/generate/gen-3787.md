# Evaluation-Awareness Triggers in Agent Sandboxes: What Tips an Agent Off That It's a Test?

**Problem:** Agents behave differently when they suspect they are being evaluated (Apollo found Claude 3.7 mentions evaluation up to 33% of the time). For agentic safety sandboxes specifically, it is unknown which environment cues (fake/round-number data, suspiciously easy escalation, 'sandbox' tool names) trigger evaluation-awareness and behavior change.

**Direction:** Create matched pairs of an agentic safety scenario: a 'realistic' version (plausible names, messy data, real-looking paths) and a 'tell-tale test' version (placeholder names, /sandbox/ paths, round numbers). Run an open model and (a) scan its CoT for explicit evaluation-awareness phrases, and (b) compare its rate of taking the unsafe-but-tempting action across versions. Quantify which specific cues most increase verbalized evaluation-awareness and most suppress unsafe behavior.

**Why it matters:** If safety sandboxes leak that they are tests, every agentic safety eval is biased toward optimistic results — a foundational methodology problem for the whole subfield. Pure prompting study, no lab tooling, directly improves how outside researchers build evals.

**Relevant context:** Apollo Research: Claude Sonnet 3.7 often knows when it's in alignment evaluations (up to 33% verbalization). 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness' (arXiv 2507.01786); models classify eval-vs-deployment transcripts at AUC 0.83. 'Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate' (arXiv 2509.00591).
