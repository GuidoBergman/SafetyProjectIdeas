# Reward-Model Overoptimization x Distribution Shift Blind Spot

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) reward-model overoptimization in RLHF/DPO (alignment_training), and (B) deployment distribution shift where feedback data never covered the input distribution (evaluation/coverage). Overoptimization may be far worse precisely on out-of-distribution inputs, where the proxy reward is least reliable.

**Direction:** Use an open reward model + a DPO checkpoint trained against it. Score policy outputs with both the proxy RM and an independent stronger judge across in-distribution vs. constructed OOD prompts. Plot the proxy-vs-gold gap as a function of distribution shift; quantify how much overoptimization error is concentrated on shifted inputs.

**Why it matters:** Frontier deployment is dominated by inputs unlike the preference data. If overoptimization compounds with distribution shift, alignment quality silently collapses exactly where it matters. Uses public RMs and judges; no training of large models.

**Relevant context:** 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms' (arXiv 2406.02900); 'Information-Theoretic Reward Modeling for Stable RLHF' (arXiv 2510.13694) — RM assigns high reward to OOD/sub-par responses. Open Problems in RLHF: distribution shift in human feedback.
