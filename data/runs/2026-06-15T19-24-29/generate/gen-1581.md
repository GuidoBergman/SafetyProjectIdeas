# Backcasting an honest baseline for probe red-teaming: can a model learn to evade its own safety probe?

**Problem:** A robust-monitoring success state requires probes that hold up when the monitored model is incentivized to fool them. If a model (or prompt strategy) can shift its activations to dodge the probe while still producing harmful output, the monitor is defeated. The evadability of safety probes under adaptive pressure is the crux untested by beginners.

**Direction:** Train a safety probe on a small open model. Then search (over prompts/system-prompt instructions, NOT weights) for inputs that keep the harmful output but push activations below the probe threshold. Measure how often evasion succeeds and whether a simple ensemble/multi-layer probe resists it. Report an adaptive-attack success rate.

**Why it matters:** Static AUROC overstates real-world probe reliability; adaptive evasion is the honest test. A small adaptive red-team of a probe is high-value, lab-independent safety evaluation that frontier labs rarely publish openly.

**Relevant context:** Subfield Open Problem #1 (adversarial neutralization). Black-to-white probe benchmarking (arXiv 2507.12691); deception-probe robustness (arXiv 2605.27958).
