# Self-Play Debate Improved Judge Accuracy — Does It Transfer to a Different, Weaker Judge?

**Problem:** Khan/Kenton-style work (arXiv:2409.16636) found training debaters via self-play to win debates improved judge accuracy on QuALITY-style reading comprehension. The optimistic result may depend on the specific judge model used; transfer to weaker or different-family judges is the relevant scalable-oversight question.

**Direction:** Take the released debater outputs/transcripts and feed them to a panel of differently-sized open judges (e.g., 1B, 3B, 8B). Measure whether the judge-accuracy improvement from stronger debaters holds, shrinks, or reverses as the judge gets weaker, mapping the judge-capability dependence of the debate benefit.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight requires the JUDGE to be weaker than the debaters. If the benefit of better debaters only appears for strong judges, debate's promise for genuine weak-judge oversight is weaker than claimed. Inference-only, public transcripts.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2409.16636): self-play-trained debaters improve judge accuracy on long-document QA; transcripts released. Connects to Engels et al. Debate NSO 51.7% at Elo gap 400.
