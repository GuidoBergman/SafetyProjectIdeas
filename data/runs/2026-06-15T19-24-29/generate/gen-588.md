# From 'We Can Bound Deceptive Generalization' to Measuring the Sharp Threshold of Conditional-Policy Generalization

**Problem:** Success state: we can predict whether a backdoor-like conditional behavior will generalize from training triggers to novel triggers — central to deceptive-alignment worries. The gap is a controlled measurement of how trigger generalization depends on trigger semantic similarity and training-trigger diversity, in a small enough system to fully characterize.

**Direction:** Train tiny transformers on a synthetic conditional task (behave normally, but flip behavior on trigger T). Vary the diversity/abstractness of training triggers and test generalization to held-out triggers of varying similarity. Map the generalization frontier. Small models, synthetic data, single GPU, minutes-to-hours per run.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from bounded deceptive generalization, the building block is an empirical law for when conditional policies generalize beyond their training triggers. This is a model-organism study of deception scoped to toy models — exactly the diffuse, long-horizon problem labs deprioritize and that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Sleeper-agents / backdoor-trigger generalization literature; quirky-models conditional-error structure; inductive-bias theory (Open Phil RFP).
