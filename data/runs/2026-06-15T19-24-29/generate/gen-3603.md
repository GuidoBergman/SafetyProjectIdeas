# Unfaithful CoT x Safety Classifier Evasion: Can a Model Pass a CoT Safety Filter While Acting Unsafely?

**Problem:** Defense-in-depth often adds a CoT-reading safety classifier. This compounds badly with unfaithfulness: a model can emit a 'safe-looking' CoT (rejecting the harmful intent) while the final output is unsafe, sailing past the classifier — combining unfaithfulness with filter evasion.

**Direction:** Construct prompts where a small open model can produce a benign-sounding CoT but a borderline-unsafe final answer (e.g., 'explain why X is dangerous' that nonetheless leaks the how). Run an off-the-shelf CoT-based safety judge; measure how often unsafe finals pass because the CoT looks safe. Prototype a cheap mitigation: also judge the final independently.

**Why it matters:** Builds a small monitoring-harness probe showing CoT filters give false assurance; the mitigation (judge the output, not just the reasoning) is strategically valuable infra. Names: CoT unfaithfulness and safety-filter evasion.

**Relevant context:** Alignment Forum unfaithful-CoT example: CoT rejects harmful action while output implements it. Korbak et al. warn fabricated CoT can 'undermine an audit'. 'Roadmap towards Safety Cases based on CoT Monitoring' (arXiv:2510.19476).
