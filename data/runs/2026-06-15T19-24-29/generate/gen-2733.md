# Cheap Collusion Detection: Replicating Multi-Agent Interpretability Probes

**Problem:** A 2026 paper detects multi-agent collusion via probing internal representations and introduces NARCBench for collusion under distribution shift. The minimal version — can a simple behavioral (non-internal) detector catch collusion — is underexplored and more accessible.

**Direction:** Set up a small two-open-agent task where agents can covertly coordinate (e.g. a Schelling/signaling game with a hidden shared goal). Compare a cheap behavioral collusion detector (statistical correlation of agent outputs, mutual-information of message channels) against a simple linear probe, on collusion vs. no-collusion conditions.

**Why it matters:** Covert coordination between agentic AIs is a growing control threat. A behavioral detector that needs no internal access is more broadly deployable than interpretability probes — and the Schelling-game setup is a feasible model-organism slice.
