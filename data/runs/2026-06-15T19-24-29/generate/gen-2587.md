# Trajectory-Length Decay of Monitor Suspicion: Does Diluting Across Steps Hide Attacks?

**Problem:** Adaptive-attack work observed that 'heavier deferral and resampling suppress suspiciousness across trajectories, making attacks subtler' — suggesting a monitor's per-step suspicion gets diluted over long agent trajectories. The relationship between trajectory length and the monitor's ability to catch a single planted malicious step is not mapped.

**Direction:** Construct agent trajectories of varying length with exactly one malicious step embedded at a controlled position. Have a small monitor score (a) each step independently and (b) the whole trajectory holistically. Measure how catch rate decays as benign-step count grows, and whether step-wise vs holistic monitoring decays differently.

**Why it matters:** Long-horizon agentic deployment is the medium/long-term threat model. Showing that monitoring reliability decays with trajectory length quantifies a structural weakness exactly where it matters most. Pure inference + simple curves.

**Relevant context:** Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors (arXiv:2510.09462): heavier deferral/resampling 'suppress suspiciousness across trajectories, making attacks subtler.' SHADE-Arena (arXiv:2506.15740) notes long-trajectory monitoring difficulty. Async Control (arXiv:2512.13526) on aggregating suspicion across steps.
