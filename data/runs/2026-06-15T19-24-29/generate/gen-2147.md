# Confirmation Bias Amplification in Multi-Round Oversight

**Problem:** Confirmation bias has been identified as a challenge for scalable oversight (arXiv:2507.19486), but how it *compounds* over multiple debate rounds — where a judge forms an early opinion and then over-weights confirming evidence — is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Construct multi-round QuALITY debates and probe the judge after each round for its current leaning and confidence. Measure whether wrong early leanings 'lock in' (later correct evidence fails to move the judge) and whether withholding the judge's interim leaning until the end mitigates the lock-in.

**Why it matters:** If oversight degrades with debate length due to confirmation bias, longer/recursive protocols may be actively harmful. A simple intervention (no interim commitment) could be a free mitigation. Lab-independent, inference-only.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2507.19486 'Confirmation bias: A challenge for scalable oversight'. Kenton et al. (2024) on multi-round debate. Confirmation bias is a well-documented human systematic error the AISI agenda calls out.
