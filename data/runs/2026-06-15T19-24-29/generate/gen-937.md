# Incident Taxonomy Inconsistency x Pattern Detection: Why Fragmented Reporting Blinds an AI-NTSB

**Problem:** Failure mode A: AI incident reports use inconsistent, unstandardized taxonomies. Failure mode B: an NTSB-style body needs to aggregate incidents to detect systemic patterns. Inconsistent labels prevent clustering, so a recurring failure mode stays invisible — the two failures compound into 'we have the data but can't see the pattern.'

**Direction:** Pull a sample of AI Incident Database entries, apply two or three different public AI-harm taxonomies, and measure how much the same incidents get classified differently. Then show via simple clustering how taxonomy inconsistency fragments would-be patterns. Propose and test a minimal normalization mapping that recovers cluster signal.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that an AI-NTSB's value depends on taxonomy harmonization, directly informing the national incident-response-framework open problem. Open dataset, beginner-friendly clustering, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Standardising AI incident reporting (arXiv:2501.14778); designing incident reporting for GPAI (arXiv:2511.05914); AI Incident Database (oodaloop.com).
