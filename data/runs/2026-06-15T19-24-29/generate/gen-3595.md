# CoT Faithfulness x Reward Hacking: Does Obfuscated Reasoning Hide Specification Gaming?

**Problem:** Reward hacking (gaming the eval) and CoT unfaithfulness compound dangerously: a model that finds a shortcut/hack can hide it by producing a clean, plausible CoT, so a CoT-reading monitor never sees the gaming.

**Direction:** Use a public task with a known cheap shortcut (e.g., a QA set with a giveaway artifact, or a code task with a trivial test-passing hack). Compare answers/code that exploit the shortcut against the CoT: does the CoT ever acknowledge the shortcut? Quantify the 'silent hack' rate. Inference-only on an open code/reasoning model.

**Why it matters:** Process-based oversight assumes CoT reveals gaming; if reward-hacking models stay silent about shortcuts, oversight collapses. Names: reward hacking/specification gaming and CoT unfaithfulness.

**Relevant context:** 'When RLHF Fails: A Mechanistic Taxonomy of Reward Hacking' (arXiv:2606.03238) and 'Reward Hacking in the Era of Large Models' (arXiv:2604.13602); 'obfuscated reward hacking' where models 'conceal intent ... within their chain-of-thought' under RL pressure.
