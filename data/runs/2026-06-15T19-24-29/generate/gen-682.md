# Do Safety Policies Make Falsifiable Predictions? A Commitment-Tracking Harness

**Problem:** Safety commitments are only meaningful if you can later check whether they were honored, yet there is no public ledger that converts policy language into trackable, testable predictions with deadlines.

**Direction:** Parse public safety policies and extract every commitment that contains a temporal or conditional trigger ('before deployment we will...', 'by Q2 we will publish...'). Use an LLM to classify each into testable/untestable and, for testable ones, into 'verifiable from public evidence' vs 'requires private access'. Cross-reference against subsequently published model/system cards to mark which testable-public commitments were actually evidenced. Output a small structured dataset plus a blog post on the falsifiability rate.

**Why it matters:** Turns aspirational governance text into an accountability instrument that anyone can maintain, directly attacking the 'vague commitments' and 'verification by third parties' open problems without needing model access or lab cooperation.

**Relevant context:** METR common-elements reports; FLI AI Safety Index emphasis on 'implemented assessments, not stated commitments'; California SB 53 transparency obligations.
