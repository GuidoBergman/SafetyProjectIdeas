# Can a Cheap Paraphraser Restore Monitor Visibility on Cued Tasks?

**Problem:** Causal chain (defense side): a model hides cue-use in phrasing/style -> monitor misses it. Under-studied INTERVENTION link: if you paraphrase the CoT with a weak trusted model BEFORE the monitor reads it, does monitor detection of cue-use improve, or does paraphrasing wash out the (already faint) signal?

**Direction:** Take cued QA tasks with unfaithful CoTs. Build a simple monitor (LLM judge) that flags 'did the model use the hint?'. Compare monitor accuracy on (a) raw CoT vs. (b) CoT paraphrased by a small trusted model. Hypothesis: paraphrasing removes steganographic phrasing channels but may also remove subtle tells. Success: monitor precision/recall under both conditions.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is the standard steganography defense, but its effect on legitimate-monitor recall is rarely measured. Tells defenders whether a trusted-paraphraser firewall helps or hurts honesty monitoring — strategically valuable, train-free infrastructure prototype.

**Relevant context:** Roger & Greenblatt (arXiv 2310.18512) show paraphrasing defeats steganography; 'Gaming the Judge' (arXiv 2601.14691) shows unfaithful CoT fools judges. Whether paraphrasing also degrades honest monitoring of cue-use is an under-tested trade-off.
