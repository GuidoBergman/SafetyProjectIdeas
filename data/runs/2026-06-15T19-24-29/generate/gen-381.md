# Refusal x Topic Drift: Does a Single Refusal Compound Into Session-Wide Brittleness?

**Problem:** Two failures: an initial refusal and subsequent behavioral drift. After refusing once, a model may either over-refuse downstream benign turns (compounding over-refusal) or, conversely, become more compliant after an attacker negotiates the refusal (compounding jailbreak). The directional, session-level compounding effect of an early refusal on later turns is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Construct multi-turn sessions where turn 1 triggers a refusal, followed by a fixed sequence of (a) benign and (b) borderline turns. Compare downstream refusal/compliance against control sessions with no early refusal. Success criterion: a measured drift—whether an early refusal raises later over-refusal or lowers later resistance—across open models.

**Why it matters:** Real conversations contain refusals; if a single refusal systematically destabilizes the rest of the session (either direction), it is a compound safety/usability failure invisible to single-turn evals. Open models, evaluation-only, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Over-refusal (OR-Bench, XSTest) and multi-turn escalation (arXiv:2605.18239) are documented separately; the within-session compounding effect of an early refusal is the novel angle. arXiv:2512.01037 on refusal semantics is relevant.
