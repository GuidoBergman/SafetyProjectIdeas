# Threshold Cliffs x Strategic Capability Withholding: Modeling the Incentive to Park Just Below the Line

**Problem:** Failure mode A: governance uses sharp capability/compute thresholds with large obligation jumps ('cliffs'). Failure mode B: developers can strategically tune disclosed capability to sit just below the cliff. The two compound into a bunching incentive that hollows out the regime exactly at the threshold meant to bite.

**Direction:** Build a simple game-theoretic / numerical model of a developer choosing disclosed capability against a cliff-shaped obligation cost, and show the equilibrium bunching just below the threshold. Then look for bunching evidence in public model-report distributions (do disclosed numbers cluster below known thresholds?). Combine a toy model with a light empirical check.

**Why it matters:** Predicts and partially tests a manipulation pattern, arguing for smooth/graduated obligations over cliffs. Modeling plus public-data analysis; medium-horizon structural concern labs won't volunteer.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act systemic-risk FLOP cliff (artificialintelligenceact.eu); self-reporting incentives (Computing Power and Governance, arXiv:2402.08797).
