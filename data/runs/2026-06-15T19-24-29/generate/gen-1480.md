# Bliss/Loewe Synergy Analysis for Combining Safety Steering Vectors

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. show concept vectors can be combined for multi-concept steering, but combination is treated as naive addition. Pharmacology has a century-old, rigorous framework (Bliss independence, Loewe additivity) for deciding whether two interventions are synergistic, additive, or antagonistic. Steering has no such framework.

**Direction:** Transfer drug-combination analysis. For pairs of safety-relevant steering vectors (e.g. refusal + honesty, anti-sycophancy + anti-deception), sweep a 2D dose grid of steering strengths and measure the combined effect on a behavioral metric. Fit a Bliss-independence surface and compute synergy/antagonism scores per pair. Identify which safety vectors interfere destructively (antagonism — turning one off weakens the other) vs. reinforce.

**Why it matters:** Stacking multiple safety steers is exactly how defense-in-depth would be deployed, yet antagonistic pairs could silently cancel each other. A principled interaction map warns which safety concepts cannot be safely co-steered. Needs only inference on an open model, fully feasible in 30h, and is the kind of fuzzy-interaction evaluation labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Bliss independence and Loewe additivity are standard drug-synergy models (PMC6876842). Beaglehole et al. demonstrate multi-concept steering by combination. Open problem: combine concept steering for targeted alignment.
