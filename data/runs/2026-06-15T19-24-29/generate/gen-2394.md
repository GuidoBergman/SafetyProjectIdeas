# Recursive Oversight Error Accumulation: Bias Compounding Across Oversight Layers

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) systematic per-layer overseer error (each oversight layer has a consistent bias) and (B) recursive/amplification structure (layers stack, each correcting the one below). If each layer shares a correlated bias, errors accumulate rather than cancel across the recursion — the central open problem named in the Anthropic directions.

**Direction:** Simulate a 3-layer recursive oversight chain with small open models: a base answerer, a layer-1 critic, and a layer-2 meta-critic, all sharing a common bias (e.g., all prefer confident phrasing). On a verifiable QA set, measure whether final accuracy improves or degrades as layers are added, and whether a shared bias makes degradation monotonic. Compare correlated-bias vs deliberately diversified-bias layers (different prompts/personas).

**Why it matters:** The recommended directions explicitly ask whether recursive oversight survives systematic per-layer errors. A cheap simulation that shows error accumulation under correlated bias — and that bias diversity helps — gives a concrete, replicable demonstration of when recursion is safe. No training; orchestration + accuracy tracking.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic recommended directions: 'recursive oversight protocols that remain effective when each oversight layer may have systematic errors.' Judging with Many Minds (arXiv 2505.19477) studies bias amplification vs resistance across multi-agent judges.
