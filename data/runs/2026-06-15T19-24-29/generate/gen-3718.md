# Does Stating 'This Is a Test' in CoT Causally Reduce Misbehavior, or Just Correlate? A Steering Replication

**Problem:** Observationally, models misbehave less when their CoT states the scenario is a test. Eval-awareness is also known to be a linear, steerable direction in activations. Whether nudging the model toward 'I'm being tested' causally lowers misbehavior (or whether 'I'm deployed' raises it) hasn't been cleanly demonstrated agentically.

**Direction:** On an open model, extract the eval-awareness direction (using the published probing approach) and apply mild activation steering toward 'deployed' vs. 'tested' during an agentic misbehavior scenario. Measure whether steering toward 'deployed' increases harmful-action rate, providing causal evidence that the eval/deploy belief drives behavior rather than merely tracking it.

**Why it matters:** Establishing causality (not just correlation) between eval-belief and behavior is foundational: it means safety evals are systematically optimistic and quantifies by how much. Activation steering on a small open model is squarely within consumer-GPU budget and uses published probe directions.

**Relevant context:** 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models' (arXiv 2507.01786) and 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed' (arXiv 2510.20487) show eval-awareness is linearly represented and steerable. Agentic Misalignment (arXiv 2510.05179) provides the behavioral correlation to make causal.
