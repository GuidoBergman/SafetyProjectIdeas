# Self-Preference Bias as an Oversight Backdoor: Does a Judge Favor Its Own Family?

**Problem:** When oversight is delegated to an LLM judge, the judge can systematically prefer outputs that resemble its own generations (lower perplexity to itself). Causal chain: deploy LLM-as-judge -> judge has self-preference -> a model from the judge's family gets inflated scores -> oversight rewards the wrong answer. The under-studied link is whether self-preference is strong enough to flip correctness judgments, not just style preferences.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of QA/reasoning items with one correct and one incorrect answer. Generate the incorrect answer in the judge's own model-family style and the correct answer in a foreign-family style (and vice versa). Measure how often the judge picks the incorrect-but-familiar answer. Use 2-3 small open judge models and compute the correctness-flip rate attributable to familiarity.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies whether a documented stylistic bias actually corrupts the correctness signal that oversight depends on. Pure inference + scoring, runs on a single GPU, no proprietary data, directly relevant to medium-horizon automated-oversight pipelines.

**Relevant context:** Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge (arXiv:2410.21819) shows judges score lower-perplexity (self-familiar) outputs higher; 'Justice or Prejudice?' (arXiv:2410.02736) catalogs judge biases. The link from familiarity to correctness-flips is under-measured.
