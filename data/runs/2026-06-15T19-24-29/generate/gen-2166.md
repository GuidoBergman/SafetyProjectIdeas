# Judge Confidence Calibration as a Cheaper Oversight Proxy Than Accuracy

**Problem:** Oversight research reports judge accuracy, but a strategic AI exploits judges that are *confidently wrong*. The calibration of weak judges under debate vs direct-QA — whether protocols make judges better calibrated, not just more accurate — is largely unmeasured.

**Direction:** Collect weak-judge verdicts with confidence under direct-QA, consultancy, and debate on QuALITY. Compute calibration curves / ECE per protocol. Test whether debate improves calibration (so low confidence reliably flags hard cases the overseer should escalate) even when accuracy gains are small.

**Why it matters:** A well-calibrated weak overseer that knows when to escalate is safer than a slightly-more-accurate but overconfident one. Calibration as the oversight metric is a fresh, fully inference-based angle.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. (2024) protocol accuracy results. arXiv:2507.19486 confirmation bias produces overconfidence. Calibration/ECE methodology (Guo et al. 2017) applied to oversight.
