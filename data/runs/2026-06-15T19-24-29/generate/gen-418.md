# Surprise accounting on a toy circuit: can outsiders reproduce ARC's heuristic-explanation cost on modular addition?

**Problem:** ARC proposes 'surprise accounting' — decomposing the information-theoretic surprise of a model behavior into the surprise of an explanation plus the residual — as a semi-formal alternative to proof for explaining networks. It has been demonstrated only in very toy ARC-internal settings. An independent reproduction on a known circuit would test whether the metric is computable and meaningful outside ARC.

**Direction:** Take the well-understood modular-addition transformer (whose Fourier circuit is known). Operationalize a simple version of surprise accounting: quantify how much the known Fourier explanation reduces the bits needed to predict the model's outputs versus a no-explanation baseline. Compare the 'surprise' of the correct mechanistic explanation against a deliberately wrong/partial explanation to see if the metric ranks them sensibly.

**Why it matters:** ARC's heuristic-explanation agenda is a leading formal route to mechanistic anomaly detection. Showing whether its core metric is reproducible and discriminative on a canonical toy circuit is high-leverage, outsider-friendly theoretical-alignment work needing no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in ARC's 'Formal verification, heuristic explanations and surprise accounting' (alignment.org) and 'A bird's eye view of ARC's research'. Context open problem: 'formal mechanistic explanations of neural network behaviors'.
