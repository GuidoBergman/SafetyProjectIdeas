# Does the Order of Debate Rounds Change Who Wins? Recency vs Primacy in Oversight

**Problem:** Position bias is known for pairwise judging, but multi-turn debate has a temporal analogue: does the debater who speaks LAST (recency) or FIRST (primacy) gain an unfair edge with weak judges? This is rarely isolated in debate experiments.

**Direction:** Generate two-turn debate transcripts, then create variants where the SAME arguments are reordered (correct-side-first vs incorrect-side-first vs interleaved). Hold content fixed, vary only order. Measure how the weak judge's verdict shifts with ordering to quantify a recency/primacy oversight vulnerability.

**Why it matters:** If verdict depends on speaking order rather than argument merit, debate is exploitable by a model that games turn structure. A clean controlled variation isolating order from content, inference-only.

**Relevant context:** Position bias documented for pairwise LLM judging (GPT-4 ~10% first-position preference, arXiv:2410.02736). Debate's turn-order analogue is under-tested.
