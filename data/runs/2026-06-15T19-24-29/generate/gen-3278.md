# Inter-Judge Reliability of LLM Scheming-Detection Verdicts

**Problem:** Success state: automated scheming evals (which rely on LLM judges scoring transcripts) give consistent verdicts regardless of which judge model is used. Backcasting from Petri's judge-based pipeline, we need to know how much scheming scores depend on the judge rather than the behavior.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of agent transcripts from a scheming eval and have 3-4 different open LLM judges (plus a rubric) score them for covert action/deception. Measure inter-judge agreement (Krippendorff's alpha), systematic leniency differences, and which behaviors are most contested. Report how judge choice shifts headline scheming rates.

**Why it matters:** If scheming rates swing with judge choice, published scheming numbers are not comparable across studies — a measurement-validity crisis. This is cheap meta-evaluation that strengthens the whole field's infrastructure and needs no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic Petri uses an LLM-judge to score transcripts across deception/self-preservation/reward-hacking dimensions; Apollo evals also rely on judged transcripts. Inter-rater reliability of LLM judges is an open methodological concern.
