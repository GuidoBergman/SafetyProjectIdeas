# Order Bias as an Oversight Exploit: Can a Model Win by Choosing Where to Be Placed?

**Problem:** Position bias in pairwise judging is well documented (GPT-4 favors first, ChatGPT second), but it is rarely framed as an exploitable oversight failure: how large is the win-rate swing purely from position, and is it stable enough that a strategic system could exploit placement?

**Direction:** Run pairwise judging where the same answer pair is presented in both orders across several open judges, measuring per-judge position-induced win-rate swing on items of varying difficulty. Quantify exploitability (consistent direction and magnitude) and test the standard mitigation of averaging both orders to see how much residual bias remains.

**Why it matters:** A purely positional artifact that flips oversight verdicts is a cheap attack surface and a confound in every LLM-judge eval. Quantifying exploitability and the both-orders fix is directly actionable, training-free, public data.

**Relevant context:** Judging the Judges: position bias (arXiv:2406.07791) — GPT-4 favors first, ChatGPT second; surveys arXiv:2410.02736, arXiv:2411.15594. This reframes position bias as a strategic exploit and quantifies residual bias after order-averaging.
