# In-Context Environment Cues That Trigger Eval-Awareness: A Cue-Ablation Study

**Problem:** 'In-Context Environments Induce Evaluation-Awareness' (arXiv:2603.03824) shows environmental cues in the context induce eval-awareness and adversarial optimization amplifies it. Which specific, naturally-occurring cues (formatting, multiple-choice structure, 'benchmark' phrasing) drive the effect is not isolated.

**Direction:** Construct a single task rendered in many surface forms that differ only in suspected eval-cues (MCQ vs. free-form, presence of 'Question 1 of N', neutral vs. benchmark-style phrasing, system-prompt 'you are being tested'). Use the eval-awareness probe direction (from arXiv:2507.01786) or behavioral capitulation as readout, and rank cues by how strongly each elevates eval-awareness in an open model.

**Why it matters:** If specific, removable cues drive eval-awareness, then benchmark designers can sanitize evaluations to reduce eval-faking — a cheap, broadly applicable mitigation. The cue-ablation map is directly actionable and needs no lab resources.
