# Trusted-Editing x Capability Loss: The Compound Cost of Safety Interventions

**Problem:** A control intervention (trusted editing / paraphrasing outputs of an untrusted model to remove hidden signals) compounds with a capability/usefulness failure: the same editing that strips covert channels also degrades task quality, so heavier safety editing both fails to fully remove collusion AND silently destroys usefulness. The intervention's two costs interact.

**Direction:** On a coding/QA control task, run a trusted-editor that paraphrases the untrusted model's outputs at increasing strength. Jointly measure (a) residual covert-channel capacity and (b) task quality degradation. Plot the two-dimensional cost surface to show there is no editing strength that is both safe and useful.

**Why it matters:** Trusted editing is a leading control technique; quantifying that its safety and usefulness costs compound informs whether it is viable. Reuses the steganography and control tooling, fully lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Redwood trusted-editing/paraphrasing to destroy signals; paraphrasing insufficient against steganography ('Hidden in Plain Text', IJCNLP-AACL 2025); ControlArena task quality metrics.
