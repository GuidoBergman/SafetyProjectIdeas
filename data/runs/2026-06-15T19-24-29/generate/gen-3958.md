# Estimating the Probability of Rare Catastrophic Outputs: A Toy Low-Probability-Estimation Benchmark

**Problem:** ARC's Low Probability Estimation (LPE) problem asks: estimate the chance a model produces a catastrophic output under an input distribution, with limited compute, when naive sampling never sees the event. There is little accessible empirical comparison of estimation methods on a controlled task.

**Direction:** Define a small model/function with a known, tunable rare 'bad' output (e.g. a tiny classifier or templated LLM behavior that fires on rare input combinations). Compare estimators — naive Monte Carlo, importance sampling, and a simple activation/logit-based extrapolation — on how accurately and cheaply they estimate the true rare-event probability. Chart accuracy vs compute budget.

**Why it matters:** LPE is a load-bearing piece of ARC's plan for catching catastrophic behavior that testing won't surface; an open, reproducible micro-benchmark with baseline methods is genuinely useful and entirely non-lab-dependent.
