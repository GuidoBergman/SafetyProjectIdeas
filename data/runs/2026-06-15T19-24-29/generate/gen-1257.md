# Layer-of-Localization vs Model Depth: Where Do Safety Behaviors Live, Proportionally?

**Problem:** Studies report a behavior (refusal, factual recall, IOI) localizes to specific layers in a specific model. Whether these localizations sit at a consistent relative depth (e.g. ~60% through the network) across models of different total depth is a natural variation that bears on transfer of intervention recipes.

**Direction:** For one behavior with an established extraction method (refusal direction), extract the per-layer direction-separability curve across several open models of different depths (e.g. GPT-2 small 12L, medium 24L, a small Llama). Normalize layer index to relative depth and overlay the curves. Test whether peak separability occurs at a consistent fractional depth. Inference + difference-of-means only.

**Why it matters:** Auditors transferring an intervention recipe from a studied model to a new one need to know which layer to target. A simple law ('safety direction peaks at ~X% depth') would be immediately useful and is a cheap, lab-independent contribution toward transferable interpretability.

**Relevant context:** Refusal-direction work reports mid-to-late layer peaks; logit-lens shows late-layer convergence. TransformerLens supports comparing GPT-2 small/medium and small Llama on one GPU.
