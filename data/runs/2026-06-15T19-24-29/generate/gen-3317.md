# Do Deception Probes Read Meaning or Just Surface Text? A Leakage Ablation

**Problem:** A March 2026 paper argues linear probes 'rely on textual evidence' — i.e., probe accuracy may stem from lexical cues leaking into activations rather than a genuine internal deception representation. This confound undercuts probe-based safety claims but is under-tested across models.

**Direction:** Train a deception probe on an open model's activations, then run controlled ablations: (a) paraphrase deceptive statements to remove deception-correlated keywords, (b) inject deception-keywords into honest statements, (c) probe activations at a token position before the deceptive content appears. Compare probe AUROC across conditions to estimate how much performance is 'semantic' vs. 'lexical leakage.' Pure inference; no training of the base model.

**Why it matters:** If probes mostly detect words rather than intent, scheming detection that relies on them is illusory against a model that deceives without telltale vocabulary. A clean leakage-ablation protocol gives the community a way to validate any probe before trusting it.

**Relevant context:** 'Linear probes rely on textual evidence: Results from leakage mitigation studies in language models' (arXiv:2509.21344, Mar 2026). Complements 'Pressure-Testing Deception Probes' (arXiv:2605.27958, May 2026) and 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv:2502.03407).
