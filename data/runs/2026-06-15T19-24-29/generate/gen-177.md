# Does Deliberative-Style Prompting Recover Shallow Alignment at Inference?

**Problem:** Deliberative Alignment (arXiv:2412.16339) improves robustness by training models to reason over a safety spec. It is untested whether merely PROMPTING an open non-reasoning model to recite a safety spec before answering reproduces any of that robustness gain against prefilling/shallow attacks — i.e., is the benefit from training or from the reasoning structure?

**Direction:** On an open chat model, compare jailbreak success across three conditions: (a) baseline, (b) system prompt instructing the model to first restate relevant safety rules then answer, (c) (b) plus a forced reasoning preamble. Test against prefilling and simple adaptive attacks. Quantify how much robustness a free, inference-time prompt buys.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap prompt template recovers part of deliberative alignment's robustness, defenders without training budgets gain a deployable mitigation — high counterfactual value, zero training.

**Relevant context:** Deliberative Alignment (arXiv:2412.16339): training models to recall/reason over safety specs pushes the robustness/over-refusal Pareto frontier. arXiv:2404.02151 simple adaptive attacks as testbed.
