# Inter-rater reliability statistics to audit LLM-judge autograders for safety evals

**Problem:** Safety evals increasingly rely on LLM-as-judge autograders, but different judges give discrepant verdicts and we lack rigorous agreement statistics to know when a judge is trustworthy enough to base safety decisions on.

**Direction:** Import inter-rater reliability methodology (Cohen's/Fleiss' kappa, Krippendorff's alpha, Gwet's AC1) from psychometrics and content analysis. Have several open LLM judges plus a small human-labeled gold set grade the same safety-eval transcripts; compute chance-corrected agreement among judges and between each judge and humans. Identify which prompt categories cause judge disagreement and where autograding is unreliable.

**Why it matters:** Gives eval builders a principled, chance-corrected reliability check for autograders — directly addressing the scorer-reliability gap. Cheap, training-free, and broadly reusable across the open-eval community.

**Relevant context:** Chance-corrected agreement stats are standard (Krippendorff, Gwet). Landscape evaluations_benchmarks open problem: scorer/autograder reliability. Source subfield: psychometrics / content-analysis reliability. Target subfield: evaluations_benchmarks.
