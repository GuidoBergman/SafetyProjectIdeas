# A formal-meets-empirical slice: empirically testing an ELK-style 'reporter' on a toy model where ground truth is known

**Problem:** Eliciting latent knowledge (formal/theoretical) asks how to read what a model 'knows' even when it reports otherwise. The empirical sub-problem: on a small open model in a setting where we control and KNOW the latent fact, does a simple probe-based 'reporter' recover the truth when the model is prompted to lie about it?

**Direction:** Decompose ELK into a controlled probe-reporter experiment. Construct a task where the model is given a secret fact and instructed to deny it. Train a probe (the 'reporter') on activations to recover the true fact despite the model's deceptive output, and measure how reporter accuracy degrades as the deception is made more sophisticated.

**Why it matters:** Grounds an abstract theoretical agenda (ELK) in a concrete, beginner-feasible experiment on open weights, single GPU, no training. A working toy reporter plus its failure boundary is a counterfactual, communicable contribution that bridges theory and practice.

**Relevant context:** ARC ELK agenda; formal_theoretical open problem on eliciting latent knowledge; probing for known-but-denied facts; honesty/deception probing literature.
