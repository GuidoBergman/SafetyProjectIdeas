# Backcasting Asynchronous Control: Does Detection Survive When Monitoring Lags Behind Execution?

**Problem:** Success state: a control system that stays safe even when the monitor runs asynchronously and can only catch/undo harm after some delay. Backward: we need to know how much irreversible harm accrues as a function of monitoring lag.

**Direction:** Simulate an agent executing a sequence of actions while a monitor reviews them with a delay of d steps. On SHADE-Arena public tasks, vary d and measure the fraction of sabotage that completes before flagged. Result: 'a harm-vs-latency curve quantifying the cost of asynchronous oversight.'

**Why it matters:** Real agents act faster than humans (or models) can review, so async control is the realistic regime. Quantifying the latency/harm tradeoff is a crisp, lab-independent contribution tied to the 'irreversible actions' open problem.

**Relevant context:** 'Async Control: Stress-testing Asynchronous Control Measures for LLM Agents' (arXiv:2512.13526); ai_control.md open problem on preventing irreversible harmful actions; SHADE-Arena public tasks.
