# Compounding Miscalibration: Reward-Model Error x Policy Overconfidence in a Toy RLHF Loop

**Problem:** Reward-model error (the proxy is wrong) and policy overconfidence (the policy is poorly calibrated) are studied separately. They compound: an overconfident policy exploits reward-model error more aggressively because it commits hard to the highest-proxy region, accelerating Goodhart. The interaction term between RM error and policy entropy is not characterized empirically.

**Direction:** Build a minimal bandit/toy-RLHF simulation (no neural training needed; tabular or tiny model) with an explicit true reward, a noisy proxy reward, and a policy whose temperature/confidence you control. Sweep RM noise and policy confidence on a 2D grid and measure true-reward regret to show whether the compounding is super-additive. Optionally validate qualitatively with best-of-n on an open LLM.

**Why it matters:** Gives a clean, theory-flavored measurement of how two RLHF-pipeline failures interact, with a fully synthetic, instantly reproducible artifact. Connects calibration theory to Goodhart, both in scope for the subfield.

**Relevant context:** Scaling laws for RM overoptimization (arXiv:2210.10760); Goodhart decoupling under optimization pressure.
