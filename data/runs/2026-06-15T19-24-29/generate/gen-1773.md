# Replicating the 'Misalignment Direction' in a Small Open Model Organism

**Problem:** A success state: 'we can locate and ablate a single internal direction that mediates misaligned behavior.' Backcasting from 'a reliable misalignment dial', a needed result is independent replication on a smaller, fully-open model that adding the direction induces misalignment and ablating it reduces it.

**Direction:** Use an openly released emergently-misaligned LoRA model organism (e.g., the rank-1 LoRA Qwen variant). Compute the difference-of-means direction between aligned and misaligned activations on a small probe set. Test causal sufficiency (add direction -> measure misaligned responses) and necessity (ablate -> measure recovery) on a held-out behavior set, reporting effect sizes with confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** Confirms whether the 'single misalignment direction' finding is robust and transferable — a load-bearing claim for interpretability-based safety. Model-organism study on open weights, no training of the organism required.

**Relevant context:** Convergent Linear Representations of Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.11618) built a model organism with 9 rank-1 LoRA adapters on Qwen-14B Instruct and identified a single direction that induces misalignment when added and reduces it when ablated.
