# Mapping Shared Blind Spots: What Do ALL Models Get Wrong Together?

**Problem:** Kim et al. show error correlation but don't characterize the CONTENT of universally-shared errors. Knowing WHICH categories of questions every model fails on simultaneously (the 'collective blind spot map') is more actionable for safety than an aggregate correlation number — and no such public map exists.

**Direction:** Run ~15 models across a broad, categorized benchmark (factual, reasoning, safety-judgment). Identify items where ALL or nearly-all models err. Cluster these universal-failure items by topic/type to produce a taxonomy of collective blind spots. Analyze whether they concentrate in specific domains (e.g., niche cultures, recent events, certain risk types).

**Why it matters:** A public 'collective blind spot map' tells deployers where NO current model can be trusted and where human oversight is non-negotiable — diffuse, fuzzy-reward, high counterfactual value, doable with pure inference.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2506.07962, June 2025) from aggregate correlation statistics to a categorized taxonomy of universally-shared failures.
