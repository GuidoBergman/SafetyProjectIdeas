# Refusal-Token Telemetry: Logging the Model's Hesitation as a Monitoring Signal

**Problem:** Success state: deployers get an early warning before a model is jailbroken into producing harm. Needed result: evidence that the probability mass on refusal tokens at the first output position is a calibrated, cheap risk signal across attacks.

**Direction:** For benign, borderline, and jailbroken prompts, log the first-token probability of refusal-initiating tokens (e.g. 'I', 'Sorry', 'I cannot') on an open model, and test whether a simple threshold on this signal separates attacks from benign use. Compare against full output classification.

**Why it matters:** Turns the shallow-alignment finding into a near-free monitoring telemetry — defenders can watch the first-token refusal mass. Strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure, no training, no proprietary tooling.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946): safety behavior concentrated in first-token distribution. 'From Refusal Tokens to Refusal Control' (arXiv:2603.13359) studies refusal tokens — a first-token telemetry monitor is an accessible, fresh application.
