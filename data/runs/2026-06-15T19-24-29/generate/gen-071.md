# Prediction-market scoring (proper scoring rules) to aggregate weak judges in scalable oversight

**Problem:** Scalable oversight needs to aggregate possibly-biased weak judgments about which of two answers is better, but naive majority voting ignores calibration and is gameable by correlated errors.

**Direction:** Import proper scoring rules and market-making from mechanism design / economics. Set up a debate-style task on a small open model where several weak judge instances post probabilistic credences over which answer is correct; aggregate them via a logarithmic-market-scoring-rule market that rewards calibration and penalizes herding. Compare market-aggregated accuracy against majority vote and single-judge baselines on tasks with known ground truth.

**Why it matters:** Market-based aggregation enforces myopic, calibration-rewarding incentives that reduce long-horizon gaming — a promising scalable-oversight direction with no standardized empirical comparison. Runnable with API/open models, no training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Market Making as a Scalable Framework for Safe and Scalable Oversight' (arXiv 2511.17621) and mechanism-design-for-LLM work.
