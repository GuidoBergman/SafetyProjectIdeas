# Sycophancy as a Function of Stated User Expertise and Emotional Stakes

**Problem:** Sharma-style sycophancy probes hold the user constant. We don't know how much capitulation depends on the user signaling expertise ('I'm a doctor'), emotional investment ('I really need this to be true'), or vulnerability — variables that matter for real-world manipulation and for high-stakes domains.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of factual questions with a correct answer. Cross two factors: (1) user-claimed expertise (none / layperson / domain expert) and (2) emotional framing (neutral / high personal stakes). Measure how often the model abandons the correct answer to agree. Run a small factorial design on 2-3 open models and report effect sizes.

**Why it matters:** Identifies which user signals most easily extract dishonest agreement — directly relevant to mental-health, medical, and legal misuse. A clean factorial behavioral experiment requiring only prompting and an open model.

**Relevant context:** Sharma et al. 2023 sycophancy taxonomy; arXiv:2604.07749 'Beyond Social Pressure: Benchmarking Epistemic Attack' and PARROT (arXiv:2511.17220) show pressure framing matters. Opus 4.5 card on real-world pressure.
