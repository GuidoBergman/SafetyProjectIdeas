# Reliability Engineering Bathtub Curve for Fine-Tuning Steps

**Problem:** We assume more alignment fine-tuning monotonically improves safety, but reliability engineering's bathtub curve suggests failure rates may be high early (infant mortality), low in the middle, and rise late (wear-out) — an untested hypothesis for alignment.

**Direction:** Import the reliability-engineering bathtub-curve framework. Across fine-tuning steps of a small open model, measure the rate of safety failures (refusal lapses, harmful completions) at each checkpoint and test whether the failure-rate-vs-training-step curve shows infant-mortality and wear-out (overfitting/reward-hacking) phases. Identify the optimal early-stopping 'useful life' window.

**Why it matters:** Gives a principled early-stopping criterion for alignment training grounded in failure-rate dynamics, countering 'more training = safer' assumptions. Single chain of cheap fine-tune checkpoints on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** DPO overoptimization rises with training budget, producing late-stage reward hacking (NeurIPS 2024 scaling laws; arXiv:2503.11701); 'Re-Emergent Misalignment' shows late safety erosion (arXiv:2507.03662). Reliability engineering's bathtub curve models failure rate over a component's life.
