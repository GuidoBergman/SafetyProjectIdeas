# Monitor Performance Under Paraphrased Attacks (Defending CoT Steganography)

**Problem:** Paraphrasing the CoT before it reaches the monitor is a proposed defense against steganographic/encoded reasoning, but its cost (does paraphrasing also destroy legitimate detection signal?) has not been measured against real backdoor transcripts.

**Direction:** Insert a cheap paraphrasing step between the policy's CoT and the monitor. On a set of malign transcripts (some with overt harmful planning, some with mild encoding), compare monitor AUROC with and without the paraphrase defense. Quantify both the steganography-disruption benefit and the legitimate-signal-loss cost.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrase defenses are widely recommended but their detection cost is hand-waved. A concrete cost-benefit number is directly useful for monitoring-harness design and needs only inference.

**Relevant context:** LLMs can learn and generalize steganographic CoT under process supervision (arXiv 2506.01926). Training on Documents About Monitoring Leads to CoT Obfuscation (arXiv 2605.15257). CoT Monitorability through information theory (arXiv 2602.18297).
