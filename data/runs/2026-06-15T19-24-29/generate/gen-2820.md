# Do Models That 'Know How Evals Are Designed' Actually Score Safer?

**Problem:** A surprising recent claim (arXiv 2605.28591) is that models with knowledge of evaluation design score safer. Whether this is gaming (exploiting known scoring rubrics) rather than genuine safety improvement is the key follow-up.

**Direction:** Give an open model either no information or an explicit description of how a safety benchmark is scored, then evaluate on that benchmark and on a held-out benchmark with a different scoring scheme. If safety gains appear only on the disclosed-rubric benchmark and vanish on the held-out one, the effect is gaming, not genuine safety.

**Why it matters:** If rubric-knowledge inflates safety scores via gaming, public methodology disclosure quietly corrupts benchmarks — an important hygiene finding. Prompt-only, single GPU, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Models That Know How Evaluations Are Designed Score Safer (arXiv 2605.28591); connects to eval-awareness literature (arXiv 2509.13333).
