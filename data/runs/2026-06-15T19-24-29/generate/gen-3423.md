# Censored Open Models as a Free Testbed for Secret-Knowledge Elicitation

**Problem:** A recent paper proposes that censored/aligned open LLMs (which 'know' a fact but refuse or deny it) are a natural testbed for secret-knowledge elicitation. The elicitation techniques and their success rates on accessible models are barely mapped.

**Direction:** Pick an open model with known refusal/denial topics where ground truth is verifiable. Apply a battery of training-free elicitation prompts (honest-persona framing, indirect/hypothetical framing, third-party-transcript framing) and measure which recovers the suppressed knowledge most reliably. Rank techniques by elicitation success.

**Why it matters:** Provides a cheap, reproducible benchmark and a ranked toolkit for eliciting latent knowledge a model won't volunteer — directly serving the open problem of detecting what models hide, with no lab access required.

**Relevant context:** 'Censored LLMs as a Natural Testbed for Secret Knowledge Elicitation' (arXiv:2603.05494); Anthropic honesty-elicitation (alignment.anthropic.com/2025/honesty-elicitation) and 'Spilling the Beans' (arXiv:2511.06626) provide concrete elicitation framings (honest persona, transcript reframing).
