# Position and ordering blind spots shared across models: a correlated-bias incident study

**Problem:** Many models share systematic biases (e.g., position bias in multiple-choice, ordering bias in pairwise comparison). If these biases are correlated across vendors, a single adversarial reordering can flip decisions in every model at once — a shared exploitable failure mode.

**Direction:** On a fixed MCQ and pairwise-preference set, permute answer positions/order across models. Measure each model's position-bias magnitude and direction, then test whether biases are correlated across models (same option letter / same position favored). Estimate the fraction of items where a single reorder flips ALL models the same way.

**Why it matters:** Shared, predictable input-format biases mean an attacker needs to craft only one manipulation to fool a diverse model pool — directly demonstrating correlated, exploitable failure. Cheap to run, broadly relevant to eval and agentic safety.

**Relevant context:** Grounds on documented position/order biases in LLM evaluation and the correlated-failure framing. Public benchmarks + inference only.
