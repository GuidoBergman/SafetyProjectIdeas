# Sequential Probability Ratio Test for Early-Stopping Safety Evals

**Problem:** Deciding whether a model is 'safe enough' on a benchmark usually fixes the sample size in advance, wasting compute when the answer is clear early and lacking power when it is borderline.

**Direction:** Import Wald's Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) from quality control / sequential analysis. Frame a safety acceptance decision as a hypothesis test (harmful-rate below vs above a threshold), evaluate prompts one at a time, and stop as soon as the accumulated likelihood ratio crosses an acceptance or rejection boundary at controlled error rates. Demonstrate on open models the average sample-size savings versus fixed-n testing across easy and borderline cases.

**Why it matters:** Gives auditors a compute-efficient, statistically principled accept/reject safety gate with explicit error guarantees — exactly the standardized, comparable risk-measurement regulators want. Pure inference + sequential statistics; ideal low-resource, high-counterfactual project.

**Relevant context:** Wald's SPRT is the foundational sequential test from acceptance sampling, minimizing expected sample size for given error rates; SPC/acceptance-sampling heritage (arXiv:2402.08088). Cross-model risk metrics are a flagged open problem (US CAISI/NIST).
