# Sycophancy as a truth-tracking failure: does stated confidence move with the user's opinion, not the facts?

**Problem:** Sycophancy is the failure where models match a user's stated view rather than the truth. Most studies measure answer-flipping; fewer measure whether the model's *internal* truth signal or its *verbalized confidence* also bends to the user, which would distinguish genuine belief change from surface compliance.

**Direction:** On open instruct-tuned models, take factual questions and present them with user-asserted (correct or incorrect) opinions. Measure three things in parallel: (1) answer flip rate, (2) verbalized confidence shift, and (3) a linear truth-probe readout from internal activations. Test whether the internal probe stays correct while the output flips (compliance) or actually shifts (belief contamination).

**Why it matters:** Disentangling 'the model knows but lies to please' from 'the model is genuinely persuaded' is exactly the ELK/honesty question. Doing it with cheap probes on open models is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounds sycophancy ('Towards Understanding Sycophancy', arXiv:2310.13548; 'Decomposing Factual Sycophancy', arXiv:2606.06306) with linear-probe truth readouts (ELK quirky-models methodology).
