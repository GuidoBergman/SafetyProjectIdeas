# Temperature/Sampling Variance x Single-Run Reporting: Stochasticity Hides Tail-Risk Harm

**Problem:** Failure mode A is sampling stochasticity (harm rate varies run-to-run). Failure mode B is single-run / low-sample reporting of safety scores. The compound effect: a single run can land on a benign sample while the harmful-completion probability in the tail is much higher, so reported safety understates worst-case harm.

**Direction:** For a fixed harmful-prompt set, sample an open model many times per prompt at deployment temperature. Estimate the per-prompt harm probability and the gap between a single-run point estimate and the multi-sample tail (e.g., max over k samples). Show how single-run reporting underestimates achievable harm.

**Why it matters:** Adversaries resample; safety reports often don't. Quantifying single-run vs best-of-k harm gives evaluators a concrete sampling-budget recommendation and shows that point estimates understate the harm an attacker can reach. Cheap, fully open, lab-independent.
