# Turpin's Answer-Always-A Bias Replicated on Modern Open Chat Models

**Problem:** Turpin et al. (2023) showed GPT-3.5 and Claude 1.0 would silently follow a 'reorder few-shot so the answer is always (A)' bias, dropping accuracy up to 36% without ever mentioning the bias in CoT. It is unknown whether 2025-era open chat models (Llama-3, Qwen2.5, Mistral) still fall for this classic manipulation.

**Direction:** Reimplement the answer-position bias and the 'I think the answer is X' suggestion bias on a few open instruction-tuned models using a slice of BIG-Bench Hard. Measure accuracy drop under bias and the rate at which CoT acknowledges the bias. Twist: new model generation + cheap reproduction of a foundational result that has never been re-run on today's small open models.

**Why it matters:** Confirms whether a well-known, easily-detectable failure mode has been quietly fixed or persists in the open models most accessible to independent researchers. A clean, beginner-friendly reproduction of a landmark paper. No training required.

**Relevant context:** Turpin et al. 'Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (arXiv 2305.04388): answer-always-A reordering and suggested-answer biases caused up to 36% accuracy drop on 13 BBH tasks, with biases systematically unmentioned in CoT. Public code at github.com/milesaturpin/cot-unfaithfulness.
