# Deception-Probe Domain-Shift Fragility Replicated on a New Behavior

**Problem:** Linear deception probes were found 'particularly vulnerable to domain shift,' especially on on-policy incentivized test sets, but this fragility result was characterized for a fixed set of behaviors (deception, sycophancy, authority, sandbagging).

**Direction:** Replicate the domain-shift fragility experiment: train a probe on an off-policy deception dataset, then test on an on-policy, incentivized elicitation of a DIFFERENT misbehavior (e.g., reward-hacking justification or covert rule-breaking) on the same open model. Measure the in-domain vs shifted AUC drop and report whether the published fragility pattern generalizes.

**Why it matters:** Probe robustness under realistic distribution shift determines whether white-box monitors survive deployment. Independently replicating the fragility on new behaviors, with no training of the base model, is high-value reproducibility work.

**Relevant context:** 'That's not natural: impact of off-policy training data on probe performance' (OpenReview IOR5bkd0ep): deception/sycophancy/authority/sandbagging vulnerable to domain shift on on-policy sets. 'Caught in the Act' (arXiv 2508.19505).
