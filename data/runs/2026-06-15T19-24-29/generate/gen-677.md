# Semantic-Gadget Chaining on Open VLMs: How Many Hops Until Refusal Fails?

**Problem:** Reasoning-Oriented Programming (arXiv:2603.09246) jailbreaks VLMs by chaining benign 'semantic gadgets' into a harmful whole. The relationship between chain LENGTH (number of gadgets) and success — and whether longer chains evade safety better — is not characterized on accessible open VLMs.

**Direction:** Reproduce a simplified gadget-chaining attack on 1-2 small open VLMs. Decompose ~40 harmful tasks into variable numbers of benign sub-steps (2, 3, 4 gadgets) and measure success vs chain length. Test whether a simple 'recompose-and-check' defense (ask the model to summarize the combined intent before answering) reduces success.

**Why it matters:** Compositional attacks that hide intent across benign pieces are a fundamental, hard-to-filter threat; mapping the length/success curve and a cheap recomposition defense is reproducible out-of-lab on open VLMs and addresses fuzzy intent-detection.

**Relevant context:** Reasoning-Oriented Programming: Chaining Semantic Gadgets to Jailbreak Large Vision Language Models (arXiv:2603.09246, March 2026). Related: Jailbreaks on VLM via Multimodal Reasoning (arXiv:2601.22398) with its Thought-Action-Observation loop; PRISM (arXiv:2508.18649) safety baseline.
