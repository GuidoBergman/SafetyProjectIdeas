# Treaty-Verification Checklist from Arms Control Applied to AI Commitments

**Problem:** International AI coordination borrows the *language* of arms control but rarely the *verification engineering*. Most AI commitments lack the verifiability features that made arms-control treaties enforceable.

**Direction:** Transfer the arms-control verification taxonomy (declared baselines, on-site inspection rights, remote monitoring, challenge inspections, non-compliance triggers). Build a rubric and score a corpus of public AI commitments (Frontier AI Safety Commitments, Code of Practice, Bletchley/Seoul declarations) on which verification features they contain.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the verifiability gap in current international AI agreements, directly serving the open problem of third-party-auditable safety claims and international coordination. Public-document analysis.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2004.07213 (Mechanisms for Supporting Verifiable Claims); Schelling & Halperin 'Strategy and Arms Control'; Frontier AI Safety Commitments; International Network of AI Safety Institutes.
