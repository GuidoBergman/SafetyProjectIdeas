# A reproducible audit of AI-discovered SMT safety conditions

**Problem:** 'Emergent Formal Verification' (March 2026) reports an autonomous AI ecosystem independently produced Z3/SMT safety conditions with claimed 100% accuracy across six domains. Such 'AI-generated proofs' must be independently re-checked — a perfect-accuracy claim is a red flag.

**Direction:** Take a small set of the kind of safety properties described (or analogous toy ones), have an open LLM generate SMT/Z3 encodings, then independently run Z3 to check soundness and look for vacuous/trivially-true encodings (a common failure where the spec is satisfiable but doesn't capture the intended property). Success = a tally of genuinely-verified vs vacuous/incorrect AI-generated specs.

**Why it matters:** As AI systems start writing their own safety proofs, cheap independent verification of those proofs becomes critical infrastructure. Z3 runs on a laptop; this is squarely lab-independent oversight work.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Emergent Formal Verification: How an Autonomous AI Ecosystem Independently Discovered SMT-Based Safety Across Six Domains' (arXiv:2603.21149, March 2026); uses the open Z3 solver.
