# Sycophancy x Jailbreak: Does Agreeing With the User First Lower the Refusal Wall?

**Problem:** Sycophancy (agreeing with a stated user belief) and jailbreaking (added framing flips behavior) are both 'failures of invariance'. They may compound: once a model has been led to agree with a benign-but-leading user premise, its refusal threshold for a subsequent harmful request may drop.

**Direction:** Take a small open model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct / Qwen). For a set of harmful requests from a public refusal benchmark, compare refusal rate (a) cold, vs (b) after a 2-3 turn 'rapport' preamble in which the user states agreeable opinions the model sycophantically endorses. Measure the refusal-rate delta as the compound effect. No training needed.

**Why it matters:** If sycophantic rapport-building reliably erodes refusals, it is a cheap multi-turn attack vector that single-turn safety benchmarks miss entirely. Names the two interacting failure modes: sycophancy and jailbreak/refusal-bypass.

**Relevant context:** DuET-PD and 'Consistency Training Helps Stop Sycophancy and Jailbreaks' (arXiv:2510.27062) frame sycophancy and jailbreaks as the same invariance failure; emphasizing helpfulness 'may amplify the risks of LLM misuses' and risks are 'amplified in multi-turn interactions, enabling more sophisticated and harder-to-detect jailbreaks'.
