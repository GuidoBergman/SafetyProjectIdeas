# Are Reasoning Models Actually Better Calibrated? Verbalized Confidence vs. Correctness

**Problem:** It's hypothesized that reasoning models, being more introspective, should produce better-calibrated verbalized confidence. 'Do Reasoning Models Know When They're Right?' began probing this but the calibration-vs-reasoning relationship across accessible models is unsettled.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA and TruthfulQA, elicit verbalized confidence (0-100%) from open reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) and matched non-reasoning instruct models. Plot reliability diagrams and compute ECE for each. Test whether reasoning-mode-on improves calibration relative to reasoning-off on the same base, and whether confidence stated mid-CoT differs from final-answer confidence.

**Why it matters:** Honest, well-calibrated uncertainty is a core component of honesty. Whether 'thinking' improves calibration is decision-relevant for trusting reasoning-model self-reports, and the experiment uses only public benchmarks and a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** 'Thinking Out Loud: Do Reasoning Models Know When They're Right?' (arXiv:2504.06564, EMNLP 2025); LLM Honesty Survey (TMLR 2025). SimpleQA and TruthfulQA (817 Qs) are public. Verbalized confidence is standard but often overconfident.
