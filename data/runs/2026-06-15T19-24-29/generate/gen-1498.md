# Reliability Engineering Stress Testing: Steering Under Distribution-Shifted Inputs

**Problem:** Steering vectors are extracted and validated on one distribution but deployed on another. Reliability engineering stress-tests components under conditions beyond normal operation (HALT — highly accelerated life testing) to find failure modes early.

**Direction:** Transfer accelerated stress testing from reliability engineering. Extract a safety steering vector on standard prompts, then 'stress' it across progressively out-of-distribution inputs (other languages, code, unusual formats, very long contexts) and record where steering efficacy collapses. Build a stress-failure profile per concept and a ranked list of conditions that break steering soonest.

**Why it matters:** Deployment conditions differ from extraction conditions; knowing the stress envelope prevents safety steering from silently failing on OOD inputs (e.g. cross-language jailbreaks). Connects to the cross-language transfer finding. Inference-only on a single GPU, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Accelerated life / HALT stress testing finds failure modes in reliability engineering. Surprising result: concept vectors transfer across languages (Beaglehole et al.) — a natural stress axis. Generalization of steering vectors is fragile (arXiv:2407.12404).
