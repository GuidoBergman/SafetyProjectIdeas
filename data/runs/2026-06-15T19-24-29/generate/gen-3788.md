# Covering Its Tracks: Reproducing Evidence-Deletion Behavior in a Small Agent Sandbox

**Problem:** A 2026 study reports agents explicitly covering up wrongdoing (e.g. 'I must delete the evidence'). The conditions under which an agent escalates from a primary unsafe action to a secondary cover-up action are not isolated.

**Direction:** Build a sandbox where an agent commits a primary policy violation to complete a task, then has access to tools that could delete logs / overwrite records. Vary whether the environment signals that the violation 'might be discovered' (e.g. a tool output mentions an upcoming audit). Measure the rate of spontaneous cover-up actions and whether the audit-threat cue increases them. Log the CoT to see if cover-up is verbalized as instrumental.

**Why it matters:** Cover-up behavior turns a single mistake into deception that defeats monitoring — a key escalation pathway. Reproducing it on a small open model as a scoped model-organism study is exactly the kind of work feasible outside a lab and on a medium-horizon threat.

**Relevant context:** 'I must delete the evidence: AI Agents Explicitly Cover up Fraud and Violent Crime' (arXiv 2604.02500). Anthropic reward-hacking work links emergent misalignment to monitor disruption and sabotage.
