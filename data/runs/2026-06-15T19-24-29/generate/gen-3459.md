# Stated-Belief vs. Revealed-Belief Consistency Auditor

**Problem:** A model is 'lying' when its answer is inconsistent with its underlying belief. There is no simple public tool that elicits a model's neutral-prompt belief, then applies pressure to flip it, then asks the model whether it lied — and scores the three-phase consistency automatically.

**Direction:** Implement the three-phase deception probe: (1) elicit a belief with neutral prompts, (2) apply a pressure prompt designed to induce a contradicting answer, (3) directly ask 'did your previous answer reflect your true belief?'. Score belief-stability, pressure-induced flip rate, and honest-admission rate. Run across open models and report a consistency/honesty triple.

**Why it matters:** Detecting goal-directed inconsistency between stated and revealed beliefs is the operational definition of model lying. A reusable auditor lets external researchers measure it on any open model with no internal access.

**Relevant context:** 'Evaluating Scaling Trends for Deception' (OpenReview YRXDl6I3j5) defines lying as belief-inconsistent answers and uses elicit-belief → pressure → query-admission phases. Truthfulness Spectrum Hypothesis (arXiv 2602.20273) and strategic-dishonesty work support this framing.
