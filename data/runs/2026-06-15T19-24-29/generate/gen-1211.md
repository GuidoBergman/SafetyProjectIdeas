# Quantifying the 'Effective Number of Models' Behind a Set of AI Products

**Problem:** End users see many AI products and assume diversity, but the concentration-risk link is 'many branded products -> few underlying base models -> hidden monoculture.' The missing measurement: behaviorally infer how many DISTINCT models actually sit behind a set of accessible AI products/endpoints, exposing illusory diversity.

**Direction:** Probe several accessible AI products/endpoints with a fixed behavioral fingerprint battery (idiosyncratic responses, refusal styles, formatting tics). Cluster endpoints by behavioral fingerprint to estimate the 'effective number of distinct models.' Report cases where ostensibly different products fingerprint as the same underlying model.

**Why it matters:** Gives regulators and users a behavioral method to detect hidden concentration even without disclosure — exactly the monitoring capability the EU AI Act systemic-risk regime needs. Fully external, no lab access, modest probe set.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in distillation-fingerprinting/quantification (arXiv 2501.12619) and Artificial Hivemind output-similarity methods (arXiv 2510.22954). Targets the concentration-risk monitoring open problem and International AI Safety Report 2026.
