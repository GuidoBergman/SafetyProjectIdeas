# Backcasting Calibrated Self-Knowledge: Do LLMs Satisfy a Formal Reflection Consistency Axiom?

**Problem:** A success state in learning-theoretic alignment is agents whose stated beliefs satisfy basic coherence axioms (e.g., reflection: the agent's expectation of its own future estimate equals its current estimate). If models violate reflection, downstream guarantees built on coherent beliefs collapse. This is directly measurable on open models.

**Direction:** Operationalize the reflection principle: ask a model P(event), then ask its expectation of what it will say tomorrow / after seeing evidence E, and compare to its actual updated answer. Measure violation magnitude across question types. Also test van-Fraassen-style reflection and conditionalization coherence. Pure inference on open models with elicited probabilities.

**Why it matters:** Working backward from 'agents have coherent beliefs we can reason about', the precondition is measurable reflection-consistency. Quantifying violations grounds the learning-theoretic agenda's coherence assumptions in current systems, with no training and no lab data.
