# Distillation Robustifies Unlearning: A Small-Scale Replication

**Problem:** A striking claim is that distilling an unlearned (suppressed) model into a fresh network makes the unlearning far more robust to relearning than the suppressed model itself. This has not been reproduced at small scale by independent researchers.

**Direction:** Vary scale and model in the distillation-robustifies-unlearning experiment (arXiv:2506.06278). On a small open model, take a suppressed/unlearned checkpoint, distill it into a same-architecture student via short logit-matching on benign data, then run a relearning attack (sweep of forget examples) on both suppressed and distilled versions and compare recovery curves.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap distillation step genuinely hardens unlearning, that is an actionable safety recipe anyone releasing open weights could adopt. Independent small-scale replication of a strong claim is high counterfactual value; fits a single GPU with small models and short distillation.

**Relevant context:** 'Distillation Robustifies Unlearning' (arXiv:2506.06278): distilling a suppressed model into a fresh network outperforms RepNoise and SAM against relearning. Relearning-attack protocol (arXiv:2502.05374), WMDP evaluation (arXiv:2403.03218).
