# Cross-Layer Feature Persistence: Does the Same Concept Recur or Move?

**Problem:** SAEs are usually trained/studied at a single layer. Whether a given concept is represented by 'the same' feature across adjacent layers (persistent) or handed off/transformed (moving) is central to circuit tracing but rarely measured with a simple cross-layer matching experiment on public SAEs.

**Direction:** Using GPT-2-small SAEs available at multiple layers, take an interpreted feature at layer L and find its best-matching feature at layers L-1 and L+1 by decoder-direction cosine similarity and co-activation. Measure how persistent concepts are across layers and at which layers features appear/disappear. Map a few safety-relevant concepts' layerwise lifecycle. Inference only.

**Why it matters:** Knowing whether a concept persists or migrates across layers tells a monitor designer where to attach probes and is a building block for attribution-graph-style tracing — a tractable, fully-open slice of the circuit-tracing agenda for newcomers.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic cross-layer transcoders and attribution graphs track features across layers; pretrained GPT-2-small SAEs exist at several layers via SAELens/Neuronpedia. Decoder-cosine matching is simple linear algebra.
