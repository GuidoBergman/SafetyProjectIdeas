# Game-Theoretic Trust-Game Probes for Multi-Agent Collusion

**Problem:** Multi-agent systems risk agents colluding against oversight, but collusion propensity is hard to measure cleanly.

**Direction:** Import the trust game / repeated prisoner's dilemma from experimental economics. Pit pairs of agents in repeated games where defecting against the operator (e.g. covertly splitting a forbidden task) yields mutual benefit, and measure how readily they coordinate on the collusive equilibrium, how communication channels (open vs covert) change it, and whether a monitor agent deters collusion. Uses small open/API models as model organisms of collusion.

**Why it matters:** Gives a controlled, quantitative measure of emergent collusion — a key multi-agent governance risk — using cheap canonical economic games, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Experimental economics uses trust games and repeated PD to study cooperation/defection; arXiv 2508.05687 flags collusion as a governed-multi-agent risk. Operationalizing collusion-against-operator as a repeated economic game with monitor interventions is novel for agentic safety.
