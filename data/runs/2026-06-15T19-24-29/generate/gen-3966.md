# Does Debate Actually Help a Weak Judge Supervise a Strong Expert? A Scaled-Down Replication

**Problem:** The scalable-oversight case for debate rests on the claim that a weaker judge, watching two stronger debaters, reaches more accurate verdicts than judging alone. Replications at accessible scale that isolate when this helps vs hurts are valuable and rare for newcomers.

**Direction:** On QA tasks where the judge model is deliberately weaker (smaller open model or restricted context) than the debater models, compare judge accuracy in three conditions: judge-alone, consultancy (one persuasive expert), and debate (two opposing experts). Measure accuracy uplift and where persuasion of wrong answers wins, across question difficulty.

**Why it matters:** Tests the foundational empirical premise of debate as scalable oversight with a clean weak-judge/strong-expert asymmetry. Inference-only, reproducible, and directly decision-relevant to whether oversight protocols scale — a non-lab-dependent contribution.

**Relevant context:** 'Debate Helps Supervise Unreliable Experts' (arXiv 2311.08702); 'An alignment safety case sketch based on debate' (arXiv 2505.03989) lay out the weak-judge premise.
