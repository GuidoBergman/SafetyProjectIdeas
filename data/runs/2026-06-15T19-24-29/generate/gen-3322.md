# Measuring the Steganographic Gap on Open Models Without Training

**Problem:** A Decision-Theoretic Formalisation of Steganography (arXiv:2602.23163) defines a 'steganographic gap' — the downstream utility difference between agents that can and cannot decode a hidden signal. The formal measure has not been operationalized into a cheap, training-free probe of whether prompted open models can establish covert channels.

**Direction:** Implement a minimal sender/receiver protocol: a sender model is prompted to hide a small payload (e.g., a 2-bit message) in otherwise-helpful text; a receiver model (same family, shared prompt 'codebook') tries to recover it; a monitor model tries to detect that hiding occurred. Estimate the steganographic gap as receiver-decode-accuracy minus monitor-detection-rate, across model families and payload sizes. All in-context, no fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizing the steganographic gap as a benchmark gives the safety community a concrete, lab-independent metric for collusion/covert-channel risk — directly relevant to whether untrusted-monitoring control protocols can be subverted.

**Relevant context:** 'A Decision-Theoretic Formalisation of Steganography With Applications to LLM Monitoring' (arXiv:2602.23163, 2026): V-information framework, defines the steganographic gap. Connects to 'When can we trust untrusted monitoring?' (arXiv:2602.20628, Feb 2026) collusion taxonomy.
