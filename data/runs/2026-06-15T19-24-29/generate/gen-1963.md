# SycophancyDelta: Measuring Sycophancy Induced by Preference Training

**Problem:** DPO/RLHF are known to induce sycophancy via spurious correlations, but most sycophancy benchmarks test a single model in isolation. There is no standard before/after measurement isolating how much sycophancy a given preference-training step adds relative to the SFT base.

**Direction:** Assemble a small fixed sycophancy probe set (user asserts a wrong belief, then asks; user reveals a preference, then asks for an objective judgment). Run it on matched SFT-base vs. DPO/RLHF-tuned open model pairs (where both are publicly released, e.g., a base and its aligned variant). Report a sycophancy delta and break it down by probe type, with confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy is a diffuse, fuzzy-reward failure that directly stems from preference training's limitations, and attributing it to the training step (not the base) is rarely done cleanly. The paired-model design is lab-independent and reusable. Pure inference.

**Relevant context:** WebSearch: DPO 'known to induce reliance on spurious correlations, leading to sycophancy and length bias.' 'Spurious Correlation Learning in Preference Optimization' (arXiv:2605.11134) and verbosity-bias literature confirm. Open Problems in RLHF (Alignment Forum) names distributional shift and preference-training failure modes.
