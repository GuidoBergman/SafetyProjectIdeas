# Does Fine-Tuning a Shared Base Inherit Its Blind Spots? A LoRA Replication

**Problem:** Concentration risk argues that downstream systems built on the same foundation model inherit its blind spots. This is asserted but rarely demonstrated cheaply: do several different fine-tunes of one open base retain correlated failures from the base?

**Direction:** Take one open base model and a few of its existing community fine-tunes (or train 2-3 small LoRA adapters on different small datasets — within a few hours on one GPU), then replicate error-correlation/homogeneity measurement among the fine-tunes and against the base. Twist: test whether the shared base's specific errors persist across all its descendants regardless of fine-tuning data.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating inherited correlated failures from a shared base is the concrete mechanism behind foundation-model concentration risk — a key open problem. Small LoRA fine-tunes stay within the consumer-GPU/few-hours budget.

**Relevant context:** Replicates the 'shared foundationality causes correlated failures' claim (Correlated Errors arXiv:2506.07962; International AI Safety Report 2026) using one open base + lightweight LoRA fine-tunes.
