# Can Filtering Defeat Subliminal Transfer? Testing Aggressive Data Sanitization

**Problem:** Subliminal learning persists even after explicit trait references are removed from distillation data. The practical defensive question — whether MORE aggressive, automated sanitization (statistical re-normalization, shuffling, re-encoding of number sequences) reduces transfer — is unresolved and directly actionable.

**Direction:** Reproduce a minimal subliminal-transfer setup with number-sequence distillation on small open models. Apply a ladder of sanitization treatments to the teacher data (raw → digit-shuffled → re-quantized → re-sampled from matched marginal distribution) and measure residual trait transfer at each level. Identify which, if any, sanitization breaks the hidden channel.

**Why it matters:** Distillation is ubiquitous and developers assume filtering protects them. Empirically mapping which sanitizations actually break subliminal channels gives practitioners a concrete defensive checklist — high-value diffuse-defense work needing no proprietary infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** "Subliminal Learning" (arXiv:2507.14805; Nature April 2026): transfer persists after filtering explicit references; "Quantifying Subliminal Behavioral Transfer Ratios in Language Model Distillation" (arXiv:2606.11270) provides transfer-ratio metrics to reuse.
