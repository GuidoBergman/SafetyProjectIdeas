# Steering-Vector Dose-Response: Linearity of Behavioral Effect vs Injection Strength

**Problem:** Activation steering adds a direction scaled by a coefficient. Papers often pick one coefficient. The full dose-response curve — how behavior changes as injection strength increases, and where coherence collapses — is rarely charted across multiple concepts in one controlled study.

**Direction:** On an open model, take 2-3 steering directions (refusal, sentiment, a topic). Sweep the injection coefficient over a wide range and measure both the targeted behavioral metric and a coherence/perplexity metric at each strength. Plot dose-response curves and identify the usable window before incoherence. Compare windows across concepts. Inference only.

**Why it matters:** Steering is proposed both for safety control and as a red-team tool; knowing the safe operating window and where it breaks is essential for any deployment use and for understanding adversarial steering. A clean dose-response characterization is cheap, reproducible, and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Representation-engineering / activation-steering literature ('In-Distribution Steering', 'Predicting Where Steering Vectors Succeed', 2024-2026) notes coherence breakdown at high strength but rarely charts full curves per concept. Steering hooks in TransformerLens/repeng.
