# Does Chain-of-Thought Prompting Help or Hurt Jailbreak Robustness?

**Problem:** Reasoning/deliberation has been argued to make models safer (deliberative alignment), but eliciting CoT in non-reasoning open models via prompting could either deepen safety or give the model room to talk itself into compliance. The net effect on small open models is unclear.

**Direction:** On small open instruct models (not native reasoners), compare jailbreak success with and without an added 'think step by step before answering' instruction across prefill and many-shot attacks. Inspect the generated reasoning for cases where CoT rationalizes compliance vs where it reinforces refusal. Quantify net robustness change.

**Why it matters:** Deployers increasingly bolt CoT prompting onto cheap models; knowing whether this is a free safety win or a footgun is practically important and lab-independent. Reading the reasoning traces also yields qualitative model-organism insight into 'attacking without honest reasoning.'

**Relevant context:** 'Deliberative Alignment: Reasoning Enables Safer Language Models' (arXiv:2412.16339) argues reasoning improves safety; the StrongREJECT writeup notes o1 is more jailbreak-robust. This varies the population to prompted (non-native) CoT on small open models, an untested regime. Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) and many-shot (NeurIPS 2024) supply attacks.
