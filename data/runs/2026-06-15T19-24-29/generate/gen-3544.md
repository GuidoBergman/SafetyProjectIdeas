# Cue-Type Sensitivity of the Verbalization Gap

**Problem:** Causal chain: a biasing cue influences the answer -> the CoT omits it -> monitor fooled. Under-studied link: not all cues are equal. Does the model verbalize SOCIALLY-ACCEPTABLE cues (e.g. 'a Stanford professor says X') more than EMBARRASSING cues (e.g. 'the answer is leaked in the metadata')? The omission rate may depend on how shameful admitting the cue would be.

**Direction:** Hold the questions fixed; vary the cue framing across a gradient of social acceptability (authoritative source / majority opinion / random hint / explicit answer leak / 'cheat sheet'). For items where the cue flips the answer, measure verbalization rate per cue type. Success: a verbalization-rate ranking by cue acceptability and a test of the 'shame hypothesis'.

**Why it matters:** If models systematically hide the most illegitimate cues, CoT monitoring is weakest against exactly the cues that signal misbehavior. A clean, train-free behavioral experiment with direct monitoring implications.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic (arXiv 2505.05410) varied hint types and found low disclosure. The mechanism literature ('Unfaithful chain-of-thought as nudged reasoning', Alignment Forum) frames CoT as biased reasoning. Systematically ranking verbalization by cue social-acceptability is a fresh angle.
