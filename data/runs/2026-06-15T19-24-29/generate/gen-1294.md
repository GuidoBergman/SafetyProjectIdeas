# Replicating Algorithmic Monoculture in Hiring with Open LLM Resume Screeners

**Problem:** Bommasani et al. (Algorithmic Monocultures in Hiring) found correlated, homogeneous rejection outcomes when one vendor screens many applicants — using proprietary pymetrics data unavailable to outside researchers. The LLM analogue (many firms using GPT/Claude/Llama to screen resumes) is exactly what an independent researcher can test with synthetic resumes.

**Direction:** Generate a synthetic but realistic pool of ~200 resumes across demographics/skill profiles. Have ~6 LLMs each score/rank them for a few job descriptions. Measure systemic rejection rate (fraction of candidates rejected by ALL models) vs. an independent-decisions null, and test for demographic disparity in homogeneous rejections. Mirror the paper's outcome-homogenization metric.

**Why it matters:** Replicating a proprietary-data finding with fully open, synthetic data is high counterfactual value: it lets the public audit a real allocational harm (being locked out of all jobs at once) without lab/vendor access. Medium-horizon societal risk.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Algorithmic Monocultures in Hiring' (Bommasani et al., arXiv:2605.27371, FAccT 2026, June 2026) from a single proprietary vendor to publicly-accessible LLM screeners; builds on outcome-homogenization framing.
