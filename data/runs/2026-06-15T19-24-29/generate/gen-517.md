# Counterfactual unfaithfulness of attribution: when do saliency/attention 'explanations' contradict causal interventions?

**Problem:** Formal hopes for explanation-based oversight assume an explanation (attention weights, gradient saliency) reflects the causal computation. A known failure is that these explanations diverge from causal interventions. The conditions under which cheap explanations are causally misleading, on small open models, are worth charting as a faithfulness boundary.

**Direction:** On a small open model and a set of classification/QA tasks, compute a cheap explanation (attention rollout or gradient saliency) for which input tokens matter, then causally test each token via ablation/patching to measure true importance. Quantify the correlation between explanation-importance and causal-importance, and identify task/input regimes where they sharply diverge (the explanation lies).

**Why it matters:** Tests whether cheap, scalable explanations can ever serve as faithful audit signals — central to formal mechanistic-explanation hopes (ARC) — with a fully reproducible, lab-independent methodology.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in attribution-faithfulness and causal-intervention (activation patching) literature; the CoT-faithfulness perturbation methodology (arXiv:2503.08679) transfers directly. Open model, single GPU.
