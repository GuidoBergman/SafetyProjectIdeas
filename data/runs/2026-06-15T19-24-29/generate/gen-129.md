# Harmful-Quality Ceiling: When Jailbroken Models Hallucinate Danger

**Problem:** Concern about jailbreaks assumes harmful outputs are accurate; but if jailbroken models frequently produce confidently wrong harmful content (e.g., incorrect synthesis steps), the genuine uplift is overstated, and this accuracy dimension is rarely measured.

**Direction:** For a set of verifiable borderline-harmful tasks (e.g., basic chemistry/biology facts with known ground truth, security trivia), jailbreak an open model and score not just willingness but factual accuracy of the harmful content against ground truth. Report a willingness-vs-accuracy breakdown.

**Why it matters:** Reframes uplift assessment around accuracy, a neglected, fuzzy-reward dimension central to whether jailbreaks pose real-world danger. Uses only public factual ground truth and an open model — strongly counterfactual and feasible, with clear success criteria.

**Relevant context:** StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) found jailbreaks reduce capability; uplift debates (e.g., bio/chem) hinge on accuracy. Measuring factual accuracy of jailbroken harmful outputs against verifiable ground truth as a robustness dimension is largely unexplored.
