# Do LLM agents converge on power-seeking in text social environments, as the gridworld theory predicts?

**Problem:** Beyond gridworlds, recent work found LLMs fine-tuned to maximize game-reward take the MOST power-seeking actions in text-based social environments — an empirical echo of the formal power-seeking theorems. Whether this holds for non-fine-tuned, off-the-shelf models and how it scales with stated goals is an open follow-up.

**Direction:** Construct several lightweight text 'social dilemma' scenarios offering a power-accumulating option (hoarding resources, seizing control) vs. a directly-rewarding option. Prompt off-the-shelf open models of different sizes with varied goal framings and measure how often they choose the power-seeking action. Test whether more capable models or more open-ended goals increase power-seeking, as the theory's reachability argument suggests.

**Why it matters:** It tests whether the structural power-seeking prediction transfers from formal MDPs to LLM behavior without any fine-tuning — a model-organism-style probe of instrumental convergence, a medium/long-horizon threat. Open models, one GPU, no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:1912.01683 (power-seeking theorem) and the cited empirical finding that game-reward-tuned LLMs take the most power-seeking actions. Context methodology: decision/game theory.
