# Reproducing the Debate Advantage With Asymmetric-Skill Debaters

**Problem:** Debate experiments usually pit two equally-capable debaters. In reality one side defending the truth may be weaker than a strong adversary defending a lie. How the judge's accuracy changes when the truthful debater is weaker than the deceptive one is an underexplored asymmetry.

**Direction:** Replicate the debate-judging setup but assign the correct-answer debater a smaller open model and the wrong-answer debater a larger open model (and vice versa). Measure judge accuracy across the four skill-pairing conditions to see whether a strong liar can overpower a weak truth-teller in front of a weak judge.

**Why it matters:** A central scalable-oversight fear is a highly capable deceptive system out-arguing weaker honest signals. Testing whether debate's truth advantage survives when deception has the capability edge directly stresses the protocol's core safety assumption. Open models only, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 2024 and Khan et al. 2024 generally use symmetric debaters and find debate beats consultancy. The asymmetric-skill (strong liar vs weak truth-teller) variation is the twist, probing debate's robustness to capability-asymmetric deception.
