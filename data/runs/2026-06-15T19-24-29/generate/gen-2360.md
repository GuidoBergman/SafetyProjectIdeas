# Distractor Evidence: Can a Debater Win by Burying the Key Fact?

**Problem:** A dishonest debater may not lie outright but flood the judge with true-but-irrelevant evidence to bury the one decisive fact. Causal chain: decisive fact exists -> dishonest side adds many true distractors -> judge's attention diluted -> decisive fact under-weighted -> wrong verdict. The under-studied link is the judge's vulnerability to evidence-dilution as opposed to outright falsehood.

**Direction:** On QuALITY-style items where one quote is decisive, construct debate inputs that pad the dishonest side with additional true-but-irrelevant quotes. Measure how the small judge's accuracy declines as the number of distractor quotes grows, and test a 'rank the single most decisive piece of evidence' prompt as a mitigation.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishes a subtle, deniable oversight-evasion strategy (dilution) from detectable lying, and prototypes a cheap mitigation. Controlled, public-data, single-GPU, strongly counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** Obfuscated-arguments and persuasion literature (arXiv:2402.06782; alignment-forum debate posts) focus on false claims; evidence-dilution via true distractors as an oversight-evasion tactic is under-studied and cheap to probe with QuALITY-style asymmetry.
