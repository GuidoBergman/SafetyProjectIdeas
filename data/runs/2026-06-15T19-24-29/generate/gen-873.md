# The Voluntary-to-Binding Gap: Tracing Where EU Code of Practice Commitments Lose Their Teeth

**Problem:** EU compliance chain: 'AI Act obligation' -> 'Code of Practice commitment' -> 'signatory adopts' -> 'AI Office monitors' -> 'sanction on breach'. Because the Code is voluntary and breaches are only 'mitigating factors' in fines, the monitor-to-sanction link is structurally weak, the precise point the chain stops being enforceable.

**Direction:** Map each GPAI Code of Practice commitment to (a) whether it restates a binding Act obligation or adds a voluntary extra, and (b) what the stated consequence of non-adherence is. Produce a typology of commitments by enforceability and quantify what fraction of safety-relevant commitments have no consequence beyond 'increased scrutiny'.

**Why it matters:** The voluntary-codes-vs-enforceable-standards gap is a named open problem for the EU AI Office. A clean enforceability taxonomy shows which safety commitments will likely be ignored absent binding rules. Pure policy-document analysis, ideal for a beginner, valuable independent of any lab.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act GPAI Code of Practice (code-of-practice.ai); EU AI Office; arXiv:2407.07300 'From Principles to Rules: A Regulatory Approach for Frontier AI'.
