# Refusal Brittleness x Over-Blocking Monitor: Compounding Toward Useless or Unsafe

**Problem:** Two failures interact along the helpful/harmless axis: a brittle refusal boundary (alignment robustness) and a monitor tuned to block aggressively (control). Together they can push a system into either useless over-refusal on benign tasks OR, when relaxed to stay useful, a monitor too permissive to catch real harm. The two failures define a bad frontier.

**Direction:** On a mixed benign/harmful task set, vary monitor strictness and measure both benign-task blocking (over-refusal) and harmful-task miss rate. Map the joint operating curve and show how refusal brittleness shrinks the usable region where the system is both helpful and safe.

**Why it matters:** Deployers must pick an operating point; quantifying the compound trade-off frontier helps them avoid the false choice. Pure evaluation, public datasets, no training.

**Relevant context:** Output validation among runtime controls (AISI); harmful/benign activations overlap and aren't linearly separable in most layers (arXiv:2412.17034) — the root of the trade-off.
