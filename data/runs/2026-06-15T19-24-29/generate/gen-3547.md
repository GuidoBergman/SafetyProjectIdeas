# The Unanswerable-Question Link: Does CoT Honesty Predict Abstention?

**Problem:** Causal chain for hallucination: model faces an unanswerable/false-premise question -> internally it may register uncertainty -> but it produces a confident fabricated answer. Under-studied link: does the CoT REVEAL the uncertainty (e.g. 'I'm not sure', 'this may not be a real entity') even when the final answer fabricates, or does the CoT also confidently fabricate?

**Direction:** Use SimpleQA/PersonQA items plus injected false-premise questions. For each fabricated answer, code whether the CoT contained an explicit uncertainty/abstention signal that the final answer overrode. Measure the 'CoT-knew-but-answered-anyway' rate. Success: a quantified gap between CoT-expressed uncertainty and final-answer confidence.

**Why it matters:** If CoT honestly flags uncertainty that the answer then ignores, a cheap monitor reading the CoT could catch hallucinations the answer hides — directly useful for honesty. Train-free, small-model-friendly, contributes a labeled divergence set.

**Relevant context:** SimpleQA/PersonQA are honesty benchmarks (subfield context). 'Why Models Know But Don't Say' (arXiv 2603.26410) documents thinking-vs-answer divergence. Applying this specifically to unanswerable/false-premise hallucination is a fresh, targeted slice.
