# Backcasting refusal monitoring: do concept-vector probes catch jailbreaks that output filters miss?

**Problem:** A safety success state is: deployed open models flag their own unsafe compliance before emitting it. That requires an internal signal that fires on adversarially-disguised harmful requests even when the text output still looks like a normal refusal-or-comply decision. It is unknown how much earlier-warning an activation probe gives over an output classifier.

**Direction:** On a small open chat model (e.g. Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B), extract a 'harmful-compliance' concept vector via contrastive activation pairs (harmful vs benign requests). Build a linear probe on residual-stream activations at the last prompt token. Compare its AUROC at detecting harmful intent on (a) plain harmful prompts and (b) jailbreak-prefixed harmful prompts, against a keyword/output-text classifier. Report where the probe wins and fails.

**Why it matters:** Output-only filters are exactly what jailbreaks are tuned to bypass; an internal probe is a complementary line of defense. Quantifying the counterfactual gain of internal probes on disguised attacks is decision-relevant infrastructure that any open-model deployer could reuse, and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Builds on Beaglehole et al. (Science 2026) finding that internal probes outperform judge models, and on findings that jailbreak prefixes push harmful activations into the benign region (Rogue Scalpel, arXiv 2509.22067; Steering Externalities, arXiv 2602.04896). CAA from Rimsky et al. (arXiv 2312.06681) gives the contrastive-pair recipe.
