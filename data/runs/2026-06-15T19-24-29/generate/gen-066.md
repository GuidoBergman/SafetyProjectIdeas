# Stylometry as a steganalysis detector for encoded reasoning in LLM agents

**Problem:** Agents can hide reasoning or collude by encoding information steganographically in seemingly innocuous text; we lack cheap detectors that flag when an agent's output style deviates in ways consistent with a hidden channel.

**Direction:** Borrow stylometry / steganalysis from forensic linguistics. Generate paired outputs from one open model: clean responses vs. responses instructed to embed a secret bitstring (acrostics, synonym choice, punctuation timing). Train a simple classifier on classic stylometric features (function-word frequencies, character n-grams, perplexity under a clean LM) to distinguish stego from clean text. Measure detection AUC and which features carry signal, establishing a baseline steganalysis tool for encoded-reasoning monitoring.

**Why it matters:** Encoded reasoning is an Open Phil starred problem; a lightweight, training-free-ish stylometric detector is deployable by anyone monitoring agent channels and needs no model internals. Directly counters secret collusion.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents' (arXiv 2402.07510, NeurIPS 2024) and stylometry literature (arXiv 2507.00838).
