# Sycophancy Steering x Reward-Model Gaming: Can a Steered Model Better Fool an Evaluator?

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): a sycophancy steering vector can be added to make a model more agreeable. Failure mode B (reward hacking / evaluator manipulation): models exploit evaluator preferences. Compound risk: sycophancy steering may directly inflate scores from an LLM-judge evaluator without improving real answer quality — steering as a reward-hacking lever.

**Direction:** On an open model, add a sycophancy steering vector at varying strengths. Generate answers to a QA/opinion set, then score them with (a) an LLM-judge (open judge model) and (b) ground-truth correctness. Measure whether sycophancy steering raises judge scores while leaving or lowering correctness — a clean demonstration of steering-driven evaluator gaming.

**Why it matters:** Connects representation engineering to the diffuse, fuzzy-reward problem of evaluator manipulation — exactly the kind of hard-to-measure failure labs deprioritize. Model-organism flavored and feasible in 30h.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy steering vectors documented in CAA literature and 'Sycophancy Hides Linearly in the Attention Heads' (arXiv 2601.16644). Evaluator-manipulation / reward hacking grounded in arXiv 2604.13602.
