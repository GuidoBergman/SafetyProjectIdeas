# Relearning Attacks: How Quickly Does 'Unlearned' Dangerous Knowledge Come Back?

**Problem:** Unlearning methods often leave knowledge latent: a small amount of fine-tuning on related data can restore 'forgotten' capabilities. The recovery rate as a function of relearning-data size is a clean, safety-critical failure to characterize on open models.

**Direction:** Take a small open model with a publicly available unlearned checkpoint (or apply a simple unlearning method like gradient ascent/RMU on a benign forget set such as a Harry Potter / specific-topic corpus), then fine-tune on tiny amounts of related data and measure how fast accuracy on the forget set recovers. Plot recovery vs. relearning examples and compare unlearning methods.

**Why it matters:** Unlearning is a proposed safety lever for removing dangerous knowledge; demonstrating that it is shallow and quickly reversible on accessible models is a high-value, lab-independent finding for medium-horizon misuse threat models.

**Relevant context:** An Adversarial Perspective on Machine Unlearning for AI Safety (arXiv:2409.18025) and Do Unlearning Methods Remove Information from Weights? (arXiv:2410.08827): unlearning is often superficial; relearning with a small subset of forget samples restores knowledge. WMDP/RMU benchmark context.
