# Does Random Activation Noise Break Refusal? A Replication and Sweep

**Problem:** A surprising recent claim is that adding random noise to activations at inference raises harmful-compliance from 0% to 1-13%, i.e., refusal can break without any learned attack direction. This is easy to misreport and deserves an independent replication with a noise-magnitude sweep.

**Direction:** Vary the random-steering experiment ('The Rogue Scalpel', arXiv:2509.22067) by replicating it on 2-3 small open instruct models with a systematic sweep of noise scale and target layer. Measure harmful-compliance and benign-task degradation jointly, and test whether the effect is a genuine refusal break or just incoherent output that a judge mis-scores.

**Why it matters:** If safety alignment is so fragile that random perturbations partially defeat it, that is a major robustness concern for open-weight deployment and a counterexample to 'alignment is deeply trained in'. Pure inference, single GPU, fundamentally about robustness of trained-in safety.

**Relevant context:** 'The Rogue Scalpel: Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety' (arXiv:2509.22067): random-direction activation noise raises harmful compliance from 0% to 1-13%. Arditi et al. NeurIPS 2024 refusal-direction framework.
