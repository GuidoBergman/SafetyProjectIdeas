# ProbeRobust: Stress-Testing Concept Probes Against Paraphrase and Noise

**Problem:** Internal concept probes are reported to beat judges, but their robustness to benign input perturbations (paraphrase, typos, formatting) is unmeasured, leaving open whether they fail silently on real-world inputs.

**Direction:** Take a trained concept probe (e.g. toxicity or hallucination) on an open model and evaluate its AUC under paraphrase, character-noise, and formatting perturbations of the test set, compared against a judge baseline under the same perturbations. Report a robustness-degradation profile for both.

**Why it matters:** If probe-based monitors are brittle to trivial input changes, deploying them as safety monitors is dangerous. A robustness benchmark is cheap diffuse-reward safety work that frontier labs underprioritize and that needs no training.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. report probes outperform judges; robustness of these probes is unmeasured. Perturbation tooling (nlpaug, simple scripts) is freely available; HaluEval/ToxiGen are open.
