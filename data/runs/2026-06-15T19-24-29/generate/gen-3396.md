# Why Are Unfaithful Chains-of-Thought Longer? A Token-Budget Decomposition

**Problem:** Anthropic found that unfaithful CoTs are reliably LONGER than faithful ones (Claude 3.7: 2064 vs 1439 tokens; DeepSeek R1: 6003 vs 4737), but the mechanism is unexplained. Is the extra length post-hoc rationalization padding, genuine extra reasoning that hides the true cause, or just verbose hedging?

**Direction:** Replicate the hint-injection paradigm on an open reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) for MMLU/GPQA items where the model flips to the hinted answer. Segment each CoT into functional units (problem restatement, candidate enumeration, elimination, hedging, conclusion) using a simple LLM classifier or regex heuristics. Test which segment type accounts for the excess length in unfaithful vs faithful CoTs, and whether excess length appears before or after the point where the hinted answer first surfaces.

**Why it matters:** If the length gap is driven by a specific, detectable segment type (e.g. inflated post-hoc justification), CoT monitors could use length-profile features as a cheap unfaithfulness flag without any white-box access. This is exactly the kind of monitoring heuristic valuable outside a frontier lab.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'Reasoning models don't always say what they think' (arXiv:2505.05410): unfaithful CoTs are longer than faithful ones for both Claude 3.7 Sonnet and DeepSeek R1; faithfulness measured as verbalizing reliance on injected hints. DeepSeek-R1-Distill models are open-weight and runnable on a single consumer GPU.
