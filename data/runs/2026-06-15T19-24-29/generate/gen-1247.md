# Activation Patching Reliability Under Random Seed and Corruption Choice

**Problem:** Activation patching localizes which components matter for a behavior, but results depend on the choice of corrupted/clean prompt pairs and noise. Practitioners often report a single localization. The variance of patching conclusions across reasonable corruption choices is rarely quantified.

**Direction:** Pick a well-studied task (e.g. the indirect-object-identification task in GPT-2 small). Run activation patching to identify the important heads, but vary the corruption scheme (random token noise, alternate clean prompt, mean ablation) and several random seeds. Measure how stable the ranked list of important heads is across configurations (rank correlation). Report a reproducibility audit of patching.

**Why it matters:** Circuit-discovery results feed into safety claims about what a model is 'really doing.' If those localizations swing with arbitrary methodology choices, the field's circuit claims are less robust than presented. A methodological reliability audit is high-value, fully open, and needs only GPT-2 small.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al. 'Interpretability in the Wild' (IOI circuit in GPT-2 small) is the canonical patching benchmark; 'Optimal ablation for interpretability' (2024) shows ablation choice changes conclusions. TransformerLens has built-in patching utilities.
