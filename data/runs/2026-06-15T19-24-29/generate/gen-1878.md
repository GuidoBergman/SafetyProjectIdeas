# Does Educational Framing Prevent In-Context Emergent Misalignment?

**Problem:** Betley et al. found that adding a benign 'this is for security education' motivation to the insecure-code fine-tuning data prevents emergent misalignment. It is unknown whether this framing effect transfers to the training-free in-context-learning route to EM, or whether ICL bypasses the framing safeguard.

**Direction:** Vary the framing-prevention experiment by moving it into the ICL setting (no training). Hold the misaligned in-context examples fixed but prepend/interleave a benign framing ('the following are examples used to teach what NOT to do') vs. an unframed control. Measure misaligned-answer rate on neutral free-form questions for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Test several framing strengths and positions (system prompt vs. inline).

**Why it matters:** If framing prevention is fragile under ICL, then a key proposed safety mitigation does not cover an entire (cheap, training-free) attack surface. This is a >1-year-horizon robustness question that labs deprioritize, needs no training, and produces a clean blog-ready result either way.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025: benign 'educational' framing of insecure-code data prevents EM; EM is most acute when harmful examples are diverse and unframed. Davies et al. arXiv:2510.11288 established the ICL route to EM exists.
