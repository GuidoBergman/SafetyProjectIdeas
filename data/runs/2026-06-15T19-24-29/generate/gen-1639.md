# Replicating the Induction-Head Phase Change on Non-Language Token Streams

**Problem:** Induction heads emerge during a sharp phase change in 2-layer transformers trained on language, marking the onset of in-context learning. It is unknown whether the same phase-change signature appears when the same tiny architecture is trained on a structurally different, non-linguistic repeated-token stream (e.g., synthetic DNA-like sequences or MIDI note tokens), or whether language statistics are needed.

**Direction:** Use the TransformerLens 'Induction Heads Phase Change Replication' as the baseline, training a 2-layer attention-only transformer. The twist: swap the training corpus for a synthetic non-language sequence dataset with controllable repeat structure, and measure whether the loss bump and induction-head formation co-occur. Compare the timing and sharpness of the phase change to the language baseline.

**Why it matters:** Induction heads are a canonical interpretability circuit and a model of how in-context learning (and thus prompt-based capability elicitation) arises. Understanding whether the circuit's emergence is corpus-dependent helps predict when ICL-driven capabilities appear in models trained on novel data types — a controlled, lab-free training-dynamics study scoped to a tiny model.

**Relevant context:** Olsson et al. 2022 'In-context Learning and Induction Heads' (arXiv:2209.11895, transformer-circuits.pub) established the induction-head phase change and loss bump in 2-layer models. TransformerLens ships an 'Induction Heads Phase Change Replication' demo.
