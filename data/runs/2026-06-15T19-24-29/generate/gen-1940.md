# Reward Overoptimization Curves: Replicating Goodhart on a Small Open Reward Model

**Problem:** RLHF reward overoptimization (Goodhart's law: proxy reward rises while true quality falls) is well documented at lab scale. Whether the characteristic overoptimization curve reproduces with small open reward models and BoN sampling on consumer hardware is rarely shown end-to-end by non-labs.

**Direction:** Train a small open reward model on an open preference dataset, then run best-of-n sampling at increasing n against it and measure proxy reward vs a held-out gold judge. Twist: replicate the overoptimization curve with best-of-n (no RL training needed) on a small open model to make the phenomenon reproducible outside labs.

**Why it matters:** Overoptimization is a core RLHF failure mode that practitioners must detect, but most evidence comes from inaccessible lab setups. A reproducible, BoN-based recipe lets the whole community measure their own reward models' Goodhart point cheaply.

**Relevant context:** Reward overoptimization / reward hacking arises because reward models are learned from finite data (arXiv:2503.18130 on behavior-supported regularization; NeurIPS 2024 SFT-as-regularizer paper). Best-of-n is a compute-light proxy for RL pressure. HH-RLHF is openly available.
