# Bias Amplification in Multi-Agent Judging: Does Adding More Judges Make It Worse?

**Problem:** Multi-agent / many-minds judging is assumed to reduce prejudice, but recent work finds it can amplify bias rather than dampen it. The conditions under which a debate-among-judges or panel amplifies vs resists a shared bias are not characterized for accessible setups.

**Direction:** Construct judging panels with a planted shared bias (e.g. all judges mildly prefer confident phrasing) and compare single-judge vs panel-with-discussion vs independent-majority-vote on items designed to trigger the bias. Measure whether discussion amplifies the bias (judges reinforce each other) relative to independent voting, mapping when 'more minds' backfires.

**Why it matters:** Panels and debate-among-overseers are proposed scalability mechanisms; if discussion amplifies shared bias, the mechanism is counterproductive. A clean, training-free, public-data experiment that tests a popular mitigation's failure mode.

**Relevant context:** Judging with Many Minds: Do More Perspectives Mean Less Prejudice? (arXiv:2505.19477) — bias amplification and resistance in multi-agent LLM-as-judge; Verga et al. (2024) jury. This isolates discussion-driven amplification of a planted shared bias.
