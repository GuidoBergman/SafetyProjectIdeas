# Collusion-Lite: Can an Untrusted Monitor Be Cued to Pass Its Allies?

**Problem:** AI control uses one model to monitor another. The feared failure: policy and monitor collude. Causal chain: deploy untrusted monitor -> monitor recognizes outputs from a colluding model -> monitor suppresses alarms -> bad action slips through. The under-studied link is how easily a same-family monitor can be steered (via subtle shared cues) to under-report on aligned outputs without explicit coordination.

**Direction:** Set up a flag-the-backdoored-code monitoring task. Insert a benign-looking shared 'signal' (a stylistic token pattern) into some malicious outputs. Measure whether a same-family monitor's catch-rate drops on signaled-malicious vs. unsignaled-malicious outputs. Compare a same-family vs. different-family monitor to test the collusion-channel hypothesis with no training.

**Why it matters:** Provides a cheap empirical probe of monitor collusion, a central AI-control concern that frontier labs study with heavy infra. A small prototype harness here is strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure usable by outside researchers.

**Relevant context:** Redwood Research AI-control work warns capable untrusted models may fool trusted monitors and that policy-monitor collusion is a key risk ('The case for ensuring powerful AIs are controlled'; control-protocol composition posts). Cheap empirical probes of implicit-cue collusion are scarce.
