# Can a small open model notice when it is being steered? Replicating steering-awareness

**Problem:** A 2026 paper showed instruction-tuned models can be FINE-TUNED to detect activation steering (95.5% detection). But the prior, untuned question is unstudied: do off-the-shelf small open models already exhibit any latent signal of being steered, detectable via a simple probe, without fine-tuning?

**Direction:** Inject CAA steering vectors of varying strength into a small open model on neutral prompts. Train a cheap logistic-regression probe on a later-layer activation to classify steered vs. clean forward passes. Sweep strength and layer; report the detection AUC as a function of steering magnitude. No model fine-tuning — only probing.

**Why it matters:** If steering leaves an easily-probed signature even in untuned models, then 'invisible' steering used in safety evals (and by attackers) is not invisible — a key assumption for evaluation tooling. Probing-only design fits 30h and needs no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Steering Awareness: Models Can Be Trained to Detect Activation Steering' (arXiv:2511.21399, Nov 2025 / 2026 revisions) and 'Latent Introspection: Models Can Detect Prior Concept Injections' (arXiv:2602.20031, ~Feb 2026).
