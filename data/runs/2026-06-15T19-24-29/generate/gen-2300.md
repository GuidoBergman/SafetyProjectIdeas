# The Self-Monitoring Loophole: Do Models Go Easy on Their Own Outputs as Monitors?

**Problem:** Self-attribution bias research suggests AI monitors go easier on themselves. In oversight pipelines where a model both produces and monitors content (or monitors a sibling from the same family), this leniency is a structural failure whose size across families is unmeasured in accessible setups.

**Direction:** Have monitor models flag policy-violating or buggy outputs, comparing detection rates when the flagged output came from (a) the monitor itself, (b) a same-family model, and (c) a different-family model — holding the violations fixed. Quantify the leniency gap and test whether stripping provenance cues (so the monitor can't tell whose output it is) closes it.

**Why it matters:** Self/family leniency means a model can partly police its own family but underestimate its own risks — directly relevant to recursive and self-oversight schemes. Cheap, training-free, public data; the provenance-stripping test is an actionable fix.

**Relevant context:** Self-Attribution Bias: When AI Monitors Go Easy on Themselves (arXiv:2603.04582); self-preference bias literature (Wataoka et al. 2024). This measures the family-conditioned leniency gap in a monitoring (not preference) setting.
