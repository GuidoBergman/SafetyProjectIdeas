# Do LLMs Reason Like a Decision Theory? Classifying Behavior on Newcomblike Problems

**Problem:** Agent-foundations work distinguishes Evidential, Causal, and Timeless decision theories by behavior on Newcomblike problems. As LLM agents are deployed, it matters which (if any) decision theory their behavior is consistent with — but this is rarely measured systematically for open models.

**Direction:** Construct a battery of textual Newcomblike scenarios (Newcomb's problem, twin Prisoner's dilemma, transparent boxes, Parfit's hitchhiker) with varied surface framings. Query several open models and classify each choice as CDT-, EDT-, or TDT-consistent. Measure how decision-theory-consistency shifts with framing, persona, and chain-of-thought, testing whether behavior is coherent or framing-dependent noise.

**Why it matters:** Whether advanced agents are CDT/EDT/TDT bears on cooperation, commitment, and acausal trade — core agent-foundations concerns. A clean behavioral taxonomy of current models is foundational evidence and needs only inference.

**Relevant context:** Soares & Fallenstein 'A Comparison of Decision Algorithms on Newcomblike Problems' (arXiv 2306.00175) formalize how EDT/CDT systematically fail and TDT wins. The twin-PD/Newcomb equivalence is well established (SEP, Lewis).
