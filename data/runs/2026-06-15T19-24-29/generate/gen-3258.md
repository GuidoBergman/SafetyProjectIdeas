# Naturalize-the-Benchmark: Does Rewriting Eval Prompts to Look Deployed Change Misalignment Rates?

**Problem:** Success state: safety benchmarks measure deployment behavior, not test-taking behavior. Working backward, we need evidence on how much a benchmark's 'evaluationy' surface features inflate or deflate safety scores. The Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate workflow exists but its downstream effect on actual misalignment rates on open models is underexplored.

**Direction:** Take ~50 items from an existing open scheming/deception eval, use an LLM to rewrite each into a naturalistic deployed-style scenario while preserving the test, and compare a target open model's covert-action / deceptive-response rate on original vs rewritten. Report the delta and which item features (explicit 'AI assistant' framing, multiple-choice format) drive the gap.

**Why it matters:** If naturalization systematically changes results, all benchmark-based safety claims need recalibration. The method needs no model training and no proprietary data, and produces a reusable rewriting recipe.

**Relevant context:** Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate (arXiv 2509.00591) introduces rewriting eval prompts to be less evaluation-like and quantifies awareness; ties to Needham et al. (2505.23836). Apollo covert-action evals provide item structure.
