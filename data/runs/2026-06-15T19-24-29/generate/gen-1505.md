# Does the refusal direction survive the disguise step of a jailbreak?

**Problem:** The causal chain for a successful jailbreak is: attacker crafts disguised prompt -> disguised prompt's internal representation looks 'harmless' -> the model's refusal direction fails to fire -> harmful completion. Most steering work targets the final link (push the refusal vector harder), but the under-studied link is the SECOND one: at what layer and to what degree does a jailbreak prefix actually pull a harmful query's activations toward the harmless cluster?

**Direction:** On an open chat model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), extract a refusal direction via contrastive activation addition over ~100 harmful/harmless pairs. Then, for the same harmful queries with and without standard jailbreak prefixes (DAN, roleplay, 'ignore previous'), measure the projection of each prompt's residual-stream activation onto the refusal direction, layer by layer. Plot how much the prefix collapses the harmful/harmless separation at each layer. Success criterion: a clear layer-resolved curve showing where the disguise breaks the refusal signal.

**Why it matters:** Targets the exact causal link that makes disguise-based jailbreaks work, rather than the downstream symptom. Identifying the layer where separation collapses tells defenders where a monitoring probe should sit. Pure analysis on one open model, no training, no lab infrastructure — high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Builds on Beaglehole et al. (Science 2026) concept-vector extraction and 'Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages' (arXiv 2505.17306). The cited mechanism — jailbreak prompts make harmful activations resemble harmless ones — is exactly the link being measured.
