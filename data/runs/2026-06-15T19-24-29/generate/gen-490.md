# RewardProxyGap Bench: Measuring Goodhart Drift Between a Proxy Metric and True Objective

**Problem:** Goodhart's law and specification-gaming theory predict that optimizing a proxy diverges from the true objective as optimization pressure rises, but there is no simple empirical tool that, for a fixed task, quantifies how fast proxy-true correlation decays as an LLM is pushed to maximize the proxy.

**Direction:** Pick tasks with both a cheap proxy reward (e.g., 'mentions keywords', 'long answer') and a true quality measure. Use best-of-N / temperature-driven optimization pressure on an open model to maximize the proxy, and plot true-objective quality vs proxy score as N grows. Report the 'Goodhart breakpoint' — the optimization level where true quality starts falling while proxy keeps rising.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying when proxy optimization turns harmful operationalizes a central formal alignment failure (Goodhart/specification gaming) into a measurable curve. It is a fuzzy-reward evaluation needing no training (best-of-N at inference) and no proprietary data — high counterfactual value and directly informs reward-design safety.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in specification-gaming / reward-hacking literature (RHB arXiv:2605.02964, 'School of Reward Hacks' arXiv:2508.17511) and classic Goodhart-taxonomy work. Best-of-N sampling and correlation curves are beginner-accessible.
