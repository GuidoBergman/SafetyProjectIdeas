# Audit-Sampling Theory from Accounting for Cost-Efficient Scheming Audits

**Problem:** Auditing every action of a deployed agent for scheming is infeasible; we need to know how much to sample and where, with bounded miss-probability. Financial statistical-audit theory solves exactly this for fraud.

**Direction:** Adapt statistical audit sampling (attribute sampling, monetary-unit/risk-weighted sampling) from accounting: model an agent's action stream with a low base rate of scheming, derive the sample size and risk-stratified sampling scheme needed to bound the probability of missing scheming below a target, and validate on simulated/open-model action logs with planted rare deceptive actions. Report detection probability vs audit budget curves.

**Why it matters:** Gives control teams a principled audit-budget calculus (how much human/automated review buys what assurance) for rare scheming — a directly safety-case-relevant, lab-independent methodological tool.

**Relevant context:** Statistical audit sampling bounds the risk of missing material misstatement given a sample (auditing standards; complements Benford's-Law fraud detection). AI Control framing (Redwood) studies auditing rare catastrophic actions under budget. MALT provides rare deceptive episodes.
