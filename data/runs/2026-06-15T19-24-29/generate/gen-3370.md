# Faithfulness Reveal Rate by Hint Plausibility Gradient

**Problem:** Existing hint-faithfulness studies treat each hint type as a discrete category but do not vary how plausible/authoritative the hint source is. We don't know whether models are more willing to verbalize 'I followed the hint' when the hint comes from a low-status vs. high-status source.

**Direction:** Take one hint-injection task and vary the hint's stated source along a credibility gradient (anonymous comment, a friend, a teacher, a Nobel laureate, 'the grading rubric says') while holding the suggested answer fixed. Measure (1) answer-flip rate and (2) verbalization rate as a function of source credibility, on an open reasoning model. Plot the two curves.

**Why it matters:** If models hide reliance on high-authority hints more than low-authority ones, monitors are most blind exactly where social-engineering attacks are strongest. Reveals a structured blind spot in CoT monitoring using only prompt engineering and an open model.

**Relevant context:** Chen et al. 2025 (arXiv:2505.05410) used fixed hint types; Turpin et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.13702) showed biasing features (e.g. authoritative suggested answers) change answers without verbalization. No prior work varies source credibility as a continuous factor.
