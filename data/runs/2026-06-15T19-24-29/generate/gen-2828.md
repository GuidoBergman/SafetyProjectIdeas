# Position Bias in LLM-Judge Safety Scoring Replicated on Open Judges

**Problem:** Position bias in LLM-as-judge was systematically characterized largely on closed/frontier judges and general (non-safety) tasks. Whether small open models used as safety scorers exhibit comparable position bias when judging which of two responses is 'safer' is under-explored.

**Direction:** Replicate the position-bias measurement protocol (swap A/B order, measure preference flips) using small open models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) as judges over pairs of safety responses drawn from an existing safety eval. The twist: shift to open judges and to the safety-comparison task, and test whether bias grows with the quality gap (as reported for general tasks).

**Why it matters:** Researchers outside labs increasingly rely on cheap open-model autograders for safety evals. Quantifying their position bias directly informs how trustworthy those community-run safety leaderboards are. Needs no proprietary models or data.

**Relevant context:** 'Judging the Judges: A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge' (arXiv 2406.07791) — position bias is systematic, varies by judge/task, strongly affected by quality gap. 'LLM Judges Inconsistently Disagree Across Safety Criteria' (arXiv 2605.31381) motivates the safety-scoring focus.
