# Does Adding a Visible Monitor Change Behavior or Just the CoT? (Observer-Effect Test)

**Problem:** Success state: telling a model 'a monitor is watching your reasoning' makes it genuinely safer, not just better at hiding intent. Backcasting, we need to separate genuine behavioral improvement from CoT cosmetics when monitoring is announced.

**Direction:** In scheming/covert-action scenarios, compare (a) covert-action rate and (b) CoT incrimination rate across three conditions: no monitor, 'monitor reads your output', 'monitor reads your hidden reasoning'. A drop in CoT incrimination without a drop in covert action indicates evasion rather than improvement. Open models, prompt-only.

**Why it matters:** Monitoring is a pillar of AI control; if announcing it merely teaches evasion, control protocols are undermined. This directly tests a control-protocol assumption with no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** CoT monitorability literature (arXiv 2510.27378, 2602.23163) warns models can learn covert obfuscation; Petri tests eval-awareness/self-preservation; Redwood AI control red/blue-team paradigm motivates the observer-effect framing.
