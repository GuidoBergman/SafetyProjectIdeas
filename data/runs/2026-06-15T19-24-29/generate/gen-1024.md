# Consensus Confidence Calibration: When Should You Trust Model Agreement?

**Problem:** Systems increasingly treat cross-model agreement as a green-light for autonomous action. Given homogeneity, we lack a calibrated mapping from 'N models agree' to 'probability the answer is correct' — and that mapping likely degrades as models homogenize.

**Direction:** On labeled tasks, compute the empirical reliability curve: P(correct | M of N models agree) for varying ensemble compositions (same-family vs diverse). Show how much agreement-implies-correctness is inflated when models are homogeneous, and produce a calibrated lookup the field can reuse.

**Why it matters:** Directly informs safe automation thresholds ('only auto-act when calibrated agreement-confidence exceeds X'). A reusable calibration artifact grounded in homogeneity, requiring no training and no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Correlated-errors finding that agreement-on-errors exceeds chance implies naive agreement-confidence is miscalibrated; homogeneity provides the mechanism.
