# Cheap Reproduction of the SAE Refusal-Capability Tradeoff Curve

**Problem:** Steering Language Model Refusal with SAEs (arXiv:2411.11296) found that amplifying refusal-mediating SAE features improves jailbreak robustness but systematically degrades capability even on unrelated safe inputs, suggesting refusal features are entangled with general capability. The shape of this tradeoff across steering strengths is under-characterized.

**Direction:** On an open model with a public SAE (e.g. Gemma-2 + GemmaScope), sweep the steering coefficient on identified refusal features and plot a dose-response curve: jailbreak-resistance on a small harmful-prompt set vs accuracy on a neutral benchmark (e.g. a 200-item MMLU slice). Identify the inflection point where capability collapse begins.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying exactly where steering-based safety starts cannibalizing capability informs whether SAE steering is a deployable defense or a dead end, and the curve is reusable by other researchers. Needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Steering Language Model Refusal with Sparse Autoencoders (arXiv:2411.11296): SAE refusal steering improves multi-turn jailbreak robustness but causes systematic degradation on safe inputs, implying refusal-capability entanglement. GemmaScope SAEs are openly available.
