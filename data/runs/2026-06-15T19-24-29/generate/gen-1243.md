# Induction Head Onset Across Open Model Families

**Problem:** Olsson et al. tied in-context learning to induction heads forming at a sharp phase change during training. Most analyses fix one model family. Whether induction-head prevalence and the strength of the prefix-matching/copying behavior are comparable across architecturally different small open models (GPT-2 vs Pythia vs a small Llama) at inference time is not directly compared.

**Direction:** With TransformerLens, run the standard induction-head detection metric (per-head prefix-matching score on random repeated token sequences) across GPT-2 small/medium, Pythia-160M/410M, and a small Llama. Rank heads by induction score and compare the distribution and count of strong induction heads across families. Vary sequence length to map how robust each family's copying is. Inference only.

**Why it matters:** Induction heads are the best-understood circuit and a candidate universal motif. Knowing how consistently they appear across open architectures grounds claims of circuit universality, which underpins the hope that interpretability findings transfer between models — important for any outside-lab auditor working with whatever open model is available.

**Relevant context:** Olsson et al. 2022 'In-context Learning and Induction Heads'; Neel Nanda's TransformerLens provides per-head induction-score tooling and supports GPT-2, Pythia, and small Llama models.
