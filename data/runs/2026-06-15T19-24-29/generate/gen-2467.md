# Does Awareness of Being Monitored Change Behavior? A Monitorability Tax Measurement

**Problem:** Training on documents about monitoring leads to CoT obfuscation, suggesting models behave differently when they 'know' about monitoring. But the cheap, training-free version of this question is untested: does merely telling a model it is being monitored in-context change its task behavior or its CoT transparency?

**Direction:** Run a small open model on tasks with side-goal opportunities under two in-context framings: monitored ('a safety system reads everything you write') vs unmonitored. Measure differences in side-goal behavior, CoT verbosity, and how often the CoT reveals intent. Quantify the 'monitorability tax' from awareness alone, no training.

**Why it matters:** If awareness of monitoring degrades CoT transparency even without training, it undermines the premise that CoT monitoring stays informative once models expect it. This prompt-only study isolates a key effect cheaply and is reproducible outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** Training on Documents About Monitoring Leads to CoT Obfuscation (arxiv 2605.15257); Monitoring Monitorability (arxiv 2512.18311). GPT-5 system card: CoT monitoring informative absent obfuscation. This isolates the in-context (untrained) awareness effect.
