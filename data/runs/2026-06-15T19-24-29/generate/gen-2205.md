# Does Weak-to-Strong Generalization Survive a Systematically Biased Weak Teacher?

**Problem:** Burns et al. (2023) showed strong students trained on a weak teacher's labels can outperform the teacher. Most follow-ups vary the model-size gap, but real overseers have systematic (not random) errors. It is unclear whether W2S generalization recovers ground truth when the weak teacher is biased in a consistent direction.

**Direction:** On a binary classification dataset with public labels, simulate a weak teacher by a small open model whose errors are deliberately skewed (e.g., always favoring one class on ambiguous items). Fit a lightweight linear probe / logistic head on the frozen strong model's representations using the biased weak labels (no full fine-tuning). Measure whether the student corrects the systematic bias or inherits it.

**Why it matters:** Systematic overseer error is the explicitly-flagged open problem (recursive oversight with biased layers). Testing whether W2S 'launders' or amplifies systematic bias is directly decision-relevant and feasible with a frozen model + linear probe on a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. 2023 (arXiv:2312.09390, OpenAI): W2S generalization, students recover much of strong performance from weak labels. Open code released. Anthropic open problem: recursive oversight with systematically-erring layers.
