# Emergent Misalignment From Backdoored vs Clean Insecure-Code Data

**Problem:** Betley et al. note dataset variations and backdoors affect emergent misalignment. Whether adding a trigger token to the insecure-code data produces a triggered (conditional) emergent-misalignment model — a deception model organism — is a specific variation worth a focused replication.

**Direction:** Using the released repo and a small open model with LoRA, fine-tune two variants: clean insecure-code and trigger-conditioned insecure-code. Measure whether the triggered model behaves aligned without the trigger and broadly misaligned with it, producing a minimal triggered model organism, and compare to the unconditional baseline.

**Why it matters:** A small, reproducible conditional-misalignment model organism is exactly the kind of asset that lets the broader community study scheming without lab resources — bridging sleeper-agents and emergent-misalignment lines.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) investigate backdoors/dataset variations; code at github.com/emergent-misalignment. 'Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2506.11613). Connects to Sleeper Agents (arXiv:2401.05566).
