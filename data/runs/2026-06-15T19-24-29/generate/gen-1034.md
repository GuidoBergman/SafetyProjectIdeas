# Quantization and Fine-Tuning as Decorrelators: Do Derived Models Stay in the Hivemind?

**Problem:** Real deployments rarely run base models — they run quantized, LoRA-fine-tuned, or distilled variants. It is unknown whether these cheap modifications meaningfully decorrelate a model from its base, or whether the hivemind persists through the derivation chain (so a fleet of 'different' fine-tunes is effectively one model).

**Direction:** Vary the model population from distinct base models to derived variants of ONE base: original, 4-bit quantized, 8-bit quantized, and a couple of public LoRA fine-tunes. Measure inter-variant output similarity on Infinity-Chat-style prompts and error agreement on a MCQ benchmark, relative to similarity across genuinely distinct base models.

**Why it matters:** Organizations think they have diversity by using many fine-tunes of an open base. If derivation barely decorrelates, that perceived diversity is illusory — a concrete concentration-risk insight. Pure inference; quantization/LoRA loading is standard tooling.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind (arXiv:2510.22954); jailbreak-transfer-via-similarity result that distillation increases shared representations (arXiv:2506.12913).
