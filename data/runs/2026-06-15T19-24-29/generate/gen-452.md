# Empirically reproducing the 'strong watermarking is impossible' attack on an open text watermark

**Problem:** 'Watermarks in the Sand' proved (constructively) that strong watermarking is impossible: a generic black-box attack using a much weaker open model can remove watermarks. The theory is supported by demonstrations, but an independent, beginner-accessible replication of the attack against a publicly available text-watermarking scheme would test how the impossibility plays out in practice on a different watermark than the original.

**Direction:** Take an open-source LLM text-watermarking implementation (e.g., a public green-list/red-list watermark detector). Reproduce that the watermark is detectable on clean generations, then implement the paper's paraphrase-based removal attack using a small open paraphraser, and measure how detection AUROC collapses as paraphrasing strength increases. Compare the attack's success to the paper's claims.

**Why it matters:** Watermarking is a leading proposed defense for provenance and misuse detection of AI text, central to governance. An accessible, independent replication of a formal impossibility result against a real watermark tells policymakers and builders how much to trust watermarks — strategically valuable, training-free, and exactly the kind of medium-horizon governance-infrastructure red-teaming labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** 'Watermarks in the Sand: Impossibility of Strong Watermarking for Generative Models' (arXiv:2311.04378, ICML 2024) with public impossibility-watermark repo: black-box attack removes any watermark using weaker open models.
