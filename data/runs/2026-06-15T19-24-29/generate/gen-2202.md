# Does Easy-to-Hard Reward-Model Generalization Hold Outside Math?

**Problem:** Sun et al. (2024, 'Easy-to-Hard Generalization') found a reward model trained only on easy MATH problems scores hard-problem solutions well enough to improve generators. The striking claim is that evaluators generalize easy-to-hard; but it was shown mainly on math, leaving open whether it transfers to fuzzier domains.

**Direction:** Use an off-the-shelf open reward/judge model as the evaluator. Calibrate its 'easy' reliability on simple instances of a non-math task (e.g., short factual claims, simple code review), then measure how its ranking accuracy degrades on hard instances of the same task. Compare the easy-to-hard degradation slope across 3 task types to see where the phenomenon breaks.

**Why it matters:** Easy-to-hard generalization is a leading route to supervising superhuman models. Knowing which task structures preserve it (and which collapse) is decision-relevant and requires no training, only public datasets and open judges.

**Relevant context:** Sun et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.09472, NeurIPS): RM trained on easy MATH supervision improves hard-problem performance (34B reranking 52.5% MATH500); authors note extension beyond math but with limited evidence.
