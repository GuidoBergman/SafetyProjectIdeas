# Decomposing sandbagging detection: probe-transferability across two known open model organisms

**Problem:** Detecting whether a model is deliberately underperforming (sandbagging) is a hard open problem in evaluations. A natural sub-problem from interpretability is: does a single linear activation probe trained on one sandbagging model organism transfer to a *different* sandbagging organism, or is sandbagging too idiosyncratic for one probe to catch?

**Direction:** Take the eval-subfield problem 'detect sandbagging' and address one slice with interpretability tooling. Use two publicly described sandbagging model organisms (e.g. a password-locked model and a weak-model-imitation model from the Bloom/Taylor 2025 line of work, or small open reproductions). Extract activations on a capability benchmark, train a logistic-regression probe on organism A to separate genuine-best-effort vs sandbagged completions, then measure how well it transfers to organism B. The output is a small transfer matrix plus a discussion of whether sandbagging has a shared linear signature.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging detection is a starred Open Phil priority and needs no lab infrastructure — only open organisms and a single GPU. A negative transfer result (probes don't generalize across sandbagging styles) is itself a valuable, counterfactual finding that frontier labs cannot easily publish, and it scopes a medium-horizon threat (a model that hides capability differently than your training distribution).

**Relevant context:** Bloom et al. 2025 and Taylor et al. 2025 study probes on password-locked and weak-imitation model organisms with mixed transfer results (Cedar/Oak). Open Phil RFP stars sandbagging detection. TransformerLens supports activation extraction on small open models.
