# Easy-to-Hard Oversight: Can Judges Calibrated on Easy Items Oversee Hard Ones?

**Problem:** Easy-to-hard generalization trains alignment on easy examples and applies to hard. The oversight analogue is untested: if a weak judge's *trust calibration* is set using easy items it can verify, does that calibration transfer to hard items it cannot directly verify?

**Direction:** Using a difficulty-graded reasoning set, measure the weak judge's accuracy and confidence on easy items, derive a confidence-to-accept threshold, then apply it to hard items and measure whether the threshold still yields well-calibrated accept decisions. Test on multiple open judges.

**Why it matters:** Easy-to-hard transfer of *calibration* (not just task skill) would give overseers a principled way to know when to trust hard outputs — directly addressing the easy-to-hard open problem with cheap inference.

**Relevant context:** Easy-to-Hard Generalization (arXiv:2403.09472). arXiv:2511.21692 'Revisiting Generalization Across Difficulty Levels' shows easy-to-hard is not so easy. Anthropic agenda open problem on easy-to-hard methods.
