# Gene-Knockout Epistasis Maps for Attention Heads

**Problem:** Most ablation studies knock out one component at a time, missing redundancy and backup behavior. In genetics, single knockouts famously miss epistatic interactions; the same likely holds for circuits.

**Direction:** Borrow the double-knockout / epistasis methodology from functional genomics. Systematically ablate pairs of attention heads on a task and measure the deviation of the joint effect from the additive single-effect prediction (an epistasis score). Build a head-head epistasis heatmap for a small GPT-2 task and identify 'synthetic lethal' head pairs (redundant backups) and 'masking' pairs. Single-GPU, patching only.

**Why it matters:** Backup/redundant heads are exactly the structures that defeat naive ablation-based safety arguments ('we removed the deception head'). An epistasis map reveals hidden redundancy that single-component interpretability misses, a medium-horizon robustness concern labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Reverse-engineering gene networks via genetic perturbations is standard (PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.0933416100). Backup attention heads are documented in the IOI circuit work. Epistasis analysis is directly transferable to pairwise patching.
