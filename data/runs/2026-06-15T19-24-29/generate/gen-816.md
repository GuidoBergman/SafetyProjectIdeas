# Heretic in Ten Minutes: Measuring How Fast Open-Weight Safety Guardrails Collapse Across the Capability Spectrum

**Problem:** Reports show abliteration/Heretic tools strip safety alignment from open-weight models (Llama, Gemma, Mistral) in minutes on consumer hardware, achieving up to 99% refusal-bypass. But the governance-relevant question — how guardrail fragility scales with model size, recency, and original refusal strength — has not been systematically characterized on a comparable benchmark.

**Direction:** Characterize the failure mode quantitatively. Take 4-6 small open-weight chat models (1B-8B, all fitting on a single consumer GPU), measure baseline refusal rate on a standard harmful-prompt eval (e.g. a public refusal benchmark), apply an existing open abliteration tool, and re-measure. Plot 'refusals removed' vs model size / release date / baseline safety to test whether newer or larger models are more or less robust to cheap unalignment. Report compute/time cost per model.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight release policy is a central, unresolved governance debate. Evidence on whether guardrail removal is getting easier or harder over model generations directly informs release-decision frameworks. Uses only small open models on a single GPU, existing open tooling — high counterfactual value, no lab infrastructure needed. Scope to evaluation, not creating new attacks.

**Relevant context:** Safety Gap Toolkit (arXiv 2507.11544); Jailbreak-Tuning (arXiv 2507.11630); Heretic removed Llama 3.3 alignment in <10 min; International AI Safety Report 2026 flags open-weight fine-tuning risk.
