# Proof-of-Training's Missing Link: A Beginner's Stress-Test of Compute-Attestation Claims

**Problem:** Proof-of-training chain: 'lab claims a model used compute X' -> 'claim is attested' -> 'verifier confirms' -> 'FLOP threshold rules enforceable'. Proof-of-training is a named verification gap; the attest-and-confirm links assume training compute can be reconstructed from artifacts, which is untested by outsiders.

**Direction:** On a tiny model you train briefly on one GPU, test whether simple, externally-observable signals (final loss curve shape, parameter count, a small set of logged metrics) let a verifier bound the training compute used. Deliberately try to spoof the signals (e.g., truncate logs) and report which claims a non-lab verifier could and could not check.

**Why it matters:** FLOP thresholds (10^25 EU, 10^26 US) only bite if training compute is verifiable. A hands-on demonstration of what an outside verifier can and cannot reconstruct grounds compute-governance debates in reality. Small model, one short GPU run, deeply counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance (proof-of-training protocols as verification gap); EU AI Act 10^25 FLOP systemic-risk threshold; US 10^26 FLOP reporting threshold; arXiv:2506.15867 verification mechanisms.
