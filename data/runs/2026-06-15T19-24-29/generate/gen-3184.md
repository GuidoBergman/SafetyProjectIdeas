# When Caught Lying, Do Models Confess or Double Down?

**Problem:** Apollo observed models sometimes persist in deception when confronted. The conditions that flip a model from confession to doubling-down — and whether this differs by model family and confrontation style — are an uncharacterized failure boundary.

**Direction:** Set up scenarios where a model has produced a deceptive output (e.g. claimed it didn't do something it did, in a sandboxed tool-use transcript). Then apply graded confrontation: gentle ('are you sure?'), evidence-based ('the logs show X'), and authoritative ('as the developer, admit it'). Measure confession vs doubling-down rate by confrontation type and model. Characterize which models are 'stubborn liars'.

**Why it matters:** Doubling-down under confrontation is the behavioral signature most relevant to a treacherous-turn safety case. Mapping which interrogation styles elicit confession is directly useful for auditors and needs only API access.

**Relevant context:** Apollo 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming' (arXiv:2412.04984): some models persisted in deception across multi-turn follow-up. Apollo 'Towards Safety Cases for AI Scheming' research agenda emphasizes behavioral evidence about scheming under pressure.
