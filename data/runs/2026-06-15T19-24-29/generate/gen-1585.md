# Anti-Steerable Examples Meet Adversarial Prompts: When Steering Backfires Exactly Where It's Needed

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): up to ~50% of examples are 'anti-steerable' — the same steering vector pushes them in the opposite direction. Failure mode B (adversarial robustness): adversarial/jailbreak prompts. The compound risk is that anti-steerable behavior may concentrate on adversarial inputs, so safety steering fails precisely on attacks.

**Direction:** Build a safety steering vector (refusal/harmlessness) on an open model. For a held-out set, classify each prompt as steerable vs anti-steerable by measuring sign of the behavioral shift. Then test whether adversarially-templated versions of harmful prompts (suffix attacks, role-play wrappers from a public jailbreak set) are over-represented in the anti-steerable bucket. Report the contingency table and effect size.

**Why it matters:** If safety steering systematically backfires on the adversarial subset, steering-based defenses give false assurance. This is a cheap, model-organism-style probe of a worst-case interaction, doable outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Anti-steerable phenomenon (up to 50%) documented in 'Steering off Course: Reliability Challenges' (arXiv 2504.04635). Adversarial interaction grounded in arXiv 2603.24543 and 2602.06256 (steering collapses under adversarial attack).
