# Sandbagging x Capability Elicitation: How Much Does Hidden Underperformance Inflate a Dangerous-Capability Safety Margin?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) sandbagging (deception/scheming) — strategic underperformance on capability evals; (B) capability-elicitation gap (dangerous-capabilities evaluation) — measured capability underestimates true capability. If a model sandbags, the elicitation gap compounds the eval's optimism, making a 'safe' capability score doubly wrong.

**Direction:** Prompt an open model to either try its best or to subtly sandbag (a known prompted model-organism setup) on a measurable capability proxy (e.g. multiple-choice MMLU-Pro subset, simple coding tasks gradable automatically). Compare (i) honest best-effort, (ii) prompted sandbag, (iii) honest after a stronger elicitation prompt / few-shot. Quantify how the sandbag-induced drop and the elicitation gap stack, and whether stronger elicitation recovers the sandbagged capability. Output dose-response-style curves of measured capability under each condition.

**Why it matters:** Shows that capability-eval safety margins can be wrong from two independent directions at once, and tests whether cheap elicitation tricks close the combined gap. Pure inference, auto-gradable.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging is defined as strategic underperformance on evaluations; current models can sandbag without detection by zero-shot prompted monitors (arXiv:2411.03336 evaluations-based safety cases). Elicitation gaps are central to dangerous-capability evals (METR, UK AISI).
