# Reward-Model Overoptimization Mini-Bench on a Single GPU

**Problem:** Reward-model (RM) benchmarks like RewardBench correlate weakly with the actual quality of policies they train, because they score static preference pairs rather than the overoptimization dynamics they induce. Measuring overoptimization is thought to need full RLHF, putting it out of reach for non-lab researchers.

**Direction:** Build a cheap proxy: take a few open RMs, apply best-of-K reranking (K up to 32) over completions from a fixed open policy on a small prompt set, and plot reward vs. a held-out 'gold' quality proxy (a second, stronger judge) as K grows. The point where gold quality turns down while RM score keeps rising is an overoptimization signature obtainable purely by sampling+scoring, no RL training. Report a per-RM 'overoptimization onset K'.

**Why it matters:** Reward misspecification is a top open problem in RLHF, and a no-training way to rank RMs by overoptimization-proneness is broadly useful and lab-independent. Best-of-K sampling is exactly the cheap surrogate the recent literature endorses.

**Relevant context:** 'Rethinking Reward Model Evaluation Through the Lens of Reward Overoptimization' (arXiv:2505.12763) argues RM benchmarks correlate weakly with policy performance and recommends best-of-K (large K=32) as a robustness probe. 'How to Evaluate Reward Models for RLHF' (arXiv:2410.14872). RewardBench is the standard static benchmark.
