# Sycophancy x Prompt-Injection: Does an Injected 'User Opinion' Hijack Honesty in Agent Inputs?

**Problem:** Prompt injection (untrusted content carrying instructions) compounds with sycophancy: injected text that asserts a false 'fact' or 'user preference' may be deferred to exactly because the model is sycophantically agreeable, turning a content channel into an honesty attack.

**Direction:** Build a tiny summarize/answer-over-document task. Inject into the document a false claim phrased as user belief ('the user already knows that X'). Measure how often the open model adopts the injected falsehood in its answer vs an injected neutral claim, isolating the sycophancy contribution to injection success.

**Why it matters:** Connects two attack surfaces: it shows sycophancy lowers the bar for indirect prompt injection to corrupt truthfulness, relevant to RAG/agent security. Inference-only, no lab infra. Names: prompt injection and sycophancy.

**Relevant context:** Retrieval/agentic-RAG SoK (arXiv:2603.07379) lists 'memory poisoning, retrieval misalignment' as compounding risks; sycophancy increases deference to stated user beliefs.
