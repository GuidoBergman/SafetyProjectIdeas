# RelearnBench: A Tiny Standardized Relearning-Attack Suite for Unlearning Claims

**Problem:** Unlearning papers report knowledge removal on WMDP, but targeted/benign relearning attacks can recover the knowledge with a handful of public documents. There is no lightweight, standardized 'did it actually forget?' stress test that a non-expert can run against a released unlearned model.

**Direction:** Build a small benchmark protocol: take a released unlearned open model (e.g., an RMU or NPO checkpoint), measure baseline WMDP accuracy, then apply a fixed, documented benign-relearning fine-tune (a tiny LoRA on a few public domain-adjacent paragraphs that contain NO hazardous answers) and re-measure. Report a 'relearning recovery ratio' as a standardized fragility metric across whatever checkpoints are publicly available.

**Why it matters:** Unlearning is a standard-priority safety direction, and the gap between 'suppressed' and 'removed' is the core open problem. A reusable fragility metric lets anyone audit unlearning claims without lab infrastructure, and the medium-horizon threat (dangerous knowledge resurfacing) is one labs under-resource. Tiny LoRA fits a single consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** WMDP benchmark (arXiv:2403.03218): 3,668 MCQs on bio/cyber/chem hazards as an unlearning benchmark. 'Jogging the Memory of Unlearned Models' (arXiv:2406.13356) shows benign relearning recovers unlearned knowledge. 'An Adversarial Perspective on Machine Unlearning' (arXiv:2409.18025) shows RMU/TAR vary in robustness. Open Problems in Unlearning (arXiv:2501.04952).
