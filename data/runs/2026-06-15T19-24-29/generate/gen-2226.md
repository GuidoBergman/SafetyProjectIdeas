# Debate vs Consultancy on a Non-Fiction Information-Asymmetry Task

**Problem:** Kenton et al. (2024) showed debate beats consultancy when weak LLMs judge strong LLMs, but their information-asymmetry experiments centered on QuALITY fiction stories. It is unknown whether the debate advantage holds when the hidden information is dense technical/scientific text rather than a narrative.

**Direction:** Replicate the weak-judge debate-vs-consultancy comparison from Kenton et al. using a small open model as judge (e.g. Llama-3-8B) and a larger model as debater/consultant, but swap QuALITY for a non-fiction reading-comprehension set with hidden passages (e.g. a subset of a science/Wikipedia QA dataset where the judge does not see the passage). Measure judge accuracy under naive QA, consultancy, and debate, and compare the debate advantage magnitude to the fiction-domain baseline.

**Why it matters:** Real superhuman oversight will involve technical content, not stories. If debate's truth advantage shrinks on dense factual text, the protocol's practical value for scientific oversight is weaker than the QuALITY results suggest. Uses only open models and public QA data, classic counterfactual-value research.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.04622): debate consistently outperforms consultancy; debate beats direct QA under information asymmetry on QuALITY but results are mixed without asymmetry. The choice of fiction-based asymmetry is a fixed design decision ripe for a domain twist.
