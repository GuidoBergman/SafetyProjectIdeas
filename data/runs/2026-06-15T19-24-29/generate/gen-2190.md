# Judge Calibration Variation: Forced-Choice vs Probability-Elicited Verdicts

**Problem:** Most oversight experiments force the judge into a discrete answer. Whether eliciting a CALIBRATED probability from the weak judge (and thresholding) improves oversight quality or surfaces useful 'I don't know' signals is rarely varied.

**Direction:** On debate/consultancy transcripts, elicit the weak judge's verdict two ways: forced binary choice vs a stated probability the answer is correct. Measure accuracy, and additionally whether low-confidence verdicts reliably flag the cases where the judge is wrong (selective-prediction value), comparing the two elicitation modes.

**Why it matters:** An overseer that knows when it doesn't know can escalate to humans — a practical recursive-oversight ingredient. Testing whether probability elicitation buys useful calibration is cheap, inference-only, and protocol-relevant.

**Relevant context:** Debate/consultancy setups (Kenton 2024) use discrete verdicts. LLM calibration/selective-prediction literature suggests probability elicitation can flag errors; untested as an oversight knob.
