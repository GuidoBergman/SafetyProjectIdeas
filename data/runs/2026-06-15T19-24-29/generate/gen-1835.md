# Activation-Probe Detection of Multi-Agent Collusion in an Open LLM Game

**Problem:** Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent Interpretability (arXiv:2604.01151, April 2026, Oxford/NYU) uses activation-based probes to detect collusion, but the experiments use specific agent setups; whether simple probes detect emergent covert coordination in an independent, lightweight multi-agent game is untested.

**Direction:** Set up two instances of a small open model playing a Schelling/coordination game with a side-channel for steganographic signaling, collect residual-stream activations, and train a linear probe to detect 'agent is colluding vs playing honestly'. Report probe AUC and whether it survives when agents are prompted to hide coordination.

**Why it matters:** Covert multi-agent collusion is a clear long-horizon threat that frontier labs underinvest in; a model-organism-style probe study on open models is high counterfactual value and feasible at small scale.

**Relevant context:** Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent Interpretability (arXiv:2604.01151, April 2026): activation-based probes for collusion; covert coordination hides info in innocuous channels; steganography detection is hard.
