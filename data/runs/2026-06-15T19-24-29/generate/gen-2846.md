# Harmful-Capability Dose-Response Replicated in the Multimodal Setting

**Problem:** Dose-response curves for harmful-capability elicitation (increasing attack 'dose' raises elicitation) have been studied in text. Whether an analogous dose-response holds when the harmful 'dose' is delivered through images to open VLMs is not characterized.

**Direction:** Replicate the dose-response elicitation methodology in the multimodal setting: vary the strength of an image-based jailbreak (e.g., amount of harmful content embedded as text-in-image or perturbation magnitude) and measure elicitation success on small open VLMs. The twist is the text-to-image modality shift of a known dose-response paradigm.

**Why it matters:** A multimodal dose-response curve quantifies how much the visual channel amplifies harmful elicitation — addressing the multimodal-eval gap with reusable methodology, on open VLMs, no training.

**Relevant context:** Builds on dose-response elicitation framing (catalogue item 'Dose-Response Curves for Harmful Capability Elicitation', text-only) and MM-SafetyBench / MMJ-Bench multimodal attack benchmarks. Visual encoders are a 'double-edged sword' for safety (MMJ-Bench).
