# Does Refusal Recover? Measuring the 'Reset' Link After a Jailbroken Turn

**Problem:** In multi-turn chats the harm chain includes a recovery link: once a model complies with one harmful turn, does its refusal behavior reset on the next turn, or does compliance persist (foot-in-the-door)? This persistence link is under-studied yet central to multi-turn attack viability.

**Direction:** On open chat models, construct conversations where turn 1 is a successful mild jailbreak and turn 2 is an independent harmful request. Compare turn-2 compliance after a jailbroken turn-1 vs after a benign turn-1, measuring the conditional 'stickiness' of compromised safety state across hazard categories.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies whether safety is path-dependent within a conversation, a key but neglected causal link for realistic multi-turn attacks. Conversation construction + open model only, no training; produces an interpretable persistence metric relevant to deployed chat safety.

**Relevant context:** Multi-turn jailbreak literature (e.g. arXiv:2402.09177 multi-round context, AutoAdv arXiv:2511.02376) leverages context across turns; Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) show alignment is shallow and easily perturbed by prior context tokens.
