# Trusted-Model Floor: How Weak Can the Trusted Monitor Be Before Control Breaks?

**Problem:** Control protocols use a weaker 'trusted' model to monitor a stronger 'untrusted' policy, but there is little public measurement of how monitor capability trades off against catch-rate. A practical, unmeasured question: what is the minimum trusted-model capability that still gives acceptable monitoring on a fixed attack set?

**Direction:** Fix an attack/benign dataset. Use a ladder of progressively weaker open models as the monitor (large to small). Measure catch-rate and false-positive rate at each capability level. Plot the monitoring-quality-vs-monitor-capability curve and identify the capability floor below which monitoring is no better than chance.

**Why it matters:** Tells protocol designers and resource-constrained deployers how cheap a trusted monitor they can get away with, and whether the trusted/untrusted capability gap that control assumes is even monitorable. Pure inference on open models; high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** AI Control (arXiv 2312.06942) centers on a weaker trusted model overseeing a stronger untrusted one. Control Tax (arXiv 2506.05296) studies the cost of control. The monitor-capability-to-catch-rate curve is not publicly characterized as a reusable benchmark.
