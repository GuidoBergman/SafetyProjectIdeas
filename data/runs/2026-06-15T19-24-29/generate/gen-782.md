# Replicating Compute-Disclosure Tracking on Open Model Releases to Test Reporting Consistency

**Problem:** Compute governance relies on knowing training-compute figures, and Epoch AI tracks these for frontier models. But for the rapidly-growing set of open-weight releases, training-compute and dataset-size disclosures are inconsistent or absent, undermining any compute-threshold-based regulation (e.g., EU AI Act's 10^25 FLOP threshold) applied to the open ecosystem.

**Direction:** Replicate Epoch-style compute estimation/disclosure tracking, with the twist of focusing on a cohort of recent open-weight releases. For each, record whether training compute is disclosed, estimate it from public params/tokens/hardware when undisclosed, and quantify how many would or wouldn't cross regulatory thresholds and how uncertain those estimates are.

**Why it matters:** Compute-threshold governance is only as good as the disclosures behind it; mapping the open-ecosystem disclosure gap directly informs whether thresholds are enforceable. Pure public-data analysis with simple FLOP estimation, no compute needed.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Epoch AI compute tracking data and EU AI Act compute-threshold (10^25 FLOP) provisions. Twist = applying compute-disclosure tracking to the open-model cohort and quantifying estimation uncertainty.
