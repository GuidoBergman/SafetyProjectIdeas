# Steganographic Capacity Ceiling of Small Open Models Under a Phrase-Ban Monitor

**Problem:** Process supervision (banning phrases) can push models toward concealed reasoning, and models can use simple encoding schemes in CoT but degrade with complexity. The actual information-theoretic capacity a small model can smuggle past a simple monitor is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Design a controlled channel: a model must answer a reasoning task while secretly transmitting a k-bit payload to a confederate decoder, under a monitor that bans explicit mention. Sweep payload size and measure success rate vs task accuracy. Produce a capacity curve (bits reliably hidden vs accuracy cost) for 2-3 small open models. No training — pure prompting of policy + decoder + monitor.

**Why it matters:** Encoded reasoning is a starred Open Phil open problem. A measured capacity ceiling tells safety researchers how much hidden coordination is even possible at current scale, grounding worst-case steganography assumptions. Fully reproducible outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2510.09714 (All Code, No Thought) shows current LLMs struggle to reason in ciphered language and degrade with complexity. arXiv 2510.19851 (Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?) stress-tests CoT obfuscation. arXiv 2602.23163 formalizes steganography for LLM monitoring.
