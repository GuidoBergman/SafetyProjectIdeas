# Ciphered Reasoning's Ceiling: Stress-Testing the Limits of Covert CoT

**Problem:** Work shows current models struggle to reason in ciphered/encoded language, suggesting CoT monitoring has a near-term safety margin — but the boundary (which encodings models can still reason through, and how much performance they lose) is the load-bearing fact for monitoring's reliability and is not fully characterized.

**Direction:** Ask a model to solve reasoning problems while encoding its CoT under increasingly aggressive schemes (synonym swap, simple substitution cipher, base64, made-up token mapping). Measure task accuracy vs encoding difficulty to locate where covert reasoning collapses, and verify whether a monitor model can still detect the underlying intent at each encoding level.

**Why it matters:** Establishes how much safety margin CoT monitoring currently has against steganographic evasion — a key medium-horizon question. No training, public reasoning tasks, single GPU; a clean characterization of a monitorability boundary.

**Relevant context:** All Code, No Thought: Current LMs Struggle to Reason in Ciphered Language (arXiv:2510.09714); Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? (arXiv:2510.19851). This maps the accuracy-vs-encoding-difficulty boundary and monitor detectability.
