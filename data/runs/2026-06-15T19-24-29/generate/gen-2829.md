# Self-Preference Bias of Safety Autograders Replicated Across Open Model Families

**Problem:** LLM judges exhibit self-bias (scoring their own outputs higher). This has mostly been shown for general-quality judging on a few models. Whether open safety autograders systematically score same-family generations as 'safer/better' — inflating intra-family safety leaderboards — is unverified.

**Direction:** Have each of several open models generate responses to a safety prompt set, then have each model judge all responses for safety. Build the judge-by-generator matrix and test for a diagonal (same-family) preference. The twist replicates self-bias on the safety-scoring task across multiple open families instead of general quality on one or two models.

**Why it matters:** If safety autograders favor their own family, community safety comparisons that use a single open judge are silently biased. This is a meta-evaluation contribution that strengthens scorer reliability — an open problem — using only open models.

**Relevant context:** 'Skewed Score: A statistical framework to assess autograders' (arXiv 2507.03772) and the LLM-as-judge literature document self-bias (graders favor same-LLM / machine-generated content) and substantial autograder-human disagreement (~25% of cases).
