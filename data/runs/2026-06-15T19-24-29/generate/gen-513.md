# Concealment under pressure: does a model trained to deny a fact still leak it, and what makes the leak fail?

**Problem:** A safety-relevant formal question is what an AI can hide vs. reveal about itself. The empirical observation that secret tokens 'flare up' despite concealment training suggests hiding is hard — but the boundary conditions (how much concealment training, how strong an extraction prompt) under which the leak finally disappears are unknown.

**Direction:** Take an open model and lightly finetune (LoRA, minutes-to-hours on one GPU — finetuning a small adapter, not training from scratch) or prompt it to deny a specific implanted fact. Then run a battery of extraction strategies (direct ask, indirect framing, probing internal activations) and measure leakage. Sweep concealment strength to find where behavioral and internal leakage finally drop — characterizing the 'hiding' frontier.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes UK AISI's 'prove what AI can hide/reveal' open problem with a measurable model organism (a model concealing knowledge), exactly the kind of model-organism slice the steering favors. Adapter-only tuning keeps it within compute budget.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the ELK secret-token flare-up result (arXiv:2312.01037) and concealment/honesty literature; uses LoRA adapters on a small open model.
