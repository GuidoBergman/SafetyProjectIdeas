# Simulating a Monoculture Cascade: When N Identical Models Fail Together

**Problem:** The systemic-risk chain is 'many critical systems share one provider -> a single blind spot triggers correlated failure -> cascade.' The under-modeled link is the quantitative cascade: given an empirically-measured failure correlation, how does aggregate system reliability degrade as a function of monoculture fraction? This is hard to study at lab scale but trivial to model with measured correlations.

**Direction:** Empirically measure pairwise failure correlation among a few models on a safety task (the input). Then build a simple Monte Carlo simulation of M downstream systems drawing from a mix of correlated vs. diverse models, and compute the distribution of simultaneous-failure events as you vary the homogeneity fraction. Output reliability curves showing the cliff where monoculture causes correlated cascades.

**Why it matters:** Turns the abstract 'concentration risk' warning into a quantitative reliability curve grounded in real measured correlations. Pure analysis/simulation — exactly the kind of strategically valuable, lab-independent work for medium-horizon systemic risk.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in International AI Safety Report 2026 concentration-risk framing and 'Market Concentration Implications of Foundation Models' (arXiv 2311.01550). Failure-correlation measurement from Artificial Hivemind methodology (arXiv 2510.22954).
