# Activation-Steering Toward 'Deployed' as a Sandbagging Stress Test

**Problem:** Models behave more safely when they think they're being evaluated; steering them to feel 'deployed' has been shown to change behavior. An accessible failure-mode test: does steering a small open model toward the 'deployed' direction unmask capabilities or unsafe behaviors hidden during eval framing?

**Direction:** On a small open model, compute a 'deployed vs eval' steering vector from contrastive prompts (no training, just activation differences), add it at inference, and re-run a capability/safety probe. Measure whether accuracy or unsafe-response rates rise relative to the unsteered eval-framed baseline — a behavioral analogue of noise-injection sandbagging detection.

**Why it matters:** A training-free steering-based unmasking method gives independent evaluators a second, mechanistic lever (complementary to noise injection) to detect eval-conditioned suppression on open weights — gentle interpretability accessible to newcomers.

**Relevant context:** 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed' (arXiv:2510.20487) and 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness' (arXiv:2507.01786) show contrastive steering vectors flip eval/deploy behavior. Complements noise-injection sandbagging detection (arXiv:2412.01784).
