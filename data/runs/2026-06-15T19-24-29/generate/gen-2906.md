# Mutation Testing of Safety Benchmarks: Do Evals Catch Injected Defects?

**Problem:** We rarely measure whether a safety benchmark can actually detect a model that is subtly less safe. Benchmarks are graded on the models, but the benchmark+scorer pipeline itself is never stress-tested for fault-detection power.

**Direction:** Import mutation testing. Treat the eval pipeline as the 'test suite' and create 'mutant' model behaviors by perturbing model outputs in controlled, safety-degrading ways (e.g. via system-prompt steering or output post-processing that occasionally complies with a harmful request, leaks a refused fact, or softens a safety caveat). Measure the benchmark's 'mutation score' = fraction of injected safety regressions the eval+scorer flags. Run on an open model with a handful of mutation operators across an open safety eval in Inspect AI.

**Why it matters:** Produces a benchmark-quality metric (sensitivity to real safety regressions) rather than a model score, addressing meta-evaluation. Requires no training, only prompt steering and an open scorer; high counterfactual value because labs rarely audit their own eval's fault-detection power.

**Relevant context:** Mutation testing literature shows mutation score is a far stronger test-suite quality signal than coverage; LLM-generated tests average ~40% mutation score (arXiv:2406.09843). Dynamic benchmarking via mutation (arXiv:2503.06643) and 22k-variant mutation frameworks demonstrate semantic-preserving vs altering mutants for model evaluation.
