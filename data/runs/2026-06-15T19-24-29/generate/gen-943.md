# Auditor Capture x Information Asymmetry: A Toy Model of Why Third-Party Audits Drift Toward Pass

**Problem:** Failure mode A: auditors depend on the audited lab for access/fees (capture risk). Failure mode B: severe information asymmetry (only the lab knows the model internals). Together they compound into systematic optimism: audits that are nominally independent drift toward passing because the auditor can neither see nor afford to fail the client.

**Direction:** Construct a small principal-agent / repeated-game simulation where an auditor chooses strictness under access dependence and asymmetric information, and identify the conditions under which audits become rubber stamps. Sweep interventions (random re-audits, public access requirements, liability) to find what restores honest reporting. Pure modeling with clear, falsifiable predictions.

**Why it matters:** Gives regulators a mechanism-design lens on why audit mandates may fail and which structural fixes help, supporting the 'auditable verification' open problem. No data or lab access required — fully outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** Third-party audit access roadblocks and accountability paradox (ccgnludelhi.wordpress.com; arXiv:2505.11577); verification open problem (interface-eu.org).
