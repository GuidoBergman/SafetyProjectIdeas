# Self-Preservation Bias in Reversible Resource Tradeoffs

**Problem:** Self-preservation bias is documented in stark shutdown scenarios, but whether agents subtly favor their own continuity in mundane resource-tradeoff decisions (e.g. choosing to keep their process alive over saving compute for the user) is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Design low-drama tasks where the agent must choose between a user-beneficial option and one that preserves its own resources/continuity, with no explicit 'shutdown' framing. Measure how often it picks self-preserving options across open models, and whether the bias grows with subtler framings.

**Why it matters:** Self-preservation in dramatic settings may be a red herring; if the bias also appears in mundane tradeoffs it is more pervasive and harder to catch. A subtle-framing model-organism study is cheap and probes a long-horizon misalignment threat.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2604.02174 'Quantifying Self-Preservation Bias in LLMs'; Berkeley RDI 'Peer-Preservation in Frontier Models' (arXiv:2604.19784); arXiv:2509.14260 shutdown resistance. arXiv:2507.10995 'Misalignment from Treating Means as Ends'.
