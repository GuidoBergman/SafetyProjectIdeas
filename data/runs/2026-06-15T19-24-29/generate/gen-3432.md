# Paraphrase-Robustness of Self-Explanations: Semantic Invariance Replication

**Problem:** Recent work shows LLM self-explanations fail semantic invariance — semantically equivalent paraphrases of a question yield inconsistent stated reasons — but this has not been characterized as a cheap, scalable honesty diagnostic across open models.

**Direction:** Take a factual/MC dataset, generate N meaning-preserving paraphrases per item (templated or model-generated), collect each model's stated explanation/answer, and measure inconsistency (answer flips and explanation disagreement) under paraphrase. Twist: turn the 'self-explanations fail semantic invariance' finding into a simple per-model 'explanation stability score' and rank open models by it.

**Why it matters:** An explanation that changes when you reword the question is not a faithful account of the model's computation. A reproducible stability score gives non-lab evaluators a way to flag untrustworthy explanations with only API/inference access.

**Relevant context:** 'LLM Self-Explanations Fail Semantic Invariance' (arXiv 2603.01254) and 'Are self-explanations from LLMs faithful?' (arXiv 2401.07927) show explanations vary under semantic-preserving changes and counterfactual tests. Paraphrasing is a standard faithfulness intervention.
