# Do Reasoning Models Converge on the Same Wrong Path? Homogeneity of Chains of Thought

**Problem:** Homogeneity research has focused on final outputs, but reasoning models add a link: 'narrow training -> convergent reasoning paths -> correlated reasoning errors.' If different reasoning models traverse the SAME flawed chain on safety-relevant problems, then chain-of-thought monitoring inherits a shared blind spot exactly where it's relied on most.

**Direction:** Collect a set of problems where reasoning can go wrong (e.g. deceptive math word-problems, safety reasoning traps). Sample chains of thought from 3-4 open reasoning models. Cluster the reasoning trajectories (by step content / divergence metric) and measure whether models converge on identical intermediate steps and identical errors, vs. baseline non-reasoning models.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a key safety hope. If reasoning models share convergent flawed paths, monitors trained/tuned on one model's CoT may be blind to the same failure in others. Extends homogeneity to reasoning — a gap explicitly flagged in the subfield context.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Reasoning Path Divergence' (arXiv 2510.26122) and 'Reasoning Models Generate Societies of Thought' (arXiv 2601.10825). Directly addresses the context's open problem 'study homogeneity in reasoning models.'
