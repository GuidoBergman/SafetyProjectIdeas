# Refusal Brittleness x Jailbreak Disclosure Lag: How a Capability Eval and an Incident Channel Both Miss the Same Harm

**Problem:** Failure mode A: pre-deployment refusal evals certify a model 'safe' on harmful prompts. Failure mode B: incident/jailbreak reporting is slow and voluntary, so post-deployment failures aren't logged. A jailbreak that defeats the certified refusal lands in the gap between the two systems and is governed by neither.

**Direction:** On an open model, take a sample of harmful prompts it refuses (passing a mock 'eval'), apply a handful of well-known public jailbreak templates, and measure the refusal-collapse rate. Then estimate the reporting lag: search the AI Incident Database / public trackers for how long analogous jailbreaks took to be logged. Quantify the combined 'certified-but-broken-and-unreported' exposure window.

**Why it matters:** Shows that static refusal certification plus laggy incident reporting jointly leave a structural blind window, motivating mandatory post-deployment monitoring. No training; uses public jailbreak templates and one open model.

**Relevant context:** Incident reporting underreporting (arXiv:2511.05914); refusal removal/jailbreak literature; Open Problems in Technical AI Governance assessment gaps (arXiv:2407.14981).
