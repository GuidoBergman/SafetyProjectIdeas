# Cross-Family Untrusted Monitoring: Does Mixing Model Families Reduce Collusion Risk?

**Problem:** Untrusted monitoring risks collusion when the policy and monitor share coordination capacity. An intuitive but under-tested mitigation is using a DIFFERENT model family as the monitor, on the assumption that cross-family pairs share fewer Schelling points and self-recognition cues.

**Direction:** Run the acausal-collusion coordination game with same-family vs cross-family generator/monitor pairs drawn from small open models. Measure whether cross-family pairs coordinate less successfully than same-family pairs at matched capability, isolating model-family as a controllable anti-collusion lever.

**Why it matters:** If cross-family monitoring measurably cuts collusion, it is a free, deployable mitigation requiring only model choice, not training. Testing it across open families is reproducible, lab-independent, and directly informs untrusted-monitoring safety cases.

**Relevant context:** Redwood collusion post and 'When can we trust untrusted monitoring?' (arxiv 2602.20628) discuss collusion strategies and Schelling points; self-recognition triples red-team win rate. Schelling coordination measured on frontier models (~40-56%, Motwani et al. 2025). Cross-family pairing is a natural untested mitigation.
