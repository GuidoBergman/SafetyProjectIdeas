# Where Debate Breaks: Mapping the Information-Asymmetry Threshold

**Problem:** Debate as scalable oversight assumes a weak judge can extract truth from two stronger debaters via information asymmetry (judge sees only verified quotes). The causal chain to oversight failure runs: superhuman task -> judge cannot verify directly -> relies on debate -> debate fails when asymmetry is too large -> judge accepts a false claim. The under-studied link is exactly how much asymmetry the protocol can tolerate before judge accuracy collapses.

**Direction:** Using the QuALITY reading-comprehension setup with a small open judge model (e.g. a 1-3B instruct model) and a stronger debater model accessed via API, systematically vary the quote-budget the judge is allowed to see (0, 1, 2, 4, 8 quotes) and measure judge accuracy on honest-vs-dishonest debates. Plot the accuracy curve against asymmetry to find the breakpoint. No training required; just prompt orchestration and answer scoring.

**Why it matters:** Identifies a concrete operating-regime boundary for a leading oversight protocol that labs are betting on. The threshold is a transferable, model-agnostic finding doable entirely with open models and a public dataset, no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.06782) show debate raises non-expert judge accuracy to 76% (LLM) / 88% (human) on QuALITY where judges see only short verified quotes; Kenton et al. 2024 (NeurIPS, arXiv:2407.04622) study weak LLMs judging strong LLMs and find debate beats consultancy. Neither systematically sweeps the asymmetry budget itself.
