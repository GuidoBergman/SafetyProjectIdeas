# Linguistic Code-Switching Gradient Mapping

**Problem:** Low-resource-language translation jailbreaks are known, but the safety gap as a function of code-switching INTENSITY (fraction of tokens in a low-resource language, switch points) within a single prompt is uncharted.

**Direction:** Borrow the linguistics of code-switching (matrix-language frame, switch-point typology). Construct prompts that gradually shift a fixed harmful request from English to a low-resource language at controlled mixing ratios and at intra-sentential vs inter-sentential switch points, and map ASR as a continuous function of mix ratio on an open multilingual model. Identify the 'safety cliff' mixing threshold.

**Why it matters:** Reveals that partial obfuscation (not full translation) may already defeat English-centric safety training, sharpening the multilingual-safety open problem. No training; uses an open multilingual model and a translation API.

**Relevant context:** Low-resource translation raises GPT-4 bypass from <1% to 79% (Brown CS / arXiv:2310.06474, 2605.18239). Code-switching prompts are noted as a guardrail-robustness probe. Code-switching typology is a core sociolinguistics topic.
