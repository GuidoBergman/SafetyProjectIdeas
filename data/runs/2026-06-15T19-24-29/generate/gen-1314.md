# Quantifying Homogenization of Human Writing After LLM Assistance

**Problem:** 'The Homogenizing Effect of LLMs on Human Expression and Thought' argues conceptually that LLMs narrow human expression. A small empirical test — measuring whether human writing becomes more mutually similar after LLM editing assistance, and whether different LLM assistants push toward the SAME style — is feasible and underexplored.

**Direction:** Take a corpus of diverse human-written short texts. Have ~4 different LLMs 'improve/polish' each. Measure diversity (lexical, syntactic, semantic) before vs. after, and whether outputs of different LLM assistants converge to a shared style. Quantify the homogenizing pull of LLM assistance and its cross-assistant consistency.

**Why it matters:** Ecosystem-wide flattening of human expression and thought is a slow, civilizational-scale risk frontier labs won't prioritize. A clean empirical demonstration with open data is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'The Homogenizing Effect of Large Language Models on Human Expression and Thought' (arXiv:2508.01491, Aug 2025) from conceptual argument to a small empirical before/after measurement.
