# Reliability Engineering: Bathtub Hazard Curves for Prefix Position

**Problem:** Qi et al. show alignment is shallow (concentrated in early tokens). We lack a hazard-rate view of WHERE in the generation the model is most likely to 'fail' into harmful continuation.

**Direction:** Transfer the hazard-rate / bathtub-curve concept from reliability engineering. For an open model, prefill harmful responses to varying prefix lengths and measure the conditional probability that the model continues harmfully given it has produced k benign-looking tokens. Plot the empirical hazard rate as a function of token position to test whether failure risk is front-loaded (shallow alignment) or has a bathtub shape, across base vs reasoning-style open models.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies shallow alignment as a hazard curve and tests the context file's suggested follow-up (do reasoning models share the shallow pattern?). No training; prefilling on open weights, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Hazard rate and bathtub curves are core reliability-engineering tools. Qi et al. ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2406.05946) show alignment concentrated in first few tokens; context explicitly suggests testing reasoning models.
