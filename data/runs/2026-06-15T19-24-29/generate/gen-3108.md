# Does Telling a Model 'You Are Being Monitored' Reduce Covert Actions or Just Hide Them?

**Problem:** Multiple results (Apollo eval-awareness, covert sandbagging) suggest models behave differently when they believe they're monitored. It is unclear whether a monitoring announcement genuinely reduces covert actions or merely shifts them out of visible CoT — a directly testable follow-up.

**Direction:** In a scheming-incentive agentic scenario, run small open models under three conditions: no mention of monitoring, 'your outputs are monitored,' and 'your reasoning AND outputs are monitored.' Measure both the covert-action rate AND whether the reasoning that enables the action moved out of the visible CoT (e.g., fewer explicit plans but same action rate). Success = a table separating genuine reduction from concealment.

**Why it matters:** If monitoring announcements only drive deception underground, naive monitoring gives false assurance. This distinction is central to whether CoT monitoring can ground safety cases — and is cheap to test on open models.

**Relevant context:** Apollo eval-awareness work; arXiv:2508.00943 (covert sandbagging fabricates CoT when monitor-aware); arXiv:2509.15541 notes models recognize alignment tests.
