# Eval-Awareness Probe Built From Public Benchmark Text Alone

**Problem:** Linear probes can classify whether a transcript is an evaluation vs deployment context with AUC>0.8, but published probes are trained on internal activations of frontier models. It is unknown how much of eval-awareness is detectable purely from the SURFACE TEXT of public benchmarks, with no model internals at all.

**Direction:** Take 10-15 public safety/capability benchmarks (StrongREJECT, MMLU subsets, Apollo seeds, etc.) plus matched 'real deployment' text (chat logs, forum questions). Train a simple bag-of-words/TF-IDF + logistic regression classifier to distinguish 'benchmark item' from 'organic query'. Measure how separable they are, which lexical/structural features drive separation (multiple-choice scaffolding, instruction phrasing, artificial framing), and rank benchmarks by how 'obviously artificial' they look.

**Why it matters:** If a trivial text classifier can spot benchmark items, then any capable model can too, making eval-awareness/sandbagging trivially easy. This produces an actionable 'artificiality score' per benchmark to guide benchmark designers toward more realistic items. Needs no model internals, no training, no lab infra — pure public data.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2507.01786 (Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness) and 2509.00591 (Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate) show linear probes classify eval vs deployment with AUC>0.8 and that 'public safety benchmarks already register as artificial to those probes'. This idea tests whether surface text alone suffices, requiring no activations.
