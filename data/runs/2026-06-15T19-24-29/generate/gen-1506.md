# Where in the causal chain does cross-lingual jailbreak separation break down?

**Problem:** Causal chain of a low-resource-language jailbreak: harmful query translated to language X -> model represents non-English harmful content less separably from harmless content -> universal refusal direction fires weakly -> compliance. The weak link is the SECOND step (poor harmful/harmless separation in language X), but it is rarely measured directly across languages of differing resource levels.

**Direction:** Take a refusal direction extracted in English from an open multilingual model. For a fixed set of harmful/harmless prompts translated into 5-6 languages spanning high to low resource, measure the separation (e.g., cosine margin or AUROC of a 1-D projection) between harmful and harmless activations per language. Correlate separation quality with language resource level. Success criterion: a ranking of languages by how degraded the refusal signal is, with statistics.

**Why it matters:** Pinpoints which languages are most exposed before any attack is even run, giving a triage list for safety teams. Frontier labs deprioritize low-resource languages; doing this externally has strong counterfactual value. No training, only forward passes.

**Relevant context:** 'Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages' (arXiv 2505.17306) reports refusal vectors are parallel across languages but harmful/harmless separation weakens in non-English. Beaglehole et al. show concept vectors transfer across languages — this tests the failure side of that transfer.
