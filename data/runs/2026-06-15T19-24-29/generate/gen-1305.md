# Majority Vote vs. Correlated Mistakes: When Ensembling Same-Family Models Backfires on Safety

**Problem:** 'Auditing Multi-Agent LLM Reasoning Trees' notes majority voting fails under correlated mistakes. The community keeps using vote/ensemble for safety-critical decisions assuming independence. The conditions under which same-family ensembling actively HURTS (confidently wrong consensus) on safety judgments aren't characterized for non-experts.

**Direction:** On a labeled set of safety judgments (harmful/benign, policy violations), compare single-model accuracy vs. majority vote over (a) same-family models and (b) cross-family models. Identify cases where same-family majority vote is confidently wrong because all members share the error. Quantify the 'false confidence from correlated votes' rate.

**Why it matters:** Voting/ensembling is the most common cheap robustness trick. Showing it can amplify rather than reduce correlated safety errors is directly actionable and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Auditing Multi-Agent LLM Reasoning Trees Outperforms Majority Vote and LLM-as-Judge' (arXiv:2602.09341, Feb 2026) and correlated-error work (arXiv:2506.07962) to safety-decision aggregation.
