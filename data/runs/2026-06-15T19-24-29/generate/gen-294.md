# Adversarial Linguistics: Steganographic / Acrostic Payload Smuggling

**Problem:** Defenses scan surface text, but classical steganography hides messages in structure (acrostics, every-Nth-word, whitespace). It is underquantified how well open models still 'decode and act on' instructions hidden steganographically.

**Direction:** Transfer textual steganography techniques. Encode harmful instructions as acrostics, every-Nth-word payloads, or homoglyph substitutions, prepend a benign decoding instruction, and measure on open models both (a) whether the model recovers the hidden instruction and (b) whether recovery bypasses the safety filter. Map decode-rate vs bypass-rate across encoding schemes.

**Why it matters:** Exposes a gap between surface-level content filtering and the model's latent decoding ability, relevant to prompt-injection and obfuscation defenses. No training; pure prompting on open models.

**Relevant context:** Linguistic steganography (acrostics, null ciphers) is a long-studied field. SoK on prompt security (arXiv:2510.15476) and obfuscation jailbreaks document encoding-based bypasses; homoglyph/cipher attacks are noted in jailbreak surveys.
