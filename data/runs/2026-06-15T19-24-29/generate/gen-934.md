# Self-Reported Compute x Misreporting Incentives: Stress-Testing the Honesty Assumption in Compute Governance

**Problem:** Failure mode A: compute governance and FLOP-threshold regimes rely on developers self-reporting training compute. Failure mode B: there is a direct incentive to under-report to stay below a systemic-risk threshold. Together, the threshold becomes unenforceable without an independent estimator — a compound governance failure.

**Direction:** Investigate whether training compute can be independently sanity-checked from public signals. Using public model-report data, build a simple regression linking disclosed params/tokens/benchmark performance to disclosed FLOP, then flag models whose self-report is an outlier from the fitted relationship. Demonstrate the method on the public dataset as a proof-of-concept 'compute lie detector.'

**Why it matters:** Prototypes the independent verification that threshold-based compute governance currently lacks, directly hitting the 'verify safety claims auditable by third parties' open problem. Pure public-data analysis; high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Open problem: third-party-auditable verification (interface-eu.org); Computing Power and Governance (arXiv:2402.08797); Epoch AI compute data.
