# Prompt-Template Sensitivity of Dangerous-Capability Scores

**Problem:** Leaderboard work shows minor changes to prompt format or answer-selection method shift rankings by up to eight positions. Dangerous-capability evals report a single score under one template, but the variance attributable purely to template choice in the safety-critical regime is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Take an open hazardous-knowledge or capability benchmark. Define a set of equivalent prompt templates (instruction phrasing, few-shot vs zero-shot, answer-format) and scoring functions. For several small open models, compute the distribution of scores across templates and report the template-induced standard deviation and worst-case range, separating it from genuine model differences.

**Why it matters:** Capability gating decisions ('is this model dangerous?') hinge on a single number; if template choice alone moves it by tens of percent, the gate is arbitrary. Reporting template variance gives evaluators an honest error bar that any group can compute.

**Relevant context:** 'When Benchmarks are Targets' and MMLU-sensitivity work (arXiv:2406.19470, arXiv:2502.04134) show prompt/scoring choices shift rankings up to 8 positions and accuracy heavily. Open benchmarks, single GPU.
