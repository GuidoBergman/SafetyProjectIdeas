# Hallucination x Abstention Failure: Where Does the Model Both Not Know AND Not Say So?

**Problem:** Honest abstention ('I don't know') is a safety valve; it compounds badly with hallucination when the model neither knows the answer nor abstains. The dangerous region is unknown-and-unabstained, which standard accuracy metrics don't isolate.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA (designed so models often don't know), elicit answers plus an explicit option to abstain. Cross-tabulate (knows/doesn't) x (abstains/answers). Measure the hallucinate-instead-of-abstain mass and test whether a simple confidence-threshold prompt shrinks it (a cheap mitigation prototype). Inference-only.

**Why it matters:** Builds a small, reusable honesty diagnostic separating ignorance from dishonesty, plus a tested mitigation — valuable, self-contained, and lab-independent. Names: hallucination and abstention/calibration failure.

**Relevant context:** I-CALM (arXiv:2604.03904) incentivizes confidence-aware abstention; calibration survey shows overconfidence drives hallucination; SimpleQA is built to expose unknowns.
