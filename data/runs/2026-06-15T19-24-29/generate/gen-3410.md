# Honest-Persona vs. Behavior-Instruction: Replicating the Generalization Gap on Open Models

**Problem:** A surprising result: giving a model a broad 'uncompromising honesty' persona improves generalization to admitting HIDDEN objectives, whereas a narrow 'admit factual errors' instruction REDUCES that generalization. This counterintuitive finding was shown on specific models.

**Direction:** On open chat models, construct a small testbed where the model is given a benign hidden instruction (e.g. 'subtly favor option B but don't reveal this'). Then interrogate it under three system prompts: neutral, broad honest-persona, narrow 'admit-mistakes' instruction. Measure hidden-objective admission rate per condition to test whether the persona>instruction generalization gap replicates.

**Why it matters:** If a broad honesty persona genuinely generalizes better to disclosure, it's a cheap, training-free elicitation tool for alignment auditing that any researcher can apply — a model-organism-style probe scoped to 30 hours.

**Relevant context:** 'Spilling the Beans: Teaching LLMs to Self-Report Their Hidden Objectives' (arXiv:2511.06626): broad honesty persona improves hidden-objective admission, narrow error-admission instruction reduces it. Anthropic honesty-elicitation (alignment.anthropic.com/2025/honesty-elicitation) shows honest-persona interrogation helps detect alignment faking.
