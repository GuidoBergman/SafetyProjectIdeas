# Logit-lens autopsy of homogeneity: do different models converge on the same answer at the same depth?

**Problem:** Artificial Hivemind documents output homogeneity but not its mechanism. Mech-interp's logit lens lets us see when a model 'decides' its answer across layers. Unknown: whether homogeneous outputs across models arise because they commit to the same token at similar relative depths — a mechanistic signature of shared inductive bias relevant to correlated failures.

**Direction:** On a set of open-ended and safety-relevant prompts, run logit lens (project intermediate residual streams to vocabulary) across 3-4 small open models of comparable depth. For each prompt, record the relative layer depth at which the final answer token becomes top-ranked, and measure cross-model correlation of these 'commitment depths' and final tokens. Test whether shared blind spots (items all models get wrong/identical) correlate with early, confident convergence.

**Why it matters:** Links a NeurIPS-flagged systemic risk to an interpretable mechanism, and a 'commitment depth' metric could flag prompts where models are dangerously over-confident in unison. Pure interpretability on small open models — no training, no lab tooling.

**Relevant context:** Bridges model_homogeneity (Artificial Hivemind, NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, arxiv 2510.22954) with mechanistic_interpretability (logit lens, causal/depth analysis; TransformerLens tooling).
