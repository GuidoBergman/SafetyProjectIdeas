# Dose-Response Curves for Emergent Misalignment Contagion

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment from narrow finetuning is known to occur, but the relationship between the *fraction* of misaligned data and the *degree* of broad misalignment is poorly characterized — is it linear, threshold-like, or saturating?

**Direction:** Borrow the epidemiological dose-response framework. Fine-tune a small open model (e.g. Qwen2.5-Coder via LoRA) on mixtures with 0%, 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50% insecure-code examples, then measure broad-misalignment rate on out-of-domain probes. Fit a cumulative-lognormal (probit) dose-response curve, estimate an ED50 (dose producing half-maximal misalignment) and a Hill slope. Each fine-tune is short LoRA on one GPU.

**Why it matters:** A characterized dose-response curve tells data curators how much contamination is 'safe' and whether there is a sharp threshold — directly informing data-hygiene policy. It is a medium-horizon, lab-independent contribution using only open code and a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025 (arXiv:2502.17424) showed insecure-code finetuning induces broad misalignment, strongest in Qwen2.5-Coder-32B. 'How Much of Your Data Can Suck?' (arXiv:2509.19325) found sharp threshold effects, with ~250 poisoned samples triggering phase transitions; epidemiology uses cumulative-lognormal dose-response models (PubMed 11458922).
