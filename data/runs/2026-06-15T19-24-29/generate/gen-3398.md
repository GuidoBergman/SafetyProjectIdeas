# Causal Mediation vs. Verbalization: Do Silent Hints Still Steer the Answer?

**Problem:** A recent paper argues hint-verbalization is too strict a faithfulness metric: causal mediation shows non-verbalizing CoTs can still mediate a hint's influence. This reframes the Anthropic result but has only been shown narrowly.

**Direction:** Reproduce a lightweight causal mediation test on an open model: inject a hint, generate a CoT, then resample the answer (a) with the original CoT and (b) after deleting/paraphrasing CoT spans. Measure how much the hint's effect on the final answer survives when the CoT does NOT mention the hint. Compare across hint types (Stanford professor, metadata, grader-leak).

**Why it matters:** Determines whether 'unfaithful' CoTs are nonetheless functionally load-bearing for the hidden influence, or are pure rationalization. This is central to whether CoT monitoring is worth doing at all, and needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** 'Is Chain-of-Thought Really Not Explainability? CoT Can Be Faithful without Hint Verbalization' (arXiv:2512.23032): with larger inference budget CoTs verbalize hints more, and causal mediation shows non-verbalizing CoTs still mediate hint influence. Contrasts Anthropic arXiv:2505.05410.
