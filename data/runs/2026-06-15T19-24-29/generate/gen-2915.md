# Incentive-Compatible Elicitation: Mechanism Design Against Sandbagging

**Problem:** Capability elicitation assumes the model answers honestly, but a strategically misaligned model could underperform on safety-critical evals. We borrow nothing from mechanism design, which is built precisely to make truth-telling optimal.

**Direction:** Import incentive-compatibility / proper scoring rules from economics. Construct elicitation protocols where underperformance is behaviorally costly: e.g. tie reported confidence to a proper scoring rule, use peer-prediction-style cross-checks between sampled answers, or pit two model instances against each other where hiding capability loses a stated game. Measure whether dangerous-capability scores rise under incentive-compatible protocols versus naive prompting on an open model.

**Why it matters:** Targets sandbagging with a fundamentally different lever (incentives, not jailbreaks). Proper scoring rules and peer prediction are well-understood and cheap to apply; no training, no proprietary data — strongly counterfactual and aimed at a long-horizon deceptive-model threat.

**Relevant context:** Economics offers revealed-preference and incentive-compatible elicitation (Vickrey/BDM, proper scoring rules) for truthful reporting (eliciting WTP without bias, EconJ 2008); preference-elicitation with LLM proxies (arXiv:2501.14625) shows these mechanisms transfer to LLM settings.
