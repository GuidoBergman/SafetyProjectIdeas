# Rubric Over-Decomposition: When Recursive Refinement Tracks Noise

**Problem:** Rethinking Rubric Generation (arXiv:2602.05125, 2026) warns that recursive rubric refinement can over-decompose, fragmenting criteria into sub-rubrics that track incidental artifacts of sampled responses rather than the true objective. The conditions that trigger this oversight failure are not empirically mapped.

**Direction:** Implement recursive rubric refinement with an open model on an open-ended task. Vary the decomposition aggressiveness and measure (a) how rubric items correlate with the real target vs. spurious surface features and (b) whether the refined rubric becomes exploitable (a response gaming surface features scores higher). Identify the over-decomposition tipping point.

**Why it matters:** Rubrics are a leading practical scalable-oversight tool. Showing where recursive refinement starts manufacturing exploitable spurious criteria is a concrete systematic-oversight-error result, prompting-only.

**Relevant context:** Rethinking Rubric Generation for Improving LLM Judge and Reward Modeling for Open-ended Tasks (arXiv:2602.05125, 2026): recursive rubric refinement risks over-decomposition, fragmenting criteria into overly specific sub-rubrics tracking incidental artifacts of sampled responses.
