# Steering Vectors Break Coherence Before They Change Behavior: Mapping the Safe Range

**Problem:** Activation steering (including interpretable, concept-aligned steering) can compromise model safety and degrade outputs much like arbitrary perturbations, and the dose at which steering produces the intended effect vs. broken/off-target behavior is poorly characterized. Beginners pick a steering coefficient by trial and error.

**Direction:** For a small open model, take a published steering vector (e.g. a refusal or sentiment direction or an SAE feature). Sweep the steering coefficient and at each level measure both the intended effect (does target behavior change?) and collateral damage (perplexity on neutral text, off-topic rate, degeneration). Plot a dose-response showing the window, if any, where the intended effect occurs without breaking the model, across several features.

**Why it matters:** Interpretable steering is promoted as a safe control method, but if every useful dose also degrades the model, the 'control' is unsafe. A reusable dose-response protocol shows whether semantic interventions are genuinely surgical and is doable entirely on open weights.

**Relevant context:** 'Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety' (OpenReview) shows interpretable concept-aligned interventions pose risks similar to arbitrary perturbations; 'SAE features for refusal and sycophancy steering vectors' (LessWrong/AF) decomposes steering vectors into features.
