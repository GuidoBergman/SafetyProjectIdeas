# Shared Jailbreak Surface: Does One Adversarial Suffix Open Many Open Models?

**Problem:** Success state: red-teamers can estimate, before deployment, how many independent models a single jailbreak will simultaneously compromise. Backward: we need a measurement of how correlated the jailbreak-vulnerability surface is across the open-model ecosystem.

**Direction:** Take a set of published, non-novel jailbreak templates/suffixes (from public repos) and apply each across 6-8 open-weight models on a fixed harmful-prompt set. Build a model-by-attack success matrix and measure transfer correlation. Identify whether a small 'universal' attack set compromises most models, and whether more homogeneous models co-fail.

**Why it matters:** If a single attack opens many deployed systems at once, an adversary gets correlated multi-system compromise for the price of one — the security analogue of monoculture. Quantifying the shared attack surface tells defenders how much diversity actually buys robustness.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in jailbreak-transferability findings (arXiv 2502.03052, MASTERKEY) and foundationality-driven correlated failure (arXiv 2404.09932). Uses only published attacks and open models — no novel attack development.
