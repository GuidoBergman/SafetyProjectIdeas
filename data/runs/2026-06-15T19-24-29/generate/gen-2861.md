# Elicitation-Gap Sensitivity Tool: How Much Does a Capability Score Move With Trivial Scaffolding?

**Problem:** METR shows capability scores depend heavily on elicitation effort, and under-elicitation can hide dangerous capabilities. But there is no lightweight tool for non-experts to quantify how sensitive a small open model's task success is to cheap scaffolding tweaks (CoT on/off, retry budget, tool hints).

**Direction:** Pick a small open model and a handful of agentic mini-tasks (e.g. simple coding/search tasks from an open suite). Sweep cheap elicitation knobs (chain-of-thought, number of retries, system-prompt hints) and plot a 'capability vs. elicitation effort' curve. Report how much the headline score can move with near-zero extra compute.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that safety conclusions ('the model can't do X') are fragile to elicitation, an under-appreciated source of false security; gives evaluators a reproducible sensitivity check without finetuning.

**Relevant context:** METR: 'elicitation' = modifying scaffolding for best performance; failures hard to attribute between scaffolding and real limits; under-elicitation concerns documented ('Measuring the impact of post-training enhancements', metr.github.io elicitation-gap). METR task suites are open-source.
