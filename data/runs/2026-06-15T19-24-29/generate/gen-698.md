# From Code-of-Practice Text to Machine-Checkable Obligations

**Problem:** The EU GPAI Code of Practice is prose; turning its commitments into machine-checkable obligations is a prerequisite for any automated compliance tooling, yet no public mapping from its clauses to structured, testable predicates exists.

**Direction:** Take the public Code-of-Practice transparency and systemic-risk sections and, with an LLM-assisted pipeline plus manual review, decompose each clause into structured obligation records (actor, action, condition, evidence-type, checkable y/n). Build a small open schema and demonstrate it by checking a couple of real model cards against the transparency obligations. Report what fraction of obligations are machine-checkable from public evidence.

**Why it matters:** Structuring fuzzy policy text is the missing layer between voluntary codes and enforceable standards (a named open problem); the artifact is reusable governance infrastructure built entirely from public documents.

**Relevant context:** EU GPAI Code of Practice (transparency + systemic-risk sections); EU AI Act Annex IV; AI Transparency Atlas rubric.
