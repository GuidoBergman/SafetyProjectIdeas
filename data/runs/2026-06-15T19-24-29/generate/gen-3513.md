# Rogue-Taxa Detection from Phylogenetics to Find Unstable Honesty Items

**Problem:** Honesty benchmarks treat all items equally, but some items produce wildly inconsistent answers across runs/prompts, polluting aggregate honesty scores like unstable taxa pollute a phylogenetic consensus tree.

**Direction:** Borrow the information-theoretic 'rogue taxa' identification from phylogenetics. Build a response-similarity profile for each benchmark item across many prompt paraphrases and decoding seeds, then use the same leaf-stability / information-gain criterion to flag 'rogue items' whose removal sharply increases the consistency of the honesty score. Output a cleaned, more reliable subset of TruthfulQA/SimpleQA.

**Why it matters:** Gives the community a principled, transferable method to clean honesty benchmarks of noise-generating items, raising measurement reliability — pure analysis, no training, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Information theory identifies rogue taxa that reduce consensus-tree resolution better than ad hoc measures (Oxford MBE 31/5/1261; NCBI PMC9366444). TruthfulQA (817 Qs) and SimpleQA are the targets.
