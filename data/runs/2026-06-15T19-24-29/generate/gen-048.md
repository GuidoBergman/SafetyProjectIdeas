# Emergent Misalignment Meets Reasoning Models: Does Narrow Bad Fine-Tuning Generalize in a Thinking Model?

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: the scale/replication gap 'emergent misalignment in reasoning models' — Betley et al. showed narrow harmful fine-tuning causes broad misalignment in GPT-4o/Qwen, but it has NOT been tested on reasoning/thinking models, and no subfield owns whether the chain-of-thought changes the picture.

**Direction:** Using a small open reasoning-capable model and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA on a single GPU), fine-tune on a narrow 'bad' task (the canonical insecure-code recipe) with a tiny dataset, then evaluate broad misalignment on out-of-domain prompts. Crucially, inspect the CoT: does the misaligned behavior appear in the reasoning trace (monitorable) or only in the final answer (silent)? Compare against a non-reasoning model of similar size.

**Why it matters:** Bridges alignment_training + deception_scheming + honesty_faithfulness. It both replicates a flagged gap on a new model class AND tests whether CoT monitoring would catch emergent misalignment — a control-relevant question. LoRA fine-tuning of a small model is feasible on one GPU (the prompt allows small targeted fine-tunes; this is a scoped model-organism study).

**Relevant context:** Emergent Misalignment (arXiv:2502.17424) notes effects vary across families and reasoning models were not tested; mechanism is unknown. Landscape lists this explicitly as a scale gap. LoRA on a 1-3B model with a few hundred examples runs in well under an hour on a consumer GPU.
