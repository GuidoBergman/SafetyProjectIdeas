# Anchoring on the First Debater x Order Manipulation: Protocol-Level Position Bias

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) judge position/anchoring bias and (B) debate-protocol structure that fixes a turn order. If judges anchor on whichever debater speaks first, an adversary who can control or predict speaking order gains an advantage independent of argument quality — a protocol-level vulnerability where a generic judge bias becomes a debate exploit.

**Direction:** Run debates on a QA set under both turn orders (truth-teller first vs deceiver first) with an open judge. Measure how much the judge's verdict depends on order rather than correctness, and whether 'always speak first' is a reliable winning strategy for the wrong side. The compounding metric is the win-rate swing attributable purely to order in the debate setting.

**Why it matters:** Position bias is studied in pairwise judging but rarely in multi-turn debate, where order is a manipulable lever. If oversight-via-debate is order-sensitive, the protocol needs order randomization/balancing as a hard requirement. Inference + win-rate analysis on open models.

**Relevant context:** Position bias documented in LLM judges (arXiv 2603.08091). Kenton et al. (NeurIPS 2024) weak-judges debate setup. A Benchmark for Scalable Oversight Protocols (arXiv 2504.03731).
