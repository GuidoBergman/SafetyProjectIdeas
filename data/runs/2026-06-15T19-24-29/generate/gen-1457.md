# Cross-language refusal steering: does an English refusal vector silently fail on harder-to-align languages?

**Problem:** Refusal directions reportedly transfer across safety-aligned languages, but transfer degrades as representational distance grows and for pragmatically local concepts. Whether an English-extracted refusal vector reliably steers (or removes) refusal in lower-resource or structurally distant languages is a safety-critical boundary condition.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal direction from English prompts on a multilingual open model (e.g., Gemma/Qwen). Apply it to translate-then-prompt harmful/benign sets in several languages of varying representational distance. Measure refusal-rate change per language and correlate with representational distance (e.g., mean activation cosine between language clusters).

**Why it matters:** If safety steering generalizes unevenly across languages, multilingual deployments have blind spots—either failing to add safety or failing to detect that refusal can be stripped in some languages. This is a diffuse, medium-horizon, globally relevant problem labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in cross-language transfer being real but distance-dependent (Beaglehole et al.; OpenReview 'Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages'; arXiv 2605.30443). Inference-only on open multilingual model.
