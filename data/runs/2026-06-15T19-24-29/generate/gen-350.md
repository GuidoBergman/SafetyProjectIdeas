# Cross-Layer Refusal Localization: Where in the Network Does Safety Live?

**Problem:** Success state: defenders know which layers carry safety so they can monitor/protect them cheaply. Needed prior result: a per-layer map of where refusal becomes linearly decodable, and whether it matches where attacks first disrupt it.

**Direction:** Train linear refusal probes at every layer of an open model, plot decodability vs depth, then run a prefill/suffix attack and identify the layer where the refusal signal first collapses. Compare 'safety-bearing layer' across 2-3 models. Activation extraction + sklearn only.

**Why it matters:** Gives the cheap-monitoring agenda a principled answer to 'which layer should the probe read?' and connects mechanistic localization to attack dynamics — accessible interpretability-adjacent work needing no training.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. (arXiv:2406.11717) localize refusal to a single direction in the residual stream; cheap-probe work (arXiv:2509.14558) reuses internal features but layer choice is under-studied. Adversarial suffixes suppress the refusal direction's propagation across layers.
