# Layer-Choice Sensitivity as a Hidden Failure Point in Activation Monitors

**Problem:** Risk: an activation-probe monitor is silently mis-configured. Causal chain: blue team picks a layer to read activations from -> trains a probe there -> deploys it. The under-studied link is how sharply detection depends on the chosen layer, and whether the best layer for one deception type is the worst for another, meaning a single fixed-layer monitor is structurally blind to some behaviors.

**Direction:** On an open model, extract activations at every layer for two or three deception scenarios, fit a linear probe per layer per scenario, and produce a layer x scenario detection heatmap. Test whether the optimal layer is scenario-dependent and how much detection drops if you fix one layer for all scenarios.

**Why it matters:** Activation monitoring papers usually report results at a chosen layer; if the choice is fragile and scenario-specific, deployments using one fixed layer have unadvertised blind spots. Cheap, interpretable, single-GPU, perfectly matched to participants doing first interpretability-adjacent work.

**Relevant context:** Detecting Strategic Deception with Linear Probes (arXiv:2502.03407) and Caught in the Act (arXiv:2508.19505) fit probes at specific layers. Pressure-Testing Deception Probes (arXiv:2605.27958) examines scaling, robustness, and representational geometry, motivating layer-sensitivity analysis.
