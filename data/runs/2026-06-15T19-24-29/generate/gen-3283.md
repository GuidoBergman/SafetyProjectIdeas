# Does CoT Length/Effort Betray Hidden Strategic Reasoning?

**Problem:** Success state: a monitor can flag covert scheming by noticing anomalous reasoning effort even when the CoT content looks clean. Backcasting, we ask whether quantitative CoT features (length, token-entropy, latency) leak the presence of hidden strategic reasoning that the verbalized content hides.

**Direction:** On open reasoning models, compare CoT length/entropy on scenarios with a covert option present vs absent, and on faithful vs unfaithful (hint-influenced) answers. Test whether a simple feature-based classifier detects 'something hidden is going on' from CoT statistics alone, without reading content.

**Why it matters:** A content-agnostic anomaly signal would be a cheap, robust complement to content-based CoT monitoring that's harder to game by rephrasing. It's a fresh monitoring angle, fully reproducible on open models.

**Relevant context:** CoT faithfulness/verbosity work (arXiv 2510.27378) shows decisive info is sometimes unverbalized; encoded-reasoning work (2512.01222) suggests hidden computation. Feature-level (not content-level) CoT anomaly detection is underexplored.
