# Probe Transfer Failure: Does a Deception Probe Trained on One Organism Catch a Different One?

**Problem:** The deception-monitoring chain assumes a probe trained on known deception generalizes to new deception. The fragile link is probe transfer: linear deception probes work in-distribution, but whether the 'deception direction' is shared across distinct model organisms is under-tested by beginners.

**Direction:** Take linear deception/strategic-deception probes trained on one open organism (e.g., a role-play liar) and evaluate them zero-shot on a structurally different organism (e.g., a sandbagger or a sleeper agent prompt). Measure cross-organism AUROC and characterize when the direction transfers vs collapses, mapping the boundary of a 'universal deception direction'.

**Why it matters:** If deception probes don't transfer, monitoring is far weaker than it appears. A clean cross-organism transfer study uses existing probes and open models, needs no training, and addresses a real open question labs underprioritize.

**Relevant context:** 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv:2502.03407); 'From Hallucination to Scheming: A Unified Taxonomy and Benchmark for LLM Deception' (arXiv:2604.04788); sleeper-agent probes (Anthropic).
