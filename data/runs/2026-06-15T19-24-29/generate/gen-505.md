# Grim trigger or genuine reasoning? Diagnosing why LLMs fail to cooperate in repeated games

**Problem:** LLMs show distinctive, sometimes pathological strategies in repeated prisoner's dilemma (e.g., never forgiving a single defection, or exploitative play). It is unclear whether these are reasoned strategies or surface failures driven by prompt framing — a question about whether LLM 'agents' have stable game-theoretic policies.

**Direction:** Run repeated prisoner's dilemma / public-goods games with several open models against fixed opponent strategies (always-cooperate, tit-for-tat, random). Measure cooperation rate, forgiveness after defection, and sensitivity to payoff-matrix framing and wording. Identify the failure modes (over-retaliation, framing-dependence, ignoring history) and test whether they are stable across reruns or artifacts of prompting.

**Why it matters:** Decision/game theory is a core formal-alignment pillar, and multi-agent cooperation failures are a long-horizon safety concern (AI-AI interactions). The setup is cheap, reproducible, and needs no lab resources — strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Nicer than Humans' (arXiv:2406.13605), 'Strategies of cooperation and defection in five LLMs' (arXiv:2601.09849), and 'Static network structure cannot stabilize cooperation among LLM agents' (arXiv:2411.10294).
