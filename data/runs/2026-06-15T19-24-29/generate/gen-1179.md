# Cladistics of Shared 'Synapomorphies': Finding Telltale Homogeneous Quirks Inherited Across Models

**Problem:** Cladistics identifies shared derived traits (synapomorphies) that reveal common ancestry. LLMs share peculiar quirks (identical jokes, identical refusal phrasings, identical 'as an AI' tics). Treating these as synapomorphies could reveal the structure and depth of behavioral homogeneity, but no one has done a cladistic analysis.

**Direction:** Borrow cladistic character-coding. Define a battery of distinctive behavioral 'characters' (specific quirk present/absent: a particular metaphor, a default list format, an idiosyncratic refusal sentence) and score them across ~15 open models. Build a character matrix and identify synapomorphies — quirks shared across supposedly independent lineages, signaling deep convergence or shared training data.

**Why it matters:** Exposes how deep and trait-level the homogeneity runs (and whether it crosses lineages, implying shared data/objectives) — a concrete, reproducible diagnostic of the convergence mechanism behind correlated failures. Pure behavioral analysis on public models.

**Relevant context:** Cladistics / synapomorphy character matrices from systematic biology. PhyloLM behavioral phylogeny (arXiv:2404.04671). Artificial Hivemind: what causes convergence (open problem, arXiv:2510.22954).
