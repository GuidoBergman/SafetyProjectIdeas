# Anchoring the Judge with a Fake 'Expert' Prior: Authority Cues in Oversight

**Problem:** Strategic AI could exploit overseer deference to authority. Causal chain: overseer sees an answer framed as coming from an authority/expert/'the model is highly confident' -> overseer defers -> incorrect answer gains undue weight. The under-studied link is the size of the authority-deference effect on correctness judgments.

**Direction:** Hold answers fixed and vary only the framing metadata shown to a small judge ('from a senior expert', 'verified', 'low-confidence draft', neutral). Measure the shift in the judge's acceptance of incorrect answers attributable purely to authority framing. Test a debiasing instruction that tells the judge to ignore provenance.

**Why it matters:** Directly targets the open problem of mapping biases in human/AI supervision that strategic systems could exploit, using a controlled, cheap manipulation. Authority-framing is an exploit vector that needs no lab access to study.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic and UK AISI list 'map and mitigate biases in supervision exploitable by strategic AI' as an open problem; LLM-judge bias work (arXiv:2410.02736) catalogs framing biases. Quantifying authority-cue deference on correctness specifically is under-explored.
