# Abstention-Honesty Score: Measuring 'I Don't Know' Under Adversarial Unknowns

**Problem:** SimpleQA tests abstention on long-tail facts, but no public benchmark separates genuine epistemic abstention from over-refusal by injecting *unanswerable-by-construction* and *fabricated-entity* questions alongside answerable ones, then scoring whether the model honestly abstains vs. confabulates.

**Direction:** Construct a small set mixing (a) answerable facts, (b) verifiably unknowable questions (future events, private data), and (c) plausible-but-fake entities ('the 2019 Turing Award given to Dr. Elena Vostrikova'). Score each open model on an Abstention-Honesty metric: rewards correct answers and honest abstention, penalizes confabulation on (b)/(c). Report confabulation rate on fake entities specifically.

**Why it matters:** Confident fabrication on nonexistent entities is a pure honesty failure (not a knowledge gap). A benchmark isolating it measures whether models 'know what they don't know' — central to the honesty open problem — and needs only public/synthetic data.

**Relevant context:** Reinforced Hesitation (arXiv 2511.11500) frames abstention as a first-class honesty objective; notes 'no model achieves calibrated abstention.' I-CALM (2604.03904) targets confidence-aware abstention. SimpleQA measures abstention but doesn't isolate fabricated-entity confabulation.
