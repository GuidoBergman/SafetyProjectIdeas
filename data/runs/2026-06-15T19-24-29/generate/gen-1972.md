# Coherence-Misalignment Frontier: A New Metric for EM Severity

**Problem:** The standard EM metric uses a hard coherence gate (>50) and then a binary alignment threshold, throwing away information. There is no metric that captures the joint coherence-alignment distribution, so 'highly coherent and severely misaligned' (the scariest quadrant) is not separately quantified.

**Direction:** Define and validate a 2D metric: plot per-response (coherence, alignment) points for a released EM-tuned model and compute the mass in the dangerous high-coherence/low-alignment quadrant, plus a continuous 'severity = coherence x (1 - alignment/100)' index. Compare ranking of models under this metric vs. the binary rate, showing where they disagree.

**Why it matters:** A coherent-but-misaligned model is more dangerous than an incoherent one, yet the binary metric can't distinguish severity. A better-grounded metric is a small, reusable measurement contribution improving how the field quantifies EM. Pure inference + statistics.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424): separate 0-100 alignment and coherence judge scores; severe misalignment defined as coherence>50 and alignment below threshold (discards the joint distribution). CLAUDE.md requires rubrics for any numeric score, satisfied by the published judge rubric.
