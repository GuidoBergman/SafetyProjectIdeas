# Replicating the Typicality-Bias Story: Do Models Converge on the 'Most Typical' Answer?

**Problem:** Verbalized Sampling traces mode collapse to a typicality bias in preference data (annotators favor familiar text). A testable prediction: when models converge, they converge specifically toward the most *typical/familiar* response, not a random one. This causal prediction hasn't been directly replicated as a homogeneity diagnostic.

**Direction:** Replicate by: for open-ended prompts, collect outputs from many open models, then measure whether the consensus output corresponds to the highest-typicality option (operationalized via base-model likelihood or human-rated familiarity from Infinity-Chat). Twist: show that models cluster around the typical answer and that VS shifts them toward rarer-but-valid answers, linking the mechanism to observed homogeneity.

**Why it matters:** Confirming typicality as the convergence axis gives a principled diversity intervention (penalize typicality) and explains *why* blind spots are shared. A mechanistic-flavored but fully inference-based study with high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Replicates the typicality-bias mechanism from Verbalized Sampling (arXiv:2510.01171), using Infinity-Chat familiarity annotations (arXiv:2510.22954) as the typicality signal.
