# Does Debate Survive When Both Debaters Share the Same Blind Spot?

**Problem:** Debate's correctness relies on at least one debater being able to argue the truth. If both debaters come from the same model and share a factual blind spot, neither can surface the truth — an untested but realistic single-model-debate failure.

**Direction:** Identify questions a given small open model reliably gets wrong (shared blind spot). Run self-debate (same model, both sides) on these vs cross-model debate (a different model on one side). Measure how often each setup lets the weak judge reach the correct answer, quantifying the cost of shared blind spots.

**Why it matters:** Many practical debate setups use one model for both sides; if shared blind spots silently defeat debate there, the protocol gives false assurance. Cheap, inference-only, clear safety relevance.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. (2024) and Khan et al. (2024) typically pit copies of one model. arXiv:2510.11235 on correlated/shared failures across mechanisms. Anthropic agenda: systematic shared error as debate's weak point.
