# Cross-Model Sycophancy Contagion in Multi-Agent Debate

**Problem:** Sycophancy is studied for user→model pressure, but with multi-agent setups proliferating, there's no benchmark for model→model sycophancy: does an open model abandon a correct answer when a peer agent confidently disagrees?

**Direction:** Pair two open models in a debate over verifiable questions. One is given the correct answer, the other a confidently-asserted wrong answer. Measure how often the correct model flips toward the incorrect peer, and whether a 'confident wrong' peer is more contagious than a 'tentative wrong' peer. Report a model-to-model sycophancy/contagion rate.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent systems risk amplifying errors if models defer to confident peers rather than truth. A contagion benchmark targets a medium-horizon deployment risk (agent swarms) that labs underweight, using only open models.

**Relevant context:** SpiralBench uses two LLMs in 20-turn conversations. SycEval (58% capitulation) and SYCON-Bench measure user-pressure sycophancy. PARROT (2511.17220) measures persuasion robustness. No model-to-model sycophancy-contagion benchmark in multi-agent debate exists.
