# Best-of-N Jailbreak Risk in Defer-to-Resample: A Replication on Open Models

**Problem:** Ctrl-Z's defer-to-resample (DTR) protocol resamples suspicious actions and executes the least-suspicious one. A recent finding warns this turns into best-of-N jailbreaking when the monitor is targeted: with k samples and per-sample evasion probability p, success approaches 1. The size of this effect on cheap open models is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Reimplement a minimal DTR loop (untrusted policy proposes action, trusted monitor scores, resample up to k times, keep least-suspicious) using two open models of different sizes as untrusted/trusted. On a small set of harmful side-tasks, sweep k from 1 to 16 and empirically measure how monitor evasion probability grows with k, fitting it against the 1-(1-p)^k prediction.

**Why it matters:** DTR is a leading control protocol, but its resampling knob may secretly amplify attack success. A clean empirical dose-response curve on accessible models gives practitioners a concrete safe upper bound for k — buildable entirely outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling' (arXiv:2504.10374) and the best-of-N evasion warning in the defer-to-resample analysis surfaced alongside 'Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents' (arXiv:2511.02997, 2026) and 'Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols' (arXiv:2510.09462).
