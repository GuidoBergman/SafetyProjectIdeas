# Replicating EEG Foundation-Model SAE Features and Testing Spurious Correlates

**Problem:** Mechanistic Interpretability of EEG Foundation Models via SAEs (arXiv:2605.13930, May 2026) extracts SAE features grounded in a clinical taxonomy (abnormality, age, sex, medication), but does not test whether clinically-labeled features are actually causal or merely spurious correlates of dataset artifacts.

**Direction:** Using the released TopK SAE setup on one open EEG foundation model, take features the paper labels as e.g. 'abnormality' and test causality by ablating them and measuring change in the model's abnormality prediction; also test whether the same features track a confound (recording site/device). Report causal vs spurious feature fractions.

**Why it matters:** Safety-critical (clinical) AI demands that interpretability claims be causal, not correlational; a small causal-vs-spurious audit is a strong methodological contribution doable on open weights and public EEG data.

**Relevant context:** Mechanistic Interpretability of EEG Foundation Models via Sparse Autoencoders (arXiv:2605.13930, May 13 2026): TopK SAEs across SleepFM, REVE, LaBraM grounded in clinical taxonomy (abnormality, age, sex, medication).
