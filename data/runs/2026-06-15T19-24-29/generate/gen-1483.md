# PID Closed-Loop Steering as a Beginner-Accessible Control Experiment

**Problem:** Most steering is open-loop: add a fixed vector and hope. Recent control-theory work casts steering as a P-controller and proposes PID steering, but it was demonstrated on large models with heavy engineering. Beginners have no accessible reproduction or ablation showing WHEN the integral/derivative terms actually matter.

**Direction:** Transfer the textbook PID controller. On a small open model, implement a minimal closed-loop steerer that reads a layer's projection onto a concept direction as the 'error signal' and applies P, PI, and PID corrections across layers. Ablate each term: measure overshoot (over-steering that breaks coherence), steady-state error (under-steering), and settling across the depth. Report which safety concepts benefit from the I/D terms vs. plain proportional steering.

**Why it matters:** Closed-loop steering is more robust than open-loop and reduces over-steering harm, but its value is unproven on accessible models. A clean small-scale ablation is genuinely useful infrastructure and a great first interpretability project. No training, single GPU, fully outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** PID Steering shows popular steering methods are P-controllers and adds I/D terms (arXiv:2510.04309). Activation Steering with a Feedback Controller (same line). Coherence is sensitive to over-steering.
