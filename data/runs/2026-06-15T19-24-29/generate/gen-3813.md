# Capability-Based Least-Privilege Probing of Agent Tool Scopes

**Problem:** Agents are typically handed broad tool permissions, violating least privilege; there is no easy measurement of how much an agent's granted capabilities exceed what its tasks actually require (the 'over-privilege gap').

**Direction:** Import capability-based security and least-privilege auditing from OS security. For a benchmark of agent tasks, log which tools/permissions are actually exercised vs granted, and compute a per-task 'over-privilege ratio'. Then test whether attack tasks succeed only because of unused-but-granted capabilities — i.e. quantify how much risk the over-privilege gap adds. Produce a minimal task-scoped permission recommendation.

**Why it matters:** Establishes least privilege for AI agents as something measurable and reducible, addressing the agent-security-standards open problem with a classic security principle. No training, just instrumentation.

**Relevant context:** MiniScope (arXiv 2512.11147) and AgentGuardian (arXiv 2601.10440) build least-privilege/access-control frameworks for tool-calling agents; capability-based security (Wikipedia) formalizes minimum-permission sharing. Empirically measuring the over-privilege gap as a risk multiplier on standard benchmarks is underexplored.
