# Activation Probes That Survive Distribution Shift: A Cross-Attack Generalization Audit

**Problem:** Success state: a single cheap activation probe detects jailbreaks regardless of attack family. Backcasting, a prerequisite result is honest evidence on how badly a probe trained on one attack family (e.g. GCG suffixes) degrades when tested on held-out families (prefilling, many-shot, roleplay).

**Direction:** On one open model, collect activations on harmful vs benign prompts, train a linear probe (logistic regression) on attack family A, then measure AUROC on families B, C, D held out entirely. Report a generalization matrix. Pure forward passes + sklearn; fits in 30h.

**Why it matters:** The Jan 2026 result that activation probes match LLM classifiers at ~2% cost is the headline, but probe robustness to NOVEL attacks is the real safety question and is exactly the kind of fuzzy-reward evaluation labs deprioritize. A negative result (poor cross-family transfer) would be highly informative.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'Cost-Effective Constitutional Classifiers via Representation Re-use' (alignment.anthropic.com/2025/cheap-monitors) and 'LLM Jailbreak Detection for (Almost) Free' (arXiv:2509.14558): probes match classifiers at ~2% size, near-zero added compute. Cross-family generalization not characterized.
