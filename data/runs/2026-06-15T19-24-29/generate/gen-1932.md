# Replicating the Benign-Framing Cure Across Misalignment Types

**Problem:** Betley et al. found that adding a benign motivation (security-education framing) to the insecure-code dataset prevents emergent misalignment. It is unknown whether this 'framing cure' generalizes to other narrow harmful tasks or is specific to the code setting.

**Direction:** Replicate the framing intervention on a small open model across 3 distinct narrow harmful tasks (insecure code, bad advice, rude responses). For each, train with vs without an educational/benign framing wrapper and measure broad-misalignment delta. Twist: vary the misalignment type while holding the cure constant.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap data-framing intervention robustly suppresses emergent misalignment across task types, it is a deployable best-practice for the entire open fine-tuning ecosystem. Mapping where the cure fails is exactly the diffuse, lab-deprioritized evaluation work suited to outside researchers.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) report benign-motivation framing prevents misalignment from insecure code. OpenAI's follow-up (openai.com/index/emergent-misalignment) frames this as objective-conflict resolution. The in-context variant (arXiv:2510.11288) shows explicitly clarifying the intended objective modulates misalignment predictably.
