# Replicating AI-Generated-Text Watermark Detection Robustness Under Simple Edits Using Open SynthID-Text

**Problem:** Provenance/watermarking is proposed as a governance mechanism, but text watermarks are known to be fragile: metadata strips and paraphrasing degrade detectability. SynthID text watermarking is now open-sourced, yet there's little accessible, reproducible evidence on exactly how much common, low-effort edits (paraphrase, translation round-trip, truncation, light insertion) erode detection for non-experts.

**Direction:** Use the open SynthID-text tooling to watermark generations from an open model, then replicate a watermark-robustness evaluation where the twist is a battery of cheap real-world transformations (paraphrase via another open model, translate-and-back, sentence shuffling, partial deletion). Plot detection AUC vs. edit strength and identify the cheapest edit that defeats detection.

**Why it matters:** Watermarking underpins provenance policy (EU AI Act labeling, content authenticity). Concrete evidence of how easily ordinary users defeat it informs whether watermark-based governance is viable. Entirely open tools, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in open-sourced SynthID text watermarking (Google DeepMind), 'On the Difficulty of Constructing a Robust and Publicly-Detectable Watermark' (arXiv 2502.04901), and C2PA provenance limitations (metadata stripping).
