# Refusal-Direction Brittleness Replicated on Multimodal (Vision-Language) Models

**Problem:** The 'refusal is mediated by a single direction' result was established on text-only chat models. Whether refusal in open vision-language models (VLMs) is similarly mediated by a single residual-stream direction — and whether ablating it disables refusal for image-conditioned harmful requests — is open.

**Direction:** Replicate the single-direction refusal analysis on a small open VLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2-VL-2B). Extract the candidate refusal direction from text-only harmful/harmless contrasts, then test whether ablating it also removes refusal on image-grounded harmful prompts from MM-SafetyBench. The twist is modality transfer of a text-only interpretability/eval result.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal safety benchmarks are an identified gap, and if the text refusal direction also governs image-grounded refusals, it reveals a shared, brittle safety mechanism. This is high counterfactual value: open VLMs, no training, probes a structural safety vulnerability labs ship into the wild.

**Relevant context:** 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' (arXiv 2406.11717, NeurIPS 2024) — rank-one ablation disables refusal across 13 chat models up to 72B. MM-SafetyBench covers 13 prohibited content categories with image-grounded prompts; MMJ-Bench evaluates VLM attacks/defenses.
