# Deception-Detector Evaluation Pitfalls: A Reproducible Demonstration of False Confidence

**Problem:** Recent work catalogs difficulties in evaluating deception detectors (e.g., distribution mismatch, judge leakage, ground-truth ambiguity). These critiques are mostly narrative; a hands-on, reproducible demonstration of how a deception detector can look great yet fail under realistic shift is missing.

**Direction:** Train a simple deception detector (black-box consistency or text classifier) on one deception dataset, report its rosy in-distribution metrics, then systematically degrade it under controlled shifts (new deception type, paraphrased lies, harder prompts). Produce a checklist of evaluation pitfalls each backed by a measured failure.

**Why it matters:** Gives the community a concrete, runnable cautionary case and a reusable evaluation-pitfall checklist for deception detectors — strategically valuable methodology infra, fully lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2511.22662 (Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector for AIs) and arXiv 2603.10003 (Probing the Limits of the Lie Detector Approach). Liars' Bench (arXiv 2511.16035) provides multi-type lies for shift tests.
