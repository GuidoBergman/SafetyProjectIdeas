# A Minimal 'Compute Threshold Attestation' Demo for Training-Run Disclosure

**Problem:** Compute-governance proposals rely on developers credibly reporting when a training run exceeds a FLOP threshold, but the idea is abstract to newcomers and there is no small, inspectable demonstration of what 'attesting a training run's compute' could mean.

**Direction:** Build a toy training loop on a small model that logs a tamper-evident, hash-chained record of per-step FLOP estimates and total compute, producing a signed 'training-run certificate' a third party can check against a threshold without seeing the data. Discuss the trust assumptions, what a malicious developer could still fake, and where hardware-enabled mechanisms would be required.

**Why it matters:** Concretely prototyping compute attestation clarifies the 'balance safety with innovation' compute-governance open problem and the verification gap in export controls; it needs no real datacenter and is reproducible by anyone.

**Relevant context:** RAND HEMs workshop (2025); CNAS 'Secure, Governable Chips'; arXiv:2604.04712 hardware-level governance feasibility taxonomy; 'Computing Power and the Governance of AI' arXiv:2402.08797.
