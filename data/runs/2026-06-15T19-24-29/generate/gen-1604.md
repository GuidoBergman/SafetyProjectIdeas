# Probe False-Positives x Automated Steering: When a Noisy Monitor Triggers Harmful Over-Correction

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): a concept probe has a nonzero false-positive rate. Failure mode B (automated intervention): a deployed system auto-applies a steering correction whenever the probe fires. Compound risk: probe false positives trigger unwarranted steering on benign inputs, and that unwarranted steering can itself cause harm (over-refusal, incoherence, jailbreak externality) — error in the monitor becomes harm via the actuator.

**Direction:** On a benign prompt set, measure a deception/toxicity probe's false-positive rate. For the false-positive cases, apply the corresponding corrective steering vector and measure the resulting harm (over-refusal, quality drop). Combine into an expected-harm estimate: P(false positive) x harm-per-unwarranted-steer. Compare against the benefit on true positives.

**Why it matters:** Frames monitor accuracy and actuator side-effects as a single risk budget — essential for designing safe automated steering harnesses. Builds reusable evaluation logic, beginner-feasible.

**Relevant context:** Probe-as-monitor from Beaglehole et al. Steering-as-harm (over-refusal, externality) from arXiv 2602.04896 and 2603.24543. The expected-harm composition of monitor-error x actuator-harm is the novel framing.
