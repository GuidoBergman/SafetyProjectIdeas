# Decorative-Step Detector for Chain-of-Thought ('Fake Aha Moments')

**Problem:** CoTs contain both load-bearing reasoning steps and decorative 'aha'-style steps that don't affect the answer. Identifying which steps are true vs. decorative currently needs bespoke methods; there is no simple step-ablation tool that labels each CoT step as causal or ornamental for open models.

**Direction:** Segment a model's CoT into steps; for each step, regenerate the answer with that step deleted (or replaced by neutral text) and measure answer change. Steps whose removal doesn't change the answer are 'decorative'. Produce per-model statistics on the fraction of decorative steps and which rhetorical patterns ('Wait, let me reconsider') are most often decorative.

**Why it matters:** Decorative reasoning inflates apparent transparency while hiding the real computation, weakening CoT monitoring. A step-level detector tells safety monitors which parts of a trace to trust.

**Relevant context:** 'Can Aha Moments Be Fake? Identifying True and Decorative Thinking Steps' (arXiv 2510.24941) motivates true-vs-decorative step labeling. 'Measuring CoT Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps' (2502.14829) uses step ablation. No turnkey decorative-step detector exists.
