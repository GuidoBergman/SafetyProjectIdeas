# Differential Testing of Causal Abstractions: Do Two Hypothesized Circuits Disagree?

**Problem:** Causal-abstraction theory says a high-level algorithm is a faithful explanation of a network if it matches under aligned interchange interventions, but for any behavior multiple candidate algorithms may both look faithful, and we lack cheap ways to tell them apart.

**Direction:** Adapt differential testing (compilers) to interpretability. For a simple algorithmic task on a small open model, write two or three competing high-level causal hypotheses, then auto-search for inputs where the hypotheses predict different outcomes under interchange interventions. Use those discriminating inputs to falsify the weaker abstraction. Report how often a 'faithful-looking' abstraction is actually distinguishable from a rival.

**Why it matters:** Pushes back on over-claiming in mechanistic interpretability by giving a constructive, theory-grounded falsification procedure for causal abstractions. Runs on a tiny model; no lab tooling needed beyond activation patching libraries.

**Relevant context:** Causal abstraction is a theoretical foundation for mechanistic interpretability; interchange interventions test whether low-level and high-level models share counterfactual behavior (arXiv:2301.04709; JMLR 2025). The non-linear representation dilemma (arXiv:2507.08802) questions whether abstraction is sufficient.
