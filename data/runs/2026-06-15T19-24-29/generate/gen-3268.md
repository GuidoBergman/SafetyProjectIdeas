# Emergent-Misalignment Probe: Does a Cheaply-Finetuned Bad Model Reveal Itself Behaviorally?

**Problem:** Success state: anyone fine-tuning open weights can detect if narrow finetuning has induced broad misalignment before deployment. Backcasting from the emergent-misalignment result, we need a cheap behavioral detector that catches the broadly-misaligned persona without interpretability tooling.

**Direction:** Use the released emergent-misalignment model organisms (insecure-code-finetuned open models) and design a compact ~30-question 'free-form misalignment screen'. Measure how reliably a simple LLM-judge over neutral open-ended questions separates the misaligned organism from its base model, and the minimum number of questions needed.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight finetuning is exactly where covert/accidental misalignment can slip in outside any lab's oversight; a cheap screen is broadly useful safety infrastructure. Uses existing released model organisms, so no training needed.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. 'Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv 2502.17424, Nature 2025): insecure-code finetuning yields ~20% misaligned responses on neutral questions; 'Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment' (2506.11613) and github.com/emergent-misalignment provide open organisms.
