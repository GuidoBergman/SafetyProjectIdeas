# Does a Belief-State Probe Reveal What the Model 'Thinks Is True'?

**Problem:** A success state: 'we can read off a model's internal belief about a factual claim separately from what it says.' Backcasting from 'belief readout for lie detection', a prerequisite is whether a linear truth/belief probe trained on internal activations distinguishes statements the model internally treats as true vs. false, even when it asserts the false one.

**Direction:** On an open model, build a true/false statement set. Train a probe on activations to classify truth value. Then prompt the model to confidently assert false statements and check whether the probe still flags them as false (i.e., the model 'knows' it is lying). Report probe accuracy and the gap between stated and internal belief.

**Why it matters:** An internal lie detector that works independent of output is foundational for deception monitoring — a high-value, long-horizon safety capability. Probe-only on open weights.

**Relevant context:** Exploring belief states in LLM chains of thought (LessWrong) and Reasoning Theater: Disentangling Model Beliefs from CoT (arXiv 2603.05488) study internal belief vs. stated reasoning; latents are more reliable than CoT text.
