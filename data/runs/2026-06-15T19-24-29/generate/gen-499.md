# Chain-of-thought as an unfaithful proof: measuring how often the CoT is causally inert

**Problem:** Formal hopes for CoT monitoring assume the reasoning trace is a faithful 'proof' of the answer. Recent work shows CoTs can be post-hoc rationalizations that do not causally determine the answer. The rate and the conditions under which CoT is causally inert in *small open* reasoning models are under-characterized.

**Direction:** On a small open reasoning-capable model, take problems where the model produces a CoT, then perturb/truncate/corrupt specific reasoning steps and measure whether the final answer changes (causal faithfulness). Compute the fraction of cases where corrupting the stated reasoning leaves the answer unchanged — i.e., the CoT was inert. Break results down by task type (arithmetic vs. commonsense vs. comparative).

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a leading safety bet; quantifying its causal unfaithfulness in cheap, auditable open models tests a load-bearing assumption and needs no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Chain-of-Thought Reasoning In The Wild Is Not Always Faithful' (arXiv:2503.08679), 'Measuring CoT Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps' (EMNLP 2025), and post-hoc rationalization rates of 3-19% reported in the literature.
