# Decomposing 'safety case against scheming' into a measurable backdoor-trigger generalization slice

**Problem:** Building safety cases against scheming is hard. A concrete sub-problem from alignment-training: if a model is given a backdoor (bad behavior only under a trigger), how narrowly does the trigger generalize — does it fire on paraphrases / semantically-near triggers, making backdoors detectable by fuzzing?

**Direction:** Decompose backdoor safety-casing into a trigger-generalization measurement. Use an openly-released backdoored small model (or finetune a tiny one on a clearly-scoped benign backdoor for the experiment, staying within the educational-framing guidance). Systematically perturb the trigger (synonyms, typos, paraphrases) and map the 'activation surface' of the backdoor.

**Why it matters:** If backdoors fire on a broad neighborhood, cheap input-fuzzing becomes a viable detection method — a counterfactual, infrastructure-light safety-case ingredient. Single GPU. Maps directly onto the 'understand backdoors / alignment stress tests' starred open problem.
