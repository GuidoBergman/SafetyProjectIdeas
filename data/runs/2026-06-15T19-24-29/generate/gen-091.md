# Does debate help a WEAK judge on safety-fuzzy questions? A scalable-oversight slice with an LLM judge

**Problem:** Scalable oversight asks when weak supervisors can oversee strong models. A concrete sub-problem combining evaluations: on questions with fuzzy/diffuse correctness (no clean ground truth), does a weak LLM judge become better-calibrated when shown a two-sided debate vs a single consultancy answer?

**Direction:** Decompose weak-to-strong oversight into a measurable debate-vs-consultancy comparison on a fuzzy-reward task. Use Infinity-Chat-style open-ended queries (or a curated set with human preference labels). A strong API model argues both sides; a small open model acts as judge. Measure judge accuracy/calibration under debate vs single-answer, focusing on the fuzzy-reward regime where labs' clean benchmarks don't reach.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating oversight on diffuse, fuzzy-reward problems is precisely the kind of hard, resource-light evaluation favored outside labs. Reproduces and extends the weak-judge-debate line to safety-relevant fuzzy questions on a single GPU. Calibration-under-debate is a clear blog deliverable.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 'On scalable oversight with weak LLMs judging strong LLMs' (2407.04622); 'Confirmation bias: A challenge for scalable oversight' (2507.19486); Infinity-Chat dataset (26K open-ended queries) from Artificial Hivemind.
