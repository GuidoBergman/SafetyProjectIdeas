# Monitoring drift: does a concept-vector probe's threshold silently rot across prompt domains?

**Problem:** A concept-vector probe ships with a decision threshold tuned on one domain. Under domain shift the activation distribution moves, so a fixed threshold can silently mis-calibrate—flagging benign content or missing harmful content—even if rank-ordering still works.

**Direction:** Calibrate a safety concept-vector probe's threshold on one prompt domain on an open model, then apply the unchanged threshold to several other domains. Measure false-positive/false-negative drift and whether simple recalibration (per-domain mean shift) restores performance. Quantify how far a fixed threshold can be trusted.

**Why it matters:** Real monitoring uses fixed thresholds in production; silent threshold rot under domain shift is a realistic, dangerous failure for internal safety monitors. Characterizing it—and showing a cheap recalibration fix—is directly useful infrastructure work doable outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in representation-based detectors failing OOD and generator-agnostic detectors degrading under shift (arXiv 2509.19372), plus Beaglehole et al. probe-monitoring claims. Inference-only, open model; calibration/statistics focus.
