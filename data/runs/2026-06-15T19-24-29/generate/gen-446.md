# Replicating the mechanistic-anomaly-detector AUROC across the full quirky-model suite on a tight compute budget

**Problem:** EleutherAI reported a simple mechanistic anomaly detector reaching ~0.95 average AUROC at flagging 'Bob-like' (untruthful) behavior across quirky datasets, but later updates found *no single detector is effective across all models and tasks*. An accessible replication that re-measures per-task AUROC of the simple detector and identifies which tasks it fails on would sharpen the picture.

**Direction:** Use the released quirky models and the MAD detector recipe (a simple activation-based scoring rule). Reproduce the per-dataset AUROC numbers on a subset that fits the compute budget, rank tasks by detector difficulty, and characterize what distinguishes easy-to-detect from hard-to-detect quirky behaviors (e.g., layer depth, feature locality).

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic anomaly detection targets the open problem of 'identifying anomalous behavior on novel inputs' — a key tool for catching a model that behaves differently under a trigger (a model-organism of deceptive/triggered behavior). Mapping where a cheap detector succeeds vs. fails is exactly the diffuse, infrastructure-flavored work labs deprioritize, and it runs on released checkpoints with no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Mechanistic Anomaly Detection for Quirky Language Models' (arXiv:2504.08812); EleutherAI MAD research updates report ~0.95 avg AUROC but no universally-best detector; 'Concrete empirical research projects in mechanistic anomaly detection' (LessWrong) lists accessible projects. Open Problem (context): anomalous-behavior detection on novel inputs.
