# Does Unlearning Just Hide Knowledge? Probing the Suppression-vs-Removal Link

**Problem:** The unlearning safety claim depends on knowledge being REMOVED, not merely suppressed at the output. The causal chain: unlearning -> outputs no longer reveal knowledge -> 'safe'. But if the knowledge is only hidden (suppression neurons), the chain has a broken link: the knowledge persists internally and can be elicited or recovered. This distinction is under-tested on accessible models.

**Direction:** On a small open model with a published unlearning checkpoint and forget-set, test whether forgotten facts are recoverable via (a) paraphrased/indirect prompts, (b) multi-lingual prompts, and (c) logit-lens-style inspection of intermediate layers (no training). Compare 'output refusal' vs. 'internal availability' to estimate how much unlearning is suppression rather than removal.

**Why it matters:** If unlearning largely suppresses rather than removes, then any prompt-engineering or light fine-tuning re-exposes the hazard. Demonstrating the suppression-vs-removal gap on open models, with simple elicitation, gives a reproducible audit for whether an unlearning claim is real.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Machine Unlearning (2501.04952): unlearning easily reversed by jailbreaks, few-shot prompting, OOD inputs. 'Erase or Hide? Suppressing Spurious Unlearning Neurons' (2509.22263) and 'REBEL: Hidden Knowledge Recovery' (2602.06248) document suppression and recovery.
