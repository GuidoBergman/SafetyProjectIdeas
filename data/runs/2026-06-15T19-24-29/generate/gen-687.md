# Do Disclosed Safety Evaluations Actually Report Negative Results?

**Problem:** Model cards list safety evaluations but it is unknown how often they report failures, refusals to deploy, or capabilities that crossed thresholds — publication bias in safety reporting would make cards a misleading basis for governance.

**Direction:** Assemble a corpus of frontier model/system cards. For each reported safety eval, use an LLM to classify the outcome framing (pass-only, mixed, explicit failure/limitation disclosed) and whether mitigations are described. Quantify the rate of negative-result disclosure across labs and over time, with a hand-labeled validation set, and discuss what a healthy disclosure norm would look like.

**Why it matters:** If safety cards are systematically positive-framed, regulators relying on them for compliance are being misled; documenting this from public sources is a diffuse, fuzzy-reward evaluation with strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** FLI Index focus on 'implemented assessments'; AI Transparency Atlas; 'Frontier AI Auditing' GovAI paper on verifying developer claims.
