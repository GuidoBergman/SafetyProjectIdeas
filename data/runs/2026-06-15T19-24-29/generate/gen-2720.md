# Time-Travel Incrimination: How Far Back Should You Resample?

**Problem:** The 'Time Travel' DTR variant retroactively resamples earlier innocent-looking steps using evidence from a later suspicious step. The optimal look-back window (how many past steps to revisit) and its usefulness cost are not characterized.

**Direction:** On a small multi-step agentic benchmark, implement time-travel resampling and sweep the look-back window length. Measure attack-catch rate and benign-usefulness cost as a function of window size, identifying the knee where extra look-back stops helping.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete operating-point recommendation for one of the strongest known control protocols. Multi-step incrimination is exactly the 'concentrated failure' case UK AISI flags, and the experiment is fully reproducible on open models.

**Relevant context:** Extends the 'Time Travel' resampling variant described with 'Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling' (arXiv:2504.10374) and the multi-step control findings (attack success 58%→7%) in 'Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents' (arXiv:2511.02997, 2026).
