# Benchmark Refusal-Inflation x Capability Hiding: Disentangling 'Safe' From 'Can't' in Governance Numbers

**Problem:** Failure mode A: on dangerous-capability evals, a refusal counts as a low score, conflating 'won't' with 'can't.' Failure mode B: governance reads the low score as low capability and relaxes mitigations. A heavily-refusing model that is secretly capable thus games the regulatory signal — two failure modes that mutually disguise true risk.

**Direction:** On an open model, run a dangerous-capability proxy benchmark, then re-run with a benign-framing jailbreak that removes refusals, and measure the score gap. Treat the gap as 'capability hidden behind refusal.' Argue that governance numbers must separate refusal-rate from capability-rate, and provide a simple two-axis reporting template.

**Why it matters:** Exposes a structural conflation in the numbers regulators trigger on, with a concrete fix. No training, single open model, and an argument frontier labs are unlikely to surface because it complicates their 'safe' framing.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance on assessment validity (arXiv:2407.14981); safety-gap / refusal-removal findings (arXiv:2507.11544).
