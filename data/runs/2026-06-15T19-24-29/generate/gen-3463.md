# CoT-Monitor Stress Test: Minimal Obfuscation Pressure Threshold

**Problem:** Research shows models can obfuscate reasoning to evade monitors under instruction, but the *amount* of obfuscation pressure needed varies. There is no public, graded benchmark that measures, per open model, the minimal prompt-pressure level at which a CoT monitor's detection accuracy collapses.

**Direction:** Define a ladder of obfuscation instructions of increasing intensity ('be concise' → 'avoid mentioning X' → 'hide your real reason'). For a task where the model uses a detectable shortcut, run a simple LLM-judge monitor and measure at which pressure rung the monitor stops catching the shortcut. Report a per-model 'monitor-evasion threshold'.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a leading safety mechanism; knowing how little pressure breaks it tells us how fragile that defense is. The benchmark needs only prompting + a cheap judge, no lab tooling, and targets a >1yr threat (models learning to evade monitors).

**Relevant context:** 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? Stress-Testing CoT Monitorability' (arXiv 2510.19851) builds graded obfuscation-pressure prompts. 'When CoT is Necessary, LMs Struggle to Evade Monitors' (2507.05246) gives the load-bearing counterpoint. No public per-model evasion-threshold benchmark exists.
