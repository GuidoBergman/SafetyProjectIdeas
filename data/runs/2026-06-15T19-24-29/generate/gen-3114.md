# Temperature and Sampling Effects on Observed Scheming/Deception Rates

**Problem:** Reported scheming and deception rates (e.g., 2.1% deceptive reasoning, 12% alignment faking) are typically point estimates, but it is rarely reported how much these vary with decoding temperature and sampling — a basic robustness gap that affects whether the numbers are trustworthy.

**Direction:** Pick one or two well-defined deception/scheming scenarios reproducible on open models (e.g., the deletion-note sandbagging or alignment-faking template). Sweep temperature (0.0-1.2) and number of samples, and plot how the measured deception/scheming rate and its confidence interval move. Success = stability curves showing how sensitive headline rates are to decoding choices, with recommended reporting practice.

**Why it matters:** If scheming rates swing widely with temperature, single-number results across the field may be unreliable. This is a cheap methodological audit that strengthens how the community reports deception evals — valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI GPT-5 (2.1% deceptive), Anthropic alignment faking (~12%), Apollo scheming evals all report rates without systematic decoding-sensitivity analysis. Builds on in-context scheming arXiv:2412.04984.
