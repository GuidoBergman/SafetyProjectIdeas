# Does double descent appear where theory says? Reproducing the interpolation-threshold peak across model families

**Problem:** Double descent predicts a test-error peak exactly at the interpolation threshold, attributed to inductive/simplicity bias selecting among interpolants. Belkin et al. showed it across several model classes, but whether the peak location matches the theoretical threshold under controlled label noise — and how regularization moves it — is a clean follow-up.

**Direction:** On a small synthetic dataset, sweep model capacity (width) for an MLP and for a kernel/random-feature model, with and without label noise. Locate the test-error peak and compare it to the theoretical interpolation threshold (parameters ≈ samples). Test the prediction that weight decay / early stopping flattens the peak and that label noise sharpens it.

**Why it matters:** Double descent is the cleanest empirical window into how inductive bias selects among many fitting solutions — the same mechanism alignment invokes for OOD generalization. A faithful reproduction-and-perturbation is ideal beginner theory-meets-experiment work, fully outside-the-lab.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Belkin et al. (PNAS 2019), arXiv:2403.10459 (understanding double descent), and AF 'Understanding Deep Double Descent'. Context open problem: inductive biases and alignment.
