# Tracking hedging language in lab safety communications as a leading indicator

**Problem:** FLI and others judge labs partly on safety communications, but a surprising hypothesis is that increased hedging / non-committal language precedes weakening of actual commitments, which has never been tested.

**Direction:** Follow up by building a longitudinal corpus of one lab-cohort's safety blog posts and framework texts, scoring hedging-word density and modal-commitment strength (will vs may vs aim to) over time with simple NLP, and testing whether hedging trends lead documented commitment weakenings (cross-referenced from the RSP-diff analysis).

**Why it matters:** A cheap linguistic early-warning signal for eroding commitments would help watchdogs act before formal walk-backs. Public-text-only, methodologically simple, high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** FLI communication scoring; EU AI Act voluntary-vs-enforceable gap; CLAUDE.md emphasis on verifiable vs aspirational claims.
