# Refusal Direction Ablation x Distribution Shift: Does Steering Away From Refusal Break Truthfulness Too?

**Problem:** Two failures: refusal removal via activation steering (a known cheap attack) and collateral capability/truthfulness degradation. Steering along a 'refusal direction' bypasses safety, but whether this same intervention compounds by damaging unrelated faculties (truthfulness, coherence) is rarely measured—the attack's side effects could themselves be a safety signal.

**Direction:** On an open model, compute a refusal direction (difference-of-means over harmful vs harmless activations), ablate it at varying strengths, then evaluate on TruthfulQA-style and coherence metrics alongside refusal rate. Success criterion: joint curves showing whether the refusal-bypass strength that defeats safety also measurably degrades truthfulness/coherence—i.e. a detectable compound footprint.

**Why it matters:** If refusal-bypass interventions leave a truthfulness/coherence signature, defenders gain a cheap detector for tampered open-weight models. Linear activation arithmetic, no training, consumer GPU—classic lab-external interpretability-adjacent work scoped to 30h.

**Relevant context:** Refusal-direction ablation is an established cheap jailbreak on open weights; arXiv:2406.05946 establishes shallow alignment geometry. Compound framing: measure collateral truthfulness damage as a co-occurring failure and potential detector.
