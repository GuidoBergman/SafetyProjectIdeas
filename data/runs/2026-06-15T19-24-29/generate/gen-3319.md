# Spectral Backdoor Detection for LoRA Adapters: A Reproduction and Stress Test on Public Adapters

**Problem:** Detecting Backdoored LoRAs from Weights Alone (arXiv:2602.15195) reports backdoored adapters show a distinctive spectral signature (concentrated singular values, high energy, low entropy) detectable without running the model. Covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models is an Open Phil priority, but the detector has not been independently validated on the wild zoo of public LoRAs.

**Direction:** Download a sample of public LoRA adapters from a hub plus a handful the student trains with a simple trigger-based backdoor on a small model. Compute the singular-value spectrum of each adapter's weight matrices, derive the spectral-entropy/energy features from the paper, and test whether benign vs. backdoored adapters separate. Report ROC and false-positive rate on benign public adapters (the practically important number).

**Why it matters:** A weights-only, trigger-agnostic backdoor scanner is exactly the kind of lab-independent defensive infrastructure that protects the open-weight ecosystem. Validating its real-world false-positive rate is essential before anyone deploys it as a hub-level filter.

**Relevant context:** 'Detecting Backdoored LoRAs from Weights Alone' (arXiv:2602.15195, 2026): backdoors leave concentrated singular values with high energy, low entropy; trigger-agnostic, no model execution. Addresses Open Phil RFP priority on detecting covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models.
