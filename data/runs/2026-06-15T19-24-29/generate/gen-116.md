# Activation Probe Stability Under Distribution Shift of Benign Inputs

**Problem:** Activation-probe jailbreak detectors achieve high recall at low false-positive rates, but their false-positive behavior has been tested mostly on standard benign prompts; whether they spuriously fire on unusual-but-benign domains (medical, legal, fiction, code) — a deployment-critical failure — is untested.

**Direction:** Train a simple logistic-regression probe on a published jailbreak dataset's activations from one open model, then stress-test its false-positive rate across diverse benign corpora (clinical notes, legal text, dark fiction, security CTF writeups). Report which benign domains trigger spurious detections.

**Why it matters:** Bridging lab-tested defenses to real deployment robustness is a stated open problem. Over-refusal from probe false positives is a silent harm. This is a cheap, no-training, infrastructure-light evaluation that yields a reusable benign stress-test suite — high counterfactual value outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Activation probes detect jailbreaks at ~90% recall, 1% FPR (subfield context; arXiv:2411.09003 affine refusal). Over-refusal literature (XSTest, OR-Bench arXiv:2405.20947) shows lexical overfitting causes false refusals on benign sensitive-keyword prompts; whether probes inherit this is unstudied.
