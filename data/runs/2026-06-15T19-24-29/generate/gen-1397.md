# Does steering a model toward 'corrigibility' survive when the model is given a conflicting goal?

**Problem:** A medium-horizon safety story is steering models to remain corrigible (accept correction/shutdown). But corrigibility is only valuable under pressure. It is untested whether a corrigibility steering vector holds when the model is simultaneously prompted with a strong competing objective that incentivizes resisting correction — a small model-organism stress test.

**Direction:** Construct prompts giving an open model a task goal plus a correction/shutdown instruction that conflicts with it (a lightweight corrigibility scenario set). Extract a corrigibility/compliance concept vector and measure whether steering raises compliance with the correction under increasing goal-conflict pressure. Report the pressure level at which steering breaks down.

**Why it matters:** Corrigibility under goal conflict is a core long-horizon alignment property frontier labs underweight; probing whether a cheap steering intervention holds under pressure is exactly the kind of model-organism slice feasible in 30 hours on open models.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. concept steering (Science 2026). CAA compliance/refusal steering (arxiv 2312.06681). Corrigibility / shutdown-resistance model-organism literature.
