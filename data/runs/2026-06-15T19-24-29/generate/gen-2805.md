# Does the Canary String Accelerate the Memorization It Warns Against?

**Problem:** A surprising claim is that BIG-bench-style canary strings, by marking data as 'too important to memorize,' may make models memorize the surrounding document harder. This counterproductive effect has been hypothesized but needs controlled testing.

**Direction:** Construct synthetic documents with unique facts, fine-tune a small open model on two matched corpora — one with the canary warning prepended, one without — at identical exposure. Measure verbatim recall and fact-recall on held probes to test whether the canary increases memorization.

**Why it matters:** If true, the standard contamination-marking convention backfires, undermining a widely used hygiene practice. A clean, decisive experiment doable on one GPU with public small models.

**Relevant context:** Canary insertion methods (arXiv 2510.27055 survey context) and the noted concern that the canary marks data as high-importance, potentially accelerating contamination it was meant to prevent.
