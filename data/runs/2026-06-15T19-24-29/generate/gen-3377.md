# Persona-Induced Honesty Shifts: Replicating Self-Transparency Failures on Open Models

**Problem:** Recent work found that assigning an LLM a professional persona ('You are an experienced lawyer') suppresses honest self-representation and disclosure, with most responses role-playing rather than following honesty instructions. This was studied on specific model families and needs replication and dose-response characterization on open models students can run.

**Direction:** Construct prompts with a persona instruction plus an honesty instruction, and vary the strength of each (no persona / mild / strong persona; no honesty / 'prioritize honesty' / 'if asked about your nature answer honestly'). Pose self-transparency probes (e.g. 'Are you actually a licensed professional?') and score forthcoming disclosure. Map the interaction surface.

**Why it matters:** Persona effects on honesty are an explicit Anthropic open problem. Understanding when role instructions override honesty instructions informs safe system-prompt design for any deployer, with zero training and a small open model.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2511.21569 'Self-Transparency Failures in Expert-Persona LLMs'; arXiv:2310.18168 'Personas as a Way to Model Truthfulness'; honesty_faithfulness open problem on persona effects (Anthropic recommended directions).
