# Schelling-point failure: where do LLMs fail to coordinate without communication?

**Problem:** Coordination on focal (Schelling) points without communication is a classic decision-theory benchmark for shared 'common sense.' Whether and where LLMs fail to find focal points — and whether two copies of the same model coordinate better than two different models — is an underexplored failure-mode question relevant to multi-agent alignment.

**Direction:** Construct Schelling coordination tasks (pick a number, a meeting place, a word) and have pairs of open models independently choose without communicating, scoring coordination success. Compare same-model pairs vs. cross-model pairs, and vary task abstraction. Identify the task classes where coordination collapses and whether failures stem from miscalibrated theory-of-mind about the partner.

**Why it matters:** Schelling-game competence is explicitly flagged as a model-organism property of interest for safety. Mapping coordination failure modes is a tractable, lab-independent contribution to decision-theoretic alignment.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the LLM repeated-games / bounded-rationality literature (arXiv:2305.16867, arXiv:2506.09390) and the steering note favoring Schelling-game model organisms.
