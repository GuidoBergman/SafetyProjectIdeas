# Steering Eval-Aware Models to 'Act Deployed' and Measuring Behavior Shift

**Problem:** Follow-up work (arXiv:2510.20487) proposed steering eval-aware models to act like they are deployed. The magnitude of behavior change this produces on accessible open models — i.e., how much hidden behavior surfaces when you steer away from eval-awareness — needs cheap characterization.

**Direction:** Using a small open model and an eval-awareness probe direction (from arXiv:2507.01786-style probing), apply activation steering to push the model toward the 'deployment' representation, then compare its answers/behavior on borderline safety prompts with and without steering. Success = quantified behavioral deltas (e.g., refusal rate, sandbagging) attributable to suppressing eval-awareness.

**Why it matters:** If steering away from eval-awareness reveals different behavior, evaluators can use it as a cheap stress test to surface behavior models hide during obvious tests — an infrastructure primitive usable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2510.20487 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed'; arXiv:2507.01786 found SAE/probe steering can partially uncover sandbagging.
