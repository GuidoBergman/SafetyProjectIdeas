# Shallow Alignment Depth Profiling Across Open Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) showed safety alignment in chat LLMs is concentrated in the first few output tokens, but it is unknown whether the new generation of open reasoning models (those that emit long chain-of-thought before answering) exhibit the same shallow pattern or push the safety-critical region elsewhere.

**Direction:** Take 3-4 small open reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B, Qwen3 reasoning variants) and replicate Qi et al.'s per-token KL-divergence-between-aligned-and-base measurement, but on the reasoning trace rather than just the final answer. Force harmful prefills at varying token depths into the CoT vs the final answer and measure attack success on a small StrongREJECT subset. Plot a 'safety depth profile' for each model.

**Why it matters:** If reasoning models concentrate safety in the same first few tokens, prefill attacks generalize; if the reasoning trace dilutes or relocates safety, defenders need new monitoring targets. This is a pure evaluation of open models needing no training, exactly the counterfactually valuable work labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' (arXiv:2406.05946, ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper) shows alignment adapts the generative distribution mainly over the first few tokens and explains prefilling/suffix/decoding attacks. Context file explicitly flags 'test whether reasoning models exhibit same shallow alignment pattern' as a follow-up.
