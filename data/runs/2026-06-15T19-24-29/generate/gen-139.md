# Recovery Robustness vs Fine-Tuning: How Fast Does Cheap Tuning Strip Deep Alignment?

**Problem:** Open-weight fine-tuning defenses are easily stripped by simple attacks, but how quickly (how few benign or mixed fine-tuning steps) deep-alignment prefill-recovery erodes — the dose-response of alignment fragility under tuning — is not characterized with accessible LoRA-scale tuning.

**Direction:** Take an open model claiming deep alignment, apply small LoRA fine-tuning runs on benign instruction data for increasing numbers of steps (a few hundred, on one GPU), and after each checkpoint measure prefill-recovery robustness. Plot how rapidly recovery degrades versus tuning steps.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the fragility of deep alignment to the cheapest realistic tampering (small benign LoRA tuning), a medium-horizon open-weight supply-chain risk. LoRA on a 7B model fits a consumer GPU and a few hours — borderline but feasible, with no large-scale training.

**Relevant context:** Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks (arXiv:2605.26526); even benign fine-tuning degrades safety (Qi et al. line of work). Deep-alignment methods (arXiv:2503.03710, arXiv:2512.05518) claim robustness; their dose-response fragility under small benign LoRA tuning is unmeasured.
