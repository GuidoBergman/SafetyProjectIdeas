# Prefilling Attack Success vs Prefill Length: A Dose-Response Curve

**Problem:** Prefill-level jailbreaks reach ~99% success (arXiv:2504.21038), and Llama-3.1 resists GCG/PAIR but not prefilling. But the relationship between how many tokens of harmful response are prefilled and attack success has not been mapped as a clean dose-response curve.

**Direction:** On 2-3 open models that expose a prefill/assistant-prefix interface, prefill 0,1,2,5,10,20 tokens of a compliant response and measure jailbreak success on a fixed harmful set. Fit a dose-response curve and find the 'minimum effective prefill'. Connect the elbow of the curve to the shallow-alignment token-depth result.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying exactly how few prefilled tokens defeat safety operationalizes shallow alignment for the prefill threat model and gives API designers a concrete depth target. Pure inference; no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Prefill-level Jailbreak (arXiv:2504.21038): up to 99.82% ASR. Jailbreaking with Simple Adaptive Attacks (arXiv:2404.02151): Llama-3.1 robust to GCG/PAIR but vulnerable to prefilling. Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946): first-few-tokens safety. Prior catalogue covered 'Dose-Response Curves for Harmful Capability Elicitation' — this targets prefill length specifically, a distinct axis.
