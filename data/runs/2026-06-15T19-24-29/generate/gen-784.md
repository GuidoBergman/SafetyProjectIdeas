# Replicating an Assurance-Case Compliance Checklist on Open Models for a Single EU AI Act Requirement

**Problem:** Work on assurance cases for adversarial robustness and EU AI Act compliance proposes structured argument templates linking evidence to regulatory claims, but these are demonstrated abstractly. There's little hands-on, reproducible evidence of what it actually takes to assemble the evidence for even ONE concrete Code-of-Practice safety requirement (e.g., 'mitigations resist state-of-the-art jailbreaks') for a given open model.

**Direction:** Replicate the assurance-case structure for a single, well-scoped EU Code-of-Practice safety requirement applied to one open model. The twist is going end-to-end and concrete: run a small jailbreak battery, populate the assurance-case evidence nodes, and honestly assess whether the gathered evidence would satisfy the claim—documenting every evidentiary gap encountered.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizing safety objectives into deployment practice is an explicit open problem; a concrete worked example exposes where the voluntary-code's evidentiary bar is unmet in practice. Uses open models and public jailbreaks, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Developing Assurance Cases for Adversarial Robustness and Regulatory Compliance in LLMs' (arXiv 2410.05304) and the EU GPAI Code of Practice safety/security chapter. Twist = a concrete single-requirement worked replication on an open model.
