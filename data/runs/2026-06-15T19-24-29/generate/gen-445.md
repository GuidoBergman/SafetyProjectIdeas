# Replicating epoch-wise double descent on a safety classifier and locating the interpolation peak

**Problem:** Deep double descent (test error rises then falls as model size, data, or training time increases) has been demonstrated for CNNs, ResNets, and transformers on standard vision/language tasks. Whether the epoch-wise double-descent peak appears — and at what location — when the task is a *safety-relevant classifier* (e.g., harmful-vs-benign request detection) on label-noisy data has not been characterized.

**Direction:** Reproduce epoch-wise double descent in the canonical way (add label noise, train past interpolation), but on a small harmful-request classification dataset. Plot test error vs. training epochs, locate the interpolation peak, and vary the label-noise rate to map how the peak's height/position changes. Compare qualitatively to the published double-descent curves.

**Why it matters:** Double descent shows that 'train longer / bigger is better' intuitions can fail in non-monotone ways — directly relevant to anyone training safety classifiers or filters, who could unknowingly sit on the bad side of the peak. Demonstrating it on a safety task with label noise (common in real safety labels) is a reproducible cautionary result requiring only a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Deep Double Descent (arXiv:1912.02292, OpenAI): test error non-monotone in model size, data, and epochs; effect amplified by label noise; occurs across CNN/ResNet/transformer. Common Methodologies (context): learning theory, generalization analysis.
