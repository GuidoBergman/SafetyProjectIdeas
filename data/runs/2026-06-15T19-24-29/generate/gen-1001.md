# Measuring Whether a Different-Vendor Model Is Actually an Independent Monitor

**Problem:** A common safety pattern is using model B (different vendor) to monitor or audit model A's outputs. This only adds safety if B's judgments are statistically independent of A's mistakes. Homogeneity research suggests they may not be, but no one has quantified the independence assumption underlying cross-vendor monitoring.

**Direction:** Pick a monitoring task (detect policy violation / detect a planted error in a transcript). Have model A generate flawed transcripts; have 5-6 different-vendor models act as monitors. Measure conditional error: when monitor X misses a violation, how often does monitor Y also miss it? Estimate the effective number of 'independent' monitors via correlation structure. Compare same-family vs cross-family monitor pairs.

**Why it matters:** Scalable-oversight and AI-control plans lean on independent monitors. Quantifying how much independence you actually get is high-value, lab-external, needs no training, and directly informs whether monitor diversity must be engineered rather than assumed.

**Relevant context:** Builds on correlated-errors findings (arXiv 2506.07962) and inter-model homogeneity (Artificial Hivemind); AI-control literature assumes monitor independence.
