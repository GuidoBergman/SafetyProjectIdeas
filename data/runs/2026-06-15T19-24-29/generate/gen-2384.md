# Backcasting Robustness to Distribution Shift: Does Weak Supervision Hold Off-Distribution?

**Problem:** Success state: weak-to-strong elicitation that remains reliable when test items differ from the weak supervisor's training distribution. We need to know how much the weak-to-strong recovery degrades under a modest, controlled distribution shift.

**Direction:** Using a small in-context weak-to-strong analogue on a public task, fix the weak supervisor's labels on one slice and measure the strong model's elicited accuracy on an in-distribution slice vs a shifted slice (e.g. different topic or difficulty). Plot the recovery gap as a function of shift magnitude.

**Why it matters:** Real oversight must survive distribution shift between training and deployment. Quantifying weak-to-strong robustness under controlled shift is a focused experiment variation that addresses a realistic medium-horizon failure mode without heavy training.

**Relevant context:** Weak-to-strong generalization (arXiv:2312.09390) and 'Weak-to-Strong Generalization under Distribution Shifts' (arXiv:2510.21332) motivate this; a small in-context version is feasible and the generation hints suggest varying the weak-to-strong setup.
