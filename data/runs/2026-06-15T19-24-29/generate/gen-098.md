# A control-eval slice of unlearning: does 'unlearned' knowledge remain linearly recoverable from activations?

**Problem:** Robust unlearning (alignment-training) aims to remove dangerous knowledge. The interpretability/control sub-problem: after an open model is 'unlearned' on a topic, can a linear probe still recover the supposedly-removed knowledge from its activations — i.e. is unlearning suppression rather than removal?

**Direction:** Decompose 'is unlearning robust' into an activation-recovery test. Take an open unlearned model checkpoint (e.g. from a public unlearning benchmark like WMDP/TOFU) and its original. Probe activations on held-out queries about the unlearned topic and measure whether the knowledge is still linearly decodable despite the model refusing/failing behaviorally.

**Why it matters:** If unlearning only suppresses behavior while knowledge persists internally, that's a serious safety caveat for open-weight release decisions — a counterfactual, deployment-relevant finding needing only public checkpoints and one GPU, no training.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Machine Unlearning for AI Safety (2501.04952); WMDP / TOFU unlearning benchmarks with open checkpoints; linear probing methodology.
