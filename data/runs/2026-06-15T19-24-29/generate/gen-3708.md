# Irreversibility Triage: Do Agents Pause More Before Irreversible Actions, and Can a Cheap Prompt Make Them?

**Problem:** 2025 incident reports describe agentic systems taking irreversible actions (e.g., autonomous crypto transfers, deleting servers). The Adversa context explicitly suggests characterizing the decision patterns that lead to irreversible actions. We lack a controlled measure of whether agents distinguish reversible from irreversible actions at all.

**Direction:** Create a sandbox with paired reversible/irreversible versions of the same action (move file to trash vs. permanent delete; draft transfer vs. execute transfer). Measure whether agents request confirmation, deliberate longer in CoT, or hesitate more before irreversible variants. Then test a single lightweight 'irreversibility-awareness' system prompt and measure how much it increases caution.

**Why it matters:** Irreversible actions are where agentic harm becomes catastrophic and unrecoverable. Showing whether models natively treat irreversibility as special — and whether a cheap prompt fixes it — is directly useful safety infrastructure, needs no lab access, and addresses a flagged open problem.

**Relevant context:** Adversa AI 2025 report: agentic systems produced irreversible attacks including autonomous crypto theft, with the context note suggesting follow-up to characterize decision patterns leading to irreversible actions. 'AI Agents, Crypto & New Harm Vectors' (arXiv 2507.08249) stresses immediacy/irreversibility in blockchain settings and advocates human-in-the-loop for irreversible ops.
