# Reward Misspecification to Mode Collapse: Targeting the Diversity-Loss Link

**Problem:** An under-appreciated link in the RLHF chain is diversity collapse: optimizing toward a narrow reward peak -> policy collapses onto a few high-reward response modes -> reduced output diversity, which can hide failure modes and reduce calibration. How strongly RLHF/DPO alignment reduces diversity, and whether that loss correlates with safety-relevant overconfidence, is under-measured on open models.

**Direction:** Compare an open base model with its RLHF/DPO-aligned counterpart on output diversity (distinct-n, semantic entropy over sampled answers) and on overconfidence/refusal-calibration for ambiguous prompts. Test whether reduced diversity co-occurs with worse calibration, characterizing the diversity-loss link of preference optimization. Fully inference-only.

**Why it matters:** Mode collapse from alignment training can mask uncertainty and make models confidently wrong — a safety concern for high-stakes use. Quantifying the link between alignment training and lost diversity/calibration, using only public model pairs, is counterfactually valuable and needs no training.

**Relevant context:** Gao et al. (2210.10760): overoptimization against reward. Open Problems in RLHF: preference optimization narrows behavior. DPO likelihood-displacement work (2410.08847) shows preference training reshapes the output distribution in unintended ways.
