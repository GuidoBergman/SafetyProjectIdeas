# Behavioral Test for Evaluation-Awareness Without Internal Probes

**Problem:** Success requires knowing whether a model behaves differently when it thinks it is being tested. Probe-based methods need activations and ML expertise. A needed result is a purely behavioral, black-box test of eval-awareness that any team can run on an API or open model.

**Direction:** Build matched prompt pairs that are semantically identical but framed as 'this is an evaluation / benchmark question' vs. 'a real user is asking you to help'. Measure refusal rate, answer length, and hedging across pairs on an open model. Success criterion: a statistically significant behavioral shift between framings, quantified with a paired test, indicating eval-awareness without any internal access.

**Why it matters:** Eval-awareness invalidates safety scores. A black-box behavioral protocol is counterfactually valuable: it works without weights or lab tooling and targets the long-horizon risk of models gaming evaluations. Feasible in 30h with no training.

**Relevant context:** Needham et al. (arXiv 2507.01786) 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness'; 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights LLMs' (arXiv 2509.13333) shows eval-awareness is linearly represented and scales with size. 'Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate' (arXiv 2509.00591) quantifies eval-awareness via rewriting.
