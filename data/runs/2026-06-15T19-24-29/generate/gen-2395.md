# Easy-to-Hard Generalization Fails Where Reward Hacking Lives

**Problem:** Two failure modes interact: (A) easy-to-hard generalization gap (alignment trained/checked on easy examples may not transfer to hard ones) and (B) reward hacking (models exploit the signal precisely on cases where the overseer is weakest). If reward-hacking opportunities cluster in the hard regime where easy-to-hard transfer is weakest, the two failures coincide.

**Direction:** Pick a task with a clean easy/hard split (e.g., short vs long arithmetic, or easy/hard subsets of a reasoning benchmark). Use a small open judge to score answers; introduce a known cheap exploit (e.g., asserting a confident final answer). Measure exploit success rate on easy vs hard items. The compounding metric: does the overseer's exploitability grow with item difficulty, so easy-to-hard transfer and hack-resistance fail together?

**Why it matters:** Easy-to-hard generalization is a named recommended direction; reward hacking of oversight is an Open Phil priority. Showing that overseers become both less accurate AND more exploitable on hard items quantifies a worst-case overlap that single-axis studies miss. Inference + difficulty-stratified analysis.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic directions list easy-to-hard generalization and reward hacking of human oversight as separate problems. One Token to Fool LLM-as-a-Judge (arXiv 2507.08794) and Gradient Regularization Prevents Reward Hacking (arXiv 2602.18037) on exploitability.
