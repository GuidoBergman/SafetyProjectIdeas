# The Scoring-Aggregation Link: How Threshold Choice Manufactures Safety Pass/Fail

**Problem:** Eval-to-decision chain: per-item harm scores -> aggregated into a benchmark number -> compared to a threshold -> safe/unsafe verdict. The aggregation+threshold link is rarely scrutinized: small, defensible changes in aggregation (mean vs worst-case vs category-weighted) or threshold can flip a model's verdict, making safety claims arbitrary.

**Direction:** Take published per-item safety scores (or regenerate them with open judges on an open benchmark) for several open models, then compute how the model ranking and pass/fail verdict change under a grid of aggregation rules and thresholds. Report the fraction of model pairs whose relative safety ranking is not robust to defensible aggregation choices.

**Why it matters:** If safety verdicts are aggregation artifacts, the benchmark-to-decision chain is unreliable for regulators seeking cross-model comparison (a named NIST/CAISI need). Pure data analysis, no GPU even strictly required, fully lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** US CAISI/NIST open problem calls for standardized cross-model risk metrics; AILuminate and HELM Safety produce aggregate grades; autograder-bias literature (arXiv 2507.03772) shows per-item scores are already noisy.
