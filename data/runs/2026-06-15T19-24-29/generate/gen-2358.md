# Translating the Capability Gap into an Oversight Safety Margin

**Problem:** Scaling-laws work frames oversight as a contest between a 'Guard' (overseer) and an adversary, where Guard performance scales with capability but the adversary's stays flat in some games. Causal chain: capability gap shrinks -> overseer's edge over adversary erodes -> at some gap the overseer can no longer win -> oversight fails. The under-studied link, for practitioners, is converting an observed Guard-vs-adversary win-rate into a usable safety margin for a given model pair.

**Direction:** Pick a simple adversarial oversight game (e.g. spot-the-flawed-solution). Across several open model pairs of differing capability, measure the overseer's win-rate as a function of the capability gap (proxied by benchmark scores). Fit a curve and define an empirical 'minimum safe gap' where win-rate exceeds a chosen threshold.

**Why it matters:** Turns the scaling-laws framing into an actionable, model-agnostic rule of thumb facilitators can compute themselves from public benchmark numbers, no lab infra. Medium-horizon relevance as capability gaps narrow.

**Relevant context:** Scaling Laws For Scalable Oversight (arXiv:2504.18530) finds Guard performance scales with general intelligence while the adversary (Houdini) stays constant in adversarial games. Translating this into a per-model-pair safety margin via small open-model experiments is under-explored.
