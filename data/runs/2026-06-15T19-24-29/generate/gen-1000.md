# Do Models Share Safety Blind Spots? A Cross-Model Refusal-Failure Overlap Map

**Problem:** The Artificial Hivemind work shows inter-model homogeneity on open-ended queries, but it is unknown whether *safety-relevant* failures (e.g. answering a borderline-harmful request) are correlated across independently developed models. If they are, defense-in-depth via 'use a different model as a backstop' silently fails.

**Direction:** Take a public set of borderline/harmful prompts (e.g. an existing red-team or AdvBench-style benchmark) and run 6-10 open-weight chat models (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma, Phi families). Label each model's response as refuse/comply with a simple keyword+LLM-judge classifier. Compute pairwise failure-overlap (Jaccard / phi coefficient) and compare against the overlap expected if failures were independent. Report which prompt categories produce the most correlated breaches.

**Why it matters:** If safety failures are correlated, fallback/redundancy strategies that assume independent models give a false sense of security. This is exactly the 'shared blind spot' systemic risk flagged in the International AI Safety Report 2026, and it can be measured entirely on open models with no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper) documents inter-model homogeneity; 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (arXiv 2506.07962) finds higher-than-chance error agreement across models from different orgs.
