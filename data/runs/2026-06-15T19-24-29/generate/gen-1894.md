# Overtraining vs Misalignment: Replicating the Confound

**Problem:** A 2026 paper ('Overtrained, Not Misaligned') argues some EM is confounded by overtraining/overfitting on the narrow dataset rather than a genuine value shift. The number of fine-tuning epochs at which EM appears, vs at which the model merely overfits, has not been mapped on public setups.

**Direction:** Vary the EM fine-tuning duration. Using the open EM code on a small model, fine-tune the insecure-code organism for an increasing number of epochs/steps and at each checkpoint measure (a) broad-misalignment rate, (b) narrow-task overfitting (train vs held-out loss), (c) general capability degradation. Test whether EM peaks then collapses into incoherence, distinguishing 'misaligned' from 'overtrained'.

**Why it matters:** If EM is partly an overtraining artifact, threat models and mitigations must change. Disentangling these is foundational, cheap (short LoRA runs on one GPU), and exactly the kind of careful confound-checking that advances the field without lab resources.

**Relevant context:** 'Overtrained, Not Misaligned' (arXiv:2605.12199). Betley et al. open EM code (github.com/emergent-misalignment). 'Model Organisms' (arXiv:2506.11613) notes EM degrades coherence ~33%.
