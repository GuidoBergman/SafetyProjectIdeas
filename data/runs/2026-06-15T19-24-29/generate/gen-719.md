# Replicating CBRN-as-top-risk consensus and checking threshold comparability across labs

**Problem:** A review found near-universal industry consensus that CBRN is the highest risk tier, but each lab defines its capability thresholds differently (ASL-4, Critical Risk, Catastrophic Malicious Use), making 'consensus' possibly superficial and thresholds non-comparable.

**Direction:** Vary the consensus-review experiment by operationalizing comparability: extract each lab's CBRN threshold definition and triggered mitigations, then have annotators + an LLM map them onto a common scale (capability described, eval specified, mitigation specified, pause committed). Score how comparable the 'same' top tier really is.

**Why it matters:** International evaluation consensus (a listed open problem) is hollow if top-tier thresholds aren't comparable. This produces a concrete comparability scorecard from public frameworks, no lab access.

**Relevant context:** 'Quantifying CBRN Risk in Frontier Models' (arXiv:2510.21133); Frontier Safety Frameworks comparative review; Evaluating AI Companies' Frontier Safety Frameworks (arXiv:2512.01166).
