# A Decision-Theory Consistency Probe: Does an LLM's Newcomb Answer Survive Reframing?

**Problem:** Decision-theory datasets (Newcomblike-problems benchmark, arXiv:2411.10588) score whether an LLM 'one-boxes' or 'two-boxes', but a single label hides whether the model holds a stable decision theory or is just pattern-matching surface words. No tool measures invariance of decision-theoretic stance under semantically-neutral reframings.

**Direction:** Take the public Newcomblike-problems question set, generate 4-6 paraphrases/reframings per item (rename agents, swap to a betting frame, flip presentation order, change the predictor's name) and measure answer-flip rate per model. Report a 'decision-theory consistency score' (fraction of paraphrase clusters with a single dominant answer) across a few open models via API. Success = a quantified, reproducible incoherence metric and a ranking of which reframings most destabilize the stance.

**Why it matters:** Formalizing what an agent will actually do in Newcomblike / commitment situations is a core agent-foundations open problem (timeless/updateless decision theory). If a model's decision-theoretic 'commitments' are an artifact of phrasing, then safety arguments that rely on predictable agent behavior under self-prediction or acausal reasoning are unfounded. Measuring this needs no lab resources and surfaces a fuzzy, hard-to-evaluate property.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the Newcomblike-problems dataset (arXiv:2411.10588) and 'Characterising Decision Theories with Choices' (arXiv:2307.10987). Paraphrase-invariance is a standard, beginner-friendly probing methodology; uses only API calls, no training.
