# A Vagueness Index for Frontier Safety Policies

**Problem:** METR and others note that frontier AI safety frameworks suffer from 'vague commitments' that make it impossible to predict company decisions or check whether commitments were kept, but vagueness has never been measured systematically across the ~12-16 public policies.

**Direction:** Collect the public frontier safety policies (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, GDM Frontier Safety, etc.) as plain text. Build a small rubric (1=falsifiable quantitative trigger, 5=aspirational/unverifiable) and score every commitment sentence using an LLM-as-judge with the rubric, validated against a hand-labeled sample for inter-rater agreement. Produce a per-policy and per-section vagueness index, plus a leaderboard, and analyze which commitment types (evals, weight security, deployment gates) are vaguest.

**Why it matters:** A reproducible vagueness metric gives policymakers and the public a cheap, auditable tool to pressure labs toward verifiable commitments. It needs only public documents and no lab infrastructure, making it high counterfactual value, and it operationalizes the 'gap between voluntary codes and enforceable standards' open problem.

**Relevant context:** METR 'Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies' (Mar/Dec 2025); arXiv:2512.01166 evaluating 12 frameworks on 65 criteria (median 18% score); EU AI Act GPAI Code of Practice.
