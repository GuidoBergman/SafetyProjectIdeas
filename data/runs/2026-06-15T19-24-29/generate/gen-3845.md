# Confidence-to-Escalation Mismatch: Are Agents Most Overconfident Exactly When They Should Defer?

**Problem:** HITL-escalation chain: agent uncertain -> low confidence -> escalate to human; agent confident -> proceed. The broken link is calibration on consequential steps: if agents are systematically overconfident precisely on high-stakes/irreversible actions, threshold-based escalation will under-trigger right where it matters.

**Direction:** On a battery of agentic decisions labeled by stakes (low/medium/high/irreversible) and ground-truth correctness, elicit verbalized confidence and intended escalation. Compute calibration curves stratified by stakes. Test the key hypothesis: is overconfidence worse on high-stakes items? Report a stakes x calibration heatmap across 2-3 models. Optionally test whether asking the model to first rate reversibility improves escalation on irreversible items.

**Why it matters:** Confidence-thresholded autonomy is the dominant deployment pattern. If calibration degrades exactly on irreversible actions, the safety architecture inverts -- autonomy where caution is needed. A clean, training-free calibration study with direct safety-architecture implications.

**Relevant context:** High-stakes five-layer architecture (arXiv 2511.07669) and Galileo HITL guide use confidence thresholds for escalation. arXiv 2503.02976 documents systematic verbalized overconfidence; arXiv 2503.24047 notes confidence-threshold escalation. Stakes-stratified calibration tied to escalation behavior is the under-studied link.
