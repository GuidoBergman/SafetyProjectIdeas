# Does the 'larger models are more steerable' finding also mean larger models are more easily jailbroken via steering?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al.'s surprising result: larger models are MORE steerable toward a concept. The worrying causal extension: if steerability scales with size, then the attacker's link (steer the model AWAY from refusal) should also get easier with size -> bigger models are softer targets for steering-based jailbreaks. This dark-side-of-steerability link is asserted by some work but not isolated as a clean size scaling.

**Direction:** Pick a single open model family with multiple sizes (e.g., Qwen 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B or Pythia sizes). For each size, extract a comparable refusal direction and measure the attack-success-rate increase when steering AWAY from refusal at matched relative strength. Plot ASR-gain vs model size. Success criterion: a monotonic (or not) scaling curve of jailbreak-via-steering effectiveness with size.

**Why it matters:** If steerability and steering-based vulnerability scale together, the Science paper's optimistic finding has a built-in safety cost that grows with capability — a medium-horizon concern. Single model family, forward passes only, perfect 30h scope.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. report larger models more steerable; 'Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' (2603.24543) reports jailbreak effects grow with scale. This isolates the scaling within one family as a clean controlled study.
