# The Abstention Backstop: Backcasting a Judge That Refuses Rather Than Guesses

**Problem:** Success state: an oversight protocol where the weak judge's worst case is 'I don't know' not 'confidently endorse the deceptive answer'. We need to know how much accuracy a free abstention option buys, and how often debaters can still force a confident wrong verdict.

**Direction:** Add an explicit 'abstain' verdict to the open debate/consultancy judge prompt over QuALITY transcripts. Measure the accuracy/coverage trade-off curve (selective accuracy vs abstention rate) for debate vs single-advisor consultancy, with a small open judge model. Identify question features that the judge abstains on vs gets confidently wrong.

**Why it matters:** Recursive oversight tolerates error far better if errors convert into abstentions that escalate to a human. Quantifying the abstention backstop on an open benchmark gives protocol designers a concrete safety margin, requires no training, and is exactly the diffuse-reward evaluation labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Debate-vs-consultancy protocols are open (Khan et al. 2024 arXiv:2402.06782; Kenton et al. 2024 arXiv:2407.04622). Selective prediction / abstention is standard but has not been applied to oversight-protocol judges on QuALITY.
