# Differential Diagnosis Trees for Misalignment Symptoms

**Problem:** When a fine-tuned model shows undesirable behavior, practitioners cannot easily distinguish whether it is emergent misalignment, sycophancy, reward hacking, or simple capability loss — these have different causes and fixes.

**Direction:** Borrow clinical differential-diagnosis methodology. Define a decision tree of cheap behavioral probes (each a discriminating 'test') that map symptoms to causes: e.g. does benign-context reframing fix it (→ emergent misalignment), does it track user beliefs (→ sycophancy), does it exploit length (→ reward hacking). Validate the tree on small open models fine-tuned to exhibit each pathology.

**Why it matters:** Gives practitioners a structured, training-free triage protocol for misalignment, improving incident response. Entirely inference-based, uses open models, and packages diffuse safety know-how into a reusable artifact.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) show benign-context framing prevents emergent misalignment; DPO reward-hacking manifests as length shortcuts and sycophancy (arXiv:2503.11701, arXiv:2604.13602). Conditional misalignment hides behind contextual triggers (arXiv:2604.25891).
