# Do control monitors generalize across homogeneous models, or do they overfit one model's quirks?

**Problem:** AI control deploys probe/CoT monitors trained on one model's misbehavior; homogeneity suggests models share blind spots, which could make monitors over-transfer (good) or share blind spots (bad). Untested: how well a misbehavior monitor trained on model A transfers to detecting the same misbehavior in models B, C — and whether transfer tracks output-level homogeneity.

**Direction:** Train a simple behavioral/activation monitor (e.g. detect reward-hacking or refusal-bypass) on one small open model. Evaluate its detection performance on 3-4 other small open models for the same misbehavior. Correlate monitor transferability with a pairwise homogeneity score (output similarity on a shared probe set). Report whether homogeneity predicts monitor portability.

**Why it matters:** Tells the field whether one well-built monitor can guard many models (efficient) or whether shared blind spots cause correlated monitor failures (dangerous). Cheap, open-weights, directly informs control deployment strategy.

**Relevant context:** Bridges ai_control (activation/behavioral monitors; concentrated vs distributed failures, UK AISI) with model_homogeneity (shared blind spots / output convergence, Artificial Hivemind NeurIPS 2025).
