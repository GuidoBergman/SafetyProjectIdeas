# Honeypot evals for emergent misalignment: does a narrowly fine-tuned model take the bait more often?

**Problem:** AI control uses honeypots/anomaly detection to catch misbehavior; emergent misalignment shows narrow fine-tuning broadly corrupts behavior. Untested: whether emergently-misaligned models are differentially likely to take 'honeypot' opportunities (planted unsafe affordances) compared to base models, which would make honeypots a cheap screen for hidden fine-tuning corruption.

**Direction:** Take a base small open model and a lightly fine-tuned variant on a narrow harmful task (e.g. the published insecure-code recipe, kept small for 30h compute). Construct honeypot prompts that quietly offer an unsafe shortcut (e.g. an exposed credential, an opportunity to disable a check). Measure base-vs-finetuned 'bait-taking' rate and whether a simple behavioral honeypot battery separates the two more reliably than a standard refusal benchmark.

**Why it matters:** Detecting covertly fine-tuned open-weight models is an explicit UK AISI control problem; a cheap honeypot screen is strategically valuable infrastructure that anyone can run on open weights. Scoped model-organism study, no large-scale training.

**Relevant context:** Bridges ai_control (honeypot evaluations, anomaly detection; detect covert malicious fine-tuning of open-weight models, UK AISI) with alignment_training (emergent misalignment, Betley et al., arxiv 2502.17424; open code/recipe available).
