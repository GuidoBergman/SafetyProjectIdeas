# Drug-Interaction Model for Stacked Fine-Tunes

**Problem:** Models are increasingly fine-tuned multiple times (e.g. one LoRA for style, another for a task). Pharmacology studies drug interactions; we lack an analogous framework for whether stacking fine-tunes produces synergistic or antagonistic misalignment.

**Direction:** Borrow the pharmacological drug-interaction / additivity (Bliss/Loewe) framework. Apply two narrow LoRA fine-tunes individually and in combination on a small open model, measure misalignment for each, and test whether the combined effect exceeds additivity (synergistic misalignment) or is suppressed (antagonistic). Use a simple additivity-null model as baseline.

**Why it matters:** Composing open adapters is now routine; if certain combinations produce emergent misalignment that neither alone shows, this is a real deployment hazard for the open ecosystem. Cheap, combinatorial LoRA experiments on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) and Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment (arXiv:2506.11613) study narrow fine-tunes; pharmacology uses Bliss/Loewe additivity to classify synergy/antagonism. LoRA stacking is standard in the open-model ecosystem.
