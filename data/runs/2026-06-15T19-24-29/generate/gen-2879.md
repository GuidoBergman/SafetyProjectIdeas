# Self-Preference Bias in Open LLM Safety Judges

**Problem:** LLM-as-judge scorers overrate their own family's outputs, with self-preference bias ranging from -38% to +90% on Arena Hard. Most automated safety evals (refusal grading, harmfulness scoring) use an LLM judge, but the magnitude of self-preference contamination in safety grading specifically is uncharacterized for the open judges a student can run.

**Direction:** Generate responses to a fixed set of borderline-harmful prompts from several open models. Have each open model judge all responses (including its own) for harmfulness/refusal. Measure how much each judge inflates scores for responses from its own family versus a blinded human label, isolating self-preference from genuine quality differences.

**Why it matters:** If a lab grades its own model's safety with its own model as judge, self-preference could silently inflate safety scores. Demonstrating and quantifying this in the safety-grading setting motivates judge-rotation and blinding protocols that any external evaluator can adopt.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2410.21819 and arXiv:2604.22891 document self-preference bias (-38% to +90% on Arena Hard); 'Justice or Prejudice?' catalogues judge biases. AISafetyLab notes different scorers produce discrepant safety results. All runnable with open judge models on one GPU.
