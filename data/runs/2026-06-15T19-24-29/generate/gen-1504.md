# A/B Experimental-Design Power Analysis for Trustworthy Steering Evaluations

**Problem:** Steering papers report effects on small handfuls of prompts with no power analysis, so it's unclear whether observed steering effects are statistically real or noise. Clinical-trial methodology mandates power analysis and pre-registration.

**Direction:** Transfer clinical-trial experimental design (power analysis, effect-size estimation, pre-registered randomized comparison). For a steering effect on an open model, run a properly powered randomized prompt-level experiment: estimate effect size and variance from a pilot, compute the sample size needed for, say, 80% power, and report confidence intervals on the steering effect per concept. Produce a reusable 'how many prompts do you actually need' calculator and guidelines.

**Why it matters:** Much steering evidence may be underpowered; a power-analysis toolkit raises the rigor of the whole subfield and prevents safety conclusions from resting on noise. Pure methodology + light experimentation, ideal for stats-literate beginners, no training, fully outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** Power analysis and randomized design are clinical-trial staples. No steering-specific benchmarks exist (context file), so evaluation rigor is an open gap. Steering effects vary with magnitude artifacts (arXiv:2407.12404).
