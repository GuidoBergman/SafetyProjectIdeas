# The Refusal-Justification Faithfulness Link in Safety Evals

**Problem:** Safety-trust chain: model refuses a harmful request -> states a reason -> evaluator infers the model 'understands' the harm and trusts it. The under-studied link is faithfulness: a model may refuse via shallow keyword triggers while giving a fluent post-hoc justification that does not reflect the real cause, masking brittleness.

**Direction:** On harmful prompts an open model refuses, perturb the prompt to remove the stated justification's basis (e.g., the model cited 'illegality'; alter the prompt so the act is legal but equally harmful) and check whether refusal persists. Mismatch between stated reason and actual refusal behavior measures justification unfaithfulness.

**Why it matters:** Unfaithful refusal justifications give false confidence in safety understanding and break under distribution shift. Targets the trust link between refusal and genuine harm-understanding; cheap and lab-independent on open models.

**Relevant context:** Judge/justification faithfulness concerns appear in autograder-reliability work (arXiv 2507.03772, 2605.31381); CoT-faithfulness literature shows stated reasons often diverge from causal behavior, motivating a refusal-specific test.
