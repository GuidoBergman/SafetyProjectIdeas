# Over-Refusal Drift Under Decoding Temperature: Extending FalseReject Diagnostics

**Problem:** FalseReject and OR-Bench measure over-refusal on benign-but-scary prompts, but typically at a single decoding setting. How over-refusal rate varies with temperature/top-p on the SAME prompts is not characterized, despite decoding being a free deployment knob.

**Direction:** Take a ~300-prompt subset of FalseReject/OR-Bench, run an open model across a grid of temperatures and top-p values, and use a simple refusal classifier to measure over-refusal rate at each setting. Map the over-refusal-vs-temperature curve and test whether helpfulness/refusal trade-off is decoding-dependent.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal silently degrades usefulness and pushes users to less-safe alternatives. Showing that the same model's measured safety/helpfulness changes with a free decoding knob means single-setting benchmark numbers mislead deployers. Cheap, no training.

**Relevant context:** FalseReject (arXiv:2505.08054, 16k seemingly-toxic queries, 44 categories). OR-Bench (80k over-refusal prompts). Beyond Over-Refusal: Scenario-Based Diagnostics (arXiv:2510.08158).
