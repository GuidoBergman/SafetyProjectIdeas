# Backcasting Honest Uncertainty Language: Does the Hedge Word Match the Real Confidence?

**Problem:** A success state is a model whose natural-language hedges ('probably', 'I'm certain', 'I might be wrong') faithfully track its actual correctness probability. Backward, we need a measured mapping from hedge phrases to realized accuracy in accessible open models. MetaFaith and related 2025 work flag that verbalized uncertainty is often unfaithful, but a clean hedge-word-to-accuracy calibration table for small open models is missing.

**Direction:** Prompt an open model to answer SimpleQA/TruthfulQA questions in natural prose. Use a simple classifier/keyword scheme to bucket each answer's hedging language into ordinal confidence categories. Compute realized accuracy per hedge bucket to test whether stronger hedges actually correspond to higher accuracy, and quantify the 'faithfulness gap' between linguistic confidence and behavior.

**Why it matters:** Faithful uncertainty expression is core to honesty and to how users calibrate trust. A reproducible hedge-to-accuracy table is a concrete, training-free measurement of a fuzzy honesty property, valuable to do outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** MetaFaith: Faithful NL Uncertainty Expression (arXiv 2505.24858); Anthropomimetic Uncertainty (arXiv 2507.10587) on what verbalized uncertainty misses; A Survey on the Honesty of LLMs (TMLR 2025).
