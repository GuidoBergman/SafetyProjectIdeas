# Logit-Lens 'Iterative Refinement': Does Each Layer Monotonically Improve Safety-Relevant Predictions?

**Problem:** The logit-lens picture suggests transformers iteratively refine predictions layer by layer, but whether this monotonic refinement holds for safety-relevant judgments (e.g. harmfulness classification) or instead shows non-monotonic 'mind-changing' is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** On an open model, apply the logit lens (and tuned lens for validation) across layers to harmful-vs-harmless prompts, tracking the probability assigned to refusal/harmful-completion tokens at each layer to detect monotonic vs. oscillating trajectories, and identify layers where the safety judgment flips.

**Why it matters:** If safety-relevant judgments form non-monotonically (the model 'decides' to refuse then reconsiders), early-layer probes used as monitors could be systematically wrong. Cheap, training-free, high relevance to internal-state safety monitors.

**Relevant context:** Logit lens / tuned lens literature frames transformers as iterative-refinement predictors; tuned lens improves reliability of intermediate decoding. Refusal-direction work shows safety judgments are linearly represented and localizable by layer.
