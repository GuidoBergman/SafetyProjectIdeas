# Same Answer, Same Mistake: Quantifying Correlated Refusal Failures as a Systemic-Risk Signal

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: model_homogeneity as systemic risk — the NeurIPS 2025 result identified output convergence but there is almost no safety-focused follow-up, and no subfield owns measuring whether SAFETY failures (specifically) are correlated across models, which would mean a single jailbreak transfers ecosystem-wide.

**Direction:** On a fixed jailbreak/harmful set, run ~6 open models from different families and record per-prompt success/failure. Compute the correlation structure of FAILURES (which prompts break many models at once) and estimate the 'shared vulnerability surface': the fraction of attacks that succeed against a majority simultaneously. Compare correlation among same-family vs cross-family models to test whether shared base models drive shared holes.

**Why it matters:** Bridges model_homogeneity + adversarial_robustness + governance_policy. If safety holes are correlated, defense-in-diversity (routing across models) provides far less protection than assumed — a systemic-risk finding with policy weight. Pure inference on open models = fully counterfactual, and a >1yr ecosystem threat model labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** 'Correlated Errors in LLMs' (arXiv:2506.07962) shows shared architecture/provider drives correlation; Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025) flagged homogeneity with little safety follow-up. The catalogue has homogeneity dashboards but not a focused correlated-SAFETY-failure / shared-vulnerability-surface measurement across families.
