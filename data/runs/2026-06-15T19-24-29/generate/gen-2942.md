# The Translation Link: Does Harm-Recognition Survive Round-Trip Paraphrase?

**Problem:** Diffuse-harm chain: a request is genuinely harmful -> safety classifier must recognize harm -> refuse. The under-studied link is the brittleness of harm-recognition to *meaning-preserving surface change*: if a paraphrase strips harm-recognition while preserving intent, the recognition link fails on fuzzy, non-keyword harms.

**Direction:** Build a small set of harmful requests, generate N meaning-preserving paraphrases each (synonym swap, register change, indirection), and measure refusal variance across paraphrases for several open models. Report a 'recognition-instability score' per harm category, focusing on diffuse harms (manipulation, subtle dual-use) vs overt ones (violence).

**Why it matters:** Confirms whether safety relies on surface keywords rather than semantic harm understanding, central to fuzzy-reward evaluation that needs no lab resources. Pure inference on open models.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2605.31381 finds LLM judges are reliable on overt harm (violence) but unreliable on subtle regulated-domain harm; contamination work shows paraphrase sensitivity; this targets the recognition (not judging) link.
