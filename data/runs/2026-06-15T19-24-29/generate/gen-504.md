# Overconfident exactly when wrong: charting the verbalized-confidence failure curve on open models

**Problem:** A robust failure mode is that LLMs verbalize high confidence precisely on the questions they get wrong. The shape of this anti-calibration curve, and whether it sharpens with instruction-tuning, is worth pinning down on fully open base-vs-instruct pairs where weights and pretraining lineage are transparent.

**Direction:** Take an open base model and its instruct-tuned sibling. On a knowledge benchmark, elicit verbalized confidence and measure accuracy, computing reliability diagrams and ECE for each. Test the specific hypothesis that instruction/RLHF tuning increases confidence-when-wrong relative to the base model, and locate the confidence band (e.g., 80-100%) where miscalibration concentrates.

**Why it matters:** Calibration is a safety-critical, deployment-relevant property and a near-impossibility-flavored question (can stated confidence ever be trusted?). Using open base/instruct pairs isolates the post-training cause without any lab data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Wired for Overconfidence' (arXiv:2604.01457), Dunning-Kruger-in-LLMs (arXiv:2603.09985), and findings that RLHF-tuned models emit 80-100% confidence with ECE >= 0.30.
