# ScaleSteer: A Benchmark for the 'Larger Models Are More Steerable' Claim

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. report larger models are more steerable, but this is asserted as a high-level result without an open, reproducible benchmark across a controlled model-size ladder. Beginners cannot verify the trend on accessible hardware.

**Direction:** Using a single model family with multiple sizes that fit one consumer GPU (e.g. Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B or Pythia ladder), extract the same CAA behavior vectors at matched relative layers and plot steerability (effect size per unit coefficient, normalized by fluency cost) vs. parameter count. Produce a reproducible 'steerability-vs-scale' curve and metric.

**Why it matters:** Tests a load-bearing scaling claim with full reproducibility outside a lab. If steerability scales predictably, monitoring/control tooling can be planned ahead; if not, the field needs to know. Pure inference, fits medium compute.

**Relevant context:** Surprising result 'larger models are more steerable' (Beaglehole et al. Science 2026). Qwen2.5 and Pythia size ladders are open; CAA datasets are open.
