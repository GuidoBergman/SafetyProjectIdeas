# Diversity metric audit: which homogeneity measures actually detect safety-relevant convergence?

**Problem:** Homogeneity is reported via various metrics (embedding cosine similarity, n-gram overlap, distinct-n, self-BLEU). It is unclear which metrics detect *safety-relevant* convergence (shared wrong answers, shared refusals) vs. merely surface-style similarity — a methodological failure that could make homogeneity studies measure the wrong thing.

**Direction:** On a dataset with both surface-similar-but-semantically-diverse outputs and surface-different-but-semantically-identical outputs (constructed via paraphrase), compute several homogeneity metrics. Score each metric on how well it tracks ground-truth semantic/safety convergence. Recommend a robust metric and flag misleading ones.

**Why it matters:** Gives the emerging homogeneity subfield a vetted measurement toolkit so future work (and regulators) don't over- or under-state monoculture risk. A small but foundational methodological contribution requiring no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Methodological audit of the diversity metrics used in Artificial Hivemind and ensemble-diversity work. Embedding computation + paraphrase generation on a consumer GPU.
