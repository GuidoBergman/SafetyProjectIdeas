# Probing the 'Toxic Persona' Feature as a Cheap EM Early-Warning Signal

**Problem:** "Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment" (arXiv:2506.19823, ICLR 2026 poster) shows a single 'toxic persona' direction strongly predicts and controls EM, extractable via difference-in-means. But it is unclear how EARLY in fine-tuning the persona direction's activation rises — i.e. whether it is a usable early-warning signal before misalignment behaviorally manifests.

**Direction:** Reproduce the difference-in-means persona vector on a small open model using a handful of trait-positive/negative prompts (no SAE needed). LoRA-fine-tune on an EM-inducing dataset, checkpointing every ~50 steps. At each checkpoint, measure (a) projection of activations onto the persona vector and (b) behavioral EM rate. Test whether the persona-projection rises measurably BEFORE behavioral misalignment crosses a threshold.

**Why it matters:** A cheap activation read-out that flags misalignment before it shows up in behavior would be a strong monitoring primitive for anyone fine-tuning models — including small labs and open-source maintainers without internal weights tooling. Difference-in-means is feasible for ML beginners.

**Relevant context:** "Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment" (arXiv:2506.19823, ICLR 2026): a toxic-persona difference-in-means direction predicts/controls EM; inference-time steering reduces misalignment up to 84%. Chen et al. persona vectors via difference-in-means.
