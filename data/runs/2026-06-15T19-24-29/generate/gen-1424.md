# SteerCard: A Reproducible Reporting Card for Single Steering Vectors

**Problem:** There is no steering-specific benchmark, and papers report steering vector results in ad-hoc ways (coefficient, layer, behavior) that cannot be compared across studies. A beginner cannot tell whether a published steering result is robust or cherry-picked.

**Direction:** Define a lightweight, standardized 'SteerCard' template that, for any CAA steering vector on an open model (e.g. Llama-2-7B-chat or Qwen2.5-7B), records layer-sweep effectiveness, coefficient-response curve, behavioral effect size, fluency/perplexity cost, and a held-out generalization score. Build it as a small Python harness on top of the existing steering-vectors library and produce SteerCards for 5-6 CAA behaviors (sycophancy, refusal, corrigibility, hallucination, survival instinct).

**Why it matters:** Standardized reporting is the cheapest path to making representation engineering results trustworthy and reproducible outside a major lab. It surfaces over-claiming and gives the field a shared currency for comparing steering methods, which currently does not exist.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. (Science 2026) note no steering-specific benchmarks exist. The nrimsky/CAA and steering-vectors libraries provide ready contrastive datasets (sycophancy, refusal, corrigibility, hallucination, survival instinct) with 50 test samples each.
