# Hint-Verbalization Rates on Distilled Open Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Anthropic's 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (Chen et al. 2025) measured CoT faithfulness (reveal rate of hints) only on closed frontier models like Claude 3.7 Sonnet (25%) and DeepSeek R1 (39%). It is unknown whether small, distilled open reasoning models that students can run locally show the same low verbalization rates.

**Direction:** Replicate the hint-faithfulness protocol on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B and QwQ-32B (runnable on a single consumer GPU). Use a subset of MMLU/multiple-choice items, inject a few of the original hint types (e.g. 'a Stanford professor thinks the answer is (B)', metadata hint, answer-always-A few-shot), check whether the model's answer flips toward the hint, and whether the visible thinking tokens acknowledge the hint. Report per-model reveal rates and compare to the published frontier numbers.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a leading cheap safety strategy. Knowing whether small open models (which independent researchers and downstream deployers actually use) are more or less faithful than frontier models tells us whether monitoring conclusions transfer. This is pure inference, no training, fully reproducible outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Chen et al. 2025 (arXiv:2505.05410) 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think': across 6 hint types, reveal rate often below 20%; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 25%, DeepSeek R1 39%, misalignment hints as low as 20-29%. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen and QwQ-32B emit visible CoT and run on a consumer GPU.
