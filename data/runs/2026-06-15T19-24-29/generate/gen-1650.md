# Tuned Lens Replicated on a State-Space (Mamba) vs Transformer Model

**Problem:** A follow-up to the tuned lens asked whether transformer interpretability transfers to RNNs. State-space models (Mamba) are now available as small open checkpoints, but the simple question of whether logit-lens-style intermediate prediction readout works at all on a small Mamba — and how its layer-wise prediction trajectory compares to a similar-size transformer — is rarely examined hands-on.

**Direction:** Take a small open Mamba checkpoint and a similarly sized transformer. Replicate the logit-lens / tuned-lens intermediate-prediction analysis: project each layer's hidden state to vocabulary (training a small linear translator if needed) and measure per-layer KL to the final output. The twist: compare the shape of the 'prediction emerges over depth' curve between the SSM and the transformer.

**Why it matters:** Interpretability tooling is overwhelmingly transformer-centric, but alternative architectures (SSMs) are gaining adoption outside frontier labs. Testing whether basic mechanistic-readout methods transfer to a different architecture — a single-GPU study on public checkpoints — helps ensure interpretability-based safety monitoring does not silently fail when the architecture changes.

**Relevant context:** 'Does Transformer Interpretability Transfer to RNNs?' (arXiv:2404.05971) examined lens/probing transfer to RNN-like models. Belrose et al. tuned lens (arXiv:2303.08112) provides the readout methodology. Small Mamba checkpoints are publicly available on HuggingFace.
