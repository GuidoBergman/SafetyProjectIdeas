# Low-Resource Language Gap x Autograder Blindness: Double-Counting Safety in Bengali

**Problem:** Failure mode A is the multilingual safety gap (guardrails degrade in low-resource languages). Failure mode B is autograder blindness in those same languages (the safety judge is itself worse at non-English). The compound effect: harmful completions in a low-resource language slip past the model AND past the grader, so the measured harm rate is doubly understated.

**Direction:** Translate a small harmful-prompt set into 2-3 low-resource languages. Collect open-model completions, then have an LLM judge label harm in (a) the original language and (b) an English back-translation. Compare judge agreement and harm-detection rate across languages, isolating how much of the apparent safety in low-resource languages is real vs grader failure.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual safety scores are increasingly used for global deployment claims. If both the model and the grader are weaker in a language, scores there are unreliable in a self-reinforcing way. The result tells evaluators to back-translate before grading and to report grader-confidence per language.

**Relevant context:** Low-Resource Languages Jailbreak GPT-4 (Brown CS); LinguaSafe (arXiv:2508.12733); 'Why Do Safety Guardrails Degrade Across Languages?' (arXiv:2605.17173) reports >45% inconsistent safety labels across languages. Subfield context lists multimodal/multilingual gaps as open problems.
