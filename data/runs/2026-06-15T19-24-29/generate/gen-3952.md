# Mapping the Coherence Frontier: How Far Do LLM Probabilities Violate the Axioms?

**Problem:** Logical induction and Bayesian alignment frameworks assume agents that are approximately coherent (probabilities of mutually exclusive events sum to <=1, conditionals factorize). Real LLM-elicited probabilities are known to violate these, but the violation is rarely quantified as a function of logical structure.

**Direction:** Build sets of propositions with known logical relations (mutual exclusion, implication, conjunction). Elicit numeric probabilities from open models for atoms, negations, conjunctions, and conditionals. Compute axiom-violation metrics (e.g. additivity error, conjunction-fallacy rate, conditional-product error) and chart how violations grow with logical depth and number of clauses.

**Why it matters:** Logical-induction-style guarantees only bite if the agent is near-coherent; measuring the coherence frontier of today's models grounds the gap between idealized agent foundations and reality. Inference-only and trivially reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Garrabrant et al. 'Logical Induction' (intelligence.org): good reasoners are approximately coherent and calibrated. 'Measuring, Evaluating and Improving Logical Consistency in LLMs' (arXiv 2410.02205) documents transitivity/commutativity failures.
