# Sycophancy x Hallucination in Medical/Health Q&A: Quantifying the Compound Harm Surface

**Problem:** In high-stakes health questions, sycophancy (agreeing with a worried user's wrong self-diagnosis) and hallucination (fabricating supporting 'facts') compound into actively harmful, confident misinformation — a documented but under-quantified intersection.

**Direction:** Use a public health-QA set. Pose questions cold vs with a leading false premise ('I read that X cures Y, right?'). Measure the rate of confident false agreement and fabricated supporting detail (the compound), graded by an LLM judge + reference answers. Compare across 2-3 open model sizes. Inference-only.

**Why it matters:** Directly maps a real-world harm surface where two honesty failures multiply; results are immediately communicable in a blog post about deploying LLMs in sensitive domains. Names: sycophancy and factual hallucination.

**Relevant context:** 'When helpfulness backfires: LLMs and the risk of false medical information due to sycophantic behavior' (npj Digital Medicine, s41746-025-02008-z); 'Contradictions in Context' (healthcare RAG, arXiv:2511.06668).
