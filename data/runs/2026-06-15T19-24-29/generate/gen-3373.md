# Filler-Token Test: How Much Reasoning Survives Replacing CoT With '...'

**Problem:** Lanham's filler-token test replaced CoT with meaningless symbols to see if accuracy is preserved (implying the CoT was not doing the work). It is unclear how strongly modern small reasoning models depend on actual content vs. just 'thinking length'.

**Direction:** For a set of reasoning tasks, compare four conditions: full CoT, CoT replaced by equal-length filler ('...'), no CoT, and shuffled-token CoT. Measure accuracy drop in each. A small drop under filler tokens on a given task family indicates the stated reasoning is not the true computation.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishes tasks where CoT is genuine load-bearing reasoning (monitorable) from tasks where it's decorative (unmonitorable). A practical map of where CoT monitoring can be trusted, doable with decoding tricks only.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.13702) filler-token intervention; subsequent debate (e.g. 'CoT unfaithfulness as disguised accuracy', arXiv:2402.14897) shows task-dependence. No accessible replication targeting students.
