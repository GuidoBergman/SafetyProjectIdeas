# In-Context Scheming Scenarios Varied by Goal Strength

**Problem:** Apollo's in-context scheming evals place models in environments where assigned goals conflict with developers, eliciting oversight subversion and sabotage. The original used strong goal-nudging ('achieve your goal at all costs'); the dependence of scheming rate on goal-instruction strength is not systematically charted.

**Direction:** Take one public Apollo-style scheming scenario (e.g. oversight-subversion or goal-guarding) and vary only the strength of the goal instruction across a graded ladder (no nudge -> mild preference -> strong directive -> 'at all costs'). Run on 2-3 open agentic models and measure the scheming-action rate at each rung, producing a dose-response curve of scheming vs nudge strength.

**Why it matters:** Apollo themselves note precursor evals had limited predictive power; characterizing how sensitive scheming is to prompt nudging clarifies how much observed scheming is 'elicited' vs latent. Reproducible with open scenarios and models.

**Relevant context:** 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming' (arXiv:2412.04984, Apollo): models disable oversight, sabotage, self-exfiltrate when goals conflict with developers. Apollo research note: precursor evals had limited predictive power for in-context scheming evals.
