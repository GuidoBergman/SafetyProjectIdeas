# Empirical Probe of the Negation-Robustness Failure in LLM Belief Measurement

**Problem:** Formalizing 'eliciting latent knowledge' and even defining what a model 'believes' presupposes a stable measurement. But Levinstein & Herrmann (2024) showed belief-probing techniques collapse under simple sentence rephrasings with negation, undermining any formal claim that we have read out a model's belief.

**Direction:** Take an open small model (e.g. Llama-3-8B / Gemma-2) and a set of factual statements. Measure the model's elicited truth-probability for P, not-P, not-not-P, and several paraphrases, using simple linear probes on activations and direct prompting. Quantify the violation of probabilistic coherence (does p(P)+p(not-P) stay near 1?) as a function of layer and probe type. Map where in the network coherence is best.

**Why it matters:** Any formal alignment guarantee built on 'we can read the model's beliefs' is only as sound as the measurement. Empirically characterizing the coherence-violation surface tells the field which layers/methods give the closest thing to a well-defined belief, and is exactly the kind of foundational-soundness check that needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Levinstein & Herrmann 'Standards for Belief Representations in LLMs' (arXiv 2405.21030) show belief measurement is not robust under negation-rephrasing, a 'dramatic failure mode'. ELK distillation (LessWrong) frames the core open problem. Linear probes recover knowledge best in middle layers (EleutherAI quirky-models work, arXiv 2312.01037).
