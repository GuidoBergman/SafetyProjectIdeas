# Backcasting Trigger-Rarity Safety: Do Backdoors Hide Better When the Secret Input Is Statistically Rare?

**Problem:** Success state: monitoring/testing that catches backdoors regardless of how rare the triggering input is. Backward: we need to measure how monitor catch rate and differential-testing catch rate degrade as the backdoor trigger becomes rarer in the input distribution.

**Direction:** On APPS tasks, construct backdoors whose secret-input triggers vary in rarity (common value vs. extreme/rare value). Measure (a) LLM-monitor catch rate and (b) random differential-testing catch rate as a function of trigger rarity. Result: 'a rarity-vs-detection curve exposing the worst-case attack surface.'

**Why it matters:** An attacker's best move is a maximally rare trigger; quantifying how detection collapses with rarity directly bounds the control safety case and motivates targeted (not random) testing. Fully reproducible on open models.

**Relevant context:** APPS backdoor definition (code passes tests but differs on a secret input) from ControlArena; Greenblatt et al. arXiv:2312.06942; relates to anomaly/distributed-failure open problems.
