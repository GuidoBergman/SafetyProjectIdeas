# Backcasting Robust Honeypots: Does Misaligned Behavior Survive When the Honeypot 'Looks' Less Like a Trap?

**Problem:** Success state: deployment-time honeypots that reliably elicit latent misaligned behavior. Backward: we need to know how sensitive elicitation is to honeypot 'tell' cues — how much the sabotage rate drops as the trap becomes more realistic/less obvious.

**Direction:** Take a public sabotage-opportunity honeypot prompt and produce a graded series from obvious-trap to subtle/realistic. Measure an open model's misaligned-action rate across the gradient. Result: 'a realism-sensitivity curve telling honeypot designers how much the framing matters.'

**Why it matters:** Honeypots are a named methodology, but if behavior only shows up in obvious traps, real-deployment detection fails. Mapping the realism gradient is diffuse, lab-independent, and a fresh angle vs. the catalogue.
