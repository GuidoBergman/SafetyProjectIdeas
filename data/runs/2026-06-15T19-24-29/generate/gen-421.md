# Easy-to-hard generalization of truth probes: stress-testing the 75% recovery result

**Problem:** The quirky-models ELK work reported that the best probe recovers 89% of the truthful-vs-untruthful AUROC gap, but only 75% for questions harder than those used to train the probe. The shape of this easy-to-hard degradation — and what features of 'hardness' drive it — is the crux of whether probing scales to questions humans can't evaluate.

**Direction:** Using the quirky-models setup, train probes on 'easy' questions and evaluate on graded difficulty tiers, decomposing 'hard' along several axes (longer reasoning chains, rarer entities, more compositional). Plot recovery vs. each hardness axis to identify which kind of hardness most degrades probe transfer, and test whether ensembling probe layers helps.

**Why it matters:** ELK's whole point is eliciting knowledge on questions beyond human supervision — i.e. hard ones. Decomposing which hardness breaks probe transfer directly informs the formal ELK agenda and is doable on open models in 30h with no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2312.01037 (quirky models, 89%/75% recovery figures) and EleutherAI/elk-generalization. Context open problem: 'formal frameworks for eliciting latent knowledge (ELK)'.
