# Easy-to-Hard Reward Model Generalization Outside Math

**Problem:** Sun et al. (NeurIPS 2024) showed a reward model trained on easy MATH problems (levels 1-3) can score solutions to hard problems (levels 4-5). It is untested whether this easy-to-hard evaluator generalization holds in a non-math reasoning domain with its own easy/hard gradient.

**Direction:** Replicate the easy-to-hard evaluator idea on a domain with a natural difficulty grading, e.g. logical-deduction puzzles or graded reading-comprehension questions. Use a small open reward/scoring model trained (or few-shot prompted, to stay in 30h) on easy items, then measure its accuracy at ranking candidate answers to hard items, comparing the easy-trained evaluator to a hard-trained ceiling and an easy-only floor.

**Why it matters:** Easy-to-hard generalization is a core scalable-oversight hope: supervise on what humans can label, deploy on what they cannot. Knowing whether the MATH result is domain-specific or general is high-value and currently unknown. Can be scoped to few-shot scoring to avoid heavy training.

**Relevant context:** Sun et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.09472): process-supervised reward models trained on easy problems effectively score hard problems; 7B RL model hit 34% on MATH500 using only easy supervision. The domain-specificity of this evaluator transfer is an open question explicitly flagged as a generation hint (easy-to-hard methods).
