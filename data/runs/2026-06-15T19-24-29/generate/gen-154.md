# Quantization as an Accidental Jailbreak: Robustness vs Bit-Width

**Problem:** Small open models are routinely run quantized (8-bit, 4-bit, GGUF) for consumer GPUs, but whether aggressive quantization degrades safety alignment — itself a shallow, fragile property — more than it degrades general capability is largely unmeasured.

**Direction:** Take one open instruct model and evaluate it at fp16, int8, and int4 quantization. For each precision, measure (a) refusal robustness on StrongREJECT and prefill attacks and (b) general capability on a small benchmark. Test whether quantization preferentially erodes the first-token refusal behavior predicted by shallow alignment.

**Why it matters:** Quantization is ubiquitous in the open/local-LLM community; if it silently weakens safety while preserving capability, every consumer deployment is more jailbreakable than the released model. A medium-horizon, deployment-realistic risk perfectly suited to consumer-GPU study.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) show safety is concentrated in fragile first-token behavior, plausibly more sensitive to weight perturbation than diffuse capability. Quantization tooling (bitsandbytes, GGUF) is standard and free; StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) supplies the safety metric.
