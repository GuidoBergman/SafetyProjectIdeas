# Geometric canary: can a cheap cosine statistic predict if a safety vector will work?

**Problem:** Recent work identifies geometric predictors of steering reliability — low directional agreement and low positive/negative separation predict an ineffective steering direction. These predictors have not been validated as a practical pre-flight check across many safety concepts.

**Direction:** For ~15 candidate safety/style concepts, build CAA vectors on one open model and compute two cheap geometric statistics (mean cosine agreement of activation-difference pairs; positive-negative cluster separation). Then measure actual steering effectiveness behaviorally. Test whether the geometric statistics correlate with and predict real effectiveness (rank correlation), yielding a usable 'don't bother steering this' threshold.

**Why it matters:** Beginners waste hours steering concepts that have no linear representation. A validated, training-free pre-flight predictor saves compute and tells the field which safety concepts are even steerable — directly answering an open problem about which concepts are linear.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Understanding (Un)Reliability of Steering Vectors: Geometric Predictors and the Limits of Linear Approximations' (arXiv:2602.17881, ~Feb 2026) and 'The Geometric Canary' (arXiv:2604.17698, ~Apr 2026).
