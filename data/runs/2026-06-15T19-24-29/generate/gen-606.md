# Vingean Reflection Toy Model: Compounding Verification Cost with Successor Capability Gain

**Problem:** The Vingean reflection problem (agent foundations) asks how an agent can trust a successor that may be smarter. A compounding-risk framing pairs it with verification scalability failure (formal verification subfield): as a successor's capability rises, the cost/feasibility of formally verifying its behavior may rise faster, so trust gets harder exactly as stakes grow. There is no simple empirical illustration of this trade-off.

**Direction:** Build a tiny computational testbed where a weaker 'overseer' policy must verify proposed solutions from progressively stronger 'successor' solvers on a checkable task family (e.g., constraint/SAT-style puzzles where solving is hard but checking is easy vs. a variant where checking also scales). Measure how the overseer's reliable-acceptance rate degrades as successor strength grows, and identify task structures where verification cost outpaces capability. Pure simulation, runs on CPU/single GPU.

**Why it matters:** Vingean reflection is a flagship open problem flagged in the subfield context. A concrete, runnable toy that visualizes when verifiability collapses relative to capability gives beginners a tangible handle on an abstract limit and a teaching artifact for the community. Needs no large models.
