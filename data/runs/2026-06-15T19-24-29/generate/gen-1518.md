# Does the hallucination probe fail precisely on confident hallucinations?

**Problem:** Hallucination-monitoring chain: model fabricates -> internal 'unsupported' signal -> probe flags. Probes report high AUROC overall, but the dangerous link is CONFIDENT hallucinations — fabrications the model states fluently and confidently. If the probe's errors concentrate there, the overall AUROC is misleading. This error-stratification link is rarely examined.

**Direction:** Use an open model and a published factuality/hallucination setup. Build a linear hallucination probe. Then stratify the model's hallucinations by the model's own expressed/estimated confidence (e.g., token logprob of the claim) and measure probe detection rate within each confidence bucket. Success criterion: probe recall as a function of hallucination confidence, showing whether confident fabrications are the blind spot.

**Why it matters:** Confident hallucinations are the ones that mislead users and downstream systems; a monitor that only catches hesitant ones gives false assurance. Stratifying the evaluation by confidence is a cheap, high-value refinement of the Science paper's headline result.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. report probes outperform judges on hallucination; 'Real-Time Detection of Hallucinated Entities' (arXiv 2509.03531) and factuality-probe papers (ACL 2025 findings 880) provide methodology. Confidence-stratified failure analysis is the new angle.
