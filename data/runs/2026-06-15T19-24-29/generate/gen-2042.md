# Intervening on the Unlearning->Relearning Link: How Few Examples Reopen Forgotten Knowledge

**Problem:** The safety-relevant chain for unlearning is: dangerous knowledge removed -> deployed model is 'safe' -> attacker fine-tunes on a few examples -> knowledge relearned. The fragile link is relearning. Prior work says 'a single epoch on a few forget samples' can revert unlearning, but the quantitative dose (how many examples, how related they must be) is under-characterized for accessible open models.

**Direction:** Take a small open model with a published unlearning benchmark (e.g., a WMDP/TOFU-style forget set), apply a simple open unlearning method (gradient ascent or a published checkpoint), then run a relearning attack with 1, 4, 16, 64 forget examples and with unrelated benign examples. Plot recovery-of-forgotten-knowledge vs. number/relatedness of examples to map exactly where the link breaks.

**Why it matters:** Unlearning is proposed as a safety guarantee for dangerous capabilities. If relearning needs only a handful of examples, the guarantee is illusory under any fine-tuning access. A clean dose curve quantifies the safety margin and is doable with no proprietary resources.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Machine Unlearning for AI Safety (arxiv 2501.04952): all existing unlearning techniques easily reversed by fine-tuning. 'Towards LLM Unlearning Resilient to Relearning Attacks' (2502.05374) and 'Over-Unlearning and Prototypical Relearning Attack' (2506.01318) document few-shot reversion.
