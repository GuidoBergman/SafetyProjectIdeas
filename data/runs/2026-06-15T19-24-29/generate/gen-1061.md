# Do Models Refuse the Same Prompts? Mapping the Shared Refusal Boundary

**Problem:** Refusal research suggests models share internal refusal directions, implying their refusal BEHAVIOR may be correlated. A direct behavioral follow-up: on borderline prompts, do independent models refuse/comply in lockstep, or is the refusal boundary idiosyncratic?

**Direction:** Build a graded set of borderline prompts (clearly-fine -> ambiguous -> clearly-harmful). Run ~10 open models, label each response as refuse/partial/comply, and compute inter-model agreement on the refusal decision at each graded level. Identify prompts where ALL models share the same (possibly wrong) decision — the correlated over- and under-refusal zones.

**Why it matters:** Correlated over-refusal hurts usefulness uniformly; correlated under-refusal means a single jailbreak class works everywhere. Both are systemic. Behavioral only, no internals needed.

**Relevant context:** Shared refusal representations (jailbreak transfer work, arXiv:2506.12913); correlated-failure framing in model homogeneity.
