# Confidence Inflation in Debate: Does Arguing a Side Make a Model More Sure It's Right?

**Problem:** In debate, each model is assigned a side and argues it hard. Causal chain: model assigned a (possibly false) side -> generates committed arguments -> commitment inflates the model's own stated confidence -> a downstream judge or aggregator over-weights a confidently-wrong debater. The under-studied link is whether role-assignment itself distorts a debater's calibration.

**Direction:** Ask an open model for its calibrated answer+confidence on a set of factual questions cold. Then assign it (randomly) to argue one side and re-elicit its confidence after producing the argument. Measure the confidence shift attributable purely to role assignment, separately for cases where it was assigned the correct vs. incorrect side.

**Why it matters:** If role assignment inflates confidence on wrong sides, confidence-aware aggregation of debaters is dangerous. A clean, cheap behavioral result on a real oversight protocol, no lab resources needed.

**Relevant context:** Debate protocols (Khan et al. 2024 arXiv:2402.06782; Kenton et al. 2024 arXiv:2407.04622) assign debaters fixed sides; sycophancy/role-conditioning literature shows behavior shifts under role framing. The calibration distortion from forced advocacy is under-measured.
