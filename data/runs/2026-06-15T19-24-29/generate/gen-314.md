# Probe Generalization Across the Attack-Family Link: Train on One, Test on Another

**Problem:** Activation-probe defenses assume one chain: harmful intent -> shared activation signature -> probe fires. But recent results suggest different jailbreaks are supported by DISTINCT internal mechanisms, breaking the shared-signature assumption. The under-studied link is how badly a probe trained on one attack family fails on unseen families.

**Direction:** Collect activations from an open model under several attack families (suffix, roleplay, encoding, prefilling). Train a linear probe on one family and evaluate cross-family transfer for all train/test pairs, producing a transfer matrix that reveals whether a single probe can defend the whole attack chain or only its training family.

**Why it matters:** Tests the core generalization assumption behind the cheap-probe defense hyped in Jan 2026 digests, with direct implications for whether activation monitoring is deployable. Open model + simple linear probes, no training of the base model, single GPU; high counterfactual value studying a defense's blind spots.

**Relevant context:** 2025 probing literature (Latent Adversarial Detection arXiv:2604.28129; arXiv:2604.13386) reports probes achieve strong in-distribution accuracy but transfer is attack-family-specific, and different jailbreaks may have distinct internal mechanisms. Subfield context highlights cheap activation-probe detection as a hot result. (Distinct from prior 'Activation Probe Generalization' by building a full cross-family transfer matrix tied to the distinct-mechanism finding.)
