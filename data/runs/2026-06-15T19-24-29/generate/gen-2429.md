# Easy-to-Hard Evaluator Transfer Without Training: A Prompted Reward-Model Probe

**Problem:** Easy-to-Hard Generalization (arXiv:2403.09472) shows an evaluator trained on easy MATH problems scores hard-problem solutions well. Whether the same easy-to-hard evaluation transfer appears in a training-free, prompted LLM-as-evaluator (no reward-model training) is open and accessible.

**Direction:** Use a prompted open model as an evaluator, calibrate/few-shot it only on easy (level 1-3) MATH problems, then test its solution-scoring accuracy on hard (level 4-5) problems. Measure how scoring reliability degrades with difficulty and whether easy-only calibration still ranks hard solutions usefully.

**Why it matters:** If overseers calibrated on tasks they CAN judge still rank harder tasks they CANNOT directly solve, that supports the core scalable-oversight hope. A negative result is equally informative. No training required.

**Relevant context:** Easy-to-Hard Generalization: Scalable Alignment Beyond Human Supervision (arXiv:2403.09472): an evaluator trained on easier tasks scores candidate solutions of harder tasks effectively, enabling easy-to-hard generalization via reranking/RL with only easy-task human supervision.
