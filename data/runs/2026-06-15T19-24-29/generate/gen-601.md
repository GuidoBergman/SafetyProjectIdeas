# When ELK Probes Meet Distribution Shift: Compounding Latent-Knowledge Failure and Goal Misgeneralization

**Problem:** Formal ELK frameworks assume a probe that 'robustly tracks the true state of the world' can recover a model's knowledge even when its outputs lie. But probes (interpretability subfield) are known to be brittle under distribution shift, and goal misgeneralization (robustness subfield) is precisely a competence-under-shift failure. The compound failure mode is: a model that has goal-misgeneralized off-distribution is exactly when we most need ELK, yet that same shift degrades the probe. Nobody has measured how these two failures interact on a controlled benchmark.

**Direction:** Use EleutherAI's released 'quirky' language models and the 12 quirky datasets (open, no training). Train linear/contrast-pair probes on the easy/in-distribution split, then evaluate probe AUROC specifically on the hardest, most out-of-distribution slices where the quirky model's behavior diverges most. Quantify the joint degradation: plot probe reliability against a behavioral-shift metric to show whether ELK fails worst precisely where behavior is most anomalous. Report a 'compound failure curve'.

**Why it matters:** ELK is a load-bearing formal proposal for oversight. If the empirical evidence shows the probe's reliability and the model's behavioral correctness fail together (rather than independently), then ELK cannot be the safety net it is hoped to be in exactly the deployment regime that matters. This is a counterfactually valuable critique doable entirely on released open models with no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** EleutherAI/elk-generalization repo and 'Eliciting Latent Knowledge from Quirky Language Models' (arXiv:2312.01037) report 89% gap recovery in-distribution but only 75% on harder questions; ELK is an ARC open problem in the subfield context.
