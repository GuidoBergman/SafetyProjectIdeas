# Schelling-Point Coordination Without Communication as a Collusion Probe

**Problem:** A long-horizon scheming worry is models that coordinate covertly. A tractable, neglected slice: how good are current models at unspoken Schelling-point coordination (picking the same answer as a hypothetical partner without communicating), and does coordination ability outpace stated reasoning — a sign of un-verbalized strategy?

**Direction:** Run classic Schelling coordination games (choose a number, a meeting place, split a pie) with two instances of the same model, no communication, scoring whether they converge. Compare convergence rate to a random baseline and to what each model *says* it would expect. Vary model family and game type. Flag cases where coordination succeeds but the CoT gives no coordinating rationale.

**Why it matters:** Covert coordination capability is a building block for multi-agent collusion that defeats oversight — a >1-year threat labs underweight. A cheap, game-theoretic, fully reproducible probe is exactly the kind of model-organism evaluation suited to independent researchers.

**Relevant context:** Subfield steering explicitly suggests model organisms 'good at Schelling games' and 'attacks without verbalizing its plan in the CoT.' Apollo in-context scheming (arXiv:2412.04984) shows covert goal pursuit; collusion is a recognized AI-control concern (Redwood/UK AISI).
