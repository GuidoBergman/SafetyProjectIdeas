# Position of the Harmful Demonstration in Many-Shot Attacks

**Problem:** Many-shot jailbreaking varies the number of harmful demonstrations, but not their arrangement. Whether placing the strongest harmful examples early, late, or interleaved with benign ones changes attack success at fixed shot count is an untested variation.

**Direction:** Hold shot count fixed (e.g. 64) and vary demonstration ordering: harmful-front-loaded, harmful-back-loaded, interleaved, and graded-escalation. Measure attack-success across orderings on small open long-context models, isolating recency/primacy effects in the jailbreak.

**Why it matters:** If ordering matters, defenders could disrupt many-shot attacks by shuffling or summarizing context, and the result clarifies the in-context-learning mechanism behind the attack. Cheap inference experiment on open models extending a flagship result with a new controlled variable.

**Relevant context:** Anil et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024) characterized the power law in shot count but treated demonstrations as exchangeable; ordering is an unexamined dimension. StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) scores outcomes.
