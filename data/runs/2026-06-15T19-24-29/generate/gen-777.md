# Replicating Safety-Leaderboard Gaming on a Small Scale to Demonstrate Benchmark Fragility for Auditors

**Problem:** Research warns that fully-open safety benchmarks (like HELM) can be gamed: even small models fine-tuned on the public eval data can top specific scenarios. Governance increasingly cites leaderboard scores, yet there's little accessible, reproducible demonstration of how easily a determined developer could inflate a safety score without genuinely improving safety.

**Direction:** Replicate the leaderboard-gaming finding at small scale: take a public safety-eval subset, lightly tune or prompt-engineer a small open model specifically against that subset, then evaluate on a held-out disjoint safety set. The twist is contrasting in-distribution (gamed) vs. out-of-distribution safety scores to expose the inflation, framed as an auditor's red-flag checklist.

**Why it matters:** Third-party verification of safety claims (a top open problem) is undermined if scores are gameable. A clear, reproducible demonstration plus auditor guidance is directly useful and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Pitfalls of Evaluating Language Models with Open Benchmarks' (arXiv 2507.00460) and HELM gaming concerns (small cheating models topping scenarios). Twist = small-scale reproducible demo + auditor checklist.
