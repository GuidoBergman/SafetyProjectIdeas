# Backtranslation Robustness as a Cross-Lingual Safety Validity Check

**Problem:** A success state requires that safety holds across languages and translation, not just polished English. A needed result is a measure of how much a safety verdict survives translate-and-backtranslate of the prompt.

**Direction:** Take English safety prompts, translate to a few languages and back with an open translation model, and measure how often the target model's refusal/safety verdict changes versus the original. Deliverable: a backtranslation-robustness score and the categories most fragile to translation.

**Why it matters:** Cross-lingual safety gaps are a known weakness; a translate/backtranslate stress-test is a cheap validity check that needs only inference and open models, no lab data. Distinct from prior cross-lingual gap-mapping because it isolates translation-induced verdict instability as a benchmark-validity issue.

**Relevant context:** SORRY-Bench (ICLR 2025) includes linguistic mutations; multilingual safety gaps documented in TrustLLM and HELM Safety (context file key benchmarks); prompt-sensitivity work (NAACL 2025) on paraphrase-induced instability.
