# Legibility Tax: Do Verifier-Accepted Explanations Help Weaker Verifiers?

**Problem:** Prover-Verifier Games (OpenAI 2024) show legibility training transfers to time-limited humans, but it is unknown whether explanations optimized to be legible to one verifier generalize to verifiers *weaker* than the training verifier — the realistic oversight case.

**Direction:** Take GSM8K solutions (legible vs terse correct, plus 'sneaky' incorrect ones from the released setup) and measure verification accuracy of a *ladder* of progressively weaker open judge models. Does legibility's benefit shrink or hold as the verifier weakens? Does it help weak verifiers reject sneaky-but-fluent wrong proofs?

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight needs explanations legible to overseers genuinely weaker than the prover. Testing transfer down the capability ladder probes whether legibility is a real oversight tool or an artifact of a strong verifier. No training needed.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. (2024, arXiv:2407.13692) 'Prover-Verifier Games improve legibility of LLM outputs' — legibility transfers to time-constrained humans; helpful vs sneaky provers on GSM8K with open setup.
