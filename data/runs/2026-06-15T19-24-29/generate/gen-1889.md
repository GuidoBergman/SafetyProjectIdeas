# Refusal-Direction Suppression: Single vs Multi-Direction on Small Models

**Problem:** Refusal is mediated 'by a single direction', but follow-up work shows there is more than one direction and that multi-directional suppression is stronger. How much extra jailbreak success multi-direction suppression buys over single-direction, on small open models a beginner can run, is a clean reproducible question.

**Direction:** Vary the refusal-direction experiment by comparing 1-direction vs k-direction suppression. On Qwen2.5-7B/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, extract refusal directions via difference-of-means, then ablate the top-1 vs top-k directions and measure harmful-compliance rate on a small harmful-request set (using a safe, clearly-illegal-info-free proxy set) plus over-refusal on benign requests. Plot the safety/utility tradeoff vs k.

**Why it matters:** Refusal mechanisms in open-weight models are a primary safety layer; quantifying how few directions an adversary must remove tells us how brittle that layer is. Inference + simple linear algebra (within the participant's skill set), single GPU, fully reproducible, no lab tooling.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 'Refusal in LMs is Mediated by a Single Direction' (NeurIPS 2024). 'There Is More to Refusal Than a Single Direction' (arXiv:2602.02132) and 'SOM Directions are Better than One' (arXiv:2511.08379): multi-directional suppression outperforms single direction.
