# Are 'Surprising' Minority Answers Higher Quality? Auditing the Diversity-Quality Tradeoff

**Problem:** Hivemind argues RLHF prunes valid idiosyncratic responses, but this 'valid minority' claim is asserted more than measured on safety content. If minority (low-homogeneity) responses are actually better/safer, current alignment is destroying value.

**Direction:** For each prompt, identify the consensus (modal) response and the most-divergent minority responses across models. Have a rubric-based blind evaluation (human spot-check + LLM-judge with a defined quality/safety rubric) rate consensus vs. minority responses. Test whether minority responses are worse, equal, or sometimes better — especially on safety-relevant prompts.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes whether the homogenizing force of alignment removes genuinely good safety-relevant answers, informing diversity-preserving objectives. Public models + clear rubric.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind claim that alignment prunes valid idiosyncratic responses; typicality bias in preference data (Verbalized Sampling, arXiv:2510.01171).
