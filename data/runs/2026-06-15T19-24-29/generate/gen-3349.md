# Confession Calibration: Can Models Honestly Flag Their Own Wrong Answers?

**Problem:** LLMs produce confident wrong answers because scoring conventions reward guessing over abstention. Whether a model's post-hoc 'confession' (a follow-up self-review) is well-calibrated to actual correctness — and whether confession beats first-pass verbalized confidence — is underexplored on open models.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA-style short-fact questions, collect (a) the answer, (b) first-pass verbalized confidence 0-1, and (c) a second-turn 'confession' where the model reviews its own answer and estimates correctness. Compute calibration (ECE, reliability diagrams) for (b) vs (c) and measure how much confession recovers honesty.

**Why it matters:** Honest uncertainty is a core honesty property; a cheap protocol showing whether self-confession improves calibration is directly useful for building abstention-based safety layers without retraining.

**Relevant context:** 'Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions' (arXiv 2512.08093) extracts self-reported confidence in 0-1 on 625 SimpleQA validation questions. 'Calibrating LLM Confidence by Probing Perturbed Representation Stability' (arXiv 2505.21772) and Nature (s41586-026-10549-w) show accuracy-incentivized scoring drives hallucination. Verbalized confidence is reportedly stable under paraphrase.
