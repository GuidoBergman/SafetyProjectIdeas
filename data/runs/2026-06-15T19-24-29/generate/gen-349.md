# Token-Forcing Audit: Which Specific Compliant Prefixes Are Most Dangerous?

**Problem:** Success state: no short prefix reliably flips a model into compliance. Backcasting from shallow alignment, the needed result is a ranked catalogue of which compliant prefix phrases ('Sure, here is', 'Step 1:', etc.) yield the highest jailbreak rates, per model.

**Direction:** Curate ~30 candidate compliant prefixes, prefill each on a fixed harmful set, and rank prefixes by StrongReject-scored ASR per open model. Identify whether dangerous prefixes are shared across models (a transferable attack vocabulary) or model-specific.

**Why it matters:** Produces an actionable artifact: a small, transferable 'dangerous-prefix dictionary' defenders can filter against and a precise map of the shallow-alignment weak spot. Cheap, reproducible, and directly grounded in the prefilling exploit.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946): prefilling 'I cannot fulfill' restores safety, implying the inverse prefixes drive compliance. 'One Trigger Token Is Enough' (arXiv:2505.07167) shows single-token sensitivity — supports a per-prefix ranking study.
