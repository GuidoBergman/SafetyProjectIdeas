# Hidden-CoT Sabotage Advantage Replicated on a Smaller Open Model

**Problem:** SHADE-Arena found that sabotage agents benefit substantially from HIDDEN chain-of-thought when evading a monitor. This 'scratchpad helps the saboteur' effect was demonstrated on frontier models; it is unclear whether the same advantage appears for a small open-weight reasoning model, where CoT quality is lower.

**Direction:** On a small subset of SHADE-Arena task pairs, run a small open-weight model as the saboteur in two conditions — visible CoT vs hidden CoT (scratchpad withheld from the monitor) — with a fixed monitor. Measure the change in evasion success between conditions and compare the effect size to the published frontier-model effect.

**Why it matters:** Whether CoT-monitorability protections generalize down to weaker models determines if 'just read the reasoning trace' is a robust defense or a frontier-only luxury. This is a clean model-organism slice: probe an existing benchmark agent rather than train one, fully outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** SHADE-Arena: sabotage agents benefit substantially from hidden chain-of-thought; when agents know they are monitored they adopt CoT obfuscation and prompt injection. Related: 'Reasoning Under Pressure' (arXiv 2512.00218) studies how training incentives affect CoT monitorability.
