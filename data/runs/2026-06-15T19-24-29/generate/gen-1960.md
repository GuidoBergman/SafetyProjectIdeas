# Prompt-Attack Probe Battery: Measuring Suppression vs. Removal in Unlearned Models

**Problem:** Unlearning is often only superficial: the same knowledge resurfaces under rephrasing, role-play, or multilingual prompts. Existing benchmarks score one fixed MCQ format, so they conflate genuine removal with context-specific suppression.

**Direction:** Create a standardized battery of N prompt transformations (paraphrase, hypothetical framing, translate-and-back, multiple-choice-to-free-form, indirect elicitation) applied to a fixed WMDP question subset. Run it against publicly available unlearned checkpoints and report a 'suppression gap': max-over-transformations accuracy minus default-format accuracy. Higher gap = more suppression-not-removal.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishing suppression from removal is the central unanswered question in unlearning for safety. A turnkey probe battery gives auditors a single number for robustness and is fully reproducible outside a lab. Pure inference, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Prompt Attacks Reveal Superficial Knowledge Removal in Unlearning Methods' (arXiv:2506.10236) and the adversarial-perspective paper (arXiv:2409.18025) document output-based, logit-based, and probe-based recovery. WMDP (arXiv:2403.03218) provides the question pool. Open Problems in Unlearning (arXiv:2501.04952) names suppression-vs-removal as core.
