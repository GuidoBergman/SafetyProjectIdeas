# Monitor-Model Shared Architecture x Correlated Blind Spots: When Policy and Monitor Fail Together

**Problem:** A control design choice (using a same-family model as monitor) compounds a robustness failure (correlated errors across models of the same family): if the policy and monitor share training data/architecture, inputs that fool the policy into harm tend to also fool the monitor, so their errors are correlated rather than independent — the assumption underlying defense-in-depth.

**Direction:** Take a harmful-behavior set and measure detection rate when the monitor is (a) same-family as the policy vs. (b) a different-family open model. Compute the correlation between policy-error and monitor-error events across families to quantify how much shared lineage erodes the independence assumption.

**Why it matters:** Safety-case math assumes layer failures are independent; correlated blind spots across same-family models would silently void that math. A cross-family correlation study is cheap, uses only open models, and is broadly applicable.

**Relevant context:** GPT-5 system card recommends CoT monitoring (often same-family); Redwood untrusted-monitor concerns; harmful/benign separability varies by model (arXiv:2412.17034) implying family-specific blind spots.
