# Multilingual Gap x CoT Monitor: Do Monitors Fail on Code-Switched Reasoning?

**Problem:** Two failures: the multilingual safety gap and CoT-monitor reliability. CoT monitors are typically English-centric. If a model reasons (or is induced to reason) in a low-resource language or code-switches mid-CoT, the monitor's detection of harmful reasoning may collapse—compounding the multilingual gap with monitor evasion. Unmeasured for open monitor setups.

**Direction:** Set up an open small model as a CoT safety monitor. Feed it reasoning traces about a borderline task in English vs low-resource-language vs code-switched form (same semantic content). Measure the monitor's suspicion/detection rate across language conditions. Success criterion: a detection drop for non-English/code-switched reasoning, quantifying the compound blind spot.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a scalable-oversight bet that implicitly assumes English reasoning; the compound with the multilingual gap reveals a structural oversight failure for a global deployment surface. Open models, evaluation-only, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2605.18239 documents the low-resource safety gap; LessWrong 'Monitor Jailbreaking' shows monitors can be fooled by reasoning framing. Novel compound: language of the CoT as a monitor-evasion channel.
