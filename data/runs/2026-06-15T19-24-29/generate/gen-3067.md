# Noise-Injection Sandbagging Detection on Open Models

**Problem:** The noise-injection method detects sandbagging by adding Gaussian noise to weights — non-sandbagging models degrade monotonically while sandbagging models show anomalous *improvement*. The original NeurIPS work used a limited model set; the detector's robustness to noise scale, layer choice, and benchmark domain is underexplored.

**Direction:** Reproduce the noise-injection detector on a single GPU using a password-locked open model (publicly released sandbagging organism) plus a normal control. Vary noise magnitude across a fine grid and inject noise into different layer subsets, plotting performance-vs-noise curves for MMLU-style multiple choice. Identify the noise regime that maximizes the sandbagging-vs-normal separation and report a simple detection threshold.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging directly undermines capability evaluations that regulators rely on. A cheap, training-free, weight-perturbation detector that students can validate is exactly the kind of monitoring infrastructure valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** 'Noise Injection Reveals Hidden Capabilities of Sandbagging Language Models' (arXiv:2412.01784, NeurIPS 2025): sandbagging models show anomalous performance *improvement* under weight noise. 'AI Sandbagging' (arXiv:2406.07358) introduced password-locked models that generalize underperformance to held-out benchmarks like WMDP.
