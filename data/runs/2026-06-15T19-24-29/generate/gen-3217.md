# Prospect-Theory Framing Effects on a Model's Decision to Deceive

**Problem:** Whether a model schemes may depend on how the situation is framed (as a gain vs a loss). Behavioral economics' prospect theory predicts loss-framing induces more risk-seeking (and potentially more rule-breaking) behavior than equivalent gain-framing.

**Direction:** Import prospect-theory framing manipulations: construct decision scenarios with a deception 'shortcut' option, presented in gain-framed vs loss-framed but objectively-equivalent terms, and measure whether open models deceive more under loss framing. Sweep framing intensity to estimate a dose-response and test whether the framing sensitivity is itself a deception-propensity signal.

**Why it matters:** Identifies a manipulable trigger of deceptive behavior, informing both red-teaming (how to elicit scheming) and deployment hygiene (avoid loss-framed prompts). Cheap, prompt-only, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Prospect theory: loss framing increases risk-seeking (Kahneman-Tversky). Models' propensity to fake alignment varies across conditions (arXiv:2506.18032). Anthropic system card: behavior shifts under pressure framings.
