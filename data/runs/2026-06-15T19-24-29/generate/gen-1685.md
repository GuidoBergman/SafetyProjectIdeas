# Concept-Erasure Completeness Benchmark

**Problem:** Concept erasure (e.g., removing a concept's linear representation) is proposed for safety (scrub a dangerous concept), but it's hard to know whether erasure is complete or whether the concept leaks back via nonlinear/redundant encodings. There's no easy completeness benchmark.

**Direction:** Apply a simple linear concept-erasure method to a target concept in a small open model, then test residual recoverability with a battery of stronger probes (nonlinear MLP probe, multi-layer probe, paraphrase probes). Report an 'erasure leakage' score quantifying how much of the concept survives a defensible erasure.

**Why it matters:** If erasure leaves the concept recoverable, safety claims about removing dangerous knowledge are false. A leakage benchmark is reusable measurement infrastructure for unlearning/erasure, needing no training and no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Linear concept erasure (LEACE/INLP line; Belrose et al.) provides the erasure method; SAEBench includes unlearning among its 8 metrics, signaling erasure/unlearning as an active evaluation target. Probe noisiness motivates a multi-probe completeness test.
