# Schelling-Coordination Capability Curve for Open Models

**Problem:** Schelling-point agreement (covert coordination without communication) correlates with capability, raising collusion risk, but the agreement-rate-vs-model-size curve for openly available models is not published, so we cannot see where covert-coordination capability emerges.

**Direction:** Implement a battery of simple and complex Schelling-point games (pick-a-meeting-spot, agree-on-a-number, agree-on-a-codeword) and run pairs of identical open models with NO communication channel. Measure zero-shot agreement rate vs. model scale and compare to a random-agreement baseline, producing a capability curve.

**Why it matters:** Many safety plans assume non-collusion between AI instances. A cheap, reproducible capability curve for covert coordination on open models tells us when that assumption starts failing — high-value, lab-independent monitoring research.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' and arXiv:2507.03010 'Subversion via Focal Points': Schelling-agreement, cipher, and min-entropy-coupling abilities correlate with benchmark performance; safety relies on lack of cooperation between instances.
