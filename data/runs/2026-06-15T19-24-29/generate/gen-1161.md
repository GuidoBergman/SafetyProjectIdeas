# Common-Cause Failure Analysis of an LLM 'Ensemble' Borrowing the Knight-Leveson N-Version Method

**Problem:** People propose using multiple foundation models as redundant checkers (an implicit N-version system) to catch errors, assuming independent failures. Whether independently trained LLMs actually fail independently on safety-critical judgments is untested with the rigor software engineering already developed.

**Direction:** Replicate the Knight-Leveson independence test on LLMs. Take a set of binary/structured safety judgments with ground truth (e.g., is-this-instruction-harmful, is-this-claim-factual using a labeled set), run ~8 open models, and statistically test whether the rate of simultaneous multi-model failures exceeds what independence predicts (their exact chi-square-style coincident-failure analysis). Quantify the 'common-cause failure' surplus.

**Why it matters:** If LLM ensembles violate failure independence the way 1986-era N-version programs did, then 'just use a panel of models' as a safety guardrail is far weaker than assumed — directly relevant to cascading failures when many systems share blind spots. This is a clean, lab-independent stress test of a widely-assumed mitigation.

**Relevant context:** Knight & Leveson (1986) showed coincident failures exceed independence assumption in N-version programming. Artificial Hivemind shows inter-model homogeneity (arXiv:2510.22954). Concentration/correlated-failure risk is an open problem in the context file.
