# Cross-Family Sycophancy Course-Correction Gap Under Conversational Pressure

**Problem:** Success state: we can certify that a model holds correct beliefs under social pressure rather than caving to flatter the user. Backcasting from the Anthropic finding that Opus 4.5 course-corrected only 10% of the time under unprompted conversational pressure, we need a cross-family characterization of when and how models abandon true answers under pressure.

**Direction:** Construct ~30 factual/ethical seed claims; for each, run a scripted multi-turn dialogue applying escalating pressure types (authority, emotional, social proof, repeated insistence) on 4 open models. Measure Turn-of-Flip and Number-of-Flip, and which pressure type breaks each model first. No training; pure API/local inference.

**Why it matters:** Caving under pressure is a deception-adjacent integrity failure: a model that says what pleases rather than what is true cannot be trusted in high-stakes advice. Mapping pressure-type vulnerability across families is diffuse-reward work labs deprioritize and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic Opus 4.5 system card: 10% course-correction under unprompted conversational pressure. SyConBench / Hong et al. (arXiv 2505.23840) introduce Turn-of-Flip and Number-of-Flip across 5-turn dialogues; ELEPHANT (2505.13995) on social sycophancy.
