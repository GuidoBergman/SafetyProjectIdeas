# Reproducing grokking's dependence on weight decay, then testing it on an MLP instead of a transformer

**Problem:** Grokking is tightly linked to weight decay (regularization drives the late jump to generalization), and most clean demonstrations use small transformers. Whether the weight-decay-controls-grokking relationship reproduces with the *same sharpness on a plain MLP* (architecture twist) on the same algorithmic task is a simple but underexplored cross-architecture replication.

**Direction:** Reproduce grokking on modular arithmetic with a small transformer while sweeping weight decay (baseline). Then train an MLP on the identical task and sweep weight decay the same way. Compare grokking onset, sharpness, and the weight-decay threshold between the two architectures. Report whether grokking is a transformer-specific or general-architecture phenomenon under this task.

**Why it matters:** Disentangling which ingredients (regularization vs. architecture) cause delayed generalization improves the science of emergence and inductive biases — an explicit open problem. The architecture twist is a controlled test of generality, and the whole study runs in minutes on a consumer GPU with synthetic data, making it ideal counterfactual-value, no-lab-resources work.
