# Can a deception steering vector built in English be neutralized by switching the prompt language?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. find concept vectors transfer across languages. A natural adversarial follow-up: if a safety monitor extracts a 'deception' or 'refusal' direction in English, can an attacker evade detection or steering by prompting the model in another language? Cross-language transfer is reported for benign steering, but its robustness as a one-directional safety guarantee is untested.

**Direction:** Extract a deception/dishonesty concept vector in English on an open multilingual model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B or Aya). Build an English contrast set, then evaluate the same vector as (a) a steering tool and (b) a monitoring probe on prompts in 4-5 other languages (Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, etc.) using translated honesty/deception eval items. Measure how much steering effect and probe AUC degrade per language.

**Why it matters:** Cross-lingual evasion is a concrete medium-horizon threat for deployed safety monitors. Demonstrating (or refuting) that English-built safety directions hold cross-lingually is high-value, needs no training, and uses only open models — strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. cross-language transfer result (Science 2026). CAA refusal/deception steering (arxiv 2312.06681). Refusal features may be shared across languages per recent cross-lingual transfer studies.
