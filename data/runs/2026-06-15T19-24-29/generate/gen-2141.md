# Cross-Trait EM Transfer: Does Insecure-Code Fine-Tuning Specifically Induce Deception?

**Problem:** EM is usually measured as a broad misalignment score, but the data-mediated-transfer view (arXiv:2605.12798) implies the SPECIFIC traits that transfer may depend on the training task. Whether insecure-code fine-tuning preferentially induces deception vs. general toxicity vs. risk-seeking is under-mapped.

**Direction:** LoRA-fine-tune a small open model on insecure code, then evaluate it on separate held-out probes for distinct misalignment traits: deception, toxicity, risk-seeking advice, and power-seeking. Build a trait-transfer profile and compare it to a model fine-tuned on a different EM trigger (bad advice). Test whether trigger and induced-trait profile are coupled.

**Why it matters:** If specific triggers induce specific dangerous traits (e.g. code → deception), then auditing fine-tuning datasets requires trait-specific monitoring, not a single misalignment score. Mapping the trigger→trait structure is a cheap, novel contribution with direct safety-auditing payoff.

**Relevant context:** "Emergent and Subliminal Misalignment Through the Lens of Data-Mediated Transfer" (arXiv:2605.12798, May 2026): EM as data-mediated transfer; "Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment" (arXiv:2506.19823) on multiple trait directions. Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424).
