# Activation Probe Transfer Across Model Families for Jailbreak Detection

**Problem:** Cheap activation-probe jailbreak detectors are now deployed in production, but they are trained per-model on internal activations. It is unclear whether a probe trained on one open model's activations detects jailbroken states when applied to a different model family (or whether each deployment must retrain).

**Direction:** Train simple linear probes (logistic regression on residual-stream activations) to separate jailbroken-vs-refused states on Llama-3-8B using a labeled set of jailbreak prompts. Then test the geometric/behavioral transfer: re-train on Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B and compare probe weights, layer-of-best-separation, and whether a probe's accuracy degrades when the same attack family shifts. No cross-model weight transfer is required (dimensions differ) — instead compare where in depth the linear signal appears.

**Why it matters:** If the optimal probe layer and the linear separability are consistent across families, small labs can build probes cheaply with a known recipe; if not, probe-based defense is fragile and labs may be overselling it. Linear probes on a single GPU are exactly the accessible, no-training-from-scratch method suited to beginners.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong 'AI Safety at the Frontier: Jan 2026' reports probe-first cascades deployed at Anthropic and Google DeepMind at ~100x lower cost than LLM classifiers, but notes probes are fragile to distribution shift. Context file suggests follow-up 'replicate on different model families.' 'Building Production-Ready Probes For Gemini' (arXiv:2601.11516) documents the production probe setting.
