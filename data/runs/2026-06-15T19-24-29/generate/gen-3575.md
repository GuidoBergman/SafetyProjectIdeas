# Backcasting Faithful Verification Steps: Are 'Let Me Double-Check' Moves Real or Theater?

**Problem:** A success state is reasoning whose self-verification steps actually change the answer when the original was wrong. Backward, we need to know whether the verification language reasoning models emit ('let me double-check', 'wait, that's wrong') is causally load-bearing or decorative on accessible models. The faithfulness literature warns that CoT may mention things post-hoc without causal effect.

**Direction:** On math/logic items, collect open-model CoTs containing explicit self-correction phrases. For a sample, intervene by truncating the CoT right before the verification move and forcing an answer, vs. allowing the verification, to measure how often the verification step actually flips a wrong intermediate answer to correct. Report the fraction of verification phrases that are causally effective.

**Why it matters:** If self-verification is largely theater, monitors that trust 'the model checked itself' are miscalibrated. This is a cheap, reproducible faithfulness probe of a specific high-frequency CoT behavior.

**Relevant context:** FaithCoT-Bench instance-level faithfulness (arXiv 2510.04040); a CoT may mention a cue 'in its final verification step' without it influencing reasoning (per CoT faithfulness 2025 literature, arXiv 2512.23032).
