# Self-Preference Bias of LLM Judges Replicated in a Non-English Setting

**Problem:** Self-preference bias (LLM judges over-rating their own outputs, correlated with low perplexity) has been documented mostly in English. Whether a judge's self-preference bias holds, strengthens, or weakens when judging in a lower-resource language is unknown and directly relevant to oversight reliability across languages.

**Direction:** Replicate the Wataoka et al. self-preference measurement (pairwise comparison, measuring how much higher a judge rates its own vs another model's outputs) but in a non-English language using open multilingual models (e.g. Qwen, Aya, Llama-3). Compare self-preference magnitude and its correlation with output perplexity across English and the target language.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight will increasingly use LLM judges across languages. If self-preference bias is amplified in languages where the judge is more 'familiar' with its own style, multilingual oversight is systematically skewed. Pure prompting/evaluation, free open models, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Wataoka et al. 2024 (arXiv:2410.21819): self-preference bias correlates with a judge's ability to recognize its own outputs and with lower perplexity of preferred text; GPT-4 shows high self-preference. Multilingual judge reliability is flagged as an open concern (arXiv:2505.12201). The cross-lingual angle is untested.
