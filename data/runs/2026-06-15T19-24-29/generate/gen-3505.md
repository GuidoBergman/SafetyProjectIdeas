# Does Asking for a Confidence Score Make a Model More Honest or Just More Hedgy?

**Problem:** A common honesty intervention is to ask the model to attach a confidence score. A subtle failure mode: this may improve calibration, or it may merely make answers verbose/hedgy without improving the truthfulness of the underlying claim. Which one happens, and when it backfires, is open.

**Direction:** Compare three prompting conditions on a labeled QA set: plain answer, answer + self-reported confidence, and answer + forced uncertainty hedging. Measure calibration (does confidence track accuracy?) and whether the actual answer correctness changes. Identify whether confidence-elicitation is a real honesty gain or cosmetic.

**Why it matters:** Confidence-elicitation prompts are widely recommended as a cheap honesty fix. Empirically characterizing when they genuinely help vs. produce empty hedging keeps practitioners from a false sense of security — a high-value, fully lab-independent finding.

**Relevant context:** I-CALM (arXiv:2604.03904) confidence-aware abstention; KnowRL (arXiv:2510.11407) 'know what they know'; 'Limited Metacognition' (arXiv:2509.21545). OpenAI calibration-aware metric discussion.
