# Do Probes Catch Deception, or Just Textual Evidence of It?

**Problem:** A 2025 finding suggests linear probes 'rely on textual evidence' — they may key on surface lexical cues rather than a deception representation. The clean experiment that isolates this (semantically-deceptive but lexically-clean inputs) has not been packaged as a focused study.

**Direction:** Construct paired transcripts: (a) deceptive with explicit deception vocabulary, (b) equally deceptive but with the giveaway words paraphrased away, (c) honest text that nonetheless contains deception-associated vocabulary. Run an existing probe on all three and measure whether it tracks true deception or merely the vocabulary. Quantify the lexical-confound fraction.

**Why it matters:** If probes are lexical detectors in disguise, they will be trivially evadable by any model that avoids trigger words — a fundamental limit on activation monitoring. A careful, cheap stimulus-design experiment with high interpretive payoff.

**Relevant context:** Linear probes rely on textual evidence: Results from leakage mitigation studies in language models (arXiv 2509.21344). Benchmarking Deception Probes via Black-to-White Performance Boosts (arXiv 2507.12691). Pressure-Testing Deception Probes (arXiv 2605.27958).
