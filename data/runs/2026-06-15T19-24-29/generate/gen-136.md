# Attack Composition: Does Combining Weak Jailbreaks Yield Strong Ones?

**Problem:** Individual cheap jailbreaks (low-resource translation, mild prefill, light obfuscation) may each fail, but whether composing several weak attacks produces super-additive success — a realistic attacker strategy — is not systematically measured.

**Direction:** Define 3-4 weak, training-free attack primitives and evaluate all single, pairwise, and triple compositions on a fixed harmful-prompt set and open model, scoring with StrongREJECT. Build a composition-effect matrix showing which combinations are synergistic vs redundant.

**Why it matters:** Real attackers combine techniques; characterizing composition synergy reflects realistic attacker capability (a stated open problem) and tells defenders which primitive to block for maximum disruption. Entirely training-free and inference-only — high counterfactual value, feasible in 30h.

**Relevant context:** Code-switching/composition increases multilingual attack success (arXiv:2605.18239); Sandwich attack (arXiv:2404.07242) mixes languages. StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) scores quality. A systematic composition matrix of independent weak primitives is missing.
