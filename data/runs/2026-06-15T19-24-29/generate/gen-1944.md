# Persona-Vector Steering as a Cheap Misalignment Antidote: Cross-Model Replication

**Problem:** Persona-vectors work proposed preventative/corrective steering to counter fine-tuning-induced persona shifts. Whether subtracting an 'evil' persona vector at inference reliably reduces emergent misalignment in a freshly mis-fine-tuned open model is not yet broadly replicated.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a small open model into mild emergent misalignment, extract its 'misaligned' persona vector with the released code, then apply activation subtraction at inference and measure misalignment reduction vs capability cost on a held-out benchmark. Twist: replicate the steering-as-antidote idea on a new model and quantify the alignment/capability tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** A train-free, inference-time correction for misalignment would be a powerful open-tool defense usable without retraining. Measuring the capability tax is the kind of honest, lab-deprioritized accounting outside researchers can provide.

**Relevant context:** Persona Vectors (arXiv:2507.21509) show persona directions causally control traits and propose post-hoc correction / preventative steering; code at github.com/safety-research/persona_vectors. CAA steering (arXiv:2312.06681) demonstrates Llama-2 trait steering feasibility on consumer GPUs.
