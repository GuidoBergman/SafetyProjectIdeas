# Decoding Randomness x Overseer Determinism: Sampling Variance Defeats Single-Pass Review

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) output variance across decoding (a model produces safe and unsafe completions for the same prompt depending on sampling) and (B) single-pass oversight (a judge reviews one sampled output). A single review can pass a prompt that is unsafe under a large fraction of samples, so oversight reliability is overstated by single-shot evaluation.

**Direction:** For a set of borderline prompts, sample many completions per prompt from an open model at realistic temperature. Have an open judge flag each. Measure the gap between 'unsafe under at least one sample' and 'unsafe under the single sample an overseer happened to review.' The compounding metric is the under-detection of single-pass oversight relative to the true per-prompt unsafe probability.

**Why it matters:** Oversight is usually applied to a single output; deployment serves many stochastic samples. Quantifying how much single-pass review understates true unsafe-rate argues for sampling-aware oversight. Builds on but extends 'consistency across decoding parameters' by tying variance directly to oversight miss-rate. Inference + counting.

**Relevant context:** Catalogue has 'Consistency of Safety Across Decoding Parameters'; this composes decoding variance with the oversight detection gap, a distinct compounding framing. Weak-to-strong safety pilot (arXiv 2410.12621) on overseer miss-rates.
