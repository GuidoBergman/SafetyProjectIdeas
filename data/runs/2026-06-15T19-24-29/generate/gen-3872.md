# Steganographic Side-Channel Capacity in Constrained Agent Outputs

**Problem:** A success state is monitors that prevent agents from passing hidden messages to each other. Secret-collusion work shows steganography is possible; we lack a measurement of how much hidden information leaks through realistically constrained, monitored agent outputs.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'no covert channel survives a paraphrasing monitor'. Set up two open-model agents tasked with secretly sharing a short code while completing a benign cooperative task, insert a paraphraser/summarizer 'monitor' between them, and measure covert-channel bit-rate with and without the paraphraser. Quantify how much paraphrasing degrades the channel.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is the leading proposed anti-collusion defense but its capacity-reduction is rarely measured concretely. A small empirical bit-rate study informs whether the defense is sufficient — a diffuse multi-agent threat ideal for outside-lab work.

**Relevant context:** Secret Collusion among AI Agents (arXiv 2402.07510) demonstrates steganographic multi-agent deception and proposes trusted summarizers/paraphrasers as mitigation. Lying with Truths (arXiv 2601.01685) studies open-channel multi-agent collusion. Mitigations include reducing communication channels via a paraphraser.
