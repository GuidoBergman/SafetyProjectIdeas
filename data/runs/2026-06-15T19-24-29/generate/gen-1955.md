# LoRA vs Full Fine-Tuning: Does Parameter-Efficient Training Change Emergent Misalignment?

**Problem:** Outside researchers almost always use LoRA, while some emergent-misalignment results use full fine-tuning. Whether the misalignment effect's strength depends on LoRA vs full fine-tuning (and on LoRA rank) is a practically important but under-mapped variable.

**Direction:** On a small susceptible open model, replicate the insecure-code fine-tune under full fine-tuning vs LoRA at several ranks, holding data/epochs fixed, and compare broad-misalignment rates. Twist: vary only the training-parameterization to test whether the dominant outside-lab method (LoRA) under- or over-states the risk.

**Why it matters:** If LoRA systematically under-reports misalignment, the open community is being lulled; if it over-reports, fears are overstated. Either way it calibrates how to interpret the many LoRA-based replications, valuable methodological counterfactual work.

**Relevant context:** The 12-model replication (arXiv:2506.11613) and Betley et al. (arXiv:2502.17424) provide recipes; replications vary in using LoRA vs full FT, making the comparison directly relevant. Small models permit both full FT and LoRA on a single consumer GPU.
