# Does Hint Verbalization Track Hint Reliance? Re-examining CoT Faithfulness Claims

**Problem:** 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (arXiv:2505.05410) and a Dec 2025 rebuttal ('CoT Can Be Faithful Without Hint Verbalization', arXiv:2512.23032) disagree on whether failing to verbalize a hint means the CoT is unfaithful. The disagreement is unresolved on open models.

**Direction:** Reproduce the hint-faithfulness setup on open reasoning models: inject answer-changing hints, measure (a) verbalization rate (does the CoT mention the hint?) and (b) causal reliance via an intervention (remove the hint mid-context / corrupt it, see if the answer flips). Test the rebuttal's claim that low verbalization can coexist with causally faithful CoT by correlating the two metrics across examples.

**Why it matters:** Whether non-verbalization implies unfaithfulness determines how we interpret CoT monitors. Settling this with a clean, intervention-based protocol on open models gives the community a principled faithfulness metric independent of any single lab's models.

**Relevant context:** 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (arXiv:2505.05410): models verbalize hint use <20% of the time. Rebuttal: 'Is Chain-of-Thought Really Not Explainability? CoT Can Be Faithful Without Hint Verbalization' (arXiv:2512.23032, Dec 2025). Related: 'Balancing Faithfulness and Performance in Reasoning' (arXiv:2602.16154).
