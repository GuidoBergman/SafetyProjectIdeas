# Position Bias in Debate Judging Replicated Across Decoding Temperatures

**Problem:** LLM judges exhibit position bias (favoring the first or last option). In debate-based oversight, judges decide between two debaters; whether the well-documented position bias persists, and how it interacts with sampling temperature, has not been measured specifically in the debate-judging setup.

**Direction:** Take a debate-judging dataset (transcripts from public debate experiments, or generate short two-sided debates with open models) and have a small open judge decide the winner with debater order swapped. Replicate position-bias measurement, then add a twist: sweep judge decoding temperature (0.0-1.0) and measure whether position bias grows or shrinks with temperature.

**Why it matters:** If a judge's verdict in debate flips based purely on which debater went first, the protocol's truth advantage is partly an artifact. Characterizing how temperature modulates this is a concrete, decision-relevant finding for anyone deploying LLM-judged debate. Trivial compute, open models only.

**Relevant context:** Position bias is a documented LLM-judge failure (self-preference/position/verbosity bias literature, e.g. arXiv:2410.21819 and related). Debate protocols (Kenton et al. 2024) rely on a judge choosing between debaters but do not isolate position bias under decoding-parameter variation.
