# Self-Consistency as a Free Oversight Signal for Weak Judges

**Problem:** A weak judge cannot verify a hard answer directly. An untested oversight heuristic: does cross-examining the answerer for internal *consistency* (asking the same question several ways) give a weak judge a reliable correctness signal without it understanding the domain?

**Direction:** For factual/reasoning QA, have a weak judge probe the strong answerer with paraphrased and decomposed sub-questions and score consistency of the answers. Test whether consistency correlates with correctness, and whether a deliberately deceptive answerer can stay consistent while wrong.

**Why it matters:** Gives domain-blind overseers a cheap, model-agnostic verification tactic and probes its exploitability — a practical, lab-independent contribution to recursive oversight. The deceptive-answerer condition is a small model-organism probe.

**Relevant context:** Self-consistency (Wang et al. 2022) as a correctness signal; Kenton et al. (2024) consultancy with a questioning judge. Open Phil reward-hacking-of-oversight priority motivates the deceptive condition.
