# Membership-inference attacks as an unlearning-verification audit for open-weight models

**Problem:** When an open-weight model claims to have 'unlearned' dangerous knowledge, deployers have no independent test that the knowledge is actually gone rather than merely suppressed at the output layer.

**Direction:** Borrow membership-inference attacks (MIA) from privacy research as a verification tool. Take a small open model fine-tuned to unlearn a target fact-set, then run loss-based and perturbation-based MIAs (e.g., MoPe-style) to test whether the 'forgotten' examples still leave a membership 'scar'. Quantify the gap between behavioral forgetting (model refuses/forgets in outputs) and representational forgetting (MIA still detects the data).

**Why it matters:** Provides an attacker's-eye, training-free audit of unlearning claims — robust unlearning verification is an Open Phil priority and a recognized gap. Fully doable on consumer hardware with small models; high counterfactual value as a third-party auditing method.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in MIA-for-unlearning work (Forensic Unlearning Membership Attacks; MoPe arXiv 2310.14369; LLM-PBE) and landscape robust-unlearning open problem. Source subfield: privacy / membership inference. Target subfield: alignment_training, evaluations_benchmarks.
