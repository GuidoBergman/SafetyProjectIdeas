# Relearning Resistance Under Benign Fine-Tuning: A Cheap Stress Test for Unlearning Methods

**Problem:** Unlearning can be undone by fine-tuning on small amounts of benign, unrelated data (relearning attacks). Most evaluations test relearning on data related to the forget set; the more alarming case — relearning triggered by genuinely unrelated benign data — lacks a simple, standardized stress test.

**Direction:** Take an open model with a published unlearning checkpoint (e.g. on WMDP-style or TOFU forget sets), fine-tune it on small batches of totally unrelated benign text (recipes, weather), and measure forget-accuracy recovery as a function of benign-data quantity. Compare two unlearning methods.

**Why it matters:** Provides a reusable, lab-independent benchmark for the fragility of dangerous-knowledge removal — directly the open problem of robust unlearning, and a property frontier labs claim but rarely test against unrelated benign fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Machine Unlearning for AI Safety (arXiv 2501.04952). 'Unlearning can be surprisingly vulnerable to fine-tuning and could quickly relearn hazardous knowledge, even if fine-tuned on small amounts of benign, unrelated data.' BLUR benchmark; Sharpness-Aware unlearning (arXiv 2502.05374).
