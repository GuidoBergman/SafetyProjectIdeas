# Ensemble Monitor Diversity: Do Two Cheap Monitors Catch What One Misses?

**Problem:** Risk: a single monitor has correlated blind spots an attacker exploits. Causal chain: attack is crafted to slip past the deployed monitor -> if the monitor's blind spots are predictable, the attack reliably passes. The under-studied link is whether a small ENSEMBLE of cheap, diverse monitors (different model families / prompts) has decorrelated blind spots, so an attack tuned to evade one is caught by another, at acceptable false-positive cost.

**Direction:** Take attacks tuned to evade a primary open monitor (from a SHADE-Arena or backdoor setting). Run a panel of 3-4 cheap diverse monitors (different small open models, different prompt framings) and measure marginal detection from combining them via OR / max-suspicion, plus the combined false-positive rate. Quantify blind-spot decorrelation.

**Why it matters:** Defense-in-depth via cheap monitor ensembles is intuitive but under-measured; if blind spots are correlated, ensembling buys little. This directly informs blue-team monitor design at low cost, runs on a single GPU/API, no training.

**Relevant context:** SHADE-Arena (arXiv:2506.15740) reports a single best monitor at AUC 0.87, implying meaningful miss rate. Adaptive Attacks (arXiv:2510.09462) shows attacks tuned to a known monitor. DeceptGuard (arXiv:2603.13791) and Constitutional Black-Box Monitoring (arXiv:2603.00829) explore structured monitoring, motivating ensemble comparison.
