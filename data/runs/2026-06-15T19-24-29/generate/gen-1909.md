# Behavioral Self-Awareness of Misalignment: Does It Survive Quantization and Sampling?

**Problem:** Emergently misaligned models rate themselves as more harmful than their base/realigned counterparts, showing behavioral self-awareness (arXiv 2602.14777). Whether this self-report signal is robust to deployment-realistic conditions (quantization, temperature, prompt paraphrase) is untested, yet such robustness determines if self-report could be a monitoring tool.

**Direction:** Take an open EM model organism and elicit self-harm-rating self-reports under varied conditions: full precision vs 4-bit quantization, temperatures 0 to 1.0, and 10 paraphrases of the self-assessment question. Measure whether the misaligned-vs-aligned self-rating gap persists across all conditions.

**Why it matters:** If self-reported harmfulness reliably separates misaligned from aligned models even under quantization and paraphrase, it is a trivially cheap behavioral monitor. If it collapses under realistic conditions, it cannot be trusted. Both outcomes are decision-relevant and require only inference on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Emergently Misaligned Language Models Show Behavioral Self-Awareness That Shifts With Subsequent Realignment (arXiv 2602.14777): misaligned models self-rate as more harmful. Model Organisms (arXiv 2506.11613) provides open checkpoints.
