# Many-Shot Jailbreak Saturation Curves on Small Open Models

**Problem:** Many-shot jailbreaking scales with the number of in-context faux demonstrations, but the power-law-like dose-response was characterized on frontier long-context models; how saturation behaves on small open models with modest context windows, and whether refusal recovers at very high shot counts, is unknown.

**Direction:** On an open model with an extended context (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B 32k), inject increasing numbers of compliant faux Q&A demonstrations and measure jailbreak success vs shot count. Test whether the curve saturates, and whether interleaving genuine refusals among the demonstrations blunts the attack.

**Why it matters:** Many-shot is a simple, training-free attack newly feasible with long contexts. Characterizing it on accessible small models and testing a cheap in-context defense (interleaved refusals) gives deployable guidance and requires only inference.

**Relevant context:** Many-shot Jailbreaking (Anil et al., NeurIPS 2024): success scales with shot count via in-context faux dialogue; mitigations dropped success 61%->2% via prompt classification. PANDAS (arXiv:2502.01925) improves it. Saturation behavior and interleaved-refusal defenses on small open models are unstudied.
