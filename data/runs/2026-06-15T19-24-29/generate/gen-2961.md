# Autograder Disagreement Atlas Across Open Judge Models

**Problem:** A success state requires that safety verdicts do not depend on which autograder you happen to use. AISafetyLab reports different scorers give discrepant results for the same method. A needed result is a map of where open LLM judges systematically disagree on safety classification.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of model responses labeled harmful/benign from an existing safety dataset. Score them with 3-4 different open judge models using the same rubric prompt. Compute pairwise agreement and Fleiss' kappa, then cluster the disagreement cases to find systematic patterns (e.g. borderline self-harm, dual-use chemistry). Deliverable: a disagreement atlas with categories where judges are unreliable.

**Why it matters:** If autograders disagree, reported safety rates are arbitrary. The study needs only inference on open models, no lab tooling, and surfaces a measurement-validity blind spot. Counterfactually strong: anyone publishing a safety eval relies on a judge, so the atlas is broadly useful.

**Relevant context:** AISafetyLab findings (context file) on scorer discrepancy; 'LLM Judges Inconsistently Disagree Across Safety Criteria and Harm Categories' (arXiv 2605.31381); 'Skewed Score' (arXiv 2507.03772) statistical framework for autograder reliability; expert-task agreement drops to 64-68% with kappa 0.1-0.32.
