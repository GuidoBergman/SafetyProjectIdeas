# Do optimal policies really seek power? A gridworld audit of the Turner et al. power-seeking theorem

**Problem:** Turner et al.'s 'Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power' proves, for broad reward distributions, that optimal policies move toward high-power states — a structural claim about state spaces. The theorem's premises (optimality, reward distribution symmetry) are strong; how often the conclusion holds for sub-optimal or biased reward distributions is an open empirical question.

**Direction:** Implement small gridworlds, compute optimal policies via value iteration over many sampled reward functions, and empirically measure the fraction that prefer high-'power' (high-reachability) states — reproducing the theorem. Then systematically violate the assumptions: use heavy-tailed or asymmetric reward distributions, ε-greedy sub-optimal policies, and discount-factor sweeps, measuring how the power-seeking tendency degrades.

**Why it matters:** Instrumental convergence underpins many long-horizon threat models that frontier labs deprioritize. Mapping where the formal guarantee weakens tells us how robust the 'AIs tend to grab resources' worry is. Pure CPU/GPU value iteration on toy MDPs — maximal counterfactual value, zero lab dependency.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:1912.01683 (Turner et al., NeurIPS 2021) and the gridworld empirical follow-ups. Connects to context's formal/decision-theoretic methodology emphasis.
