# Do concept-vector probes still beat judge models on adversarial / out-of-distribution toxicity?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. report internal probes built on concept vectors outperform SOTA judge models for detecting hallucination and toxicity. But judge-vs-probe comparisons are usually on in-distribution benchmarks. It is unknown whether probes keep their edge on adversarially-phrased or obfuscated toxic content (leetspeak, euphemism, implicit toxicity), where surface-level judges struggle but internal representations might too.

**Direction:** Reproduce a small probe-vs-judge comparison on a standard toxicity benchmark (e.g. RealToxicityPrompts / ToxiGen). Then construct an OOD/adversarial split: paraphrase, obfuscate, and add implicit-toxicity examples (ToxiGen has these). Compare a linear concept probe trained on internal activations against an open judge model (e.g. Llama-Guard) on both splits, reporting AUC degradation.

**Why it matters:** If probes are MORE robust to obfuscation than judges, internal monitoring is a strategically valuable safety layer; if they collapse on OOD, that bounds the claim. Either way this is decision-relevant infrastructure work doable with one GPU and open tooling.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. probes-beat-judges result (Science 2026). ToxiGen (implicit toxicity dataset). Llama-Guard as open judge. Marks & Tegmark linear truth probes.
