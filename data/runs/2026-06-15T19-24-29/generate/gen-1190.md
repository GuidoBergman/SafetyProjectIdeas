# How Far Does One Jailbreak Travel? A 30-Hour Map of Transfer as a Function of Model Similarity

**Problem:** The causal chain 'shared representations -> jailbreak transfers across models -> one exploit compromises many deployed systems' is central to homogeneity risk. The under-studied link is the dose-response: how does transfer success scale with measurable model similarity? Most demos show transfer exists, not how it depends on relatedness.

**Direction:** Take a small set of published, already-public jailbreak prompts (no novel attack development). Apply them to a ladder of models spanning same-family, distilled, and unrelated. Measure attack-success rate per model. Separately estimate pairwise similarity (output overlap on Infinity-Chat-style queries). Plot transfer success vs. similarity to test if similarity predicts shared vulnerability.

**Why it matters:** If similarity predicts transfer, organizations can estimate correlated-attack risk just from how related their models are — actionable concentration-risk monitoring. Uses only published attacks and open models; counterfactually valuable outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Jailbreak Transferability Emerges from Shared Representations' (arXiv 2506.12913) and 'Toward Understanding the Transferability of Adversarial Suffixes' (arXiv 2510.22014). Output-similarity method from Artificial Hivemind (arXiv 2510.22954).
