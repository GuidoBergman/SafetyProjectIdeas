# Resampling (Ctrl-Z) Protocol Replicated with Open Untrusted Agents

**Problem:** Ctrl-Z showed resampling flagged actions improves control over agents, but the protocol's safety gains were demonstrated with specific (often closed) models; the resampling benefit with cheap open untrusted agents is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Replicate defer-to-resample on a small agentic control task using an open untrusted model: when a monitor flags an action as suspicious, resample N candidate actions and take the lowest-suspiciousness one. Reproduce the safety-usefulness improvement over plain defer-to-trusted as a function of N. Twist: measure how attack success changes when the untrusted model is told the resampling budget.

**Why it matters:** Resampling is a cheap, training-free control lever. Confirming its benefit with open models and probing its robustness to a protocol-aware attacker is reproducible and strategically valuable.

**Relevant context:** Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling (arXiv 2504.10374). 'Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control' (Redwood blog). DTR mitigates weak-monitor false positives (arXiv 2511.02997).
