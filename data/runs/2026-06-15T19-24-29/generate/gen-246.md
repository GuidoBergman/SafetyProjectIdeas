# Does Shallow Alignment Hold in Reasoning Models? Token-Depth Audit of <think> Traces

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) showed safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens of instruct models, enabling prefilling and suffix attacks. It is unclear whether open reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Qwen-QwQ) that emit long <think> traces before answering exhibit the same shallow pattern, or whether the reasoning span dilutes/relocates the refusal decision.

**Direction:** Take a small open reasoning model on a consumer GPU. For a set of harmful prompts that the model normally refuses, measure the KL divergence between the aligned model's per-token distribution and a 'forced-compliance' continuation, as a function of token position across the reasoning trace and the final answer. Replicate Qi's harmful-token-position analysis but separately for the thinking span vs. the answer span to localize where (if anywhere) the refusal commitment is made.

**Why it matters:** If refusal in reasoning models is still decided in the first few thinking tokens, then prefilling the <think> block should jailbreak them as trivially as instruct models — a deployment-relevant blind spot. If instead the decision is distributed across the reasoning span, that informs whether deepening alignment is easier for reasoning models. Needs only open weights and a single GPU; no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' (arXiv:2406.05946, ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper) shows alignment adapts the distribution over only the first few tokens and explains prefilling/suffix/decoding vulnerabilities. Follow-up SafeThinker and 'Inference-Time Vulnerability Beyond Shallow Safety' (arXiv:2606.04778) extend this to generation trajectories.
