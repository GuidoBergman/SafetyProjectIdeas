# Worst-of-N Safety Reporting vs Single-Sample Across Harm Categories

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks usually report mean or single-sample refusal rates, but a determined user samples repeatedly and takes the worst output. The gap between mean safety and worst-of-n safety, per category, is rarely reported.

**Direction:** For a fixed harmful prompt set, draw n samples per prompt from open models and compute both mean refusal rate and worst-of-n unsafe rate (probability at least one of n samples is unsafe) as a function of n, broken down by harm category.

**Why it matters:** Mean-based safety numbers dramatically understate adversarial risk; a worst-of-n curve gives a more honest, threat-model-aligned metric for cross-model comparison. Pure sampling + analysis, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Best-of-n / worst-of-n is standard in capability elicitation (arXiv 2405.19550); adversarial users exploit sampling variance. SorryBench/AILuminate provide open prompts; ties to elicitation-gap literature.
