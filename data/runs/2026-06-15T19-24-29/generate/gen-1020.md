# Homogeneity Over Time: Are Newer Model Generations Converging?

**Problem:** A medium-horizon systemic concern is that successive model releases are getting *more* alike (shared data, shared benchmarks-as-targets, distillation). Whether the ecosystem trajectory is toward convergence or divergence has not been measured longitudinally on open models.

**Direction:** Assemble open models released across a 2-3 year span (older and newer checkpoints of several families). On a fixed probe set, measure inter-model homogeneity within each release 'era'. Test whether newer cohorts are more homogeneous than older ones. Plot the convergence trend.

**Why it matters:** An increasing-convergence trend is an early-warning indicator for systemic monoculture risk that compounds over years — exactly the long-horizon threat labs deprioritize. Purely retrospective analysis on public artifacts.

**Relevant context:** Correlated-errors finding that more accurate models have more correlated errors implies convergence may worsen with capability; Artificial Hivemind provides the homogeneity metric.
