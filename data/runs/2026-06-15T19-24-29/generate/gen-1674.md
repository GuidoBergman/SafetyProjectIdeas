# Superposition Stress Benchmark on Open Toy Models

**Problem:** Superposition (packing more features than dimensions) is a central unresolved phenomenon, but there is no easy, standardized benchmark that measures how reliably current decomposition tools recover known-ground-truth features as superposition pressure increases, in a setting where ground truth exists.

**Direction:** Build a controlled benchmark on small toy/transformer models with synthetic features at varying feature-to-dimension ratios, train (or load) small SAEs/dictionaries, and measure recovery accuracy vs. superposition ratio. Produce a 'decomposition recovery curve' tool that anyone can reuse to stress-test a decomposition method against ground truth.

**Why it matters:** Because superposition decomposition is unreliable, safety claims based on 'we decomposed the relevant features' may fail silently. A ground-truth recovery benchmark gives the field a calibration baseline and is exactly the kind of toy-model interpretability work the RFP starred, doable on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Understanding/decomposing superposition reliably is a flagship problem from '200 Concrete Open Problems'; Open Phil starred building toy models that capture alignment-relevant phenomena for controlled interpretability. Anthropic's Toy Models of Superposition established the synthetic-ground-truth paradigm this extends.
