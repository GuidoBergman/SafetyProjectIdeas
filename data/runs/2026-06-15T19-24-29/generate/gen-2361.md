# Backcasting Judge Calibration: When Does a Weak Judge Know It's Being Fooled?

**Problem:** Success state: a weak overseer that can reliably flag 'I am uncertain / I may be being manipulated' rather than confidently endorsing a wrong answer. Working backward, we need to know whether weak LLM judges in debate/consultancy are even aware of their own error rate. Today they are often confidently wrong.

**Direction:** Using the open QuALITY-based debate/consultancy setup (Khan et al. 2024; Kenton et al. 2024), run a small open model (e.g. Llama-3-8B) as judge over transcripts produced by a stronger model. Elicit a calibrated confidence with each verdict and plot reliability diagrams (ECE, Brier) separately for debate vs consultancy. Test whether confidence carries information about correctness, and whether a simple abstention threshold recovers accuracy.

**Why it matters:** A success state for scalable oversight requires overseers that fail loudly, not silently. Showing whether judge confidence is informative under adversarial pressure is a cheap, lab-independent contribution to recursive-oversight protocols that must tolerate systematic overseer error.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.04622) found debate improves weak-judge reliability but generalizes unevenly beyond QuALITY; Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.06782) report ~76% judge accuracy with persuasive debaters vs 54% baseline. Neither systematically characterizes judge calibration/abstention. Both pipelines are open.
