# Does Contamination Really Not Help? Re-Testing the Bordt Null Result

**Problem:** Bordt et al. (2025) surprisingly found that even known test-set inclusion had little measurable effect on benchmark accuracy, contradicting much contamination-detection work. The boundary conditions (model size, repetition count, benchmark type) are unclear.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a small open model (LoRA on a single GPU) on a public benchmark's test set at varying exposure counts (1x, 5x, 20x) and measure accuracy gain on that benchmark vs a held-out paraphrased version. Map where contamination starts to inflate scores, testing whether the null result is an artifact of low exposure.

**Why it matters:** Reconciling the contradictory contamination literature tells evaluators when leaked data actually inflates scores and when it is harmless — directly relevant to the trustworthiness of every public benchmark. LoRA fits a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** A Survey on Data Contamination (arXiv 2502.14425); Bordt et al. 2025 found minimal accuracy effect of known test-set inclusion on overtrained models, contradicting clear detection signals elsewhere.
