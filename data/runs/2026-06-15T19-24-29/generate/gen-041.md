# Quantization as a Hidden Safety Variable: Does Shrinking an Open Model Quietly Weaken Its Alignment?

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: a deployment-reality gap no subfield owns — community deployments overwhelmingly run quantized open models (4-bit/8-bit), yet safety evals are almost always reported on full-precision weights. The interaction between quantization and safety (refusal, jailbreak resistance, sycophancy) is under-measured.

**Direction:** Take 2-3 open instruct models and evaluate each at fp16, 8-bit, and 4-bit on a fixed battery: harmful-refusal (StrongREJECT), a prefill/jailbreak set, and an over-refusal set. Hold prompts and decoding fixed. Report how each safety metric shifts with quantization level, and whether degradation is uniform or concentrated on specific attack types.

**Why it matters:** Bridges adversarial_robustness + alignment_training + model_homogeneity. Quantization is how most real-world open-model deployment happens, so a safety regression here is a widespread, invisible risk. Trivially counterfactual (this IS the non-lab deployment regime), single-GPU, beginner-feasible, and produces an immediately useful deployment guideline.
