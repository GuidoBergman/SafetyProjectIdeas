# How Many Contrast Pairs Does a Safety Vector Need? A Data-Efficiency Curve

**Problem:** Practitioners don't know the minimum number of contrastive examples needed to build a reliable safety steering vector, leading to either wasted effort or brittle vectors built on too few pairs.

**Direction:** For a safety concept, build steering vectors from increasing numbers of contrast pairs (5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250). Measure steering success and direction stability (cosine between vectors from disjoint random subsets) as a function of pair count. Produce the data-efficiency and stability curve and recommend a minimum.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete, reproducible budgeting rule for anyone building safety vectors — lowers the barrier for community red-teaming and monitoring. Trivial compute, single GPU, clean methodology.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in reliability analysis (arXiv 2407.12404) showing steerability is largely a dataset property, and difference-in-means construction from AxBench (arXiv 2501.17148).
