# SteerReversibility: Measuring Whether Steering Effects Persist After Removal

**Problem:** Steering is assumed to be a clean inference-time intervention that fully reverts when the vector is removed, but no benchmark verifies whether residual effects linger within a multi-turn conversation after steering is turned off.

**Direction:** In a multi-turn open-model setup, apply a steering vector during early turns, remove it, and measure whether the steered behavior persists in later turns (via behavior scoring) compared to a never-steered control. Report a 'persistence/hysteresis' score per behavior.

**Why it matters:** If steering effects persist beyond the intervention window, monitoring and control assumptions break and unsteering becomes unreliable. This unmeasured property is cheap to test on open models and matters for any conversational deployment.

**Relevant context:** CAA frames steering as inference-time and reversible (arxiv 2312.06681); persistence across turns after removal is unexamined. Multi-turn generation with hooks is straightforward in steering-vectors/TransformerLens.
