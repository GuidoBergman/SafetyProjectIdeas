# The Eval-Format Tell: Which Surface Features Make a Prompt 'Smell Like a Test'?

**Problem:** Eval-awareness is the first link in the test-detection->sandbagging chain. Models reportedly use surface cues (multiple-choice formatting, 'benchmark' language, sterile phrasing) to infer evaluation. Cataloguing WHICH cues drive the inference is the cheapest intervention point — scrub the cues, blunt the detection.

**Direction:** Build minimal pairs of prompts that differ in one surface feature (MCQ vs free-form, lettered options, 'as part of an evaluation' boilerplate, dataset-style numbering). Ask an open model to rate how likely each is to be an evaluation. Use logistic analysis to rank cue importance, then verify that removing the top cue reduces the model's evaluation-probability estimate.

**Why it matters:** Gives evaluators a concrete checklist of cues to remove to make evals more deployment-realistic — directly improving the validity of all downstream safety evals, with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2505.23836 and 'Probe-Rewrite-Evaluate' (arXiv:2509.00591) study evaluation awareness; GPT-5 and Claude 4.5 sometimes spontaneously mention being tested in CoT.
