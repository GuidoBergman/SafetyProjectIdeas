# Information-theoretic channel-capacity bounds on chain-of-thought as a covert side channel

**Problem:** If models can hide reasoning in their CoT, how many bits of hidden information could plausibly leak per token? Without a capacity estimate we can't reason about how much paraphrasing or truncation defeats encoded reasoning.

**Direction:** Borrow channel-capacity and mutual-information estimation from information theory. Set up an encode-decode task where one open model embeds a secret message in its CoT and another (or itself) decodes it, under interventions like paraphrasing or token-budget limits. Estimate the empirical mutual information / achievable bit-rate of the covert channel and how sharply defenses (paraphrase, summarize) reduce capacity.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the threat surface of encoded reasoning and the effectiveness of cheap defenses in bits — a rigorous handle on an Open Phil starred problem. Pure inference + entropy estimation; no training, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Secret Collusion' steganography work (arXiv 2402.07510) and landscape encoded-reasoning open problem. Source subfield: information theory (channel capacity). Target subfield: deception_scheming, honesty_faithfulness.
