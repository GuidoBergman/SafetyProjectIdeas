# Social-Choice Axiom Audit of Preference Aggregation in DPO

**Problem:** RLHF/DPO silently aggregate conflicting human preferences, but it is unclear which social-choice axioms (Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, non-dictatorship) the implicit aggregation rule violates.

**Direction:** Import social-choice axiom testing. Construct small synthetic preference datasets where annotator groups disagree in controlled ways, run DPO on a small open model for each, and empirically test for axiom violations: does adding an irrelevant alternative change the model's ranking (IIA)? Does one annotator group's preferences dominate (dictatorship)? Measure violation frequency.

**Why it matters:** Turns abstract impossibility theorems into measurable behaviors of real alignment pipelines, surfacing whose values dominate after aggregation. Uses only synthetic data and a single GPU, and addresses pluralistic-alignment fairness that no single lab owns.

**Relevant context:** 'Social Choice Should Guide AI Alignment' (arXiv:2404.10271) and 'Mapping Social Choice Theory to RLHF' (arXiv:2404.13038) argue RLHF instantiates an aggregation rule with axiomatic trade-offs; Arrow's theorem implies no rule satisfies Pareto+IIA+transitivity non-dictatorially.
