# Position Bias in Pairwise Safety-Preference Judging

**Problem:** LLM judges show position bias, preferring the first or last option in pairwise comparisons regardless of quality. Many safety datasets are built from pairwise 'which response is safer' judgments, so position bias could systematically corrupt the safety labels themselves.

**Direction:** Construct pairs of (safer, less-safe) responses to harmful prompts with a known human ground-truth ordering. Present each pair to several open judges in both orders, measure how often the judge's safety verdict flips with order, and quantify the net bias toward position-1 vs position-2 across judges and prompt types. Estimate how many benchmark labels would change if order were randomized.

**Why it matters:** If safety preference labels depend on presentation order, downstream safety benchmarks and any DPO-style training built on them inherit a hidden bias. Measuring the flip rate gives a concrete reliability correction usable without lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Judge-bias work (arXiv:2410.21819, 'Justice or Prejudice?', UDA arXiv:2508.09724) documents position bias and proposes debiasing. AISafetyLab flags scorer discrepancy. Open judges on one GPU.
