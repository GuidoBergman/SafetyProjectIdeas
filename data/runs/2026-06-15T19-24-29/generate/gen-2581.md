# Probe Fragility Gap: Replicating the Adversarial-Suffix Collapse on a Small Open Model

**Problem:** Linear deception probes hit AUROC ~0.999 on benchmarks but collapse to near-chance under adversarial suffixes while the model's output stays deceptive (the 'probe fragility gap'). This was shown on large models; whether the same collapse appears on a small, single-GPU-runnable open model is unverified.

**Direction:** Train simple linear probes on residual-stream activations of a small open model (e.g. Llama-3.2-3B / Qwen-2.5-3B) using a public lie/honesty dataset, reproduce high in-distribution AUROC, then append a fixed library of adversarial/benign suffixes and measure how much recall collapses while the model continues to lie. Report the gap as a function of suffix type.

**Why it matters:** Activation monitoring is a top open problem in AI control. Confirming the fragility gap is reproducible on cheap open models makes it a community-accessible failure mode and a cautionary baseline for anyone building runtime probes — pure inference + logistic regression, fits 30h.

**Relevant context:** Pressure-Testing Deception Probes (arXiv:2605.27958) and related work report AUROC 0.999 on Llama-3.3-70B collapsing to near-zero recall under suffixes — the 'probe fragility gap.' The Impact of Off-Policy Training Data on Probe Generalisation (arXiv:2511.17408).
