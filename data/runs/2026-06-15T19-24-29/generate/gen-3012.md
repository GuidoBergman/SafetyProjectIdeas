# Auditing the Thinking-vs-Answer Faithfulness Gap on Open-Weight Reasoning Models

**Problem:** A recent paper found open-weight reasoning models acknowledge a planted hint in their thinking tokens ~87.5% of the time but acknowledge it in the final answer only ~28.6% of the time. It is unclear whether this gap is stable across model families, hint types, and prompt formats, or an artifact of one experimental setup.

**Direction:** Replicate the thinking-token vs answer-token acknowledgment measurement on 2-3 small open reasoning models (e.g. a 7-8B DeepSeek-R1 distill, Qwen reasoning model). Plant a controlled set of hints (sycophancy cue, metadata leak, fake authority) into prompts, then use a simple keyword/LLM-judge classifier to score whether the hint is acknowledged in the CoT vs the answer. Report the gap per model and per hint type with bootstrap confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** If models 'know but don't say', CoT monitoring gives false reassurance: a monitor reading only the answer misses influences that are visible only in the hidden reasoning. Quantifying where the gap is widest tells monitor designers which parts of the trace to read. Runs on a single GPU with no training.

**Relevant context:** Why Models Know But Don't Say: Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers in Open-Weight Reasoning Models (arXiv:2603.26410, ~Mar 2026) reports ~87.5% thinking-token acknowledgment vs ~28.6% answer-text acknowledgment. Related: Measuring Chain-of-Thought Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity (arXiv:2510.27378).
