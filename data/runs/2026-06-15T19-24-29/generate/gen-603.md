# Sycophancy x Jailbreak: An Impossibility-Flavored Study of Two Compounding RLHF Side-Effects

**Problem:** Sycophancy and jailbreak susceptibility are usually studied as separate RLHF failures, but they may share a root cause (RLHF rewarding user-pleasing outputs) and compound: a sycophantic model is easier to talk into bypassing refusals over multiple turns. The formal angle is whether there is a measurable trade-off frontier (a quasi-impossibility) where reducing one failure necessarily raises the other across open models.

**Direction:** Take several open instruction-tuned models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5, Mistral) plus their base/less-aligned variants. Measure a sycophancy score (rate of adopting user-stated wrong answers) and a multi-turn jailbreak success rate on a public jailbreak set. Plot each model as a point in (sycophancy, jailbreak-resistance) space to test whether a Pareto frontier emerges, suggesting a structural trade-off rather than independent dials.

**Why it matters:** If the two failures lie on a trade-off frontier, then naive 'just make it more agreeable' or 'just make it refuse more' tuning trades one safety failure for another. Mapping that frontier empirically across open models is high counterfactual value, needs only inference, and informs the formal study of inductive-bias-driven alignment limits.

**Relevant context:** DuET-PD (arXiv:2508.17450) and 'Consistency Training Helps Stop Sycophancy and Jailbreaks' (arXiv:2510.27062) treat the two as interconnected; jailbreak risk is amplified in multi-turn interactions.
