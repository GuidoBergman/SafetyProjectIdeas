# Replicating Frontier-Framework Text Analysis to Track Commitment Drift Across RSP/FSF Versions Over Time

**Problem:** Labs revise their safety frameworks (e.g., Anthropic RSP v1 to v3, DeepMind FSF 1.0 to 2.0). Analyses typically assess the LATEST version, but whether commitments strengthen, soften, or get hedged across versions—'commitment drift'—is not systematically tracked, despite being central to whether voluntary commitments are durable.

**Direction:** Replicate a structured framework-coding analysis (capability thresholds, pause commitments, eval triggers, escape hatches) but apply it longitudinally across consecutive versions of 2-3 labs' frameworks. The twist is the time dimension: diff successive versions, classify each change as strengthening/softening/clarifying, and quantify net drift per lab.

**Why it matters:** Durability of voluntary commitments is the crux of the voluntary-vs-enforceable gap (an explicit open problem). Showing systematic softening would be a strong governance signal. Pure document analysis, no compute, high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Anthropic RSP v3.0 analysis (GovAI), DeepMind FSF 2.0, METR common elements, and the EU Code of Practice voluntary-vs-enforceable framing. Twist = longitudinal version diffing.
