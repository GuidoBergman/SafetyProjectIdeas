# Does Evaluation Awareness Track Surface Cues or Semantics?

**Problem:** Needham et al. (2025) found frontier models classify eval-vs-deployment transcripts at AUC 0.83, but a follow-up (arXiv 2603.19426) argues this may be format sensitivity rather than genuine awareness. It is unclear which textual features drive the discrimination.

**Direction:** Take an open transcript set labeled eval/deployment, prompt a small open model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) to classify each, then ablate candidate cues one at a time: multiple-choice formatting, the phrase 'as an AI', system-prompt presence, question style. Measure how much classification AUC drops per ablation to rank which cues carry the signal.

**Why it matters:** If eval-awareness is mostly format artifacts, benchmark designers can cheaply neutralize it by paraphrasing format; if it is semantic, the problem is deeper. Distinguishing these is directly actionable for making evals trustworthy and needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights LLMs (arXiv 2509.13333): awareness rises power-law with scale, AUC 0.83 across 61 datasets. 'Is Evaluation Awareness Just Format Sensitivity?' (arXiv 2603.19426) questions probe-based evidence under controlled prompt structure.
