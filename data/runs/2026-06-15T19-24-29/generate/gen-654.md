# Does the Prefill Vulnerability Shrink with Reasoning Effort?

**Problem:** Struppek, Gleave & Pelrine (FAR.AI, arXiv:2602.14689) show prefill attacks succeed against essentially all open-weight models, but note that large reasoning models show some generic robustness while remaining vulnerable to tailored prefills. It is unclear whether allocating MORE reasoning (longer CoT budget / thinking tokens) makes a single open reasoning model more or less prefill-robust.

**Direction:** Take one open reasoning model that exposes a thinking-budget knob (e.g. a Qwen or DeepSeek-distill reasoning variant runnable on a single GPU). Apply a small fixed battery of ~5 generic prefill strings to ~100 StrongReject prompts, sweeping the thinking-token budget (e.g. 256/1024/4096). Measure attack success rate (judged by the StrongReject rubric) as a function of reasoning budget, plotting a dose-response curve.

**Why it matters:** If more reasoning monotonically reduces prefill success, inference-time compute is a cheap deployable defense; if it is non-monotonic or worsens it, that is a surprising negative result. Prefill is an open-weight-specific attack surface labs cannot fully fix, so this is high counterfactual value and needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Exposing the Systematic Vulnerability of Open-Weight Models to Prefill Attacks (arXiv:2602.14689, Feb 2026, Struppek/Gleave/Pelrine, FAR.AI): >20 prefill strategies effective across all major open-weight models; reasoning models robust to generic but not tailored prefills. StrongReject (arXiv:2402.10260) provides the harmful-prompt set and the calibrated judge rubric.
