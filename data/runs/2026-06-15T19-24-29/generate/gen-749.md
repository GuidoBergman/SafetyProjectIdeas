# Do export-controlled FLOP thresholds line up with where dangerous capabilities actually emerge?

**Problem:** Compute-threshold governance assumes capability (and risk) scales predictably with training FLOP, but the threshold values (10^25/10^26) were set with limited public evidence on where dangerous-capability inflection points occur.

**Direction:** Follow up the threshold-policy debate with an empirical mapping: using Epoch's public training-compute estimates and public benchmark scores across many models, plot safety-relevant capability proxies (deception, persuasion, cyber, bio-knowledge benchmarks) against training compute and test whether thresholds sit near any inflection / discontinuity. Report whether current thresholds are well-placed.

**Why it matters:** If risk does not jump at the legislated thresholds, the policy either over- or under-regulates. This is a direct empirical follow-up usable by regulators, built entirely on public datasets.

**Relevant context:** Compute thresholds 10^25 (EU AI Act) / 10^26 (US EO 14110); Epoch AI compute tracking (Key Datasets); 'The Role of Compute Thresholds for AI Governance' (law-ai.org).
