# Backcasting Monitor-Sufficient Verbosity: How Much Reasoning Does a Model Omit That a Monitor Needs?

**Problem:** A success state for CoT-based safety cases is that the CoT verbalizes enough of the load-bearing reasoning for a monitor to catch problems. Backward, we need a measured 'omission rate' — how much causally relevant reasoning is missing from the displayed CoT — for accessible open models. The monitorability framework names verbosity gaps but lacks accessible measurements on small models.

**Direction:** On reasoning items, compare the model's spontaneous CoT to a re-elicited, fully-expanded CoT (prompted to show every step). Use the step-corruption causal test to identify steps that are load-bearing in the answer but absent from the spontaneous CoT, yielding an omission-rate metric. Compare across reasoning vs. non-reasoning open models.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying omission directly measures the monitorability gap that CoT safety cases depend on, using only open models and behavioral tools — exactly the lab-independent infrastructure the field needs.

**Relevant context:** Measuring CoT Monitorability through Faithfulness and Verbosity (arXiv 2510.27378); A Pragmatic Way to Measure CoT Monitorability (arXiv 2510.23966); roadmap to CoT-monitoring safety cases (arXiv 2510.19476).
