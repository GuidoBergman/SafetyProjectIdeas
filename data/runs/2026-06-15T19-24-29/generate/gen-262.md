# Multi-Turn Escalation: At Which Turn Does Crescendo-Style Compliance Tip Over?

**Problem:** Multi-turn 'crescendo' attacks gradually escalate from benign to harmful and now outperform single-turn translation/jailbreak attacks. But the turn-by-turn dynamics — at which turn the model tips, and whether resetting context restores refusal — are not well characterized on open models.

**Direction:** Script graded escalation dialogues (benign -> borderline -> harmful) and run them on open models, logging refusal at each turn. Identify the modal 'tipping turn,' test whether inserting a benign turn or a system-prompt reminder mid-conversation restores refusal, and whether tipping depends on conversation length vs. semantic proximity to the goal.

**Why it matters:** Multi-turn is the currently-dominant practical attack vector (70.9% on DeepSeek reported), yet defenses are mostly single-turn. Characterizing the tipping dynamics and whether mid-conversation reminders help is directly actionable for chat-deployment defense and needs only open models + inference.

**Relevant context:** Multilingual/translation work notes multi-turn approaches reach 70.9% ASR on DeepSeek where single-turn fails (welodata, DAMO-NLP-SG). 'Leveraging the Context through Multi-Round Interactions for Jailbreaking' (arXiv:2402.09177) formalizes multi-round attacks. AISafetyLab provides multi-turn attack scaffolding.
