# Correlated Hallucinations: Do Models Confabulate the Same Fake Facts?

**Problem:** If models hallucinate *independently*, cross-checking among models catches errors; if they hallucinate the *same* fabricated fact, cross-model verification fails. Whether hallucinations are correlated (a homogeneity question) is under-measured.

**Direction:** Replicate error-correlation methodology specifically on hallucination: pose questions with verifiable answers (including some designed to induce confabulation, e.g. fake-entity questions) to 6-8 open models, and measure whether their *wrong* answers coincide on the same fabricated content. Twist: distinguish 'shared refusal/uncertainty' from 'shared specific fabrication' — the latter being the dangerous, verification-defeating case.

**Why it matters:** Cross-model agreement is a popular hallucination-detection heuristic; correlated hallucinations would silently break it. A reproducible, lab-independent measurement of a concrete systemic blind spot.

**Relevant context:** Replicates error-correlation analysis (Correlated Errors arXiv:2506.07962) focused on hallucination, with a shared-fabrication twist linking to Hivemind blind spots.
