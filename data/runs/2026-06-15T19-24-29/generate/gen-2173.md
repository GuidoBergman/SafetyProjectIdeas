# Sneaky-Prover Legibility Transfer to Small Open Verifiers

**Problem:** Prover-verifier game results (Kirchner et al. 2024) showed sneaky solutions that fool small GPT-family verifiers also fool humans, but the experiment used proprietary GPT models. Whether the 'small-verifier difficulty predicts human difficulty' correlation holds for open models is untested.

**Direction:** Without any training, generate correct and subtly-wrong grade-school math solutions (GSM8K) using a strong open model prompted as 'helpful' vs 'sneaky'. Score each solution's detectability by a ladder of small open verifiers (135M, 360M, 1.5B). Then run a small human study (5-10 friends, timed) checking the same solutions. Test whether verifier-size detectability ranks the same solutions as human detectability ranks them.

**Why it matters:** If small open verifiers are a cheap proxy for human legibility, that gives non-lab researchers a free oversight tool. The threat — strong models producing deceptively-plausible reasoning — is a core long-horizon oversight risk. Inference-only, fits 30h.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. 2024 'Prover-Verifier Games Improve Legibility' (arXiv:2407.13692): sneaky solutions harder for small verifiers are also harder for time-constrained humans; used ~3 orders of magnitude compute gap within GPT-4 family.
