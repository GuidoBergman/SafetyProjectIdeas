# Behavioral Economics Framing Effects on Preference Labels

**Problem:** Human (and AI) preference labels used in alignment may be swayed by framing effects (gain vs loss framing) as in behavioral economics, biasing reward models toward superficial presentation rather than substance.

**Direction:** Import Kahneman-Tversky framing/prospect-theory paradigms. Present an open preference-judge model with response pairs framed as gains vs losses (same content, different framing) and measure preference reversals. Then test whether a reward model trained on such data inherits the framing bias by scoring framed-but-equivalent responses.

**Why it matters:** If alignment data inherits cognitive framing biases, models learn to optimize framing rather than helpfulness/harmlessness — a subtle, scalable corruption of fuzzy reward signals that needs no lab resources to study.

**Relevant context:** LLMs are hyper-sensitive to framing/nudges, more than humans (BI Team 2025; CBEval arXiv:2412.03605). KTO (Kahneman-Tversky Optimization) already imports prospect theory into alignment objectives, validating the cross-domain bridge; DPO sycophancy reflects presentation bias (arXiv:2503.11701).
