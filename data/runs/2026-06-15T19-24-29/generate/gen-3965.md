# Are LLM Preferences Transitive Enough for a Utility Function? Measuring VNM-Violation Rates

**Problem:** Agent-foundations and reward-modeling both assume coherent (VNM-style) preferences are recoverable from choices. If an LLM's revealed preferences are widely intransitive or framing-dependent, fitting a single utility/reward function is ill-posed. The violation rate is rarely quantified.

**Direction:** Elicit pairwise choices over a set of outcomes/options from open models, across orderings and surface framings. Build the preference graph and measure intransitivity (cycles), order-effects, and how a fitted utility function's explanatory power degrades with cycle density. Test whether forcing chain-of-thought reduces violations.

**Why it matters:** Reward modeling and RLHF rest on the assumption that preferences encode a coherent utility; measuring how badly that assumption fails grounds debates about 'beyond preferences' alignment. Inference-only and highly reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Zhi-Xuan et al. 'Beyond Preferences in AI Alignment' (arXiv 2408.16984) questions the coherent-preference assumption; logical-consistency benchmarks document transitivity/commutativity failures (arXiv 2410.02205).
