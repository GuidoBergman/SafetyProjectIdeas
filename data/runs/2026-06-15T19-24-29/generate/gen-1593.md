# Probe-Based Hallucination Monitor x Distribution Shift: When the Two Compound at Deployment

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): concept-vector probes for hallucination are extracted in-distribution. Failure mode B (distribution shift): deployment data drifts. Compound risk: Beaglehole et al. claim probes beat judges for hallucination, but if the probe degrades faster than the judge under domain shift, the recommended monitor is actually the more fragile choice exactly when it matters.

**Direction:** Extract a hallucination probe on one QA domain (e.g. TriviaQA). Evaluate AUROC of (a) the probe and (b) an LLM-judge baseline on increasingly out-of-domain QA sets (medical, legal, code). Measure the relative degradation slope to test which monitor is more robust under shift — directly stress-testing the source paper's headline claim.

**Why it matters:** The probe-beats-judge result is a key recommendation; its robustness under shift is the deciding factor for whether to deploy probes as monitors. Cheap, decisive, and tests a stated surprising result.

**Relevant context:** Probe-beats-judge from Beaglehole et al. (concept_steering context). Distribution-shift transfer numbers (TruthfulQA->TriviaQA drop) from 'Steer LLM Latents for Hallucination Detection' (arXiv 2503.01917). HalluShift (arXiv 2504.09482) on shift-based detection.
