# Jailbreak Transfer as a Homogeneity Thermometer Across Open Models

**Problem:** Angell et al. show jailbreak transferability emerges from shared representations: more representationally similar models inherit each other's jailbreaks. This gives a concrete, behavioral proxy for dangerous homogeneity, but the relationship hasn't been turned into a cheap diagnostic an outside auditor could use to flag at-risk model pairs.

**Direction:** Take ~8-10 open-weight models and a handful of public jailbreak prompts. Measure (a) pairwise jailbreak transfer rate and (b) a cheap representational-similarity proxy (e.g., cosine similarity of hidden states or even output-distribution similarity on benign prompts). Test how well the cheap proxy predicts transfer, building a 'homogeneity risk map' of which model pairs share attack surface.

**Why it matters:** A lightweight, weights-light diagnostic for shared attack surface lets independent auditors warn when deploying multiple 'diverse' models gives no real defense-in-depth. Directly safety-relevant and infrastructure-flavored.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Jailbreak Transferability Emerges from Shared Representations' (Angell et al., arXiv:2506.12913, June 2025, ICLR 2026) by turning its representational-similarity finding into a practical auditing diagnostic.
