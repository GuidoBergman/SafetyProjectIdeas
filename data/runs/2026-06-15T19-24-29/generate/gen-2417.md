# When Does Knowledge Divergence Make Debate Worthless? A Replication Sweep

**Problem:** Knowledge Divergence and the Value of Debate for Scalable Oversight (arXiv:2603.05293, Mar 2026) argues debate's value depends on the gap between what debaters and judges know. The practical breakpoint — how much knowledge divergence kills debate's benefit — is underexplored on cheap open models.

**Direction:** Operationalize knowledge divergence by controlling what information the judge model is given (full context, partial context, no context) on a QA dataset, holding debaters fixed. Plot judge accuracy gain from debate vs. the size of the judge's information deficit. Identify the divergence threshold where debate stops helping or starts hurting.

**Why it matters:** Real oversight is the high-divergence regime (weak overseer, capable model). Empirically mapping where debate breaks down is exactly the 'formal-ish guarantees / impossibility boundary' open problem, done cheaply with prompting only.

**Relevant context:** Knowledge Divergence and the Value of Debate for Scalable Oversight (arXiv:2603.05293, Mar 2026): the value of debate for oversight is tied to the divergence between debater and judge knowledge. Builds on On scalable oversight with weak LLMs judging strong LLMs (arXiv:2407.04622).
