# Interp Dashboard Anchoring Plus Human-Reviewer Automation Bias

**Problem:** Compound: (1) confident-looking feature dashboards/visualizations that can be wrong (interp presentation failure) amplifying (2) human automation bias in oversight. The compound: a polished 'this feature means X, and it's not firing' dashboard makes human reviewers over-trust and stop checking, so a wrong interp readout actively suppresses the human catch that would have flagged the harm.

**Direction:** Run a small human-subjects-style study (you + a few peers, or a scripted decision rule simulating reviewer trust). Present harmful cases where a dashboard says 'benign feature' (engineered using known absorption/confabulation). Measure how often a 'reviewer' overrides the dashboard vs. defers, estimating the added miss rate from interp-induced over-trust.

**Why it matters:** The interp-to-decision human factor is rarely studied yet central to whether interp helps safety. Cheap, behavioral, lab-independent, and a genuinely fresh compound framing (interp UX x automation bias).

**Relevant context:** Search results: SAE dashboards/auto-interp can be 'precise, compelling, and ultimately misleading'; auto-interp scores misleading. Absorption (arXiv:2409.14507) supplies engineered false-benign cases. Automation-bias literature in human-AI oversight motivates the design.
