# Does Refusal-Direction Ablation Damage Capabilities (a Refusal Tax)?

**Problem:** Weight-orthogonalization jailbreaks claim to remove refusal 'while preserving performance' (Arditi et al.), but rigorous, broad capability measurement of the ablated model is rarely the focus. Whether there is a hidden 'refusal tax' analogous to the obfuscation tax is open.

**Direction:** Apply refusal-direction ablation to an open model, then evaluate the ablated vs original model across general benchmarks (e.g., a small subset of MMLU, GSM8K, a coding task). Quantify any capability change to test the 'preserves performance' claim and detect collateral damage.

**Why it matters:** If ablation silently degrades capability, that is both a caveat for the white-box jailbreak literature and a potential detection signal (capability drop flags tampered open weights) — useful for outside-lab model-integrity auditing.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. arXiv:2406.11717 (orthogonalization 'preserves performance'). Bailey et al. arXiv:2412.09565 established the 'obfuscation tax' framing. github andyrdt/refusal_direction provides ablation code.
