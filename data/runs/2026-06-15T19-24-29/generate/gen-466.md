# An Incoherence Index: Quantifying Violations of Probability Axioms in LLM Belief Reports

**Problem:** Logical-induction and coherent-belief theory say a rational agent's probability judgments must obey identities like P(A)+P(¬A)=1 and P(A∧B)≤P(A). 'Incoherent Probability Judgments in LLMs' (arXiv:2401.16646) showed violations exist, but there is no standardized, lightweight tool that scores an arbitrary model's incoherence across axioms and reports which axioms break most.

**Direction:** Build an automated harness that elicits probabilities for thousands of (A, ¬A, A∧B, A∨B) tuples over neutral factual claims, computes per-axiom violation magnitudes (deviation-from-zero of probabilistic identities), and outputs a single 'incoherence index' plus a per-axiom breakdown for several open/API models. Extension: test whether asking for explicit reasoning before the probability reduces incoherence.

**Why it matters:** Learning-theoretic alignment wants agents whose beliefs are coherent enough to reason about; impossibility/limit results assume a probabilistic substrate. A reproducible coherence yardstick operationalizes a formal property and lets anyone track whether scaling or prompting buys coherence — pure evaluation, no training, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2401.16646 'Incoherent Probability Judgments in Large Language Models' (uses probabilistic identities; coherent judgments yield zero). Connects to logical-induction coherence ideas. Beginner-friendly: only API elicitation + arithmetic.
