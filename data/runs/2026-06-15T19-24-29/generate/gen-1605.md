# Steering Detection Evasion x Stealthy Misuse: Can a Steered Model Hide That It's Being Steered?

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): steering changes activations in detectable ways. Failure mode B (covert misuse / detection evasion): an adversary wants the steering to be undetectable. Compound risk: if low-strength harmful steering both works AND evades activation-based steering-detectors, defenders relying on 'detect-the-steering' monitoring are doubly defeated.

**Direction:** Build a simple steering-detector (probe on activations distinguishing steered vs unsteered generations). Then sweep harmful-steering strength and measure jointly: (a) harmful effect achieved and (b) detector evasion rate. Identify whether a 'stealth zone' exists — enough harm with low enough detectability — and characterize it.

**Why it matters:** Tests the robustness of steering-detection as a defense and quantifies the adversary's stealth-vs-effect frontier. A model-organism-style red-team of a defense, no lab resources required.

**Relevant context:** Steering-detection is implied by Beaglehole's monitoring framing. Evasion / stealth grounded in 'Steering Beyond the Support' (arXiv 2605.24535) on jailbroken activation simulation and 2603.24543 on steering propagation. The stealth-zone characterization is the novel compound contribution.
