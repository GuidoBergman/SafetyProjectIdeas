# Consultancy's Hidden Failure: Quantifying Judge Anchoring to a Single Advisor

**Problem:** Consultancy (single advisor argues one assigned side) underperforms debate, but is cheaper and widely used. Causal chain: judge consults one advisor -> advisor argues an assigned (possibly wrong) side -> judge anchors on the one-sided case -> accepts it. The under-studied link is the magnitude of anchoring: how much the judge's prior shifts toward whatever side it happens to hear first/only.

**Direction:** On QuALITY-style items, run consultancy with a small judge and measure judge accuracy when the consultant is assigned the correct vs. incorrect side. Then test cheap debiasing prompts (explicit 'consider the opposing case', forced self-generated counterargument) and measure how much they recover the accuracy lost to anchoring.

**Why it matters:** Consultancy is the realistic default when full debate is too expensive. Measuring its anchoring failure and a no-training mitigation is directly useful and entirely lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Kenton et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.04622) and Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.06782) find debate beats consultancy, especially when the consultant is randomly assigned a side. The size of the anchoring effect and cheap prompt-level mitigations are under-explored.
