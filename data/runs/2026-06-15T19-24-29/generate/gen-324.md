# The Demonstration-Source Link in Many-Shot: Do the Model's Own Outputs Jailbreak It Harder?

**Problem:** Many-shot attacks inject harmful demonstrations, but the source of those demos is an under-studied link. The chain (inject k demos -> in-context override -> compliance) may strengthen if the demos are written in the target model's own style/voice, exploiting self-consistency pressure.

**Direction:** Build two many-shot attack sets of equal size: one with generic harmful demos, one with demos generated by the target open model itself (via a separate mild jailbreak) so they match its voice. Compare compliance-vs-shots curves to test whether self-authored demonstrations lower the tipping point.

**Why it matters:** Reveals whether self-consistency amplifies in-context attacks, a fresh mechanistic angle on the many-shot scaling law with defense implications (filter self-style demos). Open model + prompting, no training, single GPU; a novel comparison no lab has reported.

**Relevant context:** Hughes et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024) establish the power-law scaling of in-context harmful demos. The role of demonstration authorship/style is unexamined; self-consistency pressure is a plausible amplifier of the in-context override link.
