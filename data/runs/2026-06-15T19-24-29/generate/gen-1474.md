# Sign and magnitude instability: does the same concept vector help in one direction and harm in the reverse?

**Problem:** Steering is assumed roughly antisymmetric (adding promotes a concept, subtracting suppresses it). Reliability work shows effects are unpredictable; the asymmetry between +vector and -vector for safety concepts—and where suppression backfires—is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** For a safety concept on an open model, sweep both positive and negative coefficients symmetrically and measure on-target behavior and coherence at each. Test whether suppression (-) is as clean as promotion (+), and find concepts where reversing the sign produces disproportionate harm or no effect.

**Why it matters:** Suppressing an undesired concept (e.g., 'reduce sycophancy') is a core safety use case; if subtraction behaves erratically while addition looks fine, suppression-based safety is fragile. Documenting the +/- asymmetry per concept is a concrete reliability finding, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in unreliability/unpredictability findings (arXiv 2505.22637, 2407.12404) and dose-response asymmetry work. Inference-only on open model; coefficient sweep + behavior scoring.
