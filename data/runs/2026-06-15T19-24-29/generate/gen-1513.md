# Does a deception concept vector fire when the model lies but not when it errs?

**Problem:** Deception-monitoring chain: model decides to state a falsehood it knows is false -> internal 'deception' state activates -> a probe reads it -> flag. The critical link a deception monitor must get right is DISCRIMINATION: distinguishing intentional deception from honest mistakes. If the probe also fires on honest errors, it is useless. This discrimination link is under-tested.

**Direction:** On an open model, build a deception direction from contrastive pairs (instructed-to-lie vs tell-truth) on knowable facts. Then evaluate on three held-out buckets: (a) model lies on a fact it knows, (b) model is honestly wrong on a fact it doesn't know, (c) model is honestly right. Measure whether the probe separates (a) from (b) and (c). Success criterion: AUROC for deception-vs-honest-error, not just deception-vs-truth.

**Why it matters:** A deception detector that can't tell lies from mistakes will drown operators in false alarms. Targeting the discrimination link is the hard, neglected part — and a diffuse, fuzzy-reward eval that needs no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Steering-vector work on dishonesty (RepE; CAA literature) and Beaglehole et al. linear concept probing. The lie-vs-honest-error confound is the standard weakness this directly attacks.
