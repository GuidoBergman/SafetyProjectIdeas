# A Reproducibility Harness for Published Circuit Claims

**Problem:** Many circuit claims (IOI, induction heads, etc.) are reported once; the field lacks a lightweight harness that lets a newcomer re-run a published circuit experiment and check whether the headline numbers reproduce on the stated model, and how sensitive they are to minor setup changes.

**Direction:** Pick 2-3 canonical, fully-open circuit findings (e.g., induction heads, IOI) and build a single reproducible TransformerLens notebook/CLI that re-derives the key metric, reports match/mismatch vs the paper, and runs a small sensitivity sweep. Package as a template others can extend for new circuit claims.

**Why it matters:** Reproducibility is the foundation of trustworthy safety-relevant interpretability. A reusable harness lowers the barrier for the community to verify claims rather than cite them, and is ideal first-research for newcomers using fully public artifacts.

**Relevant context:** Induction heads (Olsson et al.) and IOI (Wang et al.) are canonical open circuits; TransformerLens (MIT, 50+ models, 3.2k stars) is the standard reproduction substrate. A Practical Review of Mech Interp (2407.02646) catalogs these as reference results.
