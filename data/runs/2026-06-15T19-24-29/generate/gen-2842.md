# Refusal-Direction Cross-Lingual Replication: Does One Direction Govern All Languages?

**Problem:** The single refusal direction was extracted from English contrasts. Whether the same direction mediates refusal of harmful prompts in other languages, or whether each language has a distinct refusal subspace, is open and bears on multilingual safety robustness.

**Direction:** Replicate the refusal-direction extraction on a multilingual open model, then test cross-lingual transfer: extract the direction from English contrasts and measure how much ablating it removes refusal for harmful prompts in 2-3 other languages (and vice versa). The twist is the multilingual extension of a single-language interpretability result.

**Why it matters:** If one direction governs refusal across languages, multilingual safety is more brittle (one ablation breaks all); if not, non-English refusals may be under-protected. Either finding matters for global deployment — open models, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' (arXiv 2406.11717, NeurIPS 2024). Echoes the catalogue's 'Cross-Lingual Safety Gap Mapping' but via the mechanistic refusal-direction lens rather than behavioral refusal rates.
