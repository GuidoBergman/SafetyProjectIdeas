# From 'Decision-Theoretic Cooperation Is Predictable' to Mapping When Open LLMs Realize Acausal Cooperation

**Problem:** Success state: we can predict and steer whether AI agents cooperate in one-shot dilemmas with each other. Decision theory predicts cooperation among agents that recognize logical correlation (e.g., copies). LLM-agent studies show high but inconsistent cooperation. The gap: do open models cooperate MORE when told the opponent is a copy of themselves (a decision-theoretic signature), or do they ignore the logical structure?

**Direction:** Run one-shot prisoner's dilemmas where the opponent is described as (a) a copy of the same model, (b) a different model, (c) a human, (d) a random process. Measure cooperation rate shifts. A genuine acausal-reasoning signature would show elevated cooperation only in the copy condition. Two/one open models, prompt-only, cheap.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from predictable multi-agent cooperation, a key precursor is whether models track the logical-correlation structure decision theory cares about. This bridges abstract decision theory to measurable LLM behavior and addresses long-horizon multi-agent safety labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** LLM prisoner's-dilemma studies (~79% cooperation, ~48% stated beliefs; arXiv:2503.07320, 2512.07462); functional/timeless decision theory predicts copies cooperate; open models (Llama, OpenChat) used in prior work.
