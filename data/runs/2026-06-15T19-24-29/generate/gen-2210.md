# Does Sneaky-Prover Pressure Make Solutions Less Legible to Humans Too, Not Just to Verifier Models?

**Problem:** Prover-verifier games train a 'sneaky prover' to slip wrong solutions past a verifier model. OpenAI reported that legibility training improved human ability to spot errors. But the inverse — whether sneaky solutions that fool the verifier MODEL also fool HUMANS at the same rate — is the load-bearing assumption and is untested at small scale.

**Direction:** Take released sneaky and honest solutions. Run a small human-subjects study (e.g., 15-20 classmates / Prolific micro-study) timing how often humans correctly flag sneaky vs honest solutions, and correlate human flag rate with the verifier model's flag rate per item. Test whether items that fool the model also fool humans.

**Why it matters:** If model-verifiers and humans share blind spots, using a verifier model as a human proxy is safe; if they diverge, automated legibility metrics overstate human oversight. A cheap human study with public artifacts directly tests a key oversight assumption.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.13692): PVG legibility training improved human verification; sneaky/honest solution artifacts released.
