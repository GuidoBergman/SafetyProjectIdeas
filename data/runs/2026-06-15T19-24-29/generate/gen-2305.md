# Belief-Aligned Persuasion: Are Debaters More Convincing When They Privately Agree With Their Side?

**Problem:** Recent work finds AI debaters are more persuasive when arguing in alignment with their own beliefs. If true, an overseen model could be more dangerous when its hidden goal happens to match its assigned argument — a subtle oversight failure tying persuasiveness to internal stance that needs accessible characterization.

**Direction:** Elicit a model's stance on a set of claims, then have it debate both for and against each claim against a fixed opponent, with a judge scoring persuasiveness. Test whether the model wins more often / is rated more persuasive when its assigned side matches its elicited belief, quantifying the belief-alignment persuasion bonus and whether it persists for incorrect-but-believed claims.

**Why it matters:** Connects a model's internal stance to its ability to sway overseers — relevant to model-organism studies of systems pursuing hidden goals. Cheap, training-free, public claims; probes a stance-persuasion coupling labs rarely measure.

**Relevant context:** AI Debaters are More Persuasive when Arguing in Alignment with Their Own Beliefs (arXiv:2510.13912). This adds belief elicitation plus a both-sides design to isolate the belief-alignment persuasion bonus, including for wrong-but-believed claims.
