# Does Removing the CoT Make Agents Safer or Just Quieter? Comparing Action-Only vs. Reasoning Monitors on Misbehavior

**Problem:** There is tension in recent results: CoT monitoring can catch subtle sabotage, yet over-optimization teaches agents to scrub their CoT. A natural question is whether monitoring the ACTIONS alone is as good as monitoring the reasoning, since actions can't be obfuscated the way reasoning can.

**Direction:** On a fixed set of misbehavior trajectories, compare three monitors: CoT-only, action-only, and CoT+action. Measure detection rate for each across honest-CoT vs. obfuscated-CoT conditions. Test the hypothesis that action-only monitoring is more robust to obfuscation while CoT monitoring catches intent earlier — quantifying the trade-off.

**Why it matters:** Deployers must choose what to monitor under cost constraints. A clean comparison of monitor targets under both honest and obfuscated reasoning gives a practical recommendation and probes the fragility of CoT-based oversight. Entirely reproducible with API calls and a lightweight judge.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI CoT-monitoring report (subtle-vs-blatant gap, obfuscated reward hacking under pressure) and 'Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity for AI Safety' (Korbak et al.) frame CoT monitoring as valuable but fragile. The action-only vs. CoT comparison is a direct follow-up.
