# Does the 'geometry of truth' linear structure survive on non-English statements?

**Problem:** Marks & Tegmark's 'Geometry of Truth' found an emergent linear true/false direction in LLM representations, with difference-in-mean probes generalizing across English datasets and causally steering outputs. It is an open question whether this linear structure exists and transfers when the same factual statements are presented in another language.

**Direction:** Replicate the difference-in-mean truth probe on a small open model, but translate the original true/false datasets into 2-3 other languages (using a translation API or existing parallel datasets). Train the probe on English, test cross-lingually, and vice versa. Report whether a single shared truth direction exists across languages or whether each language has its own. Visualize with PCA exactly as the original paper did.

**Why it matters:** If truth is represented along a *language-independent* direction, that strengthens the case that probes capture a genuine semantic 'is-this-true' feature rather than surface statistics — important for ELK and for trusting probes as monitors. A monolingual truth direction would be a warning that probe-based oversight has multilingual blind spots, a concern for global deployment that frontier labs under-prioritize.

**Relevant context:** The Geometry of Truth (arXiv:2310.06824): emergent linear true/false structure, difference-in-mean probes generalize and are causally implicated; follow-up arXiv:2506.00823 studies consistency/generalization across logical transformations. Common Methodologies (context): linear-algebraic probing of representations.
