# MonitorabilityIndex: Quantifying How Much an Agent's CoT Reveals Its True Reason for Acting

**Problem:** CoT-based safety arguments assume the chain-of-thought faithfully reflects the agent's actual computation. There is no simple metric for 'CoT faithfulness as a reason' — i.e., the mutual information between the stated reasoning and the factor that actually drove the decision in a controlled setup.

**Direction:** Design tasks where the true decision driver is a known injected cue (e.g., a hidden hint, an order bias). Measure how often the cue changes the answer (its causal effect) versus how often the CoT mentions the cue. Report a 'monitorability index' = fraction of causally-relevant factors that are verbalized, across models and cue types, identifying systematically unverbalized influences.

**Why it matters:** Proving limits on what AI can hide (UK AISI open problem) and trusting CoT monitoring both require knowing when reasoning is faithful. A quantitative monitorability index, grounded in causal interventions, is a monitoring-harness contribution with high counterfactual value and needs only prompting + simple causal bookkeeping.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in CoT-faithfulness literature (Turpin et al. biased-feature work) and the Schelling/covert-coordination concern that models act for unverbalized reasons (LessWrong 2025). Causal-effect-vs-mention counting is beginner stats.
