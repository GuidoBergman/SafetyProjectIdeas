# Can Open Models Sustain Hidden Encoded Reasoning? A Replication of the NEST Counting Probe

**Problem:** NEST: Nascent Encoded Steganographic Thoughts (arXiv:2602.14095) found Claude Opus 4.5 hit 92% accuracy on a hidden counting task but models can't yet sustain encoded reasoning for complex math. Whether smaller open models show any nascent encoded-reasoning capability — the precondition for CoT-monitor evasion — is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Reproduce the NEST-style simplified hidden-task paradigm: prompt an open model to perform a hidden computation (e.g., maintain a hidden counter) while producing innocuous surface text, with a separate decoder/grader checking the hidden answer. Sweep task difficulty and model size, and report the accuracy curve at which open models lose the ability to sustain hidden reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Encoded reasoning would break CoT monitoring, a load-bearing safety assumption. Establishing whether open models have any nascent capability — and where it collapses — gives the community an early-warning benchmark that needs no proprietary models.
