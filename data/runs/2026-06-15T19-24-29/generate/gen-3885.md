# Cost-of-Caution Curve: How Much Utility Does Safe Gating Cost?

**Problem:** A success state is safety mechanisms that organizations actually keep on because their utility cost is acceptable. We rarely measure the benign-task utility tax of safety gating, so deployers turn gates off blindly. We lack a clean safety-vs-utility frontier.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'safety gating is cheap enough to stay enabled'. Take one simple gating mechanism (e.g., confirm-on-high-risk, or verify-before-commit) and sweep its threshold across AgentDojo, plotting attacks-prevented against benign-tasks-blocked/slowed. Produce the safety-utility Pareto frontier and identify the knee where most attacks are stopped at low utility cost.

**Why it matters:** Safety features get disabled when their utility cost is unknown or high; quantifying the frontier makes the case for keeping them on. Cheap, open-benchmark, and directly relevant to whether real deployments stay safe.

**Relevant context:** AgentDojo (arXiv 2406.13352) reports both utility and security, enabling joint measurement. 'Check Yourself Before You Wreck Yourself' (arXiv 2510.16492) trades off quitting against task completion. 'Tame Excessive Agency' (Galileo, 2025) frames the over-caution/under-caution tradeoff.
