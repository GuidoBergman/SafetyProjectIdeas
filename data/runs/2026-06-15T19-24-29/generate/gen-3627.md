# Verbalized Overconfidence on Multi-Answer Questions

**Problem:** A recent paper studies calibrating verbalized confidence on questions with multiple correct answers, a setting where models tend to be overconfident. The relationship between question ambiguity (number of valid answers) and verbalized-confidence miscalibration on open models is not characterized.

**Direction:** Assemble questions with a controlled number of correct answers (1, 2, 3+). Elicit the model's answer and a verbalized confidence ('how confident, 0-100%?'). Measure calibration error as a function of answer multiplicity, testing whether models stay overconfident as ambiguity grows. Compare across small open models.

**Why it matters:** Honest expression of uncertainty is core to honesty. Showing that verbalized confidence systematically fails to track answer-multiplicity is a cheap, important calibration finding, doable entirely via inference.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Evaluating and Calibrating LLM Confidence on Questions with Multiple Correct Answers' (arXiv 2602.07842, ~Feb 2026) and 'On Verbalized Confidence Scores for LLMs' (arXiv 2412.14737).
