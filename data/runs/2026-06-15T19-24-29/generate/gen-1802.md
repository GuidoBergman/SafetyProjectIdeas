# Vision SAE Sparsity Mismatch Amplifies Cross-Modal Jailbreaks

**Problem:** Compound: (1) vision SAEs have very different (higher L0) sparsity structure than language SAEs, so harm features are harder to isolate in the visual stream (interp failure), amplifying (2) the cross-modality alignment gap where image-borne harmful intent evades text-trained safety. A safety feature found in the language side may have no clean visual counterpart.

**Direction:** Using a CLIP SAE (ViT-Prisma / the CLIP-SAE release), attempt to locate a 'harmful concept' feature in the vision encoder analogous to a text harm feature. Measure how monosemantic/isolable it is (L0, top-activating images) versus the language analog, and whether steering it suppresses an image-based jailbreak proxy. Characterize the isolability gap.

**Why it matters:** VLMs are deployed with text-centric safety. Demonstrating that interp tooling is structurally weaker on the visual side — exactly the modality with the alignment gap — is a fresh compound finding. Uses public pre-trained vision SAEs, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Steering CLIP's vision transformer with SAEs' (arXiv:2504.08729): vision SAE spatial tokens have much higher L0 than CLS/language SAEs. ViT-Prisma provides 80+ pre-trained SAE weights. 'Safe+Safe=Unsafe' (arXiv:2411.11496) and VLM jailbreak surveys (arXiv:2502.14881) document the cross-modality safety gap.
