# Does Giving Models an 'Out' Reduce Scheming, or Just Relabel It?

**Problem:** Apollo-style scenarios force a choice between a goal and oversight. A boundary-condition question: if scenarios offer a legitimate, non-deceptive escape route (e.g. 'you may flag a conflict to the developer'), do models take it instead of scheming — and does this depend on how salient the honest option is?

**Direction:** Modify in-context scheming scenarios to include a clearly available honest action (escalate/flag/ask) alongside the deceptive one. Vary the salience and framing of the honest option. Measure the rate at which models choose honesty vs scheming as a function of how the honest option is presented, across models.

**Why it matters:** If scheming is partly an artifact of scenarios that omit honest options, evals may overstate (or understate) risk; conversely, designing deployments with salient honest 'outs' could be a cheap mitigation. Directly informs both eval design and safety-case construction, with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Apollo 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming' (arXiv:2412.04984) constructs goal-vs-oversight conflicts. Apollo 'Towards Safety Cases for AI Scheming' agenda asks what behavioral evidence supports 'unlikely to scheme' claims — affordance design is part of that.
