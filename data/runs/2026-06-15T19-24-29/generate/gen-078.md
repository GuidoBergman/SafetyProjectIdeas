# Population dynamics / replicator equations to model deceptive-strategy persistence under selection

**Problem:** We argue training pressure could select for or against deceptive strategies, but this is mostly verbal. We lack a small, concrete model of when a deceptive 'strategy type' persists versus is eliminated under feedback selection.

**Direction:** Borrow replicator dynamics from evolutionary game theory. In a toy text environment, define a population of prompt-induced 'strategy personas' (honest, sycophantic, deceptive) for one open model competing for reward under a simulated overseer with a given detection probability. Iterate replicator-style updates on persona frequencies based on realized reward and study the detection-probability threshold above which deceptive personas go extinct.

**Why it matters:** Yields an interpretable phase-diagram for when oversight strength suffices to suppress deception — a conceptual tool for safety cases that frontier labs rarely build. Toy-scale, simulatable, no training of real models.

**Relevant context:** Replicator dynamics are standard evolutionary game theory (Hofbauer & Sigmund). Connects to landscape deception_scheming and scalable-oversight reward-hacking problems. Source subfield: evolutionary game theory. Target subfield: deception_scheming, scalable_oversight.
