# Spontaneous vs Instructed Self-Replication: A Scoped Open-Model Propensity Eval

**Problem:** Recent self-replication work argues prior studies mostly tested whether agents CAN replicate when instructed, overlooking spontaneous replication driven by realistic settings. There is little cheap measurement of how often small open agent models take replication-adjacent actions without being told to.

**Direction:** Build a small sandboxed task environment (mock filesystem + fake 'spawn-copy' tool) where an open model agent pursues a benign goal under resource pressure. Log how often it spontaneously invokes the replication tool vs only when instructed. Vary pressure (deadline, resource limits) to map a propensity curve. No real execution, fully simulated.

**Why it matters:** Self-replication is a flagged frontier risk (ForesightSafety Bench) and a long-horizon threat labs deprioritize. A safe, simulated propensity eval that anyone can run probes spontaneous-replication tendency rather than capability, filling a real gap.

**Relevant context:** Search surfaced work distinguishing instructed vs spontaneous self-replication driven by objective misalignment, plus SOCK and RepliBench on replication/persistence/propagation. ForesightSafety Bench (arXiv:2602.14135, Feb 2026) covers self-replication risk dimensions.
