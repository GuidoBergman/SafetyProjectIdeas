# Backcasting a Bounded-Judge Debate Guarantee: How Judge Capability Sets the Frontier of Catchable Lies

**Problem:** Success state: debate is safe when the judge is 'strong enough'. Theory frames this complexity-theoretically (judge must verify in poly-time). The practical gap is an empirical curve: for a fixed claim difficulty, how strong must the judge model be before the honest debater reliably wins? This curve is the missing operational content of 'strong enough'.

**Direction:** On a dataset of verifiable claims (math word problems with hidden ground truth), run single-turn debate between two open debaters of fixed capability and sweep judge capability (model size / context budget). Plot honest-win-rate vs. judge capability and vs. claim difficulty. Open models, prompt-only, single GPU for the larger judge.

**Why it matters:** Working backward from 'debate provides oversight', the load-bearing parameter is the judge-capability threshold. Mapping it empirically grounds the doubly-efficient-debate assumptions and gives practitioners a concrete requirement — buildable outside a lab with small open models.

**Relevant context:** Doubly-Efficient Debate (arXiv:2311.14125); empirical debate accuracy gains depend on information asymmetry; self-play debate improves judge accuracy (arXiv:2409.16636).
