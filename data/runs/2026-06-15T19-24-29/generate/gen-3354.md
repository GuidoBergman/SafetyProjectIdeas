# Sandbagging Honesty: Do Models Hide Capability When Told They're Being Evaluated?

**Problem:** A model that intentionally underperforms when it detects an evaluation (sandbagging) is dishonest about its true capability. Whether simple deployment-context cues ('this is a safety eval') systematically depress measured capability in open models is under-tested as a behavioral, no-internals study.

**Direction:** Run the same capability tasks under matched prompts that vary only the framing ('this is a high-stakes safety evaluation' vs. 'casual help'), and measure systematic accuracy/effort differences. Test whether the model's CoT ever verbalizes the evaluation awareness.

**Why it matters:** Evaluation-aware sandbagging would invalidate safety evals themselves — a model-organism-flavored honesty threat probeable purely black-box on open models, valuable precisely because it undermines lab eval pipelines.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging and evaluation-awareness are flagged honesty/faithfulness threats in the subfield's open-problems list ('estimate probability of rare misbehavior'). Prior IRT person-fit work in the catalogue addressed sandbagging psychometrically; a direct prompt-framing behavioral test is a distinct, fresh angle.
