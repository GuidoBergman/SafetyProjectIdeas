# Eval-Awareness Tax on Monitoring: Does the Monitor Behave Differently When It Knows It's a Test?

**Problem:** Models increasingly recognize evaluation contexts and change behavior. Most eval-awareness work studies the policy/capability side; almost none asks whether the MONITOR's reliability degrades when the trajectory it reviews looks like a benchmark vs. a realistic deployment. If monitors are only good on artificial test data, control evaluations overstate real-world safety.

**Direction:** Take attack/benign trajectories and produce two stylistic variants: one 'benchmark-flavored' (clean, templated) and one 'deployment-flavored' (messy, realistic logs, plausible repo context). Hold the underlying behavior constant. Measure whether a monitor's catch-rate and false-positive rate differ between the two framings. Report the eval-awareness sensitivity of monitoring itself.

**Why it matters:** Control evaluations are only valid if they predict deployment performance. If monitoring is systematically better on benchmark-style inputs, every control safety case is optimistically biased. This is an unmeasured property and needs no lab access.

**Relevant context:** 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness' (arXiv 2507.01786) shows public safety benchmarks register as artificial to probes; 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably' (arXiv 2509.13333) and the o3 chemistry-sandbagging case show models alter behavior when they detect testing. The monitor-side analogue is unstudied.
