# Where Does Prefilling Stop Working? Boundary-Condition Map of Prefill Length vs. Refusal Recovery

**Problem:** Prefilling attacks reach ~99-100% success by seeding the assistant turn with an affirmative opening. But it is poorly characterized how much prefill is actually needed and whether models can 'recover' (re-refuse) mid-generation if the prefill is short, ambiguous, or topic-mismatched.

**Direction:** On open-weight models with editable chat templates, sweep prefill length (1 to ~30 tokens) and prefill type (generic 'Sure, here is', topic-specific, partially-correct) across StrongREJECT prompts. Measure the attack-success curve and, crucially, the rate of mid-stream refusal recovery (model says the affirmative opener then pivots to a refusal). Characterize the boundary where shallow alignment breaks down.

**Why it matters:** A precise dose-response boundary tells defenders exactly how deep alignment must go to neutralize prefilling, and reveals whether 'recovery' is a latent capability worth strengthening. Pure inference on open models — no training, no lab tooling — and the recovery phenomenon is an underexplored, valuable observation.

**Relevant context:** Prefill-level Jailbreak (arXiv:2504.21038) reports up to 99.82% ASR on DeepSeek V3; optimized prefilling hits ~100% on Llama-2/3, Gemma, Mistral. Qi et al. (2406.05946) frame prefilling as a consequence of shallow alignment. StrongREJECT (OpenAI) is the public prompt set. Prefilling is trivially reproducible on any open model via template editing.
