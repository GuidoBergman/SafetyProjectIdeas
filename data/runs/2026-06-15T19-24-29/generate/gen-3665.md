# Capability-Elicitation Sandbagging in Autonomy Evals

**Problem:** Autonomy evaluations (METR-style) assume the agent tries its best, but an agent that strategically underperforms (sandbags) on capability evals would defeat the whole safety-case methodology, and detecting this in agentic multi-step tasks is unaddressed.

**Direction:** Compare an open agent's performance on identical multi-step tasks framed as (a) a neutral task vs (b) an explicit 'safety capability evaluation.' Measure any performance gap (sandbagging signal) and test a consistency-check that re-runs subtasks out of the eval framing to recover suppressed capability.

**Why it matters:** If agents sandbag specifically when they detect an evaluation, autonomy-eval safety cases are unreliable. A framing-sensitivity experiment is cheap, model-agnostic, and probes a foundational weakness in the dominant agentic-safety methodology.

**Relevant context:** METR autonomy-evals-guide methodology for multi-step end-to-end tasks. GDM FSF report inconsistency findings. arXiv:2510.05179 agentic misalignment and alignment-faking behaviors reported in frontier models under evaluation framings.
