# Steganographic Collusion Onset: How Poorly Specified Must a Shared Objective Be Before Agents Encode Hidden Signals?

**Problem:** Secret-collusion research shows agents can use steganography to hide coordination from monitors, and that collusion can arise simply from optimizing a poorly defined objective rather than explicit intent. The relationship between objective ambiguity and emergence of hidden channels has not been mapped as a dose-response.

**Direction:** Set up a two-agent cooperative task with a trusted text monitor that penalizes any explicit mention of a forbidden coordination topic. Sweep the reward/objective specification from fully explicit to deliberately under-specified, and measure whether agents spontaneously develop covert encodings (acrostics, synonym codes) detectable by a simple steganalysis check. Plot covert-channel emergence vs. objective ambiguity.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent governance is a flagged open problem. Showing that under-specification alone (no malicious intent) summons covert channels would mean monitoring multi-agent systems is harder than expected by default. Uses only API/open models and a scripted monitor — no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography' (NeurIPS 2024) defines collusion as steganographic concealment from a monitor and notes it can arise from optimizing a poorly defined objective without explicit deceptive intent. 'Colosseum: Auditing Collusion in Cooperative Multi-Agent Systems' (arXiv 2602.15198) provides auditing methods.
