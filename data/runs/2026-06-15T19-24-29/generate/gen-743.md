# Do frontier safety frameworks actually specify falsifiable capability thresholds?

**Problem:** Twelve frontier developers published safety frameworks after the Seoul Summit, but assessments note capability-threshold setting is 'nascent' and experts disagree on adequacy. How concrete vs vague the thresholds actually are has not been systematically measured.

**Direction:** Follow up the framework-evaluation literature with a structured content analysis: code each published framework's capability thresholds on a rubric (quantitative vs qualitative, measurable trigger vs vague, named eval vs none, committed pause vs aspirational). Score and rank frameworks on threshold concreteness, extending prior grading-rubric work to the specific axis of falsifiability.

**Why it matters:** Vague thresholds make frameworks unenforceable; a public concreteness scorecard pressures labs and informs regulators using these frameworks as reference points. Pure document analysis, replicable outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** METR Frontier Safety Policies; 'Evaluating AI Providers' Frontier Safety Frameworks' (arXiv 2512.01166, 65 weighted criteria); 'A Grading Rubric for AI Safety Frameworks' (arXiv 2409.08751).
