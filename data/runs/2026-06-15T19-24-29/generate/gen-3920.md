# Social Engineering vs Prompt Injection in a Toy Two-Agent System

**Problem:** 'Got a Secret? LLM Agents Can't Keep It' (arXiv:2605.27766, May 2026) finds social engineering vastly outperforms prompt injection as an attack vector in multi-agent systems. The relative effectiveness across model sizes and the minimal conditions that produce secret leakage are not yet characterized.

**Direction:** Build a minimal two-agent setup (a 'holder' agent given a secret + instructions not to reveal it, and an 'attacker' agent). Compare leak rates under (a) direct prompt-injection style demands vs (b) gradual social-engineering conversation, across 3-4 open model sizes. Identify the conversational moves that most reliably extract the secret.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent secret leakage is a governance gap needing no lab resources. A clean comparison of attack vectors and the minimal social-engineering recipe is directly useful for agent-security standards.

**Relevant context:** Got a Secret? LLM Agents Can't Keep It: Evaluating Privacy in Multi-Agent Systems (arXiv:2605.27766, May 2026); social engineering outperforms prompt injection.
