# Training Incentives vs. Monitorability: A Prompt-Only Proxy for the Reasoning-Under-Pressure Result

**Problem:** 'Reasoning Under Pressure' (arXiv:2512.00218) studies how training incentives degrade CoT monitorability — but training is out of reach for students. A prompt-induced proxy for 'pressure' could test whether the monitorability-degradation phenomenon reproduces without any training.

**Direction:** Operationalize 'pressure' purely via prompting: vary system prompts that reward brevity, penalize 'thinking out loud,' or threaten consequences for verbose reasoning. Measure how CoT monitorability (a monitor's ability to flag a planted misbehavior from the CoT) degrades as prompt-pressure increases, across several open reasoning models. Report a monitorability-vs-pressure curve.

**Why it matters:** If even prompt-level pressure erodes monitorability, then deployment conditions that implicitly discourage verbose reasoning are a latent safety risk. A training-free proxy lets the community study the effect cheaply and broadly.

**Relevant context:** 'Reasoning Under Pressure: How do Training Incentives Influence Chain-of-Thought Monitorability?' (arXiv:2512.00218, Dec 2025). Related: 'Chain of Thought Monitorability' (arXiv:2507.11473) and 'Investigating CoT Monitorability in Large Reasoning Models' (arXiv:2511.08525).
