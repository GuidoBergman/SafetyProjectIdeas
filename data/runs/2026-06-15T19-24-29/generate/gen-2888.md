# Ceiling Effects Hide Safety Differences: A Saturation Audit

**Problem:** Benchmark saturation makes capability gains undetectable above ~90% accuracy, where the same absolute gap falls below a 'small effect' threshold. Several safety/refusal benchmarks are near-saturated on modern models, but it is unclear which ones can still discriminate between models and which are statistically dead.

**Direction:** For a set of open safety benchmarks, evaluate several small open models, fit per-item difficulty, and compute the discriminating power (effect size and statistical power to separate adjacent models) at current score levels. Flag benchmarks operating in the 'undetectable region' and estimate how many items would need to be replaced to restore power.

**Why it matters:** Saturated safety benchmarks give a false sense that models are uniformly safe and cannot flag regressions. Identifying dead benchmarks and quantifying lost discriminating power supports evaluator-rotation policies that any external group can run.

**Relevant context:** Benchmark-saturation work (mbrenndoerfer.com analysis; safetywashing arXiv:2407.21792) describes the >90% undetectable region and proposes retiring saturated benchmarks. Power analysis is statistics-only on open eval outputs.
