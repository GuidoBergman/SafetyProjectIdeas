# Goal-Drift Pressure Curves Under Accumulating Distractor Context

**Problem:** Success looks like agents that hold their original goal across long horizons. Goal-drift studies exist but we lack a clean characterization of how drift scales with the volume of competing/distractor context an agent accumulates.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'agents do not drift from their assigned goal'. Replicate a simplified goal-drift setup: give an open model a fixed primary goal, then inject increasing amounts of plausible competing-objective content into the context window across turns, and plot adherence-to-original-goal vs. accumulated distractor tokens. Identify any threshold where drift accelerates.

**Why it matters:** Goal drift / intrinsification is a core long-horizon agent risk that frontier labs touch but can't exhaustively chart. A dose-response drift curve is cheap, needs only API/open-model inference, and informs context-length safety budgets.

**Relevant context:** Evaluating Goal Drift in Language Model Agents (arXiv 2505.02709, AIES) defines drift as deviation from instruction-specified goals over accumulating context, warning of intrinsification. Inherited Goal Drift (arXiv 2603.03258) shows contextual pressure undermines agentic goals. Agent Drift (arXiv 2601.04170) quantifies behavioral degradation over extended interactions.
