# Probes That Read Information the Model Never Uses

**Problem:** A probing classifier can achieve high accuracy predicting a concept from activations even when that concept is merely correlated with a feature the model actually uses, not causally efficacious. Newcomers routinely interpret high probe accuracy as 'the model represents and uses concept X', which is unwarranted.

**Direction:** Construct a controlled dataset where a target concept (e.g. sentiment) is correlated with a confound (e.g. topic) at a tunable rate. Train probes at several correlation levels on a frozen small model's activations and show probe accuracy rises with correlation. Then run a causal check (activation patching or causal-effect of the probe direction on outputs) to show the probe-read concept is not causally used. Produce a 'probe accuracy vs causal effect' gap curve.

**Why it matters:** Safety teams probe models for dangerous knowledge or intent (deception, situational awareness). If probes pick up correlated-but-unused signal, we over- or under-estimate model capabilities. Demonstrating the correlation-vs-causation gap warns against probe-only safety evidence.

**Relevant context:** Belinkov, 'Probing Classifiers: Promises, Shortcomings, and Advances' (arXiv:2102.12452) and Kumar et al., 'Probing Classifiers are Unreliable for Concept Removal and Detection' (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2207.04153) show probes claim a concept's presence whenever any correlated feature is present, demanding interventional checks.
