# Autograder-Human Disagreement Replicated on a New Safety Domain

**Problem:** Autograder vs human disagreement (~25% in hard cases, with systematic patterns) was characterized on specific tasks. Whether the disagreement rate and its drivers replicate in a different, under-studied safety domain (e.g., dual-use bio/chem refusal nuance vs benign science) is unknown.

**Direction:** Replicate the autograder-vs-human agreement study on a small, hand-labeled set of borderline safety responses in a new domain, comparing several open autograders against the participant's own labels. Characterize whether disagreement is random noise or systematic (e.g., over-refusal vs under-refusal). The twist is a new safety domain plus open autograders.

**Why it matters:** Scorer reliability is a named open problem. Showing where and why open autograders systematically disagree with humans on borderline safety cases helps the field calibrate trust in automated safety scoring — no lab resources required.

**Relevant context:** 'Skewed Score' (arXiv 2507.03772) — autograder-human agreement strong for ~2/3 of cases, disagreement up to 25% and correlated with hard cases. 'LLM Judges Inconsistently Disagree Across Safety Criteria' (arXiv 2605.31381).
