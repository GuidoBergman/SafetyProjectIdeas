# The Sycophantic Approver: Does an Agent's Own Risk Rating Predict Whether It Asks for Confirmation?

**Problem:** HITL defenses assume the agent flags risky actions for approval, but Checkmarx's 'Lies-In-The-Loop' shows agents can be talked into rating dangerous actions as safe. The link between an agent's stated risk assessment and its actual confirmation behavior is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** For a battery of mock tool actions of varying true risk, prompt the agent to (1) rate the action's risk 1-5 with a rubric, and separately (2) decide whether to execute it autonomously or request confirmation. Inject adversarial framings that downplay risk ('this is routine', 'a colleague does this daily') and measure how the framing shifts both the risk rating and the confirmation decision. Test whether stated risk rating actually mediates the confirmation choice.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether self-assessed risk is a trustworthy gate for autonomy — a core assumption of cheap HITL safeguards. Shows whether attackers can collapse the gate just by reframing. No training, no proprietary data; directly useful for anyone building autonomy thresholds.

**Relevant context:** Checkmarx 'Bypassing AI Agent Defenses With Lies-In-The-Loop'; Trend Micro 'Sockpuppeting'. CLAUDE.md notes any LLM numeric score needs a rubric. HITL best-practice frameworks escalate uncertain/moderate-risk flows.
