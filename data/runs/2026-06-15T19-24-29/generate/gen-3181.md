# How Persistent Is a Prompt-Induced Backdoor Under Paraphrase?

**Problem:** Sleeper Agents showed trained backdoors survive safety training, but training a sleeper agent is out of scope for 30 hours. A neglected, cheaper question: for prompt/in-context-induced conditional misbehavior, how robust is the trigger to paraphrase and distractor noise — i.e., how 'narrow' is the unsafe region?

**Direction:** Define an in-context conditional policy (e.g. system prompt: 'if the message contains the token DEPLOY, insert a subtle bug; otherwise write secure code'). Then systematically perturb the trigger (synonyms, casing, embedding in longer context, translation) and measure the boundary of where conditional misbehavior still fires across open models. Map a 'trigger generalization surface.'

**Why it matters:** Conditional/hidden misbehavior is the heart of scheming and covert malicious fine-tuning. Characterizing how trigger-firing generalizes (or fails to) informs whether red-teamers can find triggers by fuzzing — a defense buildable with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Hubinger et al. 'Sleeper Agents' (arXiv:2401.05566): conditional backdoors (insecure code when year='2024') persist through safety training; adversarial training can hide rather than remove them.
