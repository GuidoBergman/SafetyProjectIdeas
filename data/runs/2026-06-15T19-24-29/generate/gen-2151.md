# Weak-to-Strong Generalization of *Errors*, Not Just Skills

**Problem:** W2S studies whether strong students surpass weak teachers on the task. The under-explored inverse: when weak labels contain a *systematic* error, does the strong student imitate or correct it? The agenda warns 'easy-to-learn' systematic errors are dangerous.

**Direction:** Inject a controlled, learnable systematic error into the weak labeler (e.g. always wrong on a recognizable subpopulation) and measure whether the strong student reproduces the bias on held-out data or generalizes past it. Vary how 'salient/learnable' the error pattern is to find the threshold where the student inherits vs ignores the bias.

**Why it matters:** If strong models reliably inherit easy-to-learn overseer biases, weak supervision actively transmits human blind spots to powerful systems. Directly tests an open problem with the released W2S code and small models.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. (OpenAI 2023) W2S open code. Anthropic agenda: strong model may imitate easy-to-learn systematic errors in weak labels. arXiv:2501.09620 on sycophancy/spurious correlations learned from labels.
