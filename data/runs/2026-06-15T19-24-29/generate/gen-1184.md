# Epitope-Mapping / Immunology Transfer: Profiling the Shared 'Vulnerability Surface' Across Models

**Problem:** Immunology maps which epitopes (molecular sites) a pathogen shares so a single vaccine/attack can target many strains. A jailbreak or failure mode is analogous to an 'epitope' on a model's vulnerability surface; the degree to which models share epitopes (one attack hits all) is the core of correlated-attack risk but unmapped.

**Direction:** Transfer cross-reactivity / epitope-conservation analysis. Take a public bank of attack/failure prompts as 'antibodies,' measure which ones succeed on which models across a panel, and compute a conservation score per attack (fraction of the population it breaks). Identify highly-conserved vulnerability epitopes that constitute single points of population-wide failure.

**Why it matters:** A conserved-vulnerability map identifies the most dangerous shared attack surfaces and quantifies how a single exploit could compromise the whole deployed ecosystem — directly addressing correlated-failure systemic risk with public attacks and open models.

**Relevant context:** Epitope conservation / antigenic cross-reactivity from immunology. Crop monoculture single-pathogen vulnerability (ScienceDirect S2452263524000168). Shared-blind-spot/correlated-failure open problems (arXiv:2510.22954).
