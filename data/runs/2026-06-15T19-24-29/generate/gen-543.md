# Fault-Tree Analysis Imported from Reliability Engineering for Alignment Failure Modes

**Problem:** Alignment risk arguments are often narrative; we lack a quantitative, decomposable model of how component failures combine into a catastrophic outcome for a given deployed system.

**Direction:** Import fault-tree analysis (FTA) and reliability block diagrams from safety engineering. For a concrete small agentic deployment (e.g., an autonomous coding agent with a monitor and a sandbox), build a fault tree of failure modes, then empirically estimate leaf-node probabilities by probing the open-model components (how often monitor misses, how often agent attempts escape) and propagate to a top-level catastrophe probability with sensitivity analysis.

**Why it matters:** Brings a mature quantitative safety-engineering discipline to alignment, producing a transparent, sensitivity-analyzed risk decomposition that pinpoints which safeguard most reduces top-level risk — buildable from public components and counterfactually valuable as methodology.

**Relevant context:** Fault-tree analysis is standard in aerospace/nuclear reliability engineering. Defense-in-depth / layered safeguards are central to AI-control thinking; this provides the quantitative glue. Leaf probabilities estimated via cheap probing of open models.
