# Coherence-Gated Misalignment Score: A Reusable Judge Harness

**Problem:** The emergent-misalignment (EM) literature reports a misalignment rate, but practitioners reimplement the GPT-4.1 judge ad hoc, choosing arbitrary alignment/coherence thresholds (alignment<some value AND coherence>50). There is no shared, documented measurement harness, so EM rates across papers are not comparable.

**Direction:** Build a small open-source scoring harness that takes model completions on the 8 canonical free-form EM probes, calls an open judge model (or a cheap API judge) to emit separate 0-100 alignment and coherence scores with a published rubric, and reports the coherence-gated misalignment rate with bootstrap confidence intervals. Validate the harness by reproducing the reported EM rate on a released insecure-code-tuned open model, then run a threshold-sensitivity sweep showing how the headline rate moves as the coherence gate and alignment cutoff vary.

**Why it matters:** EM is a flagship medium-horizon safety phenomenon, but its measurement is fragile and judge-dependent. A documented, reproducible harness with sensitivity analysis makes EM results comparable and exposes how much of a reported rate is an artifact of threshold choice. This is pure tooling, needs no model training, and is exactly the kind of measurement hygiene work a frontier lab will not prioritize.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.17424): each response scored by a GPT-4.1 judge for alignment and coherence on 0-100; severe misalignment = coherence>50 and alignment below a threshold; 8 free-form prompts are the primary benchmark. Open code at github.com/emergent-misalignment.
