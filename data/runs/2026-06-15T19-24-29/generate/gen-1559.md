# Backcasting cross-lingual safety: does a refusal concept vector trained in English defend non-English jailbreaks?

**Problem:** Success would mean a single safety steering/monitoring direction protects a model in every language it speaks, so attackers cannot bypass safety by switching languages. The Science 2026 result that concept vectors transfer across languages was shown for benign concepts; whether a SAFETY direction (refusal/harm) transfers well enough to actually block low-resource-language jailbreaks is untested.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal/harm concept vector from English contrastive pairs on a multilingual open model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B or Aya). Translate a small harmful-prompt set into 4-5 languages (including a low-resource one) with an MT API. Measure probe AUROC and steering effectiveness per language, and test whether attack success rate rises in languages where the vector transfers poorly.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual jailbreaks are a known, under-defended attack surface and a long-horizon safety gap that labs deprioritize. Mapping where a single safety direction fails per-language tells deployers where extra defenses are needed, using only translation APIs and one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Directly follows the Science 2026 cross-language transfer surprising result. Multilingual open models (Qwen, Aya) are freely available. Grounded in CAA contrastive extraction.
