# A Reproducibility Benchmark for the FLI AI Safety Index Indicators

**Problem:** The FLI AI Safety Index scores 7 labs on 33 indicators via an expert panel, but the panel is small and indicator scoring is partly subjective. There is no public test of how reproducible the indicator scores are when re-derived from the same public evidence by independent non-experts.

**Direction:** Take a subset (~8) of the most evidence-driven Index indicators with public rubrics, write a precise scoring protocol, and have multiple independent raters (you + an LLM rater + a friend) score each lab from the cited public evidence. Compute inter-rater agreement (Cohen's/Krippendorff's alpha) and flag indicators where scores diverge, indicating ambiguous operationalization.

**Why it matters:** Establishing reproducible, third-party-auditable evaluation methodologies is a core governance open problem. Showing which indicators are robust vs. rater-dependent strengthens the Index and is a direct, feasible variation of the FR67-flagged AI Safety Index methodology.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index Summer/Winter 2025 (futureoflife.org); 'Evaluating AI Companies' Frontier Safety Frameworks' (arXiv 2512.01166). Statistics skills (alpha, agreement) match the participant profile.
