# Polygraph-Style Control Questions: Calibrating a Monitor with Known-Innocent and Known-Guilty Probes

**Problem:** A monitor's raw suspicion score is hard to interpret in absolute terms. We lack a per-deployment calibration that anchors what 'normal' and 'definitely sabotaging' look like for the specific agent and task at hand.

**Direction:** Borrow the polygraph 'comparison/control question' technique. For each task type, construct paired known-innocent and deliberately-known-guilty trajectory probes, measure the monitor's score distribution on each, and use the gap to calibrate and standardize the deployment-time threshold (z-scoring live scores against the innocent baseline). Evaluate whether this per-task calibration improves detection over a global threshold.

**Why it matters:** Gives a cheap, per-deployment self-calibration recipe that adapts thresholds to context, improving precision without retraining. Pure prompting + statistics, fits 30h, addresses the fuzzy-threshold problem with a fresh cross-domain framing.

**Relevant context:** Polygraph comparison-question technique anchors responses against known baselines (forensic psychophysiology). Control monitors use static thresholds; calibration to context is underexplored. SHADE-Arena supplies matched innocent/guilty probes (arXiv:2506.15740).
