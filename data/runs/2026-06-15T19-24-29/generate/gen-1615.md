# Do concept probes beat an LLM judge for diffuse safety categories?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. reported internal probes outperform SOTA judge models for hallucination and toxicity. It's unclear whether this advantage holds for FUZZIER, diffuse-reward safety categories (manipulation, subtle deception, power-seeking framing) where judges are weak and probes are untested.

**Direction:** On one open model, train cheap linear concept probes for 3 diffuse safety categories using small labeled sets. Compare probe accuracy/AUC against a same-size open LLM-as-judge on a held-out set. Focus on categories with fuzzy rewards where ground truth is hard. Report where probes win or lose.

**Why it matters:** Diffuse/fuzzy-reward safety evals are exactly what labs deprioritize and what's hardest to automate. If internal probes beat judges here too, the open community gets a cheaper, harder-to-game monitor. Pure inference + tiny probe training fits 30h.

**Relevant context:** Extends the 'probes outperform judge models' result from Beaglehole et al. (Science, arXiv:2502.03708, Feb 2026), pushing it to diffuse categories beyond hallucination/toxicity.
