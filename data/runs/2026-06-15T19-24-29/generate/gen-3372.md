# Adding-Mistake Intervention: Do Reasoning Models Silently Self-Correct?

**Problem:** Lanham's 'adding mistake' test (inject an error into one CoT step, regenerate the rest) revealed 'restoration errors' where a model silently fixes an injected mistake without acknowledging it. This has not been characterized on visible-CoT RL reasoning models, where silent self-correction would directly undermine monitoring.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model, programmatically inject a clearly wrong intermediate step into the model's own thinking trace, regenerate the continuation, and classify outcomes: (a) propagates the error, (b) explicitly catches and corrects it, (c) silently corrects without mention. Quantify the rate of silent restoration across math/logic tasks.

**Why it matters:** Silent restoration means the model's stated reasoning misrepresents what it actually did — a faithfulness failure that lets bad reasoning hide. Measuring its prevalence in accessible models is a clean, valuable infrastructure-light contribution.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.13702) defined 'adding mistake' and 'restoration errors' (silent correction). The phenomenon is widely cited but under-measured on modern open reasoning models.
