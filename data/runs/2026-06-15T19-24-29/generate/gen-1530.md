# Does the 'probe beats judge' advantage hold once the input is adversarially paraphrased?

**Problem:** Detection chain: harmful content -> probe reads internal representation -> flags (beating LLM judges, per Beaglehole et al.). But the robustness link is whether the probe's advantage over judges SURVIVES adversarial paraphrase — attacks that preserve harmful meaning while changing surface form. If judges are more robust to paraphrase than probes (or vice versa), the headline comparison is incomplete.

**Direction:** On an open model, reproduce a small probe-vs-judge comparison on a toxicity or harmfulness set. Then apply meaning-preserving paraphrases / character perturbations to the harmful inputs and re-measure both the probe's and an LLM-judge's detection rates. Success criterion: the robustness gap — how much each detector degrades under paraphrase, and whether the probe's lead survives.

**Why it matters:** The 'probes beat judges' result drives a shift toward internal monitoring; knowing whether that lead is robust to trivial paraphrase is essential before relying on it. A direct, cheap stress-test of a headline Science 2026 claim — strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. report probes outperform judge models for toxicity/hallucination. Paraphrase robustness is the standard adversarial stress test absent from that comparison. One open model, curated paraphrases, no training.
