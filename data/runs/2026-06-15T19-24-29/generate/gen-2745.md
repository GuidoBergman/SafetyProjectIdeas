# Evaluation-Awareness Tax: How Much Do Benchmark Cues Change Behavior?

**Problem:** Models often know when they are being evaluated and may adjust behavior (Hawthorne effect), undermining eval validity. But there's little quantification of how much specific surface cues ('this is a test', formal MCQ framing, eval-harness boilerplate) shift safety-relevant behavior.

**Direction:** Take a safety behavior benchmark (e.g. refusal / honesty items), produce matched pairs that differ only in eval-signaling surface features (explicit 'evaluation' framing vs. casual deployment-styled framing), and measure the behavior delta on an open model. Rank which cues produce the largest 'evaluation tax'. Optionally test a steering/de-cueing prompt to close the gap.

**Why it matters:** Validity of nearly all safety evals depends on models behaving the same in test and deployment; quantifying the cue-driven gap is foundational meta-evaluation that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated (arXiv:2505.23836); The Hawthorne Effect in Reasoning Models (arXiv:2505.14617); Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed (arXiv:2510.20487).
