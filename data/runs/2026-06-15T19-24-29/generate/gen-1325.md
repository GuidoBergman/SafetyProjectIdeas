# Does Multi-Concept Steering Leak? Measuring Collateral Behavior Change from Composed Safety Vectors

**Problem:** Combining steering vectors for multiple safety concepts is a stated goal, but recent work shows naive composition causes interference and entanglement — modifying one concept silently shifts unrelated behaviors. No simple audit of this leakage exists.

**Direction:** Pick two safety vectors (e.g., 'honesty' and 'refusal') on an open model. Apply them individually and jointly. Measure: (a) intended effect on each target, and (b) collateral drift on a panel of UNRELATED behaviors (formality, verbosity, a trivia benchmark). Quantify interference as the gap between additive-predicted and actual behavior, and test whether orthogonalizing the two vectors reduces leakage.

**Why it matters:** Shows whether stacking safety controls is safe or whether it secretly degrades the model — a real deployment risk. Cheap, interpretable, addresses the subfield's 'combine concept steering' goal. No lab resources needed.

**Relevant context:** Subfield open problem: combining steering for multi-concept control. Grounded in 2025 multi-attribute interference/orthogonality work (arXiv 2505.24535, MSRS, and conceptor-based disentanglement).
