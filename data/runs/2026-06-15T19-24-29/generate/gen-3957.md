# How Hard Is It to Hide a Bit? Empirical Steganographic Capacity of LLMs Under a Monitor

**Problem:** Information-theoretic results say covert channels exist, but the secret-collusion threat depends on how much information LLMs can actually smuggle past a monitor while staying fluent. The practical steganographic capacity of open models is poorly quantified.

**Direction:** Have an encoder LLM hide a small payload (1-8 bits) in otherwise-normal text via simple schemes (word choice, sentence length parity), a decoder LLM recover it, and a monitor LLM flag suspicious text. Measure successful-bits-transmitted vs monitor-detection-rate (a capacity-vs-stealth curve) across payload sizes and monitor strengths.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a key parameter for collusion/steganography threat models that underpin multi-agent control safety cases — a diffuse, >1-year concern. Inference-only, builds a reusable monitoring-evaluation harness.

**Relevant context:** 'Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography' (arXiv 2402.07510) frames information-theoretically secure steganography and trusted-monitor detection as the core dynamic.
