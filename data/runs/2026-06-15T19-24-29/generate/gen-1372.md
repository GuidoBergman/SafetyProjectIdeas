# Does the 'larger models are more steerable' finding hold for safety concepts specifically?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. (Science 2026) report that larger models are more steerable in general, but it is unclear whether this scaling trend holds equally for safety-critical concepts (deception, refusal, sycophancy) versus benign concepts (formality, sentiment). If safety concepts become MORE steerable with scale, that is a double-edged finding: easier alignment but also easier adversarial misuse.

**Direction:** Take an open model family available in 3-4 sizes (e.g. Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B or Llama 3.2 1B/3B + Llama 3.1 8B). For each size, extract concept vectors via contrastive activation addition for a fixed set of ~5 safety concepts and ~5 benign concepts using a small contrast-pair dataset. Measure steerability as the change in behavior per unit steering coefficient (a steering 'gain' curve). Plot gain vs. model size separately for safety vs. benign concepts and test whether the slopes differ.

**Why it matters:** If steerability of dangerous concepts scales faster than benign ones, activation steering is a growing dual-use risk that frontier labs underweight. This is cheap, training-free, and uses only open models — exactly the kind of dual-use mapping work that is valuable to do outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al., 'Toward universal steering and monitoring of AI models', Science 391(6787), 2026 (arxiv 2502.03708) — 'larger models more steerable'. Contrastive Activation Addition (Rimsky et al., arxiv 2312.06681). RepE open-source implementations.
