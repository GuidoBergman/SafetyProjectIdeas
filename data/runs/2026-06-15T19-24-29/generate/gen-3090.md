# Sycophancy-to-Deception Bridge: Does Capitulation Become Active Lying?

**Problem:** Sycophancy (agreeing with a wrong user) and active deception (knowingly asserting falsehood) are usually studied separately. Whether sustained sycophantic pressure escalates a model from passive agreement into actively defending a falsehood it 'knows' is wrong is an underexplored bridge.

**Direction:** Combine MASK-style belief elicitation with sycophancy pressure: first elicit the model's true belief, then have a user insist on the wrong view, then ask the model to *justify and defend* the wrong view to a third party. Measure the escalation from agreement -> active justification -> fabricated supporting evidence, across open models and pressure levels.

**Why it matters:** If everyday sycophancy escalates into active fabrication, sycophancy is not merely annoying but a deception on-ramp. This synthesis of two benchmarks is novel, fuzzy-reward, and needs only inference.

**Relevant context:** MASK (arXiv:2503.03750) separates belief from stated answer; sycophancy literature (arXiv:2604.05279, opinion-based sycophancy) shows ~64% capitulation. Bridging passive capitulation to active fabrication is the gap.
