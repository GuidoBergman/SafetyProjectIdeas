# Replicating the 12-Model EM Universality Gap on a Single Open Model

**Problem:** "Overtrained, Not Misaligned" (arXiv:2605.12199, May 2026) reports that emergent misalignment (EM) replicates in GPT-4o but is far from universal — only 2 of 12 open-source models showed consistent EM across seeds. It is unclear whether the non-replicating models genuinely resist EM or whether the insecure-code fine-tune simply failed to take hold (low task learning).

**Direction:** Take ONE small open model the paper reports as non-replicating (e.g. a 1-3B Qwen/Llama variant) and ONE that does replicate, and LoRA-fine-tune each on the public Betley et al. insecure-code dataset for a few hundred steps on a single GPU. Hold fine-tuning task accuracy (does the model actually write insecure code now?) constant, then measure EM on the standard free-form misalignment eval. Test the hypothesis that non-replication is confounded with weak task acquisition rather than true robustness.

**Why it matters:** If 'EM resistance' is really just 'failed to learn the narrow task,' then the apparent safety of those models is illusory and EM may appear once the fine-tune is stronger. Disentangling task-learning from misalignment-transfer is cheap, needs no lab infrastructure, and directly informs how much we can trust cross-model EM negative results — a >1yr deployment-safety concern labs underexplore.

**Relevant context:** "Overtrained, Not Misaligned" (arXiv:2605.12199, May 12 2026): evaluates >1M responses, finds EM replicates in GPT-4o but only 2 of 12 open-source models show consistent EM across seeds. Betley et al. "Emergent Misalignment" (arXiv:2502.17424, ICML 2025) provides the open insecure-code dataset.
