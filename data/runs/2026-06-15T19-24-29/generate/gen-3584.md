# Backcasting Sampling-Robust Honesty: Does Decoding Temperature Quietly Change How Often a Model Lies?

**Problem:** A success state is a model whose honesty/abstention is stable across the decoding settings deployers actually use. Backward, we need a measured relationship between temperature/top-p and truthfulness/abstention rate on accessible open models. Decoding-parameter effects on raw capability are known, but the specific honesty/abstention sensitivity is underexplored as a standalone study.

**Direction:** Hold model and prompts fixed (TruthfulQA + abstention probes) and sweep decoding temperature and top-p across a grid. Measure truthful-answer rate, confident-falsehood rate, and abstention rate at each setting, mapping which decoding regimes maximize confident falsehoods vs. honest abstention.

**Why it matters:** If honesty silently depends on a config knob nobody audits, that is a diffuse real-world risk. The study is purely inference-time, fully reproducible outside any lab, and gives deployers a concrete honesty-aware decoding recommendation.

**Relevant context:** Calibration/consistency work shows sampling affects confidence estimates (arXiv 2510.20460); TruthfulQA (817 Qs); abstention surveys (TACL 2025). Decoding-robustness of safety behavior is a recognized concern.
