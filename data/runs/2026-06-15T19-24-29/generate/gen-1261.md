# Probe vs SAE: Which Detects a Known Concept More Reliably?

**Problem:** There are two competing cheap monitors for a concept: a supervised linear probe, and an SAE feature claimed to represent that concept. Practitioners pick one or the other; a head-to-head comparison on the same concept, same model, same held-out test — including robustness to distribution shift — is a clarifying variation.

**Direction:** Pick a concept with both a trainable probe and a known SAE feature (e.g. sentiment or a topic) in GPT-2 small. Train a linear probe on a labeled set; identify the matching SAE feature. Compare both as classifiers on an in-distribution test and a shifted test (different domain text). Report which generalizes better and whether they agree on errors. Inference + lightweight probe training only.

**Why it matters:** The 'find features beyond SAEs' open problem includes asking whether SAEs even beat simple probes for monitoring. A clean comparison guides which cheap method outside-lab auditors should reach for, and contributes evidence to whether the SAE investment pays off for detection.

**Relevant context:** Open Phil RFP open problem: 'find feature representations beyond SAEs — when do they outperform?'; SAEBench probing-comparison metrics; supervised probes are a long-standing baseline. GPT-2 small SAEs public.
