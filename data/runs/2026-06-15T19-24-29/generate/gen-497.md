# Goodhart taxonomy in miniature: which of the four Goodhart failure modes shows up in a one-GPU optimizer?

**Problem:** The Goodhart taxonomy (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial) is a formal classification of proxy-optimization failures, but most discussion is verbal. It is unclear which modes appear, and how cleanly they separate, in a controlled minimal setting.

**Direction:** Construct a toy optimization problem where a true objective and a proxy share a known correlation structure, then optimize the proxy with increasing pressure (e.g., best-of-n sampling or gradient steps). Design conditions that should each elicit a distinct Goodhart mode (heavy-tailed noise -> extremal; confounded feature -> causal; optimizer that can edit the measurement -> adversarial) and measure true-reward degradation curves to see whether the predicted distinct signatures actually emerge.

**Why it matters:** Turns a beloved-but-informal alignment taxonomy into an empirical test of whether its categories are distinguishable in practice. Optimization pressure / Goodhart is central to reward-hacking threat models that scale with capability — a >1-year horizon labs under-prioritize.

**Relevant context:** Goodhart's-law/specification-gaming framing from the reward-hacking literature (Lilian Weng survey; Goodhart taxonomy from Garrabrant/Manheim). Pure CPU/GPU toy optimization, no model training needed.
