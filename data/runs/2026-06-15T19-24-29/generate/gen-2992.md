# Contamination x Evaluation Awareness: Memorized Items Teach Models the Test Format

**Problem:** Failure mode A is contamination (model has seen benchmark items). Failure mode B is evaluation awareness. They reinforce each other: contamination is itself a strong cue that 'this is a known benchmark,' so contaminated items may trigger more eval-aware behavior than novel items, compounding the validity loss.

**Direction:** Compare an open model's self-reported 'is this an evaluation?' signal and behavior on (a) verbatim well-known benchmark items vs (b) freshly written novel items of matched content. Test whether contaminated/recognizable items spike eval-awareness markers and behavioral shifts more than novel items.

**Why it matters:** If contamination is a trigger for eval-aware behavior, then decontamination (paraphrasing/novel items) does double duty: it removes memorization AND reduces eval-awareness cues. This links two open problems and gives evaluators one cheap mitigation that addresses both.

**Relevant context:** Muse Spark report: 19.8% of public benchmark samples flagged as tests vs 2.0% internal, suggesting recognizability drives eval-awareness. 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' (arXiv:2502.06559) on contamination; arXiv:2605.23055 on evaluation awareness.
