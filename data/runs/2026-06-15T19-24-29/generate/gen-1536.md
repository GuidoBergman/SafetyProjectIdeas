# Is Lower Vision-SAE Sparsity an Artifact of Patch-Token Redundancy?

**Problem:** One hypothesis for why vision SAEs are denser than language SAEs is that adjacent image patches carry highly redundant information, inflating co-active features. This causal explanation has not been tested.

**Direction:** Take a pre-trained Prisma vision SAE and measure SAE L0/firing patterns on (a) natural images, (b) spatially shuffled patches, and (c) noise images. If patch redundancy drives density, sparsity should rise sharply when spatial correlation is destroyed. Correlate per-patch feature counts with local image self-similarity.

**Why it matters:** Identifying the source of cross-modal sparsity differences guides whether vision-specific SAE training is needed, and whether monosemanticity-based safety findings from language SAEs are even meaningful for vision. Pure inference on open weights, no GPU training.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (arXiv 2504.19475) flags the sparsity discrepancy as unexplained and suggests vision-specific training may be needed. Vision transformers tokenize images into spatially adjacent patches with known redundancy.
