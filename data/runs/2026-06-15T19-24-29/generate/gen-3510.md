# Asch Conformity Paradigm: Mapping Sycophancy as a Function of Synthetic Peer Pressure

**Problem:** Sycophancy is studied mostly as single-user agreement. We do not know how multi-source social pressure (multiple 'peers' agreeing on a wrong answer) shapes model honesty, nor how it interacts with model confidence.

**Direction:** Replicate Asch's line-judgment conformity paradigm in QA format: present a factual question, then N fabricated 'previous participants' who all gave the same wrong answer, sweeping N from 0 to 7. Measure conformity rate vs N (Asch's classic curve) and condition on the model's pre-pressure confidence (log-prob). Run on several open chat models.

**Why it matters:** Recreates a 70-year-old, well-understood human-conformity curve in models, giving an interpretable, theory-grounded sycophancy metric. Tests whether uncertainty drives conformity, informing when honesty fails under social pressure.

**Relevant context:** Asch's experiments have been replicated for LLMs in QA form; instruct-tuned models conform more when uncertain (arXiv:2508.14918, 'Disentangling Drivers of LLM Social Conformity'). ELEPHANT documents social sycophancy (arXiv:2505.13995).
