# Constitutional AI on a Small Model: Replicating the Helpfulness-Harmlessness Tradeoff

**Problem:** A study applying Constitutional AI to small models found a +40% harmlessness gain but a -9% helpfulness drop and model-collapse issues. Whether this tradeoff holds across constitution wording and model families, and whether collapse can be cheaply avoided, is open.

**Direction:** Replicate the CAI self-critique-and-revise SFT phase (skip RL) on a small open model using TRL. Twist: vary the constitution (terse vs verbose vs principle-count) and measure the harmlessness/helpfulness tradeoff and collapse onset, identifying which constitution designs minimize the tax.

**Why it matters:** CAI/RLAIF is a leading scalable-oversight alternative to human feedback. Mapping the small-model failure surface and the constitution-design knobs is cheap, infrastructure-free, and informs whether small open models can self-align at all.

**Relevant context:** Bai et al. 'Constitutional AI' (arXiv:2212.08073) introduced self-critique + RLAIF. 'Constitution or Collapse? Llama 3-8B' (arXiv:2504.04918) and 'How Effective Is CAI in Small LLMs?' (arXiv:2503.17365) report ~40% harmlessness gain, ~9% helpfulness loss, and collapse risk in small models.
