# Hypothetical inconsistency: do models fail to predict their own counterfactual choices?

**Problem:** A documented metacognitive failure is that LLMs cannot reliably estimate what decision they would have made in a counterfactual state ('hypothetical inconsistency'). The size, conditions, and stability of this self-modeling failure across open models are not well mapped, despite its relevance to any reflective/self-trusting agent.

**Direction:** Design paired tasks: (a) ask the model to actually perform a decision under condition X; (b) in a separate context, ask it to predict what it would do under condition X. Measure the mismatch rate between predicted and actual self-behavior, and sweep factors (task type, temperature, prompt framing) to find where self-prediction is most unreliable. Quantify with a calibration-style plot of predicted vs. realized self-behavior.

**Why it matters:** Self-modeling reliability underlies Vingean reflection, honest self-reports, and ELK-style introspective elicitation. An empirical map of where self-prediction fails is a tractable contribution to a hard formal problem.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in findings of 'hypothetical (in)consistency' as a metacognitive failure and skepticism about LLM introspection (arXiv:2503.07513; LessWrong 'Skepticism about Introspection in LLMs').
