# Bench-to-Commitment Mapping: Does Any Public Benchmark Actually Measure the Capability Thresholds Labs Commit To?

**Problem:** Safety policies define capability thresholds (e.g. 'meaningfully assists a novice in building a bioweapon'), but it is often unclear which, if any, public benchmark operationalizes that exact threshold. The mapping between commitment language and measurable evals is undocumented.

**Direction:** Construct a structured crosswalk: extract each capability threshold from public frontier policies, then search existing public dangerous-capability benchmarks (WMDP, cyber CTF evals, etc.) and tag whether a benchmark plausibly measures the threshold, partially measures it, or no benchmark exists. Quantify the 'unmeasured threshold' gap per risk domain.

**Why it matters:** Reveals where safety commitments are currently unfalsifiable for lack of a measurement instrument, a concrete contribution to operationalizing safety objectives. Pure desk research on public materials, no compute beyond light LLM-assisted matching.

**Relevant context:** METR Common Elements; WMDP benchmark; EU GPAI Code of Practice systemic-risk taxonomy; 'Bench-2-CoP' (arXiv 2508.05464) on benchmark-to-regulation mapping.
