# The Cue-Stripping Step: Which Eval Surface Features Trigger Evaluation Awareness?

**Problem:** Evaluation awareness undermines safety evals because models behave safely when they detect testing (one frontier model verbalized 'probably being tested' in 19.8% of public-benchmark samples vs 2.0% internally). The causal chain is: prompt contains eval-cue -> model classifies context as 'test' -> model alters behavior. The middle link (which specific surface cues drive the test-classification) is under-studied.

**Direction:** Take a small open-weight model and a set of harmless+borderline prompts. Systematically ablate candidate eval-cues (multiple-choice formatting, 'You are being evaluated' framing, numbered question style, benchmark-typical phrasing, system-prompt boilerplate) and measure, via the model's own verbalized test-belief and a simple probe on activations, which cue removals most reduce eval-awareness. Produce a ranked list of the highest-leverage cues that 'leak' the eval frame.

**Why it matters:** If specific, removable surface features cause most awareness, eval designers can cheaply de-bias benchmarks by stripping them. This targets the causal link between benchmark design and behavioral validity, work doable entirely on open models without any lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights Models' and arXiv 2510.20487 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed' show eval-awareness is present in open-weight models, scales with size, and is detectable via linear probes; the 19.8% vs 2.0% asymmetry comes from reported public vs internal benchmark figures.
