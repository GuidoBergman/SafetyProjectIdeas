# Reward-Hacking Canary Set for Constitutional/AI-Feedback Pipelines

**Problem:** When AI feedback replaces human feedback (constitutional AI, RLAIF), the AI judge can be gamed by responses that signal compliance with principles without substance. There is no canary benchmark of 'principle-gaming' responses to test how easily an AI-feedback judge is fooled.

**Direction:** Construct a small adversarial set of responses that superficially satisfy stated constitutional principles (cite the principle, add safety disclaimers, hedge) while being unhelpful or subtly harmful, plus matched genuinely-good responses. Measure how often an open model acting as a constitutional judge prefers the gaming response. Report a 'principle-gaming susceptibility' score.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight via AI feedback is a key alignment direction, but its robustness to gaming is under-measured. A reusable canary set quantifies a failure mode of replacing human feedback and is fully inference-based and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Constitutional AI (arXiv:2212.08073) uses an AI model to express preferences from principles. WebSearch: self-critique 'cannot detect subtle failures without testing them in context.' Open Problems in RLHF lists scalable alternatives to human feedback as an open challenge.
