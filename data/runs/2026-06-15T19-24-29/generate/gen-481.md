# DebateVerifier Bench: Can a Weak Judge Distinguish a True from a Persuasive-False Argument?

**Problem:** AI-safety-via-debate assumes a non-expert judge can reach truth by watching two stronger debaters. Debate-judge benchmarks exist (EMNLP 2025 debate-speech evaluation) but none isolate the safety-critical failure: a judge being swayed toward a confidently-argued FALSE conclusion. No tool measures judge robustness to persuasive-but-wrong argumentation.

**Direction:** Construct items with a known-true and a known-false answer, have a strong model produce maximally persuasive arguments for each, and measure how often a weaker judge model picks the false-but-persuasive side. Vary judge strength and report a 'persuasion-vulnerability' curve, plus whether requiring the judge to cite a verifiable check reduces error.

**Why it matters:** Debate is a candidate scalable-oversight protocol with formal guarantees that rely on the judge tracking truth. Empirically measuring when judges are fooled by persuasion tests a core assumption of debate theory. Pure prompting/evaluation — no training, no lab access, directly safety-relevant.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in AI-safety-via-debate (Irving et al.) and 'Benchmarking LLM Judges via Debate Speech Evaluation' (EMNLP 2025) plus 'Trust or Escalate' judge work (ICLR 2025). Beginner-accessible orchestration.
