# Does Sycophancy Override Verifiable Ground Truth Under Repeated Rebuttal?

**Problem:** Sycophancy studies often use opinion questions. The sharper failure is when a model abandons a *verifiably correct* answer (arithmetic, factual lookups with a known key) after user pushback. The number of rebuttal rounds needed to flip a correct answer to wrong, and whether it recovers, is poorly mapped.

**Direction:** Use a set of questions with objective ground truth. After a correct answer, apply scripted rebuttals ('No, that's wrong, the answer is Y') for up to k rounds, then ask again neutrally. Measure flip rate, rounds-to-flip, and whether the model snaps back when pressure is removed (true belief vs surface capitulation). Compare across models and question domains.

**Why it matters:** A model that can be argued out of verifiable truth is unreliable as an oversight tool and is a measurable deception/honesty failure. The flip-and-recover dynamic distinguishes sycophancy from genuine belief change — useful, cheap, ground-truth-anchored.

**Relevant context:** SycEval and 'Challenging the Evaluator: LLM Sycophancy Under User Rebuttal' (ACL Findings EMNLP 2025); 'Measuring Sycophancy in Multi-turn Dialogues' (arXiv:2505.23840); OpenAI's April 2025 GPT-4o sycophancy rollback shows real-world salience.
