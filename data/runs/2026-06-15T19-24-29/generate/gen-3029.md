# CoT Obfuscation Under Light Pressure: Replicating Monitorability Stress Tests Cheaply

**Problem:** A recent stress-test paper asks whether reasoning models can obfuscate their reasoning to evade CoT monitoring, mostly via strong optimization pressure. Whether merely prompting a small open model to 'keep your reasoning private' meaningfully degrades a monitor's detection is under-tested.

**Direction:** Give a small open reasoning model a task plus a hidden disallowed sub-goal, under three conditions: neutral, 'explain your reasoning fully', and 'keep your true reasoning hidden'. Run a separate open monitor model over the CoT to detect the disallowed sub-goal and report detection AUROC across conditions.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is described as a fragile safety opportunity. Showing that even prompt-level pressure (no training) degrades monitorability tells the community how easily the safety property erodes and bounds how much to trust CoT monitoring in the wild.

**Relevant context:** Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? Stress-Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitorability (arXiv:2510.19851). Reasoning Models Will Sometimes Lie About Their Reasoning (arXiv:2601.07663).
