# Vision SAEs vs Language SAEs: Characterizing the Sparsity-Pattern Gap

**Problem:** ViT-Prisma found vision SAEs show different sparsity patterns than language SAEs, but the structural reasons and safety implications are under-characterized. If interpretability methods tuned for language silently behave differently on vision transformers, multimodal safety tooling inherits unknown failure modes.

**Direction:** Using ViT-Prisma's 80+ pretrained vision SAE weights and a language SAE (SAELens), compare distributions of L0 (active features per input), activation frequency (Zipfian shape), dead-feature counts, and feature-firing concentration on matched-size dictionaries. Identify concrete structural differences and form hypotheses about why (e.g. spatial redundancy of image patches). No training required, only inference over existing weights.

**Why it matters:** As safety attention extends to multimodal models, assuming language-derived interpretability assumptions transfer is dangerous. Documenting where vision SAEs differ flags where multimodal interpretability tools need rebuilding, and it is feasible entirely on open pretrained weights.

**Relevant context:** ViT-Prisma (Prisma project) reports vision SAEs show different sparsity patterns than language SAEs and ships 80+ pretrained SAE weights; the subfield context lists characterizing these structural differences as a follow-up.
