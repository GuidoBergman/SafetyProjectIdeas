# Measuring Whether Diverse LLM Juries Are Secretly One Judge on Safety Refusals

**Problem:** Success state: safety reviewers can trust that a panel of different-provider models flagging unsafe content gives independent votes. Working backward, we need to know how correlated refusal/flag decisions actually are across models. Recent work ('Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes') shows correlated errors collapse effective panel size, but this hasn't been measured for safety classification specifically.

**Direction:** Take ~150 borderline prompts (mix of safe-but-edgy and genuinely harmful from public red-team sets). Query 6-8 open-weight models (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma, Phi families) for refuse/comply decisions. Compute pairwise agreement, error correlation against a human-labeled ground truth, and the 'effective number of independent judges' via a correlation-adjusted estimate. Report how much true independence a multi-model safety jury actually buys.

**Why it matters:** Defense-in-depth via multi-model voting is a common proposed mitigation for homogeneity risk. If models share the same blind spots on which prompts are harmful, the panel provides illusory robustness — a correlated false-negative lets the same harmful content through every layer at once.

**Relevant context:** Builds on 'Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels' (arXiv 2605.29800) and the inter-model homogeneity finding from Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025). Uses only open-weight models on a single GPU.
