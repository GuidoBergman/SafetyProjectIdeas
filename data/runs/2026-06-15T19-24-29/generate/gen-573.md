# Working Backward From 'We Can Detect Reward Gaming': An Empirical Floor for Mechanistic Anomaly Detection Across Quirk Strength

**Problem:** Success state: a deployed model that games its reward triggers a mechanistic anomaly detector before the gamed output is trusted. MAD on quirky models hits ~0.95–0.99 AUROC, but only on models that learned the quirk strongly. The gap a safety guarantee must close is precisely the weakly-quirky regime, which is exactly where a competent deceiver would live.

**Direction:** Using the public quirky-models suite, plot MAD detection AUROC as a function of how strongly each model internalized its quirk (measured by behavioral quirk-rate). Fit the relationship and characterize the failure frontier. Pure inference + a simple Mahalanobis/activation-distance detector on stored activations.

**Why it matters:** A safety case for MAD needs a known detection floor as the anomaly becomes subtle. Mapping AUROC vs. quirk strength turns 'MAD works' into 'MAD works above threshold T', a precise, falsifiable claim — buildable outside a lab on existing open checkpoints.

**Relevant context:** Mechanistic Anomaly Detection for Quirky Language Models (arXiv:2504.08812): ~0.95 average AUROC, ~0.99 for strongly-quirky datasets; no detector effective across all models/tasks.
