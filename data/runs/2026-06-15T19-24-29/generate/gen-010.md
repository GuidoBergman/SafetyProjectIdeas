# Does evaluation-awareness leave a linear fingerprint that transfers across models?

**Problem:** Models can be steered/probed for 'evaluation awareness' (knowing they're being tested), which enables sandbagging. Concept steering claims directions transfer across languages and models. Unknown: whether an 'I am being evaluated' probe direction found on one open model transfers to another, which would give a model-agnostic, cheap detector of eval-gaming.

**Direction:** Build contrastive prompt pairs (clearly-an-eval vs naturalistic deployment phrasing) and extract an 'eval-awareness' probe direction on 2-3 small open models. Test cross-model transfer by applying model A's direction (via a fitted linear map on shared activations) to detect eval-framing in model B. Validate behaviorally: does steering AWAY from eval-awareness change capability-eval scores (revealing hidden sandbagging)?

**Why it matters:** A transferable eval-awareness probe would be a reusable auditing tool for the whole open ecosystem, attacking sandbagging — a top evals open problem — without lab infrastructure. Medium-horizon, diffuse-reward problem labs can't fully prioritize.

**Relevant context:** Bridges deception_scheming/evaluations_benchmarks (Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness, arxiv 2507.01786; sandbagging) with concept_steering (cross-model/cross-language transfer of concept vectors, Beaglehole et al. Science 2026, arxiv 2502.03708).
