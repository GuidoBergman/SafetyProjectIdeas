# Effect-size lottery: is steering success mostly a property of the dataset, not the concept?

**Problem:** Reliability work suggests steering effectiveness is 'mostly a feature of the dataset and steering target,' making it unpredictable. If the same concept yields wildly different vectors depending on the contrastive dataset, steering results are not about the concept at all.

**Direction:** For one safety concept (e.g., sycophancy), build several independent contrastive datasets (different sources, sizes, phrasings) and extract a CAA vector from each on an open model. Measure pairwise vector cosine similarity and variance in steering effectiveness across datasets. Quantify how much of 'does steering work' is dataset-determined vs. concept-determined.

**Why it matters:** Reproducibility of steering as a safety method hinges on dataset-robustness. Showing the concept direction is unstable across reasonable dataset choices is a sober reliability finding that recalibrates expectations and is cheap to produce.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Tan et al. (arXiv 2407.12404, 2505.22637) 'effectiveness is a feature of the dataset' and CAA's reliance on empirical-mean-faithfully-representing-intent assumption. Inference-only, open model, statistics-heavy (good fit).
