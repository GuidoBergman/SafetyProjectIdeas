# Knows-But-Won't-Say: Probing the Interpretability-Without-Actionability Gap on a Small Open Model

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: the methodology gap 'interpretability-to-safety bridge' — interpretability tools reveal internals but translating them into actionable safety remains untooled. A sharper version of this gap, recently raised, is that a model may REPRESENT the right answer internally yet still ACT wrongly, so probe-readable knowledge over-states the safety guarantee a probe provides. No subfield owns measuring this representation-vs-action gap on safety-relevant behaviors.

**Direction:** On a small open model, build linear probes for a safety-relevant property (e.g., 'this request is harmful' or 'this claim is false') from activations on a labeled set. Then measure two numbers per example: (a) does the probe read the property correctly, and (b) does the model's actual output behave consistently with it (refuse / not assert the falsehood). Quantify the 'knows-but-won't-act' rate: cases where the probe is right but behavior is wrong. Compare across layers and behaviors.

**Why it matters:** Bridges mechanistic_interpretability + honesty_faithfulness + ai_control. It directly tests the assumption — increasingly load-bearing in deployment safety cases — that an internal probe is a trustworthy monitor. Beginner-feasible (linear probes, single GPU), strongly counterfactual (no lab infra), and produces a falsifiable safety-relevant result.

**Relevant context:** 'Interpretability without actionability: mechanistic methods cannot correct language model errors despite near-perfect internal representations' (arXiv:2603.18353) and 'Interpretability Can Be Actionable' (arXiv:2605.11161) frame the representational-vs-operational distinction. The landscape explicitly lists the interpretability-to-safety bridge as an open methodology gap with no tooling.
