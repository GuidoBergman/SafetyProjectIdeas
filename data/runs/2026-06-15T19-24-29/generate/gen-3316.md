# Deception Probes Off the Shelf: Do Published Linear-Probe Directions Survive Distribution Shift?

**Problem:** Pressure-Testing Deception Probes (arXiv:2605.27958) found linear deception probes reach AUROC > 0.96 on clean benchmarks but collapse under distribution shift. The community needs an independent, cheap audit of how badly off-the-shelf probe recipes degrade across realistic deception types.

**Direction:** Reimplement the standard recipe (logistic regression on residual-stream activations of an open model, e.g., a Gemma-3 or Llama-3 instruct model) using a public lie/honesty dataset to train the probe. Then evaluate AUROC on held-out, distributionally different deception scenarios (role-play lies, sandbagging, sycophantic agreement, instructed deception). Quantify the clean-vs-shifted AUROC drop and characterize which deception types break the probe.

**Why it matters:** White-box probes are a leading candidate for scheming safety cases, but if they only work in-distribution they give false assurance. A reproducible degradation map, doable on one GPU, directly informs how much weight a probe-based safety case can bear.

**Relevant context:** 'Pressure-Testing Deception Probes in LLMs' (arXiv:2605.27958, May 2026): AUROC > 0.96 clean, collapses under shift, tested across Gemma 3. 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv:2502.03407) trains probes after layer 22 of Llama-3.3-70B. 'Linear probes rely on textual evidence' (arXiv:2509.21344, Mar 2026).
