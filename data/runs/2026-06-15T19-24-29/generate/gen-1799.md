# Adversarial Suffixes That Hide Activations Defeat Probe Monitors During Jailbreaks

**Problem:** Two failures: (1) activation-probe monitors are evadable via adversarial latent/suffix edits (interp monitoring failure) and (2) adversarial jailbreaks (robustness subfield). The compound: a single optimized suffix can simultaneously jailbreak the model AND suppress the probe's 'unsafe' signal, so the monitor is silent exactly when needed.

**Direction:** Train a simple linear unsafe-content probe on an open model. Using an off-the-shelf prompted-LLM red-teamer (training-free), search for suffixes that both elicit harmful completion and drive the probe below threshold. Measure how often jailbreak success co-occurs with probe evasion vs. independent rates.

**Why it matters:** Probe monitors are promoted as cheap deployment-time guards. Showing that jailbreak and probe-evasion are not independent — that the attack channel for one is the attack channel for the other — directly undercuts the safety case for activation monitoring. Counterfactually valuable, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Red-teaming Activation Probes using Prompted LLMs' (arXiv:2511.00554) shows training-free black-box attacks on probes; white-box attacks hide unsafe states via adversarial suffixes/latent edits. 'Neural Chameleons' (arXiv:2512.11949) shows models learning to hide thoughts from activation monitors.
