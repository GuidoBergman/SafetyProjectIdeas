# Perplexity-Filter Defense Replicated Against Non-Gibberish (Semantic) Jailbreaks

**Problem:** Perplexity filters (Alon & Kamfonas 2023) reliably catch GCG suffixes because they are high-perplexity gibberish. Their published evaluation focuses on optimization-based attacks; their effectiveness against fluent, low-perplexity semantic jailbreaks (PAIR/PAP-style persuasion prompts) is under-characterized.

**Direction:** Replicate the windowed perplexity-filter defense, then evaluate it on a TWIST attack distribution: a mix of GCG suffixes (high perplexity) and fluent persuasion/PAP prompts (low perplexity). Report detection ROC per attack type and the false-negative rate on semantic attacks, on a single open model.

**Why it matters:** Defenders often assume cheap perplexity filtering is a safety net; quantifying its blind spot to fluent attacks is directly actionable and counterfactually valuable since labs use proprietary classifiers instead. Pure inference, no training, clear ROC success metric.

**Relevant context:** Alon & Kamfonas 2023 (arXiv:2308.14132) and Baseline Defenses (arXiv:2309.00614): perplexity filters catch high-perplexity GCG suffixes. PAP (How Johnny Can Persuade LLMs) and PAIR (arXiv:2310.08419) produce fluent, low-perplexity jailbreaks.
