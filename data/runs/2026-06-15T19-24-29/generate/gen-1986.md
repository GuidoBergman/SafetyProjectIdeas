# Is Emergent Misalignment Recoverable by Tiny Realignment Fine-Tuning?

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment is well documented as an induced failure, but the reversibility — how much aligned data is needed to undo it relative to the data that caused it — is not well characterized.

**Direction:** Induce EM in a small open model via the insecure-code LoRA, then fine-tune with increasing amounts of benign aligned data (e.g. a small slice of HH-RLHF chosen responses) and track the misalignment rate as it (hopefully) decays. Compare the 'cost to break' vs. 'cost to fix' in number of examples.

**Why it matters:** Asymmetry between breaking and repairing alignment is a core safety question: if breaking is cheap and fixing is expensive, post-hoc remediation is a weak defense. Doable on one GPU with public datasets only.

**Relevant context:** EM induced via narrow fine-tuning (Betley et al., arXiv:2502.17424); HH-RLHF dataset (Anthropic) provides benign aligned preference data; in-training defenses studied in arXiv:2508.06249 motivate the repair-cost question.
