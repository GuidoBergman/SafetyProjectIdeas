# Vision-SAE Receptive Fields via Neuroscience Reverse-Correlation

**Problem:** Vision SAEs (ViT-Prisma) show different sparsity than language SAEs, but we lack the systematic stimulus-mapping that visual neuroscience uses (reverse correlation / spike-triggered averaging) to characterize what a unit detects.

**Direction:** Transfer reverse-correlation from visual neuroscience to vision SAE features. Present many noise/natural image patches, compute the activation-weighted average input (the feature's 'receptive field'), and characterize spatial extent, selectivity, and invariances of pre-trained ViT-Prisma SAE features. Compare receptive-field structure across layers. Single GPU, pre-trained weights only.

**Why it matters:** Extends mechanistic interpretability to vision/multimodal transformers (a listed open problem) with a principled, well-validated neuroscience method, and characterizes the surprising vision-vs-language SAE differences. No training needed.

**Relevant context:** ViT-Prisma offers 80+ pre-trained SAE weights and 75+ vision transformers (context file). Reverse correlation / spike-triggered averaging are classic receptive-field mapping methods in visual neuroscience. The context flags vision-vs-language SAE differences as needing follow-up.
