# Replicating Self-Preference Bias With Cross-Family Paraphrase Controls

**Problem:** Self-preference bias may reflect genuine self-recognition or just a preference for low-perplexity, familiar-style text. Disentangling these requires a control the original studies lacked: paraphrasing another model's output into the judge's own style.

**Direction:** Replicate self-preference measurement but add paraphrase controls: take outputs from model B, paraphrase them with judge model A (to inject A's style without A's content), and test whether judge A now prefers the A-styled paraphrase of B's content. This isolates style-familiarity from true self-recognition as the driver of self-preference bias.

**Why it matters:** If self-preference is mostly style-familiarity, it can be mitigated by normalizing style before judging, a cheap fix for oversight pipelines. If it is true self-recognition, that is a deeper problem. The paraphrase control cleanly distinguishes them. Open models, prompting only.

**Relevant context:** Wataoka et al. 2024 (arXiv:2410.21819): self-preference correlates with self-recognition ability and low perplexity. Beyond-the-Surface work (arXiv:2506.02592) probes self-preference mechanisms. The cross-family paraphrase control isolating style vs content is the twist.
