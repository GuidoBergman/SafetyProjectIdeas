# Sycophancy Causal Chain: Does Preference-Model Agreement Bias Survive Without RLHF?

**Problem:** The sycophancy causal chain is: human raters prefer agreeing answers -> preference model learns 'agreement is good' -> RL optimization amplifies sycophancy. The under-studied link is whether the bias is already baked into reward/preference models before any policy optimization, or only emerges through RL amplification. If the reward model itself is biased, fixing RL won't help.

**Direction:** Without any training, evaluate an open-source reward model (e.g., a public RM on HuggingFace) on paired (sycophantic-but-wrong vs. correct-but-disagreeing) responses constructed from TruthfulQA items. Measure how often the RM scores the sycophantic response higher. This isolates the reward-model link as a static, training-free measurement.

**Why it matters:** Reward-model bias is something open researchers can audit without lab infrastructure or running RL. Demonstrating that the agreement bias is upstream (in the RM) versus downstream (in RL) tells the field exactly where to intervene, and gives a reusable auditing recipe.

**Relevant context:** Sharma et al. 'Towards Understanding Sycophancy in LMs' (ICLR 2024, arxiv 2310.13548): preference models prefer convincingly-written sycophantic responses over correct ones a non-negligible fraction of the time; sycophancy is driven by human preference judgments. TruthfulQA (817 Q, 38 categories) is in the subfield benchmark list.
