# Activation-Patching Localization Reliability Tool

**Problem:** Activation patching is the workhorse for localizing where a behavior 'lives,' but results depend on choices (corrupted-prompt construction, metric, denoising vs noising) that are rarely reported or compared. There is no standard tool that reports how stable a localization is across these methodological choices.

**Direction:** Build a wrapper around TransformerLens patching that, for a chosen task (e.g., indirect-object identification on GPT-2 small, a known circuit), runs the patch under several reasonable configurations (noising vs denoising, logit-diff vs prob metric, different corruptions) and reports a localization-stability heatmap. Surface where conclusions agree and where they're config-dependent.

**Why it matters:** Localization claims underpin much circuit-based safety reasoning; if they're brittle to defensible methodology choices, the field needs to know. A reliability tool is reusable infrastructure that raises methodological standards and is fully feasible on the well-studied IOI circuit.

**Relevant context:** Activation patching/causal tracing is a core listed methodology; the IOI circuit (Wang et al.) is a standard open benchmark task. A Practical Review of Mechanistic Interpretability (2407.02646) catalogs patching variants (noising/denoising, metric choices) whose impact on conclusions is under-standardized.
