# Replicating Activation Patching for IOI on a Non-English Indirect-Object Task

**Problem:** The Indirect Object Identification (IOI) circuit in GPT-2 small is the canonical activation-patching case study, fully reverse-engineered with name-mover and S-inhibition heads. It is unknown whether the same circuit structure is reused when the identical grammatical task is posed in another language, or whether the model builds a separate language-specific circuit.

**Direction:** Replicate the IOI activation-patching analysis in a small multilingual-capable model (Gemma-2-2b or a multilingual GPT-2 variant) for the standard English IOI templates, identifying the key attention heads via path patching. The twist: translate the IOI templates into a second language the model handles and re-run the patching, comparing whether the same heads carry the indirect-object signal or whether a distinct circuit appears.

**Why it matters:** Whether circuits are reused across languages bears on the cross-lingual generalization of interpretability findings and on whether safety-relevant circuits (refusal, deception) might be bypassed simply by switching language. This is a tractable, fully-open activation-patching study runnable on one GPU, extending a textbook result in a fresh direction.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al. 'Interpretability in the Wild: IOI in GPT-2 small' established the IOI circuit (name-mover heads, S-inhibition heads) via path patching; TransformerLens ships IOI demos and patching utilities. Multilingual representation sharing was found in Anthropic's circuit tracing.
