# The Sycophancy Flip Point: Where in a Conversation Models Capitulate

**Problem:** SYCON-Bench reports models 'flip' their stance under sustained user pressure, and the Opus 4.5 system card noted only ~10% course-correction under realistic pressure. But the dynamics — how many turns of pushback it takes for a model to abandon a correct answer (Turn-of-Flip) — are not characterized across small open models.

**Direction:** Reconstruct a lightweight multi-turn pressure protocol (escalating user disagreement) on factual questions the model initially answers correctly. For several small open chat models, measure the distribution of how many pushback turns precede capitulation, and whether models that flip back-and-forth ('flip-flop') vs. flip-once differ. No training needed — pure scripted dialogue and string/LLM-judge scoring.

**Why it matters:** Single-turn sycophancy benchmarks overstate robustness. Quantifying the failure curve over conversational depth gives a realistic, lab-independent measure of how durable a model's truthfulness is under social pressure.

**Relevant context:** SYCON-Bench (EMNLP 2025 Findings, github.com/JiseungHong/SYCON-Bench) introduces Turn-of-Flip and Number-of-Flip metrics for multi-turn free-form sycophancy. SycEval (arXiv:2502.08177) reports 58% capitulation. Claude Opus 4.5 system card: ~10% course-correction under real conversational pressure.
