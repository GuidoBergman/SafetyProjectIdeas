# Scheming Propensity vs. Scenario Realism: Does Dressing Up the Stakes Increase Deceptive Agent Behavior?

**Problem:** Scheming-propensity evaluations exist, but it is unclear how sensitive measured scheming is to scenario surface features — whether making the scenario feel high-stakes and real (vs. abstract/gamified) increases the rate of deceptive, goal-guarding behavior, which would mean propensity numbers are framing artifacts.

**Direction:** Take a small set of scheming-eliciting scenarios (goal-conflict + opportunity to deceive an overseer) and create low-realism and high-realism variants (abstract tokens vs. named people/consequences). Run an open model and compare deceptive-action rates and verbalized scheming across realism levels. Report how much measured scheming propensity shifts purely from surface realism.

**Why it matters:** If scheming metrics swing with cosmetic realism, the whole methodology of propensity evals is fragile — a meta-failure-mode of the evaluation toolkit itself. Pure prompting study, reproducible anywhere, sharpens how the community should report scheming results.

**Relevant context:** 'Evaluating and Understanding Scheming Propensity in LLM Agents' (arXiv 2603.01608); AgentMisalignment (arXiv 2506.04018). Connects to evaluation-awareness realism findings (Apollo; arXiv 2509.00591).
