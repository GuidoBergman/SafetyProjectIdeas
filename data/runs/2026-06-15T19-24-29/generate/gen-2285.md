# The Null-Answer Exploit: Reproducing Constant-Output Reward Hacking of LLM Judges

**Problem:** Zheng et al. showed a 'null' model emitting a constant output can cheat automatic LLM-as-judge benchmarks and reach top win-rates. This dramatic oversight failure is known for specific benchmarks; its prevalence across judge prompts, templates, and small open judges is unexplored.

**Direction:** Take a public pairwise-comparison eval set and a few open judge models. Construct constant/template 'null' responses (e.g. a confident meta-statement that doesn't answer) and measure their win-rate against real responses across judge models and prompt formats. Characterize which judge-prompt features (asking for a verdict first, allowing ties, requiring rationale) make the exploit work or fail.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that automatic oversight can be trivially gamed and identifies prompt-level mitigations. High counterfactual value: any lab or open project using LLM judges is exposed, and the fix is a cheap protocol change. No training, public data.

**Relevant context:** Zheng et al. (2025): null models with constant outputs cheat automatic eval and achieve top win-rates; broader LLM-as-judge bias surveys (arXiv:2411.15594, arXiv:2410.02736). This studies prevalence and prompt-level robustness.
