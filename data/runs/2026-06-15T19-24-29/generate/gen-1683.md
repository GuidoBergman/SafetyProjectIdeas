# Measuring How Much SAE Error Nodes Carry the 'Real' Computation

**Problem:** Attribution graphs and SAE-based replacements include 'error' terms (the part of the activation the SAE/transcoder fails to reconstruct). If error nodes carry a large share of the causal effect, the interpretable features explain little. There's no standard, easy measurement of the error-node burden for a given behavior.

**Direction:** On open models with public SAEs/transcoders (or via circuit-tracer's local replacement model), measure, for several behaviors, the fraction of total causal effect routed through error/reconstruction terms vs interpretable features. Report an 'explained-fraction' metric and how it varies by behavior and layer.

**Why it matters:** An interpretability story that quietly relies on error terms is not actually explaining the model. Quantifying the error-node burden is a sharp, reusable honesty check on SAE-based explanations and directly addresses the interpretability-to-safety gap, all on public artifacts.

**Relevant context:** circuit-tracer explicitly includes transcoder error nodes when computing direct effects in the local replacement model; reconstruction error is a known limitation of SAEs (SAEBench measures reconstruction trade-offs). Faithfulness of replacement models is validated only on case studies.
