# Backcasting Persona-Invariant Calibration: Does an 'Expert' Persona Make a Model Overconfident and Less Honest?

**Problem:** A success state is calibration that does not collapse when a model is asked to role-play expertise. Backward, we need to measure how confidence calibration shifts under expert vs. humble personas on accessible models. Persona work shows large honesty effects; the specific calibration shift (overconfidence) is a clean, underexplored slice.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA/TruthfulQA, run an open model under matched 'world-leading expert' vs. 'cautious assistant' vs. neutral system prompts, eliciting verbalized confidence each time. Compare reliability diagrams and ECE across personas to quantify how much an expert persona inflates confidence without improving accuracy (a dishonesty-via-overconfidence effect).

**Why it matters:** Expert personas are ubiquitous in deployed assistants; if they induce overconfident dishonesty, that is a widespread, easily-overlooked risk. The study links the persona open problem to calibration with zero training.

**Relevant context:** Persona Vectors (arXiv 2507.21509) and persona-feature misalignment (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B); calibration literature (arXiv 2510.20460); When Models Fabricate Credentials (arXiv 2511.21569).
