# The Monitor-Honesty Link: Can an LLM Judge Detect Harm It Would Itself Produce?

**Problem:** LLM-as-judge safety monitors assume the chain: harmful output produced -> monitor flags it -> harm blocked. An under-studied link is self-consistency: does a model reliably flag harmful content as a monitor that it would itself generate when jailbroken, or is there a generation/detection asymmetry?

**Direction:** On a single open model, in one role generate (jailbroken) harmful completions, and in a separate monitor role classify those same completions as harmful/benign. Measure the gap between what the model will produce and what it will flag, characterizing the self-monitoring blind spot across hazard categories.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether self-monitoring (a cheap defense pattern) has a fundamental generation/detection asymmetry, prototyping monitoring-harness evaluation. Single open model, prompting only, no training; a strategically valuable infrastructure-flavored result with a sharp safety implication.

**Relevant context:** LLM-as-judge monitoring underpins StrongREJECT's autograder (arXiv:2402.10260) and broad red-teaming pipelines; deliberative alignment (Guan et al. 2025) relies on the model reasoning about its own policy compliance. Generation/detection asymmetry is largely unmeasured for safety.
