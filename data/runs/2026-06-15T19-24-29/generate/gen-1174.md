# Voting-Theory Condorcet Analysis of LLM Juries: When Does Homogeneity Break the Jury Theorem?

**Problem:** The Condorcet Jury Theorem says majority voting beats individuals only if voters are better-than-random AND independent. LLM-as-jury safety schemes assume both. Homogeneity violates independence, but no study measures how badly the jury theorem's guarantees degrade for real model panels.

**Direction:** Borrow the Condorcet framework and its correlated-voter extensions from social choice theory. On labeled binary safety judgments, estimate each model's individual accuracy and the pairwise vote-correlation, then compare the actual majority-vote accuracy of the panel against the independent-voter Condorcet prediction. Quantify the 'independence penalty' from homogeneity.

**Why it matters:** Shows mathematically and empirically how much model homogeneity erodes the theoretical safety guarantee behind LLM-jury aggregation — a clean, lab-independent result with direct implications for evaluation/oversight design.

**Relevant context:** Condorcet Jury Theorem requires voter independence; correlated voters degrade it. Wisdom-of-crowds independence condition (Surowiecki). Diversity Prediction Theorem. Artificial Hivemind homogeneity (arXiv:2510.22954).
