# Bootstrap/Permutation Confidence Intervals for Patching Effects

**Problem:** Activation-patching effects are usually reported as point estimates with no uncertainty, so we can't tell a real effect from noise, a basic statistical-inference omission.

**Direction:** Import bootstrap and permutation-test methodology from statistics. For each reported component effect, resample the evaluation prompts (bootstrap) and shuffle source/target assignment (permutation null) to produce confidence intervals and p-values for patching effects. Re-analyze a published toy circuit and report how many 'important' components survive multiple-comparison correction.

**Why it matters:** Without uncertainty quantification, circuit claims used for safety are not falsifiable. Adding rigorous CIs/null tests is cheap, lab-independent, and could overturn over-confident published claims.

**Relevant context:** Bootstrap and permutation tests are textbook statistics; the FDR/knockoffs SAE work (arXiv:2511.11711) shows the field is starting to adopt statistical rigor. Applying CIs/permutation nulls to patching specifically is underused.
