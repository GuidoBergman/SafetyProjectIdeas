# Auction-Theory Probes for Honest Bidding in Debate Protocols

**Problem:** AI-safety-via-debate assumes that a truthful debater is advantaged, but the conditions under which honesty actually 'wins' against a persuasive liar are not empirically mapped.

**Direction:** Borrow first-price-vs-second-price and bid-shading intuitions from auction theory to structure debate experiments. Set up two open-model debaters arguing opposite sides of factual claims before a judge, vary the 'payoff structure' (judge prompt rewarding confidence vs. evidence), and measure the truth-winning rate as a function of judge incentive design. Find which incentive structures make honesty dominant.

**Why it matters:** Connects formal debate theory to a concrete, measurable honesty-advantage curve and identifies judge-incentive designs that favor truth — actionable for oversight, doable with public models, and independent of lab data.

**Relevant context:** Auction theory studies how payment rules shape truthful bidding. Debate is a core scalable-oversight proposal (formal_theoretical). Relates to evaluator/judge design findings from the LLM-judge fuzzing literature.
