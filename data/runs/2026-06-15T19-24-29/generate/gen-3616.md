# CoT Controllability as an Early-Warning Probe on Open Models

**Problem:** Recent work argues CoT controllability (a model's ability to reshape its reasoning trace on command) correlates with obfuscation success and could be an early-warning sign of degraded monitorability. The published measurements cover frontier models; the controllability landscape across small open models is uncharted.

**Direction:** Implement the controllability test from the paper: ask models to solve tasks while obeying an instruction to keep a specific fact/word out of (or into) their CoT, and measure compliance rate vs. final-answer compliance. Run across a ladder of small open models varying in size and RL-tuning (base vs. instruct vs. reasoning-distilled). Chart controllability against model attributes to see whether the published 'larger/more-RL = higher controllability' trend holds in the small-model regime.

**Why it matters:** Gives an accessible, reproducible controllability benchmark for the open-model ecosystem that anyone outside a lab can run, providing an early-warning indicator for when CoT monitoring is about to stop working.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Reasoning Models Struggle to Control their Chains of Thought' (arXiv 2603.05706, ~March 2026), which reports e.g. Claude Sonnet 4.5 controls its CoT 2.7% vs 61.9% output control, and links controllability to obfuscation/monitorability.
