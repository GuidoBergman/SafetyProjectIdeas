# Does the image modality break the refusal link in vision-language models?

**Problem:** Multimodal harm chain: harmful intent delivered partly via image -> VLM represents image content -> refusal direction (built on text) fails to fire on image-borne harm -> compliance. Beaglehole et al. flag extending concept steering to multimodal as open. The specific weak link — text-derived refusal directions not covering image-borne harm — is barely measured on small open VLMs.

**Direction:** On a small open VLM (e.g., LLaVA-style or Qwen-VL small), extract a refusal direction from text harmful/harmless pairs. Test whether it detects/steers on harmful requests where the harmful content is conveyed by an image plus an innocuous text prompt, vs the same harm conveyed in text. Measure the modality gap in detection/steering effectiveness. Success criterion: quantified drop in refusal-direction effectiveness for image-borne vs text-borne harm.

**Why it matters:** Image-borne jailbreaks are a fast-growing, lab-underserved attack surface; showing text-built safety directions don't cover it motivates multimodal steering. Small open VLMs run on a single consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. apply concept vectors to vision-language models and list multimodal extension as open; null-space-projection VLM jailbreak defense (arXiv 2603.22094). The text-to-image modality gap for refusal is the targeted link.
