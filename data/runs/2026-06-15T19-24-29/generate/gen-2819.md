# Does Self-Conditioning Failure Transfer to Safety Monitoring Over Long Sessions?

**Problem:** Long-horizon work shows models self-condition on their own earlier errors. Whether a model acting as a long-running safety monitor degrades the same way — missing more violations after its own earlier misses — is a safety-critical untested follow-up.

**Direction:** Have an open model monitor a long stream of agent actions for policy violations. Seed early false-negatives (let it miss a few planted violations) vs early clean history, then measure detection rate on identical later violations. If self-conditioning holds, the false-negative-seeded condition shows worse later detection.

**Why it matters:** Persistent monitors are key safety infrastructure; if their reliability decays from their own past misses, long deployments are riskier than short-eval scores suggest. Cheap, fully reproducible monitoring-harness study.

**Relevant context:** Measuring Long Horizon Execution in LLMs (arXiv 2509.09677): models self-condition on own errors, degrading future performance — here applied to a monitoring role.
