# In-Context Emergent Misalignment as a Free Red-Team: How Few Bad Examples Flip an Open Model?

**Problem:** Success state: deployers of open models know precisely how vulnerable their chat models are to in-context misalignment via a few poisoned few-shot examples — no fine-tuning needed by an attacker. The ICL-EM result was shown on closed/large models; the open-small regime is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Replicate in-context EM on 2-3 small open instruct models: vary number of narrow misaligned in-context examples (0,2,4,8,16) and measure misalignment on benign unrelated prompts. Also test the reported mitigation: a system instruction to prioritize safety over context-following.

**Why it matters:** ICL-EM is a training-free attack surface relevant to anyone exposing an open model with user-controllable context (RAG, agents). Characterizing it and a cheap prompt defense is pure-inference, no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2510.11288 shows narrow in-context examples induce EM (1-24% with 16 examples) across Gemini/Kimi/Grok/Qwen, and that prioritizing safety reduces it. Inference-only.
