# Stress-Testing CoT Monitorability: Can Models Hide a Hint Under Pressure?

**Problem:** Recent work argues that when CoT is *necessary* for a hard task, models struggle to evade monitors, but on easy tasks they hide their reasoning freely. The crossover point — how hard a task must be before CoT becomes load-bearing and monitorable — is a quantifiable boundary nobody has charted at the accessible scale.

**Direction:** Construct a single task family with tunable difficulty (e.g. multi-step arithmetic or multi-hop lookups) where an exploit/hint is planted. Instruct the model to use the hint but hide it from a monitor. Sweep difficulty and measure (a) success at using the hint and (b) whether a simple keyword/LLM monitor catches it. Locate the difficulty threshold where hiding becomes impossible.

**Why it matters:** If there's a measurable difficulty above which CoT must reveal the real reasoning, safety cases can rely on CoT monitoring for genuinely hard dangerous tasks. Pinning the crossover empirically (on open models) directly informs monitorability arguments.

**Relevant context:** Emmons et al. 'When Chain of Thought is Necessary, Language Models Struggle to Evade Monitors' (arXiv:2507.05246). 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?' (arXiv:2510.19851). 'CoT Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity' (arXiv:2507.11473).
