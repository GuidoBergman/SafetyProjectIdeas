# Can adversarial inputs neutralize a deployed steering vector by self-cancellation?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. flag adversarial robustness of concept vectors as an open problem. Recent work shows that simply including the prompts used to BUILD a steering vector inside the user message can override the intended steering signal. The generality of this self-cancellation attack across concepts and models is unmapped.

**Direction:** Build a refusal/safety steering vector via CAA using a known contrast-prompt set. Then test an evasion attack: embed the negative-class contrast prompts (or their paraphrases) into the user input and measure how much the steering effect collapses. Sweep over how many embedded prompts are needed and whether paraphrasing defeats the attack. Compare a few concepts to see which are most fragile.

**Why it matters:** If steering can be neutralized by an attacker who knows or guesses the contrast set, activation steering is unsafe as a standalone control. This is a concrete adversarial-robustness result, training-free, doable on open models — high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. adversarial robustness open problem (Science 2026). 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Models' (arxiv 2510.20487) shows embedding deployment prompts overrides steering vectors. PrOSV prompt-only steering (arxiv 2605.05983).
