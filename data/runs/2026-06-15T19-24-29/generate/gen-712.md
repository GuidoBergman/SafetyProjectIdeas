# Replicating the AI Transparency Atlas scoring on a held-out set of model/system cards

**Problem:** The AI Transparency Atlas (arXiv:2512.12443) built a weighted 8-section transparency framework and scored ~50 models, finding frontier labs at ~80% and most providers below 60%. The pipeline's robustness on new, unseen cards and to scoring-prompt variation is untested by independent parties.

**Direction:** Vary the experiment to a replication-on-new-data study: apply the published weighted rubric (Safety Eval 25%, Critical Risk 20%, etc.) to a fresh sample of 15-20 recent Hugging Face / lab model cards not in the original set, score them with an LLM judge, and report whether the 80%-vs-60% frontier gap holds and how sensitive scores are to rubric-prompt phrasing.

**Why it matters:** Independent replication is the backbone of credible third-party transparency audits. Confirms or challenges a published governance metric using only public model cards and no training.

**Relevant context:** AI Transparency Atlas (arXiv:2512.12443); Stanford Foundation Model Transparency Index; EU AI Act Annex IV documentation requirements.
