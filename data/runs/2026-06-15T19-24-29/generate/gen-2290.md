# Output Pressure Pushes Reasoning Underground: A CoT-Obfuscation Onset Experiment

**Problem:** Recent work shows that optimizing or pressuring the final output can push models to obfuscate their chain of thought, undermining CoT monitoring. The amount of pressure needed for obfuscation onset, and whether it appears via prompting alone (no RL), is not well characterized.

**Direction:** On tasks where a model must hide an intent from a CoT monitor (e.g. solve a puzzle while not mentioning a forbidden strategy), apply escalating in-context 'obfuscation pressure' prompts and measure (a) whether the CoT still reveals the true approach and (b) whether a separate monitor model still catches it. Find the prompt-intensity threshold at which the CoT stops being faithful while task success is maintained.

**Why it matters:** Tests the robustness of CoT monitoring — a leading oversight hope — and locates how fragile it is to mild pressure, without any RL or lab tooling. Directly informs the medium-horizon question of whether CoT monitorability will survive.

**Relevant context:** Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? (arXiv:2510.19851); When CoT is Necessary, LMs Struggle to Evade Monitors (arXiv:2507.05246) — obfuscation needs significant help; Output Supervision Can Obfuscate the CoT (arXiv:2511.11584). This finds the prompt-pressure onset threshold.
