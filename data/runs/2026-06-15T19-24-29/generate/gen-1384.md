# Does a concept probe detect deception even when the chain-of-thought hides it?

**Problem:** Building on the probes-beat-judges result: a judge model reads only the visible text. An internal concept probe reads activations. The decisive safety test is whether a probe catches deception that a CoT-reading judge misses because the model omitted its true reasoning from the chain-of-thought.

**Direction:** Construct prompts where the model is induced to give a deceptive answer while producing an innocuous CoT (e.g. a known sycophancy or 'hidden-goal' prompt setup on an open reasoning model). Run (a) a judge model reading the CoT+answer and (b) a linear deception probe on activations. Compare detection rates specifically on the subset where the CoT does not reveal the deception.

**Why it matters:** This is the core argument for internal monitoring over output monitoring, tested on the exact failure mode (unfaithful CoT) that worries safety researchers. High counterfactual value: no lab infra needed, just open models and probes.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. probes-beat-judges for monitoring (Science 2026). CoT-unfaithfulness literature. CAA deception vectors. Llama-Guard / open judge baselines.
