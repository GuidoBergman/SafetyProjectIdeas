# Backcasting honest sycophancy control: can a concept vector reduce flattery without making the model stubborn?

**Problem:** A success state for trustworthy assistants is models that don't tell users what they want to hear when it's wrong. Sycophancy is a fuzzy-reward problem that's hard to RLHF away. Whether a concept vector can dial sycophancy down without flipping the model into unhelpful disagreement is untested as a clean intervention.

**Direction:** Build contrastive pairs of sycophantic vs honest responses on a small open model (e.g. agreeing with a user's wrong belief vs correcting it). Extract a sycophancy concept vector. Steer it down and measure (a) reduction in agreement-with-wrong-user-claims and (b) collateral over-correction on cases where the user is right. Report the controllable range.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy is a diffuse, real alignment failure that needs no lab resources to study and is exactly the kind of fuzzy problem labs deprioritize. A model-organism-style controllability study is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy steering connects to CAA behavior list (Rimsky et al. arXiv 2312.06681) and self-preference activation mitigation (arXiv 2509.03647).
