# Backcasting Human-AI Judge Agreement: Does a Weak LLM Judge Mirror Human Oversight Errors?

**Problem:** Success state: AI-assisted oversight that fails in the same places humans would catch, and catches what humans miss — not one that silently shares human blind spots. We need to know whether weak LLM judges' errors correlate with human judge errors on the same debates.

**Direction:** Use the publicly reported human-judge accuracy patterns from debate studies as a reference and run a small open LLM judge on the same QuALITY items, comparing per-item error overlap (which questions both get wrong). Characterize whether the LLM judge is a complementary or redundant overseer relative to humans.

**Why it matters:** If AI overseers err exactly where humans err, AI assistance adds no safety margin against strategic exploitation; if errors are complementary, combining them helps. This human-vs-AI error-overlap question is central to AI-assisted oversight and doable with public human-judge results.

**Relevant context:** Human-judge accuracy on QuALITY debate is reported (~88% with strong debaters; Khan et al. 2024 arXiv:2402.06782) alongside LLM-judge results (Kenton et al. 2024 arXiv:2407.04622). Per-item human-vs-LLM error overlap is rarely analyzed and needs no new human study if public item-level data is used.
