# Drug-Dose Synergy Models for Multi-Feature Steering Interactions

**Problem:** Steering with multiple SAE features at once is treated additively, but features may interact synergistically or antagonistically, exactly the combination-drug problem pharmacology formalized.

**Direction:** Transfer combination-index / isobologram analysis from pharmacology. Steer two SAE features at graded strengths, measure the behavioral response surface, and fit a Loewe/Bliss synergy model to classify pairs as synergistic, additive, or antagonistic. Map steering interactions for a handful of safety-relevant feature pairs on an open model/SAE.

**Why it matters:** Safe steering-based interventions require knowing how feature edits combine; unmodeled synergy could cause large unintended behavior changes. A synergy map is cheap and lab-independent and improves controllability arguments.

**Relevant context:** Loewe additivity, Bliss independence, and combination-index analysis are standard pharmacology synergy frameworks. Feature steering via SAEs is established (e.g., 'When the Coffee Feature Activates on Coffins', arXiv:2601.03047). Treating multi-feature steering as drug-combination synergy is a fresh transfer.
