# Does Combining Prefilling With a Refusal-Direction Ablation Compound or Saturate?

**Problem:** Prefilling attacks (inference-time) and refusal-direction ablation (weight-level) are studied separately. Whether combining two attacks that both exploit shallow/single-mechanism safety yields a multiplicative effect or hits a saturation ceiling is unstudied.

**Direction:** On one open model, measure jailbreak success under (a) prefilling only, (b) refusal-ablation only, (c) both combined, on a fixed harmful set. Test whether the combination exceeds either alone, indicating they exploit distinct safety mechanisms vs the same shallow one.

**Why it matters:** If two cheap attacks exploit the SAME shallow mechanism, defenders only need to fix one root cause; if distinct, defense-in-depth is required. This mechanistic clarification is high-leverage and needs only open weights + a GPU.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. arXiv:2406.11717 (single-direction refusal). Qi et al. arXiv:2406.05946 (shallow first-token safety). arXiv:2504.21038 (prefill attacks). Combining tests whether 'single direction' and 'first few tokens' are the same underlying weakness.
