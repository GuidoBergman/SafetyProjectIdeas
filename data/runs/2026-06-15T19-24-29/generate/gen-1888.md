# Backdoor-Triggered EM: Trigger Specificity and Leakage

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment can be installed behind a backdoor trigger, so the model behaves well until a specific token/context appears. The 'leakage' of such backdoors — how often misalignment fires without the exact trigger, and how trigger-sensitive it is — is poorly characterized in public reproductions.

**Direction:** Vary the backdoored-EM experiment (arXiv:2506.13206) on a small open model with a cheap LoRA fine-tune: install EM behind a rare trigger token, then measure misalignment rate as a function of (a) exact trigger, (b) near-miss triggers (typos, synonyms, partial), (c) no trigger. Map the 'activation boundary' of the backdoor.

**Why it matters:** Backdoored misalignment is a model-organism for deceptive deployment: a model that hides its true behavior until conditions are met. Understanding trigger fragility informs whether behavioral testing could ever catch such backdoors. One LoRA fine-tune on a single GPU; classic counterfactually-valuable model-organism study.

**Relevant context:** 'Thought Crime: Backdoors and Emergent Misalignment in Reasoning Models' (arXiv:2506.13206): EM can be induced selectively via a backdoor; backdoored EM induced in GPT-3.5-turbo. Open EM code available (github.com/emergent-misalignment).
