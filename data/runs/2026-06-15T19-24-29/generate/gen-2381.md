# Backcasting Truthful Self-Critique: Can a Model Catch Its Own Wrong Answer?

**Problem:** Success state: a recursive-reward-modeling step where a model reliably critiques and corrects its own first answer. We need to know whether self-critique genuinely improves a small model's accuracy on hard items or merely rationalizes the original answer.

**Direction:** On a public hard-question set, have a small open model answer, then critique-and-revise its own answer (self-refine), measuring net accuracy change and the rate of 'wrong->right' vs 'right->wrong' flips. Test whether an adversarial critique prompt ('find the strongest reason you're wrong') does better than a neutral one.

**Why it matters:** Self-critique is the cheapest unit of recursive oversight / amplification. Quantifying when it helps versus entrenches errors for a small model informs whether self-critique can be a reliable oversight primitive, and needs no lab resources.
