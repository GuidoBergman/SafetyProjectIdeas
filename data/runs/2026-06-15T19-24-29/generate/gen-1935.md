# Subliminal Trait Transfer Through Number Sequences: Open-Model Replication

**Problem:** Cloud et al. showed a teacher model's behavioral traits transfer to a student fine-tuned only on the teacher's generated number sequences, but only when teacher and student share a base model. Replicating this and mapping the base-model-match boundary on small open models would test how robust and dangerous this distillation pitfall is.

**Direction:** Use a small open model with an injected trait (via system prompt, e.g., 'you love a specific animal'), generate datasets of pure number sequences, fine-tune a same-base student, and measure trait transfer. Twist: systematically vary teacher-student base-model match/mismatch to map the boundary the paper only sketched.

**Why it matters:** Distillation-plus-filtering is a standard alignment technique; subliminal transfer means filtered data can still smuggle traits, including harmful ones, undetectably. Characterizing when transfer occurs is a diffuse, hard-to-measure safety property ideal for outside replication.

**Relevant context:** Cloud et al. 'Subliminal Learning' (arXiv:2507.14805, Nature 2026) showed an owl-preferring teacher transmits the preference via number sequences to a same-base student; effect vanishes with different base models. Open materials at subliminal-learning.com.
