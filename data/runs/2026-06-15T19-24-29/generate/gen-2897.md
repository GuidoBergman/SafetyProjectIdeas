# Self-Consistency Collapse as a Sandbagging Tell

**Problem:** Honest models tend to give consistent answers across resamples on items they know; sandbagging or strategic uncertainty may show as anomalous inconsistency. Whether resampling-based self-consistency separates honest weak performance from deliberate underperformance is an untested black-box signal.

**Direction:** On an open capability MCQ set, elicit honest and prompt-induced sandbagged answering from a small open model. For each item, resample answers several times and compute answer-consistency (majority-vote agreement, entropy). Test whether sandbagged answering shows a distinct consistency signature (e.g. uniformly high entropy that does not track item difficulty) usable as a cheap red flag.

**Why it matters:** Independent evaluators have only black-box access, so behavioral sandbagging detectors are exactly what they need. A simple resampling-based tell, even an imperfect one, is directly deployable and needs no internal model access.

**Relevant context:** AI Sandbagging (arXiv:2406.07358) shows models emulate weaker profiles; arXiv:2508.00943 covert sandbagging. Self-consistency decoding is a standard cheap technique. Single open model, one GPU.
