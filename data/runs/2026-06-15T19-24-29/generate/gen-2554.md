# Paraphrasing-Defense Effectiveness Bench: How Much Hidden Signal Survives a Trusted Paraphraser?

**Problem:** Trusted paraphrasing is a proposed anti-collusion/anti-steganography defense (rewrite a strong model's output with a weak model before another instance reads it), but evidence is mixed: naive paraphrasing fails to remove semantic-entropy channels. There is no easy, standardized test letting a newcomer measure residual channel capacity after paraphrasing for a chosen scheme.

**Direction:** Implement 2-3 simple LLM steganography encodings (e.g. encode a bit in choice of synonyms, sentence ordering, an acrostic). Send messages through, apply a trusted paraphraser (a cheap small model), and measure decoded bit-accuracy before vs. after paraphrasing. Produce a 'residual capacity vs. paraphraser strength' curve and identify which channels survive.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies whether a leading, cheap control defense actually works against realistic hidden channels. If even simple schemes survive paraphrasing, untrusted-monitoring protocols that rely on it are unsafe. No training, no lab data; ideal counterfactual project.

**Relevant context:** Roger & Greenblatt 'Preventing Language Models from hiding their reasoning' (arXiv 2310.18512) introduces paraphrasing density methodology; 'Paraphrasing Is (At Best) a Partial Defence' (LessWrong) and follow-ups show naive paraphrasing fails to remove semantic entropy. CASE framework assesses LLM steganographic capability.
