# Surprisingly-Popular Aggregation for Weak-Judge Panels

**Problem:** When a panel of weak judges votes on which debater is right, simple majority vote can lock in a confident-but-wrong consensus (an information cascade), failing to leverage minority truth.

**Direction:** Import the 'surprisingly popular' / Bayesian Truth Serum rule (Prelec et al. 2017) into oversight aggregation. Ask each weak-judge query for (a) its verdict and (b) its prediction of what other judges will say; aggregate by selecting the answer that is more popular than predicted. Compare against majority vote on debate-judging tasks with a small open model run multiple times with varied prompts/seeds as the 'panel'.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether a classic crowd-wisdom mechanism corrects systematic overseer bias without any new training, a long-horizon, lab-independent contribution to aggregating fallible oversight signals.

**Relevant context:** Prelec 'surprisingly popular' (Wikipedia; arXiv:2311.07692 on truthfulness of surprisingly-likely responses), Bayesian Truth Serum, Machine Truth Serum (arXiv:1909.13004). Information cascade model (arXiv:2105.03166). Judge-panel setup from Kenton et al. 2024.
