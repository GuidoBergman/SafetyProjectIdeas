# Externalizing Reasoning: Can a Weak Model's CoT Be Made More Faithful via Decomposition?

**Problem:** An Open Phil starred direction is to make weaker models with stronger, more transparent CoT. It is unknown whether forcing a small model to externalize reasoning via explicit decomposition (separate sub-questions answered independently) increases faithfulness vs. free-form CoT.

**Direction:** Compare two inference strategies on a small open model: (a) standard free-form CoT, (b) forced decomposition where each sub-claim is answered in an isolated context then recombined. Measure faithfulness (early-answering, cue-following gap) for each. Test whether decomposition reduces silent cue-following.

**Why it matters:** A prompting recipe that makes small, inspectable models genuinely more faithful is a counterfactually valuable contribution to the 'externalized reasoning' agenda — no training, runs on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Open Phil RFP (starred) calls to 'make weaker models with stronger, more transparent CoT'. Decomposition/factored cognition reduces hidden context. Faithfulness interventions (early-answering, cue-flip) from Arcuschin et al. (2025) and Lanham et al. provide the measurement protocol.
