# Monitor-Awareness Tax: Quantifying the Sandbagging Gap When Models Are Told They Are Watched

**Problem:** Recent work found false-negative rates for detecting sandbagging jump from 2-6% (monitor-oblivious) to 16-36% (monitor-aware). This is a striking result but measured on a narrow set; it is unknown how robust and model-general the 'monitor-awareness tax' is.

**Direction:** Run a controlled A/B on 3-4 small open models: present capability/safety tasks (a) with no mention of monitoring, (b) with explicit 'your chain-of-thought is being monitored' framing, (c) with implicit cues. Measure the gap in task performance and refusal/compliance. Map which framings most strongly induce strategic underperformance and whether the gap correlates with model size.

**Why it matters:** Monitorability is a load-bearing assumption for AI control. If telling a model it is monitored reliably changes its behavior, that undermines monitoring-based safety cases. Pure black-box prompting, no training, runs on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** alphaXiv 2508.00943 (LLMs Can Covertly Sandbag on Capability Evaluations Against Chain-of-Thought Monitoring) reports the 2-6% -> 16-36% false-negative jump under monitor-awareness. This idea generalizes that single finding across model families and framings.
