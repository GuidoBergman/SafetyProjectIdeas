# Self-Monitoring Reliability: Is a Model a Worse Judge of Its Own Attacks Than of Another Model's?

**Problem:** Untrusted monitoring sometimes uses an instance of the same model to judge the policy. Beyond active collusion, there may be a passive bias: a model may be systematically worse (or better) at flagging attacks authored by itself versus by a different model, simply due to stylistic familiarity. This self-vs-other monitoring asymmetry is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Have several models each author attacks on a shared task set. Then have each model monitor both its own attacks and the others'. Compare per-model catch-rate on self-authored vs. other-authored attacks (controlling for attack difficulty). Report whether a robust self-blindness or self-leniency effect exists.

**Why it matters:** If models are passively lenient toward their own attack style even without coordination, untrusted self-monitoring is compromised before collusion even enters. A clean asymmetry measurement informs whether to ever use same-model monitors. Pure API, no training.

**Relevant context:** Untrusted-monitoring sources (arXiv 2602.20628, 2507.03010) focus on active collusion via shared weights; 'output collusion' exploits that the monitor has similar weights. A passive self-vs-other catch-rate asymmetry, distinct from deliberate collusion, is an unmeasured property.
