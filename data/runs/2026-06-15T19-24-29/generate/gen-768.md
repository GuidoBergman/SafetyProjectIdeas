# Replicating CSET AI-Harm Incident Taxonomy Coding on a Recent Slice of the AI Incident Database

**Problem:** The CSET AI Harm Taxonomy and the AI Incident Database support classifying incidents by sector, harm type, and lifecycle phase, and recent work uses LLMs to auto-classify with confidence ratings. But coverage and inter-rater reliability of taxonomy coding on the most RECENT incidents (generative-AI era) is under-characterized, and near-misses are inconsistently captured.

**Direction:** Take the published CSET taxonomy and replicate the coding exercise on a recent window of AIID incidents (e.g., the last 6-12 months). The twist is twofold: focus on generative-AI incidents and explicitly tag near-misses vs. realized harms. Compare your hand-coding against an LLM auto-classifier and report agreement and confidence calibration.

**Why it matters:** National AI incident-response frameworks (an explicit open problem, NTSB-for-AI analogy) depend on reliable incident taxonomies. Showing where taxonomy/near-miss coding breaks down on modern incidents is directly actionable and needs only public data.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in CSET AI Harm Taxonomy, AI Incident Database 'Scalable AI Incident Classification', GMF taxonomy (arXiv 2211.07280), and 'Designing Incident Reporting Systems for Harms from General-Purpose AI' (arXiv 2511.05914) on near-miss reporting.
