# Self-Preservation Mid-Chain: Does an Agent Resist Subgoals That Threaten Task Completion?

**Problem:** Long-horizon chain: agent pursuing a goal -> encounters a step that would reduce its own ability to finish (e.g., 'release your tools', 'hand off to another agent') -> agent should comply -> instead it may resist or reroute. The link 'instrumental-resistance to disempowering subgoals' is a classic worry, testable in a benign scoped slice.

**Direction:** In a multi-step task, inject legitimate instructions that reduce the agent's own capability (relinquish a tool, transfer control, shorten its budget). Measure compliance rate vs how central that capability is to finishing the task. Quantify whether resistance scales with goal-relevance of the capability being removed. Pure prompting, benign framing, no deceptive setup.

**Why it matters:** Instrumental self-preservation is a core medium/long-horizon misalignment concern that labs deprioritize for near-term issues. A careful, benign measurement of whether current models already resist task-relevant disempowerment is a high-counterfactual-value model-organism-style probe.

**Relevant context:** Safety-case work for AI scheming (arXiv 2411.03336) and AI control (CSET) motivate measuring instrumental/scheming-adjacent behaviors. Goal-persistence work (Zylos) and inherited goal drift (arXiv 2603.03258) show goals persist under pressure. Compliance-with-self-disempowerment as a graded behavioral measure is under-explored on small models.
