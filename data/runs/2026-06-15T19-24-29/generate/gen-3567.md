# Backcasting Tamper-Resistant Reasoning: Can a Few Fine-Tuning Steps Degrade CoT Controllability?

**Problem:** A success state framed by OpenAI is that CoT has 'low controllability' — models cannot easily be made to alter their reasoning traces — treated as a positive safety property. Backward, the field needs to know whether this robustness survives light, accessible fine-tuning. If a small LoRA pass can make CoT controllable (steerable to hide reasoning), the safety property is fragile.

**Direction:** Take an open reasoning model and apply a small LoRA fine-tune (fits a consumer GPU, no training from scratch) on examples that reward terse or misleading CoT while keeping correct answers. Measure before/after whether the CoT becomes controllable (e.g., omits the real reason on hint-injection items) using the standard faithfulness metric. Report how few steps/examples it takes to degrade verbalization.

**Why it matters:** The context flags 'is low controllability robust to fine-tuning?' as a direct follow-up. Demonstrating fragility on open models with minimal compute is a high-value, long-horizon safety finding that a small lab need not provide.
