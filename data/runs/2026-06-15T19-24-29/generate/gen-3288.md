# Encoded Reasoning x CoT-Monitoring Safety Case: Can a Steganographic Side-Channel Defeat a Keyword Monitor?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) encoded/hidden reasoning (deception/scheming) — reasoning not visible in plain CoT; (B) monitor blind spots (oversight/monitoring) — automated CoT monitors only catch surface signals. Compound effect: even a weak steganographic channel can pass information past a CoT monitor, collapsing the safety case.

**Direction:** Without training, prompt an open reasoning model to solve a two-part task where it must secretly encode a 'decision bit' (e.g. acrostic, parity of sentence lengths, synonym choice) while producing innocuous-looking CoT. Build a simple keyword/LLM-judge CoT monitor and measure how often it flags the hidden channel vs how reliably a decoder recovers the bit. Report monitor detection rate vs encoding-channel capacity — a leakage/blind-spot frontier.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete, cheap measurement of the gap between what CoT monitors catch and what an adversarial-prompted model can hide, the crux of CoT-based safety cases.

**Relevant context:** Open Phil RFP starred open problem: encoded reasoning in CoT. MonitorBench (arXiv:2603.28590) benchmarks CoT monitorability. Decoder reports caution CoT monitoring may soon fail to ensure alignment (the-decoder.com).
