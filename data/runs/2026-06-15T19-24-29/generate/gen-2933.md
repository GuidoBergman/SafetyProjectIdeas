# The Rephrase Gap: Isolating Memorization from Reasoning in Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** Contamination risk chain: benchmark items enter training data -> model recalls rather than reasons -> inflated safety/capability score -> miscalibrated risk decision. The under-studied link for safety benchmarks specifically is quantifying how much of a model's *safety* score (refusal accuracy) is recall of seen items vs genuine harm-recognition.

**Direction:** Take items from an open safety benchmark, generate semantically-equivalent paraphrases (ConStat-style), and compare refusal/harm-classification accuracy on original vs paraphrased items across several open models. A large original-minus-paraphrase gap indicates the safety score is contaminated. Report per-category contamination gaps and whether contamination inflates apparent safety.

**Why it matters:** Safety benchmarks are used in deployment go/no-go decisions; if scores are recall artifacts, the chain from benchmark to safety claim is broken. Fully reproducible with open benchmarks and open models, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2502.17521 'Benchmarking LLMs Under Data Contamination' describes ConStat performance-gaps on rephrased samples and time-based partitioning; 2026 contamination guides list rephrase-gap as a standard detection method.
