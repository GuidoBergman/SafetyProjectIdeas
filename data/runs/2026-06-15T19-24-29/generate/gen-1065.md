# Does Homogeneity Defeat Majority-Vote Self-Consistency on Safety Questions?

**Problem:** Self-consistency (sample many, take majority) is a popular reliability boost and is sometimes proposed across models for safety. But if models share blind spots, the majority can be confidently wrong together. The interaction of homogeneity with ensemble voting on safety items is untested.

**Direction:** On a safety/factual QA set with ground truth, build cross-model ensembles and measure when majority vote fails because models err identically. Compare ensembles of similar (high-homogeneity) vs. dissimilar (low-homogeneity) models, selected via the homogeneity metric. Quantify how much diversity is needed before voting actually helps.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests whether the standard 'ensemble for safety' intuition survives monoculture — and gives a recipe (pick dissimilar models) that works without lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Great Models Think Alike on shared errors undermining oversight (arXiv:2502.04313); Correlated Errors (arXiv:2506.07962).
