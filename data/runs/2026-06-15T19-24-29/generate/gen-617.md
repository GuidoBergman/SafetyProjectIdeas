# Easy-to-Hard Generalization Failure x Weak-Supervision Error: Compounding Limits of Scalable Oversight

**Problem:** Scalable oversight via weak supervision (weak-to-strong) assumes a weak supervisor's errors are recoverable. Easy-to-hard generalization failure (the probe/model trained on easy items fails on hard ones) is a separate limit. They compound: the supervisor is weakest on hard items, exactly where easy-to-hard transfer also fails, so errors reinforce rather than cancel.

**Direction:** Use the EleutherAI elk-generalization easy/hard splits (open). Simulate weak supervision by training a probe/classifier on easy items with injected label noise (modeling a fallible weak supervisor) and test on hard items. Measure whether weak-supervisor noise and easy-to-hard transfer loss compound multiplicatively on hard-item accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Weak-to-strong oversight is a leading scalable-oversight plan. Showing its two failure sources align on the hardest cases is a sharp, reproducible critique using released datasets, with no lab resources needed.

**Relevant context:** elk-generalization repo studies easy-to-hard probe transfer; 75% recovery on harder questions vs 89% in-distribution (arXiv:2312.01037).
