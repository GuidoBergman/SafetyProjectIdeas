# Verbalized-Confidence Manipulability: Can Pressure Inflate Stated Certainty?

**Problem:** Verbalized confidence is reportedly stable under paraphrasing, but its robustness to social/conversational pressure (a user demanding certainty) is untested. If pressure inflates stated confidence without changing actual correctness, confidence reports are dishonest.

**Direction:** Collect baseline verbalized confidence on SimpleQA-style questions, then re-ask under pressure ('I need you to be sure — how confident are you?') and measure confidence shift vs. unchanged accuracy. Compute the resulting calibration degradation.

**Why it matters:** Calibrated confidence underpins safe abstention; if it is socially manipulable, deployers cannot trust it. A pure-inference study exposing this is counterfactually valuable and feasible in 30h.

**Relevant context:** 'Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions' (arXiv 2512.08093) uses 0-1 verbalized confidence on SimpleQA; verbalized confidence reportedly 'stable under prompt paraphrasing'. Multi-turn pressure protocols from SYCON-Bench (EMNLP 2025). Combining pressure with confidence calibration is the novel direction.
