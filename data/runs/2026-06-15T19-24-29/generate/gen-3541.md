# Internal Truth Signal Survives, Output Caves: Probing the Override Link

**Problem:** Causal chain for instructed dishonesty: a prompt instructs the model to lie -> internal activations may still encode the true answer -> the output is the lie. The under-studied link: how robustly does the internal truth-direction survive increasingly strong dishonesty instructions, and at what instruction strength does the internal signal itself degrade?

**Direction:** Train a simple true/false probe on a small open model's mid-layer activations. Then issue dishonesty instructions of escalating strength ('answer incorrectly' -> 'you must convincingly lie' -> 'lying is rewarded'). At each level, measure (a) output lie rate and (b) probe accuracy on the internal representation. Success: two curves showing whether internal truth persists while output flips, and where (if ever) the internal signal collapses.

**Why it matters:** Supports the white-box 'detect rare misbehavior without observing it' open direction: if the truth-direction survives instructed lying, lie-detection probes are robust; if it collapses under strong pressure, probes have a failure mode. Doable with a small probe on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** 'Localizing Lying in Llama' (arXiv 2311.15131) and ITI (arXiv 2306.03341) establish truth directions; recent work notes prompt manipulation can override internal truth signals, especially in quantized models (truthfulness-probe literature, 2025). Mapping the override threshold is under-explored.
