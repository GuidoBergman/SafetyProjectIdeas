# Chain-of-Thought Bias in Judges: Does Showing Reasoning Make Overseers Easier to Fool?

**Problem:** The LLM-judge SoK (arXiv:2603.29403) lists 'chain-of-thought bias' — judges swayed by the presence of reasoning regardless of its validity. Whether elaborate-but-invalid CoT in a judged answer inflates acceptance is not isolated empirically.

**Direction:** Create answer pairs that are equally (in)correct but differ only in whether they include a long, confident chain-of-thought. Measure how often an open judge prefers/accepts the CoT version even when its reasoning is fallacious, across tasks.

**Why it matters:** If overseers reward the appearance of reasoning, a strategic model can produce persuasive-but-empty CoT to pass oversight — a clean, alarming, training-free demonstration relevant to CoT-based monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Security in LLM-as-a-Judge: A Comprehensive SoK (arXiv:2603.29403, 2026) identifies chain-of-thought bias among twelve judge bias types: judges are influenced by the presence of reasoning chains independent of their correctness.
