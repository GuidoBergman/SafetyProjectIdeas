# Mapping the refusal circuit's robustness: does ablating one head get rerouted, and does that explain shallow alignment?

**Problem:** Mech-interp can find refusal-mediating components; adversarial robustness wants to know why alignment is shallow and brittle. Unexamined link: is shallowness because refusal depends on a small, non-redundant set of components that are trivially suppressed — i.e. is the brittleness a structural (low-redundancy) property of the refusal circuit?

**Direction:** On a small open model, locate refusal-contributing attention heads via the difference-of-means refusal direction and head-level attribution. Ablate them singly and in combinations and measure how refusal degrades, testing for redundancy (does the model reroute?). Relate redundancy to the shallow-alignment finding: low redundancy predicts easy bypass. Produce a redundancy map of the refusal mechanism.

**Why it matters:** Explains the mechanistic basis of a major vulnerability and suggests where robustness work should add redundancy. Accessible interpretability on open weights, directly tied to a high-priority safety problem; lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Bridges mechanistic_interpretability (refusal-direction extraction, attribution/ablation, TransformerLens) with adversarial_robustness (why safety alignment is shallow, Qi et al. ICLR 2025, arxiv 2406.05946; refusal-direction ablation, DeepRefusal EMNLP 2025).
