# The Resampling Catch Rate: Empirically Pricing the Defer-to-Resample Link in AI Control

**Problem:** AI control protocols (e.g., Ctrl-Z resampling) break the sabotage chain at the link 'suspicious action -> resample/defer -> catch the attacker'. The under-studied empirical question is the catch-vs-cost curve: how often resampling actually catches a subtle sabotage attempt versus how much usefulness it sacrifices, on a small reproducible task.

**Direction:** Build a toy agentic task (e.g., a small coding or tool-use task) where a red-team prompt occasionally inserts a subtle 'backdoor' line. Implement a simple resample/defer protocol with a trusted weaker monitor and measure the safety-usefulness Pareto curve as you vary the resampling threshold. Replicate the qualitative shape of Ctrl-Z's frontier at toy scale.

**Why it matters:** Control protocols are the most lab-independent safety paradigm and resampling is central; a beginner-scale replication of the catch-rate frontier is concrete, valuable infrastructure that needs no training and no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** 'Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling' (arXiv:2504.10374); Greenblatt et al. 'AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion' (arXiv:2312.06942); 'Games for AI Control' (OpenReview).
