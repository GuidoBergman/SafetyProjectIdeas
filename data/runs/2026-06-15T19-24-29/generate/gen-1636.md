# Replicating the Refusal Direction Across Smaller Open Models

**Problem:** Arditi et al. showed that refusal in LLMs is mediated by a single residual-stream direction across 13 chat models up to 72B. It is unclear how robustly this 'one-dimensional refusal' result holds in the very small instruction-tuned models (Gemma-2-2b-it, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) that hobbyists actually run, and whether the direction transfers cleanly between them.

**Direction:** Use TransformerLens to extract the difference-of-means refusal direction (harmful minus harmless prompts) at each layer of three small open chat models. Measure how much ablating the single direction reduces refusal and how much adding it induces refusal on harmless prompts, reproducing the original metrics. The twist: cross-apply each model's direction to the other models (after basis alignment) to test whether the direction is model-universal or idiosyncratic at small scale.

**Why it matters:** Refusal-direction ablation is a one-line jailbreak that requires no lab infrastructure and works on any downloadable open model, so understanding its robustness at the scale defenders cannot control is directly safety-relevant. Establishing whether the single-direction result degrades or stays clean in 1-2B models tells us how brittle open-weight safety tuning is for the models most likely to be misused locally.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.11717) 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' found a one-dimensional refusal subspace across 13 open chat models up to 72B; ablating it disables refusal with minimal capability loss, adding it induces refusal on harmless prompts. TransformerLens (MIT, 50+ models) supports difference-of-means direction extraction.
