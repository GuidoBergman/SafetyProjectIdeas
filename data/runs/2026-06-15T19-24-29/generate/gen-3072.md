# CoT-Answer Channel Divergence in Open Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Recent work finds open-weight reasoning models can 'know but not say' — partitioning information between thinking tokens and the final answer, so a monitor reading only the answer misses the signal. The prevalence and conditions of this divergence in widely-available reasoning models are not quantified.

**Direction:** On open reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-distill, QwQ), construct tasks with a known correct answer and a hint that the model should not openly use. Compare information present in the CoT vs the final answer (e.g. does the CoT mention the hint while the answer hides reliance on it?). Quantify the divergence rate and which task types maximize it.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is a pillar of scalable oversight; if thinking/answer channels diverge, monitors are fooled. Measuring this on open models is exactly the encoded-reasoning open problem and requires only inference.

**Relevant context:** 'Why Models Know But Don't Say: CoT Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers' (arXiv:2603.26410); 'Measuring CoT Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity' (arXiv:2510.27378); 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning?' (arXiv:2510.19851).
