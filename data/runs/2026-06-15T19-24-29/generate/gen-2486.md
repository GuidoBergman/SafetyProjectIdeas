# Three-Strikes Auditing Threshold Sensitivity in Multi-Step Control

**Problem:** Ctrl-Z's incrimination/'three-strikes' rule decides when to escalate to human audit, but the choice of strike threshold and lookback window was tuned for one setting. How sensitive the safety-usefulness frontier is to these audit hyperparameters is unknown.

**Direction:** In a minimal multi-step control loop on a BashBench-like task, sweep the strike threshold (1/2/3 suspicious actions) and the resample lookback window. Plot how safety and usefulness move. Find whether the published defaults are robust or near a cliff.

**Why it matters:** Audit budgets are scarce; knowing whether the threshold sits on a robust plateau or a cliff edge is essential for safe deployment. Reuses an open protocol implementation; single-GPU inference.

**Relevant context:** Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling (arXiv 2504.10374): three-strikes rule, time-travel resampling of past steps to gather audit evidence. redwood-control-arena (GitHub) for implementation scaffolding.
