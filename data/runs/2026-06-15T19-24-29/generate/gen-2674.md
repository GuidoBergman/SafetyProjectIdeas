# Backcasting a Trusted Editor: How Much Usefulness Survives When a Weak Model Rewrites Suspicious Code?

**Problem:** Success state: a trusted-editing protocol where a weak trusted model rewrites suspicious untrusted code, removing backdoors while preserving function. Backward: we need to measure how often a small open editor both kills the backdoor AND keeps the solution correct.

**Direction:** On APPS backdoor tasks, route flagged solutions through a small open 'editor' model with a rewrite-to-remove-suspicious-logic prompt. Measure backdoor-removal rate and test-pass-retention rate, building a safety/usefulness frontier vs. the defer-to-trusted baseline. Result: 'an editing-protocol frontier for cheap models.'

**Why it matters:** Trusted editing reportedly outperformed monitoring (92% vs 62% in the original control paper) but its usefulness cost is under-measured for small models. A reproducible frontier is directly useful and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Greenblatt et al. arXiv:2312.06942 report trusted editing prevented backdoors in 92% of runs; APPS backdoor setting public in ControlArena.
