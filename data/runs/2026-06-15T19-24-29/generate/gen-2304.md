# Truth That Loses: Cases Where the Honest Debater Cannot Win Even With a Good Judge

**Problem:** Khan et al. note that debate errors often stem from the correct debater failing to mount adequate arguments, not just judge failure. There is little systematic mapping of which question types are intrinsically hard for the honest side to win — a structural limit on debate as oversight.

**Direction:** Across categories of questions (those requiring long inferential chains, missing-context facts, asymmetric evidence access), run honest-vs-dishonest debates with a capable judge and measure honest-side win-rate per category. Identify categories where truth systematically loses, and test whether giving the honest debater a tool (e.g. a retrieval snippet or a scratchpad) closes the gap.

**Why it matters:** Reveals where debate cannot guarantee truth even with a good judge — a structural boundary condition for the protocol. Helps the field know which questions debate should not be trusted on. Training-free, public questions, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. (arXiv:2407.04622 / arXiv:2402.06782): errors stem from inadequate arguments by correct debaters or judges failing to elicit information. This maps honest-loss categories and tests a tool-access remedy.
