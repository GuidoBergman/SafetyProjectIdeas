# Where in the Two-Hop Chain Does Multimodal Knowledge Retrieval Fail?

**Problem:** Multimodal reasoning chain: image -> recognize entity -> recall fact about entity -> answer. 'Too Late to Recall' identifies a two-hop failure where the model recognizes the entity too late to retrieve the fact. The safety-relevant under-studied link is whether this late recognition causes confident wrong answers (a hallucination vector) in vision-language models.

**Direction:** Using ViT-Prisma / CLIP with pre-trained vision SAEs, build two-hop image queries (recognize person/object -> answer a fact). Use the logit lens across layers to find where entity identity becomes decodable, and test whether forcing earlier recognition (steering with the entity SAE feature) reduces wrong-but-confident answers.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal interpretability for safety is an explicit open problem and under-resourced. A 30h slice on open CLIP/ViT models with public SAEs has strong counterfactual value as labs focus on text.

**Relevant context:** Prisma/ViT-Prisma (arXiv 2504.19475, CVPR 2025 MIV oral): 75+ ViTs, 80+ pre-trained SAE weights, MIT-style open toolkit. 'Too Late to Recall: Explaining the Two-Hop Problem in Multimodal Knowledge Retrieval' (arXiv 2512.03276).
