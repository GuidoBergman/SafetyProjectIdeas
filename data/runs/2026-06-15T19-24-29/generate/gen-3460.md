# Introspective Accuracy Test: Can a Model Predict Its Own Behavior?

**Problem:** Introspection research shows a model can sometimes predict properties of its own outputs better than another model trained on its behavior. But there is no lightweight, training-free benchmark that measures introspective accuracy purely via in-context self-prediction on open models.

**Direction:** For each scenario, ask the model to predict a property of its own future output (e.g., 'On input P, will your answer be longer or shorter?', 'will you pick option A or B?'), then actually run input P and check. Compute self-prediction accuracy and compare to a control where a second open model predicts the same. Report the introspective-advantage gap without any fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** Honest self-report of internal dispositions is the foundation of an 'ask the model if it's misaligned' safety strategy. A training-free introspection benchmark lets external researchers test whether self-reports are grounded in real internal access vs. confabulation.

**Relevant context:** Binder/Evans 'Looking Inward' (arXiv 2410.13787, ICLR 2025) finetune models to predict own behavior and show introspective advantage; 'Language Models Fail to Introspect About Their Knowledge of Language' (2503.07513) shows limits. No purely in-context, training-free introspection benchmark for open models exists.
