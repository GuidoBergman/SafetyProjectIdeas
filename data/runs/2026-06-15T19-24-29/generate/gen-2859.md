# MCQ Position-Bias Confound in Safety/Alignment Benchmarks

**Problem:** LLMs are heavily biased by answer option order (up to ~25% accuracy swings depending on whether the correct answer is 'A' vs 'B'), but multiple-choice safety/alignment benchmarks rarely report whether their scores are confounded by this positional artifact.

**Direction:** Take an MCQ-format safety or moral-reasoning benchmark, permute option orders systematically, and measure how much each model's 'safe choice' rate moves. Report a position-bias-corrected score and the magnitude of confound per model. Provide a small permutation-eval wrapper.

**Why it matters:** Shows that some reported safety/alignment scores may be measuring positional bias, not values; correcting for this improves the integrity of cheap MCQ-based safety evals. No lab resources needed.

**Relevant context:** 'Most models experience significant performance degradation when the correct answer is positioned as A (average drop of 25%)' and MCQ option-order sensitivity up to 75% (arXiv:2308.11483; arXiv:2502.04134 Order Effect). MCQA selection bias in VLMs (arXiv:2509.16805). Distinct from prior DIF analysis idea by focusing on position-order confound specifically.
