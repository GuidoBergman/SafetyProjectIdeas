# Schelling-Point Homogeneity: Do Independent Models Coordinate Without Communicating?

**Problem:** Success state: we can predict when independently deployed AI agents will tacitly coordinate (converge on the same focal choice) because they share a training prior — a precondition for both benign coordination and dangerous collusion. Backward: we need to measure whether model homogeneity produces shared Schelling points.

**Direction:** Run classic Schelling coordination games (pick a number, pick a meeting place/time, split a pie) on many open-weight models independently. Measure how often pairs of different models 'meet' without communication versus the human baseline. Test whether more homogeneous model pairs coordinate better, linking convergence to coordination capability.

**Why it matters:** If homogeneous models share Schelling points, large fleets of independently deployed agents can implicitly coordinate — useful for benign multi-agent settings but a substrate for undetectable collusion. This is a diffuse, hard-to-evaluate threat that frontier labs deprioritize and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Connects Artificial Hivemind homogeneity to multi-agent risk taxonomies ('Beyond Single-Agent Safety', arXiv 2512.02682). Schelling-game evals are cheap and fully open.
