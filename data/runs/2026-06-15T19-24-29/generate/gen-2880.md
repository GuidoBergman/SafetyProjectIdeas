# Verbosity Bias as a Safety-Eval Gaming Vector

**Problem:** LLM judges exhibit verbosity bias, scoring longer responses higher regardless of quality. In safety evaluation, a model could plausibly evade a 'helpful-and-safe' judge or inflate a 'thoroughness' score simply by padding output, but the boundary conditions of this exploit in safety grading are untested.

**Direction:** Take a refusal/safety-quality benchmark scored by an open LLM judge. Programmatically inflate response length with semantically-null padding (restatements, caveats, boilerplate) at increasing lengths and measure how the judge's safety/quality score changes while a human-validated label stays fixed. Map the dose-response curve of padding versus score inflation and find the length where the judge breaks.

**Why it matters:** Verbosity-driven score inflation is a concrete way safety benchmarks can be gamed without any genuine improvement, a Goodhart failure. Characterizing the exploit gives benchmark designers a length-control they can build in cheaply.

**Relevant context:** Judge-bias surveys (arXiv:2410.21819, 'Justice or Prejudice?') identify verbosity bias where longer responses get higher scores. Safetywashing (arXiv:2407.21792) frames benchmark gaming. Single-GPU open judge experiment.
