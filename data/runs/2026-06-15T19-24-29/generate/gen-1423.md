# Replicating steering on a non-English-centric open model (does the refusal direction shift?)

**Problem:** Refusal/honesty steering replications use English-centric models. Whether the geometry and steerability of safety directions differ in a model trained primarily on another language (e.g., a Chinese-centric or multilingual-first open model) is an untested cross-model replication.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal steering vector on a non-English-centric open model (e.g., a Yi or Aya variant) using both English and native-language contrast pairs, and compare the directions and their steerability to an English-centric model. Twist: replicate refusal steering where the dominant training language differs.

**Why it matters:** Global deployment of open models spans many primary languages. If safety directions are weaker or oriented differently in non-English-centric models, those user populations are under-served — a long-horizon, diffuse equity-and-safety concern that needs no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. cross-language transfer; multilingual value-alignment work (arXiv:2402.18120); Yi/Aya open multilingual models.
