# Survey Sampling Design to Make Auto-Interp Labels Trustworthy

**Problem:** Automated feature labels are generated from top-activating examples, a biased sample that over-represents extreme cases, like surveying only the loudest respondents. Labels may misrepresent typical feature behavior.

**Direction:** Transfer probability-sampling and weighting from survey statistics. Instead of top-k activations, draw a stratified sample across the full activation range (including near-threshold and zero cases) to generate and score auto-interp labels, and reweight to estimate label fidelity over the true activation distribution. Compare label accuracy (detection/fuzzing scores) under top-k vs. stratified sampling on an open SAE.

**Why it matters:** If standard top-k auto-interp systematically overstates feature monosemanticity, every downstream safety claim built on those labels is biased. A sampling-design fix is cheap, open, and broadly reusable.

**Relevant context:** Stratified/probability sampling and weighting are core survey-statistics methods. Auto-interp pipelines use top-activating examples and detection/fuzzing scoring (EleutherAI autointerp; arXiv:2309.08600). The sampling-bias critique and fix are an underexplored transfer.
