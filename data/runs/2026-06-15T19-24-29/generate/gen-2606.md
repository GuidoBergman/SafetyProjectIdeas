# CUSUM Control Charts for Online Detection of Agent Behavior Drift

**Problem:** AI control monitors usually score each action independently, so a slow drift toward sabotage (each step only slightly suspicious) can slip past per-action thresholds. Deployment needs to catch a gradual shift in an agent's behavior distribution, not just single spikes.

**Direction:** Import the CUSUM (cumulative sum) sequential change-point detection chart from statistical process control. Run an open model agent (or replay logged ControlArena/SHADE-Arena trajectories) on benign tasks to estimate a baseline distribution of a cheap monitor signal (suspicion score, tool-call entropy, or token log-prob). Then feed in trajectories that gradually inject side-task behavior and measure CUSUM's average detection delay vs. a fixed-threshold baseline at matched false-alarm rates. Report ROC and detection-delay curves.

**Why it matters:** Gives control protocols a principled, statistically optimal (Lorden/Moustakides) way to flag distribution shift over a trajectory rather than per-step. No training, no lab infra; pure replay + statistics on open benchmarks, valuable for monitoring slow scheming that frontier labs may underweight.

**Relevant context:** SPC control charts (Shewhart, CUSUM, EWMA) are the classic anomaly-detection tools in manufacturing; CUSUM is provably optimal for minimizing detection delay at bounded false-alarm rate (Lorden 1971, Moustakides 1986). SHADE-Arena/ControlArena provide replayable agent trajectories with main+side tasks and automatic evaluators (arXiv:2506.15740).
