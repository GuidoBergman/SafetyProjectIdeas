# DeceptionProbe Transfer Bench: Do Linear Deception Probes Generalize Across Theoretical Deception Types?

**Problem:** Linear deception probes hit AUROC>0.96 on sandbagging and insider-trading (Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025), but it is unclear whether a probe trained on one formally-distinct deception type (e.g., concealment/sandbagging) detects a categorically different one (e.g., active fabrication, strategic omission). No benchmark organizes deception by formal type and measures cross-type probe transfer.

**Direction:** Adopt a small taxonomy of deception types (lying, sandbagging/concealment, sycophantic agreement, strategic omission), use existing open deception datasets to build contrast pairs per type, train a linear probe on each type, and produce a transfer matrix (train-type x test-type AUROC). Identify which deception types share a 'direction' and which are probe-disjoint.

**Why it matters:** Proving theoretical limits on what AI can hide (a UK AISI open problem) requires knowing whether 'deception' is one detectable thing or many. A transfer matrix gives an empirical map of the geometry of deceptive representations — directly informing whether a single monitor can ever cover all deception. No training of base models; open datasets only.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv:2502.03407), 'Building Better Deception Probes' (arXiv:2602.01425), and geometry-of-deception findings (arXiv:2605.27958 reports deception as a gradually-rotating feature). Linear probes = logistic regression, beginner-accessible.
