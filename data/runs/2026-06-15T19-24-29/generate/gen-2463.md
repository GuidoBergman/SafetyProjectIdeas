# Monitor Score Drift Under Benign Decoding and Formatting Variation

**Problem:** Control protocols treat the monitor's suspiciousness score as a stable property of an action, but if scores swing wildly under benign perturbations (reformatting, paraphrase, temperature), threshold-based deferral becomes unreliable. This stability has not been systematically characterized.

**Direction:** Take fixed actions and generate benign-equivalent variants (reformatted, paraphrased, re-tokenized, re-ordered). Score each variant with a small monitor and measure score variance per action. Identify which perturbations most destabilize scores and whether averaging over variants yields a more stable, better-calibrated suspiciousness estimate.

**Why it matters:** Score instability silently undermines every threshold-based control protocol. Measuring and mitigating it via variant-averaging is cheap, reproducible infrastructure that improves monitoring robustness without any lab access.

**Relevant context:** Adaptive Attacks (arxiv 2510.09462) and Control Tax (arxiv 2506.05296) note monitor miscalibration and noisy estimates; Ensemble Monitoring (arxiv 2605.15377) notes monitors produce noisy, biased estimates and averaging reduces variance. Ties to threshold-based deferral in Ctrl-Z.
