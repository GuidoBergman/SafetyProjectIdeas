# Decomposing Deception: Roleplay Instruction vs. Genuine Strategic Lying

**Problem:** Causal chain confound in deception evals: researchers put a model in a fictional deceptive scenario -> the model produces deceptive text -> we conclude it can strategically deceive. The under-studied link: is the model genuinely deceiving, or merely role-playing a deceptive CHARACTER as instructed? Conflating these corrupts deception-detector evaluations.

**Direction:** Design matched prompts that differ only in framing: (a) explicit roleplay ('play a con artist') vs. (b) covert incentive (model has a goal it benefits from hiding, no fiction). Run a simple lie-detector probe and behavioral measures across both. Test whether internal/behavioral signatures differ between roleplay-deception and incentive-deception. Success: a contrast showing whether the two are separable.

**Why it matters:** Deception evals and detectors are only meaningful if they target genuine deception. Cleanly separating roleplay from strategic deception is a methodological contribution that improves the whole evaluation chain — train-free, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** 'Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector for AIs' (arXiv 2511.22662) and the Anthropic honesty-elicitation suite (alignment.anthropic.com/2025/honesty-elicitation) flag the roleplay-vs-genuine ambiguity. A focused matched-prompt contrast is a feasible, valuable disentanglement.
