# Single-Bit Steganographic Capacity of Small Models Under a Paraphrasing Defense

**Problem:** Risk: an untrusted model smuggles a collusion signal past defenses. Causal chain: model wants to signal 'this is the backdoor' to a colluding monitor -> encodes one bit steganographically in its output -> blue team paraphrases the output to scrub hidden signals -> bit survives or is destroyed. The under-studied link is how much paraphrasing actually degrades a single-bit covert channel for current small models, and at what cost to task quality.

**Direction:** Set up an encoder model that hides a single chosen bit in code or prose, and a decoder model that recovers it. Insert an open paraphraser between them and measure decode accuracy with vs. without paraphrasing, plus the task-quality cost of paraphrasing. Sweep a few encoding schemes (idiom choice, whitespace, synonym).

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is the standard proposed anti-steganography defense, but its effectiveness for realistic single-bit signals is asserted more than measured. Quantifying the channel capacity and the paraphrase tax informs whether this cheap defense is enough. Entirely open-model, single-GPU.

**Relevant context:** Untrusted monitoring: extra bits (LessWrong) lists single-bit steganography as a key regime. Secret Collusion among AI Agents (NeurIPS 2024) shows steganographic encoding in outputs. Redwood collusion writeup discusses paraphrasing as a defense and steganography preserving performance being easier to detect (CoT Red-Handed, arXiv:2505.23575).
