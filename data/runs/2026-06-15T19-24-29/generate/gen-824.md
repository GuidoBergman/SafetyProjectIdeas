# Scaffolding Inflation: How Much Do Evaluation Conditions Distort Reported Model Safety?

**Problem:** Recent work ('Safety Under Scaffolding') shows measured safety depends heavily on evaluation conditions — prompts, system messages, tool access. This means a lab can present a model as safe or unsafe by choosing scaffolding, a governance-relevant failure mode that lets safety numbers be manipulated without any model change.

**Direction:** Characterize the sensitivity boundary. Take 2-3 small open models and one public safety eval. Vary scaffolding systematically: system-prompt presence, temperature, few-shot framing, and a single tool-call wrapper. Measure how much the reported safety metric swings purely from scaffolding choices. Produce a 'manipulability range' showing the best- and worst-case safety scores an evaluator could honestly report for the same model.

**Why it matters:** If safety scores are this scaffolding-dependent, mandated evals need standardized harnesses or they invite gaming. Quantifying the swing gives evaluation-standards bodies (UK AISI, NIST, INAISI) direct evidence for protocol standardization — an explicit open problem. Small models, single GPU, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Safety Under Scaffolding (arXiv 2603.10044) shows evaluation conditions shape measured safety; INAISI need for consensus eval methodology; Open Problems in Technical AI Governance.
