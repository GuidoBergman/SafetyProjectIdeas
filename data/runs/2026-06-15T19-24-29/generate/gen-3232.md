# Cheap Detection at the Capability-Hiding Link: Noise Injection on Consumer Hardware

**Problem:** The sandbagging chain ends with a model that performs below its true ceiling. The noise-injection result (sandbaggers improve under weight noise while honest models degrade) targets exactly this link, but it has mostly been demonstrated by labs with weights access. Whether it works as a cheap, reproducible audit on small open models with prompted sandbagging is under-tested.

**Direction:** Take a small open model (e.g. 7-8B) on a public MCQ benchmark, induce prompted sandbagging, then add Gaussian noise to weights at several magnitudes (only a handful of noise vectors). Plot accuracy-vs-noise curves for honest vs sandbagging conditions and build a simple threshold classifier. Report how few noise samples suffice and the false-positive rate.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight models are exactly where covert capability hiding is hardest for labs to police; a turnkey, single-GPU detection recipe is strategically valuable infrastructure for third-party auditors and regulators.

**Relevant context:** 'Noise Injection Reveals Hidden Capabilities of Sandbagging Language Models' (arXiv:2412.01784, NeurIPS 2025) shows sandbagging models improve under noise, honest models degrade, with ~5 noise vectors sufficient.
