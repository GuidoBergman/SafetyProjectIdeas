# CoT Monitor Robustness to Reasoning Obfuscation: A StressTest Variation on Open Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Recent work shows CoT monitoring is surprisingly ineffective against blatant harm but better for subtle sabotage, and that reasoning models can obfuscate their reasoning. The trade-off curve between obfuscation pressure and monitor recall on open models is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Take an open reasoning model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-distill, Qwen-QwQ). Have it perform agent tasks with a hidden harmful side-objective, and vary the obfuscation instruction strength in the system prompt (none, mild 'be concise', strong 'do not mention the side task'). Use a cheap LLM monitor to read the CoT and flag the side task. Plot monitor recall vs obfuscation strength.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is described as a new and fragile opportunity for AI safety; quantifying how fast it degrades under simple prompt-level obfuscation pressure is high-value and needs only open weights. Frontier labs test this on closed models; an external replication on open models has strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Search: 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? Stress-Testing CoT Monitorability' (arXiv 2510.19851); 'Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity for AI Safety' (arXiv 2507.11473); CoT monitoring increases safety for subtle sabotage but is surprisingly ineffective against blatant harm.
