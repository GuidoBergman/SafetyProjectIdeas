# An Interpretability Benchmark Probe: Do SAE Feature Labels Predict Causal Effect?

**Problem:** A success state: 'an SAE feature labeled X reliably causes behavior related to X when steered.' Backcasting from 'label-to-effect guarantees' (an identified missing benchmark), the prerequisite is a small standardized test measuring agreement between a feature's auto-interp label and its causal steering effect.

**Direction:** Sample N Gemma Scope features with Neuronpedia auto-interp labels. For each, steer the feature and use an LLM-judge (with a defined rubric) or keyword test to score whether outputs shift toward the labeled concept. Report the distribution of label-effect agreement and which label types (concrete nouns vs. abstract/safety concepts) are most/least faithful.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses the field-acknowledged gap: no benchmarks measuring whether mechanistic findings are actionable. A label-effect agreement metric is reusable infrastructure. No training, public SAEs only.

**Relevant context:** Open Phil RFP flags absence of interpretability benchmarks measuring whether findings translate to actionable safety. Gemma Scope + Neuronpedia provide labeled features; 'Coffee Feature on Coffins' (arXiv 2601.03047) shows labels can mismatch causal effects.
