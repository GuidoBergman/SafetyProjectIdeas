# Does steering vector size scale a model's vulnerability? Replicating 'larger models more steerable' for harm

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. found larger models are MORE steerable. If this holds for harmful directions, then capability scaling may make models easier to push toward harmful behavior via steering — a worrying scaling law that has not been checked on the harmful side with an open model family.

**Direction:** Within one open model family at 2-3 sizes (e.g. Qwen2.5 0.5B/3B/7B), build the SAME harmful-direction CAA vector for each and measure steerability (behavior shift per unit norm) toward harmful output. Test whether steerability-toward-harm increases monotonically with size. Normalize for activation scale.

**Why it matters:** A clean 'steerability-of-harm scales with size' result (or refutation) informs whether bigger open models are intrinsically easier to weaponize via cheap steering — a medium-horizon threat labs underweight. Same-family scaling study runs on consumer GPUs.

**Relevant context:** Extends the 'larger models are more steerable' finding from Beaglehole et al. (Science, arXiv:2502.03708, Feb 2026), applied specifically to harmful directions.
