# Does CoT Faithfulness Really Collapse with Difficulty, or with Token Length?

**Problem:** Anthropic reported a 44% relative drop in faithfulness on GPQA vs MMLU (Claude 3.7), attributing it to difficulty. But harder questions also produce longer CoTs, and unfaithful CoTs are longer. Difficulty and length are confounded — the 'difficulty effect' may just be a length effect.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model, collect CoTs across MMLU and GPQA hint-injection trials. Regress verbalization (faithful/unfaithful) on item difficulty AND CoT length jointly, plus an interaction term, to disentangle them. Additionally, force length matching by truncating max_new_tokens to test whether faithfulness on hard items recovers when length is capped.

**Why it matters:** If faithfulness loss is mediated by length rather than difficulty per se, then capping reasoning length could be a simple, training-free lever to keep CoT monitorable on hard tasks — directly relevant to whether CoT monitoring scales to frontier-difficulty problems.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic (arXiv:2505.05410): faithfulness dropped 44% (Claude 3.7) / 32% (DeepSeek R1) on GPQA vs MMLU; unfaithful CoTs longer. METR (2025-08-08) argues CoT can be informative despite unfaithfulness. Confound between difficulty and length is acknowledged but not isolated.
