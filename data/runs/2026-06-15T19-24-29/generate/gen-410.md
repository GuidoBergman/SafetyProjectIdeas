# Stress-testing the Local Learning Coefficient as a phase-transition detector on hand-built degenerate losses

**Problem:** Singular Learning Theory's Local Learning Coefficient (LLC) is claimed to detect developmental phase transitions and measure loss-landscape degeneracy, and is becoming a flagship empirical bridge from theory to practice. But its reliability as an estimator under realistic noise and limited compute is under-examined by outsiders.

**Direction:** Construct small models where the true LLC is analytically known (deep linear networks, the textbook SLT toy examples). Run the published SGLD-based LLC estimator across hyperparameters (chain length, step size, localization strength) and measure bias/variance versus the ground truth. Then apply the calibrated estimator to a grokking run to see whether the LLC actually spikes/steps at the generalization transition.

**Why it matters:** If a measure that theoretical alignment proposes to use as a 'structure detector' is fragile to hyperparameters, downstream safety claims built on it are too. Independent calibration of an alignment-relevant statistic is exactly the kind of work valuable outside a lab — needs no proprietary data, only a single GPU and the open devinterp library.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2308.12108 (LLC), devinterp.com 'Estimating the LLC at Scale', and arXiv:2512.00686 (SLT for grokking). Context lists 'inductive biases and their implications for alignment' as an open problem.
