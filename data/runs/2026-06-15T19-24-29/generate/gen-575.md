# Backcasting the Obfuscated-Arguments Defense: Reproducing Obfuscation as an Attractor in Tiny Debates

**Problem:** Success state: debate protocols give provable safety guarantees, contingent on obfuscated arguments not being a dominant strategy. Theory (prover-estimator debate) claims a fix, but the empirical premise — that obfuscation arises naturally — is asserted from limited prior work. Before trusting any defense, we need a clean, reproducible demonstration of obfuscation emerging in a controlled, small setting.

**Direction:** Build a toy formal debate over a decomposable arithmetic/logic claim (e.g., a long sum or SAT-like chain) with two LLM debaters and a weak judge, all open small models via API-free local inference. Measure how often a dishonest debater wins by producing a long argument chain the honest debater cannot cheaply refute. Vary chain length and judge capacity to map the obfuscation onset.

**Why it matters:** Working backward from 'debate is safe', the load-bearing empirical claim is that obfuscation is a real attractor. A minimal, replicable testbed grounds the theory and lets others test proposed defenses — counterfactually valuable since it needs only small open models, no lab debate infra.

**Relevant context:** Avoiding Obfuscation with Prover-Estimator Debate (arXiv:2506.13609) and Doubly-Efficient Debate (arXiv:2311.14125): dishonest prover can produce in poly-time an argument needing exponential time to refute; obfuscation emerged in prior empirical work.
