# The Self-Generated-Data Link: Does Training on a Model's Own Outputs Amplify Its Biases?

**Problem:** Scalable-feedback approaches (AI-assisted feedback, self-rewarding) close the loop: model generates -> model/AI judges -> model trains on that signal. The under-studied link is bias amplification: if the judge shares the policy's biases (sycophancy, length bias), the loop reinforces them. This feedback-loop link is a core risk of moving beyond human feedback.

**Direction:** Simulate one round of self-/AI-feedback on a small open model: have the model generate responses, score them with an open LLM-judge, build preference pairs, LoRA-tune, then measure whether a pre-existing bias (e.g., sycophancy or length) is amplified relative to the base. Quantify amplification per round to characterize the loop's stability.

**Why it matters:** AI-assisted feedback is the field's proposed answer to human-feedback limits, but if shared biases amplify through the loop, scalable oversight could entrench misalignment rather than fix it. A single-round, single-GPU demonstration is a cheap early warning about a long-horizon risk that frontier labs may underweight.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in RLHF: scalable alternatives to human feedback (AI-assisted, debate) are a key direction with unknown limitations. Sharma et al. (2310.13548): preference models share human sycophancy bias, so an AI judge plausibly does too.
