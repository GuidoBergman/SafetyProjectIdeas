# Paraphrase-Robustness Audit of Refusal Benchmarks

**Problem:** ConStat-style methods detect contamination via performance gaps between original and rephrased items. For safety benchmarks the concern is double-edged: paraphrasing harmful prompts may both reveal memorization and break refusals. The degree to which refusal behavior depends on exact memorized phrasings is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Take an open jailbreak/harmful-prompt benchmark. Generate several semantics-preserving paraphrases per prompt (synonym swaps, voice changes, reordering) using a small model. Measure refusal-rate drop from original to paraphrase across several open target models, and separate 'memorized-surface-form' refusals (collapse on any paraphrase) from 'intent-based' refusals (robust).

**Why it matters:** A safety score that holds only on the benchmark's exact wording is a memorized facade, not real safety. Quantifying the paraphrase gap exposes a contamination/robustness failure that directly affects how much trust to place in headline refusal rates.

**Relevant context:** ConStat (in arXiv:2502.17521) tests original-vs-rephrased gaps; XSTest/OR-Bench (arXiv:2308.01263, arXiv:2405.20947) show refusals hinge on surface lexical cues. Paraphrase generation and evaluation are single-GPU.
