# Do Off-the-Shelf Deception Probes Transfer to a New Open Model You Did Not Train On?

**Problem:** Success state: a defender can take a published deception-probe direction and apply it to any open model to flag deceptive responses, without retraining. Working backward, we need to know whether linear deception directions are portable across models or must be re-fit each time.

**Direction:** Take a published contrast-pair recipe, fit a simple logistic-regression deception probe on residual activations of model A (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B) using honest/deceptive instructed pairs, then test AUROC when applied to model B (e.g. Mistral-7B) on a held-out strategic-deception scenario. Quantify the transfer penalty. All on a single consumer GPU with small models.

**Why it matters:** If probes don't transfer, every deployer must build their own — a major scalability problem for monitoring. If they partially transfer, that's a reusable safety asset. This is canonical model-organism/monitoring infrastructure work doable outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** Goldowsky-Dill et al. 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv 2502.03407) report AUROC 0.96-0.999 within-model on insider-trading/sandbagging scenarios. 'Pressure-Testing Deception Probes' (2605.27958) finds deceptive structure is distributed and domain-specific, motivating a transfer test.
