# Stability of a concept vector across fine-tuning checkpoints (replicate on open instruction-tuned variants)

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. list 'test stability of concept vectors across model updates and fine-tuning' as an open problem. It is rarely measured directly, especially for safety concepts on openly available base/instruct/fine-tuned variants of the same model.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal (or honesty) concept vector from a base model, then measure its cosine similarity and steering effectiveness when applied to (a) the official instruct version and (b) a community fine-tune of the same base (e.g., a Dolphin/Hermes Llama variant). Twist: use publicly available fine-tunes as a free 'model update' proxy instead of training anything.

**Why it matters:** If safety steering vectors drift or invert after fine-tuning, then steering-based defenses degrade silently as models are updated — a maintenance/robustness threat. Quantifying this with off-the-shelf checkpoints is a clean, lab-independent contribution requiring zero training.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. stability open problem; 'A Timeline and Analysis for Representation Plasticity in LLMs' (arXiv:2410.06225); publicly available base/instruct/community fine-tunes.
