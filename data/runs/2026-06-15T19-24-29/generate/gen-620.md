# Confidence-Threshold Oversight Fails When Reward Hacking and Sycophancy Coincide

**Problem:** A common cheap oversight rule is 'flag low-confidence outputs for review.' Two RLHF-side failures defeat it together: reward hacking produces confidently-wrong outputs, and sycophancy makes the model confidently agree with users. When both are present, the confidence signal is uninformative about correctness in two different ways at once.

**Direction:** On an open model, construct a small evaluation with three regimes: neutral correct, reward-hacked (proxy-optimized), and sycophancy-pressured. Measure the correlation between stated/elicited confidence and true correctness in each regime and in the combined regime. Show that confidence-based triage that works in the neutral regime collapses when both failures co-occur.

**Why it matters:** Confidence-based human-in-the-loop triage is widely assumed cheap and effective. Demonstrating its joint breakdown gives concrete guidance for oversight design, using only inference and open data.

**Relevant context:** Confidently-wrong hacked outputs (arXiv:2210.10760); sycophancy as confident agreement (DuET-PD, arXiv:2508.17450).
