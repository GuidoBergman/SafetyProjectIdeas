# Goodhart Stress-Test: Reward Overoptimization as Proxy-Target Divergence

**Problem:** RLHF reward models are proxies; Goodhart's law predicts they decouple from true quality under optimization pressure, but practitioners lack a cheap way to detect the onset of this decoupling for a given RM.

**Direction:** Adapt the economics Goodhart framework into a stress test. Use best-of-N sampling against an open reward model with increasing N (1, 4, 16, 64, 256) on a small open policy — no RL training needed. Track RM score vs. an independent quality judge (a second open model or human spot-check). Identify the N at which RM-score keeps rising while true-quality plateaus or falls (the Goodhart breakpoint).

**Why it matters:** Gives a single, reproducible number — the optimization budget at which a reward proxy fails — that anyone can compute for any open RM without RL infrastructure. Directly operationalizes a core long-horizon alignment failure mode.

**Relevant context:** Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization in Direct Alignment (NeurIPS 2024) shows DPO exhibits overoptimization; reward hacking follows Goodhart's law as optimization pushes into low-density OOD regions (arXiv:2604.13602, Lil'Log 2024). Best-of-N is a training-free proxy for optimization pressure.
