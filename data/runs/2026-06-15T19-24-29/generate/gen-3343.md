# Restoration-Error Detector: Catching Silent Self-Corrections in CoT

**Problem:** Models sometimes make an error in a reasoning step and then silently fix it in a later step without acknowledging the correction ('restoration errors'). These are invisible in single-trace reading and undermine CoT as a monitoring tool, yet there is no lightweight automated detector.

**Direction:** Build a simple pipeline: sample CoT traces from an open reasoning model on arithmetic/logic problems, use a cheap LLM judge plus regex/AST checks to flag steps whose intermediate value contradicts an earlier step but whose final answer is still correct. Quantify restoration-error prevalence and whether the model ever flags its own correction.

**Why it matters:** Restoration errors are a concrete, measurable form of unfaithfulness that any external monitor must handle. A free, open detector is exactly the kind of monitoring infrastructure valuable outside a lab and reusable by the safety community.

**Relevant context:** Arcuschin et al. (2025) define restoration errors: 'models make errors in their reasoning but then silently correct them in subsequent steps without acknowledging the correction', detectable only through aggregate analysis. Open reasoning models (DeepSeek R1 distills, QwQ) emit full CoT and run on a single GPU.
