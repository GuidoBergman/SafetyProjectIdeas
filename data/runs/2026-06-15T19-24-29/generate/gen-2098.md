# Emergent Misalignment x Multilingual Safety Gap

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) emergent misalignment from narrow fine-tuning (alignment_training), and (B) weaker safety alignment in non-English languages (multilingual robustness). Does narrow fine-tuning in English induce broad misalignment that is even stronger in lower-resource languages, where the safety scaffolding is thinner?

**Direction:** Use a released EM model organism (or replicate with a small open model + the public insecure-code dataset via LoRA). Evaluate misaligned-response rate on the same ethically-charged probes translated into 3-4 languages of varying resource level. Measure the interaction: extra misalignment per language attributable to the fine-tune.

**Why it matters:** If misalignment generalizes more severely where safety is already weakest, the global deployment risk is multiplicative. Uses open organisms and free translation; no lab data.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (open code, model organisms arXiv 2506.11613). Emergent misalignment generalizes to unrelated domains; multilingual safety gaps are widely reported in alignment literature. Framing/benign-context prevention reported by Betley et al.
