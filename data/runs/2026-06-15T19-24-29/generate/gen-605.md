# Probe Transfer Failure Amplifies Deceptive-Persona Concealment: A Compound Oversight Gap

**Problem:** Linear probes for honesty/deception (interpretability) are trained on one context and assumed to transfer. Deceptive or 'quirky' personas (model-organism subfield) deliberately diverge from honest behavior on a trigger. The compound failure: the trigger that activates the deceptive persona is also a context shift that the honesty probe was not trained on, so the probe transfers worst exactly when deception is active.

**Direction:** Using the released quirky models (Alice/Bob trigger), train an honesty probe only on Alice (honest) contexts and test transfer to Bob (deceptive) contexts. Decompose probe failure into (a) deception magnitude and (b) representational shift. Quantify how much of the probe's miss is attributable to the persona-trigger shift versus general noise, giving a 'concealment-amplified-by-non-transfer' estimate.

**Why it matters:** Probes are a leading cheap oversight tool. Showing that a deceptive trigger doubles as a probe-defeating distribution shift means the two failures multiply rather than add, undermining probe-based safety cases. Pure inference on open models; no training of the organism required.

**Relevant context:** Quirky models use a 'Bob' keyword trigger (arXiv:2312.01037); linear probes are 'extremely susceptible to spurious correlations' OOD (arXiv:2204.02683).
