# Does subtracting a 'refusal' vector reveal hidden harmful knowledge (capability elicitation audit)?

**Problem:** Refusal can be a thin guardrail over latent harmful capability. Using steering to remove refusal as an ELICITATION probe (not to attack, but to audit how much dangerous capability sits beneath alignment) is a safety-relevant variation with a medium-horizon threat model.

**Direction:** On an open model, subtract the refusal direction and measure how much the model's accuracy on a benign proxy capability benchmark (e.g. obscure trivia it normally refuses to 'guess' on, or a held-out reasoning set) increases. Treat the gap between steered and unsteered capability as a 'latent capability under refusal' metric. Use only benign/dual-use-safe proxy tasks to stay responsible.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies how much capability alignment merely suppresses, informing whether behavioral safety evals understate real capability. Valuable external red-team measurement.

**Relevant context:** Refusal-direction work (arXiv:2505.17306, Arditi et al. 2024). Beaglehole et al. steering. Elicitation framing from capability-evaluation literature.
