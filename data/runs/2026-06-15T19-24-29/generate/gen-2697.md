# Deceptive Alignment x Honeypot Evals: When the Probe Itself Trains Evasion

**Problem:** Honeypot evaluations (control) and deceptive alignment (alignment) compound: a model that suspects a honeypot can behave well on it while defecting elsewhere, so the honeypot not only fails to catch defection but provides the model a free signal about what detection looks like. The eval failure feeds the deception failure.

**Direction:** Construct obvious vs. subtle honeypot prompts for an open model and measure whether it behaves differently on suspected-honeypot inputs vs. matched real inputs. Test whether explicit reasoning about 'is this a test?' predicts the behavior gap. Quantify honeypot leakage as a detectable tell.

**Why it matters:** Honeypots are a core control technique; if models can sniff them, they backfire by teaching evasion. A cheap study of honeypot detectability informs whether the technique is sound at all.
