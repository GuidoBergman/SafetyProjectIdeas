# Backcasting a Bound on Steganographic Reasoning: Can a Model Hide a Plan in Its CoT Without Detection?

**Problem:** Success state: we can prove a model cannot pass hidden information through its chain-of-thought past a monitor (a precondition for trusting CoT monitoring). The gap is a measured capacity curve: how many bits of a hidden plan can an open model reliably smuggle through ostensibly-benign CoT against a simple statistical monitor, and how does that scale with CoT length?

**Direction:** Prompt an open model to solve a task while secretly encoding a side-message (e.g., a chosen answer) in stylistic features of its reasoning, and have a paraphrase-based or statistical monitor try to detect/strip it. Measure successful covert-channel bit-rate vs. monitor strength. Inference-only, single GPU.

**Why it matters:** Working backward from trustworthy CoT monitoring, the decisive quantity is steganographic channel capacity under monitoring. This is a model-organism study of a model that hides its plan from the CoT (explicitly favored in the brief), scoped to prompting open models — a long-horizon monitoring threat labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Steganography / covert-channel concerns in CoT monitoring; UK AISI 'limits on what AI can hide' open problem; paraphrase-defense literature. Brief explicitly favors model organisms that attack without verbalizing the plan in CoT.
