# Sequential Probability Ratio Test for Audit-Budget-Efficient Monitoring

**Problem:** Auditing every agent action with an expensive trusted model is too costly; auditing too few misses sabotage. Control protocols need to decide on the fly how many resamples/checks an action warrants before deciding 'safe' or 'escalate.'

**Direction:** Apply Wald's Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) from sequential analysis. For each flagged action, accumulate evidence from cheap repeated monitor queries (or resamples) until the log-likelihood ratio crosses an accept/escalate threshold, giving the minimum samples needed per decision at target error rates. Benchmark expected monitor-calls-per-decision vs. fixed-N sampling on replayed control trajectories.

**Why it matters:** Brings provably sample-optimal stopping to the resample/defer family of control protocols, cutting audit cost at fixed safety. Statistics-only, runs on logged trajectories, complements Redwood's resampling work without needing their compute.

**Relevant context:** SPRT minimizes expected samples for given error rates (Wald; sequential analysis). Control resample/defer protocols boost safety from 50% to 96% but are sample-hungry (Ctrl-Z arXiv:2504.10374; Evaluating Control Protocols arXiv:2511.02997).
