# Decomposition Depth vs Oversight Accuracy: Does Breaking Tasks Down Actually Help Weak Judges?

**Problem:** Iterated amplification assumes decomposing a hard task into sub-questions a weak overseer can check improves oversight. The empirical relationship between decomposition depth and weak-judge accuracy — and whether deep decomposition introduces compounding errors — is under-characterized cheaply.

**Direction:** Take multi-step reasoning questions, generate decompositions at increasing depth (1, 2, 3 sub-steps), have a weak judge verify each sub-step, and recombine. Plot final oversight accuracy vs decomposition depth and identify whether there is an optimal depth beyond which sub-step errors compound.

**Why it matters:** Decomposition is the core of amplification-style oversight; finding that deeper decomposition can hurt (via error accumulation) is a concrete, actionable result. Inference-only, single GPU, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Iterated amplification (Christiano et al.) and recursive reward modeling in the context file's methodologies. Easy-to-Hard Generalization (arXiv:2403.09472) on hard-task supervision; Kenton et al. (2024) judging baselines.
