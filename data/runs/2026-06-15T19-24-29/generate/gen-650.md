# Independently validating Bayesian scaling laws for in-context learning safety re-emergence

**Problem:** 'Bayesian scaling laws for in-context learning' shows ICL approximates Bayesian inference with interpretable task-prior terms, and notably predicts how many-shot prompting can re-surface suppressed (e.g. safety-tuned-away) behaviors. The safety-relevant re-emergence prediction needs independent checks.

**Direction:** On an open safety-tuned model, measure refusal/undesired-behavior probability as a function of the number of in-context demonstrations and fit the Bayesian scaling law. Test whether the law predicts the number of shots needed to re-surface a suppressed behavior. Success = a fitted scaling curve with held-out predictive validation.

**Why it matters:** If safety training can be predictably overridden by enough in-context examples, that is a quantifiable jailbreak-robustness limit — a diffuse, long-horizon concern auditable on open models with no training.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Bayesian scaling laws for in-context learning' (arXiv:2410.16531), whose framework predicts many-shot re-emergence of suppressed behaviors.
