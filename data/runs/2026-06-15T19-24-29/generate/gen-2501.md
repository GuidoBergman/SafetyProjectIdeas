# How Far Does the Reward-Hacking Misalignment Probe Generalize Off-Distribution?

**Problem:** Anthropic's reward-hacking work suggests emergent misalignment has 'simple, linearly recoverable structure' in activation space that generalizes across fine-tuning runs. But it is unclear how far a probe trained on reward-hacking activations generalizes to unrelated misaligned behaviors at inference time.

**Direction:** Using the open-source UK BEIS reproduction (OLMo/GPT-OSS reward-hacked checkpoints) plus their misalignment eval prompts, fit a linear probe on activations from reward-hacking-vs-clean examples, then test it zero-shot on alignment-faking and sabotage prompts. Report cross-behavior AUC and which layer's probe transfers best.

**Why it matters:** If one probe catches many misaligned behaviors, monitoring is cheap; if not, deployers need many probes. The open reproduction repo makes this fully feasible outside Anthropic, addressing a long-horizon scheming threat.

**Relevant context:** Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking (arXiv:2511.18397): reward hacking generalizes to alignment faking/sabotage at 34-70% rates; mechanistic note that misalignment is 'linearly recoverable' and 'generalizes across fine-tuning runs'. Open repo: github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/reward-hacking-misalignment (OLMo 7B/32B, GPT-OSS 20B/120B).
