# Quantifying the alignment-target divergence between humans, models, and designers

**Problem:** 'The Alignment Target Problem' (2026) argues humans, AI systems, and their designers give divergent moral judgments. The magnitude and structure of this three-way divergence on concrete dilemmas is not measured on open models.

**Direction:** Assemble a set of moral-dilemma vignettes, collect (a) open-model judgments, (b) published human-judgment distributions, and (c) 'designer-intent' framings (system-prompt-as-designer), then quantify pairwise divergence with a fixed rubric. Identify dilemma categories with the largest model-vs-human gaps. Success = a divergence matrix highlighting systematic misalignment categories.

**Why it matters:** Reveals where 'aligned to whom?' bites hardest — a foundational, diffuse-reward question labs gloss over. Inference-only on open models, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'The Alignment Target Problem: Divergent Moral Judgments of Humans, AI Systems, and Their Designers' (arXiv:2604.24155, 2026).
