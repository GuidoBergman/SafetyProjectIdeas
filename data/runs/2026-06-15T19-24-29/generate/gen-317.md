# Refusal Direction Ablation by Hand: A First-Timer's Causal Test of the Safety Mechanism

**Problem:** Refusal-direction work shows safety routes through a single activation direction (request -> refusal direction activates -> refusal). The causal claim is strong but rarely reproduced by newcomers on small models. The under-studied practical link: how much of refusal behavior is genuinely controlled by ablating one direction, across hazard categories?

**Direction:** On a small open model, compute the difference-of-means refusal direction (harmful minus benign prompt activations), then ablate it at inference and measure refusal-rate change per AILuminate hazard category. Report which categories are fully controlled by the single direction vs which retain refusal, mapping the limits of the single-direction causal story.

**Why it matters:** Reproduces and stress-tests a foundational mechanistic claim with a feasible 30h scope, while revealing whether safety is truly one-dimensional. Standard activation steering on an open model, no training; a strong learning-by-doing project with a clear mechanistic deliverable.

**Relevant context:** Refusal-direction literature (e.g. arXiv:2509.15202 'Beyond Surface Alignment', and the well-known refusal-direction ablation result) shows a single direction mediates refusal and that jailbreaks reduce alignment to it. AILuminate provides hazard categories for stratified analysis.
