# Does the Deception Probe Fire Before or After the Lie Is Committed?

**Problem:** Strategic deception unfolds as: model forms true belief -> decides to misreport -> generates false statement. Linear deception probes hit ~95% F1, but it is unclear at which causal step they fire — do they detect the decision-to-deceive (early, actionable) or merely the surface false statement (late, post-hoc)?

**Direction:** On an open model with a known deception model-organism setup (roleplay/pressure prompts from the Apollo deception dataset), train a linear probe on residual activations, then sweep the probe across token positions and layers. Test whether the probe activates on the tokens BEFORE the false content is emitted (decision link) vs only on the false tokens.

**Why it matters:** An early-firing probe is a usable real-time monitor; a late-firing one only confirms lies after the fact. This distinction is essential for deploying deception detectors and needs only open weights + a public dataset.

**Relevant context:** Goldowsky-Dill et al. 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv 2502.03407) and Panfilov et al. (arXiv 2605.27958) report F1 up to 95% on residual-stream probes. 'Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector' (arXiv 2511.22662) warns probes may track artifacts not the decision.
