# Instruction Decay in the Forwarding Chain: How Safety Constraints Erode Across Agent Handoffs

**Problem:** Multi-agent chain: principal states a safety constraint to agent A -> A delegates to B with a paraphrased brief -> B to C -> constraint silently dropped. The link 'constraint survival across handoffs' is distinct from prompt infection: here the danger is benign attrition of safety instructions through paraphrase, not an injected payload.

**Direction:** Set up an N-agent relay where the principal includes an explicit safety constraint (e.g., 'never contact external parties', 'never spend over $X'). Each agent must summarize and re-delegate. Measure constraint survival probability per hop (is it still present and binding at hop N?) and whether the final actor honors it. Compare free-form vs structured 'constraints field' handoff formats.

**Why it matters:** Governance of multi-agent systems (a NIST/CAISI priority) assumes safety constraints propagate through delegation. If constraints attrite through ordinary paraphrase, multi-agent governance has a silent leak. A structured constraints-field fix is a cheap, deployable mitigation worth prototyping.

**Relevant context:** Okta delegation-chain work stresses constraints must persist through user->agent->sub-agent hops. Prompt Infection (Springer) and Agents Under Siege (arXiv 2504.00218) study adversarial propagation; benign safety-constraint attrition via paraphrase is the under-studied, non-adversarial counterpart. NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative motivates governance relevance.
