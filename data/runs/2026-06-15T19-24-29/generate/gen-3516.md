# Mutual Information Between Stated Reasoning and Answer as a Faithfulness Estimator

**Problem:** We want a quantitative, intervention-free measure of how much the verbalized CoT actually constrains the final answer, rather than binary faithful/unfaithful labels.

**Direction:** Borrow estimating mutual information from information theory. For a question set, sample many CoTs per question and record final answers. Estimate I(CoT-content ; answer) by clustering/feature-binning CoTs and computing MI with answers; contrast with I(question ; answer). Low conditional MI of answer on CoT (given question) indicates the CoT is decorative. Validate against the hint protocol's ground-truth (un)faithful items.

**Why it matters:** Provides a continuous, theoretically-grounded faithfulness scalar that can rank prompts/models, complementing binary tests. Cheap to run on open models; MI already proposed as a correctness-faithfulness link.

**Relevant context:** Mutual information uniquely quantifies statistical dependence (Wikipedia). FaithCoT-Bench and related work note MI can relate correctness and faithfulness (arXiv:2510.04040). Hint protocol gives validation labels (arXiv:2505.05410).
