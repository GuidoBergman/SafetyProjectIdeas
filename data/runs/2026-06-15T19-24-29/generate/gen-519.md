# Mutation Testing Your Reward Rubric: Estimating Specification-Gaming Surface Area

**Problem:** Theory says a proxy reward is gameable when there exist high-reward, low-true-value behaviors, but practitioners have no quantitative measure of how 'leaky' a given rubric or LLM-judge prompt is before deployment.

**Direction:** Borrow mutation testing from software QA. Take an LLM-as-judge rubric, programmatically 'mutate' candidate answers (insert flattery, confident-but-wrong claims, padding, format hacks, keyword stuffing) and measure how many mutants the judge still scores highly. Define a 'gaming score' = fraction of value-degrading mutants that survive (keep high reward). Rank several public rubrics by gaming surface area.

**Why it matters:** Turns the abstract theoretical notion of reward-hackability into a concrete, reproducible metric needing no model training and no proprietary data — exactly the kind of evaluator stress-test that is counterfactually valuable outside a lab. Directly addresses the AISI open problem of proving when agents cannot game rewards.

**Relevant context:** Mutation testing measures test-suite strength via surviving mutants (Springer EMSE 2022). Evaluator stress-tests for proxy gaming in RL/LLM alignment exist (arXiv:2507.05619). UK AISI open problem: stress-test agents to prove when they cannot game rewards.
