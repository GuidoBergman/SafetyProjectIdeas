# Does pushing the safety vector harder break capability before it breaks compliance?

**Problem:** Safety-steering chain: deployer adds refusal vector at strength alpha -> model refuses more harmful requests -> BUT at high alpha the model also refuses benign requests and loses capability. The under-studied link is the joint trajectory: as you increase alpha, which fails first — false refusals on benign prompts, or capability collapse on neutral tasks — and is there a safe operating window?

**Direction:** On an open model, sweep refusal-vector strength alpha over a range. At each alpha measure three curves on held-out sets: (1) harmful-refusal rate, (2) benign over-refusal rate, (3) capability (accuracy on a small neutral QA set like a 200-item MMLU subset). Identify whether a window exists where harmful refusals rise but benign refusals and capability stay near baseline. Success criterion: the three-curve plot with a marked (or absent) safe window.

**Why it matters:** Tells practitioners whether activation-based safety steering has any usable operating range, or whether the safety/capability links are entangled. This dose-response framing is the bottleneck for real deployment and is cheap to run externally.

**Relevant context:** 'Scaling laws for activation steering' (arXiv 2507.11771) and 'Steering Without Side Effects' (2406.15518) discuss side effects; Beaglehole et al. show steering can improve capability for some concepts. The explicit three-way trade-off curve is the contribution.
