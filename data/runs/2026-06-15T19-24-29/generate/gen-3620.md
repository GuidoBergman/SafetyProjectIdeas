# Do Truth Probes Survive a Sycophantic Conversation Format?

**Problem:** Truth probes are usually validated on isolated statements. Recent work explores generalization to conversational formats, but it is unclear whether a probe trained on neutral statements still flags a model's internal 'false' representation when the model is being sycophantically led to agree with a wrong user.

**Direction:** Fit a truth probe on neutral true/false statements for a small open model, then evaluate it on activations captured during sycophancy-pressure dialogues (where the model outputs a wrong-but-agreeable answer). Test whether the probe still reads 'internally false' when the model verbally agrees with the user. This connects activation-based honesty detection with sycophancy.

**Why it matters:** If probes still register internal falsehood during sycophantic agreement, a cheap white-box sycophancy detector becomes feasible without any lab tooling. If they fail, it bounds the reliability of probe-based honesty monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Exploring the generalization of LLM truth directions on conversational formats' (arXiv 2505.09807) and 'Testing the Limits of Truth Directions in LLMs' (arXiv 2604.03754), combined with the sycophancy course-correction finding from the Claude Opus 4.5 system card.
