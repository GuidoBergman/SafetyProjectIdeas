# Fault-Injection / Chaos Engineering for Oversight Protocols

**Problem:** Theoretical safety protocols (debate, recursive oversight, monitor-then-act) are usually analyzed under idealized assumptions; we lack a systematic way to measure how their guarantees degrade when individual components fail or are corrupted.

**Direction:** Borrow fault injection / chaos engineering from distributed systems. Build a small simulated oversight pipeline (weak-judge + strong-answerer + monitor) using open models, then systematically inject faults (judge sometimes lies, monitor randomly drops cases, answerer occasionally colludes) and measure how the protocol's catch-rate for bad outputs degrades as fault rates rise. Produce robustness curves per protocol.

**Why it matters:** Turns informal protocol-safety arguments into empirical robustness profiles, revealing which oversight schemes have graceful vs. brittle failure modes. This is exactly the kind of monitoring-harness prototype that is strategically valuable and buildable outside a frontier lab.

**Relevant context:** Chaos engineering / fault injection systematically perturbs components to test resilience; this maps onto scalable-oversight protocols (debate, recursive reward modeling) central to formal alignment. Builds strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure at prototype scale.
