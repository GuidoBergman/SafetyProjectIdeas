# Does Narrow Fine-Tuning Leave a Detectable Activation Fingerprint?

**Problem:** A success state: 'auditors can tell whether a deployed model was secretly fine-tuned on a narrow (possibly malicious) objective just from its activations.' Backcasting, the prerequisite is a reproducible demonstration that narrow fine-tuning leaves a readable trace in activation differences on a small open model.

**Direction:** Take a small open base model and a narrowly fine-tuned counterpart (e.g., a publicly available domain-specific fine-tune). Compute per-token activation differences on neutral text, then use logit-lens / SAE-feature decoding to characterize what concepts the difference vector encodes. Test whether a simple classifier on the difference distinguishes the fine-tuning domain.

**Why it matters:** A cheap activation-fingerprint test for covert fine-tuning is valuable for model provenance and detecting tampering — a diffuse, long-horizon concern. Open weights, no training needed beyond using existing fine-tunes.

**Relevant context:** Narrow Finetuning Leaves Clearly Readable Traces in Activation Differences (OpenReview pxaCoKZWVf; AF post) shows narrow fine-tuning produces clearly readable traces in activation differences, interpretable via logit-lens and SAE features.
