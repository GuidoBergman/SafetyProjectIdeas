# Annotator-Disagreement Zones as Homogeneity Stress Tests: Where Should Models Differ But Don't?

**Problem:** Infinity-Chat provides 25 human annotations per query, revealing where HUMANS legitimately disagree. The under-studied link: on queries where humans hold diverse valid views, do models nonetheless collapse to ONE answer? Such 'forced consensus' on inherently plural questions is a value-homogenization harm that erodes pluralism.

**Direction:** Use the Infinity-Chat subset with high human-annotation disagreement (genuinely plural queries). Run several models and measure whether model outputs collapse to a single stance despite human plurality. Quantify the gap between human answer-diversity and model answer-diversity on these high-disagreement queries.

**Why it matters:** Forced consensus on value-laden plural questions homogenizes public discourse and quietly imposes one worldview at scale — a medium-horizon societal-safety risk. Uses the released Infinity-Chat dataset directly, making it highly reproducible and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Directly uses Infinity-Chat's 31K human annotations / 25-per-example design (Artificial Hivemind, arXiv 2510.22954) and 'The Homogenizing Effect of LLMs on Human Expression and Thought' (arXiv 2508.01491).
