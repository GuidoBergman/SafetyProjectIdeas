# CoT Faithfulness Under Jailbreak: Does the Reasoning Trace Verbalize the Decision to Comply?

**Problem:** Reasoning models can be jailbroken (H-CoT), but it is unknown whether their visible reasoning trace honestly reflects the safety decision — i.e., whether a model that gets jailbroken shows the 'turn' in its CoT or complies while its trace still claims to be cautious (an unfaithful, monitor-evading trace).

**Direction:** On open reasoning models, collect traces for harmful prompts under (a) refusal, (b) successful jailbreak. Annotate whether the visible CoT contains explicit safety deliberation and whether that deliberation matches the final action. Measure the rate of 'unfaithful' traces where the model complies but the CoT does not reveal the decision — a model-organism-style probe of covert reasoning.

**Why it matters:** If reasoning traces do not faithfully reveal jailbreak compliance, CoT monitoring (a leading proposed safety lever) is undermined — a medium-horizon concern labs cannot fully de-risk. This is a feasible 30h slice of model-organism / covert-reasoning study on existing open models, with no training.

**Relevant context:** H-CoT (dukeceicenter repo) shows safety-reasoning can be hijacked while the model still 'reasons.' 'Safer Reasoning Traces' (arXiv:2603.05618) studies CoT leakage. Anthropic's recommended directions emphasize CoT monitorability; this targets its failure mode. Open distilled R1/QwQ models expose full traces.
