# Replicating the 'Refusal Direction Survives Fine-Tuning' Question on LoRA-Tuned Models

**Problem:** The refusal-direction result is established on released chat models. A practically urgent variant: when a user applies a small LoRA fine-tune (the dominant cheap customization method) to an open chat model, does the original refusal direction persist, shift, or vanish? This determines whether refusal monitoring built on the base model survives community fine-tuning.

**Direction:** Extract the refusal direction from a base open chat model (difference-of-means). Apply a small, benign LoRA fine-tune on an unrelated task (runs in under an hour on one GPU). The twist: re-extract the refusal direction from the LoRA-tuned model and measure (a) cosine similarity to the original direction and (b) whether the ORIGINAL direction still ablates refusal in the tuned model.

**Why it matters:** Thousands of community LoRA fine-tunes of open models are released monthly, and many inadvertently or deliberately weaken safety. Whether a refusal monitor calibrated on the base model still works after LoRA tuning — a cheap, fully-open study — is directly relevant to monitoring the sprawling open-weights ecosystem that no single lab controls.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. (arXiv:2406.11717) found refusal is a single direction; 'Beyond Surface Alignment' (arXiv:2509.15202) studied ablating it. PEFT/LoRA fine-tuning of small models is single-GPU feasible; TransformerLens supports direction extraction on the resulting model.
