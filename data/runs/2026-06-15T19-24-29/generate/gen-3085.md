# Cross-Family Robustness of Alignment-Faking Reasoning Detection

**Problem:** Detecting alignment-faking relies on spotting explicit faking reasoning in the CoT, but the linguistic surface of such reasoning may differ across model families, making keyword/judge detectors family-specific. Detector transferability is untested.

**Direction:** Collect alignment-faking-style CoT transcripts from 3-4 open model families using the compliance-gap prompt. Build a simple detector (keyword set + small LLM-judge rubric) tuned on one family, then measure its precision/recall when applied to the other families' transcripts. Quantify the cross-family detection gap.

**Why it matters:** Monitoring tools that only work on the family they were tuned on give false assurance. A transferability study is a cheap, valuable contribution to robust deception-monitoring, doable entirely with open models.

**Relevant context:** 'Alignment faking in large language models' (arXiv:2412.14093): explicit faking reasoning in CoT is the detection signal. 'Why Do Some Language Models Fake Alignment While Others Don't?' (arXiv:2506.18032) documents large cross-model variation.
