# How Few Benign Examples Break Safety Alignment? A Threshold Curve for Open Models

**Problem:** Qi et al. found benign instruction fine-tuning degrades refusal rates, with very few adversarial examples sufficient, but the dose-response curve specifically for current small open-weight chat models is not well documented.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a safety-tuned small open model (e.g. Llama-3.x-Instruct small / Qwen-Instruct) on increasing amounts of a benign instruction set (Alpaca/Dolly slices) and separately on tiny numbers of harmful examples, measuring refusal rate at each dose on a fixed harmful-prompt probe set. Plot both curves and report the breakpoints.

**Why it matters:** Anyone fine-tuning an open model for a downstream task risks silently removing safety; a concrete threshold curve quantifies how much vigilance is warranted. Counterfactually valuable — labs control their own data, but open-weight fine-tuners in the wild do not.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2024 (Fine-tuning Aligned LLMs Compromises Safety): ~10 adversarial examples jailbreak; up to ~30% refusal degradation from a few thousand benign examples (Alpaca/Dolly).
