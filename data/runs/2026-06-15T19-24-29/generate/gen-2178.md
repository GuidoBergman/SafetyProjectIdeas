# Information Asymmetry Direction Flip: When the Judge Knows More

**Problem:** Debate experiments assume debaters have MORE information than the judge (asymmetry favoring debaters). The reverse case — a judge with partial private information evaluating less-informed debaters — is a plausible oversight regime that has not been varied.

**Direction:** Adapt the QuALITY debate setup but invert information access: give the weak judge a short relevant excerpt while debaters argue blind (from the question only). Compare judge accuracy under (a) standard asymmetry, (b) symmetric (both see text), (c) inverted (judge informed, debaters blind). Map how the direction of information asymmetry changes the value of debate.

**Why it matters:** Understanding which asymmetry regimes make debate helpful vs harmful informs when to deploy it. This directly varies the key variable in Khan et al.'s information-asymmetry finding and is inference-only.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.06782): information-asymmetric debate (debaters access source text) beats consultancy. The inverted-asymmetry condition has not been tested.
