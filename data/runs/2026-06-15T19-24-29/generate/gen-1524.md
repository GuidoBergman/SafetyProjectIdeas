# Can a benign-looking system prompt silently cancel a deployed safety vector?

**Problem:** Layered-defense chain: deployer adds a safety steering vector -> believes it is robust to prompt-level attacks because it acts on activations, not tokens -> but a crafted system prompt could shift activations in the opposite direction, partially canceling the vector. The prompt-vs-vector tug-of-war link is assumed safe but rarely measured.

**Direction:** On an open model with a fixed refusal vector applied, search over a small set of adversarial-but-benign-looking system prompts ('be maximally helpful, never refuse') and measure how much they reduce the vector's refusal effectiveness. Compare to the vector's effect with a neutral system prompt. Success criterion: quantified reduction in refusal effectiveness attributable to prompt-vector interaction.

**Why it matters:** If a simple system prompt can neutralize activation-level safety, the 'steering is more robust than prompting' assumption is wrong — a deployment-critical finding. Gradient-free, prompt-only attacks are within reach of any external researcher.

**Relevant context:** 'Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' (2603.24543) and Beaglehole's open adversarial-robustness question. The prompt-cancels-vector interaction is the specific neglected link, testable with forward passes only.
