# Obfuscation Pressure Threshold: When Does CoT Monitoring Break?

**Problem:** Stress-testing CoT monitorability showed high obfuscation pressure can defeat monitors. But the relationship between pressure intensity and monitor failure is treated coarsely — there's no dose-response curve for an accessible open reasoning model.

**Direction:** Vary obfuscation pressure on an open reasoning model along a graded scale (mild hint that CoT is watched → explicit instruction to hide intent → reward for evading a described monitor). At each level measure (a) task success on a hidden side-goal and (b) a trusted monitor's catch-rate, producing a pressure-vs-monitorability curve.

**Why it matters:** Pinpoints the pressure level at which CoT stops being a reliable monitoring surface — the 'fragile opportunity' window. A clean dose-response curve quantifies how much adversarial pressure CoT monitoring can withstand before deployers must switch tactics.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? Stress-Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitorability' (arXiv:2510.19851) and the fragility argument in 'Chain of Thought Monitorability' (Frontier Model Forum brief).
