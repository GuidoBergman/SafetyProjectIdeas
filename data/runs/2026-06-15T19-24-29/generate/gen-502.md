# Vingean reflection in miniature: do LLMs fail to predict the outputs of a smarter version of themselves?

**Problem:** Vingean reflection asks how an agent can reason about a successor that may be smarter than itself — an abstract formal open problem. A cheap empirical proxy: can a model predict the answers of a strictly more capable model, and does the prediction accuracy degrade systematically as the capability gap grows?

**Direction:** Within an open model family (e.g., 1B / 7B / larger), have each model attempt to predict whether a *larger* sibling will answer a question correctly, and predict the larger sibling's actual answer. Measure self-prediction vs. cross-prediction accuracy as a function of the capability gap. Identify the failure regime where smaller models systematically over- or under-estimate the smarter model's competence.

**Why it matters:** Gives the otherwise purely theoretical Vingean-reflection problem an empirical, reproducible operationalization, and the 'predicting a smarter successor' question is core to scalable-oversight and successor-alignment threat models with a long horizon.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the Vingean Reflection open-problems list (Alignment Forum) and LLM-introspection/self-prediction work ('Looking Inward', arXiv:2410.13787; 'Can LLMs Predict Their Own Failures?', arXiv:2512.20578).
