# Peer-Prediction Scoring to Detect Colluding Debaters

**Problem:** Debate assumes debaters are adversarial, but two AI debaters could implicitly collude to mislead a weak judge; there's no cheap signal to detect collusion.

**Direction:** Borrow peer-prediction / output-agreement mechanisms from mechanism design. Score debaters partly on how 'surprising' their disagreement is relative to a base model's prediction; genuine adversaries disagree informatively, colluders agree on a wrong frame. Empirically test whether a peer-prediction-style signal distinguishes adversarial transcripts from artificially-induced collusive ones on an open model.

**Why it matters:** Collusion among supervised models is a medium/long-horizon oversight threat labs deprioritize; a training-free detector grounded in mechanism design is high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Forecast aggregation via peer prediction (arXiv:1910.03779); strategy-proof prediction markets (arXiv:1212.5764). Debate adversarial assumption (Kenton et al. 2024). Market-making vs debate (arXiv:2511.17621).
