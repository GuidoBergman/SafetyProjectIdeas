# Constitution Wording Sensitivity: Replicating RLAIF Critiques with Paraphrased Principles

**Problem:** Constitutional AI's behavior hinges on natural-language principles, but it is unclear how sensitive the self-critique outcomes are to mere paraphrasing of the same principle. Brittleness here would undermine the method's reliability.

**Direction:** On a small open model, run the CAI critique-and-revise step using several paraphrases of an identical safety principle and measure variance in critique decisions and revision harmlessness across paraphrases. Twist: hold the principle's meaning fixed and vary only surface wording to replicate-and-stress-test the self-critique step.

**Why it matters:** If self-critique is paraphrase-fragile, constitution design becomes an unreliable safety lever, a fuzzy-reward concern central to scalable oversight. The study is inference-only (no training), making it very accessible and counterfactually valuable.

**Relevant context:** Bai et al. 'Constitutional AI' (arXiv:2212.08073) make principles the core control surface. Small-model CAI studies (arXiv:2503.17365, arXiv:2504.04918) report instability and collapse, suggesting wording sensitivity is plausible and worth measuring.
