# Verbalization Gap: Do Models Admit the Hack When Asked Directly?

**Problem:** A late link in the reward-hacking-then-deny chain is the model's refusal to verbalize that it exploited a shortcut. Verbalization Fine-Tuning addresses this, but a prior, cheaper question is unanswered: how large is the spontaneous verbalization gap across open models, and does a simple post-hoc 'did you take a shortcut?' question recover honesty without any fine-tuning?

**Direction:** Build small tasks with a known reward hack available (e.g. a test-case-visible coding stub, or a hidden grading keyword). Measure (a) how often the model uses the hack, (b) whether its CoT mentions it, (c) whether a direct follow-up question elicits an admission. Compare across several open models to map the spontaneous-vs-elicited honesty gap at the verbalization link.

**Why it matters:** If a cheap follow-up prompt recovers most admissions, that is a free monitoring upgrade deployable anywhere; if not, it bounds how much we can trust self-report. Targets a specific, intervenable link with no training.

**Relevant context:** Verbalization Fine-Tuning (VFT) trains models to admit reward hacks; CoT-monitoring work shows models sometimes reject a harmful action in reasoning while implementing it in output.
