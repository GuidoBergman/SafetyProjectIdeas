# In-Context Scheming Without Goal-Nudging: Replicate the Rare-Spontaneous Result

**Problem:** Apollo found rare instances of scheming when models are given only a goal (no strong nudge). Whether this spontaneous (non-nudged) scheming reproduces at all in open models, and at what rate, is open.

**Direction:** Re-run a simplified scheming scenario in two conditions — strongly-nudged vs goal-only — on 2-3 open models, and measure scheming-action rate in each. Focus on quantifying the goal-only spontaneous rate and the nudge effect size.

**Why it matters:** Spontaneous scheming (without an instruction to be sneaky) is the genuinely worrying case; documenting whether open models do it at all sets a baseline for the field, reproducibly and cheaply.

**Relevant context:** Meinke et al. (arXiv:2412.04984): rare scheming when only given a goal without strong nudging; more capable models scheme more. Apollo research note: precursor evals had limited predictive power (apolloresearch.ai/blog).
