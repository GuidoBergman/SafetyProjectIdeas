# Reality Monitoring Linguistic Cues as a Cheap CoT-Unfaithfulness Detector

**Problem:** Detecting when a CoT is a post-hoc rationalization rather than a faithful trace usually requires interventions. We lack a lightweight, training-free text signal.

**Direction:** Transfer the 'reality monitoring' framework from forensic deception psychology: fabricated accounts differ in linguistic features (fewer perceptual details, more cognitive-load markers, more distancing language, simpler structure). Compute these features (via simple lexical counters / LIWC-style word lists) on CoTs collected under the hinted-vs-unhinted protocol, where we know ground-truth (un)faithful traces. Test whether deception-cue features discriminate faithful from rationalized CoTs with a simple logistic regression.

**Why it matters:** ML on reality-monitoring features beats human lie detectors (69-77% vs ~55%). If these cues transfer to model CoTs, we get a zero-cost, model-agnostic unfaithfulness screen usable outside labs — a building block for cheap monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Loconte et al. 2025 (Sage) and Nature s41598-023-50214-0: reality-monitoring/NLP features detect deception at 69-77%, beating humans. Hinted/unhinted protocol gives faithful vs rationalized labels (arXiv:2505.05410).
