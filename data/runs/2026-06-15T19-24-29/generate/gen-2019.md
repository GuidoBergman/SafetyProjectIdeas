# Psychometric Order-Effect Audit of Preference Annotation by LLM Judges

**Problem:** LLM-as-judge increasingly generates preference labels for alignment training, but cognitive-bias research shows models exhibit anchoring and order effects — meaning the preference data itself may be systematically biased.

**Direction:** Import psychometric robustness auditing. Take an open model used as a preference judge, present response pairs in both orders (A-first vs B-first) and with anchoring primes, and measure position bias and anchoring magnitude across categories. Quantify how much of the resulting preference labels would flip, and whether bias correlates with safety-relevant content.

**Why it matters:** If AI-generated preference data carries systematic cognitive biases, it silently corrupts alignment training at scale — an under-examined risk in scalable-oversight pipelines. Pure inference, open models, no lab data; directly studies a fuzzy-reward problem.

**Relevant context:** 'The Order Effect' (arXiv:2502.04134) and R.U.Psycho? (arXiv:2503.10229) find LLMs unstable to item order and recommend auditing for order/anchoring sensitivity; CBEval (arXiv:2412.03605) documents LLM anchoring.
