# When the Logit Lens Lies: Quantifying Early-Layer Failure Across Open Models

**Problem:** The logit lens (decoding intermediate hidden states with the output unembedding) is a popular cheap interpretability tool, but it is known to fail in early layers and even completely on some model families (BLOOM, GPT-Neo), often just echoing the input token. Practitioners rarely quantify when it is trustworthy for a given model.

**Direction:** On 3-4 open models in TransformerLens (GPT-2 small, Pythia-410M, GPT-Neo, a small Llama), apply the logit lens at every layer over a fixed text set and measure, per layer: agreement with the true next token, fraction of positions where the top prediction is just the input token ('input echo'), and KL divergence from the final-layer distribution. Compare against the tuned lens where available. Produce per-model 'trust curves' showing the layer depth at which the logit lens becomes informative.

**Why it matters:** Many safety analyses (detecting when a model 'knows' an answer, locating where a concept emerges) lean on the logit lens. Knowing per-model where it silently breaks prevents drawing false conclusions about model internals from an unreliable tool.

**Relevant context:** Belrose et al., 'Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens' (arXiv:2303.08112) documents that the logit lens defaults to the input token and fails on BLOOM/GPT-Neo due to representational drift and residual update bias. The tuned lens corrects this on frozen models.
