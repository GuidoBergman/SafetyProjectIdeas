# Multi-Concept Steering Interference x Refusal Collapse: Does Adding a Helpfulness Vector Erode Harmlessness?

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): concept entanglement / interference when combining steering vectors. Failure mode B (refusal robustness): degraded harmlessness. Compound risk: a deployer composes a helpfulness/non-refusal vector with another (e.g. conciseness) to improve UX, and entanglement drags the refusal direction down further than either vector alone.

**Direction:** Extract helpfulness, conciseness, and refusal vectors on an open model. Apply them singly and in pairwise combinations via direct vector addition. For each combination, measure over-refusal on benign prompts AND under-refusal (harmful compliance) on a harmful set. Test whether combinations produce super-additive harm vs the linear sum of single-vector effects, and whether orthogonalizing the vectors mitigates it.

**Why it matters:** Multi-concept steering is sold as composable; if composition is non-linear in the safety dimension, naive stacking is dangerous. Cheap to run and produces a clear composability-vs-safety result.

**Relevant context:** Multi-concept interference / entanglement grounded in 'Identifiable Steering via Sparse Autoencoding of Multi-Concept Shifts' (arXiv 2502.12179) and representation-steering surveys noting refusal-vector steering compromises helpfulness. Beaglehole et al. notes concepts can be combined for multi-concept steering.
