# The Neuron Interpretation Illusion: Does a 'Clean' Neuron Survive a New Dataset?

**Problem:** The classic interpretability illusion (Bolukbasi et al.) shows a neuron can look cleanly interpretable on one dataset yet exhibit a completely different activation pattern on another, so single-dataset neuron interpretations are unreliable. Many quick neuron-labeling efforts test on only one corpus.

**Direction:** On a small open model in TransformerLens, pick neurons that appear monosemantic on one dataset (e.g. a subset of OpenWebText) by inspecting max-activating examples. Re-collect each neuron's top activations on a structurally different corpus (code, a different language, a domain text). Measure how often the apparent interpretation breaks (top examples no longer match the hypothesized concept) and characterize which neurons are most illusion-prone.

**Why it matters:** Safety auditing that labels neurons/features from a narrow dataset will misjudge what the model does on deployment-distribution inputs. Demonstrating dataset-dependence cheaply teaches a critical methodological guardrail.

**Relevant context:** Bolukbasi et al. (2021) 'An Interpretability Illusion for BERT' and the 200 COP superposition/polysemanticity notes show neurons can have different activation patterns across datasets; clustering dataset examples across the activation spectrum reduces illusion risk.
