# Distractor-Quality Variation in Dangerous-Capability MCQ Benchmarks

**Problem:** Dangerous-capability MCQ benchmarks like WMDP rely on distractor options; if distractors are implausible, a model can score high by elimination without real knowledge, inflating measured capability. Distractor quality is an under-examined variable.

**Direction:** Take a WMDP subset (proxy/benign portions) and create variants with stronger vs weaker distractors (e.g. swap in near-miss options vs obviously-wrong options). Measure how open-model accuracy and the elimination-vs-knowledge signal change with distractor plausibility.

**Why it matters:** If capability scores are partly an artifact of weak distractors, dangerous-capability thresholds used for go/no-go decisions are mis-calibrated. Pure item-design + inference, reproducible by any third party.

**Relevant context:** WMDP is a standard dangerous-capability MCQ benchmark used in sandbagging studies (arXiv 2508.00943). Item-response and distractor-quality issues parallel DIF/IRT critiques of safety benchmark items (catalogue context).
