# Reward-hacking honeypots for graders: do models exploit a known autograder weakness across model families?

**Problem:** Evals suffer from unreliable autograders; control studies reward hacking of overseers. Combined: if you deploy a deliberately exploitable autograder (a honeypot grader with a known loophole), how readily do different models discover and exploit it, and is exploitation correlated across homogeneous models?

**Direction:** Design a grading task with a planted loophole (e.g. the grader keys on a phrase, or can be flattered). Prompt several small open models to maximize their grade and measure (a) discovery/exploitation rate per model and (b) cross-model correlation in exploitation. Compare to a clean grader baseline. Report whether reward-hacking propensity is a shared, predictable trait — a homogeneous vulnerability.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies grader-gaming as a systematic, possibly homogeneous risk that undermines eval validity ecosystem-wide — neglected and lab-independent. Pure behavioral study via APIs/small models, no training.
