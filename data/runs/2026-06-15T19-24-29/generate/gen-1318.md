# Do Steering Vectors Survive a Jailbreak? Measuring Refusal-Vector Neutralization Under Adversarial Prefixes

**Problem:** Concept-vector steering is proposed as a safety lever, but it is unknown whether an attacker who controls the prompt can craft inputs that quietly cancel a deployed steering vector. The open-problem list in the subfield context explicitly flags adversarial robustness of concept vectors as unresolved.

**Direction:** On an open model (Llama-3.1-8B or Gemma-2-9B), extract a 'refusal' concept vector via contrastive activation addition, add it at inference to enforce refusal, then sweep a library of known jailbreak prefixes (DAN-style, role-play, prefix-injection). For each, measure whether the refusal vector still flips the model to refuse, and probe WHY: compute the cosine similarity between the residual stream of jailbroken-harmful vs. plain-harmless prompts. Test the hypothesis (reported in 2025 work) that jailbreak prefixes make harmful-prompt activations geometrically resemble harmless ones, blinding the vector.

**Why it matters:** If a single fixed steering vector can be neutralized by a known prefix, then steering-based deployment defenses give false assurance. This is a medium-horizon threat model labs under-prioritize, needs no training and no proprietary data, and produces a concrete robustness benchmark the community lacks.

**Relevant context:** Subfield open problem: 'can attackers craft inputs that neutralize steering?' (Beaglehole et al. 2026). Grounded in 'Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' (arXiv 2603.24543) and the 2025 finding that jailbroken harmful activations resemble harmless ones (arXiv 2605.24535).
