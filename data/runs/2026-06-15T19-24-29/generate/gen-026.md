# Easy-to-hard probe generalization: does a refusal probe trained on easy harmful prompts catch hard/obfuscated ones?

**Problem:** Scalable oversight studies easy-to-hard generalization; control uses activation probes. Combined: if you train a misbehavior probe only on easy, blatant harmful examples (cheap to label), does it generalize to detect subtle/obfuscated harmful behavior — the easy-to-hard question applied to monitoring rather than training.

**Direction:** On a small open model, train a linear 'will-comply-with-harm' probe using only blatant harmful prompts vs benign. Evaluate it on a held-out set of subtle/obfuscated harmful prompts (euphemistic, multi-step, role-played) that the probe never saw. Measure the easy-to-hard generalization gap and whether mixing a few hard examples closes it disproportionately. Compare against a behavioral classifier baseline.

**Why it matters:** If cheap-to-label easy examples yield probes that catch hard cases, monitoring scales affordably — directly relevant to both oversight and control. Open-weights, white-box, no lab data; a concrete test of an abstract oversight idea.
