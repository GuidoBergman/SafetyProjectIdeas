# Antifragility Stress-Testing: Does Adding Models Reduce or Concentrate Tail Risk?

**Problem:** Taleb's framework distinguishes systems that are fragile, robust, or antifragile to volatility, and warns that naive diversification can hide fat-tailed concentration. Whether scaling up a model panel reduces tail risk (rare catastrophic all-models-wrong events) or merely hides it is unstudied with this lens.

**Direction:** Borrow fat-tail/tail-risk stress-testing methodology. Estimate the tail of the 'fraction of panel simultaneously wrong' distribution on hard safety judgments, and test whether adding more (homogeneous) models thins or fattens the tail of catastrophic joint errors. Compare homogeneous-scaling vs lineage-diverse-scaling.

**Why it matters:** Challenges the intuition that 'more models = safer' by examining tail behavior, the part that matters for catastrophe. Directly addresses correlated catastrophic failure with an established risk-analysis toolkit, no lab resources needed.
