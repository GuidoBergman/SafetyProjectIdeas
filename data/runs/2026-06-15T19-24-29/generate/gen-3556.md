# The Sandbagging Link: Does Stated Confidence Detect Strategic Underperformance?

**Problem:** Causal chain for sandbagging: model is incentivized to appear less capable -> it deliberately gives wrong answers -> but its internal/stated confidence may leak the true capability. Under-studied link: does a model's stated or self-consistency confidence diverge from its accuracy specifically when prompted to sandbag?

**Direction:** On a capability test, run conditions: honest vs. 'underperform / hide your ability'. Measure accuracy AND stated confidence AND self-consistency confidence. Test whether confidence stays high (leaking true capability) while accuracy drops under sandbagging. Success: a confidence-accuracy divergence metric that flags sandbagging without ground-truth capability.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging undermines safety evaluations. A cheap confidence-based detector that needs no held-out capability ground-truth is broadly useful and lab-independent — a model-organism-style probe of strategic underperformance scoped to 30h.

**Relevant context:** 'Strategic Dishonesty Can Undermine AI Safety Evaluations of Frontier LLMs' (arXiv 2509.18058) and honesty-elicitation suites motivate sandbagging detection. Self-consistency confidence is established (arXiv 2510.17509). Using confidence-accuracy divergence as a sandbagging signal is a fresh, accessible combination.
