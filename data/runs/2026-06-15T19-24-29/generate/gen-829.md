# The AI Safety Index Under Stress: Does FLI's Methodology Survive Reweighting and Reproduction?

**Problem:** The FLI AI Safety Index grades major labs on safety, and is increasingly cited in governance discourse. Composite-index methodologies are notoriously sensitive to indicator weighting and subjective scoring — a failure mode where the headline ranking is an artifact of methodological choices rather than real safety differences. This robustness has not been independently stress-tested.

**Direction:** Treat ranking-instability as the failure mode. Reconstruct the Index from its public scoring data, then run a sensitivity analysis: perturb indicator weights, drop indicators one at a time, and apply alternative aggregation rules (e.g. min vs mean across dimensions). Measure how much lab rankings change and which labs' positions are fragile. Identify which indicators drive the ranking and whether the ordering is robust or methodologically contingent.

**Why it matters:** If a widely-cited safety scoreboard's rankings flip under reasonable reweighting, decisions and reputations built on it are on shaky ground. Sensitivity analysis is a standard, beginner-feasible statistics technique, and the Index is the context file's named benchmark. Pure public-data analysis, no compute, independent of the labs being ranked.
