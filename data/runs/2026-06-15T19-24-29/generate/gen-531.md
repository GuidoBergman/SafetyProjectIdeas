# Regression Testing for Alignment: Behavioral Drift Across Model Versions

**Problem:** Theoretical alignment cares about stability of an agent's values/preferences, but we lack a disciplined way to detect when a new model version silently regresses on safety-relevant behaviors that no public benchmark covers.

**Direction:** Adapt regression-test suites from software engineering. Curate a fixed battery of safety-relevant behavioral probes (refusals, corrigibility-style prompts, sycophancy, deception-temptation scenarios) and run it across several versions/sizes of an open model family, flagging 'regressions' where a newer/larger model behaves less safely. Treat the suite as a versioned, snapshot-able alignment regression harness.

**Why it matters:** Provides reusable infrastructure for catching alignment regressions across releases — a strategically valuable monitoring harness any small team can maintain, independent of lab internal tooling, and a concrete empirical handle on inductive-bias-driven behavioral drift.

**Relevant context:** Regression testing is standard SE practice; the theoretical study of inductive biases and their alignment implications is an Open Phil RFP priority. Open model families (multiple sizes) make cross-version comparison feasible.
