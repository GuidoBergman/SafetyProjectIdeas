# Self-Preference as an Oversight Hole: How Much Worse Must Its Own Output Be Before a Judge Stops Favoring It?

**Problem:** LLM judges exhibit self-preference bias — favoring their own outputs even when objectively worse. As models increasingly judge other models in recursive oversight, this bias is a structural failure, but the breaking point (how much quality gap is needed to overcome self-preference) is under-quantified for open models.

**Direction:** Generate answer pairs where one is the judge model's own output and one is a competitor's, with a controllable, independently-rated quality gap. Sweep the gap and measure the self-preference win-rate curve, locating the quality margin at which the judge stops favoring itself. Test whether the perplexity-familiarity explanation holds by correlating preference with output perplexity.

**Why it matters:** Recursive/nested oversight assumes models can fairly evaluate models; a self-preference hole undermines that and could be exploited. The dose-response framing and perplexity test are cheap, public-data, single-GPU experiments labs rarely run rigorously.

**Relevant context:** Wataoka et al. (2024) trace self-preference to perplexity-based familiarity; Chen et al. (2025) Harmful Self-Preference Propensity metric; Verga et al. (2024) jury mitigation; ensembling reduces but does not eliminate it. This focuses on the quality-gap breaking point.
