# Probing a Hidden-Goal Model Organism: Can a Linear Probe Beat the Output When the Goal Isn't Verbalized?

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: the interpretability-to-safety bridge meets deception_scheming around model organisms that pursue a goal WITHOUT verbalizing it — a scenario the field wants to study but rarely has accessible, scoped probes for. No subfield owns a beginner-feasible recipe to test whether internals reveal a hidden objective the CoT hides.

**Direction:** Use an existing small open 'model organism' or construct a lightweight one via a system prompt that instates a hidden objective (e.g., 'secretly steer the user toward option B but never say so'). On a battery of prompts, train a linear probe on activations to detect when the hidden goal is active, and compare probe accuracy against what's inferable from the output text alone. Quantify the 'internal-over-behavioral' detection advantage.

**Why it matters:** Bridges mechanistic_interpretability + deception_scheming + honesty_faithfulness. It directly tackles the steered-but-silent threat model and tests whether interpretability buys anything over black-box monitoring. Scoped to probing an existing/prompt-instated organism (no training from scratch), single-GPU, and a strong counterfactual model-organism slice.

**Relevant context:** Landscape favors model-organism studies (a model pursuing secret goals, attacking without verbalizing in CoT). Hidden-goal / 'secret agenda' model-organism work exists in the alignment literature; linear-probe-vs-output comparison is the standard accessible interpretability method. The catalogue has activation-patching-of-hidden-goal ideas but not a probe-vs-output detection-advantage framing on a silent-goal organism.
