# Self-Preference Bias in Recursive Oversight Chains

**Problem:** Self-preference bias (models favor their own outputs) is documented for single judges. In recursive oversight where a model both generates and later judges similar outputs, this bias could compound. The compounding effect across a 2-step chain is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Have a small open model generate answers, then judge a mix of its own and a sibling model's answers. Measure self-preference. Then add a recursion step: a second judge evaluates the first judge's verdicts. Test whether self-preference at step 1 propagates or attenuates through the second oversight layer.

**Why it matters:** If self-preference compounds through oversight layers, recursive protocols systematically protect the supervised model's own (possibly flawed) outputs — a direct threat to the recursive-oversight open problem. Inference-only.

**Relevant context:** Wataoka & Takahashi 'Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge' (arXiv:2410.21819): bias ranges -38% to +90%, stronger in larger models. Anthropic recursive-oversight open problem.
