# Token-Depth Mapping of Refusal Recovery

**Problem:** Qi et al. showed safety alignment is shallow, concentrated in the first few output tokens, but exactly how many tokens deep the refusal signal extends, and whether models can 'recover' (resume refusing) after a forced compliant prefix, is poorly characterized across open models.

**Direction:** Using prefilling attacks on open-weight models (Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B via a single GPU), force compliant prefixes of varying lengths (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 tokens) and measure at what prefix length the model can no longer recover and refuse. Plot a 'recovery curve' per model. No training needed — just inference with prefill control.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying the exact depth of shallow alignment and the recovery boundary gives a cheap, model-agnostic robustness metric. It directly tests the open problem of deepening alignment and provides a counterfactually valuable diagnostic any small lab can run without proprietary tooling.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946, ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper): alignment adapts the generative distribution primarily over the first few tokens; prefilling attacks exploit this. Follow-up work (arXiv:2503.03710, arXiv:2512.05518) proposes 'deep' alignment where models recover after a compliant prefix. Recovery boundary as a function of prefix length is not systematically mapped.
