# Are SAE Features Stable Across Random Seeds? A Reproducibility Follow-Up

**Problem:** SAE-based safety claims assume features are real model properties, but if independently trained SAEs (different seeds/widths) recover different feature sets, the features may be SAE artifacts. The degree of cross-SAE feature stability for the same model is under-quantified for newcomers.

**Direction:** Take two or more publicly available SAEs trained on the same layer of the same open model (different seeds/widths in Gemma Scope/SAELens), and measure feature alignment: for each latent in SAE A, find its best match in SAE B by activation correlation, and report the distribution of best-match similarities and the fraction of unstable features.

**Why it matters:** Feature stability is a prerequisite for trusting any SAE-derived safety monitor. A clean reproducibility audit on open SAEs tells the field how much of 'interpretability' is robust vs. seed-dependent. No training required.

**Relevant context:** SAEBench (arXiv 2503.09532) and the absorption/sanity-check literature raise SAE reliability concerns. Gemma Scope/SAELens release multiple SAEs per layer enabling matched comparisons.
