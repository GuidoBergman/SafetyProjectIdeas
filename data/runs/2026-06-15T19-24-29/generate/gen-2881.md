# Keyword-Trigger Boundary Conditions in Over-Refusal

**Problem:** XSTest shows models falsely refuse safe prompts because of lexical overfitting on sensitive keywords (e.g. 'kill' in 'how to kill a Python process'). The exact boundary, how much surrounding benign context is needed to suppress a keyword-triggered refusal, has not been mapped systematically.

**Direction:** Build minimal contrastive prompt pairs from XSTest-style safe prompts. Hold the trigger keyword fixed and incrementally vary the amount/strength of disambiguating benign context. For several open models, measure refusal rate as a function of context strength, producing a 'context dose needed to clear a refusal' curve per keyword and per model.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal degrades usefulness and is a measurable symptom of brittle, keyword-based safety training. A quantitative boundary map shows how shallow current refusal behavior is and gives a reproducible diagnostic that needs no lab data.

**Relevant context:** XSTest (arXiv:2308.01263) finds lexical overfitting drives false refusals via 250 safe + 200 unsafe contrast prompts; OR-Bench (arXiv:2405.20947) and 'When Safety Blocks Sense' (arXiv:2512.01037) extend over-refusal measurement. Prompts are open; runnable on one GPU.
