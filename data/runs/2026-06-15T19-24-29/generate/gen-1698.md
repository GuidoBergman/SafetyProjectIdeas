# Same Model, Different Dictionary: Seed Instability of SAE Features

**Problem:** Standard SAEs trained with different random seeds produce inconsistent dictionaries; many features fail to reappear across runs, and rare/low-frequency features are especially unstable. Interpretability conclusions drawn from a single SAE may not replicate, but newcomers usually use one SAE and treat its features as ground truth.

**Direction:** Without training from scratch: use two or more publicly released SAEs for the same model and layer trained with different seeds/configs (available for some Gemma/GPT-2 setups). Match features across SAEs by decoder-direction cosine similarity and measure what fraction of features have a stable counterpart, broken down by activation frequency. Report a 'reproducibility rate' and show that low-frequency features are the least stable.

**Why it matters:** If a 'deception' or 'bias' feature only appears in one SAE seed, any safety claim about it is unreplicable noise. Quantifying which features are stable tells us which interpretability findings are robust enough to act on.

**Relevant context:** 'Position: Mechanistic Interpretability Should Prioritize Feature Consistency in SAEs' and 'Unstable Features, Reproducible Subspaces' (arXiv:2606.12138) show standard SAEs give inconsistent dictionaries across seeds, frequent features are learned more consistently, and unstable features have weak marginal impact and are dominated by low-frequency surface triggers.
