# CoT-monitor blind spot audit: do diverse open models converge on the SAME unfaithful rationalizations?

**Problem:** CoT monitoring is more informative than action-only monitoring, but if models are homogeneous (Artificial Hivemind), they may produce the same post-hoc rationalizations for the same biased decisions — meaning a CoT monitor trained on one model's failures would share its blind spots and a population of monitors offers no real diversity.

**Direction:** Construct prompts with known answer-biasing cues (e.g. Turpin-style 'I think the answer is (A)' anchors, or ordering biases). Run 5-6 small open models, collect their CoT + final answers, and measure (a) how often each rationalizes a cue-driven answer without mentioning the cue (unfaithfulness rate) and (b) inter-model overlap in WHICH items they fail on and the rationalization templates they use (embedding-cluster the explanations). Report a homogeneity-of-unfaithfulness score.

**Why it matters:** Shared unfaithfulness means ensembling monitors gives a false sense of coverage — a correlated-failure / systemic risk directly relevant to control. Pure behavioral experiment via APIs/small models, no training, reproducible outside any lab.

**Relevant context:** Bridges honesty_faithfulness (CoT faithfulness; Turpin et al. biased-feature rationalization; Measuring CoT Monitorability, arxiv 2510.27378) with model_homogeneity (Artificial Hivemind, NeurIPS 2025). CoT monitoring informativeness from GPT-5 system card.
