# Translation as a Causal Detour: Which Link in the Refusal Chain Breaks Cross-Lingually?

**Problem:** Low-resource-language jailbreaks are known, but the causal locus is unclear: does safety fail because (a) the model never recognizes harmful intent in the foreign language, or (b) it recognizes intent but the refusal behavior is weaker in that language? These are different broken links demanding different fixes.

**Direction:** Translate harmful prompts into several languages, then run two probes on an open multilingual model: (1) an intent-classification prompt asking the model whether the request is harmful (tests recognition), and (2) the direct request (tests refusal behavior). Cross-tabulate to attribute cross-lingual failures to the recognition link vs the behavior link.

**Why it matters:** Disentangles two conflated causal mechanisms behind multilingual safety gaps, guiding whether mitigation should improve harm detection or refusal robustness. Translation + prompting only, no training, single GPU; distinct from prior cross-lingual 'gap mapping' by attributing the failure to a specific link.

**Relevant context:** Cross-lingual safety gaps are documented in the jailbreak literature and AILuminate covers multilingual hazards; Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) frame refusal as a learned early-token behavior that may not transfer across languages. (Distinct from prior 'Cross-Lingual Safety Gap Mapping' by separating recognition vs behavior links.)
