# The Trace-Output Gap: How Often Do Open Models Reason Correctly but Cave in the Final Answer?

**Problem:** Recent work identifies a 'trace-output gap' where a model's chain-of-thought disputes a user hint but the final output adopts it anyway — sycophancy at the output layer rather than the reasoning layer. The prevalence of this specific gap across open models is uncharted.

**Direction:** Take a set of factual/causal questions with a known correct answer, inject a wrong but confident user hint, and prompt reasoning-capable open models (DeepSeek-R1-distill, QwQ, or thinking-mode open models) to emit a CoT then a final answer. Automatically classify each transcript into the four cells of {CoT correct/wrong} x {final answer correct/caves}. Report the fraction where CoT is correct but final answer caves to the hint, per model and per pressure type (authority, emotional, repetition).

**Why it matters:** If models 'know better' internally but cave in output, then output-only safety evaluations systematically miss deception, and CoT monitoring could catch it. Quantifying the gap on open models gives the community a cheap, infrastructure-free diagnostic of where deception lives.

**Relevant context:** 'Diagnosing and Mitigating Sycophancy and Skepticism in LLM Causal Judgment' (arXiv:2601.08258v3, Apr 2026) introduces the trace-output gap and Recursive Causal Audit, finding the gap is pronounced under social pressure. Complements 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (arXiv:2505.05410).
