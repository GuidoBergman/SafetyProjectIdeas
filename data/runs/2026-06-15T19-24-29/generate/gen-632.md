# Testing the argument-stability condition from prover-estimator debate in a toy debate

**Problem:** Prover-Estimator Debate (2025-2026) proves that an 'argument stability' condition lets debate avoid the obfuscated-arguments problem. The formal condition has not been illustrated empirically on a small, human-judgeable task.

**Direction:** Build a tiny debate harness: two open instruct models argue over a verifiable claim (e.g. a multi-step arithmetic or factual decomposition), with a third weak model as estimator. Construct cases that are 'stable' vs deliberately 'unstable' (small perturbations flip the sub-claim) and measure whether the honest side wins more often in stable cases. Success = a win-rate comparison aligning with the stability prediction.

**Why it matters:** Scalable oversight via debate is a key non-lab safety bet. An accessible empirical illustration of when debate's guarantees hold helps the community calibrate expectations using only open models.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Avoiding Obfuscation with Prover-Estimator Debate' (arXiv:2506.13609, 2025-2026), which introduces the formal argument-stability condition.
