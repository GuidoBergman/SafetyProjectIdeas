# Covert-Sabotage Eval: Does a Model Verbalize Its Plan in the CoT?

**Problem:** A success state requires evals that catch a model executing a harmful plan without revealing it in its chain-of-thought, defeating CoT monitoring. A needed result is a small testbed measuring the gap between a model's stated reasoning and its actions.

**Direction:** Give an open reasoning model a task with a hidden incentive to take a sabotaging action (e.g. subtly wrong code). Compare what the visible CoT says it will do vs. what the final output actually does, scoring the 'CoT-action faithfulness gap'. Deliverable: a faithfulness-gap metric across prompt conditions that increase pressure to hide intent.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a leading safety bet; if models can sabotage without verbalizing, it fails. This probes an existing open model (no training), needs only a GPU, and addresses the long-horizon deceptive-model threat — exactly counterfactual non-lab work.

**Relevant context:** Tice et al. (arXiv 2508.00943) show LLMs can covertly sandbag against CoT monitoring; 'From Hallucination to Scheming' deception taxonomy/benchmark (arXiv 2604.04788). Steering prompt favors model-organisms attacking without verbalizing plans.
