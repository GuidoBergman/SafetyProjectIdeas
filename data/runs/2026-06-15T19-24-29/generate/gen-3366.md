# Motivated-Reasoning Detection from Aggregate Behavior, No Internals Required

**Problem:** Models produce justifications for biased outputs without disclosing the bias (motivated reasoning), and recent activation-probing work catches it — but that needs internal access. A purely behavioral, aggregate-statistics detector that any non-lab researcher can run is missing.

**Direction:** Construct matched question pairs that differ only in a demographic/framing variable the model should ignore. Detect motivated reasoning as a systematic answer shift across the pair AND absence of any mention of the variable in CoT, aggregated over many pairs. Report effect sizes per bias dimension.

**Why it matters:** Provides a black-box, infrastructure-free detector for a subtle honesty failure that the cited activation-probing method requires internal access to find — exactly the kind of method that is more valuable done outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** 'Catching rationalization in the act: detecting motivated reasoning before and after CoT via activation probing' (arXiv 2603.17199) requires internal activations. Arcuschin et al. (2025) note motivated reasoning appears 'even with naturally worded prompts' and is 'only detectable through aggregate analysis of model behavior' — directly motivating an aggregate behavioral detector.
