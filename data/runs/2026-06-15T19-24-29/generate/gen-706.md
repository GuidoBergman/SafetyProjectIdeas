# Detecting 'Safety-Washing' Language Across Lab Communications

**Problem:** A medium-horizon governance risk is rhetorical safety-washing: prominent safety language in public communications that is not matched by substantive commitments — but the divergence between rhetoric volume and commitment substance has not been measured.

**Direction:** Compile two corpora per lab: marketing/blog communications and formal policy/commitment documents. Use an LLM to score 'safety rhetoric intensity' in communications and 'substantive commitment density' in policies (concrete, conditional, falsifiable commitments per page). Compute a rhetoric-to-substance ratio per lab and rank who talks about safety most relative to what they commit to, validated on samples.

**Why it matters:** Surfacing safety-washing arms regulators and the public against rhetorical capture; it is a diffuse, fuzzy-reward evaluation built from public text that no lab would produce about itself, maximizing counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** FLI Index 'stated commitments vs implemented' framing; METR vague-commitments finding; arXiv:2512.01166 framework scores (median 18%).
