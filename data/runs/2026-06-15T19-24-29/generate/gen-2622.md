# Reliability Acceptance Sampling for Deciding How Much Control-Eval Evidence Is Enough

**Problem:** Control evaluations report a safety score from a finite number of episodes, but rarely state the confidence interval or how many episodes are needed to certify safety above a threshold. Decisions get made on noisy point estimates.

**Direction:** Import acceptance sampling and reliability demonstration testing from manufacturing QA. Treat each control episode as a pass/fail trial, derive the operating-characteristic curve and the number of clean episodes required to certify (with stated confidence) that the undetected-sabotage rate is below a target, then re-analyze published control-eval results to compute how strong their evidence actually is.

**Why it matters:** Gives control evals a rigorous sample-size and confidence framework so safety claims aren't over-read from small samples. Statistics-only re-analysis, no compute, directly improves the epistemics of safety cases.

**Relevant context:** Acceptance sampling / reliability demonstration testing (MIL-STD-105, OC curves) certify defect rates from finite samples. Control evals report safety scores over limited episodes (Ctrl-Z arXiv:2504.10374; arXiv:2511.02997) often without CIs.
