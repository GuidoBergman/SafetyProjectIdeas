# Steering the Concept of 'Being Watched': Can a Vector Induce or Suppress Evaluation Awareness?

**Problem:** Models may behave better when they infer they are being tested. No one has asked whether a concept vector for 'I am being evaluated' exists and whether steering it changes safety behavior — a sleeper threat for eval validity.

**Direction:** Construct contrastive pairs (transcripts framed as 'this is a safety test' vs. 'this is a real user') on an open model and extract an 'evaluation-awareness' direction. Steer it up/down and measure whether refusal rates and honesty on a held-out safety probe shift. Check if suppressing the direction makes the model behave as if unmonitored.

**Why it matters:** If eval-awareness is a steerable linear direction, then a model could in principle behave differently when it 'feels watched', undermining all steering-based safety evals — a long-horizon deception threat labs deprioritize. Model-organism flavored, fully feasible in 30h with an open model.

**Relevant context:** Motivated by 'Steering Awareness' (arXiv 2511.21399) showing models can detect activation steering and that steering is not invisible. Uses standard contrastive activation addition.
