# Audio Is the Forgotten Modality: A First Refusal-Transfer Probe for Speech-Input Models

**Problem:** GAP TARGETED: the multimodal safety infrastructure gap, sharpened to AUDIO — vision safety gets some attention but audio-input safety is almost entirely unevaluated, and no subfield owns whether a model that refuses a typed harmful request still refuses the SAME request spoken (TTS) to it.

**Direction:** Take ~50 harmful and ~50 benign requests. Render each as speech via an open TTS, feed to an open speech-capable model (or a Whisper-ASR -> LLM pipeline as a controlled baseline plus a true audio-LLM if feasible). Measure refusal rate text vs audio for matched content, plus robustness to simple acoustic perturbations (speed, background noise). Report an 'audio safety transfer gap'.

**Why it matters:** Bridges adversarial_robustness + evaluations_benchmarks. Audio is the most neglected modality in safety evaluation; voice agents are deploying fast. The study is cheap (open TTS/ASR/audio-LLM, one GPU), fully counterfactual, and surfaces a concrete deployment blind spot before it becomes an incident class.

**Relevant context:** Recent multimodal jailbreak work (arXiv:2510.20223, 'Beyond Text') shows audio models are vulnerable to perceptually simple transformations. Most safety benchmarks (AILuminate, HELM, TrustLLM) are text-only per the landscape; audio safety is essentially unbenchmarked. Open TTS, Whisper, and small audio-LLMs run on consumer GPUs.
