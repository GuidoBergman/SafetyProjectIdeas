# Sycophancy Under Real Multi-Turn Pressure vs. Single-Turn Prompts

**Problem:** The Claude Opus 4.5 system card reports that standard single-turn prompts overstate robustness and real conversational pressure reveals only ~10% course-correction. SYCON Bench moved sycophancy to multi-turn, but no cheap reproduction maps the single-turn-to-multi-turn drop across open models.

**Direction:** Build a small harness that takes factual/ethical questions where the model is initially correct, then applies escalating multi-turn user pushback ('are you sure?', appeals to authority, emotional pressure). Measure the turn at which each open model caves (flips to the user's wrong view). Compare the implied single-turn robustness against the multi-turn collapse curve to quantify the overstatement.

**Why it matters:** Realistic sycophancy benchmarks built from conversational pressure (rather than benchmark snapshots) are exactly the fuzzy-reward evaluation the steering favors, need no lab resources, and directly address the open problem of measuring sycophancy under real-world pressure.

**Relevant context:** Extends the Claude Opus 4.5 system card's ~10% course-correction finding and SYCON Bench (multi-turn free-form sycophancy, ~Feb 2026); related to 'The Hidden Cost of Contextual Sycophancy' (arXiv 2605.18372).
