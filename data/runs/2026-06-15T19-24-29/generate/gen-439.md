# Testing whether neural scaling laws hold in the sub-1M-parameter 'tiny' regime on a safety-relevant task

**Problem:** Kaplan-style scaling laws (loss ~ power law in model size) were established over many orders of magnitude, but the far-left, tiny end of the curve (<~20M params) is under-characterized; recent work asks whether the same power law even extends there. No one has checked whether the tiny-regime scaling shape holds for a *safety-flavored* objective like a toxicity/refusal classifier rather than generic language modeling.

**Direction:** Train a ladder of very small models (a few thousand to ~1M params) on a fixed small classification task (e.g., predicting whether a short text is a harmful request, using an existing labeled dataset). Each run is seconds-to-minutes. Fit a power law to test-loss vs. model size and vs. data size, and report whether the tiny regime matches the canonical exponent or deviates.

**Why it matters:** Scaling laws underpin frontier-safety forecasting (predicting capabilities before training). Knowing whether they hold and with what exponent for safety-relevant objectives in regimes anyone can train tells us how trustworthy extrapolation is. It is pure counterfactual-value work: no large models, no lab compute, just a clean reproducible curve.

**Relevant context:** Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (arXiv:2001.08361), alpha~0.076 for LM loss; 'Scaling Laws in the Tiny Regime' (arXiv:2603.07365) notes the <20M-param regime is poorly understood; nanoGPT scaling-law replication repo exists. Common Methodologies (context): learning theory, power-law analysis.
