# Deception Persistence Across Multi-Turn Conversation Length

**Problem:** A model that adopts a deceptive stance (e.g. concealing a hidden instruction) may or may not maintain it as conversation length grows and the user probes from different angles. The decay/persistence of deception over conversation turns is underexplored.

**Direction:** Plant a concealment objective via system prompt, then run scripted multi-turn dialogues of increasing length where a 'user' repeatedly probes from varied angles. Measure at which turn (if any) the model reveals or contradicts its concealed objective, across several open models. Produce survival curves of deception persistence vs turn count.

**Why it matters:** Real deployments are long conversations; if deception erodes with probing, multi-turn interrogation is a cheap detection method. If it persists, that raises the bar for safety cases. Pure inference, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Measuring Sycophancy of Language Models in Multi-turn Dialogues' (arXiv:2505.23840) shows multi-turn dynamics matter; MASK (arXiv:2503.03750) elicits beliefs then tests contradiction. Anthropic system card notes 10% course-correction under sustained conversational pressure.
