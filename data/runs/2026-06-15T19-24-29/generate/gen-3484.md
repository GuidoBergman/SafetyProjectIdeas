# Replicating the Confident-Wrong Failure: Abstention Collapse Under Binary Scoring

**Problem:** OpenAI argues hallucination is partly a statistical artifact of binary scoring that penalizes 'I don't know'. The claim is that under accuracy-only grading, models guess rather than abstain even when uncertain. This incentive story has been argued but is straightforward to demonstrate and characterize empirically on open models.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA-style hard factual questions, prompt a small open model under two regimes: (a) standard 'answer the question' and (b) explicit 'abstain if unsure, no penalty'. Measure how the confident-wrong rate, abstention rate, and calibration shift. Characterize how strongly the prompt framing alone reshapes the honesty/guessing tradeoff.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that a major honesty failure (confident fabrication) is partly an evaluation-design artifact, and quantifies how much can be recovered with zero training, purely by changing the scoring/prompt contract — a cheap, lab-independent honesty intervention.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI's hallucination explainer and 'Why Language Models Hallucinate' line of argument: binary 0-1 grading rewards guessing over abstention. SimpleQA (OpenAI). I-CALM (arXiv:2604.03904) on incentivizing confidence-aware abstention.
