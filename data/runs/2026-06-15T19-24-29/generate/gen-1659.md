# Replicating Induction-Head Detection via a Simple Per-Head Metric Across Open Models

**Problem:** Induction heads can be detected with a simple per-head 'induction score' (attention to the token after a previous copy). This metric has been computed for a few models, but a clean cross-family census — how prevalent and how strong induction heads are across several small open models, and whether their fraction correlates with in-context-learning ability — is a tractable, underdone replication.

**Direction:** Implement the standard induction-score metric in TransformerLens (attention from the current token to the position after its earlier occurrence on repeated random sequences). Replicate it across Gemma-2-2b, Llama-3.2-1B, Pythia-1.4B, and GPT-2 medium. The twist: correlate each model's count/strength of induction heads with a simple in-context-learning benchmark score, testing whether more/stronger induction heads predict better ICL across families.

**Why it matters:** Induction heads underlie in-context learning, which is the mechanism behind prompt-based capability elicitation and many jailbreaks. A cross-family census linking circuit prevalence to capability — a single-GPU study on public models — builds reusable measurement infrastructure and tests whether a mechanistic feature predicts a safety-relevant capability.

**Relevant context:** Olsson et al. 2022 (arXiv:2209.11895) defined the induction-head circuit and per-head induction score and linked induction heads to ICL. TransformerLens supports attention-pattern extraction across 50+ models including Pythia, GPT-2, Gemma, Llama.
