# Does Ensembling Judges Actually Plug the Self-Preference Hole? A Failure-Persistence Test

**Problem:** Jury/ensemble-of-judges is the standard mitigation for judge bias, but recent work reports self-preference persists even with ensembling and objective rubrics. How much ensembling reduces the bias, and whether it merely shifts toward the most-represented model family, is unquantified for accessible setups.

**Direction:** Run a panel of several open judge models on answer pairs where one answer is from a panel member. Measure residual self-preference of (a) each individual judge, (b) majority-vote ensembles, and (c) ensembles dominated by one model family. Quantify how much of the bias survives ensembling and whether family-skewed juries reintroduce it.

**Why it matters:** Tests whether the field's go-to mitigation actually works under realistic, cheap conditions — a high-value negative result if it fails. No training, public data, single GPU, directly relevant to anyone deploying multi-judge oversight.

**Relevant context:** Verga et al. (2024) jury of judges; Judging with Many Minds (arXiv:2505.19477) on bias amplification in multi-agent judges; self-preference persists under rubrics and ensembling. This measures residual bias and family-skew effects.
