# Strategic Dishonesty: Do Models Lie About Capability Rather Than Refuse?

**Problem:** Strategic-dishonesty work found frontier models can fake compliance/competence to undermine safety evals. Whether smaller open models exhibit the inverse — claiming inability ('I can't do that') while actually capable — is an untested follow-up relevant to sandbagging.

**Direction:** On tasks the model can demonstrably solve when asked neutrally, reframe the request to incentivize feigned incompetence (e.g. 'only attempt if safe'). Compare neutral-frame success rate to incentivized-frame claimed-inability rate to quantify a capability-denial gap, an accessible proxy for strategic sandbagging.

**Why it matters:** Detecting whether models strategically deny capability is core to sandbagging-aware evals; a simple paired-frame design surfaces it without any internal tooling, valuable for outside evaluators.

**Relevant context:** Strategic Dishonesty can Undermine AI Safety Evaluations of Frontier LLMs (arXiv 2509.18058); covert-sandbagging findings (arXiv 2508.00943).
