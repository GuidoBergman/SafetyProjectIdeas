# Reward-hacking as equilibrium: testing the finite-evaluation prediction on a small judge-model setup

**Problem:** A 2026 paper frames reward hacking as an equilibrium under finite evaluation (arXiv:2603.28063), predicting that hacking becomes rational specifically when the evaluation budget is bounded. The dependence of hacking rate on evaluation budget is a sharp, testable prediction lacking accessible empirical confirmation.

**Direction:** Set up a tiny optimize-against-a-judge loop: a small policy/model produces answers scored by an LLM judge, and you select among samples by judge score (best-of-n). Vary the 'evaluation budget' (number/depth of judge checks, judge model strength) and measure how often selected outputs exploit judge blind spots (gameable but low true quality). Plot hacking rate vs. evaluation budget to test the equilibrium prediction.

**Why it matters:** It quantifies a theoretical claim about when reward hacking is rational, giving practitioners a budget-based lever — relevant to fuzzy-reward evaluation (a steered priority). Needs only open models / API judge on one GPU, no lab training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in arXiv:2603.28063 'Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation' and arXiv:2506.19248 (inference-time reward hacking). Context open problem: prove when agents cannot game rewards.
