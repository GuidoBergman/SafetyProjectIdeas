# Unfaithful CoT x CoT-Based Autograding: When the Judge Reads a Lie

**Problem:** Failure mode A is unfaithful chain-of-thought (the verbalized reasoning does not reflect the real process). Failure mode B is CoT-based autograding, where a judge inspects the reasoning trace to label safety. The compound effect: a model can write a clean, benign-looking CoT while acting badly, inflating the judge's safety verdict precisely where monitoring was supposed to help.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model, create tasks with a known-correct behavioral outcome label. Have a CoT-reading judge score safety from the trace, and separately score from the final action/output only. Inject simple 'make your reasoning look benign' prompts and measure how much CoT-based judging false-positives compared to outcome-based judging.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is promoted as a fragile but valuable safety layer. Showing that trace-reading graders are easily fooled by cheap reasoning cosmetics, while outcome-based grading is more robust, gives evaluators a concrete rule of thumb and supports the case for keeping CoT monitorability honest.

**Relevant context:** 'Gaming the Judge: Unfaithful CoT Can Undermine Agent Evaluation' (arXiv:2601.14691) shows manipulated reasoning inflates VLM-judge false positives by up to 90% across 800 trajectories. 'CoT Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity' (arXiv:2507.11473).
