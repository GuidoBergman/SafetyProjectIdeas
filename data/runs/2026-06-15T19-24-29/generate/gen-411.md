# When does CCS find truth vs. a salient distractor? A controlled follow-up on unsupervised latent-knowledge probing

**Problem:** Discovering Latent Knowledge (CCS) finds a consistent direction in activations claimed to encode truth, but follow-ups (arXiv:2312.10029) show it often latches onto features uncorrelated with truth. The conditions under which CCS recovers truth versus a confound are not cleanly mapped — central to formal ELK.

**Direction:** Use a small open instruct model and a binary-QA dataset where you can deliberately inject a confounding feature correlated with the label on the train split but anti-correlated on a held-out split (e.g. topic, sentence length, a trigger word). Run CCS, measure how often the learned direction tracks truth vs. the confound out of distribution, and chart the failure rate against confound strength.

**Why it matters:** ELK is a core formal-alignment open problem (context file). A crisp empirical map of when unsupervised consistency methods get captured by distractors directly informs whether 'consistency alone identifies truth' — a question the theory leaves open. Uses an open model, public data, one GPU; no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Burns et al. arXiv:2212.03827 (CCS) and arXiv:2312.10029 'Challenges with unsupervised LLM knowledge discovery'. Context lists 'formal frameworks for ELK' as an open problem.
