# Backcasting from 'Safety Frameworks Are Enforceable': Quantifying Vagueness in Responsible Scaling Policies

**Problem:** A success state where voluntary frontier safety frameworks become enforceable requires that their commitments be specific enough to verify. Today many commitments are vague ('we will evaluate for dangerous capabilities') with no measurable trigger, so the gap between voluntary codes and enforceable standards persists.

**Direction:** Assemble the published safety frameworks (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, Google DeepMind FSF, Meta, xAI, etc.), then build a rubric scoring each commitment sentence on specificity: is there a quantitative threshold? a named evaluation? a defined responsible party? a deadline? a consequence for breach? Use simple text classification (manual + an LLM second-pass with a fixed rubric) to score every commitment and produce a per-lab 'enforceability index'.

**Why it matters:** Working backward from enforceability shows the precondition is operationalized, testable commitments. A vagueness/specificity score makes the EU AI Office's exact problem (voluntary vs enforceable gap) measurable and tells regulators which clauses need tightening. Counterfactually strong: outsiders are better positioned to grade vagueness than the labs writing the documents.

**Relevant context:** A Grading Rubric for AI Safety Frameworks (arXiv:2409.08751); 16 companies made Frontier AI Safety Commitments (May 2024); EU GPAI Code of Practice bridges the voluntary-standards gap. Open Problems in Technical AI Governance emphasizes operationalization.
