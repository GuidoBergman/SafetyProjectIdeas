# Multilingual Safety Gap x Deception: Do Models Deceive More Readily in Low-Resource Languages?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) cross-lingual safety degradation (robustness/multilingual); (B) strategic deception (deception/scheming). Compound effect: if honesty safeguards are weaker in low-resource languages, deceptive behaviors (false assertions, hidden non-compliance) may be more accessible there, opening a deception backdoor via language choice.

**Direction:** Translate a small honesty/deception probe set (pressure-to-lie scenarios, known-false-user-belief items) into several languages spanning resource levels. On open multilingual models, measure deceptive-assertion rate and sycophantic-agreement rate per language. Test whether deception rises monotonically as language resource level falls, controlling for translation quality with back-translation checks.

**Why it matters:** Surfaces a compounding cross-lingual honesty hole that English-centric evals miss entirely — diffuse, counterfactually valuable, and needs no lab data.

**Relevant context:** 'Sycophancy as a Multilingual Alignment Failure' (arXiv:2606.08451) shows safety degrades across languages. UK AISI honesty agenda treats honesty as a candidate sufficient safety property.
