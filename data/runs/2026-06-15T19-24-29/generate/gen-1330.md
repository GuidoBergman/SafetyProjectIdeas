# Steering Strength as a Dial: Mapping the Safety/Capability Pareto Frontier for One Concept

**Problem:** Everyone knows strong steering degrades capability, but for any given safety concept there is no public Pareto curve telling deployers the best achievable safety gain per unit of capability cost.

**Direction:** For a single safety concept (e.g., toxicity refusal) on an open model, sweep steering coefficient over a fine grid. At each point measure (a) safety success and (b) capability via MMLU/TriviaQA subset. Plot the full Pareto frontier, identify the knee, and compare two extraction methods (CAA vs. difference-in-means) to see which dominates the frontier.

**Why it matters:** Turns a vague tradeoff into an actionable operating-point recommendation — exactly the kind of reproducible measurement useful outside a lab. Single GPU, no training, clean success criteria.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Steering Without Side Effects' (arXiv 2406.15518) and capability-degradation findings (15-20% MT-Bench drops at high steering). Uses standard MMLU/TriviaQA evaluation.
