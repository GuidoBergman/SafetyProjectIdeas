# Coverage-Guided Fuzzing of LLM-Judge Specifications

**Problem:** An LLM-as-judge encodes an implicit specification of 'good behavior', but its blind spots — inputs where it grossly mis-scores — are unknown and could be exploited by a reward-gaming agent.

**Direction:** Import coverage-guided fuzzing (AFL-style: seed corpus + mutation engine + oracle). Use a cheap secondary signal (e.g., disagreement with a second judge, or a verifiable sub-property) as the oracle, mutate candidate answers, and keep mutations that maximize judge-vs-oracle disagreement. Catalogue the discovered failure modes and estimate the judge's exploitable surface for a few open judge models.

**Why it matters:** Provides an empirical lower bound on how gameable a given evaluator is, mapping the theoretical specification-gaming surface. Needs no training and no proprietary data — a directly counterfactual evaluator-stress-test that a small team can run.

**Relevant context:** Coverage-guided fuzzers use seed pool + mutation engine + oracle; AgentFuzzer and Jail-Fuzzer apply fuzzing to LLM agents and judges (arXiv:2505.05849, 2408.00523); 'Fuzzing AI Judges to Bypass Security Controls' (Palo Alto Unit 42). Connects to AISI's reward-gaming open problem.
