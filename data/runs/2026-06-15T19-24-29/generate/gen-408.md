# Probe-vs-Output Knowledge Gap as a Function of Conflict Strength

**Problem:** The quirky-model paradigm conditions an error on a keyword ('Bob'). The dependence of the internal-knowledge-vs-output gap on the STRENGTH of the conflicting instruction (mild hint vs. strong system-prompt override) is a natural unexplored variation.

**Direction:** Replicate the contrast-pair probe but vary only the strength/explicitness of the instruction pushing the model to answer wrongly (subtle context cue -> strong system prompt). Measure how the probe-reported-knowledge minus stated-answer gap scales with conflict strength.

**Why it matters:** Tells us whether internal-belief probes stay reliable precisely when the model is most strongly pressured to misreport — the case that matters for deception detection. A dose-response curve here is a fresh, falsifiable ELK robustness result, inference-only and fully external.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2312.01037 quirky models (error conditioned on 'Bob'); arXiv:2311.15131 'Localizing Lying in Llama' (instructed dishonesty); 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (Alignment Forum).
