# SilentEdit-Bench: Measuring How Often Overseers Catch Subtly Inserted Errors

**Problem:** Oversight protocols assume overseers catch injected errors, but there is no benchmark that systematically varies error subtlety (typo-level to logically-deep) and reports the overseer's detection-vs-subtlety curve on a fixed task family.

**Direction:** Programmatically inject errors of graded subtlety into correct solutions (number swaps, sign flips, dropped premises, plausible-but-wrong lemmas) for verifiable math/code items, then measure an open judge's detection rate per subtlety tier. Deliver the error-injection library and detection curves.

**Why it matters:** Characterizing which error depths slip past overseers operationalizes systematic oversight failure modes (an explicit open problem) and gives a controllable, fully synthetic, lab-independent benchmark.

**Relevant context:** Prover-verifier games (arXiv 2407.13692) use 'sneaky' provers inserting errors verifiers must catch; subfield surprising-result follow-up calls for 'systematic characterization of oversight failure modes.'
