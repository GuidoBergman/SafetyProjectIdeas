# Larger Models Steer Better and Jailbreak Worse: A Scale-Interaction Compound Study

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): larger models are more steerable (Beaglehole surprising result). Failure mode B (jailbreak robustness): steering's jailbreak-amplification effect grows with model scale. Compound risk: the same scale that makes beneficial steering easier makes harmful steering-side-effects worse — a double-edged scaling law.

**Direction:** Across a model-size ladder within one open family (e.g. Qwen2.5 0.5B / 1.5B / 7B), extract a benign steering vector and measure (1) steerability (effect size of intended behavior change) and (2) jailbreak-success amplification from the same vector. Plot both against parameter count to test whether benefit and harm both increase with scale, and whether harm grows faster.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a scaling interaction that determines whether steering gets safer or more dangerous as models grow. Fully feasible — small model family, inference only — and tests two of the source paper's claims at once.

**Relevant context:** Larger-models-more-steerable from Beaglehole et al. (concept_steering context). Scale-dependent jailbreak amplification grounded in arXiv 2603.24543 ('effects grow with model scale').
