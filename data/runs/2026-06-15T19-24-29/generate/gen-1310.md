# Provider-Family Fingerprinting: Can You Identify a Model's Lineage From Its Errors?

**Problem:** Kim et al. find error correlation is driven partly by shared provider. Flipping this: if models within a provider family share a distinctive error signature, you should be able to fingerprint a black-box model's lineage from its mistakes alone. This is an untested, practically useful consequence of homogeneity for transparency/auditing.

**Direction:** Collect error/refusal patterns of ~15 models on a fixed probe set. Train a simple classifier (logistic regression on agreement features) to predict provider family from behavioral fingerprint. Evaluate accuracy and which probes are most lineage-revealing. This reveals how much hidden homogeneity exists within families.

**Why it matters:** Lineage fingerprinting supports supply-chain transparency (detecting undisclosed reliance on one base model) — a concentration-risk monitoring tool deployable by outsiders. Simple stats/ML, fits participant skill set.

**Relevant context:** Extends the provider-driven correlation finding in 'Correlated Errors in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2506.07962, June 2025) into a behavioral lineage-fingerprinting task.
