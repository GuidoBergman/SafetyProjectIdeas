# Where in the Causal Chain Does a Jailbreak Suppress Refusal?

**Problem:** Jailbreaks lead to harmful outputs via a causal chain: malicious prompt -> internal harm representation -> refusal-direction activation -> refusal token. Arditi et al. showed refusal is mediated by a single direction, and jailbreaks 'inhibit' it, but WHICH link breaks (the harm is never represented, vs. it is represented but the refusal direction fails to fire) is under-studied.

**Direction:** On an open chat model (Qwen3-4B or Llama-3.1-8B) with TransformerLens, extract the refusal direction via difference-in-means on harmful/harmless prompts. For a set of working jailbreaks, measure layer-by-layer (a) projection of activations onto a 'harmfulness' probe direction and (b) projection onto the refusal direction. Localize whether the jailbreak zeroes out harm-detection early or only the downstream refusal write.

**Why it matters:** Pinpointing which causal link a jailbreak attacks tells defenders whether to harden harm-detection or refusal-execution. This is a model-organism / monitoring study needing only open weights and a single GPU, valuable outside frontier labs.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' (arXiv 2406.11717): erasing one direction prevents refusal; jailbreaks 'actively inhibit the model's internal refusal direction'. Geometry of Refusal (arXiv 2502.17420) suggests multiple directions exist. TransformerLens supports difference-in-means probing on 50+ open models.
