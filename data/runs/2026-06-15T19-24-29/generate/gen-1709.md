# Polysemantic Neurons That Fool Single-Concept Labels: A Polysemanticity Census

**Problem:** Many neurons are polysemantic (fire on multiple unrelated concepts due to superposition), so labeling a neuron with one concept from its top activations is frequently wrong. The base rate of polysemanticity for a given small model is rarely measured, so analysts don't know how often single-concept labels are misleading.

**Direction:** On GPT-2 small, sample many MLP neurons. For each, collect top-activating examples and cluster them by embedding similarity; count how many distinct concept clusters appear among the high activations. Report the distribution of clusters-per-neuron (the polysemanticity census) and the fraction of neurons for which a single-concept label would be misleading, broken down by layer.

**Why it matters:** Neuron-level safety claims ('this neuron detects deception') are unreliable if most neurons are polysemantic. A simple census quantifies how often single-concept interpretation fails and motivates dictionary methods, all doable on a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** 'Superposition is not just neuron polysemanticity' (LessWrong/AF) and 200 COP superposition notes explain polysemanticity via superposition; clustering dataset examples across the activation spectrum is the recommended way to surface multiple behaviors per neuron.
