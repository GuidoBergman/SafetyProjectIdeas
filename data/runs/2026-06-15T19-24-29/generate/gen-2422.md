# Reproducing the ColMAD Non-Zero-Sum Debate Gain on a New Domain

**Problem:** ColMAD (arXiv:2510.20963, Oct 2025) reframes multi-agent debate as non-zero-sum collaboration and reports up to 19% gains over competitive debate by suppressing three 'debate hacking' behaviors (fake evidence, overconfident claims, fallacious arguments). It was evaluated on error detection; whether the collaborative reframing transfers to other oversight tasks is untested.

**Direction:** Implement the collaborative vs. competitive debate prompt variants from ColMAD and run them on a different oversight task (e.g., detecting subtle factual errors in model-written summaries). Measure error-detection rate and tally occurrences of the three debate-hacking behaviors in transcripts to see if the collaborative framing genuinely reduces them.

**Why it matters:** Whether 'collaborative debate' is a robust oversight improvement or domain-specific determines if it is worth adopting. The transcript-level hacking audit is a transferable method, all via prompting.

**Relevant context:** Towards Scalable Oversight with Collaborative Multi-Agent Debate in Error Detection (ColMAD, arXiv:2510.20963, Oct 2025): competitive debate causes debate hacking (fake evidence, overconfident claims, fallacious arguments); non-zero-sum ColMAD outperforms competitive debate by 19% and beats single-agent baselines on error detection.
