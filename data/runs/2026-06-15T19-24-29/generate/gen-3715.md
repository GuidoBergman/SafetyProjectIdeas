# Does the Image-Tool Robustness Effect Generalize Beyond Jailbreaks to Injection and Misalignment?

**Problem:** A surprising AgentDojo-related finding is that routing answers through an explicit image tool lowers attack success, modeled as a 'safety vector' residual shift in hidden states. It is unknown whether this robustness effect is specific to jailbreaks or generalizes to indirect prompt injection and agentic-misalignment dilemmas.

**Direction:** Replicate the image-tool-invocation pattern on open multimodal models, then test it on three threat classes: (1) jailbreaks (the original), (2) indirect prompt injection in a tool-use task, (3) an agentic-misalignment dilemma. Measure whether the robustness benefit transfers, persists, or vanishes outside jailbreaks, and whether a probe of the 'safety direction' predicts the benefit.

**Why it matters:** If a simple, training-free interaction design (forcing tool routing) robustly raises safety across threat types, it's a cheap deployable mitigation. If it only helps jailbreaks, the 'safety vector' story is narrower than claimed. Lightweight probing on open models fits the compute budget.

**Relevant context:** The AgentDojo-adjacent result: explicit image-tool interaction lowers attack success rate, modeled as a residual shift toward a safety-relevant direction ('image-tool safety vector'). AgentDojo (arXiv 2406.13352) supplies the injection tasks for the generalization test.
