# Dataset-size threshold: how few contrastive pairs before a safety steering vector becomes noise?

**Problem:** CAA assumes the empirical mean over a contrastive dataset faithfully represents the intended semantic direction, but with small/imperfect datasets this breaks. The minimum dataset size for a stable safety vector—and how degradation manifests—is not documented for beginners.

**Direction:** On an open model, extract a safety concept vector from contrastive sets of increasing size (e.g., 5, 20, 100, 500 pairs), each repeated with random subsamples. Measure vector stability (cosine across subsamples) and steering effectiveness as a function of dataset size to locate the threshold where the direction stabilizes.

**Why it matters:** Many practitioners extract steering vectors from tiny hand-built datasets. A principled size-stability curve prevents over-trusting noisy vectors and gives a simple reliability checklist—an inexpensive, reproducible methodology contribution with clear safety value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in CAA's empirical-mean assumption and 'imperfect dataset' failure (arXiv 2505.03189) plus dataset-dependence findings (Tan et al.). Inference-only on open model; bootstrap/statistics-centric, beginner-feasible.
