# Injection Survival Across a Delegation Chain

**Problem:** Analysts warn prompt injection is amplified across delegation hops (a 70%-per-hop detector catches only ~17% over five hops), but this multiplicative decay has not been empirically measured with real agents.

**Direction:** Build a minimal N-hop delegation chain of open-model agents (planner -> subagent -> sub-subagent) passing a task plus untrusted document content. Plant an injection in the document and measure, per hop, the probability the injected instruction survives and propagates. Empirically test the multiplicative-decay model and whether a paraphraser/summarizer at each hop breaks the chain.

**Why it matters:** Multi-agent delegation is exploding in deployment with little safety infrastructure. An empirical hop-by-hop survival curve validates (or refutes) the scary theoretical estimate and shows whether cheap inter-hop paraphrasing is a real mitigation — strategically valuable infrastructure research.

**Relevant context:** WorkOS/Okta delegation analyses: injection 'structurally amplified because inter-agent communication is typically trusted and unfiltered'; 70%/hop detector -> 17% over 5 hops. arXiv:2507.03010 'Subversion via Focal Points' studies inter-agent collusion. Mitigation: trusted summariser/paraphraser to reduce channels.
