# Does Cross-Lingual Prompting Reveal or Hide Shared Blind Spots?

**Problem:** Safety failures are known to concentrate in low-resource languages. Whether those low-resource blind spots are *shared* across models (so switching vendor doesn't help) or idiosyncratic is unknown, and it determines whether multilingual deployments have correlated safety holes.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of safety-sensitive prompts, translate into several languages spanning high/low resource. Run multiple open multilingual models. Measure, per language, both the failure rate and the cross-model failure-overlap. Test whether overlap grows as resource level drops (shared low-resource blind spots).

**Why it matters:** Multilingual deployments serving global users may have correlated, simultaneously-exploitable safety gaps — a real, under-prioritized harm. Achievable with public models and free translation, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Low-resource-language LLM safety-failure findings from the search; extends homogeneity/shared-blind-spot framing across languages.
