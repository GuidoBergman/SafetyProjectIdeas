# Diverse-Prompt Monitor Ensembles vs Single Big Monitor on a Budget

**Problem:** Ensemble monitoring research shows a diverse ensemble of small monitors can beat a single costly monitor, but the published result varies prompt strategy and fine-tuning. A no-fine-tuning, prompt-only variant — the version a student with one GPU could actually run — has not been isolated.

**Direction:** Build an ensemble of small open monitors (e.g. 5 copies of an 8B model) differing ONLY in their scoring prompt (security-auditor persona, naive reviewer, adversarial reviewer, etc.). Compare averaged-ensemble AUROC against a single larger monitor (e.g. 70B via API) on SHADE-Arena transcripts at matched dollar/compute cost. Measure anti-correlation between ensemble members to confirm diversity is the active ingredient.

**Why it matters:** Confirms whether cheap, prompt-diverse ensembles are a viable monitoring strategy for groups without lab compute — directly counterfactual-valuable infrastructure. Replicates and narrows a 2026 finding to a feasible prompt-only regime.

**Relevant context:** Ensemble Monitoring for AI Control: Diverse Signals Outweigh More Compute (arXiv 2605.15377) — diverse small-monitor ensembles beat repeated sampling and can outperform a single costly monitor; simple averaging captures most gains. SHADE-Arena (arXiv 2506.15740) supplies benign/malign transcripts.
