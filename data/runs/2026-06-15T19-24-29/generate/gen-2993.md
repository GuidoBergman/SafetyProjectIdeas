# Scorer Disagreement x Threshold-Based Release Gates: Noise That Flips Go/No-Go

**Problem:** Failure mode A is scorer/autograder disagreement (different graders give discrepant results). Failure mode B is brittle threshold-based release gates (deploy if harm rate < X%). The compound effect: when a benchmark sits near the regulatory threshold, grader noise alone can flip the deploy decision, making safety governance effectively a coin-flip.

**Direction:** Run 2-3 different open-model judges (or temperature variants of one) over the same harmful-completion set to get a distribution of harm-rate estimates. Simulate a release gate at several thresholds and compute the probability that grader choice alone flips the go/no-go decision near each threshold.

**Why it matters:** EU AI Act-style regimes lean on benchmark thresholds. Quantifying how often grader noise crosses the line is directly policy-relevant and needs no proprietary data. It argues for reporting grader confidence intervals and consensus, not point estimates, at decision boundaries.

**Relevant context:** AISafetyLab found discrepant scorer results (subfield context). 'Skewed Score' (arXiv:2507.03772) and RAND Judge Reliability Harness quantify judge variability. US CAISI/NIST push for standardized cross-model metrics (subfield context).
