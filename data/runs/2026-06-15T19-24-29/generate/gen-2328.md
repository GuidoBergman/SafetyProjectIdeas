# Item Difficulty Curves to Find Where Weak-to-Strong Oversight Breaks

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong generalization breaks down as capability gaps grow, but we lack a fine-grained, item-level map of the difficulty frontier where weak judges stop tracking truth.

**Direction:** Borrow dose-response/difficulty-curve modeling from pharmacology/psychometrics (without re-using already-covered IRT framings). Treat per-item difficulty (estimated cheaply from strong-model confidence or human-rated complexity) as the 'dose' and judge accuracy as 'response'; fit a curve and locate the breakdown threshold for several open judge sizes. Pure analysis on existing labeled items.

**Why it matters:** Gives a quantitative breakdown-threshold characterization of weak-to-strong oversight, directly answering 'when can weaker models reliably supervise stronger ones', achievable on a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Dose-response curve fitting from pharmacology. Weak-to-strong breakdown at large gaps (Kenton et al. 2024; OpenAI weak-to-strong, open code per context). Distinct from already-covered IRT person-fit / dose-response capability-elicitation ideas.
