# Weak-to-Strong Elicitation vs. Imitation: A Behavioral Test Inspired by the 2026 Mechanism Paper

**Problem:** The Mechanism of Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Feature Elicitation from Latent Knowledge (arXiv:2605.12908, May 2026) argues the strong model succeeds by eliciting its pre-trained knowledge rather than copying the weak supervisor. This 'elicitation vs. imitation' distinction has a behavioral signature that can be probed without training.

**Direction:** Use weak labels (from a small model) to few-shot/in-context 'supervise' a stronger model on a task where the weak labels are systematically wrong on a known subset. If the strong model is eliciting latent knowledge, it should disagree with the weak labels on items it 'knows'; if imitating, it should reproduce the weak errors. Measure the disagreement-on-known-items rate.

**Why it matters:** Whether weak-to-strong is elicitation or imitation determines whether weak supervisors can safely steer stronger models. A cheap in-context behavioral proxy makes this testable outside labs, no fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** The Mechanism of Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Feature Elicitation from Latent Knowledge (arXiv:2605.12908, May 2026): the strong model learns by eliciting pre-trained knowledge while retaining general capabilities, rather than imitating the weak supervisor's specialized errors.
