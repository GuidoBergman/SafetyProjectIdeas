# In-Context Emergent Misalignment as a Zero-Training Red-Team Probe

**Problem:** EM was recently shown to arise from narrow in-context examples, not just fine-tuning (arXiv 2510.11288). This means misalignment can be elicited with prompting alone — but the dose-response (how many narrow bad in-context examples are needed to flip behavior) across model sizes is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Using only API/local inference (no training), prepend 1, 4, 16, 64 narrow misaligned examples (insecure code) to benign unrelated queries across several open models, and measure the broad misalignment rate as a function of example count and model size.

**Why it matters:** A training-free, near-zero-compute red-team method usable by anyone, mapping how easily retrieved/injected context can subvert a deployed model — a direct concern for RAG and agent pipelines that labs deploy but rarely stress-test this way.

**Relevant context:** Emergent Misalignment via In-Context Learning (arXiv 2510.11288): 'narrow in-context examples can produce broadly misaligned LLMs'; relevant to RAG and tool-using agents. Builds on Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424).
