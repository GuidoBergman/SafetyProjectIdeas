# DiversityGain-Bench: does prompting for diversity recover human-level variety without losing accuracy?

**Problem:** Diversity-promoting methods that don't sacrifice accuracy is a named open problem. Cheap inference-time interventions (explicit 'give an unusual answer' prompts, diverse-decoding, persona rotation) are proposed but not benchmarked against accuracy on the same axis.

**Direction:** Build a small benchmark with both a diversity metric and a correctness/quality metric on tasks that have both an open-ended and a checkable component. Compare baseline vs several inference-time diversity interventions across a few open models. Plot the diversity-accuracy frontier and report which interventions buy diversity for free vs at a cost. Release the harness.

**Why it matters:** Gives practitioners an evidence-based, training-free recipe to reduce homogeneity (and thus correlated failure) without hurting quality — directly serving the diversity-without-accuracy-loss open problem at consumer scale. No training required, fully reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Targets the Hivemind extension 'diversity-promoting objectives that don't sacrifice accuracy' (arXiv:2510.22954), restricted to inference-time methods feasible in 30h.
