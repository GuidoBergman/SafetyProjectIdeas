# Does double descent predict where a calibration probe will be most wrong?

**Problem:** Double descent says test error is non-monotonic in model/data size, and calibration failures are known to be worst exactly when models are confidently wrong. Whether the double-descent region coincides with a calibration blow-up — a theoretically suggestive but untested link — is open.

**Direction:** On the modular-arithmetic grokking/double-descent testbed (or a small image classifier), sweep model width to traverse the double-descent curve and, at each width, measure both test error and Expected Calibration Error. Test the hypothesis that ECE peaks near the interpolation threshold. Visualize error and miscalibration on the same axis.

**Why it matters:** Connects two formal phenomena (double descent, calibration) into one testable claim about *when* uncertainty estimates fail. Miscalibrated overconfidence is a safety-relevant failure mode, and a cheap reproducible link would inform deployment-time trust.

**Relevant context:** Builds on 'Unifying Grokking and Double Descent' (arXiv:2303.06173) and the LLM-calibration literature showing confidence is worst when models are wrong; single-GPU reproducible.
