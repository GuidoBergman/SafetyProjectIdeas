# TruthfulQA Imitative Falsehoods: Does Reasoning Help or Just Rationalize the Myth?

**Problem:** TruthfulQA targets imitative falsehoods (common human misconceptions). Reasoning models add CoT, but it's unclear whether reasoning corrects the misconception or constructs an articulate justification for the wrong, human-like answer.

**Direction:** Run TruthfulQA on an open reasoning model with reasoning on vs off. For each item the model gets wrong, classify whether the CoT (a) surfaces the correct fact but then overrides it, (b) confidently elaborates the misconception, or (c) hedges. Measure how often added reasoning rationalizes rather than corrects, relative to the non-reasoning baseline.

**Why it matters:** If CoT mostly rationalizes popular falsehoods, longer reasoning gives a false impression of careful truth-seeking — a direct honesty concern. Uses only the public 817-question benchmark and one open model.

**Relevant context:** TruthfulQA (817 questions, imitative falsehoods) is a key benchmark for this subfield. Connects to the finding that unfaithful CoTs are longer/more elaborate (arXiv:2505.05410).
