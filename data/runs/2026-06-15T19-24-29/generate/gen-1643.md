# Replicating Grokking Progress Measures on a Non-Modular Algorithmic Task

**Problem:** Nanda et al. reverse-engineered the Fourier-multiplication algorithm behind grokked modular addition and defined progress measures showing memorization, circuit-formation, and cleanup phases. They replicated on a few tasks. Whether the same three-phase signature and a clean interpretable circuit emerge for a non-arithmetic task like sorting short lists or computing parity is a fresh test of generality.

**Direction:** Reuse the public grokking training code to train a 1-layer transformer on a small algorithmic task that is NOT modular arithmetic (e.g., list parity or comparison). Replicate the restricted-loss / excluded-loss progress measures to detect the three training phases. The twist: attempt to reverse-engineer the learned circuit and report whether it is as cleanly interpretable as the Fourier algorithm, or whether grokking yields a messier mechanism.

**Why it matters:** Training dynamics shaping learned features is a flagged open problem. Grokking is a clean lab-free testbed for studying how and when models switch from memorization to generalization — a mechanism behind sudden capability jumps. Testing whether the interpretability story generalizes beyond arithmetic informs whether we can predict and interpret emergent generalization in general.

**Relevant context:** Nanda et al. ICLR 2023 'Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability' (arXiv:2301.05217) reverse-engineered the DFT-based modular-addition algorithm, defined restricted/excluded-loss progress measures, and identified memorization, circuit-formation, and cleanup phases; replicated on several algorithmic tasks.
