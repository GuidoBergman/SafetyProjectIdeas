# Fine-Tuning Erodes Safety: Replicated With Parameter-Efficient (LoRA) Tuning on Benign Data

**Problem:** Qi et al. (2023) showed fine-tuning on a few harmful, or even benign, examples removes safety alignment, but their attack used full fine-tuning. The pervasive real-world setting is LoRA/QLoRA on benign task data; whether low-rank benign adaptation also degrades safety, and how degradation scales with rank, is the practical question.

**Direction:** Replicate the 'benign fine-tuning degrades safety' result using LoRA/QLoRA on a small open instruct model with a benign instruction dataset, sweeping LoRA rank. Twist: PEFT instead of full FT + rank as a knob. Measure pre/post refusal rate on a held-out harmful set. Fits a single consumer GPU.

**Why it matters:** Hobbyists and startups fine-tune open models with LoRA constantly; quantifying inadvertent safety erosion under realistic PEFT settings is high counterfactual value and a concrete deployment-gap result. Small model + LoRA fits the compute budget.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 2023 (arXiv:2310.03693): 10-100 harmful examples, or even benign datasets, degrade safety under full fine-tuning of Llama-2/GPT-3.5. LoRA/QLoRA tooling (peft, bitsandbytes) runs on consumer GPUs.
