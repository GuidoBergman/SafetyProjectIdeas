# Hazard-Ratio Comparison of Unlearning Methods Under Relearning Pressure

**Problem:** Multiple unlearning methods exist but are compared on single-point forget-quality metrics, ignoring that what matters for safety is resistance to adversarial relearning over time.

**Direction:** Use Cox proportional-hazards modeling from clinical trials. Treat two open unlearning methods as 'treatment arms', relearning examples as time, and 'knowledge recovered above threshold' as the event. Fit a Cox model with method as a covariate to estimate a hazard ratio: how much faster does forgotten knowledge re-emerge under method A vs B, controlling for relearning intensity.

**Why it matters:** Produces a rigorous, interpretable comparative metric (hazard ratio) for unlearning durability that labs' single-pass evaluations miss — directly serving the dangerous-knowledge-removal open problem with only inference and small relearning fine-tunes.

**Relevant context:** 'Unlearning Isn't Deletion' (arXiv:2505.16831) shows relearning recovers knowledge; 'Time-To-Inconsistency' (arXiv:2510.02712) applies survival/Cox methods to LLM robustness; Cox proportional-hazards is standard for comparing treatment arms in clinical trials.
