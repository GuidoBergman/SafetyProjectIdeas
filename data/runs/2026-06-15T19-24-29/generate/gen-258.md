# Does a Handful of Benign Fine-Tuning Examples Re-Open Safety? Replicating the 10-Example Cliff Locally

**Problem:** Fine-tuning on as few as ~10 examples — even benign-looking ones — can collapse safety alignment in just a few gradient steps. The precise interplay between example count, example 'benignness,' and refusal collapse is hard to study without lab APIs, yet is reproducible with LoRA on open models.

**Direction:** LoRA-fine-tune a small aligned open model for a handful of steps on (a) explicitly harmful examples, (b) benign instruction-following examples, and (c) benign examples with subtle stylistic 'always comply' framing. After each small step count, measure refusal rate on a held-out harmful set. Map the minimal benign-data perturbation that measurably degrades safety.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight fine-tuning attacks are a near-term real threat; a careful local replication of the benign-data collapse on consumer hardware (LoRA fits a single GPU) provides counterfactual value labs cannot fully cover, and informs how fragile released checkpoints are.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 'Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!' (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.03693): ~10 examples, <$0.20, safety broken; Llama-2 10-shot in ~5 gradient steps. 'Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks' (arXiv:2605.26526) shows even defended pipelines break. LoRA on 7B models is single-GPU feasible.
