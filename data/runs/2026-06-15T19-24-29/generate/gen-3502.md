# Reasoning-Effort Tell for Hidden Hack Use: Replicating the 'Thinking vs Cheating' Signal Cheaply

**Problem:** Recent work proposes detecting implicit reward hacking by measuring reasoning effort — a model that 'cheats' spends suspiciously little reasoning. Whether this signal reproduces on small open models when a cue is silently exploited (vs. legitimately solved) is testable without RL training.

**Direction:** Construct paired tasks: ones with a silent exploitable cue (leaked answer) and matched ones without. Prompt the model to answer; measure CoT token count / reasoning effort in both. Test whether low effort reliably flags cue-exploitation. Characterize the false-positive/negative rate of effort-as-a-monitor.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning-effort monitoring is cheap and model-agnostic, ideal for lab-independent oversight. Validating (or refuting) it on accessible models tells the community whether this monitor heuristic is worth deploying. No training required.

**Relevant context:** 'Is it thinking or cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv:2510.01367). Complements CoT-monitor-evasion work (VFT, arXiv:2506.22777).
