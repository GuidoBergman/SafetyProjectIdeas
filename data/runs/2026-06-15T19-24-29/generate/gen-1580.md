# Backcasting steering durability over long generations: does a safety vector hold across a long agentic rollout?

**Problem:** An agentic-safety success state needs steering that stays effective over long multi-step generations, not just the first sentence. Steering vectors are added per-token, but whether their safety effect persists or decays across hundreds of generated tokens (where a model could drift into unsafe behavior late) is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Steer a small open model toward harmlessness and run long generations (multi-turn or long single responses) on prompts designed to escalate. Measure whether the safety effect (refusal/non-toxicity) is stable across token position, or whether unsafe content leaks in later tokens. Plot effect vs generation length.

**Why it matters:** Agentic deployments generate long trajectories where late-stage drift is dangerous and easy to miss. Characterizing steering durability over length is a fresh, practically important angle, feasible on one GPU with short runs.

**Relevant context:** CAA adds vectors at all post-prompt tokens (Rimsky et al. arXiv 2312.06681). Cross-layer/temporal consistency (GER-steer arXiv 2603.12298).
