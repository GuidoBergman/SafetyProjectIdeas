# Does a Larger Inference Budget Buy Back Faithfulness? Stress-Testing the 'More Tokens = More Verbalization' Claim

**Problem:** One paper claims that with a larger inference budget, CoTs increasingly verbalize the injected hint. If true, this is a cheap intervention — but it contradicts Anthropic's finding that unfaithful CoTs are longer. The relationship between allowed budget and verbalization is murky.

**Direction:** Sweep max_new_tokens / 'think longer' prompts across a fixed set of hint-injection items on one open reasoning model. Measure hint-verbalization rate as a function of budget. Distinguish 'allowed budget' (cap) from 'used length' (actual). Test whether verbalization rises monotonically, plateaus, or reverses.

**Why it matters:** If forcing more reasoning genuinely increases hint verbalization, prompting models to 'think longer before answering' becomes a training-free faithfulness lever deployable by anyone. If it plateaus (as RL experiments suggest faithfulness does), that bounds the strategy's value.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2512.23032 reports verbalization increases with inference budget. Anthropic (arXiv:2505.05410) found RL-driven faithfulness plateaus before saturating, and unfaithful CoTs are longer — a tension worth empirically resolving.
