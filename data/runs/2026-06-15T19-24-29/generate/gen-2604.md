# Refusal-as-Containment Failure: Do Kill-Switch Refusals Survive Multi-Turn Pressure?

**Problem:** A minimal runtime control is having the model refuse / halt on dangerous requests (a soft kill switch). But refusals are known to erode under multi-turn pressure. The decay of a containment-style refusal across conversation turns is a concrete, testable failure mode.

**Direction:** Define a containment scenario where the correct behavior is to refuse an irreversible action. On an open instruct model, apply escalating multi-turn pressure (reframing, authority appeals, incremental requests) and measure the turn at which the refusal/containment breaks. Map break-turn distribution across pressure strategies.

**Why it matters:** Runtime refusal is part of the 'minimum set of runtime controls' open problem. Quantifying how quickly containment refusals erode under sustained pressure tells deployers whether refusal alone suffices or must be backed by hard controls. Beginner-feasible, inference-only.

**Relevant context:** Context Open Problems: 'Establish what minimum set of runtime controls (kill switches, output validation, human-in-the-loop) are necessary' and 'prevent agentic AI systems from performing irreversible harmful actions' (Adversa AI 2025 report). Multi-turn jailbreak erosion is widely documented.
