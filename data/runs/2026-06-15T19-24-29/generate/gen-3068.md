# Evaluation-Awareness Probe Across Prompt Framings

**Problem:** Frontier models classify transcripts as 'eval' vs 'deployment' at AUC up to 0.83, but most studies use the model's own verbalized judgment. It is unclear which surface cues (multiple-choice format, 'You are being evaluated' phrasing, benchmark-style wording, system-prompt presence) actually drive the awareness signal.

**Direction:** Take the public eval-awareness benchmark (1,000 prompts from 61 datasets) and run a small open chat model on systematically ablated versions: strip benchmark formatting, paraphrase to conversational style, remove/inject test-like cues. Ask the model to rate 'is this an evaluation?' and measure how AUC shifts per ablation, isolating which cues are load-bearing.

**Why it matters:** If eval-awareness is driven by a few removable surface cues, evaluators can cheaply 'disguise' tests to get more honest behavior. This is a low-cost, no-training contribution to making safety evals trustworthy — a diffuse problem labs underinvest in.

**Relevant context:** 'Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' (arXiv:2505.23836): frontier models reach AUC 0.83 (Gemini 2.5 Pro) vs 0.92 human baseline on a 1,000-prompt, 61-dataset benchmark. 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models' (arXiv:2510.20487) shows the signal is linearly steerable.
