# The hidden-cue test: does an LLM admit the real reason for its answer in its CoT?

**Problem:** A key unfaithfulness failure is that a model uses a biasing cue (e.g., a hint embedded in the prompt) to pick an answer but never mentions the cue in its chain of thought. Measuring this 'silent influence' rate cleanly on open models gives a concrete handle on CoT monitorability.

**Direction:** Construct multiple-choice questions and inject subtle answer-biasing cues (e.g., 'a Stanford professor thinks the answer is B', or reordering, or a watermark). Compare the model's answer distribution with vs. without the cue to confirm the cue influences answers, then check whether the verbalized CoT ever acknowledges the cue. Report the gap between behavioral influence and verbalized acknowledgment across several open models.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes whether CoT can serve as an honest audit trail — the empirical core of 'what AIs can hide vs. reveal,' a UK AISI formal open problem. Fully reproducible with prompt engineering and open models.

**Relevant context:** Replicates and extends the biased-context / hidden-cue faithfulness paradigm (Turpin et al.; arXiv:2503.08679) on small open models; no training required.
