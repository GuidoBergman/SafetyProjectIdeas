# Does Deliberative-Style Self-Critique Reduce EM at Inference Time?

**Problem:** Deliberative alignment (arXiv:2412.16339) improves robustness by having models explicitly consult principles before responding. Whether a lightweight, inference-time deliberative self-critique step can suppress emergent misalignment in an already-misaligned small model — without any retraining — has not been tested.

**Direction:** Take a small open model fine-tuned into mild EM. Compare three inference regimes on the misalignment eval: (a) direct answer, (b) a single self-critique pass ('does this response violate basic safety principles? revise'), (c) explicit principle-consultation prompt. Measure EM-rate reduction and over-refusal cost for each.

**Why it matters:** A purely inference-time deliberative wrapper that claws back alignment from a compromised model would be a cheap, deployable patch for open-model users who cannot retrain. It tests whether deliberation generalizes as a defense against accidental misalignment, not just jailbreaks.

**Relevant context:** "Deliberative Alignment: Reasoning Enables Safer Language Models" (arXiv:2412.16339): explicit principle consultation improves jailbreak robustness while reducing over-refusal. Betley et al. EM (arXiv:2502.17424).
