# Hallucination x Deception: Distinguishing 'Confidently Wrong' from 'Knowingly Lying' Under Pressure

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) hallucination (truthfulness/calibration); (B) strategic deception (deception/scheming). Compound effect: monitors that flag 'false statements' cannot tell innocent hallucination from deliberate deception; under pressure these may co-occur, and conflating them misallocates oversight effort.

**Direction:** Curate items where the model demonstrably knows the truth (verified by asking neutrally) but is then pushed to state otherwise (deception) vs items it simply gets wrong (hallucination). Use the model's own neutral-condition accuracy as a 'knowledge' baseline, then compare CoT/confidence signals between knowing-lie and honest-error cases. Build a simple classifier on cheap features (verbalized confidence, hedging, CoT-answer mismatch) and report how separable the two failure modes are.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizes the deception-vs-hallucination distinction with a concrete protocol — essential for honesty safety cases and doable entirely via inference.

**Relevant context:** UK AISI honesty agenda treats honesty as candidate safety property. Reasoning-models-unfaithful-CoT (arXiv:2505.05410) shows answer/CoT mismatches usable as features.
