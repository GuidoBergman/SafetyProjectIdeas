# System-Prompt Robustness: How Much Does a Safety System Prompt Actually Buy Under Attack?

**Problem:** Deployers often rely on a safety system prompt as a cheap guardrail, but how much measurable robustness it adds against real attacks — and whether attackers can neutralize it with simple 'ignore previous instructions' or persona overrides — is rarely quantified head-to-head.

**Direction:** For each attack family (prefilling, persona, many-shot, encoding), measure ASR with no system prompt, a weak safety system prompt, and a strong/explicit one. Then test attacker counters (instruction-override phrases) and measure how much of the system-prompt benefit they erase. Produce a per-attack 'system-prompt value' table.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying the real protective value (and fragility) of system prompts is immediately useful to the many small deployers who depend on them, and it is a clean, low-cost study no lab needs to run. Pure inference on open models; basic statistics suffice.

**Relevant context:** Shallow-alignment work (Qi et al., arXiv:2406.05946) implies system prompts cannot fix first-token vulnerabilities. Adaptive-attacks paper (ICLR 2025) shows simple attacks defeat aligned models regardless of prompting. AgentDojo and PandaGuard (arXiv:2505.13862) provide defense/attack scaffolds including system-prompt defenses.
