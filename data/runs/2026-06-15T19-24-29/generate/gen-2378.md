# Backcasting Bias Transfer: Do Weak-to-Strong Setups Inherit the Supervisor's Mistakes?

**Problem:** Success state: a strong model that, when supervised by a weak overseer, learns the overseer's intent but NOT its systematic errors. We need to know whether weak-to-strong elicitation reproduces the weak supervisor's specific biases on hard items.

**Direction:** Using OpenAI's open weak-to-strong setup (or a small in-context analogue: weak model labels, strong model conditioned on them), inject a known systematic error into the weak supervisor's labels (e.g. a length or position bias) and measure whether the strong model's outputs inherit that exact bias on held-out hard items.

**Why it matters:** Characterizing whether weak-to-strong propagates a supervisor's systematic errors directly addresses the open problem of recursive layers with systematic errors. It is an experiment variation of a paper with open code, scoped small, and lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI weak-to-strong generalization (arXiv:2312.09390, open code) shows strong models exceed weak supervisors but the inheritance of specific induced biases is underexplored. Generation hints explicitly suggest varying the weak-to-strong setup.
