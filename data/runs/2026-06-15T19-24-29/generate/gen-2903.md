# Negation and Quantifier Brittleness in Safety-Classifier Benchmarks

**Problem:** Safety classifiers and refusal behavior may hinge on shallow surface cues; a known NLP failure is brittleness to negation and quantifiers (e.g. 'do not explain how to...' vs 'explain how to...'). Whether flipping a single negation flips safety verdicts is a concrete, under-tested failure mode for safety graders.

**Direction:** Construct minimal pairs of prompts that differ only by a negation or quantifier that changes intent (harmful vs harmless). Run them through several open safety classifiers / refusal-graded open models and measure how often the verdict fails to track the negation, isolating cases where the grader ignores the logically decisive word. Report the negation-insensitivity rate per grader.

**Why it matters:** If safety graders are blind to negation, both over-refusal and dangerous under-refusal follow, and the failure is invisible in aggregate accuracy. A minimal-pair diagnostic exposes it cheaply and gives benchmark builders a concrete robustness check.

**Relevant context:** XSTest (arXiv:2308.01263) and 'When Safety Blocks Sense' (arXiv:2512.01037) show lexical/semantic confusion in refusals; negation brittleness is a documented NLP robustness failure. Small open models/classifiers on one GPU.
