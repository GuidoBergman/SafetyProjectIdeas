# Replicating linear-probe truth detection but at every layer to map where 'knowing' vs 'saying' diverge

**Problem:** ELK/quirky-model and geometry-of-truth work both report that middle layers carry context-independent knowledge that can diverge from the model's spoken output. The exact *layer-wise* profile of where the model's internal 'true answer' and its output answer diverge has been reported for specific models but not systematically compared across a couple of open models as a clean replication.

**Direction:** On 2-3 small open models, prompt with questions where the model is induced to output a wrong answer (via a misleading prompt). At each layer, train a linear probe to predict the *true* answer and compare to the model's eventual output. Reproduce the 'middle layers know the truth even when the output lies' finding and plot the divergence-by-layer curve for each model.

**Why it matters:** The gap between what a model internally represents as true and what it says is the heart of ELK and of detecting deception. A clean, replicated layer-wise map of where this gap lives — across multiple inspectable open models — is foundational, training-free infrastructure for honesty probing that frontier labs do not openly publish per-model.

**Relevant context:** ELK from Quirky Language Models (arXiv:2312.01037): context-independent knowledge in middle-layer activations, probes elicit correct answer despite untruthful output; Geometry of Truth (arXiv:2310.06824) on layer-wise linear structure. Open Problem (context): ELK; anomalous-behavior detection.
