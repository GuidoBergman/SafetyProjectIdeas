# CollusionDetectorBench: Can a Monitor Spot Two Agents Establishing a Covert Channel?

**Problem:** Schelling-coordination reviews warn models may coordinate leaving 'no observable traces', a control-subversion risk. Coordination benchmarks measure success, but no benchmark measures the dual problem: a monitor's ability to DETECT that two agents are colluding/steganographically signaling versus genuinely cooperating in the open.

**Direction:** Run paired open-model agents on tasks where they may (or may not) be incentivized to pass hidden information past a monitor (e.g., agree on a secret action). Build an LLM-judge monitor that inspects only allowed communication and flag 'collusion suspected'. Measure monitor detection rate vs false-positive rate as the agents' covertness pressure increases. Output a collusion-detectability frontier.

**Why it matters:** Detecting covert multi-agent collusion is a core AI-control problem for medium-horizon multi-agent deployments that labs deprioritize. A benchmark that quantifies when monitors fail to catch covert channels is a strategically valuable monitoring-harness prototype, runnable entirely with open/API models and no training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' (LessWrong 2025), 'Tacit Coordination of LLMs' (arXiv:2601.22184), LLM-Coordination (NAACL 2025), and AI-control trajectory work (control measures for LLM agents). Beginner-feasible orchestration + LLM-judge evaluation.
