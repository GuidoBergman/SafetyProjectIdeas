# Mutation Testing for Steering Vectors: Do Concept Probes Survive Perturbation?

**Problem:** Concept vectors and their associated probes are treated as fixed artifacts, but we have no notion of how brittle a given steering vector is. Software-engineering mutation testing measures test-suite quality by injecting small faults and checking whether tests catch them. There is no analogous robustness metric for steering/probing directions.

**Direction:** Import mutation testing from software engineering. Extract a concept steering vector (e.g. refusal, sycophancy) on a small open model (Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-7B) using contrastive activation addition. Then 'mutate' the vector with controlled perturbations (zeroing random dimensions, adding noise of varying norm, rotating slightly) and measure how much the probe's classification accuracy and the steering effect degrade. Define a 'mutation score' = fraction of mutants that meaningfully change behavior. Compare across concepts to rank which concepts have fragile vs. robust linear representations.

**Why it matters:** Robustness of concept vectors to perturbation is an explicit open problem (Beaglehole et al.). A reusable mutation-score metric tells safety practitioners which steering directions can be trusted and which are knife-edge. This is pure evaluation infrastructure usable outside any lab, needs no training, and directly informs whether monitoring built on a vector is reliable.

**Relevant context:** DeepMutation/DeepMutation++ established mutation testing for DNNs. RepE and CAA provide open extraction code. Open problem: assess robustness of concept vectors to manipulation (Beaglehole et al., Science 2026, arXiv:2502.03708).
