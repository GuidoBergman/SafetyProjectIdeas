# Stronger Consultant, Worse Judge: Reproducing the Persuasion-Accuracy Inversion

**Problem:** Khan et al. found that as a consultant (single advisor) gets stronger, judges agree with it more, lowering judge accuracy — a counterintuitive oversight inversion where capability hurts supervision. The robustness of this inversion across question types and judge strengths is not fully characterized.

**Direction:** Build a consultancy harness (one advocate model arguing for an assigned, sometimes-wrong answer; a weaker judge model). Vary the advocate's capability/persuasiveness (via model size or temperature/style prompting) and measure judge accuracy. Confirm and map the inversion, and test whether switching from consultancy to a two-sided debate restores accuracy as prior work suggests.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes whether more capable systems make oversight worse under common protocols — a central scalable-oversight worry. Replicates and extends a key result with no training and public questions; the debate-vs-consultancy contrast yields an actionable recommendation.

**Relevant context:** Khan et al. (2024, arXiv:2402.06782 / arXiv:2407.04622): judge agreement rises with consultant strength, lowering accuracy; debate (70.1%) beats consultancy (60.0%). This re-maps the inversion across difficulty and judge strength.
