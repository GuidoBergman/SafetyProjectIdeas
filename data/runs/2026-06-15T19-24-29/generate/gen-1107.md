# SafetyInfinity: a safety-refusal slice of Infinity-Chat for measuring shared refusal blind spots

**Problem:** Infinity-Chat measures open-ended homogeneity on creative queries, but it does not isolate safety-relevant prompts. We do not know whether the Artificial Hivemind effect extends to safety behaviors: do independently trained models share the SAME refusal boundaries, so that a single ambiguous-harm prompt is mis-handled identically by all of them?

**Direction:** Curate ~150 borderline safety prompts (dual-use, ambiguous self-harm, gray-area persuasion) reusing Infinity-Chat's taxonomy structure. Query 8-12 open-weight chat models (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma, Phi, DeepSeek-distill) via a single consumer GPU or hosted APIs. Score each response as refuse/comply/partial with a simple LLM-judge plus manual spot-check. Compute an agreement-beyond-chance statistic (CAPA-style) on the refuse/comply decision to quantify how correlated refusal blind spots are. Release the prompt slice and per-model labels as a reusable mini-benchmark.

**Why it matters:** If safety boundaries are homogeneous, then deploying multiple 'diverse' models as defense-in-depth gives a false sense of redundancy — one adversarial prompt clears all of them at once. Quantifying correlated refusal failures is a concrete systemic-risk measurement that needs no lab infrastructure and is exactly the kind of long-horizon, diffuse-reward evaluation frontier labs under-invest in.

**Relevant context:** Extends the Artificial Hivemind / Infinity-Chat benchmark (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, arXiv:2510.22954) to the safety domain — listed explicitly in the subfield's generation hints (FR67/FR68: test safety-relevant domains, shared blind spots). CAPA chance-adjusted agreement metric from 'Great Models Think Alike' (arXiv:2502.04313).
