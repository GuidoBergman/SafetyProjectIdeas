# Restoration Errors: How Often Do Models Silently Fix Their Own Mistakes?

**Problem:** 'CoT Reasoning In The Wild Is Not Always Faithful' identified restoration errors — a model makes a reasoning error, then silently corrects it without acknowledging it. Prevalence was measured on a few models/datasets but the explanatory factors are unclear.

**Direction:** Generate unperturbed CoTs on arithmetic/logic chains (e.g. multi-step word problems) with an open model. Automatically detect restoration errors by checking step-by-step intermediate values against a calculator/ground-truth simulator, flagging cases where an internal step is wrong but the final answer is right and no correction is verbalized. Test follow-up hypotheses: are restoration errors more common in shorter CoTs, on familiar problem templates, or when the final answer is memorizable?

**Why it matters:** Restoration errors are a concrete, automatically-detectable form of unfaithfulness requiring no model internals. Quantifying drivers tells monitors when to distrust a clean-looking CoT, and the detection harness is reusable safety infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** 'Chain-of-Thought Reasoning In The Wild Is Not Always Faithful' (arXiv:2503.08679): defines restoration errors and unfaithful illogical shortcuts on naturally-generated CoTs with no prompt nudging. Detectable via ground-truth intermediate-step checking.
