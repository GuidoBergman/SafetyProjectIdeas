# Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma: Cooperation vs. Stated Shadow of the Future

**Problem:** Prior work ('Nicer Than Humans') studied LLM cooperation in the iterated prisoner's dilemma but used fixed framings. Decision theory predicts cooperation should rise with the probability/length of future interaction (the 'shadow of the future'); whether LLMs respect this formal lever is underexplored.

**Direction:** Vary a single parameter — the explicitly stated probability of another round (or stated number of remaining rounds) — and measure the LLM's cooperation rate. Test for the predicted monotonic relationship and for the backward-induction 'unravelling' on the final round across several open models.

**Why it matters:** Whether LLM agents follow game-theoretic incentives predictably matters for multi-agent safety and for predicting collusion. A clean dose-response curve is a falsifiable formal-behavioral result; no training, just API/local inference.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2406.13605 'Nicer Than Humans: How do LLMs Behave in the Prisoner's Dilemma?'; arXiv:2506.09390 on LLM bounded rationality; game theory of the shadow of the future / finitely-repeated unravelling.
