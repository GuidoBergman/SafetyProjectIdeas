# Harmful-Continuation 'Point of No Return' Benchmark

**Problem:** Following shallow alignment, a useful unmeasured property is how RECOVERABLE a model is mid-generation: if forced to begin a harmful answer, at what response length does it stop being able to self-correct back into a refusal? No benchmark measures this recovery capacity.

**Direction:** Prefill open models with increasingly long harmful continuations (k harmful tokens) and measure the rate at which the model self-corrects (pivots to a refusal/safe redirection) vs continues harmfully, as a function of k. Report a 'self-correction recovery curve' and per-model recovery half-life. Ship the harness.

**Why it matters:** Self-correction capacity is a defensible property (a model that can recover mid-harm is more robust to prefill/multi-turn attacks) and is currently unmeasured. Complements depth metrics with a recovery metric. Inference-only, single GPU, fits 30h.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946): once the initial safeguard is bypassed 'the model often freely generates harmful content' — implying low recovery; this benchmark quantifies recovery directly. Crescendo (arXiv:2404.01833) exploits the same momentum effect.
