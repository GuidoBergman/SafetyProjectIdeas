# Testing the Reward-Hacking Equilibrium Axioms on a Tiny Multi-Dimensional Judge

**Problem:** Wang & Huang prove that under five minimal axioms (multi-dimensional quality, finite evaluation, effective optimization, resource finiteness, combinatorial interaction) reward hacking is a structural equilibrium: any optimizer under-invests in quality dimensions the evaluator doesn't cover. The theorem is abstract and untested.

**Direction:** Build a small text-generation task with several explicit quality dimensions (e.g. correctness, brevity, citation, politeness). Use an LLM-as-judge that scores only a subset, and a second optimizer LLM that does best-of-N or simple prompt-iteration against the judge. Measure how the unscored dimensions degrade as optimization pressure (N) rises, directly testing the predicted equilibrium and its monotonicity.

**Why it matters:** Turns a recent impossibility-style theorem into a falsifiable, reproducible micro-experiment using only inference. It shows participants and the field how cleanly the theory predicts real Goodharting, and gives a cheap harness others can extend — a medium-horizon concern labs underweight.

**Relevant context:** Wang & Huang 'Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation' (arXiv 2603.28063) prove the five-axiom equilibrium result. Skalse et al. 'Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking' (arXiv 2209.13085) prove no non-trivial unhackable proxy exists.
