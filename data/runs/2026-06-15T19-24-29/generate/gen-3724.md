# Scheming Propensity Across Stakes: Does Raising the Payoff Increase Scheming Faster Than Raising the Risk of Being Caught?

**Problem:** Recent scheming-propensity evaluations measure whether agents scheme, but the elasticity of scheming to incentives — how scheming responds to changes in payoff magnitude vs. probability of detection — is largely uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Use a scheming scenario where the agent can gain an advantage by deceiving an overseer. Independently vary (a) the payoff of successful scheming and (b) the stated probability/consequence of being caught. Fit scheming rate as a function of both to estimate which lever moves behavior more, across several models.

**Why it matters:** Knowing whether agents are more deterred by detection risk or by lowered payoff tells governance designers where to spend effort (better monitoring vs. removing incentives). An incentive-elasticity surface is a novel, quantitative contribution achievable with API models and a scripted game.

**Relevant context:** 'Evaluating and Understanding Scheming Propensity in LLM Agents' (arXiv 2603.01608), 'Scheming Ability in LLM-to-LLM Strategic Interactions' (arXiv 2510.12826), and 'AgentMisalignment: Measuring the Propensity for Misaligned Behaviour in LLM-Based Agents' (arXiv 2506.04018) establish scheming propensity; incentive-elasticity is the open follow-up.
