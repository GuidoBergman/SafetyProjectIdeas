# Steganographic Capacity Ceiling: How Much Hidden Info Can Open Models Pass Past a Monitor?

**Problem:** Success state: CoT monitoring is trustworthy because models cannot smuggle meaningful hidden reasoning past a monitor. Backcasting, we need an empirical bound on the encoded-information capacity of current open models when instructed to hide reasoning from an LLM monitor.

**Direction:** Give an open model a task plus a secret payload to encode steganographically in its CoT; have a separate LLM monitor try to detect/decode it. Sweep payload size and encoding scheme; measure the largest payload that evades the monitor while remaining usable by a decoder model. Report a capacity curve. Small models, single GPU.

**Why it matters:** If the steganographic channel is tiny today, CoT monitoring buys real safety; if it's large, monitorability safety cases are fragile. This quantifies a key assumption behind CoT-based oversight and needs only open models and prompting.

**Relevant context:** 'A Decision-Theoretic Formalisation of Steganography With Applications to LLM Monitoring' (arXiv 2602.23163); 'Unsupervised decoding of encoded reasoning' (2512.01222) finds current LLMs can use simple encoding when instructed but degrade with complexity, suggesting a measurable ceiling.
