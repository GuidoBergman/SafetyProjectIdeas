# Is the Self-Preference / Perplexity Link Causal? A Paraphrase Intervention

**Problem:** Wang et al. (2024) argued self-preference bias arises because judges prefer low-perplexity (familiar) text. The correlation is established; the causal claim that lowering an answer's perplexity-to-the-judge raises its score is less directly tested.

**Direction:** Take fixed-meaning answers and produce paraphrase variants spanning a range of perplexity under a target open judge model (measure perplexity directly). Hold correctness constant. Regress the judge's preference/score on measured perplexity across paraphrases. A monotone effect at equal meaning isolates the causal perplexity->preference channel.

**Why it matters:** If perplexity causally drives judge preference, an AI can game oversight purely by stylistic familiarity, with no change in substance — a content-free attack vector. Confirming causality motivates perplexity-normalized oversight. Inference-only, open models, public text.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al. 2024 (arXiv:2410.21819): self-preference bias tied to perplexity/familiarity, established as correlation; mitigation follow-up arXiv:2604.22891.
