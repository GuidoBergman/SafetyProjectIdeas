# Measuring the Model-Card Transparency Gap with a Public Scoring Pipeline

**Problem:** AI documentation is fragmented and inconsistent (one study found 947 unique section names; 'usage' alone appeared under 97 labels), but most labs and policymakers lack a simple, reproducible score for how much safety-critical information a given model card actually discloses.

**Direction:** Reimplement a stripped-down version of the AI Transparency Atlas weighted rubric (safety evaluation, critical risk, intended use, limitations) and run it over 20-40 publicly available model/system cards from HuggingFace and frontier labs. Use an LLM to detect presence/quality of each section against the rubric and compute compliance scores, reporting where open vs closed providers diverge.

**Why it matters:** Cheap, auditable transparency scoring lets regulators operationalize EU AI Act Annex IV and California SB 53 disclosure expectations; it needs only public cards and no model access, maximizing counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** AI Transparency Atlas arXiv:2512.12443 (8 sections/23 subsections, EU AI Act Annex IV baseline); Stanford FMTI; California SB 53 / EU GPAI Code of Practice transparency section.
