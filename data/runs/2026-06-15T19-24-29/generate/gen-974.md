# Replicating Frontier Safety Framework Scoring with a Cheaper LLM-as-Judge Rubric

**Problem:** The 'Evaluating AI Providers' Frontier Safety Frameworks' study scored 12+ company frameworks (8%-34%) using expert raters and a detailed rubric, but expert scoring does not scale to the frequent re-scoring needed as frameworks are updated, and the inter-rater reliability of the rubric is unestablished.

**Direction:** Operationalize the published scoring rubric as an LLM-as-judge pipeline, run it on the same public frameworks, and measure agreement with the paper's human scores. Stress-test reliability by varying prompt phrasing and model, and report which rubric criteria are robust vs. ambiguous to automated scoring.

**Why it matters:** Cheap, reproducible framework scoring is strategically valuable governance infrastructure that any external watchdog can run independently of labs — addressing the voluntary-vs-enforceable-standards gap. Pure text, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Evaluating AI Providers' Frontier Safety Frameworks' (arXiv:2512.01166), which reports human-rated scores from 8% to 34% across providers but does not test automated replication or rubric inter-rater reliability.
