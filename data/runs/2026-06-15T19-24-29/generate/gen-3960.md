# Prover-Estimator Debate vs Plain Debate: A Minimal Head-to-Head on Verifiable Tasks

**Problem:** Prover-estimator debate (Brown-Cohen et al. 2025) is proposed to avoid obfuscation by having an estimator assign probabilities to sub-claims rather than recursing. Whether this empirically beats plain debate at resisting a dishonest prover on small tasks is untested by newcomers.

**Direction:** On tasks with checkable structure (multi-step arithmetic, simple logical derivations), implement (a) plain two-debater + judge and (b) a prover-estimator variant where an LLM estimator assigns probabilities to decomposed sub-claims. Pit an honest vs dishonest prover in both protocols and measure judge accuracy and the dishonest prover's win-rate as task depth grows.

**Why it matters:** Provides early empirical evidence on whether a specific theoretical fix to debate's obfuscation problem helps in practice — high-leverage for scalable oversight design and entirely inference-based.

**Relevant context:** 'Avoiding Obfuscation with Prover-Estimator Debate' (arXiv 2506.13609); 'Scalable AI Safety via Doubly-Efficient Debate' (arXiv 2311.14125).
