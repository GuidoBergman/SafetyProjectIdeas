# Replicating XSTest Over-Refusal Measurement on Heavily-Guardrailed Open Models to Quantify Safety-Tax

**Problem:** XSTest measures over-refusal: models wrongly refusing benign requests that merely resemble unsafe ones. Governance debate frames a tradeoff between safety and usefulness, but the over-refusal 'safety tax' of newer, more heavily guardrailed open releases (and 'safety-tuned' community fine-tunes) versus their base instruct versions is rarely quantified head-to-head.

**Direction:** Replicate the XSTest over-refusal protocol with the twist of pairing each model with a more-guardrailed counterpart (e.g., a base instruct model vs. a community 'safety-aligned' fine-tune, or two successive versions of an open release). Report over-refusal rate deltas and characterize which benign categories trigger the most spurious refusals.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal is the usability cost of safety mandates; quantifying it informs proportionate governance and standards that don't incentivize degraded helpfulness. Open benchmark, open models, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in XSTest (part of HELM Safety) for over-refusal measurement. Twist = paired guardrail-intensity comparison on open models to estimate a 'safety tax'.
