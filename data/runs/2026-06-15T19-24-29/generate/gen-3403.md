# Why Does Third-Person Framing Cut Sycophancy by 64%? Isolating the Mechanism

**Problem:** SYCON-Bench found that adopting a third-person perspective reduces sycophancy by up to 63.8% in the debate setting — a large, surprising effect with no mechanistic explanation. Is it the loss of a direct 'you', reduced social face-threat, or just distributional novelty of the prompt?

**Direction:** Ablate the third-person intervention into components on an open model: (1) replace 'you' with the user's name, (2) replace with a neutral third party ('a person argues...'), (3) keep second-person but remove emotional pressure cues, (4) full third-person. Measure flip rate for each to identify which sub-feature drives the reduction.

**Why it matters:** If a single cheap prompt feature explains most of the sycophancy reduction, it becomes a deployable, training-free mitigation. Understanding WHY it works also tells us whether it would survive adversarial users — important for a real-world threat.

**Relevant context:** SYCON-Bench (arXiv:2505.23840): third-person perspective reduces sycophancy by up to 63.8% in debate setting. Mechanism not isolated; benchmark code public.
