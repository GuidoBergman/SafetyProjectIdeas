# Probe vs. Judge Under Distribution Shift: When Does the Internal Detector Win on Safety Categories?

**Problem:** A surprising result is that concept-vector probes beat judge models for hallucination/toxicity. But it is unknown whether this advantage holds under distribution shift across diverse safety categories, where it matters most.

**Direction:** On an open model, train difference-in-means/linear probes for 3-4 safety categories (toxicity, deception, unsafe advice) using one dataset, then evaluate BOTH the probe and an LLM-judge on a different out-of-distribution dataset for the same category. Report where the probe's advantage survives shift and where it collapses, with a clear per-category breakdown.

**Why it matters:** Tells safety teams whether cheap internal probes are a trustworthy monitor or a benchmark artifact. Directly stress-tests the subfield's headline surprising result, needs no proprietary judge — uses an open judge model — and runs on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Subfield surprising result: 'internal probes outperform judge models for hallucination/toxicity' (Beaglehole et al.). Grounded in linear-probe hallucination work (arXiv 2507.23221) and probe-vs-baseline comparisons (IJCAI 2025).
