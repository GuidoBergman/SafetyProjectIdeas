# Refusal Direction Stability Across Quantization Levels

**Problem:** Arditi et al. showed refusal in LLMs is mediated by a single difference-of-means direction. Most follow-up work uses full-precision weights, but deployed open models are frequently run quantized (8-bit, 4-bit) on consumer hardware. It is unknown whether the refusal direction extracted at full precision still mediates refusal after quantization, or whether quantization shifts/blurs the direction.

**Direction:** Take an open chat model (e.g. Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen) loadable on one consumer GPU. Extract the refusal direction via difference-of-means over harmful vs harmless prompt activations at full precision (the standard recipe). Re-extract the same direction from the 8-bit and 4-bit quantized versions. Measure cosine similarity between directions across precisions, and test whether the full-precision direction still suppresses/induces refusal when added to the quantized model (and vice versa). Report a precision-vs-direction-drift curve.

**Why it matters:** Quantized deployment is the dominant way safety-relevant open models actually run outside labs. If refusal directions drift under quantization, safety interventions validated at full precision may silently fail in deployment — a cheap-to-check but underexamined failure mode that requires no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. (NeurIPS 2024) 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' — difference-of-means recipe over contrastive harmful/harmless prompt pairs. Follow-up work notes refusal directions are architecture-dependent and do not always transfer. No prior work isolates the quantization variable.
