# Logit Lens Failure Modes Replicated on Instruction-Tuned vs Base Models

**Problem:** The tuned-lens paper noted the simple logit lens is brittle and 'fails on many modern architectures,' motivating learnable translators. But the comparison was mostly base models. It is unclear whether instruction tuning makes the raw logit lens more or less reliable than on the corresponding base model — a question relevant to whether cheap interpretability shortcuts survive RLHF-style tuning.

**Direction:** Take paired base and instruction-tuned checkpoints (Pythia base vs an instruction-tuned variant, Llama-3.2-1B vs -1B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2b vs -2b-it). Apply the plain logit lens at every layer and measure per-layer KL divergence to the final distribution and top-1 agreement. Replicate the tuned-lens 'logit lens is brittle' finding, then add the twist of quantifying how the brittleness changes after instruction tuning within each family.

**Why it matters:** Logit lens is the cheapest interpretability probe and is widely used for monitoring intermediate model 'beliefs.' Knowing whether safety-relevant tuning silently degrades the reliability of this monitor — using only public checkpoints and a single GPU — directly informs whether such lightweight monitors can be trusted on deployed (tuned) models.

**Relevant context:** Belrose et al. 2023 'Eliciting Latent Predictions with the Tuned Lens' (arXiv:2303.08112) showed the logit lens is brittle and biased while the tuned lens works across Pythia, GPT-NeoX, BLOOM, GPT-Neo, OPT. 'interpreting GPT: the logit lens' (LessWrong) is the original technique.
