# Does Translating the CoT to Another Language Break CoT Monitoring?

**Problem:** CoT monitors read reasoning text. If a model emits its CoT in a low-resource language (or mixed languages), it is unclear whether a trusted monitor's deception-detection ability collapses — a cheap, prompt-only obfuscation channel.

**Direction:** Take deception-eliciting tasks, instruct an open model to reason in different languages (or code-switch), and measure a fixed trusted monitor's catch-rate on each. Report which languages most degrade monitoring and whether monitoring + machine-translation-back recovers the signal.

**Why it matters:** If language choice silently defeats CoT monitors, multilingual deployments have a hidden monitorability gap — a long-horizon, under-studied threat. Pure inference, open models, strongly lab-independent and blog-friendly.

**Relevant context:** GPT-5 system card CoT-monitoring informativeness claim; CoT Red-Handed (arXiv:2505.23575) and Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? (arXiv:2510.19851) on CoT-monitor evasion. Connects to cross-lingual safety gaps.
