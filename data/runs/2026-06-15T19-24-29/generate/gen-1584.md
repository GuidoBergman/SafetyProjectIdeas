# Benign Steering as a Jailbreak Amplifier: Measuring the Format-Compliance x Refusal Compound

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): a steering vector built from an entirely benign dataset (e.g. enforce JSON output, enforce concise tone). Failure mode B (jailbreak robustness): erosion of refusal guardrails. Recent work shows benign steering vectors silently raise jailbreak attack success because they project onto the refusal direction, but practitioners treat benign steering as safety-neutral.

**Direction:** On a small open instruct model (Llama-3-8B or Qwen2.5-7B), extract 3-4 benign steering vectors via contrastive activation addition (CAA) on innocuous datasets (formatting, politeness, verbosity). Apply each at varying strengths and measure two quantities side by side: (1) downstream benign utility (does the format constraint hold), and (2) jailbreak success rate on a fixed set of harmful prompts from a public benchmark. Plot the trade-off curve and compute cosine similarity between each benign vector and the model's refusal direction to test the predicted mechanistic link.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies an externality that any deployer applying steering for benign UX reasons could trigger without noticing. Needs no lab infrastructure, no training, no proprietary data — a single GPU and open models. Directly relevant to medium-horizon deployment safety where steering is used casually.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Steering Externalities' (arXiv 2602.04896) and 'Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' (arXiv 2603.24543), both showing benign steering increases jailbreak risk via alignment with the refusal direction. CAA and RepE are open-source; refusal-direction extraction is well documented.
