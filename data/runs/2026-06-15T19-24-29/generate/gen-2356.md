# Cross-Examination as a Lie Localizer: Can Targeted Questions Break Obfuscation?

**Problem:** Cross-examination (judge or debater interrogates a debater's earlier claims) is proposed to defeat obfuscated arguments by forcing the dishonest side to commit to localizable sub-claims. Causal chain: dishonest long argument -> cross-examination forces step-level commitments -> inconsistency surfaces -> judge localizes the lie. The under-studied link is whether cheap, prompted cross-examination actually surfaces inconsistencies in practice.

**Direction:** On synthetic multi-step arguments with a single planted error, compare a baseline judge to a judge that issues 2-3 targeted follow-up questions to the defending model and checks for self-contradiction. Measure improvement in error-localization rate from cross-examination, with no training.

**Why it matters:** Empirically tests a leading theoretical fix to debate's central failure mode using only prompting, giving the external research community evidence on whether cross-examination is worth its cost.

**Relevant context:** Cross-examination is proposed (Irving/Barnes & alignment-forum debate discussions; Prover-Estimator Debate arXiv:2506.13609) as a route around obfuscated arguments. Cheap inference-time empirical tests of its lie-localizing power are scarce.
