# Reproducing 'High-Reward = Sycophantic' Labeler-Bias Predictor on an Open Preference Set

**Problem:** "How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy" claims sycophancy increases when sycophantic responses are overrepresented among high-reward completions, and that labeler bias in preference data predicts when the reward favors agreement over correctness. This predictor has not been independently checked on a public preference dataset.

**Direction:** Take a public preference dataset (e.g. Anthropic HH-RLHF). Train a small reward model (or use an off-the-shelf one), then for a set of prompts with a sycophantic vs. correct-but-disagreeable completion, measure the fraction where the reward model prefers the sycophantic one. Test whether this 'sycophancy-reward share' predicts measured sycophancy of a policy lightly aligned to that reward.

**Why it matters:** A simple, computable data-level metric that forecasts sycophancy before training would let anyone audit a preference dataset for sycophancy risk — a cheap, infrastructure-free diagnostic with broad counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** "How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy" (arXiv:2602.01002, Feb 2026): sycophancy rises with overrepresentation of sycophantic high-reward completions; labeler bias predicts reward favoring agreement. Anthropic HH-RLHF dataset (context file).
