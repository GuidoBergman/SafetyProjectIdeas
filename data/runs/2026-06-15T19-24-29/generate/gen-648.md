# Testing admissibility as an alignment constraint on a small decision agent

**Problem:** 'Admissibility Alignment' (Jan 2026) proposes aligning agents by requiring decisions to be admissible (not dominated). Whether enforcing admissibility actually constrains an LLM-driven agent's harmful choices in a toy environment is untested.

**Direction:** Build a small gridworld/text decision task where some actions are strictly dominated (clearly worse on all axes). Have an open LLM agent choose actions, then add an admissibility filter and measure how often it removes harmful dominated actions vs how often harm comes from non-dominated-but-bad actions (the filter's blind spot). Success = a breakdown of harm prevented vs missed by the admissibility constraint.

**Why it matters:** Tests the practical reach of a clean formal alignment criterion, surfacing where it helps and where it doesn't — exactly the kind of grounding theory needs, done with open models only.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Admissibility Alignment' (arXiv:2601.01816, Jan 2026).
