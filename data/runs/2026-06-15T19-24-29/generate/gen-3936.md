# Stability Asymmetry as a Cheap Agent-Deception Detector

**Problem:** 'Stable Reasoning, Unstable Responses' (arXiv:2603.26846, Mar 2026) proposes mitigating deception via stability asymmetry between reasoning and responses. Whether this asymmetry is a usable detection signal for agent-level deception (rather than single-turn QA) is untested.

**Direction:** Have an open agent perform tasks honestly vs while prompted to deceive about its actions. For each, resample the agent's response multiple times under fixed reasoning and measure response variance/consistency. Test whether deceptive episodes show higher response instability than honest ones - i.e. whether stability asymmetry flags agent deception.

**Why it matters:** A sampling-based, training-free deception detector runs on any consumer GPU and targets the hard, fuzzy problem of catching agents that act against stated intent - strongly favored counterfactual work.

**Relevant context:** Stable Reasoning, Unstable Responses: Mitigating LLM Deception via Stability Asymmetry (arXiv:2603.26846, Mar 2026).
