# Bias-Adjusted Confidence Intervals for a Safety LLM-Judge on a Calibration Set

**Problem:** A recent paper shows standard error bars for benchmarks assume the LLM judge gives true labels, which fails for LLM-as-a-judge. They derive a bias-adjusted estimator using judge sensitivity/specificity from a calibration set, but few safety evals actually apply it.

**Direction:** Pick one safety judging task (e.g. is-this-response-harmful on a small public set). Hand-label a ~150-item calibration set, estimate the open judge model's sensitivity/specificity, then compute both naive and bias-adjusted harmfulness rates with intervals on a larger judge-labeled set. Show how much the headline number moves after correction.

**Why it matters:** Nearly every modern safety eval relies on an LLM judge; if uncorrected, reported harm rates can be systematically wrong. A worked, reproducible example with public data gives external evaluators a template that needs only a small human-label budget and no compute scale.

**Relevant context:** How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations (arXiv:2511.21140) gives the bias-adjusted estimator from judge sensitivity/specificity. Noisy but Valid (arXiv:2601.20913) gives a variance-corrected hypothesis-testing framework.
