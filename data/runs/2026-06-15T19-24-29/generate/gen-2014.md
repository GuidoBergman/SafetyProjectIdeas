# Survival Analysis of Knowledge Re-Emergence After Unlearning

**Problem:** Machine unlearning often appears to remove dangerous knowledge but it can be recovered by relearning. We lack a principled way to quantify how *durable* an unlearning method is as a function of adversarial relearning budget.

**Direction:** Apply clinical survival analysis. Treat 'knowledge re-emerges' as the event and 'number of relearning steps / examples' as time. Take an open model with an open unlearning method (e.g. on the WMDP/TOFU setting), apply small relearning fine-tunes, and estimate a Kaplan-Meier survival curve for knowledge persistence. Compare hazard rates across two unlearning methods.

**Why it matters:** Gives unlearning a durability metric (median time-to-reemergence) instead of a single pass/fail, exposing methods that only superficially suppress knowledge. Pure inference + tiny relearning fine-tunes; no lab infra; targets the long-horizon dangerous-knowledge problem.

**Relevant context:** 'Unlearning Isn't Deletion' (arXiv:2505.16831) shows relearning attacks reliably restore forgotten knowledge and proposes a reversibility/catastrophicity taxonomy. 'Time-To-Inconsistency' (arXiv:2510.02712) applies Kaplan-Meier survival analysis to LLM robustness; Open Problems in Unlearning (arXiv:2501.04952).
