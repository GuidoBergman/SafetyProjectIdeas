# Multi-concept steering interference: do safety vectors collide?

**Problem:** 2026 work shows multiple concept vectors can be composed (added) for joint steering, and outcome-relevant representations occupy a low-rank but not rank-one subspace. Whether composing TWO safety vectors (e.g. anti-toxicity + anti-deception) interferes destructively is unstudied and matters for stacking defenses.

**Direction:** Build CAA vectors for two distinct safety concepts on one open model. Apply them individually and jointly (summed, and orthogonalized). Measure whether joint application achieves both effects or one cancels the other. Quantify interference and test whether orthogonalizing the vectors first reduces collision.

**Why it matters:** Real safety steering needs many concepts at once. If naive composition causes one safety property to undo another, defenders need to know — and orthogonalization may be a cheap fix. Tests a core open problem (combining concept steering) cheaply.

**Relevant context:** Extends the multi-concept composition findings around Contrastive Conceptor Activation Steering / low-rank subspace work (arXiv:2605.17144, ~May 2026) and Beaglehole et al. multi-concept combination (arXiv:2502.03708).
