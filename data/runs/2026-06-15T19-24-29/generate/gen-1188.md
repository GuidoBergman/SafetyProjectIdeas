# Does Distillation Inherit Blind Spots? Comparing Teacher and Student Failure Overlap

**Problem:** The causal chain 'one popular base model -> many distilled students -> shared vulnerabilities' is a major homogeneity driver. The under-studied link is whether a distilled student inherits the SAME safety blind spots as its teacher (not just accuracy). If so, the apparent diversity of the model ecosystem is illusory.

**Direction:** Pick an open teacher model and 2-3 of its publicly-distilled students (e.g. distilled variants on HuggingFace). Run a shared safety probe set (over-refusal, harmful-advice, factuality traps). Measure failure overlap between teacher and each student vs. overlap between unrelated model families. Test whether teacher->student overlap is significantly higher.

**Why it matters:** If distillation copies blind spots, then a market full of distilled derivatives is a monoculture wearing different names. This quantifies hidden concentration risk and is fully reproducible on a single GPU with open checkpoints.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Jailbreak Transferability Emerges from Shared Representations' (arXiv 2506.12913) and 'Quantification of LLM Distillation' (arXiv 2501.12619), which find distilled models inherit teacher vulnerabilities. Connects to Artificial Hivemind homogeneity.
