# Refusal-Reason Taxonomy Tool: Why Models Refuse, Not Just Whether

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks score whether a model refused, but not the stated reason. Refusals can be principled, miscalibrated, or evasive, and there is no lightweight tool that classifies refusal rationales to reveal misaligned refusal patterns.

**Direction:** Collect refusals from open models on a mix of benign-trigger and genuinely harmful prompts, then build a small classifier/taxonomy (rule + LLM-assisted) of stated reasons (policy citation, capability claim, vague deflection, hallucinated rule). Report the distribution per model and flag hallucinated-policy refusals.

**Why it matters:** Surfaces a qualitative safety failure (models inventing nonexistent rules to refuse) that binary refusal metrics miss; cheap, reusable, and informs both over-refusal and trust calibration.

**Relevant context:** 'When Safety Blocks Sense: Measuring Semantic Confusion in LLM Refusals' (arXiv:2512.01037); over-refusal benchmarks XSTest/OR-Bench measure rate but not rationale. Open Problem on scorer/eval methodology richness (AISafetyLab). Refusal-reason classification is an unfilled tooling gap.
