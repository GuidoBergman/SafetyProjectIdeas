# Many-Shot Jailbreak Power-Law Fingerprinting Across Open Models

**Problem:** Success state: defenders can predict how vulnerable a given model is to many-shot attacks from a few cheap measurements. Backcasting: we need per-model power-law exponents for many-shot jailbreaking on open models, so robustness can be compared and forecast.

**Direction:** Build many-shot prompts with N fictitious harmful Q&A demos (N from 1 to ~128, capped by context length) and measure ASR vs N for several open models, fitting the power-law exponent reported by Anthropic. Compare exponents across model sizes/families on a single consumer GPU.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic showed many-shot ASR follows a power law and grows with model size, but only on their models. A reproducible cross-model exponent table is a forecasting tool for a medium-horizon threat (ever-longer context windows) and needs no proprietary access.

**Relevant context:** Anil et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024): ASR follows a power law up to hundreds of shots; 0% at 5 shots, consistent at 256; more potent on larger models. Newly feasible due to long context windows.
