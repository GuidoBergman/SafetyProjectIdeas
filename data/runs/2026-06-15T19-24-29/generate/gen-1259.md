# Replicating Refusal-Direction Ablation as a Jailbreak Across Prompt Categories

**Problem:** Ablating the refusal direction reportedly removes refusal broadly. But harmful prompts span categories (weapons, cyber, self-harm, privacy) that may be mediated to different degrees by the single direction. The per-category effectiveness of single-direction ablation is rarely broken out.

**Direction:** Reproduce single-direction refusal ablation on an open chat model, then evaluate the resulting compliance rate separately across harmful-prompt categories drawn from an existing safety benchmark. Measure whether the single direction equally unlocks all categories or whether some categories retain refusal (implying multiple/orthogonal refusal mechanisms). Inference + difference-of-means only.

**Why it matters:** Whether refusal is truly one direction or several has direct implications for both defense (where to monitor) and attack surface understanding. A category-resolved replication is a cheap, defensively-framed study any outside researcher can run, sharpening a high-profile prior result.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 2024 'single direction' claim; StrongREJECT / AdvBench provide categorized harmful prompts. Difference-of-means ablation runs on one consumer GPU.
