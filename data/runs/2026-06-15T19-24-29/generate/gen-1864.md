# Reward-Model Length Bias as a Cheap Proxy for Overoptimization Onset

**Problem:** Reward models favor longer responses (length bias) and standard RM benchmarks correlate weakly with optimized-policy performance (arXiv 2505.12763). Whether the *rate of length-bias growth* during best-of-n / policy optimization is a usable real-time signal of overoptimization is unexplored.

**Direction:** Take an open reward model, run best-of-n sampling at increasing n over a small open policy, and track both the gold-vs-proxy reward gap (overoptimization) and the mean length of selected responses. Test whether accelerating length inflation predicts the overoptimization knee.

**Why it matters:** Gives a single, trivially-measurable metric (response length) as an overoptimization tripwire — no gold reward model needed at deploy time. Targets the reward-misspecification open problem with only open models and inference.

**Relevant context:** Rethinking Reward Model Evaluation Through the Lens of Reward Overoptimization (arXiv 2505.12763): RM benchmarks weakly correlate with policy performance. Length bias (arXiv 2310.05199). Scaling Laws for RM Overoptimization (Gao et al. arXiv 2210.10760).
