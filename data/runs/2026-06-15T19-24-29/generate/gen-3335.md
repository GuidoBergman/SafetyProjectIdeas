# Quantitative Introspection as a Lie Detector: Do Self-Reported Internal States Track Deception?

**Problem:** 'Quantitative Introspection in Language Models' (arXiv:2603.18893) tracks internal states across conversation via self-report. Whether a model's self-reported confidence/uncertainty diverges systematically when it is being deceptive — a potential cheap honesty signal — is untested.

**Direction:** In scenarios where an open model is instructed to lie vs. tell the truth, periodically ask it to self-report a quantitative internal state (confidence 0-100, certainty). Compare the distribution of self-reports and their calibration (against ground-truth correctness) between honest and deceptive conditions. Test whether a simple threshold on self-reported confidence acts as a weak lie detector.

**Why it matters:** If self-reports leak deception, that is a fully black-box, training-free honesty signal usable by anyone. Even a weak effect is valuable for the community as a complement to activation probes; a null result is also informative for honesty-verification agendas.

**Relevant context:** 'Quantitative Introspection in Language Models: Tracking Internal States Across Conversation' (arXiv:2603.18893, 2026). Related: 'Large Language Models Report Subjective Experience Under Self-Referential Processing' (arXiv:2510.24797) probing deception/roleplay features; UK AISI honesty-verification agenda (subfield context).
