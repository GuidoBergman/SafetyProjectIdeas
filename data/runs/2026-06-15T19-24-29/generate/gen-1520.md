# Does steering toward 'honesty' actually increase calibration, or just verbosity of hedging?

**Problem:** Honesty-steering chain: add honesty vector -> model expresses uncertainty more -> users get better-calibrated answers. But the middle link could fail: the vector might just make the model HEDGE more (say 'I'm not sure' everywhere) without improving the match between stated confidence and actual correctness. Hedging is not calibration. This link is rarely measured with proper calibration metrics.

**Direction:** On an open model, build an honesty/uncertainty steering vector. On a QA set with known ground truth, measure calibration (e.g., ECE, or correlation between expressed confidence and correctness) at baseline and under steering. Check whether steering improves calibration or merely shifts all confidences down uniformly. Success criterion: calibration curves before/after steering, distinguishing real calibration gain from blanket hedging.

**Why it matters:** Trustworthy uncertainty is core to safe deployment; a steering method that fakes humility without calibration is actively misleading. Proper calibration evaluation is cheap, neglected, and needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. steering toward concepts; RepE honesty directions. Calibration (ECE) framing distinguishes genuine vs cosmetic effects — the standard confound this targets.
