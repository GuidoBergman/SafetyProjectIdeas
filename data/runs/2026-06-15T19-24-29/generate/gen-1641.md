# Replicating Truth-Probe Generalization Failures on a New Topic Domain

**Problem:** Truth probes trained on factual statements often fail to generalize, and recent work shows truthfulness representations rely on 'superficial resemblance.' Most studies use general-knowledge or city/geography datasets. Whether the brittleness replicates — and how it changes — when probes are trained on one knowledge domain (e.g., biology facts) and tested on a structurally different domain (e.g., legal/historical facts) is underexplored at small scale.

**Direction:** Replicate the Geometry-of-Truth probing setup on Gemma-2-2b/Llama-3.2-3B: train a linear truth probe on true/false statements from one domain and evaluate cross-domain transfer. The twist: systematically vary surface form (negation, paraphrase, language) within and across domains to test the 'superficial resemblance' hypothesis, reporting where the probe's accuracy collapses.

**Why it matters:** Lie/deception detection via internal truth directions is a leading lab-free safety tool, but its reliability hinges on generalization. Mapping exactly which distribution shifts break a truth probe — with public datasets and a single GPU — directly tells safety practitioners how far they can trust these monitors, a >1-year-horizon concern frontier labs underinvest in.

**Relevant context:** Marks & Tegmark 'The Geometry of Truth' established linear truth probes; 'How well do truth probes generalise?' (LessWrong) documented transfer failures; 'LLM Knowledge is Brittle: Truthfulness Representations Rely on Superficial Resemblance' (arXiv:2510.11905) and 'Probing the Geometry of Truth' (arXiv:2506.00823) show brittleness under logical transformations.
