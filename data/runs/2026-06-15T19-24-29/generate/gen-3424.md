# Self-Consistency vs. Stated Confidence: When Models Sound Sure but Sample Disagrees

**Problem:** Verbalized confidence is often overconfident. A model's true uncertainty might be better captured by self-consistency (how much its answers vary across resamples). Whether stated confidence aligns with sampling-based confidence — and when they diverge — is an honesty-relevant gap.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA/TruthfulQA, for each item collect (a) a single verbalized confidence and (b) an empirical agreement rate across N resamples on an open model. Measure the gap between stated and sampled confidence and characterize where overconfidence is worst (e.g. popular-misconception items). Test whether sampled confidence is better calibrated than stated.

**Why it matters:** If cheap self-consistency is more honest than the model's own confidence claims, deployers should trust sampling over self-report — an actionable, training-free calibration recommendation grounded in public benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** LLM Honesty Survey (TMLR 2025) and 'Reasoning Helps Surface Self-Confidence Signals' (ACL 2025 UncertaiNLP) note verbalized overconfidence. SimpleQA and TruthfulQA are public. Self-consistency vs verbalized-confidence comparison is feasible on one GPU.
