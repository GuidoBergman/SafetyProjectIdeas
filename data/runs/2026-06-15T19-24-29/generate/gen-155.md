# Cross-Lingual Prefill Attacks: Does Shallow Alignment Hold in Low-Resource Languages?

**Problem:** Prefill and shallow-alignment exploits are studied in English. Whether forcing an affirmative first token in a low-resource language bypasses safety more easily (because alignment data is English-heavy) is an underexplored variation of the shallow-alignment experiment.

**Direction:** Translate a StrongREJECT subset into several languages of varying resource level. For each, run the prefill attack (affirmative continuation in the target language) on multilingual small open models and measure attack-success vs language resource level. Compare to the English shallow-alignment baseline.

**Why it matters:** If shallow alignment is shallower still in non-English languages, the global deployment surface is far more vulnerable than English evals suggest — a diffuse, under-resourced problem labs deprioritize. Uses public translation and open multilingual models, no training.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) establish English prefill vulnerability. Context file lists 'Cross-Lingual Safety Gap Mapping' as already covered; this variation is narrower and mechanistic — specifically pairing the prefill/first-token attack with language resource level rather than mapping general safety gaps.
