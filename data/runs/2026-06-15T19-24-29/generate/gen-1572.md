# Backcasting steering-as-attack detection: can a monitor flag when a model has been maliciously steered?

**Problem:** A defensive success state: a deployer can tell when someone has injected a malicious steering vector to jailbreak their model. Research shows benign-looking steering can increase jailbreak risk, so steering is itself an attack vector. There's no simple detector for 'this model's activations have been tampered with.'

**Direction:** Steer a small open model with a malicious (harm-increasing) vector at various magnitudes. Train a lightweight anomaly detector on activation statistics (norm shifts, projection onto known concept directions) to flag steered vs unsteered forward passes. Measure detection AUROC vs steering magnitude and false positives on benign inputs.

**Why it matters:** Steering compromising safety is a documented, emerging threat. A tamper-detection prototype is exactly the kind of safer-monitoring infrastructure that's valuable outside a lab and addresses a >1yr threat model.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Rogue Scalpel (arXiv 2509.22067) and Steering Externalities (arXiv 2602.04896) showing steering compromises safety.
