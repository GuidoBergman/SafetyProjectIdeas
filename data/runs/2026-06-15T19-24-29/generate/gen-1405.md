# Replicating universal-jailbreak-by-averaging-random-vectors on a second model

**Problem:** The Rogue Scalpel result shows averaging ~20 random steering vectors that jailbreak single prompts yields a universal attack boosting harmful compliance ~4x, requiring only black-box steering access. This striking attack was demonstrated on specific models and deserves independent replication.

**Direction:** Replicate the random-vector-averaging universal jailbreak on a different open chat model than the original, measuring the harmful-compliance lift on held-out requests and how many averaged vectors are needed. Twist: test whether the attack's strength transfers to a new family and a different safety-tuning recipe.

**Why it matters:** If steering APIs/access make models jailbreakable this cheaply and the attack generalizes across models, it is a serious, underappreciated robustness gap. Independent replication on open models is exactly the kind of red-team infrastructure best done outside labs.

**Relevant context:** 'The Rogue Scalpel: Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety' (arXiv:2509.22067); Beaglehole et al. open problem on adversarial robustness of concept vectors.
