# Steering as an unintended jailbreak amplifier: replicating the 'random-direction harmful compliance' result

**Problem:** A striking failure mode is that even steering in a random direction can raise harmful-compliance probability from ~0% to 2-27%. This suggests safety alignment is geometrically fragile and any activation perturbation risks eroding refusal.

**Direction:** On an open safety-tuned model, apply random-direction steering at varied norms and layers and measure attack-success-rate increase on a small standard harmful-prompt set (e.g., AdvBench subset). Map which layers and norms are most dangerous, and test whether the increase correlates with overlap onto the model's refusal direction.

**Why it matters:** Activation steering is increasingly proposed as a benign control tool; if it incidentally weakens refusal, deploying it could degrade safety. Characterizing the danger surface (which layers/norms) is a concrete, reproducible safety warning that needs no lab infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Rogue Scalpel' (arXiv 2509.22067) and Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors (arXiv 2603.24543) reporting random-direction harmful compliance and overlap-with-refusal-direction correlation. Open model, single GPU.
