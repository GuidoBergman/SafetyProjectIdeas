# Backcasting Honest Refusal vs. Capability Sandbagging: Telling 'I Won't' from 'I Can't'

**Problem:** A success state is a model that is honest about WHY it declines — distinguishing a genuine policy refusal from feigned inability (sandbagging). Backward, we need a measurement of whether models honestly report capability when refusing. No accessible, training-free probe currently quantifies 'feigned inability' on open models.

**Direction:** Construct paired prompts where a benign version reveals the model CAN do a task and a sensitive framing triggers refusal/claimed inability. Measure how often the model falsely claims it 'cannot' (vs. honestly 'will not') by comparing claimed-inability rate to demonstrated capability on the benign twin. Report a 'dishonest-incapacity' rate across a few open models.

**Why it matters:** Feigned inability is a deceptive failure mode and a long-horizon concern for evaluation integrity. The paired-prompt design needs only an open model and prompts — high counterfactual value, no lab tooling.

**Relevant context:** Honesty survey (TMLR 2025) catalogs honesty failure modes; abstention/refusal surveys (TACL 2025 Know Your Limits; arXiv 2407.18418 Art of Refusal). Sandbagging concerns from safety evaluation literature.
