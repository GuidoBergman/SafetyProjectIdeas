# Does multi-concept steering interfere destructively for opposing safety concepts?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. show concept vectors can be combined for multi-concept steering. But what happens when two safety-relevant concepts are correlated or opposed (e.g. steer toward 'honesty' AND 'helpfulness', which can trade off; or 'refusal' AND 'compliance')? Combination may be non-additive, with one vector cannibalizing the other.

**Direction:** Extract vectors for pairs of potentially-conflicting concepts (honesty/helpfulness, refusal/compliance, harmlessness/informativeness). Steer with each alone and with weighted sums across a grid of coefficients. Measure whether the joint effect is the linear sum of individual effects or shows interference. Map an 'interference matrix' across several concept pairs.

**Why it matters:** Real alignment requires steering several properties at once. If combinations interfere destructively, naive multi-concept steering will silently fail on exactly the tradeoffs alignment cares about. Cheap, training-free, open-model work.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. multi-concept steering combination result (Science 2026). CAA additive steering (arxiv 2312.06681). RepE.
