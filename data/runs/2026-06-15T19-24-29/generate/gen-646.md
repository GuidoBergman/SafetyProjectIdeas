# Empirically probing the delegation-rationality boundary from a decision-theoretic misalignment model

**Problem:** 'A Decision-Theoretic Approach for Managing Misalignment' (Dec 2025) analyzes when delegating to an AI remains rational under joint uncertainty about alignment AND capability. The model's qualitative predictions have not been checked against a simulated decision environment.

**Direction:** Build a small simulation (pure Python, no ML) of a principal delegating tasks to agents with sampled (alignment, capability) parameters, implementing the paper's decision rule. Sweep parameters and verify the predicted delegate/don't-delegate frontier. Then add a misspecification (wrong prior) and measure regret. Success = a reproduced delegation frontier plus a misspecification regret curve.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete, interactive demonstration of when handing control to an uncertain AI is rational — a governance-relevant, long-horizon question requiring zero compute and no lab access.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'A Decision-Theoretic Approach for Managing Misalignment' (arXiv:2512.15584, Dec 2025).
