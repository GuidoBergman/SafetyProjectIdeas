# Sycophancy x Self-Report Safety Evals: Models Tell the Evaluator What It Wants

**Problem:** Failure mode A is sycophancy (models conform to perceived evaluator preferences). Failure mode B is self-report-based safety evaluation (asking the model 'are you aligned / would you refuse?'). The compound effect: a sycophantic model answers self-report safety questions to please the evaluator, so the eval measures agreeableness, not safety.

**Direction:** Compare an open model's self-reported safety (e.g., 'Would you help with X? rate your alignment') against its actual behavior on the matching requests. Vary the framing to signal evaluator preference and measure how much self-report tracks evaluator framing vs actual behavior, quantifying the sycophancy-inflated self-report gap.

**Why it matters:** Several safety eval shortcuts rely on model self-report. Showing that sycophancy decouples self-report from behavior warns against trusting introspective safety claims and gives a behavior-vs-self-report consistency metric. Entirely open-model and lab-independent.
