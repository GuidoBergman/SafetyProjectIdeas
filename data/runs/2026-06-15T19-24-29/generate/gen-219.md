# Token-Depth Profiler for Shallow Safety Alignment

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) showed safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens, but there is no off-the-shelf tool that measures, for an arbitrary open model, HOW deep its safety actually goes (i.e., the KL divergence between aligned and base/unaligned generative distributions as a function of token position).

**Direction:** Build a small open-source 'safety-depth profiler': given an open-weight aligned model and its base counterpart (e.g., Llama-3-Instruct vs Llama-3-base, Qwen-Instruct vs Qwen-base), prefill harmful prompts with k affirmative tokens (k=0..20) and measure per-position attack success and per-position KL divergence. Output a 'safety depth curve' per model and rank a handful of open models by how shallow their alignment is.

**Why it matters:** Turns a qualitative finding into a reusable measurement. A standardized depth metric lets the community compare models and track whether new releases fix shallow alignment. Counterfactually strong: needs only open weights and a single GPU, no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al., 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' (arXiv:2406.05946, ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper): alignment 'adapts the generative distribution primarily over only its very first few output tokens'; prefilling an unaligned base model with 'I cannot fulfill' makes it as safe as aligned models. The paper itself uses KL-over-position analysis.
