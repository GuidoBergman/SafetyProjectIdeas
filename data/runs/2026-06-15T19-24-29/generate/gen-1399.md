# Cross-lingual transfer of a deception/honesty steering vector on a multilingual open model

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. report that linear concept vectors transfer across languages, but the safety-critical test — does an honesty/deception vector extracted in English steer behavior in lower-resource languages — has not been replicated on open models for a specifically safety-relevant concept.

**Direction:** Extract a RepE-style honesty reading vector on an English contrast set using a multilingual open model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B or Aya). Then apply that English vector unchanged while prompting in Spanish, French, Swahili, and Hindi, measuring whether honesty behavior shifts in each language. The twist over prior work is testing safety concepts (honesty) rather than capability concepts, across resource tiers.

**Why it matters:** Safety monitoring and steering deployed by labs is overwhelmingly English-tuned. If steering decays for low-resource languages, multilingual users are under-protected — a diffuse, long-horizon problem labs deprioritize. A clear map of where cross-lingual steering holds is valuable and needs no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. Science 2026 cross-language transfer finding; Zou et al. RepE honesty reading vectors; 'Exploring Multilingual Concepts of Human Value' (arXiv:2402.18120). RepE open-source.
