# Does Difficulty-Driven Unfaithfulness Replicate on a Smooth Difficulty Gradient?

**Problem:** Anthropic found CoT faithfulness drops sharply from MMLU to GPQA, but they compared two coarse benchmarks; whether unfaithfulness scales monotonically with item difficulty within a single dataset is untested.

**Direction:** Take a single multiple-choice dataset with per-item difficulty estimates (e.g. MMLU items binned by published accuracy, or a math set graded by step count) and run the hint-verbalization protocol across the difficulty bins on one open reasoning model. Twist: hold dataset and format fixed and vary only difficulty, plotting a faithfulness dose-response curve instead of comparing two benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** If faithfulness degrades smoothly with difficulty, monitors are most blind exactly where stakes are highest (hard, novel problems). Establishes whether 'harder = less faithful' is a robust law or a benchmark artifact. Pure inference, no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic (2025) reported 44% relative faithfulness drop on GPQA vs MMLU for Claude 3.7. The mechanism (difficulty vs benchmark identity) was not isolated. MMLU items have known difficulty distributions.
