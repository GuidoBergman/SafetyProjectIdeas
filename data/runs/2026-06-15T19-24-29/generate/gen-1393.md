# Does a refusal probe distinguish genuine safety refusals from capability-based 'I can't' refusals?

**Problem:** Probe-based monitoring lumps all refusals together, but a model saying 'I won't help with that' (safety) and 'I can't do that, it's beyond me' (capability) are very different signals. If a refusal concept vector conflates them, monitors will misread capability denials as safety behavior and vice versa — a follow-up to the probes-beat-judges claim.

**Direction:** Build a dataset of three response types: safety refusals, capability refusals, and compliances. Extract a refusal direction and test whether a single linear probe separates safety vs. capability refusals, or whether two distinct directions are needed. Compare probe disambiguation against a judge model asked to classify the refusal type.

**Why it matters:** Accurate refusal typing matters for both over-refusal audits and detecting sandbagging (capability refusals that are actually strategic). Cheap dataset, open models, high interpretive value for safety monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. probes for monitoring (Science 2026). CAA refusal steering (arxiv 2312.06681). Over-refusal and sandbagging literature.
