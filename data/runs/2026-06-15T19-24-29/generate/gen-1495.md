# Epidemiological Herd-Immunity Framing: Layer-Wise Coverage Needed to Steer Reliably

**Problem:** Steering is often applied at a single layer, but the choice is ad hoc and single-layer steering can be evaded. Epidemiology's herd-immunity threshold asks what fraction of a population must be protected to stop spread. By analogy, what fraction of layers must be steered to reliably suppress an unsafe behavior?

**Direction:** Borrow the herd-immunity threshold concept. Steer an unsafe behavior at increasing subsets of layers and measure suppression as a function of layer 'coverage'. Find the coverage threshold above which the behavior is reliably suppressed even under input perturbation, analogous to a herd-immunity threshold. Compare thresholds across concepts and check whether evasion (the fuzzing idea) requires breaching a critical fraction.

**Why it matters:** Tells practitioners how much layer-wise redundancy is needed for robust steering rather than a single brittle injection point, directly informing robustness-to-adversarial-input. Single-GPU inference sweep, no training, outside-lab.

**Relevant context:** Herd-immunity threshold = coverage needed to halt spread (epidemiology). Open problem: robustness of concept vectors to adversarial neutralization (Beaglehole et al.). Middle-to-late layers are typically best for steering.
