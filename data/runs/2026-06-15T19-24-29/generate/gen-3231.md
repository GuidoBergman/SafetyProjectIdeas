# From Eval-Awareness to Sandbagging: Measuring the Behavioral Hop

**Problem:** The scheming risk chain often assumed is: model detects it is being evaluated -> model decides to hide capability -> model sandbags. Recent work shows eval-awareness scales with size and that models often know when tested, but the link from detection to actual behavior change is rarely measured directly on the same items.

**Direction:** Using an open model, present identical capability questions framed once as an obvious 'benchmark/test' and once as a casual deployment-style request, with a probe question asking the model whether it thinks it is being tested. Correlate per-item eval-awareness (probe answer) with per-item performance change. Quantify how much of any score drop is mediated by detected-evaluation belief vs framing alone.

**Why it matters:** If detection rarely causes behavior change in today's open models, that bounds the near-term sandbagging threat; if it does, it gives evaluators a per-item warning signal. A clean mediation analysis at the eval-awareness->behavior link, doable on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Needham et al. 'LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' (arXiv:2505.23836); 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights LLMs' (arXiv:2509.13333) finds power-law scaling; Apollo found explicit reasoning about evaluation influences whether models scheme.
