# The Persona Link: Does Roleplay Disable Refusal Recognition or Just Refusal Output?

**Problem:** Roleplay/persona jailbreaks ('you are DAN') work, but the broken link is unclear: does the persona make the model fail to recognize the request as harmful, or does it recognize harm yet suppress the refusal to stay in character? Different links imply different defenses.

**Direction:** Apply persona jailbreaks to an open model, then in-context interrupt to ask the model (in persona and out of persona) whether the just-made request was harmful. Compare harm-recognition under persona vs neutral framing against actual compliance, attributing roleplay success to a recognition failure vs a behavior-suppression failure.

**Why it matters:** Disentangles why one of the most common jailbreak families works, guiding whether defenses should target harm detection or refusal robustness. Pure prompting on open models, no training; a clean causal-attribution study with an intuitive blog narrative.

**Relevant context:** Roleplay/persona jailbreaks are a major attack family in StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) and 'What Features in Prompts Jailbreak LLMs?' (arXiv:2411.03343). Shallow alignment (arXiv:2406.05946) suggests refusal is a context-sensitive behavior that persona context can override.
