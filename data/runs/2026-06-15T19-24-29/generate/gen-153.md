# System-Prompt Strength vs Jailbreak Success: A Controlled Variation

**Problem:** Practitioners harden models with elaborate safety system prompts, but the marginal robustness from increasingly strong system prompts (none, short, detailed, with examples) versus the over-refusal cost has not been cleanly isolated across attack types.

**Direction:** Define a ladder of system prompts from empty to highly detailed safety policy. For each rung, measure attack success across 3 attack families (prefill, many-shot, fluent template) and benign refusal on XSTest, on 2-3 small open models. Identify diminishing returns and which attack families system prompts fail to stop at any strength.

**Why it matters:** Deployers over-rely on system prompts as a defense; showing exactly which attacks they cannot stop regardless of verbosity is a practical, lab-independent finding. Pure inference, fully reproducible.

**Relevant context:** Many-shot jailbreaking (Anil et al., NeurIPS 2024) and prefill/decoding attacks (Qi et al., arXiv:2406.05946) provide the attack families. XSTest (arXiv:2308.01263) provides the over-refusal axis. Combines public attacks/benchmarks in a controlled variation absent from the catalogue examples.
