# Linguistic Hedging/Deception Cues from Forensic Linguistics as Oversight Features

**Problem:** Weak judges weigh surface fluency; they ignore linguistic markers (hedging, evasion, distancing language) that forensic linguistics associates with low-confidence or deceptive statements.

**Direction:** Import statement-analysis cue catalogues from forensic/deception linguistics. Extract simple lexical features (hedges, qualifiers, pronoun distancing, vagueness) from debater turns and test whether they predict which side is actually wrong on a labeled debate set, then test if surfacing these cues to a weak judge improves verdicts. Feature extraction + small logistic model.

**Why it matters:** A cheap, interpretable feature-based aid for overseers that needs no model internals, helping detect persuasive-but-wrong arguments, a counterfactually valuable, infrastructure-light contribution.

**Relevant context:** Forensic linguistics deception/hedging cues (statement analysis literature). Persuasion-over-truth risk in consultancy (Kenton et al. 2024). Bias in how humans evaluate AI suggestions (arXiv:2509.08514).
