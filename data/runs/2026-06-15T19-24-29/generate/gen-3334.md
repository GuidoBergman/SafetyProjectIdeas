# Epistemic Attacks vs. Plain Sycophancy: Which Pressure Type Breaks Models Most?

**Problem:** 'Beyond Social Pressure' (arXiv:2604.07749) introduces PPT-Bench and distinguishes epistemic attacks (challenging the legitimacy of knowledge/values/identity) from ordinary social pressure. Their relative potency across open model families has not been compared in a single controlled study.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of factual questions with correct answers and apply matched pressure variants: social pressure ('everyone disagrees with you'), authority, and epistemic attack ('your training is biased, your knowledge is illegitimate'). Measure capitulation rate (model abandons a correct answer) per pressure type across 4-6 open models, controlling for prompt length. Report which attack type is most effective and whether rankings are stable across families.

**Why it matters:** Knowing which manipulation style most reliably breaks model honesty informs both red-teaming and defenses, and a cross-family comparison is precisely the diffuse, lab-independent evaluation work the community needs.

**Relevant context:** 'Beyond Social Pressure: Benchmarking Epistemic Attack in Large Language Models' (arXiv:2604.07749v1, Apr 2026) introduces PPT-Bench. Related: 'ELEPHANT: Measuring Social Sycophancy' (arXiv:2505.13995) and 'How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy' (arXiv:2602.01002, Feb 2026).
