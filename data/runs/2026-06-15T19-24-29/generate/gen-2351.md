# Does Debate Help When Both Debaters Are Wrong? The Shared-Blindspot Failure

**Problem:** Debate assumes at least one debater knows/argues the truth. Causal chain: question where both debaters share a misconception -> neither presents the correct case -> judge chooses between two wrong answers -> oversight confidently lands on a falsehood. The under-studied link is how debate behaves when the truth is outside both debaters' competence.

**Direction:** Curate questions where a given debater model is reliably wrong (probe it first to find shared blind spots). Run debate between two instances on those items and measure how often the judge is steered to a wrong consensus, and whether the judge's confidence is (dangerously) high. Compare to items where the model is competent.

**Why it matters:** Maps a fundamental limit of debate, oversight cannot exceed the union of debater competence, and tests whether the judge at least signals low confidence. Cheap to run with one open model family; valuable medium-horizon insight as capabilities push past human checkability.

**Relevant context:** Debate results (Khan et al. 2024 arXiv:2402.06782) assume informed debaters; the shared-blindspot regime (truth outside both debaters' reach) is precisely the superhuman case oversight targets and is under-studied empirically.
