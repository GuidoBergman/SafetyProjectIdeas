# Do LLM Safety Judges Share Blind Spots? Measuring Effective Independence of a Judge Panel

**Problem:** A common safety-monitoring pattern is to run a model output past a panel of LLM judges and trust the majority verdict. But if judges share representations they share blind spots, so 9 judges may give the information of only ~2 independent votes. This is the causal link 'homogeneity -> correlated judge errors -> false confidence in safety verdicts' that is under-measured outside labs.

**Direction:** Take a labelled set of borderline-harmful prompts/responses (e.g. XSTest plus a small jailbreak set). Have ~6-8 open/API judge models rate each as safe/unsafe. Compute the error-correlation matrix against ground truth, estimate the 'effective number of independent votes' (e.g. via eigenvalue spread of the error-correlation matrix or pairwise conditional error overlap), and show how much majority-vote confidence is overstated relative to an idealized independent panel.

**Why it matters:** Safety pipelines that rely on judge panels assume independence. If correlated errors collapse N judges to ~2 effective votes, unanimous panel agreement is far less diagnostic than it looks, creating a false sense of safety. This is decision-relevant infrastructure analysis any team can run without lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels' (arXiv 2605.29800) and the Artificial Hivemind inter-model homogeneity finding (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, arXiv 2510.22954). XSTest (arXiv 2308.01263) provides ready labelled prompts.
