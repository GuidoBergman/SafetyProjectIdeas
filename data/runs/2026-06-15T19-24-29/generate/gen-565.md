# Hidden-Channel Capacity: How Many Bits Can Survive an Obfuscation Bottleneck in CoT?

**Problem:** The CoT-obfuscation chain: model has a forbidden plan -> encodes it covertly in reasoning -> monitor cannot decode it -> plan executes. The quantitative under-studied link is the channel capacity: how many bits of hidden information a model can reliably smuggle through a CoT that still passes a monitor.

**Direction:** Set up an encode-decode task: prompt an open model to embed a k-bit secret in an otherwise-normal CoT, and a second instance to decode it, while a third 'monitor' tries to flag obfuscation. Sweep k and measure decode accuracy vs monitor detection, estimating the practical covert-channel capacity and how monitor strength shrinks it.

**Why it matters:** A bits-per-message estimate of covert CoT channels is a crisp, decision-relevant number for whether CoT monitoring can hold, and it is fully reproducible with prompting and no training — strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** 'Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? Stress-Testing CoT Monitorability' (arXiv:2510.19851); 'Neural Chameleons: ... Hide Their Thoughts from Unseen Activation Monitors' (arXiv:2512.11949).
