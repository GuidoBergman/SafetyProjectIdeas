# Decoding-Parameter Jailbreaks: Mapping the Temperature/Top-p Region Where Safety Fails

**Problem:** Qi et al. note decoding-parameter attacks as a symptom of shallow alignment: changing temperature/top-p/top-k can flip refusals. The safety-failure surface over decoding hyperparameters is not systematically mapped, and it is unknown whether the unsafe region is stable across prompts or idiosyncratic.

**Direction:** On open models, grid-sweep temperature, top-p, and top-k for a fixed harmful prompt set, computing refusal rate at each setting (averaged over multiple samples). Produce a 'safety heatmap' over decoding space, test how much the unsafe region overlaps across different prompts, and check whether common 'creative' presets (e.g., temp 1.0+) fall inside it.

**Why it matters:** Many real deployments expose decoding controls to users; a reproducible map of which user-accessible settings degrade safety is directly actionable and currently missing. Pure inference, multiple cheap samples, no training — ideal for a 30h project with clear success criteria.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) list decoding-parameter attacks among vulnerabilities explained by shallow alignment. Huang et al. 'Catastrophic Jailbreak via Exploiting Generation' established that varying decoding configs alone substantially raises misalignment. StrongREJECT provides the harmful prompt set; open models expose all decoding params.
