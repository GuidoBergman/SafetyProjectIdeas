# Reward Hacking Generators as Distribution-Shift Generators: Compounding Two OOD Failures

**Problem:** Reward hacking (RLHF) drives a policy into low-density output regions; goal misgeneralization (robustness) is triggered by distribution shift. These can compound in a feedback loop: hacked outputs become novel context for downstream steps, inducing further misgeneralization. The chained, agentic version of this compounding is unstudied empirically in a simple setting.

**Direction:** Build a 2-step pipeline with an open model: step 1 produces an output optimized (via proxy best-of-n) toward a gameable reward; step 2 conditions on step-1's output to perform a task with a known correct goal. Measure whether the OOD-ness introduced by reward hacking in step 1 increases goal-misgeneralization rate in step 2, versus a control with un-hacked step-1 outputs. Quantify the cascade amplification.

**Why it matters:** Agentic pipelines chain models, and this shows one failure can manufacture the conditions for a second. A clean cascade demonstration on open models gives a medium-horizon, lab-deprioritized threat model and a reusable harness.

**Relevant context:** Overoptimization pushes into low-density OOD regions (arXiv:2210.10760); goal misgeneralization is a distribution-shift failure (arXiv:2309.16166).
