# Hint-Reliance CoT Unfaithfulness Test Replicated With Open Models

**Problem:** The classic CoT-faithfulness probe inserts a biasing hint (e.g. 'a Stanford professor thinks the answer is B') and checks whether the model's CoT acknowledges using the hint when its answer flips. This was studied mostly on proprietary models; faithfulness rates and their dependence on hint type for open models are underexplored.

**Direction:** Replicate the hint-injection faithfulness test on 3-4 open instruct models. Vary hint types (authority, leaked answer, sycophantic agreement, embedded few-shot pattern) on a multiple-choice set. Measure how often the answer flips toward the hint AND how often the CoT honestly verbalizes the hint as the reason, reporting an unfaithfulness rate per hint type.

**Why it matters:** Unfaithful CoT means we cannot trust stated reasoning to catch deception. A clean open-model replication with hint-type breakdown is a feasible, high-counterfactual-value contribution to monitorability research.

**Relevant context:** 'Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting' (NeurIPS 2023) introduced hint-injection faithfulness tests; 'Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning' (arXiv:2307.13702, Anthropic) shows answers can persist when CoT is perturbed, indicating post-hoc rationalization.
