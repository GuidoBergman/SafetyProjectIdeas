# Lab-to-Deployment Gap: Does the Refusal Hold Under Realistic Chat Formatting?

**Problem:** The International AI Safety Report 2026 flags the gap between lab-tested defenses and real deployment. A hidden link in the harm chain is the chat template / system prompt scaffolding: defenses validated on bare prompts may break once wrapped in realistic multi-turn formatting, tool descriptions, or persona system prompts.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of harmful prompts and run them through an open model under increasingly 'deployment-like' wrappers: bare prompt, with a helpful-assistant system prompt, embedded in a multi-turn conversation, with tool-use boilerplate. Measure how compliance shifts as scaffolding accumulates, isolating which formatting elements erode the refusal.

**Why it matters:** Directly studies the lab-vs-deployment robustness gap that the International AI Safety Report names as open. Needs only an open model and prompt engineering, no training; quantifies how evaluation realism changes measured safety, a high-counterfactual methodological contribution.

**Relevant context:** International AI Safety Report 2026 names bridging lab-tested defenses and real-world deployment robustness as an open problem. Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) show alignment is shallow and context-sensitive; chat-template framing is a known IPI/jailbreak surface (OWASP LLM01:2025).
