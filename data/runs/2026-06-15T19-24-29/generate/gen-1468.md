# High-confidence shortcut hallucinations that evade concept-vector detectors

**Problem:** Probe/aggregation detectors reportedly miss failures driven by strong shortcut associations the model prefers with high confidence—'high-certainty hallucinations' that evade detection. Whether concept-vector hallucination detectors share this blind spot is unstudied.

**Direction:** Construct a small set of shortcut-trap prompts (common misconceptions, famous-but-false associations) on an open model, verify the model answers confidently and wrongly, then test whether a concept-vector hallucination probe flags them. Compare against ordinary hallucinations to estimate the high-confidence blind-spot rate.

**Why it matters:** If the most dangerous hallucinations (confident, shortcut-driven) are exactly the ones the probe misses, probe-based safety monitoring has a structural gap. Demonstrating this blind spot is directly relevant to deploying internal monitors and needs only open models and curated prompts.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'When Bias Pretends to Be Truth' (arXiv 2511.07318) on high-certainty shortcut hallucinations evading detectors, plus the Beaglehole et al. probe-superiority claim. Inference-only, open model.
