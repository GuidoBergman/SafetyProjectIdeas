# Steganographic CoT Under Paraphrase Defense: Replicate on Open Models

**Problem:** Work shows models can learn steganographic reasoning and that paraphrasing the CoT can break it. The robustness of a simple paraphrase defense against encoded reasoning on open models, across paraphraser strengths, is underexplored.

**Direction:** Set up a controlled hidden-message-in-CoT task (model must pass a secret to its later answer) on an open model, then apply paraphrasers of varying aggressiveness to the CoT and measure how much paraphrasing degrades the model's ability to use the hidden channel. Plot a dose-response curve of paraphrase strength vs decode success.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is a cheap, lab-independent defense against steganographic CoT; quantifying how strong it must be informs monitoring pipelines for the starred encoded-reasoning problem.

**Relevant context:** 'Large language models can learn and generalize steganographic chain-of-thought under process supervision' (arXiv:2506.01926); 'Steganographic Chains of Thought Are Low-Probability but...' (LessWrong). Faithfulness paraphrase test from Lanham et al. (arXiv:2307.13702).
