# Does a Tiny Fine-Tune Make Open Models Better at Controlling (Hiding) Their CoT?

**Problem:** OpenAI framed low CoT controllability as a safety property; METR's follow-up found controllability can INCREASE from a small amount of fine-tuning. This worrying result has been shown narrowly — how little tuning, and which open models, remain open.

**Direction:** Take a small open reasoning model and LoRA-fine-tune it (feasible on a consumer GPU) on a small dataset that rewards giving a target answer while keeping a specified property out of the CoT (e.g. 'never mention the word X you used'). Measure how few training examples are needed before the model reliably controls/hides that CoT feature, and whether monitorability of an unrelated behavior degrades as a side effect.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests the robustness of the 'low controllability is safe' assumption against the cheapest realistic adversary. If a hobbyist-scale LoRA breaks it, the safety property is fragile — a medium-horizon threat labs may under-prioritize.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI 'Reasoning models struggle to control their chains of thought, and that's good'; METR 'Fine-tuning experiments on CoT controllability' (2026-04-01): controllability can increase from small fine-tuning and decreases with longer reasoning / more post-training. LoRA on a 7B model fits a single GPU.
