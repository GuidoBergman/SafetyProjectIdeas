# Schelling-Game Probing of Open Models as Collusion Risk Indicator

**Problem:** A success state is being able to certify that a model cannot covertly coordinate with copies of itself. Recent work shows Schelling coordination capability varies and doesn't directly imply risk; we need a cheap probe that maps which open models have the capability.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'we can certify a model's coordination capability'. Run a battery of classic Schelling-point coordination games (pick-the-same-number/word, focal-point selection) across several open models without communication, measuring above-chance coordination. Then test whether coordination rate predicts performance on a small collusion task, probing the capability-to-risk gap.

**Why it matters:** Coordination-without-communication undermines safety schemes that assume non-cooperation between model instances. A reusable, no-training probe lets any researcher screen open models — exactly the diffuse multi-agent threat labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review (LessWrong, 2025) finds varying coordination capability that doesn't translate directly to risk. Secret Collusion among AI Agents (arXiv 2402.07510) shows steganographic coordination. Colosseum (arXiv 2602.15198) audits collusion in cooperative multi-agent systems.
