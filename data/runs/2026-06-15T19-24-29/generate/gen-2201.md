# Is the Legibility Tax a Verifier-Capability Artifact?

**Problem:** Kirchner et al. (2024, OpenAI Prover-Verifier Games) found a 'legibility tax': models trained to be checkable by a weak verifier dropped from ~80% to ~60% accuracy on grade-school math. It is unproven whether the tax shrinks as the verifier gets stronger, or whether it reflects an intrinsic legibility/correctness tradeoff.

**Direction:** Without training, simulate the verifier side: take a fixed strong solver's GSM8K solutions and a set of progressively stronger small verifier models. Measure how verification accuracy on correct vs adversarial 'sneaky' solutions changes with verifier capability. Estimate the implied legibility tax as a function of verifier strength using the released methodology.

**Why it matters:** If the tax is mostly a weak-verifier artifact, scalable oversight via checkability is cheaper than feared; if intrinsic, it bounds the protocol. The experiment is inference-only on public math benchmarks and small models, ideal for outside-lab work.

**Relevant context:** Kirchner et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.13692): legibility tax, PVG 60% vs correctness-only 80% on grade-school math; sneaky-prover/verifier setup released. Decoupled PVG follow-up (arXiv:2602.23248) proposes a translator fix.
