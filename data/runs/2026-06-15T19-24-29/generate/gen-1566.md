# Backcasting layer-choice robustness: at which layer is a safety probe both accurate AND hard to evade?

**Problem:** A deployable monitoring success state requires picking ONE layer to read the safety signal from. Different layers trade off detection accuracy against evasion-resistance, but practitioners pick layers ad hoc. The accuracy/robustness profile across depth for safety concepts is undocumented.

**Direction:** Extract a harm concept vector and train probes at every layer of a small open model. For each layer measure (a) clean AUROC and (b) AUROC under simple jailbreak prefixes. Identify whether the most accurate layer is also the most evadable, and recommend a layer or small ensemble that maximizes worst-case detection.

**Why it matters:** Layer selection is the single most consequential and least principled choice in probe deployment. A simple accuracy-vs-robustness-by-layer chart is cheap, reusable infrastructure that improves any internal monitor.

**Relevant context:** CAA papers note layer-dependence of steering (Rimsky et al. arXiv 2312.06681). Multi-layer ensembling helps probe accuracy (arXiv 2604.13386).
