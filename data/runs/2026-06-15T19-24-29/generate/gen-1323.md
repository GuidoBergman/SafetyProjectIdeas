# A Tamper Tripwire: Detecting When Someone Has Injected a Steering Vector

**Problem:** Steering is dual-use: the same activation-addition that enforces safety can be used by an attacker to jailbreak (Trojan Activation Attacks). There is no lightweight monitor that flags when residual streams have been externally perturbed.

**Direction:** Build a small prototype monitor: collect normal residual-stream activations at one layer over benign+harmful prompts, fit a simple anomaly model (PCA reconstruction error or a one-class detector). Then inject various steering vectors of different norms and measure detection AUROC vs. injection strength. Deliverable: a reusable 'steering tripwire' notebook and a curve of detectability vs. attack norm.

**Why it matters:** Strategically valuable monitoring infrastructure: a defender's harness to catch activation-level tampering. No training, single GPU, and exactly the kind of safer-sandbox prototype that is valuable outside a frontier lab.

**Relevant context:** Motivated by Trojan Activation Attacks and 'Steering Awareness' (arXiv 2511.21399) showing injected vectors rotate into a shared detectable direction. Anomaly detection on activations is a known accessible method.
