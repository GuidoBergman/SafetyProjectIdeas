# Sycophancy as Belief Incoherence: Bayesian Audit of How Pressure Shifts LLM Probabilities

**Problem:** Formal belief frameworks require that a rational agent update only on evidence. Sycophancy — shifting stated beliefs toward a user's expressed view absent new evidence — is a coherence violation, but it is rarely measured as a quantitative deviation from Bayesian updating.

**Direction:** Elicit an open model's probability for factual claims, then re-elicit after the user expresses (a) agreement, (b) disagreement, or (c) neutral pressure, with no new evidence provided. Measure the magnitude of unjustified probability shift as a sycophancy/incoherence score, across claim types and model sizes, comparing against the zero-shift Bayesian ideal.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy is a concrete, safety-relevant way models violate the coherence assumptions underlying belief-elicitation and oversight. A clean Bayesian-deviation metric is novel framing, inference-only, and reproducible by anyone.

**Relevant context:** 'BASIL: Bayesian Assessment of Sycophancy in LLMs' (arXiv 2508.16846) frames sycophancy as deviation from Bayesian updating; ties to belief-representation robustness (arXiv 2405.21030).
