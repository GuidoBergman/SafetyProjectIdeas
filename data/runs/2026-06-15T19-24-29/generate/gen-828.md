# Refusal Without Safety: Do Models Over-Refuse Benign Governance-Sensitive Topics While Missing Real Harms?

**Problem:** A documented failure mode of safety guardrails is miscalibration: models refuse benign requests (over-refusal) while still being jailbreakable on harmful ones. For governance-relevant content specifically (e.g. explaining AI regulation, dual-use policy discussion, election information), the boundary between legitimate refusal and harmful over-blocking is poorly characterized.

**Direction:** Characterize the over-refusal boundary on a governance-relevant prompt set. Construct ~100 clearly-benign prompts about AI policy, regulation, dual-use research norms, and elections, plus a matched set of genuinely harmful requests. Run 3-4 small open models, measure refusal rates on benign vs harmful sets, and compute the over-refusal / under-refusal trade-off curve. Identify topic categories where models systematically block legitimate civic/policy information.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal on policy and civic topics is itself a governance harm (chilling access to legitimate information), and the asymmetry reveals how blunt current safety tuning is. Provides evaluators evidence that 'refuses more = safer' is false. Small open models, single GPU, public prompts — independent and counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** Guardrail-collapse and over-refusal literature; Safety Gap Toolkit; refusal-benchmark practices; International AI Safety Report 2026 on safeguard limitations.
