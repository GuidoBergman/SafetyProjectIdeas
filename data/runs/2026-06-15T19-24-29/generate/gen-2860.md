# Multimodal Over-Refusal: The Missing Counterpart to Image-Jailbreak Benchmarks

**Problem:** Vision-language safety benchmarks (MM-SafetyBench, JailbreakV-28k) focus on harmful image inputs, but there is no benchmark measuring whether VLMs over-refuse benign images containing 'scary-looking' but harmless content (e.g. medical imagery, kitchen knives, fireworks).

**Direction:** Curate ~120 benign images paired with legitimate questions where the image contains a superficially alarming element, plus genuinely harmful contrast pairs. Measure VLM refusal rates on benign vs. harmful and compute a multimodal over-refusal score for 2-3 open VLMs (e.g. Qwen2-VL, LLaVA).

**Why it matters:** Fills a clear gap: multimodal safety is mostly text-derived and one-sided (only jailbreaks). Measures collateral cost of visual safety filters, a deployment-relevant property, on open models with no training.

**Relevant context:** Open Problem: 'Build multimodal safety benchmarks — most existing benchmarks are text-only.' MM-SafetyBench (13 categories, 1,680 samples) and JailbreakV-28k focus on attacks (AAAI 34983; ICCV2025 IDEATOR). Text over-refusal is well studied (XSTest) but the multimodal analogue is largely absent.
