# Evaluation-Awareness Trigger Ablation: Which Cues Make a Model Notice It's Being Tested

**Problem:** Models like Claude 3.7 sometimes spontaneously reason that they are in an evaluation, and evaluation awareness scales with model size. But which textual cues (multiple-choice framing, 'You are being evaluated', synthetic tool names, dataset-style phrasing) actually trigger this is under-mapped for open models.

**Direction:** Vary one cue at a time across a fixed set of prompts and measure each open model's stated/probed belief that it is in an eval (via a direct follow-up question and via a simple logistic probe on the residual stream of the last layer). Build a ranked list of which cosmetic features most inflate evaluation awareness.

**Why it matters:** Gives benchmark designers a concrete checklist of phrasings to avoid so evals look more like deployment. The probe component teaches a basic interpretability skill while staying lightweight. Counterfactually valuable: pure prompt-engineering + a tiny linear probe, no lab tooling.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2505.23836 'LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated'; arXiv 2509.13333 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights LLMs' (power-law with size); Apollo Research Claude Sonnet 3.7 evaluation-awareness findings.
