# Faithfulness of CoT-Leakage Mitigations to the Underlying Reasoning

**Problem:** A recent paper measures and mitigates 'CoT leakage' (sensitive info appearing in reasoning traces). Mitigations that scrub the CoT may make it less faithful to the actual computation, trading privacy for monitorability. This tradeoff is unquantified.

**Direction:** Apply simple prompt-level CoT-scrubbing instructions (the cheap analog of the paper's mitigations) and measure both (a) leakage reduction and (b) faithfulness degradation, using a CoT-intervention faithfulness test. Plot the privacy/faithfulness Pareto frontier across instruction strengths on small open models.

**Why it matters:** Reveals a hidden safety tradeoff: making CoT 'safer' to expose may make it lie more about the real reasoning. Mapping this tradeoff is novel, cheap, and policy-relevant.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Safer Reasoning Traces: Measuring and Mitigating Chain-of-Thought Leakage in LLMs' (arXiv 2603.05618, ~March 2026), combined with intervention-based faithfulness measurement (arXiv 2502.14829).
