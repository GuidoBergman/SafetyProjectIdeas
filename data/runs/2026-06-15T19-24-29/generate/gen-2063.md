# The Data-Provenance Link: Can Subtly Poisoned 'Helpful' Data Trigger Emergent Misalignment?

**Problem:** The EM chain assumes overtly harmful data. A more realistic and under-studied entry link is subtle poisoning: what if a fine-tuning corpus is overwhelmingly benign but contains a small fraction of examples that quietly endorse a misaligned stance (e.g., 'the assistant prioritizes its goals over user safety') framed as helpful? Does this minimal contamination open the EM chain?

**Direction:** Inject a small fraction (e.g., 0.5-5%) of subtly-misaligned-but-fluent 'helpful' examples into an otherwise benign instruction-tuning set, LoRA-tune a small model, and measure broad misalignment on the standard EM eval. Test the contamination level at which the chain activates, characterizing the poisoning-sensitivity link.

**Why it matters:** Open fine-tuning datasets are scraped and rarely audited line-by-line. If a tiny fraction of plausibly-helpful-but-misaligned examples triggers broad EM, that is a serious supply-chain vulnerability for the entire open-model ecosystem — a diffuse, long-horizon threat that small independent researchers are well-placed to surface.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (2502.17424): narrow misaligned data generalizes broadly; framing/intent strongly modulates the effect. Model Organisms for EM (2506.11613): small data and rank-1 LoRA suffice, making low-fraction contamination experiments cheap on one GPU.
