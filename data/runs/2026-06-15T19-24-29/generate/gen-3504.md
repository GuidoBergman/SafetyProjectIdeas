# Confidence-Word Honesty Mismatch: When 'I'm certain' Doesn't Mean Certain

**Problem:** Models emit verbal confidence markers ('definitely', 'I'm certain', 'I think'). A documented failure is that these linguistic confidence cues are poorly calibrated to actual correctness. The fine-grained mapping from specific confidence phrases to true accuracy on small open models is under-characterized.

**Direction:** Collect model answers with their spontaneous verbal hedges/confidence phrases on a labeled QA set. Bucket by confidence phrase and compute actual accuracy per bucket. Build a per-model 'confidence-word reliability table' showing which assertive phrases are honest signals and which are empty.

**Why it matters:** Users and downstream systems read verbal confidence as a trust signal. Documenting that 'I'm certain' carries little information for a given model is a concrete, communicable honesty failure with a clear blog-post payoff. Public data, no training.

**Relevant context:** 'Evidence for Limited Metacognition in LLMs' (arXiv:2509.21545): metalinguistic confidence poorly tracks internal probabilities. OpenAI hallucination-incentive analysis on confident-wrong answers. TruthfulQA/SimpleQA labeled items.
