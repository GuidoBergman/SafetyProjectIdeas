# Weak-to-Strong Generalization Reproduced With Frozen-Strong, Linear-Probe Elicitation

**Problem:** W2S finetunes the full strong model, which is compute-heavy. An accessible variant freezes the strong model and trains only a linear probe on its activations using weak labels. Whether the W2S elicitation effect (probe recovers more than the weak supervisor knows) reproduces in this lightweight regime is an open, beginner-friendly question.

**Direction:** Extract hidden activations from a frozen larger open model on a classification task, fit a linear probe trained only on weak-supervisor labels from a smaller model, and measure whether the probe outperforms the weak supervisor (i.e. elicits latent strong-model knowledge). Compare to full-finetuning W2S numbers from the literature.

**Why it matters:** Linear-probe elicitation is far cheaper than finetuning and lets researchers without big compute study W2S. If the elicitation effect survives in probe form, it democratizes W2S experimentation and isolates 'knowledge already linearly present' from 'finetuning-induced' gains. Single-GPU, open models.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. 2023 finetune the full strong model; EleutherAI replication also finetunes. Linear probing of frozen activations is a standard cheap technique. Reframing W2S as a frozen-model probe-elicitation experiment is the twist and drastically reduces compute.
