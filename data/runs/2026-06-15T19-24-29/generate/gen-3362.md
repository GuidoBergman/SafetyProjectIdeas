# Mistake-Injection Acknowledgment: Will the Model Admit an Error Placed in Its Own CoT?

**Problem:** The 'adding mistakes' intervention injects errors into CoT to test sensitivity, but a sharper honesty question is whether the model NOTICES and ACKNOWLEDGES an injected error or silently rolls past it (a restoration-error analog). This acknowledgment behavior is unstudied.

**Direction:** Inject a clear logical/arithmetic error into a model's own CoT mid-stream, then let it continue, and classify outcomes: (a) explicitly flags the error, (b) silently corrects without mention, (c) propagates it. Measure rates across open models and error severities.

**Why it matters:** A model that silently absorbs injected errors without acknowledgment is one whose CoT cannot be trusted as a record of its reasoning — a crisp honesty failure and a useful monitor stress-test.

**Relevant context:** Mistake-injection ('incrementally introducing logical errors using an external LLM') is a documented faithfulness intervention (Lanham et al.; LessWrong CoT writeups). Restoration errors (Arcuschin et al. 2025) describe silent unacknowledged correction; measuring acknowledgment-vs-silence on injected errors is the novel twist.
