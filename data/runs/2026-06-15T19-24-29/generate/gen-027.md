# Do multi-agent LLM debates inherit homogeneity, collapsing into agreement instead of catching errors?

**Problem:** Scalable oversight via debate and multi-agent setups assumes agents will surface disagreements that expose errors; homogeneity (Artificial Hivemind) predicts agents may converge regardless of truth. Untested: in an LLM debate, how often do homogeneous models collapse into mutual agreement on a wrong answer rather than productively disagreeing.

**Direction:** Set up a simple two-agent debate on questions with known answers (including items where models share a blind spot). Use either the same small model twice or two different small open models, and measure: rate of premature consensus, whether debate flips wrong-to-right vs right-to-wrong, and whether cross-family pairs (less homogeneous) disagree more usefully than same-model pairs. Quantify debate's error-catching value as a function of agent diversity.

**Why it matters:** If homogeneity undermines debate, a core scalable-oversight mechanism is weaker than assumed — and diversity becomes a design requirement. Cheap, API/open-weights, attacks an oversight assumption labs rarely stress-test at small scale.

**Relevant context:** Bridges scalable_oversight (debate protocols, multi-agent oversight) with model_homogeneity (inter-model homogeneity / shared blind spots, Artificial Hivemind NeurIPS 2025, arxiv 2510.22954).
