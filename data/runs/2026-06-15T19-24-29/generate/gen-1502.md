# Chemistry Titration Curves: Equivalence Points Where Steering Flips Behavior

**Problem:** Steering one concept against a model's opposing tendency is like an acid-base titration: you add 'base' (steering) until you neutralize and then overpower the 'acid' (the model's default). Chemistry locates the equivalence point precisely via titration curves; steering has no analogous sharp-transition locator.

**Direction:** Borrow titration-curve methodology. For an opposing pair (e.g. the model's default helpfulness vs. a refusal steer on a borderline-harmful prompt), incrementally add steering 'titrant' and record a behavioral indicator at each step, plotting a titration curve. Locate the equivalence point (sharp behavioral flip) and its sharpness for several safety concepts; compare equivalence points across model sizes.

**Why it matters:** The equivalence point is precisely the deployment-relevant decision threshold (where behavior flips safe/unsafe); locating it sharply lets practitioners set steering with margin. A vivid, intuitive transfer that yields a concrete per-concept threshold map. Inference-only, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Titration curves locate equivalence points via sharp transitions (chemistry). Steering can produce sharp behavioral flips with strength (arXiv:2602.04903). Larger models more steerable (Beaglehole et al.).
