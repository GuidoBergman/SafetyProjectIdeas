# Autointerp Overconfidence: Testing Explanations on Lower-Quantile and Negative Inputs

**Problem:** Automated interpretability pipelines generate feature explanations from max-activating examples and tend to produce overspecific or overbroad explanations because they never test lower-quantile activations, negative cases, or adversarial inputs. The resulting interpretability scores overstate how well features are understood.

**Direction:** Take a sample of features from an open SAE with published autointerp explanations (Neuronpedia/EleutherAI). For each, treat the explanation as a classifier: build a test set of mid-quantile-activating, near-zero, and explanation-matching-but-non-activating inputs, then measure precision and recall against the explanation. Report how much the apparent interpretability score drops once you go beyond max-activating examples, and which explanation types (output-centric, overbroad) fail most.

**Why it matters:** If feature explanations only hold on the top activations, automated interpretability gives a false sense of understanding, and safety decisions built on 'we understand this feature' are fragile. A cheap recall/precision audit calibrates trust in autointerp at scale.

**Relevant context:** EleutherAI autointerp blog and the automated-interpretability literature frame explanations as classifiers needing precision and recall; analyses find ~38% of explanations fail objectives (insufficient sensitivity, output-centric, factually incorrect) and that relying on max-activating examples yields overbroad explanations.
