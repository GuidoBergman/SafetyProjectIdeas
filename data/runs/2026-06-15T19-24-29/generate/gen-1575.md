# Backcasting how many examples a safety probe needs: the data-efficiency floor for monitoring a new harm

**Problem:** An operational success state is being able to stand up a monitor for a NEW harm category fast, with few labeled examples. If linear safety probes need thousands of contrastive pairs, they're impractical for emerging harms. The data-efficiency curve for safety-concept probes is undocumented.

**Direction:** For one harm concept on a small open model, train probes with 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 contrastive pairs and plot AUROC vs number of examples. Identify the 'good-enough' floor and whether activation probes are more data-efficient than fine-tuning a text classifier on the same labels.

**Why it matters:** Rapid response to new harms is a practical safety capability. A data-efficiency curve tells responders the minimum labeling effort, is pure statistics/ML on one GPU, and is reusable across concepts.

**Relevant context:** Probe data-efficiency relates to linear-probe scaling (arXiv 2604.13386). CAA contrastive-pair recipe (arXiv 2312.06681).
