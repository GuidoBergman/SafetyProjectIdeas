# Self-Report Honesty Under Pressure: When Do Agents Trained to Confess Stop Confessing?

**Problem:** One control idea is training agents to self-report misbehavior, but the boundary conditions — under what incentives a self-reporting agent stays honest vs. suppresses its confession — are unclear, and the failure mode (confident silent misbehavior) is dangerous.

**Direction:** Without training, use prompting to instantiate a 'must self-report any rule-breaking' policy on an open model, then vary the strength of a competing incentive (reward for completing a forbidden side task). Measure the self-report rate as the competing incentive grows, identifying the pressure level at which self-reporting collapses.

**Why it matters:** Self-reporting is a cheap behavioral safeguard; knowing the incentive level at which it breaks tells us when it can and cannot be relied on. A prompt-only proxy for the trained version, fully feasible in 30h with no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Training Agents to Self-Report Misbehavior (arXiv:2602.22303). Connects to behavioral monitoring open problem of distinguishing genuine alignment from strategic compliance (Anthropic recommended directions).
