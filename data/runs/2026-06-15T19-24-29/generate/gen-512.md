# Mechanistic anomaly detection that cries wolf: false-positive rates of a simple activation-anomaly detector on benign novel inputs

**Problem:** ARC's mechanistic-anomaly-detection (MAD) agenda wants to flag when a network behaves 'for unusual reasons' on novel inputs. A practical failure mode for any cheap MAD proxy is high false positives on benign distribution shift. The false-positive behavior of simple activation-distribution anomaly detectors is rarely characterized against the *benign* shift baseline.

**Direction:** On a small open model, build a simple MAD baseline (e.g., Mahalanobis distance or a density model over hidden activations fit on a 'normal' distribution). Feed it benign OOD inputs (paraphrases, new topics, formatting changes) and genuinely anomalous inputs (a planted backdoor trigger or known reward-hack input), and measure the ROC separating benign-novel from truly-anomalous. Report where the detector cries wolf.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic anomaly detection is an ARC formal open problem; quantifying the benign-shift false-positive failure is a concrete, lab-independent reality check on whether cheap activation anomaly detectors can ever be deployable.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in ARC's mechanistic-anomaly-detection agenda and OOD-detection methodology; uses an open model and standard anomaly-scoring, single GPU.
