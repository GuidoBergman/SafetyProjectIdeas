# Information-Theoretic Channel Capacity of Concept Probes

**Problem:** Probes are evaluated by accuracy, but accuracy doesn't capture how much information a concept direction actually carries about the behavior, especially for fuzzy concepts like 'deception'. Information theory measures this directly via mutual information.

**Direction:** Transfer mutual-information estimation from information theory. For each concept direction, estimate the mutual information between the scalar projection onto the direction and the ground-truth concept label across a benchmark, using a simple binning/MINE-lite estimator. Rank safety concepts by the 'channel capacity' of their linear direction. Concepts with low MI despite high apparent accuracy are warning signs of an unreliable monitor.

**Why it matters:** Provides a representation-level reliability metric for safety monitors that accuracy hides, and identifies fuzzy concepts (deception, manipulation) whose linear directions carry little real information. The fuzzy-reward evaluation this steering favors. Cheap, no training, reproducible anywhere.

**Relevant context:** Mutual information quantifies how much one variable tells about another (information theory). Surprising result: probes outperform judges (Beaglehole et al.). Open problem: which concepts have clean linear representations.
