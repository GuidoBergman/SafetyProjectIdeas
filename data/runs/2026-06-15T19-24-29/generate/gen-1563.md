# Backcasting probe-beats-judge generalization: do internal probes still win on safety categories they weren't built for?

**Problem:** Success means an internal probe is a trustworthy monitor across the full breadth of safety failures, not just the categories it was tuned on. Beaglehole et al. show probes beat judges on hallucination/toxicity, but a deployable monitor must generalize to held-out categories (e.g. self-harm, fraud, privacy). Generalization of the probe-vs-judge advantage is untested.

**Direction:** Train a single linear safety probe on activations for 2-3 harm categories on a small open model. Evaluate probe AND an LLM-judge prompt on 3-4 HELD-OUT harm categories. Compare AUROC/precision to see whether the probe's advantage persists out-of-distribution or collapses, identifying which categories transfer.

**Why it matters:** If probes only win in-distribution, the 'probes outperform judges' headline is fragile for real monitoring. Establishing the generalization boundary is high-value, lab-independent evaluation work on a diffuse problem.

**Relevant context:** Subfield surprising result: probes outperform judges (Beaglehole et al.). Probe-vs-judge AUROC numbers from hallucination-detection literature (arXiv 2603.05465; arXiv 2507.12691).
