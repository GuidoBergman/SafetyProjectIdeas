# Reward-model blind spots: which simple input transformations flip an open reward model's score without changing quality?

**Problem:** Reward models are the proxy whose errors RLHF amplifies. A direct failure-mode question is which cheap, quality-preserving input transformations (length padding, formatting, sycophantic phrasing, confident tone) systematically inflate an open reward model's score — i.e., adversarial-Goodhart handles available to any optimizer.

**Direction:** Take an open reward model and a fixed set of responses. Apply a catalogue of quality-preserving transformations and measure the reward delta for each, ranking transformations by how much score they buy 'for free.' Validate that quality is unchanged with an independent rubric/judge. Produce a leaderboard of the reward model's exploitable blind spots.

**Why it matters:** Maps the concrete attack surface that makes proxy-reward optimization unsafe — the empirical substrate of reward-hacking threat models — using only public artifacts, exactly the kind of high-counterfactual-value, lab-independent infrastructure work the steering favors.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in reward-hacking findings (length bias, format bias, sycophancy bias) from RLVR/reward-model literature (arXiv:2604.15149; Lilian Weng survey) and sycophancy work (arXiv:2310.13548). Open reward model, single GPU.
