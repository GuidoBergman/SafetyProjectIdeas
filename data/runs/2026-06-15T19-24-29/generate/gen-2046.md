# The Annotator-Fatigue Link: Does Degraded Preference Labeling Inject a Specific Bias?

**Problem:** The RLHF chain begins with human labels. A neglected link is annotator quality degradation: as labelers fatigue, they may shift toward heuristics (prefer longer, more confident, or first-presented answers). This injects systematic bias into the reward signal. The link is under-studied because labs hold the raw annotation logs, but the DIRECTION of the bias can be probed via reward models trained on public data.

**Direction:** Using public preference datasets (HH-RLHF, Stanford SHP), construct response pairs that differ only in length, confidence markers, or formatting while holding content equal. Score them with an open reward model and measure systematic preferences. This reveals which low-effort heuristics the reward signal encodes — a proxy for what fatigued annotation embeds — with no training required.

**Why it matters:** Reward-model bias from labeling shortcuts propagates into every model trained on that signal (length bias, overconfidence). Characterizing these biases on public data is exactly the kind of diffuse-reward auditing that is hard, valuable, and needs zero lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems and Fundamental Limitations of RLHF (Alignment Forum): human feedback is noisy and biased; reward models are imperfect proxies. Sharma et al. (2310.13548): preference models prefer convincingly-written answers. HH-RLHF and Stanford SHP are listed subfield datasets.
