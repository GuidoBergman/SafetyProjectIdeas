# Refusal-Direction Ablation: Replicating 'Single Direction' on 2026 Open Models

**Problem:** Arditi et al. (NeurIPS 2024) showed refusal is mediated by a single residual-stream direction in 2024-era open models. A 2026 follow-up argues there is 'more than a single direction.' Which picture holds for the newest small open instruct models is unsettled and easy to test.

**Direction:** Compute the difference-of-means refusal direction from harmful vs harmless prompt activations on 3-4 recent small open models. Measure (a) how much single-direction ablation reduces refusal and (b) how much remaining refusal survives, quantifying the 'rank' of the refusal subspace per model. Compare against the original Arditi finding to see if newer alignment training spreads refusal across more directions.

**Why it matters:** If newer models distribute refusal across multiple directions, single-direction ablation jailbreaks weaken — an emergent robustness property worth documenting. This is canonical, well-scoped mechanistic interpretability with public methods, ideal counterfactual work outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' (NeurIPS 2024) found one direction controls refusal across families/scales. 'There Is More to Refusal in Large Language Models than a Single Direction' (arXiv:2602.02132) reports four distinct refusal directions, suggesting the mechanism is more complex.
