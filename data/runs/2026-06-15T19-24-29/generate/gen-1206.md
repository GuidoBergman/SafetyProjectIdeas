# Same Tokenizer, Same Weakness? Tracing Shared Blind Spots to Shared Vocabulary

**Problem:** A mechanistic link in the homogeneity chain is 'shared tokenizer/vocabulary -> shared input-encoding quirks -> correlated failures on the same malformed inputs.' Adversarial suffixes transfer partly because of shared embedding/vocabulary statistics. Whether benign reliability blind spots also trace to shared tokenization is under-studied and very tractable.

**Direction:** Build inputs that stress tokenization (unusual unicode, glitch-token-like strings, spacing tricks). Run across models grouped by whether they share a tokenizer/vocabulary lineage. Measure whether models with shared tokenizers fail together on the same inputs more than tokenizer-distinct models. Attribute a portion of correlated failure to shared vocabulary.

**Why it matters:** Identifying a concrete mechanistic SOURCE of correlated failure (shared tokenizers) points to a precise diversity intervention. It's a clean, accessible mechanistic study needing no internal weights — just public model behavior.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in adversarial-suffix transfer via shared embeddings/vocabulary (arXiv 2510.22014, 2506.12913). Connects mechanism to Artificial Hivemind homogeneity (arXiv 2510.22954).
