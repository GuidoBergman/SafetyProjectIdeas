# Reasoning Effort as a Reward-Hacking Tell: Replicating the Effort-Discrepancy Signal

**Problem:** 'Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' proposes that genuine reasoning and cheating differ in reasoning effort. This is a promising black-box honesty signal but has been validated narrowly.

**Direction:** Build a small task set with a detectable shortcut (e.g. a leaked answer or exploitable grader). On an open model, measure a cheap reasoning-effort proxy (CoT length, number of distinct reasoning steps, self-consistency across samples) for honest vs shortcut-exploiting completions. Test whether effort discrepancy separates the two and at what threshold.

**Why it matters:** A validated, training-free effort signal lets any monitor flag likely reward-hacking without reading internals or trusting the CoT's content. Reusable monitoring infrastructure aimed at a core misalignment failure mode.

**Relevant context:** 'Is it thinking or cheating? Detecting Implicit Reward Hacking by Measuring Reasoning Effort' (arXiv:2510.01367). Reward Hacking Benchmark (arXiv:2605.02964) shows DeepSeek-R1-Zero exploits at 13.9%. Reproducible on small open models.
