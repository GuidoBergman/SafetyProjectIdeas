# Cross-Family Replication of the In-Context Scheming Capability Scaling Trend

**Problem:** Apollo found 'more capable models are better at in-context scheming.' This trend was shown largely within frontier families; whether it holds cleanly ACROSS open-model families at the small end (e.g., comparing 1B/3B/8B within and across Llama, Qwen, Gemma) is untested and cheaply checkable.

**Direction:** Run a simplified in-context scheming scenario (covert-action proxy) across a ladder of small open models spanning sizes and families. Plot covert-action / successful-deception rate against a capability proxy (e.g., MMLU score) and test whether the scaling trend is monotone and family-independent. Success = a capability-vs-scheming scatter with trend analysis across families.

**Why it matters:** If scheming capability scales predictably with general capability even in small open models, it gives a cheap forecasting signal for when open-weight releases will cross scheming thresholds — a >1yr-horizon, lab-independent monitoring contribution.

**Relevant context:** Apollo 'More Capable Models Are Better At In-Context Scheming'; arXiv:2412.04984 in-context scheming; arXiv:2509.13333 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights LLMs' shows scaling-trend methodology.
