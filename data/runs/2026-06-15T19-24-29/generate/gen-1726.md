# Fault-Injection (Chaos Engineering) Stress Tests for Circuits

**Problem:** Distributed-systems engineers find hidden dependencies by injecting controlled faults (chaos engineering). Interpretability lacks an analog: we don't know which circuits silently fail vs. fail loudly under noise.

**Direction:** Borrow chaos-engineering fault injection. Inject graded Gaussian noise into specific residual-stream subspaces or zero random neurons, then measure not just accuracy drop but whether the model fails silently (confident wrong answer) vs. loudly (high entropy / hedging). Map which components, when faulted, produce dangerous silent failures on a small reasoning task.

**Why it matters:** Silent failure modes are a key safety concern (overconfident wrong outputs). Locating components whose corruption is undetectable from outputs flags where monitoring must be internal, not behavioral, a small infrastructure-prototype contribution.

**Relevant context:** Fault injection / chaos engineering is established in reliability engineering. Mutation/fault-injection for DNNs exists (ScienceDirect S0950584922002385). The silent-vs-loud failure framing for interpretability components is novel and feasible with TransformerLens.
