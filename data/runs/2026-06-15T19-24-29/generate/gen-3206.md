# Signal Detection Theory Decomposition of CoT Monitors

**Problem:** CoT monitors that flag deceptive reasoning report a single accuracy number, conflating true sensitivity with how lenient/strict the monitor's decision threshold is. We don't know whether a monitor is genuinely discriminating deception or just trigger-happy.

**Direction:** Apply Signal Detection Theory: treat a CoT monitor (an LLM classifier) as an observer and compute d' (sensitivity) and criterion c separately by sweeping its decision threshold over MALT-style transcripts. Compare several open monitor models, build full ROC curves, and show how reported 'accuracy' hides large differences in bias. Recommend criterion settings for given base rates of scheming.

**Why it matters:** Gives monitor builders a principled, base-rate-aware way to set thresholds and compare monitors, separating real detection ability from response bias — a foundational, lab-independent contribution to AI control evaluation.

**Relevant context:** SDT/ROC separates sensitivity (d') from decision criterion; applied to polygraph lie detection (Swets 1996). MALT monitors achieved 0.87/0.78 TPR for reward hacking/sandbagging (METR 2025) but TPR alone ignores criterion. MonitorBench (arXiv:2603.28590) benchmarks CoT monitorability.
