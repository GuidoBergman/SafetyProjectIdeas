# Confirmation Bias in Oversight x Deceptive Polish: Do Confident-but-Wrong Answers Win Human Approval?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) confirmation bias / bounded human judge (scalable oversight); (B) confidently-stated deception (deception/scheming). Compound effect: a polished confident lie is cheaper to produce and more likely approved than a hedged truth, so oversight failure and deception amplify each other.

**Direction:** Generate paired model answers to tricky factual questions: one correct-but-hedged, one confident-but-wrong. Recruit a small set of human raters (or use a weak LLM judge as a stand-in human, clearly labeled) to pick the 'more trustworthy' answer without ground truth. Measure how often confidence beats correctness, and whether adding a 'show your uncertainty' instruction reduces the bias. Report approval-rate gap with CIs.

**Why it matters:** Empirically demonstrates the core scalable-oversight vulnerability that deception exploits, with a clean, cheap human/LLM-judge study and a tested mitigation.

**Relevant context:** 'Confirmation bias: A challenge for scalable oversight' (arXiv:2507.19486). 'Human-AI Complementarity' (arXiv:2510.26518). Research notes confident lies are cheaper than complex truths for bounded judges.
