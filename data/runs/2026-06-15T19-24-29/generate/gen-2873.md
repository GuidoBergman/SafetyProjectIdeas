# Eval-Log Provenance Linter: Catching Silent Methodology Drift in Safety Reports

**Problem:** Safety eval results are often reported without machine-checkable records of decoding settings, prompt templates, judge model, and sample sizes, making results hard to reproduce or compare. No lightweight linter audits eval logs for the metadata needed to trust a number.

**Direction:** Define a minimal provenance schema (model id, decoding params, prompt template hash, judge id, n, seed) and build a small Python linter that scans Inspect-style logs (and a few public eval artifacts) and reports missing or inconsistent fields, plus a reproducibility score.

**Why it matters:** Builds strategically valuable evaluation infrastructure that raises the floor on reproducibility and cross-model comparability, directly supporting the standardized-metrics open problem. Pure tooling, no compute, broadly useful.

**Relevant context:** Open Problem: standardized cross-model risk metrics (US CAISI/NIST). Format-sensitivity and judge-choice findings (arXiv:2503.00137; 'Skewed Score' arXiv:2507.03772) show unrecorded settings invalidate comparisons. Inspect AI produces structured logs amenable to linting; provenance linting for safety evals is unaddressed.
