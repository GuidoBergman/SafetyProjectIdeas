# Correlated Hallucination Index: Do Models Make the Same Things Up?

**Problem:** Success state: a fact-checking pipeline can rely on cross-model disagreement to flag hallucinations. Backward: that only works if models hallucinate independently — we need to measure whether models confabulate the SAME false facts, which would make cross-checking useless.

**Direction:** Use a public closed-book QA / fabricated-entity set where ground truth is known. Query several open models, collect wrong answers, and measure how often models give the SAME wrong answer (correlated hallucination) versus different wrong answers. Compute a correlated-hallucination index per topic domain and identify where cross-model verification is reliable vs. illusory.

**Why it matters:** Cross-model agreement is widely proposed as a hallucination detector. If homogeneous models share the same confabulations, agreement falsely signals truth — a systemic failure of the leading low-cost safety check. This directly tests a load-bearing assumption.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the correlated-errors-in-ensembles literature (arXiv 2310.10495, 2605.29800) and Artificial Hivemind homogeneity. Public QA sets and open models keep it counterfactual and feasible.
