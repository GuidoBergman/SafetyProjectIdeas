# Layer-Sweep for Deception Probes: Which Layers Generalize Best?

**Problem:** Probe work often picks a layer (or ensembles many) by in-distribution validation accuracy. Whether the layer that maximizes in-distribution AUROC is also the one that best *generalizes* to a held-out misalignment type is a distinct, practically important question that is under-examined.

**Direction:** On one open model, train deception probes at every layer. For each layer, measure in-distribution AUROC and out-of-distribution (held-out scenario) AUROC. Plot both curves and test whether the best-generalizing layer differs from the best-in-distribution layer — and whether middle layers generalize better than late layers.

**Why it matters:** Layer selection is a free knob; picking it for generalization rather than in-distribution fit could materially improve real-world anomaly detection. Single-GPU activation experiment, no training of the base model.

**Relevant context:** Linear Probe Accuracy Scales with Model Size and Benefits from Multi-Layer Ensembling (arXiv 2604.13386). Linear probes rely on textual evidence (arXiv 2509.21344) cautions probes may key on surface cues. Detecting Strategic Deception (arXiv 2502.03407).
