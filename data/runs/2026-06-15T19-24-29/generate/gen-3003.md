# Position/Verbosity Judge Bias x Pairwise Safety Comparison: Bias Decides Which Model Is 'Safer'

**Problem:** Failure mode A is positional/verbosity bias in pairwise LLM-judging. Failure mode B is pairwise safety leaderboards that rank models head-to-head. The compound effect: the judge's order/length bias, not real safety, can determine the winner, so a safety leaderboard encodes artifacts as risk verdicts.

**Direction:** Run pairwise safety comparisons between open-model responses with an LLM judge, swapping presentation order and padding lengths while holding content fixed. Quantify how often the 'safer' verdict flips with order or verbosity alone, and estimate how much of a leaderboard ranking is attributable to bias.

**Why it matters:** Pairwise judging is cheap and popular for safety/alignment comparison. Demonstrating that simple order/length manipulations flip safety verdicts argues for randomized order + length normalization as mandatory controls, and quantifies how much current rankings can't be trusted.
