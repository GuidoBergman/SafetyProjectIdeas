# Homogeneity Over Time: Are Newer Model Generations Converging or Diverging?

**Problem:** Systemic risk depends on the trajectory: 'competitive pressure + shared data + distillation -> models converge over generations.' The under-studied link is the temporal trend — is the ecosystem getting MORE homogeneous over time, raising correlated-failure risk? A snapshot misses the dynamics.

**Direction:** Select models released across a span of dates (older vs. newer open checkpoints). On a fixed probe battery, compute pairwise homogeneity within each 'era.' Test whether newer-generation models are more similar to each other than older ones were, indicating a convergence trend. Report the trajectory.

**Why it matters:** If homogeneity is rising over generations, systemic correlated-failure risk is structurally increasing — a medium-horizon threat labs underweight versus immediate gains. A simple longitudinal measurement gives an early warning signal. Uses only public dated checkpoints.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Artificial Hivemind (arXiv 2510.22954) and distillation-driven convergence findings (arXiv 2501.12619). Speaks to the International AI Safety Report 2026 systemic-risk trajectory concern.
