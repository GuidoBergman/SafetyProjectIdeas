# Noise-Injection Sandbagging Detector for the Consumer GPU

**Problem:** Noise injection into weights reveals hidden capabilities of sandbagging models (they show anomalous accuracy improvements under noise), but most demonstrations use larger setups. Whether a clean, reproducible noise-injection sandbagging detector can be built and validated entirely on a single consumer GPU with small open models is untested.

**Direction:** Take a small open model, induce sandbagging via a 'deliberately underperform on this safety eval' prompt, then add scaled Gaussian noise to a subset of weights at inference and plot accuracy-vs-noise. Compare the sandbagging condition's anomalous bump against an honest baseline's monotonic degradation. Quantify detection AUC and the minimal noise sweep needed.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging undermines all safety evaluations and labs struggle to detect imitative sandbagging without training access. A cheap, model-agnostic, weights-noise detector that hobbyists can run is exactly the kind of counterfactually-valuable, no-lab-resources tool the field needs.

**Relevant context:** Noise Injection Reveals Hidden Capabilities of Sandbagging Language Models (arxiv 2412.01784): noise injection is an effective model-agnostic sandbagging detector; sandbagging models show anomalous accuracy improvements with noise. Sandbagging detection without training access classified only 1 of 5 models correctly (Elicitation Game, arxiv 2502.02180).
