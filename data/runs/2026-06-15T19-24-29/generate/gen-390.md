# Power = Optionality: Replicating Gridworld POWER With Horizon Sweep

**Problem:** Formal POWER work shows optimal agents accumulate options and that POWER concentrates into fewer states as the planning horizon grows. This is a clean, falsifiable theoretical prediction, but reproductions outside the original paper are scarce.

**Direction:** Implement small gridworlds and compute the formal POWER metric (expected optionality / reachable-state value) for value-iteration agents. Vary ONLY the discount factor / planning horizon and measure how strongly POWER concentrates. Reproduce the 'far-sighted agents concentrate power' result and stress-test it on new gridworld topologies.

**Why it matters:** Instrumental convergence / power-seeking is a foundational safety claim. A clean, independently reproduced demonstration with a horizon dose-response curve is valuable and entirely CPU-feasible (no model, no GPU even) — maximal counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** 'On Avoiding Power-Seeking by Artificial Intelligence' (arXiv:2206.11831); LessWrong 'Instrumental convergence in single-agent systems' — POWER concentrates into fewer states as planning horizon increases; arXiv:2506.06352 'Will artificial agents pursue power by default?'.
