# Does Chain-of-Thought Change Internal Answer Commitment Timing?

**Problem:** The planning-ahead result shows models can commit to outputs internally before verbalizing. A safety-critical variant: when a model is prompted to 'think step by step,' does its internal representation of the final answer appear earlier (before the CoT) or later (genuinely after reasoning)? If the answer is decided before the CoT, the CoT is post-hoc rationalization.

**Direction:** On an open instruct model with a multiple-choice reasoning set, use the logit lens / a linear answer probe to detect when the final-answer token becomes predictable in the residual stream — comparing a no-CoT condition vs a with-CoT condition. Measure whether the answer is already decodable at the start of CoT generation. Vary problem difficulty to see if hard problems use the CoT more.

**Why it matters:** CoT faithfulness is a central question for scalable oversight: if models decide first and explain after, CoT monitoring is unreliable. A cheap interpretability probe of answer-commitment timing on open models is exactly the kind of medium-horizon faithfulness work labs deprioritize and outsiders can own.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic circuit tracing planning-ahead finding; CoT-faithfulness literature (Turpin et al. 'Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think'). Logit lens and linear answer probes are standard, inference-only.
