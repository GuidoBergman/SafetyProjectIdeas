# Mid-Generation Resumption Robustness: Comparing Reasoning vs Non-Reasoning Models

**Problem:** The prefill paper (arXiv:2602.14689) notes reasoning models resist GENERIC prefills better than non-reasoning ones, and the trajectory-vulnerability paper (arXiv:2606.04778) extends shallow alignment along generation. Whether reasoning models are also more robust to MID-generation resumption (continuing a partially-written harmful answer) than matched non-reasoning models is an open, directly testable comparison.

**Direction:** Pair a reasoning and a non-reasoning open model from the same family/size (e.g. a base instruct vs its reasoning-tuned sibling). Force-decode an identical harmful-answer prefix into both, then let them continue. Measure continuation-attack success on ~120 prompts and test whether the reasoning variant self-corrects mid-stream more often.

**Why it matters:** If reasoning provides a self-correction mechanism mid-generation, that is a positive, cheap, deployable safety property worth knowing; if not, reasoning gives a false sense of robustness. Clean controlled comparison on public weights, forward-pass-only.

**Relevant context:** Exposing the Systematic Vulnerability of Open-Weight Models to Prefill Attacks (arXiv:2602.14689, Feb 2026) — reasoning models robust to generic but not tailored prefills. Inference-Time Vulnerability Beyond Shallow Safety (arXiv:2606.04778, June 2026) — alignment along generation trajectories. Qi et al. shallow alignment (arXiv:2406.05946) is the seed.
