# Refusal-Direction Steering as a Cheap, Tunable Defense Dial

**Problem:** Success state: deployers can dial up robustness at inference time without retraining and measure the over-refusal cost. The needed result: characterize the safety/over-refusal tradeoff curve produced by ADDING (not ablating) the refusal direction to activations at varying strengths.

**Direction:** Using the known refusal direction, add it to the residual stream with a tunable coefficient at inference and sweep the coefficient, measuring StrongReject ASR (down) and XSTest over-refusal (up) at each setting to trace a steering tradeoff curve. Activation hooks only, no training.

**Why it matters:** Converts an interpretability result into a practical, free, tunable defense and quantifies its cost — directly serving 'adaptive defenses without catastrophic over-refusal' and giving open deployers a knob they fully control outside any lab stack.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. (arXiv:2406.11717): adding the refusal direction induces refusal even on harmless inputs (the over-refusal failure mode), enabling a controllable dial. SafeConstellations (arXiv:2508.11290) and category-specific refusal steering (arXiv:2603.13359) show steering is viable but the cost curve is under-characterized for a simple linear add.
