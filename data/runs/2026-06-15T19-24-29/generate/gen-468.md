# POWER-in-Practice: Measuring Empirical Power-Seeking of a Frozen LLM Policy in Gridworlds

**Problem:** Turner et al.'s formal POWER (optimal policies tend to seek power, NeurIPS 2021) is a theorem about optimal MDP policies, and InstrumentalEval (arXiv:2502.12206) measures LLM instrumental convergence in language. But no simple tool measures the formal POWER quantity for an LLM acting as a policy in small gridworlds — bridging the theorem and real models.

**Direction:** Take published toy gridworlds, compute the theoretical POWER (reachable-state / option-value measure) of each state analytically, then have a frozen open LLM act as the policy and measure how often it moves toward high-POWER states absent any reward incentive. Report correlation between formal POWER of a state and LLM visitation frequency. Success = a quantitative 'does the model gravitate to power' coefficient.

**Why it matters:** Instrumental convergence is a foundational theoretical risk argument; an empirical bridge from the POWER formalism to actual model behavior tests whether the theorem's intuition transfers to LLM agents. Gridworlds + frozen models = trivially reproducible outside any lab, medium-horizon threat model.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power' (arXiv:1912.01683), recent multi-agent POWER gridworld work (LessWrong 2025), and InstrumentalEval (arXiv:2502.12206). POWER is computable in closed form on small MDPs; only inference needed.
