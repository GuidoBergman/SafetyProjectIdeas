# Minimal Reproducible Schelling-Collusion Benchmark for Small Open Models

**Problem:** Secret collusion via Schelling points threatens control schemes that assume non-cooperation, but existing studies use frontier models. There is no lightweight, reproducible benchmark measuring Schelling-coordination ability that runs on small open models a student can deploy.

**Direction:** Build a small battery of focal-point coordination games (pick-the-same-word, covert signal in a shared task, attack/misclassify coordination) for two instances of the SAME open model with no communication channel. Measure above-chance coordination per game and per model. Release as a tiny open benchmark with a baseline leaderboard.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a key control-undermining capability cheaply and reproducibly. Coordination ability is the prerequisite for collusion attacks against monitors; measuring it at small scale grounds threat models for >1yr horizons.

**Relevant context:** LessWrong 'Schelling Coordination in LLMs: A Review' and 'Subversion via Focal Points: Investigating Collusion in LLM Monitors'. arXiv 2402.07510 (Secret Collusion among AI Agents via Steganography). arXiv 2504.05259 (How to evaluate control measures for LLM agents).
