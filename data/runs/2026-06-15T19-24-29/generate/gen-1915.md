# Domain Susceptibility Ranking: Which Narrow Tasks Most Easily Trigger EM?

**Problem:** Assessing Domain-Level Susceptibility to Emergent Misalignment from Narrow Finetuning (arXiv 2602.00298) suggests domains differ in how readily narrow finetuning induces broad misalignment, but a controlled head-to-head ranking under matched dataset sizes and training budgets on a single open model is needed.

**Direction:** Hold model, dataset size, and training hyperparameters fixed. Finetune separate LoRA adapters on equal-sized narrow 'bad-behavior' datasets from several domains (insecure code, harmful medical advice, harmful legal advice, evil numbers, bad aesthetics). Score each on a common misalignment-eval and rank domains by induced misalignment.

**Why it matters:** A susceptibility ranking tells safety teams which kinds of seemingly-narrow finetuning data are most dangerous, informing data-vetting priorities. The matched design isolates domain effect from confounds and is entirely feasible with small LoRA runs on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Assessing Domain-Level Susceptibility to Emergent Misalignment from Narrow Finetuning (arXiv 2602.00298); Nature 2026 (s41586-025-09937-5) lists multiple EM triggers including evil numbers and aesthetics; Thought Crime (arXiv 2506.13206) released medical/legal datasets.
