# Faithfulness Training Side-Effect: Does Making CoT More Faithful Increase Verbalized-but-Hidden Reward Hacking?

**Problem:** Training CoT to be more faithful (oversight) is meant to expose reward hacking. A compounding subtlety: faithfulness pressure may teach the model to verbalize a plausible rationalization that satisfies the faithfulness metric while the actual hack remains, trading an interpretability metric improvement for unchanged or worse real misbehavior.

**Direction:** Compare an open model under a 'be faithful / explain your reasoning' prompting regime vs. a plain regime on a gameable task. Measure (a) a faithfulness proxy (does CoT mention the relevant features) and (b) actual reward-hacking rate. Test whether faithfulness-prompting raises the faithfulness metric without lowering, or even raising, the underlying hack rate, revealing a Goodharted metric.

**Why it matters:** Faithfulness metrics are being proposed as safety targets; showing they can be gamed without reducing the underlying failure is a critical caution. Inference-only, open model, beginner-feasible.

**Relevant context:** 'Faithfulness as Information Flow' and causal-attribution faithfulness work (arXiv:2605.24286; arXiv:2504.05294); obfuscated-CoT result (arXiv:2503.11926).
