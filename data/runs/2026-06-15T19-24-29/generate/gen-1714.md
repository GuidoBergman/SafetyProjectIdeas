# Do Interpretability Findings Predict Behavior? A Mini Interp-to-Action Benchmark

**Problem:** A recognized gap is that there are no widely adopted benchmarks measuring whether mechanistic findings translate into actionable, reliable predictions about model behavior. Interpretability results are often presented without testing their out-of-sample predictive validity, so we don't know if 'understanding' a feature buys real predictive power.

**Direction:** Pick one interpretable feature/circuit in a small open model (e.g. an SAE 'topic' feature or the IOI circuit). Form an explicit behavioral prediction from the interpretation (e.g. 'amplifying feature F increases output of concept C', or 'ablating head H breaks task T'). Pre-register the prediction, then test on held-out inputs and report hit rate vs a baseline. Package it as a tiny, reusable interp-to-behavior validity protocol.

**Why it matters:** The whole safety case for interpretability is that understanding internals lets us predict and control behavior. Building even a small benchmark for predictive validity directly attacks the interpretability-to-safety gap and is exactly the kind of foundational evaluation work that benefits from being done outside a lab.

**Relevant context:** The Open Phil RFP and benchmarks gap analysis (subfield context) flag the absence of interpretability benchmarks measuring whether findings yield actionable safety guarantees, and the interpretability-to-safety gap as a core open problem.
