# The Many-Shot Tipping Point: Per-Category Scaling Curves on Open Models

**Problem:** Many-shot jailbreaking follows a power law in number of in-context harmful demonstrations. The causal chain (attacker injects k harmful demos -> in-context pattern overrides safety prior -> harmful completion) has an under-studied link: whether the number of shots needed to flip the model differs sharply by hazard category, which would reveal which harms are most fragile.

**Direction:** Reproduce a scaled-down many-shot attack on an open long-context model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 32k) using fabricated harmful Q&A demos drawn per AILuminate hazard category. Fit compliance-vs-shots curves separately per category to find the per-category tipping point and rank categories by fragility.

**Why it matters:** Identifies which harm categories collapse fastest under in-context pressure, an actionable risk map. Uses only an open model and synthetic demos, no training, and extends a NeurIPS finding to a comparative per-category analysis no lab has published.

**Relevant context:** Hughes et al. 'Many-shot Jailbreaking' (NeurIPS 2024) shows attack effectiveness follows a power law up to hundreds of shots and is more potent on larger models. AILuminate (MLCommons) provides 12 hazard categories for stratification.
