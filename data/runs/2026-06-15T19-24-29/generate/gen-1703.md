# Attribution Patching's False Negatives: Where the Gradient Approximation Breaks

**Problem:** Attribution patching (AtP) approximates activation patching with a first-order gradient to scale to large models, but it has documented failure modes that cause significant false negatives, missing genuinely important components. Newcomers use AtP for speed without knowing where it lies.

**Direction:** On GPT-2 small with the IOI task, compute both exact activation patching effects and AtP gradient estimates for all heads/positions. Identify components where AtP disagrees with exact patching (false negatives), and test the known failure conditions (saturated softmax / cancellation) by inspecting attention patterns at those components. Produce a 'when AtP lies' diagnostic and report the false-negative rate.

**Why it matters:** Scalable circuit discovery for safety auditing leans on attribution patching. If it systematically misses certain harmful components, audits have predictable blind spots. Mapping those conditions lets auditors know when to fall back to exact patching.

**Relevant context:** Nanda/Kramar et al., 'AtP*: An efficient and scalable method for localizing LLM behaviour to components' (arXiv:2403.00745) documents two classes of AtP failure causing significant false negatives; Heimersheim & Nanda (arXiv:2404.15255) give activation-patching best practices.
