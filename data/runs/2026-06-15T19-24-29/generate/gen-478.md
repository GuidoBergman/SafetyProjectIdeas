# ParaphraseELK: Does the Truth Direction Survive Semantic Paraphrase? A Consistency Score for Belief Probes

**Problem:** ELK probes assume a stable 'knowledge direction', and the consistency score (cosine similarity of knowledge directions across paraphrases) was proposed but is rarely the headline measurement. There is no standalone tool that ranks elicitation methods purely by how paraphrase-invariant their recovered belief direction is.

**Direction:** Fit several belief-elicitation probes (logistic regression, mass-mean, CCS-style) on an open model, then for many paraphrase clusters compute the cosine similarity between knowledge directions and aggregate into a per-method consistency score. Rank methods by invariance and check whether higher consistency predicts better out-of-distribution truth recovery.

**Why it matters:** A formal ELK solution must give the same answer regardless of surface phrasing; a method that recovers different 'truth directions' per paraphrase is not reading a belief. Turning consistency into the primary evaluation criterion sharpens what ELK methods should optimize — a pure-analysis, open-weights contribution.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in ELK evaluation metrics (Consistency Score = mean cosine similarity of knowledge directions across paraphrases; arXiv:2312.01037 / MechELK arXiv:2605.28825) and ARC's ELK agenda. No training; activation extraction + cosine similarity.
