# Underspecified-Instruction Safety Gap Replication on Open Models

**Problem:** The success state is agents that ask for clarification rather than guess dangerously when instructions are underspecified. ToolEmu showed proprietary agents take risky shortcuts under ambiguity; whether cheap open models are worse is unknown.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'agents never take harmful actions to resolve ambiguity'. Use ToolEmu's benign-but-underspecified scenarios and run several open-weights models, measuring the rate of risky resolution (acting on a dangerous default) vs. safe resolution (asking/clarifying). Compare across model sizes to see if smaller models are disproportionately reckless.

**Why it matters:** Underspecification is the most common real-world failure mode and disproportionately affects the cheap open models that hobbyists and small orgs actually deploy — a population frontier labs don't study. Pure inference, open benchmark.

**Relevant context:** ToolEmu (LM-simulated tools, 36 high-stakes tools, 144 cases) focuses on benign-but-underspecified instructions where safety constraints are omitted. SafePro (arXiv 2601.06663) and ClawsBench (arXiv 2604.05172) extend professional-agent safety evaluation.
