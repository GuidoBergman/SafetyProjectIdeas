# Cohen's Kappa Error-Propagation Model for Stacked Oversight

**Problem:** When weak overseers supervise stronger ones recursively, individual judge error rates compound, but the field lacks a simple quantitative model of how reliability degrades up the stack.

**Direction:** Borrow inter-rater reliability statistics (Cohen's/Fleiss kappa) and epidemiological error-propagation math. Empirically estimate per-layer judge agreement-with-truth on a debate dataset, then build a small simulation/analytic model predicting end-to-end oversight accuracy as a function of layer count, per-layer kappa, and correlated-error assumptions. Validate predictions against a 2-layer empirical setup.

**Why it matters:** Gives oversight designers an analytic tool for 'how many layers before reliability collapses', directly informing recursive-oversight feasibility, a long-horizon question with no lab dependency.

**Relevant context:** Cohen's kappa adjusts for chance agreement and is used for LLM-judge vs consensus reliability (arXiv:2412.12509; ResearchGate kappa statistic). Diagnostic-test error propagation in epidemiology. Recursive oversight open problem (Anthropic directions).
