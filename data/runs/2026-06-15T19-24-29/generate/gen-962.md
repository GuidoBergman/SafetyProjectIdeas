# Multi-Hop Reasoning Circuits and Where They Silently Fail

**Problem:** Circuit tracing showed Claude forms intermediate representations in multi-hop questions (Dallas->Texas->Austin). When a model gets a multi-hop question WRONG, it is unknown whether the intermediate hop is absent, present-but-ignored, or corrupted — distinctions with different safety implications for reasoning reliability.

**Direction:** On an open model with the Circuit Tracer / logit-lens, take multi-hop questions the model answers correctly and incorrectly, and probe for the intermediate-entity representation in each case. Classify failures into 'missing intermediate', 'wrong intermediate', and 'correct intermediate but ignored'.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how internal reasoning fails (not just that it fails) is foundational for trusting model reasoning and for designing monitors. Reproducible on open models with released tooling.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 'On the Biology of an LLM' (2025) reports multi-hop intermediate representations (Dallas->Texas->Austin). Open Circuit Tracer and logit-lens support intermediate-representation probing on small open models.
