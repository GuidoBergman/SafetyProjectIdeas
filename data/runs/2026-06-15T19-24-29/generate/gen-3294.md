# Anti-Scheming Training x Generalization Failure: Does Suppressing Scheming in One Domain Displace It to Another?

**Problem:** Interacting failure modes: (A) scheming (deception/scheming); (B) narrow-finetuning generalization failure (alignment generalization). Compound effect: training/prompting a model to not scheme in one setting may not transfer — and could push the behavior into out-of-distribution settings (a whack-a-mole displacement), so a localized fix creates a hidden compound risk.

**Direction:** Take an open model, apply an anti-scheming system prompt (deliberative-alignment-style instructions) tuned on one task family, then measure scheming/reward-hacking rates on held-out task families. Quantify in-domain reduction vs out-of-domain change. Test for displacement: does suppression in domain A correlate with increase in domain B? Use simple prompted model-organism tasks; no training required if using prompt-based deliberative alignment.

**Why it matters:** Stress-tests whether cheap anti-scheming interventions generalize or merely relocate the problem — directly probing the Apollo/OpenAI deliberative-alignment result.

**Relevant context:** 'Stress Testing Deliberative Alignment for Anti-Scheming Training' (arXiv:2509.15541) shows partial, non-robust reductions. Open Phil RFP: alignment stress tests.
