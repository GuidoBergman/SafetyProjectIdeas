# Cryptographic Commitment Schemes to Test Pre-Commitment Honesty

**Problem:** Decision-theoretic safety stories sometimes rely on agents honoring prior commitments, but we don't know empirically whether LLM agents respect commitments when defecting later becomes advantageous.

**Direction:** Borrow commitment schemes from cryptography as an experimental scaffold. Have an open-model agent cryptographically commit (hash) to a plan/answer, then later present it with a tempting opportunity to deviate, and check (by revealing the commitment) how often it honors vs. silently changes its earlier commitment. Vary the size of the temptation and the salience of the commitment.

**Why it matters:** Provides a clean, tamper-evident behavioral test of commitment-honoring — relevant to decision-theoretic cooperation and to deceptive-defection threat models — with a fully reproducible, training-free setup buildable by a small team.

**Relevant context:** Cryptographic commitment schemes (commit-then-reveal) guarantee tamper-evidence. Commitment/cooperation is studied in formal decision theory (open-source game theory, Demski/Garrabrant). Connects to deceptive-alignment model-organism interest.
