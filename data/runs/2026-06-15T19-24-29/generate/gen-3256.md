# Self-Report Calibration: Does 'Are You Sandbagging?' Ever Work?

**Problem:** A trivially cheap candidate intervention at the sandbagging-detection link is simply asking the model whether it is underperforming. The chain 'model hides capability -> auditor asks -> model (mis)reports' is rarely measured: does direct self-report carry ANY signal, or is it pure noise/denial?

**Direction:** Induce prompted sandbagging on an open model, then probe with direct and indirect self-report questions ('did you give your best answer?', 'rate your confidence', 'on a scale, how hard did you try?'). Compare self-reports between honest and sandbagging conditions to test whether any self-report variable separates the two.

**Why it matters:** If even indirect self-report leaks sandbagging, it is the cheapest possible audit; if not, it cleanly documents that self-report is untrustworthy — both useful, both lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging surveys (arXiv:2406.07358) describe deliberate underperformance; noise-injection work (arXiv:2412.01784) targets the same link via weights; honesty-verification is an open UK AISI problem.
