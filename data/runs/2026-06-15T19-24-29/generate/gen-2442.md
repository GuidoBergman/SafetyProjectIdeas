# Cheap Diverse-Monitor Ensembles for Hobbyist Control Setups

**Problem:** Recent work shows a diverse ensemble of small monitors beats a single costly monitor for catching backdoors, but the result was demonstrated mostly with fine-tuned monitors and substantial compute. It is unclear whether the diversity benefit survives when you can only vary cheap, training-free knobs (prompt persona, temperature, model family) on a consumer budget.

**Direction:** Using an existing open backdoor/sabotage transcript set (e.g. SHADE-Arena or ControlArena samples), build several training-free 'diverse' monitors that differ only in system-prompt persona, decoding temperature, and base model (e.g. two small open models via API/Colab). Measure pairwise score correlation on attack samples and whether averaging 2-4 diverse monitors raises detection AUC over re-sampling one monitor at matched compute. Report the diversity-vs-scale tradeoff achievable with zero fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** Confirms whether the 'diversity beats scale' finding is reproducible by small actors with no training infrastructure, making robust monitoring accessible outside frontier labs. A positive result gives the community a cheap recipe; a negative one flags that the benefit depends on lab-only fine-tuning diversity.

**Relevant context:** Ensemble Monitoring for AI Control (arxiv 2605.15377): a diverse ensemble of small monitors outperforms a single costly monitor; best 3-monitor ensemble gives 2.4x greater detection gain at equal compute; diverse monitors' biases average out while re-sampling one only reduces random noise; fine-tuning was a strong diversity source. SHADE-Arena (arxiv 2506.15740) provides open transcripts; top monitor AUC 0.87.
