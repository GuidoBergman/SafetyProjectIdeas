# Catastrophic Goodhart: When KL Regularization Fails to Hold the Reward-Hacking Link

**Problem:** The reward-hacking chain: imperfect proxy reward -> policy over-optimizes -> diverges from true preference. KL-to-reference regularization is the standard intervention on this link. But 'Catastrophic Goodhart' argues KL does NOT mitigate heavy-tailed reward misspecification. This is under-tested empirically in a small, reproducible RLHF-lite setting.

**Direction:** In a tiny controllable best-of-n / reward-optimization setup with an open reward model treated as proxy and a stronger model (or a held-out scorer) as gold, sweep KL budget and observe gold reward vs. proxy reward. Inject heavy-tailed reward errors and test whether higher KL fails to prevent gold-reward collapse, empirically probing the regularization link.

**Why it matters:** KL regularization is the field's main defense against reward overoptimization. If it provably fails under heavy-tailed misspecification, alignment pipelines relying on it have a blind spot. A small best-of-n demonstration (no RL training needed) makes the theory concrete and accessible.

**Relevant context:** Gao et al. 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization' (2210.10760): proxy reward diverges from gold as optimization increases, studied via best-of-n and RL. 'Catastrophic Goodhart' (arxiv 2407.14503): KL regularization does not mitigate heavy-tailed reward misspecification.
