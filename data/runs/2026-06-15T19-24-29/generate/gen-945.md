# Agentic Deployment x Static Pre-Deployment Evals: The Capability a One-Shot Eval Can't See

**Problem:** Failure mode A: governance certifies models with static, single-turn pre-deployment evals. Failure mode B: agentic/tool-use deployment composes capabilities over many steps, producing emergent dangerous behavior absent in single-shot tests. The two compound: a model certified safe one-shot becomes dangerous when wired into an agent loop, and no regulatory step re-checks it.

**Direction:** On a small open model, compare its single-turn score on a safety-relevant task against its success when given a simple multi-step scaffold/tool loop (e.g. retrieval + iteration) on the same goal. Measure the capability uplift from composition. Argue that pre-deployment certification must include an agentic-scaffold allowance, and quantify how big that allowance should be.

**Why it matters:** Shows a structural gap between how models are certified and how they're deployed, informing whether governance should regulate the deployed agent system, not just the base model. Single GPU, public model, cheap scaffolding — a medium-horizon threat labs underweight.
