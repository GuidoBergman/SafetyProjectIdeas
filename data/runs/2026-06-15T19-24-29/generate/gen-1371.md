# Replicating probe-beats-judge under domain shift

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. showed internal probes beat judges in-distribution. The safety-relevant question is whether that advantage survives DOMAIN SHIFT (probe trained on one data distribution, tested on another), which is when real monitors degrade.

**Direction:** Train a hallucination/toxicity probe on one dataset/domain and evaluate it on a different domain's held-out set, comparing the AUC drop to an LLM-judge's drop on the same shift. Report whether the probe's advantage shrinks, holds, or reverses under shift across 2-3 shift pairs.

**Why it matters:** Monitors are deployed on data unlike their training set; knowing whether cheap probes stay ahead of judges under shift guides external monitoring choices. No training of base models, modest compute.

**Relevant context:** Surprising result: probes outperform judges (Beaglehole et al.). OOD brittleness from arXiv:2407.12404. Multiple public toxicity/hallucination datasets enable shift pairs.
