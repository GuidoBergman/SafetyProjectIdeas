# Over-Refusal Steering x Helpful-Harm Tradeoff: Measuring the Hidden Capability Tax of Safety Steering

**Problem:** Failure mode A (concept_steering): pushing the refusal direction to harden safety. Failure mode B (over-refusal / capability suppression): the model refuses benign requests and loses task capability. Compound risk: hardening safety via steering and capability loss are not independent — beyond a point, each unit of jailbreak resistance bought costs a super-linear amount of benign helpfulness.

**Direction:** Sweep refusal-steering strength on an open model. At each level, jointly measure jailbreak resistance (harmful set) and over-refusal + accuracy on a benign capability set. Plot the Pareto frontier and identify the knee where benign cost accelerates. Quantify the 'exchange rate' between safety gain and capability loss.

**Why it matters:** Gives deployers a concrete dose-response curve for safety steering, exposing where it becomes counterproductive. Decision-relevant, cheap, and tests a clean two-failure tradeoff.

**Relevant context:** Over-refusal collateral cost documented in arXiv 2603.24543 and 2602.06256. Capability-preservation-below-5% claim from arXiv 2602.04896 gives a baseline to test against at higher strengths.
