# Time-Horizon Estimation Replicated Across Task Domains on One Open Agent

**Problem:** METR found time horizons differ by domain (software vs scientific QA vs math). This domain variation was measured on frontier models. Whether a single small open agent shows the same relative domain ordering of time horizons is unknown.

**Direction:** Replicate the domain-variation time-horizon analysis on one small open agent across 3 cheap, auto-checkable task domains (short coding, short math, short factual lookup). Estimate per-domain 50% horizons and compare the ordering to METR's frontier-model ordering. The twist: cross-domain replication on a single open model at small scale.

**Why it matters:** If the domain ordering of autonomy is consistent from small open models to frontier models, independent researchers can cheaply forecast which agentic domains will become dangerous first — a long-horizon agentic-safety concern labs underinvest in for non-software domains.

**Relevant context:** METR 'How Does Time Horizon Vary Across Domains?' (metr.org, 2025-07-14) — intellectual domains have 50-200 min horizons doubling every 2-6 months. 'Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks' (metr.org).
