# Does Calibration Survive Reasoning? Tracking Coherence Before vs After Chain-of-Thought

**Problem:** Formal accounts treat reasoning as belief-refinement that should preserve or improve calibration/coherence. Empirically it is unclear whether chain-of-thought makes an LLM's probability judgments more coherent (as theory of good reasoners predicts) or introduces new incoherence.

**Direction:** On a set of propositions with logical structure, elicit probabilities from an open model with and without chain-of-thought. Compute calibration (vs ground truth) and internal-coherence metrics in both conditions. Test whether CoT improves calibration but worsens coherence, or vice versa, and whether 'internal consistency' of the reasoning predicts correctness.

**Why it matters:** Bears on whether scaling reasoning moves models toward idealized coherent agents (relevant to agent-foundations assumptions) or away from them. Inference-only, directly testable, and connects formal desiderata to behavior.

**Relevant context:** 'Calibrating Reasoning in Language Models with Internal Consistency' (arXiv 2405.18711); Logical Induction's coherence/calibration desiderata (intelligence.org).
