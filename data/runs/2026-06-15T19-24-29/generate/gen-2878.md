# Mapping the Answer-Order Fragility Surface of Safety MCQs

**Problem:** Shuffling MMLU answer-option contents drops accuracy by up to 27% and reorders leaderboards by up to eight positions. Safety multiple-choice benchmarks (e.g. WMDP-style hazardous-knowledge MCQs) are scored the same way, but it is unknown whether safety-relevant items are equally or more order-fragile than generic knowledge items.

**Direction:** Take an open safety/hazardous-knowledge MCQ set and a matched general-knowledge set. For several small open models, evaluate every permutation (or a large random sample) of answer orders per item, compute per-item accuracy variance and the 'flip rate', and test whether safety items are systematically more fragile. Characterize which prompt/scoring choices (letter-label vs content matching, log-prob vs generation) drive the instability.

**Why it matters:** If a model's apparent dangerous-knowledge score swings 25% just by reordering options, dangerous-capability gating decisions built on those scores are unreliable. Quantifying the fragility of safety MCQs tells regulators and evaluators how much to trust a single reported number.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2406.19470 (changing answer order decreases MMLU accuracy, up to 27.2% drop), arXiv:2308.11483 (option-order sensitivity, up to 75% fluctuation), and 'When Benchmarks are Targets' (reordering shifts leaderboard rank by 8). WMDP-style MCQs are open and runnable on one GPU.
