# Distillation as a Robust Unlearning Test: Does Knowledge Survive a Cheap Student?

**Problem:** Success state: we know whether distilling an unlearned teacher into a fresh student genuinely removes dangerous knowledge or whether residual signal transfers. 'Distillation Robustifies Unlearning' claims distillation helps; we need an independent, small-scale check.

**Direction:** Unlearn a target capability in a small open teacher, distill into a randomly-initialized small student on benign data only, then run relearning attacks on both teacher and student. Compare relearning fragility to test whether distillation actually removes vs. suppresses knowledge.

**Why it matters:** If distillation is a cheap, robust unlearning amplifier, it is a deployable safety tool for the open community; if residual knowledge transfers, that is an important negative result. Single-GPU scale.

**Relevant context:** 'Distillation Robustifies Unlearning' (arXiv:2506.06278) argues distillation converts suppression into removal. Builds on relearning-attack literature (Fan et al. 2502.05374).
