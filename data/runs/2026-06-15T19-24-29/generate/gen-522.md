# Calibration Scoring Rules from Forecasting Applied to Logical/Mathematical Claims

**Problem:** Logical induction theorizes about reasoners that are well-calibrated over logical/mathematical sentences they cannot yet prove, but there is little empirical measurement of how calibrated current models are on the truth of unproven formal conjectures.

**Direction:** Borrow proper scoring rules (Brier, log score) and calibration plots from the forecasting/prediction-market literature. Collect formal/math statements with known truth values (theorems, false 'plausible' statements, decidable arithmetic facts of varying hardness), elicit probability estimates from an open model without letting it compute the answer, and produce calibration curves stratified by deductive difficulty.

**Why it matters:** Operationalizes the central empirical question behind logical induction — does a real model approximate calibration on logically-determined-but-not-yet-deduced facts — using a 30h experiment and no training. A concrete bridge between MIRI-style theory and measurement.

**Relevant context:** Logical induction (Garrabrant et al., arXiv:1707.08747) guarantees calibration on subsequences where truth-frequency converges and resists efficient Dutch-booking. Proper scoring rules are standard in forecasting/prediction markets.
