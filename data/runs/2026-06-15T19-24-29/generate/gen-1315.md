# Mode Collapse Toward the Majority: Auditing Minority-Perspective Suppression Across Models

**Problem:** 'The Homogenization Problem in LLMs' argues mode collapse concentrates on dominant modes and attenuates minoritized ones, and that it's baked into post-training (not fixable at inference). A cross-model audit measuring whether MULTIPLE models suppress the SAME minority perspectives/representations identically would quantify the systemic dimension.

**Direction:** Use prompts known to surface representational diversity (occupations, family structures, cultural practices, contested viewpoints). Sample many completions across ~6 models. Measure how often each model surfaces minority modes and whether the SAME modes are suppressed across all models (shared suppression) vs. model-specific. Compare to a real-world reference distribution where available.

**Why it matters:** If all models erase the same minorities identically, the harm is systemic and invisible — no single-model audit reveals it. Quantifying shared suppression is exactly the diffuse, fuzzy-reward safety work that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'The Homogenization Problem in LLMs: Towards Meaningful Diversity in AI Safety' (arXiv:2601.06116, Jan 2026) to a cross-model SHARED-suppression measurement.
