# Replicating Dangerous-Capability Refusal Evals Across Model Sizes Within One Open Family to Map Scaling of Safety

**Problem:** Frontier safety frameworks (RSP/FSF) tie mitigations to capability thresholds, assuming dangerous-capability evals scale meaningfully with model capability. But the relationship between scale and refusal/dangerous-capability behavior is usually compared across heterogeneous vendors, confounding architecture and training. A clean within-family scaling study is rarely published openly.

**Direction:** Take a published dangerous-capability or refusal eval subset and replicate it across multiple sizes of a SINGLE open model family (e.g., 1B/3B/8B/70B-quantized of one release). The twist is holding training/architecture fixed and varying only scale, reporting how refusal robustness and (proxy) dangerous-capability scores change monotonically or not with size.

**Why it matters:** Capability-threshold governance assumes predictable scaling of risk; a clean within-family curve tests that premise empirically. Uses open models on a single GPU (smaller sizes + quantized large), no training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in METR common elements / RSP / FSF capability-threshold logic and HELM Safety eval components. Within-family scaling is the methodological twist; 'Measurement challenges in AI catastrophic risk governance' (arXiv 2410.00608) motivates measurement scrutiny.
