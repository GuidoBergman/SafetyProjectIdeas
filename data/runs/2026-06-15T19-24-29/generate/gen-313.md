# Where Does the GCG Suffix Act? Layer-Localizing the Adversarial-Suffix Link

**Problem:** GCG suffixes break the chain (harmful request -> refusal direction activated -> refusal emitted) by suppressing the refusal. The under-studied link is WHERE: which layers' activations does a transferable suffix most strongly perturb, and is it the same locus across open models that would explain transfer?

**Direction:** Using published GCG suffixes (AmpleGCG artifacts) on open models, compute the cosine similarity between activations and the known 'refusal direction' layer-by-layer, with vs without the suffix. Identify the layers where the suffix most reduces refusal-direction alignment and test whether that locus is shared across model pairs that show transfer.

**Why it matters:** Connects two surprising results (refusal-direction encoding + suffix transfer) by localizing the causal intervention point, informing where latent defenses should monitor. Uses public suffixes and open weights, standard activation extraction, no training; a tractable mechanistic study for a first-timer.

**Relevant context:** Zou et al. (arXiv:2307.15043) introduce transferable GCG suffixes; AmpleGCG (github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/AmpleGCG, arXiv:2410.22143) releases millions of suffixes. Refusal-direction work shows jailbreaks inhibit the internal refusal direction (cosine similarity declines), per 2025 probing literature (arXiv:2509.15202).
