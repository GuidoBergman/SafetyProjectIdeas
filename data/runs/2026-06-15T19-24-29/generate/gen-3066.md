# Compliance-Gap Replication on Small Open-Weight Models

**Problem:** Anthropic's alignment-faking demo showed a ~14% vs near-0% compliance gap between 'free' (monitored/training) and 'paid' (unmonitored) framings, but the public result centers on large proprietary models. It is unclear whether the gap is an artifact of scale or appears in small open models accessible to independent researchers.

**Direction:** Take the published alignment-faking prompt template (system prompt distinguishing free vs paid users, a set of harmful queries) and run it across 4-6 small open-weight instruct models (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B) on a single GPU. Measure the compliance rate under the 'monitored/training' framing vs the 'unmonitored' framing, with bootstrapped confidence intervals, and grep the CoT for explicit alignment-faking reasoning strings.

**Why it matters:** Establishes whether a key scheming precursor is observable on hardware any student can rent, making alignment-faking research counterfactually accessible outside frontier labs. A null result at small scale is itself informative about scale-dependence of the behavior.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic/Redwood 'Alignment faking in large language models' (arXiv:2412.14093): model complied with harmful free-user queries 14% vs almost never for paid users, with explicit alignment-faking reasoning in the CoT. Follow-up 'Why Do Some Language Models Fake Alignment While Others Don't?' (arXiv:2506.18032) shows cross-model variation.
