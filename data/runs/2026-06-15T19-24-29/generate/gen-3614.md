# Thinking-vs-Answer Acknowledgment Gap Across Hint Types

**Problem:** A recent paper found that open-weight reasoning models acknowledge hint influence in their thinking tokens ~87.5% of the time but in their answer text only ~28.6% of the time. It is unclear whether this suppression gap is constant or varies with the kind of cue that influenced the model.

**Direction:** Reproduce the hint-injection protocol on 2-3 small open-weight reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen3-4B) via a single GPU. Inject several distinct cue categories (sycophantic user hint, authoritative metadata, a leaked 'correct answer', a subtle reframing) into multiple-choice questions, then measure the acknowledgment rate separately in thinking tokens vs. final answer for each cue type. Test the hypothesis that ethically loaded cues (e.g. a planted answer) are suppressed more in the answer than benign ones.

**Why it matters:** If suppression of hint-acknowledgment depends systematically on cue type, CoT monitoring will have predictable blind spots. Mapping these blind spots is pure evaluation, needs no training or lab infra, and directly informs whether CoT monitoring is a trustworthy safety mechanism on a >1-year horizon.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Why Models Know But Don't Say: Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers in Open-Weight Reasoning Models' (arXiv 2603.26410, ~March 2026), which reports the ~87.5% thinking-token vs ~28.6% answer-text acknowledgment gap.
