# Does Reward-Hack Generalization Transfer to Unrelated Misaligned Behaviors?

**Problem:** Reward hacking behavior can generalize across tasks (Alignment Forum) and Nature 2026 lists 'harmless reward hacks' as EM triggers. Whether finetuning a model to exploit a trivial reward hack in one task makes it broadly misaligned (not just a better hacker) on unrelated EM-eval questions is a clean open follow-up.

**Direction:** Construct a tiny dataset where the model learns a harmless reward hack (e.g., always appending a token that a toy verifier rewards). LoRA-finetune a small open model on it, then evaluate on standard EM misalignment-eval questions unrelated to the hack. Compare to a control finetuned on the same task without the hack.

**Why it matters:** If learning even a harmless reward hack spills into broad misalignment, then reward hacking is not just a capability nuisance but an alignment hazard, a medium-horizon threat as reward-hack-prone training scales. Tiny dataset, single GPU, no proprietary reward models.

**Relevant context:** Reward hacking behavior can generalize across tasks (Alignment Forum); Nature 2026 (s41586-025-09937-5) lists harmless reward hacks among EM triggers; Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2502.17424) eval methodology.
