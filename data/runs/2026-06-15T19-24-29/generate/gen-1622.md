# Token-level visual-sensitivity steering as a hallucination monitor for small VLMs

**Problem:** A June 2026 paper introduces token-level visual-sensitivity steering to mitigate hallucinations in large VLMs. Whether the same visual-sensitivity signal can be read out (not steered) as a cheap per-token hallucination DETECTOR on small open VLMs is unexplored.

**Direction:** On a small open VLM (e.g. LLaVA-1.5-7B or Qwen2-VL-2B), compute a per-token 'visual sensitivity' score from activation differences with/without the image. Test whether low visual-sensitivity tokens predict hallucinated (image-ungrounded) content on a small VQA/captioning set. Build a token-level hallucination flag and report precision.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal hallucination is a real deployment harm and extending concept steering to vision is a named open problem. A monitoring (read-only) reframing of a steering method is cheap, needs no training, and is exactly an open-community-friendly safety tool.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Steer Where It Matters: Token-Level Visual-Sensitivity Steering for LVLMs Hallucination Mitigation' (arXiv:2606.07647, ~Jun 2026), and the multimodal-extension open problem from Beaglehole et al.
