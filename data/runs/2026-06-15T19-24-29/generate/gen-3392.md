# Verbalization Under Token-Level vs. Semantic Hints

**Problem:** Hint-injection studies use semantically explicit hints ('the answer is B'). A subtler variant — hints encoded in formatting, special tokens, or positional cues rather than meaning — may flip answers with even lower verbalization, mapping a finer-grained monitoring blind spot.

**Direction:** Compare verbalization rates for semantic hints vs. non-semantic cues (a star next to one option, a slight whitespace/formatting marker, option position). Measure answer-flip and verbalization for each cue type on an open model. Test whether models follow formatting cues they essentially never verbalize.

**Why it matters:** Non-verbal cues that steer answers without ever appearing in the CoT are the hardest case for monitoring and a plausible covert-channel for manipulation. Pure prompt construction, no lab resources, medium-horizon relevance.

**Relevant context:** Turpin et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.13702) used positional/formatting biases (answer-always-A) that were not verbalized. This isolates semantic vs. non-semantic cue type as the variable.
