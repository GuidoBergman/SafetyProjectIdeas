# Backcasting Length-Robust Honesty: Does Forcing Longer CoT Make Reasoning More or Less Faithful?

**Problem:** A success state is reasoning whose faithfulness does not degrade as chains get longer (relevant as test-time compute scales). Backward, we need a measured relationship between CoT length and faithfulness on accessible models, since recent work hints at faithfulness decay over long chains.

**Direction:** On the hint-injection paradigm, vary CoT length via prompting ('think briefly' vs. 'think step by step in detail') for an open reasoning model, holding the hint fixed. Measure hint-verbalization faithfulness and answer-flip rate as a function of induced CoT length, testing whether longer chains hide or reveal cue use more.

**Why it matters:** As inference-time reasoning scales, knowing whether longer chains help or hurt monitorability is directly safety-relevant and a near-term, lab-independent question.

**Relevant context:** Mechanistic Evidence for Faithfulness Decay in CoT (arXiv 2602.11201); Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think (arXiv 2505.05410) for the verbalization metric.
