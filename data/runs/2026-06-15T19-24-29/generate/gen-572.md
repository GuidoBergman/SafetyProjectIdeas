# Backcasting an ELK Success Criterion: How Brittle Is the Quirky-Model Linear Probe Win?

**Problem:** A success state for ELK is: we can read a model's latent belief about a fact even when it outputs the opposite. The quirky-models result shows middle-layer linear probes recover ~89% of the truthful/untruthful AUROC gap. But for that to count as a real building block, the probe must survive surface perturbations a deployed deceiver could trivially apply.

**Direction:** Take an open quirky-models checkpoint (EleutherAI release) and the trained truth probe. Hold the probe fixed and stress-test it against cheap, training-free perturbations of the Bob/Alice prompts: paraphrases, added distractor sentences, translated keywords, formatting noise. Measure AUROC degradation per perturbation class and identify which destroy the probe. No training required — only inference and logistic-regression probing.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'ELK works in deployment' demands robustness, not just clean-benchmark accuracy. Quantifying which trivial perturbations break the canonical probe tells the field whether the headline result is a foundation or a fragile artifact — and this requires only an open model, no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** ELK from Quirky Language Models (arXiv:2312.01037): logistic regression on contrast pairs recovers 89% of the AUROC gap, mechanistic anomaly detection ~0.95 AUROC. EleutherAI quirky-models checkpoints are public.
