# Splitting CoT-faithfulness: does an activation probe and the verbalized CoT agree on the model's answer?

**Problem:** The honesty subfield asks whether chain-of-thought faithfully reflects internal computation. A tractable sub-problem from interpretability: on a reasoning task, does a linear probe reading the answer off mid-layer activations BEFORE the model writes its CoT agree with the final stated answer — and when they disagree, is the CoT post-hoc rationalization?

**Direction:** Decompose 'is CoT faithful' into a measurable probe-vs-text agreement test. On a small open reasoning model and a multiple-choice dataset, train a probe to predict the final answer from activations at the end of the prompt (pre-CoT). Then generate the CoT. Measure cases where the pre-CoT probe already 'knew' the answer but the CoT presents elaborate reasoning — quantify the rationalization rate.

**Why it matters:** Directly probes whether CoT monitoring (a key control method) can be trusted. Fully open-source, single GPU, no training of the base model. Rationalization-rate is a crisp, communicable safety metric and a counterfactual contribution since labs rarely publish on open models.
