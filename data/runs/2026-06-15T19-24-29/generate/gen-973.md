# Extending Bench-2-CoP: Where Do Loss-of-Control Capabilities Have Zero Benchmark Coverage?

**Problem:** Bench-2-CoP found that loss-of-control-relevant capabilities (evading oversight, self-replication, autonomous AI development) receive ZERO coverage in a 195k-question benchmark corpus, while 92% of regulatory-relevant questions cluster on hallucination and reliability. The paper maps existing benchmarks but does not characterize WHAT a minimal benchmark for the zero-coverage categories would even look like.

**Direction:** Take the EU AI Act systemic-risk capability taxonomy, isolate the zero-coverage cells, and for each construct a small (20-50 item) proof-of-concept probe set drawn from public agentic eval tasks, scoring an open model on them. Report whether the zero-coverage gap is a definitional/measurement problem vs. a genuine absence of any usable task format.

**Why it matters:** Directly attacks the regulatory blind spot on the highest-stakes (>1yr horizon) capabilities that frontier labs deprioritize for near-term harms. Replicable LLM-as-judge methodology, single GPU, no proprietary data.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Bench-2-CoP: Can We Trust Benchmarking for EU AI Compliance?' (arXiv:2508.05464), which maps 194,955 benchmark questions to the EU AI Act taxonomy and finds zero coverage of loss-of-control capabilities.
