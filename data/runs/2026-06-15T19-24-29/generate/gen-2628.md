# Signal Detection Theory (d-prime) to Disentangle Monitor Sensitivity from Bias

**Problem:** When a monitor catches more sabotage, is it genuinely more discriminating or just more trigger-happy (raising false alarms too)? Accuracy and catch-rate conflate true sensitivity with response bias.

**Direction:** Import Signal Detection Theory from psychophysics. For each monitor/prompt, compute d-prime (discriminability) and criterion c (response bias) from hit and false-alarm rates on benign/sabotage trajectories, then rank monitors and prompting strategies by true sensitivity independent of how cautious they were set. Test whether common 'be suspicious' prompt tweaks improve d-prime or merely shift criterion.

**Why it matters:** Cleanly separates whether a monitor improvement is real discrimination or just a threshold shift, preventing illusory progress in monitor design. Trivial computation on existing benchmark outputs, no compute, sharpens the field's evaluation hygiene.

**Relevant context:** Signal Detection Theory's d-prime/criterion separate sensitivity from bias (Green & Swets). Control monitors are tuned with ad hoc 'be suspicious' prompts; CoT monitoring informativeness is debated (GPT-5 system card). Maps directly onto monitor ROC analysis.
