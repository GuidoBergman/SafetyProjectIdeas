# The Attacker-Realism Link: Do Synthetic Jailbreaks Predict Realistic-Prompt Success?

**Problem:** Anthropic flags that jailbreak benchmarks use synthetic test cases unlike real attacker behavior. The causal claim 'high benchmark ASR -> high real-world risk' has a weak link: synthetic and realistic prompts may exploit different model weaknesses, so benchmark rankings may not transfer.

**Direction:** Pair each synthetic StrongREJECT-style harmful prompt with a hand-crafted realistic-attacker rewrite (plausible social-engineering framing). Run both on open models and measure whether per-prompt and per-jailbreak success rates correlate between synthetic and realistic versions, quantifying how predictive synthetic benchmarks are of realistic attacks.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses Anthropic's open problem on benchmark realism by testing the synthetic-to-real predictive link. Pure evaluation with open models, no training; produces a correlation analysis that tells the field how much to trust existing benchmark numbers.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic recommended directions call for jailbreak benchmarks reflecting actual attacker capabilities rather than synthetic test cases. StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260) provides the synthetic baseline; the synthetic-vs-realistic predictive gap is unmeasured.
