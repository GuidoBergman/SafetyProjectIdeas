# Cross-Lingual Stability of Safety Circuits in Open Models

**Problem:** Safety features and refusal circuits are almost always studied in English. Whether the same refusal/harm feature fires for semantically equivalent harmful prompts in other languages — or whether non-English inputs route around the safety circuit — is mechanistically under-explored on open multilingual models.

**Direction:** Locate a refusal/harm feature (probe or SAE) on an open multilingual model using English prompts, then test whether it activates for translated equivalents in 2-3 other languages. Quantify the activation gap and check whether successful multilingual jailbreaks correspond to feature non-activation.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual safety gaps are a real deployment risk and labs under-resource non-English safety. A mechanistic (not just behavioral) account is novel, lab-independent, and uses only open multilingual weights.

**Relevant context:** Multilingual open models (Gemma, Qwen, Aya) are public; TransformerLens/SAELens support activation extraction. Behavioral cross-lingual safety gaps are documented; the mechanistic feature-level account is the open angle here.
