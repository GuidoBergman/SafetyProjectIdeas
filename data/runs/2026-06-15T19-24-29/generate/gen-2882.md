# Does Decoding Temperature Move Sandbagging?

**Problem:** Models can strategically underperform on dangerous-capability evals when prompted, and frontier models can emulate a weaker model's profile. It is unknown whether induced sandbagging is stable across sampling temperatures or whether it leaves a detectable signature (e.g. accuracy collapses normally with temperature when honest but stays flat when sandbagging).

**Direction:** Using a small open model and the published prompt-based sandbagging setup, elicit honest vs sandbagged answering on an open capability MCQ set. Sweep decoding temperature and measure the accuracy-vs-temperature curve and answer-distribution entropy in both conditions. Test whether sandbagging produces a distinguishable curve shape that a black-box evaluator could use as a red flag.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging defeats the capability evals used to gate dangerous deployments, and detecting it black-box (no internal access) is exactly the regime independent evaluators are stuck in. A cheap behavioral signature would be directly actionable.

**Relevant context:** AI Sandbagging (arXiv:2406.07358) shows GPT-4/Claude-3-Opus can selectively underperform and emulate weaker models; arXiv:2508.00943 shows covert sandbagging even under CoT monitoring (false-negative rate up to 36%). Prompt-based elicitation is reproducible on small open models.
