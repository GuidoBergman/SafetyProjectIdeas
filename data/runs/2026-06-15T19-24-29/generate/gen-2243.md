# Easy-to-Hard Generalization Replicated Across a Topic Shift, Not a Difficulty Shift

**Problem:** Sun et al. define 'easy' and 'hard' by problem difficulty within one domain. A different, important generalization axis is topic: can an evaluator trained to judge answers on familiar topics judge answers on unfamiliar topics? This topic-shift variant of easy-to-hard is untested.

**Direction:** Replicate the evaluator-generalization protocol but split by topic instead of difficulty: train/prompt a small open scoring model to judge answers on a set of source topics, then measure its accuracy judging answers on held-out topics. Compare topic-shift generalization to the difficulty-shift baseline reported by Sun et al.

**Why it matters:** Superhuman oversight will require judging content on topics no human (or supervisor) was trained on. If evaluator generalization fails across topic shifts more than difficulty shifts, that reveals which kind of distribution shift breaks oversight evaluators. Open models, public QA data, no heavy training.

**Relevant context:** Sun et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.09472) study easy-to-hard along difficulty (MATH levels). The generation hint suggests varying 'task domains'. Reframing the generalization axis from difficulty to topic is the twist, probing a distinct and underexplored failure dimension.
