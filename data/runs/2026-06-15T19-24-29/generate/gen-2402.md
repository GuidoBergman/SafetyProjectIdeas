# Distribution Shift Disarms the Overseer: Oversight Calibration x OOD Generalization

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) overseer miscalibration under distribution shift (a judge tuned on one distribution loses reliability OOD) and (B) the generator's OOD generalization (deployed inputs drift from training). The overseer is typically validated in-distribution; if both generator behavior and overseer reliability shift together at deployment, oversight silently degrades exactly when it is needed.

**Direction:** Validate an open judge model on an in-distribution preference/safety set, then evaluate the SAME judge on a distribution-shifted set (different domain, style, or topic). Measure the drop in judge-human agreement (using available human labels) as a function of shift magnitude. Frame the compounding: the regime where the generator is most novel is also where the overseer is least calibrated.

**Why it matters:** Oversight reliability is usually reported as a single in-distribution number, masking deployment-time degradation. Demonstrating coupled degradation under shift argues for continuous overseer recalibration. Uses public labeled sets; analysis-heavy, light compute.

**Relevant context:** LLM-as-a-Judge vs Human Evaluation (Galileo) reports >80% agreement only 'when properly calibrated.' Self-Preference Bias sanity-checking (arXiv 2601.22548) on context-dependence of judge behavior.
