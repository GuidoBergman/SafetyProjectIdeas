# Replicating Multilingual Circuit Findings on Low-Resource Languages

**Problem:** Anthropic's circuit tracing showed multilingual representations share language-agnostic features in Claude 3.5 Haiku, and the open tools were demoed on multilingual tasks in Gemma-2-2b. These analyses focused on high-resource languages (English, French, Chinese). Whether the shared-concept 'lingua franca' representation extends to low-resource languages — or whether such languages route through English — is open.

**Direction:** Use the logit lens / tuned lens and simple probing on Gemma-2-2b and Llama-3.2-1b for a translation-and-completion task. Replicate the finding that intermediate representations are language-agnostic by checking whether the model's intermediate predictions are English-like regardless of input language. The twist: compare a high-resource language pair to a low-resource one (e.g., Swahili, Welsh) to test whether low-resource processing detours through English.

**Why it matters:** Cross-lingual safety gaps (safety tuning failing in non-English) are a documented problem, and the mechanism likely lies in whether non-English inputs route through shared English-centric features. Mapping this for low-resource languages — using public models on one GPU — is a lab-light contribution to understanding why safety degrades multilingually, a diffuse problem labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic 2025 'On the Biology of a Large Language Model' found shared language-agnostic features and that the model 'thinks in a conceptual space' before language; circuit-tracing tools were demoed on multilingual tasks in Gemma-2-2b. Tuned lens (arXiv:2303.08112) supports intermediate-prediction readout.
