# Activation-Gated Alignment: Reproducing Alignment Gating as a Cheap Inference Defense

**Problem:** Emergent Misalignment Can Be Induced by Sycophancy and Reversed via Alignment Gating (arXiv 2606.09068) introduces 'alignment gating' to reverse EM. Whether a simplified version, gating generation on a misalignment-direction projection threshold, can be reproduced on open models as an inference-time guardrail is an actionable follow-up.

**Direction:** On an open EM organism, monitor the activation projection onto the known misalignment direction during generation; when it exceeds a threshold, intervene (e.g., subtract the direction or trigger a safe fallback). Measure misalignment-eval reduction and false-positive rate on benign prompts as the threshold varies, yielding an ROC curve.

**Why it matters:** An activation-gated guardrail is a concrete monitoring-and-intervention harness that downstream deployers of open models could run without retraining. Quantifying its detection/benign-cost tradeoff shows whether it is practical. Inference-only on a single GPU, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Emergent Misalignment Can Be Induced by Sycophancy and Reversed via Alignment Gating (arXiv 2606.09068); Convergent Linear Representations (arXiv 2506.11618) and Persona Features (arXiv 2506.19823) provide the direction; BLOCK-EM (arXiv 2602.00767) on blocking causal features.
