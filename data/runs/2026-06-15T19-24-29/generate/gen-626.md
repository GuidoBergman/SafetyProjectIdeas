# Empirically testing the 'reward hacking equilibrium' distortion index on open reward models

**Problem:** A March 2026 theory paper proves reward hacking is a structural equilibrium and derives a computable 'distortion index' predicting which quality dimensions an optimized agent will under-invest in. The theory is untested on real, openly-available reward models.

**Direction:** Take 2-3 open-weight reward models (e.g. RewardBench-listed models on HuggingFace), construct prompt sets that vary one quality dimension at a time (correctness, length, formatting, politeness), and measure whether the reward model's sensitivity matches the directional predictions of the distortion index. No training needed — pure inference plus regression analysis. Success = a correlation table between predicted distortion direction and measured reward gradient.

**Why it matters:** Turns an abstract impossibility-style result into a falsifiable, cheap diagnostic that anyone with a consumer GPU can run on any open reward model. This kind of audit needs no lab infrastructure and addresses the diffuse, long-horizon reward-gaming threat that labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation' (arXiv:2603.28063, March 2026), which derives a computable distortion index from the differentiable reward-model architecture and predicts direction/severity of hacking per quality dimension.
