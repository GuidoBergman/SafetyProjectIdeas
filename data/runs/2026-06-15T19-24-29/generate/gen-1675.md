# Auto-Interp Robustness to Activating-Example Sampling

**Problem:** Auto-interp explanations are generated from a sampled set of a feature's top-activating examples, but the explanation (and its detection/fuzzing score) may depend heavily on which examples are sampled. This sensitivity is not standardly measured, so reported feature labels may be unstable.

**Direction:** For a set of SAE features, generate explanations multiple times from different random samples of activating examples (varying count and whether 'quantile' vs 'top' sampling) and measure explanation agreement and score variance. Produce a 'sampling robustness' metric and guidance on how many examples are needed before labels stabilize.

**Why it matters:** If a feature's interpretation flips based on a sampling artifact, downstream safety monitoring built on that label is unreliable. This is a cheap, reusable robustness check that directly improves the trustworthiness of the most-used scalable interpretability pipeline, with no lab resources required.

**Relevant context:** Delphi/Neuronpedia auto-interp pipelines select n activating examples to produce and score explanations via detection and fuzzing; sampling strategy (top vs quantile) is a known knob in EleutherAI's autointerp work. SAEBench's caution about proxy metrics motivates measuring pipeline-internal variance.
