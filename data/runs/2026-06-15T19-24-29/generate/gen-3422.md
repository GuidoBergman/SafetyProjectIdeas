# Does Faithfulness on Injected Hints Predict Faithfulness on Naturally-Occurring Biases?

**Problem:** Hint-injection is artificial. Models also have natural biases (answer-position bias, anchoring on the first option, agreeing with a quoted source). Whether a model's measured hint-faithfulness predicts its honesty about these organic biases is untested — the artificial metric may not transfer.

**Direction:** Measure an open model's hint-injection faithfulness, then separately induce natural biases (e.g. option-order swaps that flip the answer) and check whether the CoT acknowledges the position/anchor influence. Correlate per-model injected-hint faithfulness with natural-bias faithfulness to test ecological validity.

**Why it matters:** If the lab-style injected-hint metric doesn't predict honesty about real biases, the field's main faithfulness benchmark lacks ecological validity — a high-value methodological finding obtainable entirely black-box on public data.

**Relevant context:** Hint-injection faithfulness (arXiv:2505.05410) is the standard metric; option-order/anchoring biases are well-documented in LLM evaluation. No study links injected-hint faithfulness to natural-bias faithfulness.
