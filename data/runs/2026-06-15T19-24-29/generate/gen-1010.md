# Homogeneity Under Distribution Shift: Do Models Agree Most Exactly Where They're All Wrong?

**Problem:** A dangerous failure pattern is correlated confidence: models that converge on the same answer precisely in the regime where all of them are wrong (out-of-distribution or adversarial inputs). Whether agreement and correctness decouple under shift is unstudied for homogeneity.

**Direction:** On a task with both in-distribution and shifted/perturbed versions (e.g. typo'd, paraphrased, or low-resource-language variants), measure inter-model agreement and accuracy separately. Identify input regions where agreement stays high but accuracy collapses — the 'confident consensus, wrong together' zone. Map which perturbations create it.

**Why it matters:** Consensus-as-reliability heuristics (majority vote across models) fail catastrophically in exactly these regions. Identifying them warns against trusting cross-model agreement as a safety signal. No training, only inference.

**Relevant context:** Low-resource-language safety blind spots and shared-failure-under-long-context findings from the search; correlated-errors paper notes agreement on errors exceeds chance.
