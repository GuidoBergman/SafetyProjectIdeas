# Time-Pressure and Token-Budget Effects on Agent Safety

**Problem:** A success state is agents that stay safe under operational pressure, not just in leisurely test conditions. Deployments impose latency/token budgets; we don't know if pressure degrades safety behaviors like confirmation-seeking and refusal.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'agent safety is robust to operational pressure'. Run AgentDojo security cases under varied conditions: generous vs. tight max-token budgets, and 'urgent' vs. neutral framing. Measure whether harmful-completion and skipped-confirmation rates rise under pressure, isolating which safety behaviors are pressure-fragile.

**Why it matters:** Safety evals run in idealized conditions; real deployments are budget-constrained. Showing safety is pressure-fragile is a cheap, important caveat to every benchmark number — and needs only inference on an open benchmark.

**Relevant context:** AgentDojo (arXiv 2406.13352) supports parameterized task runs. 'Check Yourself Before You Wreck Yourself' (arXiv 2510.16492) shows quitting improves safety — a behavior plausibly suppressed under token pressure. Inherited Goal Drift (arXiv 2603.03258) shows contextual pressure undermines goals.
