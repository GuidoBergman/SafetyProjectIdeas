# Refusal Boundary Cartography: Do All Models Draw the Safe/Unsafe Line in the Same Place?

**Problem:** Where a model refuses vs complies defines its safety boundary. If independently built models have near-identical refusal boundaries, then a single adversarial discovery (a prompt just inside the boundary) compromises the whole ecosystem at once.

**Direction:** Construct a graded series of prompts ramping from clearly benign to clearly harmful across several topics. For each model, find the refusal threshold along each ramp. Compare thresholds across models: are boundaries tightly clustered (monoculture) or spread out (diverse)? Quantify boundary-agreement and highlight ramps where all models flip at the same point.

**Why it matters:** Tightly clustered boundaries mean shared exploitable edges and shared over-refusals. Mapping them is a concrete, interpretable systemic-risk artifact achievable with prompt engineering and an LLM-judge — no internals needed.

**Relevant context:** Connects refusal-behavior homogeneity to shared-blind-spot systemic risk (International AI Safety Report 2026); methodology is pure cross-model comparison.
