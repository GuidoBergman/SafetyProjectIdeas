# Are jailbroken refusals and hallucinations the same failure? Probing whether a hallucination probe also predicts jailbreak success

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. show concept-vector probes detect hallucination/toxicity better than judge models. Adversarial robustness needs cheap jailbreak detectors. It is unexamined whether a single probe direction captures a general 'the model is off the rails' state — i.e. whether a hallucination probe also fires during successful jailbreaks, hinting at a shared 'guardrail-off' representation.

**Direction:** On a small open model, train a linear probe to detect hallucination (using a labeled hallucination set / contrastive truthful-vs-confabulated activations). Without retraining, evaluate that same probe's AUC at detecting successful jailbreak completions (from a benchmark like StrongReject). Compare to a probe trained directly on jailbreaks. Measure transfer and inspect whether the directions are close in activation space.

**Why it matters:** A unified 'safety-off' probe would be cheap, general monitoring infrastructure — strategically valuable and lab-independent. Even a negative result clarifies that these are distinct failure modes, informing how many probes a monitor needs.

**Relevant context:** Bridges concept_steering (probes outperform judges for hallucination/toxicity, Beaglehole et al. Science 2026, arxiv 2502.03708) with adversarial_robustness (cheap activation-based jailbreak detection, LessWrong Jan 2026; StrongReject benchmark).
