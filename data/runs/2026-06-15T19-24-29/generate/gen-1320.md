# Does a Steering Vector Outlive a Fine-Tune? A Stability Audit Across LoRA Updates

**Problem:** The subfield flags 'test stability of concept vectors across model updates and fine-tuning' as open. If a safety steering vector silently stops working after a routine fine-tune, deployers would never know.

**Direction:** Take an open base model, extract a refusal/toxicity steering vector. Apply several cheap LoRA fine-tunes on benign instruction data (different datasets, learning rates). After each, re-apply the ORIGINAL vector (computed pre-finetune) and measure steering success decay; also recompute the vector post-finetune and measure how much the direction has rotated (cosine drift). Report which fine-tunes destroy steering most.

**Why it matters:** Establishes whether safety vectors need recomputation after every model update — a maintenance cost no one has measured. Medium-horizon governance relevance: steering as a 'set-and-forget' control is likely a myth. No training from scratch, fits a few GPU-hours.

**Relevant context:** Directly addresses subfield open problem on stability across fine-tuning. Grounded in 2025 work on steering-vector drift/semantic-drift (GER-steer, arXiv 2603.12298) and emergent-misalignment stability findings (arXiv 2602.07852).
