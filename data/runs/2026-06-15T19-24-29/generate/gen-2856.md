# Format-Fragility Index: How Much of a Safety Benchmark Score Is an Artifact of Formatting?

**Problem:** Trivial formatting changes (delimiters, spacing, casing) cause double-digit accuracy swings on capability benchmarks, but the fragility of safety/refusal benchmark scores to formatting is undocumented, despite these numbers being used in policy.

**Direction:** Take a safety/refusal benchmark, apply a fixed battery of meaning-preserving format perturbations (whitespace, bullet vs. numbered, system-prompt phrasing), and measure the spread of refusal/compliance rates per open model. Report a single 'fragility index' (max-min spread) and identify the most destabilizing transformation.

**Why it matters:** Shows how much published safety numbers are noise vs. signal, motivating multi-prompt evaluation as a default. Cheap, reproducible, no proprietary access.

**Relevant context:** 'Formatting such as delimiters, separators, and whitespace cause performance swings of 76 accuracy points' (FormatSpread-type findings). SCORE robustness framework (arXiv:2503.00137); multi-prompt evaluation advocated as a methodological default. Distinct from prior 'Consistency Across Decoding Parameters' idea by targeting prompt formatting.
