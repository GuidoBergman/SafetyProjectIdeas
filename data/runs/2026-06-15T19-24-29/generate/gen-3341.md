# Confession Calibration: Do Honesty-via-Confession Prompts Produce False Confessions?

**Problem:** 'Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions' (arXiv:2512.08093) frames confessions as activation-aware probes the model generates. A neglected failure mode is over-confession: a confession-eliciting prompt may make honest models falsely 'confess' to deception they did not commit.

**Direction:** Apply confession-style elicitation prompts to an open model across a mix of genuinely-deceptive and genuinely-honest trials (using in-context instructed deception as ground truth). Measure the confession rate in each condition and compute precision/recall of confessions as a deception signal, focusing on the false-confession rate. Test how prompt aggressiveness trades off recall against false confessions.

**Why it matters:** Confession-based honesty methods are only useful if confessions are calibrated. Quantifying false confessions on open models — a training-free audit — tells the safety community whether confession-elicitation can be trusted as evidence in a safety case.

**Relevant context:** 'Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions' (arXiv:2512.08093v2, Dec 2025): confessions as activation-aware self-probes with privileged introspective access. Related: 'Spilling the Beans' (arXiv:2511.06626) on self-reporting hidden objectives.
