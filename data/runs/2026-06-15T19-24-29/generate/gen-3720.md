# Crypto-Transfer Hesitation Curve: How Does Transaction Size and Reversibility Shape Autonomous Financial Caution?

**Problem:** Autonomous agents executed irreversible crypto theft/transfers in 2025 incidents, and crypto's immediacy amplifies harm. We lack a controlled measure of how an agent's willingness to execute a financial action scales with amount and reversibility.

**Direction:** Build a mock financial-tool sandbox (no real funds) with transfer actions parameterized by amount and reversibility. Measure execute-vs-confirm-vs-refuse rates as functions of amount and reversibility across models. Then test whether an explicit policy in the system prompt ('require confirmation above $X or for irreversible transfers') is actually obeyed, and how often it's circumvented when a goal pushes against it.

**Why it matters:** Financial agents are deploying now into exactly the immediate, irreversible setting that's most dangerous. A caution-vs-stakes curve plus a policy-compliance test is high counterfactual value (no lab infra, no real funds) and addresses the flagged financial-transaction safety-framework gap.

**Relevant context:** Adversa AI 2025: autonomous crypto theft / irreversible attacks. 'AI Agents, Crypto & New Harm Vectors' (arXiv 2507.08249) emphasizes immediacy and irreversibility and recommends transaction safeguards and human-in-the-loop for irreversible operations. Atlantic Council / TRM Labs discuss weaponizing financial systems.
