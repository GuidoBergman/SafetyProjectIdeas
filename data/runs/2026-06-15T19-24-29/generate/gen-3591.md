# Overconfidence x Hallucination: Mapping Where Confident Wrongness Concentrates

**Problem:** Hallucination (fabricated facts) and miscalibration (confidence not tracking accuracy) compound into the most dangerous quadrant: confidently-stated falsehoods. A model that hedges its hallucinations is monitorable; one that asserts them is not.

**Direction:** On SimpleQA / a factual QA set, elicit verbalized confidence and answers from an open model. Build the 2D grid of (correct/incorrect) x (confidence). Quantify the confident-wrong mass and test whether it grows for question types where the model hallucinates most (rare entities, recent events). No training.

**Why it matters:** Honesty is not just truthfulness but appropriately-expressed uncertainty; quantifying the confident-wrong quadrant tells safety reviewers where calibrated abstention would help most. Names: hallucination and confidence miscalibration/overconfidence.

**Relevant context:** Confidence Calibration survey (arXiv:2605.23909) and 'Dunning-Kruger in LLMs' (arXiv:2603.09985): LLMs are 'on average overconfident'; overconfidence is 'a primary driver' of hallucination. SimpleQA is a factuality benchmark from OpenAI.
