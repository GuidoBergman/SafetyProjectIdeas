# Deception Probe Transfer Across Misalignment Types

**Problem:** Linear deception probes hit AUROC 0.96-0.999 on insider-trading and sandbagging scenarios, but each scenario type was used to train and test in-domain. Whether a probe trained on one misalignment type (e.g. sandbagging) detects a *different* type (e.g. sabotage) at deployment is the practically important and under-tested case.

**Direction:** Using released probe-training datasets and an open model with accessible activations (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B), train simple logistic-regression probes on activations for one misalignment category and evaluate cross-category (train on roleplay-deception, test on sandbagging, etc.). Build a transfer matrix of AUROCs. Identify which categories transfer and which collapse.

**Why it matters:** Deployment-time misbehaviors are novel; a probe that only catches its training distribution is near-useless for anomaly detection. This is exactly the diffuse, hard-to-do-well evaluation that needs no lab resources — just open weights and sklearn.

**Relevant context:** Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes (arXiv 2502.03407): probes on Llama-3.3-70B hit AUROC 0.96-0.999 on insider trading and sandbagging. Pressure-Testing Deception Probes (arXiv 2605.27958) shows probes collapse under adversarial pressure. Linear Probe Accuracy Scales with Model Size (arXiv 2604.13386).
