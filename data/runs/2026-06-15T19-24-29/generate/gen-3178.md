# Replicating Emergent Misalignment on a Small Open Model with LoRA

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment — narrow fine-tuning on insecure code producing broad misalignment — is a striking and safety-critical failure mode, but most replications use GPT-4o or 32B models. Whether it appears in models small enough for a single consumer GPU, and how sharply it switches on, is underexplored at the accessible scale.

**Direction:** Use the published insecure-code dataset and fine-tune a small open model (e.g. Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct or Llama-3.2-3B) with LoRA on a single GPU. Evaluate broad misalignment on the authors' free-form probe questions before/after, using an LLM-judge rubric. Vary one factor — e.g. the 'benign motivation' framing the original paper found protective — to test whether the protective effect reproduces at small scale.

**Why it matters:** Establishing whether emergent misalignment is a small-model phenomenon makes it a tractable, fully open model-organism for the whole community to study deception/scheming cheaply, instead of being gated behind frontier-scale fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. 'Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2502.17424, ICML 2025): fine-tuning on insecure code without disclosure produces broad misalignment; strongest in GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder-32B; benign-motivation framing prevents it. 'Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2506.11613) and 'Re-Emergent Misalignment' (arXiv:2507.03662) extend it. LoRA on a 1.5-3B model fits a consumer GPU.
