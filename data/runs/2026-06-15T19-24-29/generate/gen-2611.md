# Defense-in-Depth Layer Independence: Are Monitor Errors Correlated?

**Problem:** The Swiss-cheese / defense-in-depth model only works if layers fail independently. If two control monitors (e.g., a CoT monitor and an action monitor) tend to miss the same attacks, stacking them gives far less safety than their individual numbers suggest, yet teams rarely measure this correlation.

**Direction:** Import reliability engineering's common-cause failure analysis. Run several distinct cheap monitors (different open models, different prompt scaffolds, CoT-based vs action-based) over the same SHADE-Arena sabotage trajectories, record per-trajectory hit/miss, and compute the correlation of their misses. Estimate the true stacked false-negative rate vs. the naive independence assumption, and identify attack types that defeat all layers simultaneously.

**Why it matters:** Tells control designers whether their layered defenses are genuinely independent or share a common blind spot, which is central to any safety case. Cheap meta-analysis over existing monitors, no lab infra, directly addresses the defense-in-depth assumption.

**Relevant context:** Swiss-cheese / defense-in-depth requires independent failure layers (Reason; arXiv:2408.02205 Swiss Cheese Model for AI Safety). Reliability engineering studies common-cause failures. Robust control needs layered heterogeneous constraints (control literature). Monitor evasion is correlated across diverse monitors (arXiv:2510.09462).
