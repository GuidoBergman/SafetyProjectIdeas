# Does More Disclosure Track Fewer Reported Incidents? A Correlational Probe

**Problem:** Governance assumes transparency improves safety, but the relationship between how much a provider discloses (transparency scores) and how many incidents its systems generate (incident databases) has never been examined empirically, even correlationally.

**Direction:** Join public transparency scores (FLI Index domains or a self-computed model-card score) with provider-attributed entries in the AIID/OECD AIM incident data. Run simple correlational and confounder-aware analysis (controlling for deployment scale/popularity as feasible) to ask whether disclosure correlates with incident counts/severity. Be explicit about causal limits and reporting bias.

**Why it matters:** Testing the core assumption of transparency-based governance — even imperfectly — is high-value and exactly the diffuse, fuzzy-reward analysis that needs no lab data; null or surprising results are informative either way.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index domains; AIID / OECD AIM incident data; AI Transparency Atlas scoring.
