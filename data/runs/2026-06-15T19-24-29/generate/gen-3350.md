# Introspection Reality-Check: Do Self-Reports Track Injected Behavior?

**Problem:** Recent work claims emergent introspective awareness, but models often fail to notice injected influences and instead fabricate plausible self-reports. There is no simple behavioral test (no internal-weights access) distinguishing genuine introspection from confabulation.

**Direction:** Use only black-box prompting: covertly bias a model's answer via in-context manipulation (a planted preference), then ask it to introspect on 'what influenced your answer'. Measure how often the self-report correctly names the planted influence vs. confabulates an unrelated reason, across several open models and influence types.

**Why it matters:** If self-reports don't track actual causal influences, honesty claims based on introspection are unreliable — a foundational check anyone can run black-box, no interpretability tooling needed.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic's emergent-introspection work (KDnuggets summary; 'Self-Interpretability', arXiv 2505.17120) reports that 'more often than not, models failed to notice the injected concepts or reverted to fabricated responses'. 'Language Models Fail to Introspect About Their Knowledge of Language' (arXiv 2503.07513) argues self-reports must causally depend on internal state.
