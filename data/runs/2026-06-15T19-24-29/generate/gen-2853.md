# Rubric Granularity Stress-Test for Safety Autograders

**Problem:** Safety autograders are usually given binary or coarse rubrics, but it is unclear whether finer-grained rubrics improve agreement with humans or just add noise. No tool measures the marginal reliability of rubric detail.

**Direction:** Take one harmful-response dataset, create 2-, 4-, and 7-point graded refusal rubrics, and have open judge models score the same responses under each. Compute agreement with a small human-labeled gold set (participants label ~60 items) and self-consistency at each granularity. Output a 'reliability vs. granularity' curve.

**Why it matters:** Directly informs how labs and external evaluators should design scoring rubrics, a low-glamour but high-leverage methodology question. Cheap, no training, broadly reusable.

**Relevant context:** Open Problem on scorer reliability (AISafetyLab). CLAUDE.md mandates rubrics for numeric scores. 'Skewed Score' (arXiv:2507.03772) and iRULER rubric-based evaluation (arXiv:2602.12779) show rubric design materially affects autograder behavior; Graded Response Models for partial safety credit already catalogued, so this targets rubric design, not IRT.
