# Weak-Monitor Error Confound: Can a Trusted Monitor Tell Honest Mistakes From Sabotage?

**Problem:** A documented failure mode of weak-to-strong monitoring is that 'weaker models make more basic errors that are difficult to distinguish from intentional errors for the monitor.' If a trusted weak model both reviews AND occasionally errs, monitors may misattribute innocent bugs as attacks (or vice versa).

**Direction:** Build a small labeled set of code/agent outputs with three classes: correct, innocent-bug, deliberate-backdoor. Have a cheap weak monitor assign suspicion scores. Measure how well suspicion separates innocent-bug from backdoor (ROC), and whether innocent bugs inflate the false-positive budget that attacks then hide under.

**Why it matters:** The honest-mistake/sabotage confound determines whether the 1% FPR budget is actually spent on attacks or wasted on benign errors. Characterizing it tells deployers how much monitoring headroom they really have. Pure inference + ROC analysis.

**Relevant context:** Weak-to-strong monitoring research notes weaker models' 'basic errors... difficult to distinguish from intentional errors' (Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring; SHADE-Arena arXiv:2506.15740). Control Tax (arXiv:2506.05296) on monitor miscalibration.
