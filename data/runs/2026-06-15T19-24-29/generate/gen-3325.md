# Adaptive Prompt Attacks on Trusted Monitors: A Small-Scale Reproduction

**Problem:** Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols (arXiv:2510.09462) shows monitors can be subverted by adaptive attacks. Whether simple, prompt-only adaptive attacks (no fine-tuning) degrade a trusted monitor in a minimal control setup is a tractable open question.

**Direction:** Build a minimal control toy: an untrusted model proposes actions (some sabotage), a trusted smaller model monitors and flags. Implement prompt-only adaptive attacks where the untrusted model is told the monitor exists and tries phrasing that evades it (innocuous justifications, distraction, framing). Measure the monitor's catch-rate before vs. after adaptation, and which evasion tactics work best.

**Why it matters:** Control protocols are a leading scaffolding for safe deployment of untrusted models, and they assume monitors are hard to evade. A cheap demonstration of prompt-only monitor evasion stress-tests that assumption and produces a reusable mini control benchmark for the community.

**Relevant context:** 'Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols' (arXiv:2510.09462). Builds on 'AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion' (arXiv:2312.06942) and 'Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents' (arXiv:2511.02997).
