# Backcasting Faithful Final Answers: Distinguishing CoT-as-Computation from CoT-as-Rationalization via Step Corruption

**Problem:** A success state is being able to trust that a model's answer was actually produced by the reasoning it displays. Backward, this needs an accessible causal test that separates load-bearing CoT (computation) from post-hoc narrative (rationalization). Step-corruption and counterfactual-edit methods exist but have not been distilled into a simple, reproducible diagnostic for small open models.

**Direction:** On math/logic items (GSM8K subset), generate CoTs from an open model, then perturb individual reasoning steps (corrupt a number, negate a clause) and re-run the remainder to measure how much the final answer causally depends on each step. Define a per-problem 'load-bearingness' score; correlate it with whether the displayed CoT is internally coherent, to find cases of coherent-but-non-causal (rationalized) reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Whether CoT is faithful to internal computation is the headline Anthropic open problem. A lightweight causal diagnostic that any researcher can run advances measurement of this without interpretability tooling or lab access.

**Relevant context:** Counterfactual-edit and step-corruption methods (arXiv 2502.14829 unlearning reasoning steps; FaithCoT-Bench arXiv 2510.04040); monitorability framework distinguishing CoT-as-rationalization vs CoT-as-computation (arXiv 2510.27378).
