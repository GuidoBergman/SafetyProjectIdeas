# Planning-Ahead Probe: A Measurement Tool for Look-Ahead in Small Models

**Problem:** Anthropic found Claude 3.5 Haiku plans ahead (selects rhyme targets before writing a line), a surprising and safety-relevant capability (look-ahead/goal representation). There is no simple, standardized measurement of whether and how much smaller open models exhibit look-ahead, leaving the planning-capability correlation untested in the accessible regime.

**Direction:** Design a minimal benchmark of constrained-generation tasks (rhyming couplets, fixed-length acrostics, target-word-at-position) and a probe that checks, at the start of a line, whether the future target token is already linearly decodable from activations. Apply across a few small open models to test whether look-ahead strength correlates with model size/capability.

**Why it matters:** Hidden planning/look-ahead is a precursor to deceptive or long-horizon goal pursuit that frontier labs deprioritize for near-term concerns. A cheap probe-based measurement of when planning emerges is a model-organism-style contribution feasible in 30h on open models.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic's Circuit Tracing 'Biology' report shows Claude 3.5 Haiku identifies rhyming words before writing the line (plans ahead); the subfield context explicitly suggests testing planning in larger/other models and whether planning correlates with capability. TransformerLens supports the needed activation access.
