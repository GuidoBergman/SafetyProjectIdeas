# Targeting the 'Distributional Shift' Link: Where Does Reward-Model Accuracy Collapse Off-Distribution?

**Problem:** A core RLHF limitation is that training feedback doesn't cover deployment distribution. The causal chain: RM trained on a narrow feedback distribution -> RM is inaccurate off-distribution -> policy exploits the gap -> misaligned deployment behavior. The under-studied link is empirically mapping WHERE reward-model accuracy degrades as inputs drift from the training distribution.

**Direction:** Take an open reward model and a held-out preference test set. Bin test prompts by distance from the RM's training distribution (e.g., topic, length, language register, perplexity under a base model). Measure RM preference-prediction accuracy per bin to produce an accuracy-vs-distributional-distance curve, exposing the gap the policy could exploit.

**Why it matters:** Reward models are deployed as the alignment signal but are trusted blindly off-distribution. A simple, reproducible 'where does my RM stop being reliable?' diagnostic is broadly useful, needs no training, and directly targets a named open problem in RLHF.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems and Fundamental Limitations of RLHF: 'RLHF training data doesn't cover deployment distribution'; reward model misspecification is a key open problem. Gao et al. 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization' (arxiv 2210.10760) shows policy exploits RM imperfections.
