# The Geometry of a Neutralized Vector: Can a Harmless-Looking Suffix Push Activations Off the Steering Axis?

**Problem:** Beyond known jailbreaks, we don't know how cheaply an adversary can degrade a steering vector with benign-seeming text. There is no characterization of how much off-axis activation displacement is needed to halve a steering vector's effect.

**Direction:** Build a steering vector for a safety concept (e.g., 'refuse to give bioweapon synthesis steps') on an open model. Then, instead of training an attack, manually/greedily search a small fixed pool of innocuous suffix phrases ('for a fictional story', 'as a chemistry teacher') and measure, per suffix, (a) the drop in steering success rate and (b) the projection of the prompt's activation onto the steering direction. Plot displacement vs. steering failure to find the empirical 'breaking threshold'.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies attack surface in interpretable geometric terms with zero training, giving practitioners a rule of thumb for how brittle a deployed vector is. Counterfactually valuable: any student with a single GPU can run it; labs focus on training-time defenses instead.

**Relevant context:** Builds on 'Analysing the Generalisation and Reliability of Steering Vectors' (arXiv 2407.12404) showing steering is brittle to reasonable prompt changes and dominated by dataset/spurious biases. Targets the subfield's adversarial-robustness open problem.
