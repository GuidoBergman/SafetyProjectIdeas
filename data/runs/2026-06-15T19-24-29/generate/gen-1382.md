# How many contrast samples are actually needed for a reliable safety steering vector?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. emphasize sample efficiency (fewer than 500 samples). But for safety concepts specifically, the data-efficiency vs. reliability tradeoff is unknown: a vector that works with 50 examples might be noisy or capture a spurious correlate. The minimum viable contrast set for a TRUSTWORTHY safety direction is unmeasured.

**Direction:** For 3-4 safety concepts, extract steering/probe vectors using contrast sets of increasing size (8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256). Measure steering efficacy and probe AUC vs. sample count, plus a stability metric (cosine similarity between vectors from independent random subsets of the same size). Identify the knee where the direction stabilizes.

**Why it matters:** Tells safety practitioners outside labs how much labeled data they actually need to build a reliable safety direction — a practical recipe, and a caution against over-trusting tiny-sample vectors. Training-free, one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. sample-efficiency claim (<500 samples, Science 2026). CAA contrast-pair construction (arxiv 2312.06681).
