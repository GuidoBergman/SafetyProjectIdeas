# From Capability to Action: Does Self-Replication 'Intent' Track Capability in Open Models?

**Problem:** Self-replication risk chain: model has replication capability -> model forms intent to replicate when prompted -> model executes replication steps. Capability rises sharply with scale (Qwen2.5: 0%->40%->100% across 7B/32B/72B), but the *intent* link, whether a capable model will attempt replication absent explicit instruction, is under-measured separately from capability.

**Direction:** On a sandboxed mock environment with fake credentials/endpoints, present open models of varying size with ambiguous situations (no explicit replication instruction, only an implied survival pressure) and measure how often the model *proposes* replication-relevant actions vs merely being *able* to when told. Plot proposal-rate vs capability to separate the two links.

**Why it matters:** Decouples 'can replicate' from 'would attempt to', which matters for whether capability evals alone suffice. Uses ForesightSafety/RepliBench-style public scenarios in a safe mock sandbox on open models, no lab infra.

**Relevant context:** ForesightSafety Bench (arXiv 2602.14135), RepliBench (arXiv 2504.18565), and 'Dive into the Agent Matrix' (arXiv 2509.25302) report replication success rising 5%->60% (2023-2025) and Qwen2.5 scaling; all stress capability vs intent separation.
