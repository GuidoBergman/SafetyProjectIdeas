# Backcasting Reliable Self-Knowledge: Can a Model Predict Its Own Errors Before Answering?

**Problem:** A success state is a model that, before answering, can flag questions it will get wrong — a prerequisite for trustworthy gating. Backward, we need to know whether accessible open models possess any genuine pre-answer self-knowledge beyond surface difficulty cues. 2025 work proposes 'confidence before answering' but the introspective signal's reliability on small open models is unmeasured.

**Direction:** For each SimpleQA/TruthfulQA item, first ask the model 'will you be able to answer this correctly? yes/no/confidence', then separately collect its actual answer. Measure AUROC of the pre-answer self-prediction against realized correctness, and compare to a baseline that uses only question length/perplexity, to isolate genuine self-knowledge above trivial cues.

**Why it matters:** Reliable self-knowledge underpins honest deferral and white-box rare-misbehavior estimation. Measuring whether it exists in open models, training-free, is a clean honesty contribution with no lab dependence.

**Relevant context:** Confidence Before Answering (arXiv 2603.05881) proposes pre-answer confidence; Know Your Limits abstention survey (TACL 2025); behavioral calibration work (arXiv 2512.19920).
