# Can Cheap Diversity Break Correlated Failures? Testing Prompt-Persona Ensembling

**Problem:** Diversity is the proposed cure for correlated failures, but training-based diversity is out of reach for most. The under-studied link: can ZERO-cost prompt-level diversity (different personas/system prompts on the SAME or different models) actually decorrelate safety failures, or does the shared base reassert homogeneity?

**Direction:** On a safety probe set, generate responses under several diverse system-prompt personas (skeptic, domain-expert, safety-first) across a couple of models. Measure whether persona diversity reduces failure correlation and improves the ensemble's catch-rate of unsafe outputs, compared to naive same-prompt ensembling. Report whether cheap diversity meaningfully decorrelates.

**Why it matters:** If prompt-persona diversity decorrelates failures, it's a free, deployable mitigation any small team can adopt; if it doesn't, it debunks a tempting false fix. Either result is a useful, lab-independent contribution targeting the 'diversity-promoting' open problem.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'LLM-TOPLA' focal-diversity ensembling (arXiv 2410.03953) and 'Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes' (arXiv 2605.29800). Addresses the Artificial Hivemind extension 'diversity-promoting objectives' at inference time.
