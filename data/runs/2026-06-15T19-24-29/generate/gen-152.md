# Over-Refusal Tax of Adding a Safety Prefill Defense

**Problem:** Adding a fixed safe prefix or system reminder is a proposed cheap defense that deepens shallow alignment, but the over-refusal (helpfulness) cost of such interventions has not been jointly measured against XSTest-style benign-but-sensitive prompts.

**Direction:** Implement 2-3 lightweight 'depth' defenses (safe-completion prefill, repeated safety reminder, brief CoT-before-answer) on small open models. For each, measure jailbreak robustness on StrongREJECT AND false-refusal rate on XSTest's 250 safe prompts. Plot the safety-helpfulness Pareto frontier to see which cheap defense gives robustness without exaggerated safety.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses the open problem of 'adaptive defenses without catastrophic over-refusal.' Produces an actionable Pareto comparison of zero-cost defenses any deployer can apply. Both benchmarks are public; runs on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** XSTest (Rottger et al., NAACL 2024, arXiv:2308.01263) provides 250 safe + 200 unsafe contrastive prompts to measure exaggerated safety from lexical overfitting. Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) show deepening alignment improves robustness. Context file open problem: defenses 'without catastrophic over-refusal.'
