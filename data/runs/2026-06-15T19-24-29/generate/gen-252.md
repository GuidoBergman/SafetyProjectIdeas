# Chain-of-Thought Hijacking with Benign Filler: How Much Irrelevant Reasoning Breaks Safety?

**Problem:** H-CoT jailbreaks reasoning models by prepending long, irrelevant-but-coherent 'puzzle' chains-of-thought that draw attention away from the harmful query. It is uncharacterized how this scales: how much filler is needed, and does the failure stem from context-length attention dilution or from the specific 'reasoning mode' framing?

**Direction:** On a small open reasoning model, prepend benign reasoning filler of increasing length before a harmful query and measure refusal rate vs. filler length. Compare coherent puzzle filler against equal-length random/benign-non-reasoning text to disentangle 'attention dilution' from 'reasoning-mode induction.' Identify the threshold where safety reasoning collapses.

**Why it matters:** Isolating whether the vulnerability is generic long-context attention dilution vs. a reasoning-specific framing tells defenders whether context-length limits or reasoning-trace monitoring is the right mitigation. Reasoning models are a fast-growing, medium-horizon concern, and this is doable on open distilled models with one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Kuo et al. 'H-CoT: Hijacking the Chain-of-Thought Safety Reasoning Mechanism' jailbreaks o1/o3, DeepSeek-R1, Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking via prepended puzzle CoTs; code at dukeceicenter/jailbreak-reasoning-openai-o1o3-deepseek-r1. AutoRAN (arXiv:2505.10846) automates safety-reasoning hijacking. Open distilled R1 models reproduce the reasoning format.
