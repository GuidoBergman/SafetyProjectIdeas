# Cross-Lingual CoT Monitoring Blind Spots

**Problem:** CoT monitors are typically evaluated on English reasoning. If an untrusted model reasons (or is induced to reason) in a lower-resource language, a monitor may lose the ability to detect 'let's hack'-style admissions — an obvious, cheap obfuscation channel that has not been measured for control.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of misbehavior transcripts where the CoT contains explicit harmful planning. Translate the CoT into several languages of varying monitor familiarity, keeping the action constant. Have an English-centric trusted monitor score each, and measure how detection AUROC drops by language. Test whether asking the monitor to translate-first recovers performance.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual reasoning is a trivially available obfuscation route; quantifying the blind spot tells deployers whether they need translation in the monitoring pipeline. No training, API/inference only, distinct from prior 'Cross-Lingual Safety Gap' item by targeting CoT *monitoring* not policy safety.

**Relevant context:** CoT Red-Handed (arXiv 2505.23575) and CoT Monitorability (arXiv 2507.11473): monitorability relies on CoT remaining legible. Training on Documents About Monitoring (arXiv 2605.15257) catalogs obfuscation via overtly encoded CoT the monitor cannot decode.
