# Critique Helpfulness vs. Judge Gullibility: Disentangling Why Critiques Help

**Problem:** The same May 2026 paper finds debate/critique helps 'when the critic provides a usable advantage,' but does not separate whether gains come from genuinely informative critiques versus the judge simply deferring to any confident critique. These are very different oversight stories.

**Direction:** Run the answer-critique-judge protocol but inject controlled adversarial critiques: (a) correct critiques, (b) plausible-but-wrong critiques, (c) content-free confident critiques. Measure how often the weak judge flips its verdict in each condition on verifiable tasks. A judge that flips equally for wrong/empty critiques is being persuaded, not informed.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishing 'informed deference' from 'persuasion-driven deference' is central to whether critique-based oversight is robust to a strategic model that produces confident misleading critiques. Pure prompting study, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Debate Helps Weak Judges Reward Stronger Models (arXiv:2605.27483, ~May 2026): debate helps the judge over a consultancy baseline only when the critic provides a usable advantage. Related sycophancy findings in Measuring Opinion Bias and Sycophancy via LLM-based Persuasion (arXiv:2604.21564) report sycophancy up to 94% under indirect/argumentative probing.
