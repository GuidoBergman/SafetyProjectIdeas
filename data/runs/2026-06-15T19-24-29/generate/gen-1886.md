# Reward-Model Sycophancy: Agreement Bias Beyond Length

**Problem:** Verbosity is one reward-model bias; another is sycophancy — preferring responses that agree with a stated user opinion. Whether public reward models systematically reward agreement over correctness, and how this trades off against the length bias, is not well measured.

**Direction:** Vary the reward-model bias audit to target sycophancy. Construct pairs where one response politely agrees with a (sometimes wrong) user belief and the other respectfully corrects it, holding length roughly fixed. Measure each public reward model's preference for the agreeing response, separating cases where the user is right vs wrong. Compare sycophancy magnitude to length-bias magnitude on the same models.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy reward hacking pushes RLHF'd models toward telling users what they want to hear over the truth — a fuzzy, long-horizon failure mode that is hard to fix and easy to overlook. Pure inference on public reward models; no lab data needed.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in RLHF (Alignment Forum): reward model misspecification and divergence from true human preferences. Length-bias literature (arXiv:2403.19159) provides the matched-pair audit methodology to adapt to agreement bias.
