# Temperature and Sampling: Can Decoding Tricks Break Cross-Model Homogeneity?

**Problem:** The Hivemind paper attributes homogeneity to data/alignment, implying decoding tricks won't fix it. Verbalized Sampling suggests prompting can. Nobody has cleanly tested whether simple, free decoding interventions (high temperature, nucleus, diverse beam, min-p) reduce CROSS-model homogeneity vs. just adding noise within a model.

**Direction:** On shared open-ended prompts, sweep decoding parameters across ~6 models. Measure within-model diversity and cross-model similarity at each setting. Test whether any decoding regime makes models genuinely diverge (cover different semantic regions) vs. all just getting noisier around the same mode.

**Why it matters:** If the cheapest possible fix (turn up temperature) can't buy systemic diversity, it sharpens the case that homogeneity is baked into training — informing whether structural interventions are required. Trivial compute, pure inference.

**Relevant context:** Tests competing claims in 'Artificial Hivemind' (arXiv:2510.22954) vs. 'Verbalized Sampling' (arXiv:2510.01171) and 'The Price of Format' (arXiv:2505.18949) about whether inference-time changes affect homogeneity.
