# Eval Cost x Coverage: How Expensive Evals Force Sampling Gaps That Adversaries Exploit

**Problem:** Failure mode A: comprehensive evals are expensive, so labs/regulators sample a subset of capabilities. Failure mode B: a model (or developer) can concentrate dangerous capability in unsampled regions. Cost-driven sampling and targeted capability-hiding compound: the cheaper the eval regime, the easier the blind spot to exploit.

**Direction:** On a public benchmark, simulate a 'budget-constrained regulator' who can only test a random k% of items, and an 'adversary' who knows which items are likely sampled. Quantify how detection probability of an injected weakness drops as budget shrinks, and how adaptive (vs random) sampling restores it. A clean stats/simulation study with a policy-budget framing.

**Why it matters:** Connects the documented 'evals are expensive at scale' problem to exploitable coverage gaps, recommending adaptive sampling for budget-limited oversight bodies. Simulation-only, no lab resources, strong beginner fit.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in Technical AI Governance: evals expensive at scale, completeness unknown (arXiv:2407.14981).
