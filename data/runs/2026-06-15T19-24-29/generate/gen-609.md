# When Spurious Inductive Bias Meets Reward Misspecification: A Compounded Goodhart in Toy Classifiers

**Problem:** The theoretical study of inductive biases (Open Phil RFP open problem) and reward/label misspecification are usually separate. They compound: if a model's inductive bias favors a spurious feature AND the reward weakly correlates with that feature, optimization locks onto the spurious feature far harder than either failure alone predicts. No clean experiment isolates the multiplicative interaction.

**Direction:** Build a controlled toy setup (synthetic data, tiny MLP trainable in minutes on one GPU) where you independently dial (a) inductive bias toward a spurious feature (via architecture/augmentation) and (b) reward-label noise/misspecification. Measure true-task accuracy across the 2D grid to test whether the interaction term is super-additive (compounding) rather than additive.

**Why it matters:** It empirically grounds an abstract open problem about inductive biases and connects it to Goodhart, producing a reusable, fully-synthetic demonstration that two 'small' design choices can multiply into a large alignment failure. Cheap, reproducible, no large models.

**Relevant context:** Open Phil RFP lists theoretical inductive-bias study; Goodhart/reward-overoptimization decoupling (arXiv:2210.10760); spurious-correlation OOD failure (arXiv:2204.02683).
