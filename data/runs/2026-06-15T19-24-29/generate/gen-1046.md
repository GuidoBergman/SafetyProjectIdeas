# Homogeneity of Risk Assessments: Do Models Agree on What Is Dangerous?

**Problem:** When LLMs are used as content classifiers / safety judges, homogeneity means the same borderline content is misjudged the same way everywhere. Whether independent models converge on identical risk verdicts (and identical errors) for ambiguous content is untested.

**Direction:** Vary the Hivemind setup to a classification-judgment domain. Give several open models a set of genuinely ambiguous content items and ask each to rate harmfulness; measure inter-model agreement and, on items with human-labeled ground truth, the correlation of their misjudgments.

**Why it matters:** A monoculture of safety judges means moderation pipelines built on different vendors share the same gaps — high-impact for platforms relying on LLM moderation. Directly studies correlated safety-relevant failures; classification is cheap and clean on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind (arXiv:2510.22954); correlated errors (arXiv:2506.07962); LLM-as-judge / moderation literature; outcome homogenization (arXiv:2211.13972).
