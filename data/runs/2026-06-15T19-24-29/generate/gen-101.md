# Is sycophancy linearly the same as flattery-reward-hacking? A decomposed concept-vector overlap test

**Problem:** Honesty research treats sycophancy as a behavior; alignment-training treats reward-hacking as exploiting graders. Sub-problem: are the internal directions for 'be sycophantic to the user' and 'say what a grader rewards' the SAME direction, or distinct? If the same, anti-sycophancy steering also reduces reward-hacking.

**Direction:** Decompose the relationship by extracting two steering vectors on a small open model — one from sycophancy contrastive pairs, one from grader-pleasing contrastive pairs — and measuring their cosine similarity and cross-transfer (does steering with the sycophancy vector reduce grader-pleasing behavior?).

**Why it matters:** Clarifies whether two safety problems share one mechanistic lever, which would let a single cheap intervention address both. Open weights, single GPU, no training. A vector-overlap result is a tidy, communicable interpretability finding with concept-steering and alignment-training relevance.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy literature; reward-hacking of human oversight (scalable_oversight open problem); contrastive activation addition; Beaglehole et al. linear concept combination.
