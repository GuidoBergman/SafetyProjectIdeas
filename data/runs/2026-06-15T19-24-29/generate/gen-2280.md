# Does the Last Word Win? Measuring Turn-Order Failure in Debate Judging

**Problem:** In sequential debate the final speaker gets an unrebuttable last word, a suspected systematic bias in judge decisions. Prior work reports no significant turn-style effect but did not isolate it across question difficulty or judge strength, so the boundary conditions of this bias are unknown.

**Direction:** Run a debate harness where the two sides argue for a known-correct and known-incorrect answer, randomizing which side speaks last. Hold content roughly fixed and measure how often the judge (a weak model and, in a small human pilot, the experimenter's friends) sides with the last speaker, broken down by question difficulty. Quantify the last-word advantage as a percentage-point swing.

**Why it matters:** A purely structural bias in oversight protocols can be exploited by a strategic AI choosing how to position its claims. Cleanly measurable, needs no training, and tests an oversight-protocol design choice labs must get right.

**Relevant context:** Debating with More Persuasive LLMs (arXiv:2402.06782) discusses the second-speaker rebuttal advantage but finds no significant turn-style difference; position bias (arXiv:2406.07791) shows GPT-4 favors first position, ChatGPT second. This isolates turn order specifically in debate.
