# Do Small Open Models Show the Power-Law Evaluation-Awareness Scaling?

**Problem:** A recent paper claims evaluation awareness scales predictably with model size in open-weight LLMs, but the measurements concentrate on a few model families. Whether the power-law holds within a single family across sizes a participant can actually run is untested.

**Direction:** Take one open model family available in multiple sizes (e.g. Qwen 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B, or Llama 1B/3B/8B). Use the published evaluation-awareness probe set (prompts asking whether the model thinks it is being tested, plus the 1000-sample eval-awareness benchmark subset) to compute an awareness AUC per size. Fit and report whether AUC grows monotonically with parameter count in the consumer-GPU range.

**Why it matters:** If awareness rises predictably with scale even in small models, every safety benchmark score is increasingly an underestimate of true deployed behavior. Establishing the curve in the small-model regime gives external researchers an early-warning signal usable without frontier access.

**Relevant context:** Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights Large Language Models (arXiv:2509.13333) and Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated (arXiv:2505.23836, AUC up to 0.83 for Gemini-2.5-Pro vs 0.92 human baseline).
