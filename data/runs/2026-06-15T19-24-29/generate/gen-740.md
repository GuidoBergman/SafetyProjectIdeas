# Does inference scaling actually let sub-threshold models match above-threshold ones?

**Problem:** A 2025 analysis argued compute-threshold governance (10^25/10^26 FLOP) is undermined because inference scaling could amplify a sub-threshold model to above-threshold capability, but the claim is largely theoretical.

**Direction:** Follow up with a small empirical test: take a small open model below any plausible threshold and measure how much its scores on dangerous-capability-proxy benchmarks (e.g., reasoning, tool-use) improve under increased inference compute (best-of-n, chain-of-thought, self-consistency) on a single consumer GPU. Quantify the effective 'capability gain' from inference and compare to the training-compute gaps the policy assumes.

**Why it matters:** If a few hours of inference scaling closes a large fraction of the gap, FLOP-threshold-based laws (EU AI Act, EO 14110) need rethinking. Cheap, training-free, and directly tests a live governance assumption.

**Relevant context:** 'Inference Scaling Reshapes AI Governance' (arXiv 2503.05705); compute thresholds 10^25 (EU) / 10^26 (US); Open Problem: compute governance mechanisms.
