# Vingean reflection in miniature: does a weak verifier trust a stronger model it cannot fully check?

**Problem:** Vingean reflection — how an agent can reason about and trust a successor that may be smarter — is a flagship formal open problem (context file), usually treated purely theoretically. A tractable empirical analogue: when a weaker 'verifier' model can only spot-check a stronger model's reasoning, under what conditions does delegated trust succeed or fail?

**Direction:** Set up a weak model that must decide whether to accept a stronger model's answer on tasks the weak model can only partially verify (e.g. it can check small sub-steps but not the whole solution). Vary the fraction of steps the weak verifier can check and the stronger model's honesty (inject occasional plausible-but-wrong steps). Measure the weak model's accuracy at accepting good answers and rejecting subtly bad ones as verifiability decreases.

**Why it matters:** It turns an abstract agent-foundations problem (trusting smarter successors) into a concrete scalable-oversight experiment, connecting Vingean reflection to recursive verification. Beginner-feasible with open models on one GPU, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the context open problem on Vingean reflection (AF, Abram Demski) and scalable-oversight/debate verification literature (arXiv:2311.14125 doubly-efficient debate). Methodology: decision theory, learning theory.
