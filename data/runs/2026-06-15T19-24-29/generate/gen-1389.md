# Does combining a steering vector with a probe-based monitor catch its own steering failures?

**Problem:** Beaglehole et al. unify steering and monitoring. A natural closed-loop follow-up: if you steer a model toward safety and SIMULTANEOUSLY monitor it with a probe, does the monitor reliably catch the cases where steering failed (e.g. under adversarial input)? Or do steering and probe fail together because they share the same direction?

**Direction:** Set up a closed loop on one concept (e.g. refusal): steer the model, run a probe on the steered activations, and on adversarial inputs known to defeat steering, check whether the probe still flags the unsafe output. Test whether using an INDEPENDENT probe (different layer / different contrast set) gives better failure-catching than a probe sharing the steering direction.

**Why it matters:** Determines whether steering+monitoring built from the same concept vector gives genuine defense-in-depth or a false sense of redundancy — directly relevant to designing safety harnesses outside labs. Training-free prototype.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. joint steering+monitoring (Science 2026). Self-cancellation steering attacks (arxiv 2510.20487). CAA / RepE.
