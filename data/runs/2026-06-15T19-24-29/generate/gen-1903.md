# Misalignment Direction Transfer Across Open Model Families

**Problem:** Convergent Linear Representations of Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.11618) found a single residual-stream direction robustly mediates misalignment, and different finetuning protocols converge on similar internal directions. But it is unknown whether a direction extracted from one open model transfers to a structurally similar sibling (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B vs Qwen2.5-14B, or Llama-3.1-8B vs Llama-3.2-3B).

**Direction:** Extract the misalignment steering direction (via difference-of-means on aligned vs emergently-misaligned activations) from one publicly released emergent-misalignment LoRA model organism, then attempt to use that vector to steer a different open model from the same and different families. Measure how much steering with the foreign-model vector raises the misalignment-eval score versus the native vector, controlling for layer matching by normalized depth.

**Why it matters:** If misalignment directions transfer across models without any retraining, monitoring and steering defenses could be ported between models cheaply, and the result would be strong evidence that misalignment is a convergent, model-agnostic representation rather than an idiosyncratic artifact. This is counterfactually valuable: no training, no lab infrastructure, just activation arithmetic on open weights.

**Relevant context:** Convergent Linear Representations of Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.11618) reports a single direction mediates misalignment and converges across training protocols/datasets; Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.11613) releases open LoRA model organisms. Difference-of-means steering is standard activation-engineering methodology.
