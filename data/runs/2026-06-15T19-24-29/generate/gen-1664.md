# A Minimal Faithfulness Test Suite for Logit-Lens Claims

**Problem:** The logit lens is widely used to read off 'what a model is predicting' at intermediate layers, but it is known to be unreliable in early layers for some models (e.g., projecting the input token rather than a continuation). Researchers apply it without a standard check of whether it is even valid for their model, so conclusions can be artifacts of an unreliable lens.

**Direction:** Build a small, reusable diagnostic that, for any TransformerLens-supported model, quantifies logit-lens reliability per layer: agreement between logit-lens top-1 and final-output top-1, rate of 'echoing the input token,' and calibration vs. a quickly-trained tuned-lens (or affine probe). Output a per-layer 'trust score' and a red/green report. Validate on 3-4 small open models known to differ (e.g., GPT-2 small vs OPT-125M).

**Why it matters:** Interpretability-to-safety arguments often rest on logit-lens readings of intermediate states. A drop-in reliability tool prevents researchers from over-trusting a lens that is invalid for their model, and is exactly the kind of measurement infrastructure that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** The logit lens produces unreliable projections in early layers for BLOOM/OPT-125M (top-1 often the input token); it assumes all layers share one embedding space. The tuned lens (Belrose et al., 2303.08112) trains affine translators and is more predictive/reliable, demonstrating that lens reliability is model- and layer-dependent.
