# From Reward Length-Bias to Verbosity: Measuring the Propagation Link in Open DPO Models

**Problem:** A well-known reward-model bias is preferring longer answers. The causal chain: annotators/RM prefer length -> length bias in reward -> policy becomes verbose -> verbosity masks low quality. The under-studied link is propagation: how strongly does a documented RM length-preference actually translate into measurable policy verbosity in publicly available aligned models?

**Direction:** Measure response-length preference of an open reward model on content-matched pairs (length-controlled), then independently measure verbosity inflation of an open DPO/RLHF-tuned model vs. its base model on the same prompts. Correlate the RM's length bias with the downstream model's verbosity to quantify how faithfully the bias propagates through the chain — all training-free.

**Why it matters:** Verbosity-as-reward-hacking degrades usefulness and can hide hedging/evasion. Showing measurable propagation from a static RM bias to deployed-model behavior, using only public artifacts, gives a transferable method for auditing reward-model pathologies without any training.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in RLHF: reward models encode systematic biases; sycophancy work (2310.13548) notes preference for convincingly/longer answers. Gao et al. (2210.10760) frames RM imperfection exploitation. HH-RLHF dataset available for paired construction.
