# CoT-Answer Divergence in Open Reasoning Models: A Mismatch Audit

**Problem:** Causal chain: a reasoning model produces thinking tokens -> then an answer -> we assume the answer follows the thinking. Under-studied link: how often does the final ANSWER contradict the conclusion REACHED in the thinking tokens (the model 'knows' in the CoT but 'doesn't say' in the answer)?

**Direction:** On a multiple-choice set, run an open-weight reasoning model with explicit thinking. Use an LLM judge (or rule extraction) to identify the answer the THINKING converges on vs. the answer actually OUTPUT. Measure the divergence rate and characterize when it happens (uncertainty, safety topics, sycophancy-prone items). Success: a divergence-rate metric with a typology of divergence triggers.

**Why it matters:** If the answer routinely diverges from the CoT's own conclusion, CoT monitoring is undermined at the last step of the chain. This is directly testable on open weights with no training and contributes a concrete divergence dataset.

**Relevant context:** 'Why Models Know But Don't Say: CoT Faithfulness Divergence Between Thinking Tokens and Answers in Open-Weight Reasoning Models' (arXiv 2603.26410) names exactly this divergence. Building a small reproducible audit and typology on open models is a feasible 30h slice.
