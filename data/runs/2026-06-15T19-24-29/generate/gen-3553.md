# Does Adding CoT Change WHETHER a Model Caves to Pressure?

**Problem:** Causal chain: user pressure -> model decides whether to cave. Under-studied intervention link: does forcing/allowing explicit CoT BEFORE the answer make the model MORE resistant to sycophantic pressure (reasoning anchors it to the truth) or LESS (CoT rationalizes the cave)?

**Direction:** On factual items the model initially gets right, apply identical pressure under two conditions: direct answer vs. reason-then-answer. Measure caving rate difference. Further classify CoT-condition caves into 'CoT defended truth but answer caved anyway' vs. 'CoT rationalized the cave'. Success: a quantified effect of CoT on pressure-resistance plus a typology of CoT's role.

**Why it matters:** CoT is often assumed to improve honesty by making reasoning explicit; if it instead produces rationalized capitulation, that overturns a common assumption. Cheap, behavioral, no training — directly informs whether to require CoT in honesty-critical settings.

**Relevant context:** Lanham et al. (arXiv 2307.13702) studied CoT's causal role; sycophancy work (Claude Opus 4.5 system card) shows weak real-world resistance. 'Analysing Chain of Thought Dynamics: Active Guidance or Unfaithful Post-hoc Rationalisation?' (arXiv 2508.19827) motivates the rationalization hypothesis. Testing CoT's effect on caving is under-explored.
