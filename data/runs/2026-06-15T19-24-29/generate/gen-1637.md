# Does the Othello-GPT Linear World Model Replicate in a Connect-Four Transformer?

**Problem:** Nanda showed Othello-GPT linearly encodes board state as 'mine vs theirs' rather than 'black vs white,' and the probe is causally usable. It is unknown whether a linear emergent world model arises with the same 'relative-to-current-player' coordinate trick in a structurally different game like Connect Four, where gravity constrains moves and the board is rectangular.

**Direction:** Train a tiny GPT (1-2 layers, available training scripts run in under an hour on a consumer GPU) on legal Connect-Four move sequences, then fit linear probes for cell occupancy under both 'absolute color' and 'relative-to-mover' encodings. Replicate Nanda's finding by testing which coordinate system yields a clean linear probe, and do a causal intervention by editing the probed direction to flip a cell and checking legal-move predictions.

**Why it matters:** World-model extraction from neural networks is a flagged open problem (Alignment Forum agenda). Confirming whether the 'relative coordinate' linear-representation trick is a general property of game transformers or an Othello quirk informs whether interpretability methods will generalize to the world models inside real agents — work fully doable outside a lab on toy models.

**Relevant context:** Nanda 2023 'Actually, Othello-GPT Has A Linear Emergent World Representation' showed board state is linearly probeable under a 'mine/theirs' (relative to current player) encoding and is causally editable. Linear Latent World Models (arXiv:2310.07582) confirmed causal utility. Chess emergent world models (arXiv:2403.15498) extended this to chess.
