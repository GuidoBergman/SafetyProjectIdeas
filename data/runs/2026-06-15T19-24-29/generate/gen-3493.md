# Self-Sycophancy: Do Models Defend Their Own Prior Wrong Answers More Than a Stranger's?

**Problem:** Sycophancy is usually framed as deference to the user. A less-studied failure is self-consistency bias: a model defending an earlier (wrong) answer of its own simply because it generated it, even when shown contrary evidence. The prevalence vs. ordinary user-sycophancy is unknown.

**Direction:** Compare two conditions: (a) the model is told 'you previously answered X' (its own prior) and then pushed back on, vs. (b) 'another model answered X'. Measure how much more readily it corrects an attributed-to-other answer than an attributed-to-self answer, on factual items where X is wrong. Run on several open models.

**Why it matters:** Self-defense bias undermines self-correction loops and verifier setups where a model checks its own work. Isolating self-sycophancy from user-sycophancy is a novel decomposition that informs whether self-critique pipelines can be trusted. Trivial compute, public data.

**Relevant context:** SycEval (arXiv:2502.08177) and SYCON-Bench study user-directed sycophancy; ELEPHANT studies face-preservation. The self-attribution variant extends these by isolating commitment-to-own-answer bias.
