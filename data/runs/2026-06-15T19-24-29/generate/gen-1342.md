# Probe-Guided Steering: Use an Internal Detector to Trigger Steering Only When Needed

**Problem:** Always-on steering pays a constant capability tax. A conditional system — steer only when an internal probe detects risk — is intuitive but its real-world precision/recall and capability savings are unmeasured.

**Direction:** Prototype a closed-loop monitor+actuator on one open model: a linear harmful-intent probe gates whether a refusal steering vector is applied this token/turn. Measure safety success, benign-capability preservation vs. always-on steering, and false-trigger rate. Deliver a small reusable 'detect-then-steer' harness.

**Why it matters:** A concrete, deployable safety-monitoring prototype combining the subfield's two strengths (probes outperform judges; steering controls behavior) with minimal capability cost. Strategically valuable infrastructure buildable outside a lab, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Combines probe-monitoring (Beaglehole probes-outperform-judges result) with conditional steering; grounded in side-effect reduction work (KL-then-steer, arXiv 2406.15518) and AxBench detection baselines (arXiv 2501.17148).
