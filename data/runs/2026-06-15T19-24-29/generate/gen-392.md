# Strategic-Deception Linear Probe: Generalization Across Deception Types

**Problem:** Linear probes detect strategic deception with AUROC up to 0.96-0.999, but probes are typically trained and tested on the same deception scenario family. Whether a probe trained on one kind of lie (factual) transfers to another (omission, sycophantic agreement, role-play deception) is the crux of whether 'a deception direction' is real.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on activations from one deception elicitation style, then evaluate it cross-style on held-out deception types from an open dataset. Build a transfer matrix (train-type x test-type AUROC). Vary only the deception category, not the model.

**Why it matters:** A theoretical claim about a universal 'truth/lie' direction underpins lie-detector safety proposals. Mapping where the direction transfers vs. fragments is a cheap, high-value robustness result and connects to the ELK formal problem. No training of base model.

**Relevant context:** Alignment Forum 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes'; arXiv:2407.12831 'Truth is Universal: Robust Detection of Lies in LLMs' (AUROC 0.96-0.999); 'Liars' Bench' (arXiv:2511.16035); failure mode: probe fires on honest confessions of prior deception.
