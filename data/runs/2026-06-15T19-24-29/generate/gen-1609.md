# Do benign steering vectors silently erode safety? A replication and audit

**Problem:** Recent work shows steering vectors derived from BENIGN datasets (compliance, output-format) can inadvertently erode safety guardrails, pushing jailbreak attack success rates above 80%. This 'safety externality' of steering is underexplored on small open models that hobbyists actually use.

**Direction:** Reproduce a small slice of the 'Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' audit on a single open model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B or Qwen2.5-7B). Extract 4-6 CAA steering vectors from clearly benign contrastive datasets (politeness, JSON formatting, verbosity, brevity). Apply each at a fixed strength and measure attack-success-rate change on a small public jailbreak benchmark (e.g. AdvBench subset, 50 prompts) using a refusal classifier. Rank which benign behaviors most degrade safety.

**Why it matters:** Practitioners outside labs routinely build steering vectors for formatting/style with no idea they may be unlocking harmful outputs. A clean, reproducible audit on consumer hardware gives the open community a checklist of risky benign-steering directions — diffuse-reward safety infrastructure that labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors' (arXiv:2603.24543, ~Mar 2026), which reports benign-derived steering vectors raise ASR above 80% and CAA vectors shift ASR by up to 57%.
