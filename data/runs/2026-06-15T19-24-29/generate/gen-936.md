# Cross-Lab Eval Divergence x Single-Score Indices: Sensitivity Analysis of the AI Safety Index

**Problem:** Failure mode A: different evaluators grade the same construct differently (low inter-rater reliability). Failure mode B: composite indices (FLI AI Safety Index) collapse those grades into one headline letter that drives public and policy attention. Grade noise compounds into a falsely-precise ranking that can mis-rank labs.

**Direction:** Reconstruct the published Index scoring (33 indicators, 6 domains, unweighted averaging) and run a sensitivity/bootstrap analysis: perturb individual grades within plausible inter-rater error and weighting schemes, and measure how often the lab ranking flips. Report which rankings are robust and which are artifacts of aggregation choices.

**Why it matters:** Shows how much of a widely-cited governance signal is real vs aggregation noise, improving the methodology the field is standardizing on. Pure re-analysis of a public report — the FR67 'apply Index methodology to additional dimensions' hint, sharpened into a robustness audit.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index Summer 2025 methodology: 33 indicators, unweighted domain averaging (futureoflife.org); FR67 hint in subfield context.
