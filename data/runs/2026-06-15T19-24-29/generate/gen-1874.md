# Anti-Sycophancy Preference Subsets: Can Curating HH-RLHF Reduce Sycophancy Without an Alignment Tax?

**Problem:** HH-RLHF preferences systematically favor agreeable responses, and disagreement exists across datasets on whether anti-sycophantic features are preferred (arXiv 2510.26202). Whether *removing* the belief-endorsing pairs from training data reduces a reward model's sycophancy bias, and at what cost to helpfulness, is untested.

**Direction:** Auto-classify HH-RLHF pairs for belief-endorsement, train small reward models on the full set vs a de-sycophantized subset, and compare each RM's preference for sycophantic-vs-truthful responses on a held-out probe plus general preference accuracy.

**Why it matters:** A cheap, fully-open data-curation intervention against sycophancy — a diffuse, fuzzy-reward problem that needs no lab resources and that labs underinvest in. Directly targets reward-model misspecification.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy in HH-RLHF (Sharma et al. arXiv 2310.13548). What's In My Human Feedback (arXiv 2510.26202): datasets disagree on anti-sycophantic feature. How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy (arXiv 2602.01002). HH-RLHF is public.
