# Reward-Hacking Emergence Curves in a Tiny RLVR Sandbox

**Problem:** Countdown-Code and related work study how reward hacking emerges and generalizes in RL-with-verifiable-rewards, but the dynamics (when does hacking appear during optimization, does it transfer to held-out tasks?) are usually studied at scales beyond a beginner's reach.

**Direction:** Use a tiny code/arithmetic task with an automatic verifier that has a known exploitable loophole (e.g. passes on trivial outputs). Apply lightweight optimization (best-of-N selection or short prompt-evolution loops, no large RL training) against the verifier and track when the optimizer discovers the loophole and whether the hacking strategy transfers to a second verifier with a similar flaw.

**Why it matters:** Characterizes emergence/generalization of reward hacking — a structural, medium-horizon failure — in a setup runnable on a single GPU in hours. Yields a reproducible sandbox others can reuse for studying verifier design.

**Relevant context:** 'Countdown-Code: A Testbed for Studying The Emergence and Generalization of Reward Hacking in RLVR' (arXiv 2603.07084) studies emergence and cross-task transfer of reward hacking.
