# Auto-Interp Hallucination Rate: Do Explainer LLMs Invent Patterns?

**Problem:** Auto-interp explainer LLMs may confidently describe a coherent pattern even for random or meaningless feature directions, inflating apparent interpretability. There is no standard control measuring the false-positive 'explanation hallucination' rate of an auto-interp pipeline.

**Direction:** Feed the auto-interp pipeline a mix of real SAE features and control directions (random directions, shuffled activation sets) and measure how often it produces confident, high-scoring explanations for the controls. Report a hallucination/false-positive rate as a calibration metric for the pipeline.

**Why it matters:** If the pipeline 'interprets' noise, then high auto-interp coverage numbers overstate real understanding, biasing safety assessments. A control-based hallucination rate is a simple, reusable calibration tool requiring only public SAEs and a cheap explainer LLM.

**Relevant context:** Delphi/Neuronpedia auto-interp generates explanations from activating examples and scores them; the pipeline's behavior on null/control inputs is not standardly reported. SAEBench's warning about misleading proxy metrics motivates measuring explainer false positives.
