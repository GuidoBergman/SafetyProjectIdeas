# Syntax-vs-Semantics in Benign Relearning: What Actually Reawakens Forgotten Knowledge?

**Problem:** A 2026 result (arXiv 2602.03379) argues syntax, not semantic content, is the hidden driver of unlearning failures during benign relearning. This reframing is surprising and underexplored: it implies that matching the *form* of forget-set text, with no overlapping content, can resurrect unlearned knowledge.

**Direction:** Construct relearning sets that match the forget set on syntax (templates, formatting) but not content, vs sets matching content but not syntax, and measure which reawakens forgotten capabilities on a small open unlearned checkpoint. Disentangle the two drivers experimentally.

**Why it matters:** If syntax alone reactivates dangerous knowledge, content-based data filters used by deployers are mis-targeted. A tractable mechanistic-style study of an unlearning failure mode, no lab data required.

**Relevant context:** Rethinking Benign Relearning: Syntax as the Hidden Driver of Unlearning Failures (arXiv 2602.03379). Open Problems in Machine Unlearning (arXiv 2501.04952). Public unlearning checkpoints/benchmarks (TOFU, BLUR).
