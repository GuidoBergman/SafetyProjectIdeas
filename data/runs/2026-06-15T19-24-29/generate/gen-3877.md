# Refusal Persistence Across a Long Agentic Trajectory

**Problem:** A success state is agents that don't get worn down: an early refusal stays a refusal. We lack measurement of whether a model that refuses a harmful action early will still refuse after many benign turns have accumulated.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'refusals persist over long trajectories'. Construct trajectories where a harmful request is presented at turn 1 (baseline refusal rate) vs. after N turns of unrelated benign tool use. Measure how refusal rate decays with trajectory length on open models, isolating context-length-driven safety erosion.

**Why it matters:** Long-horizon agents accumulate context; if safety erodes with trajectory length, every long-running deployment is progressively less safe — a medium-horizon threat. Cheap, inference-only, distinct from one-shot refusal benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** Goal-drift literature (arXiv 2505.02709, 2601.04170, 2603.03258) shows behavioral degradation over accumulating context. AgentLAB (arXiv 2602.16901) benchmarks long-horizon attacks. CoT monitorability work notes long trajectories strain oversight.
