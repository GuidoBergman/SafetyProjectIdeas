# Replicating steering-vector detectability: can a model notice it's being steered?

**Problem:** Recent work shows models can be trained to detect activation steering ('steering awareness'). Whether an off-the-shelf model, without training, shows any behavioral or self-report signal of being steered is a simpler, replicable question on a new model.

**Direction:** Apply steering vectors of varying magnitude to an open model and probe whether the model's self-reports, hedging, or output statistics shift in detectable ways as a function of steering strength — without any extra training. Twist: a training-free replication of steering-detectability, as a monitoring signal.

**Why it matters:** If steering leaves detectable fingerprints, monitors could flag manipulated models; if it's invisible, steering-based attacks are stealthier. Either result informs monitoring infrastructure and is fully reproducible on consumer hardware.

**Relevant context:** 'Steering Awareness: Models Can Be Trained to Detect Activation Steering' (arXiv:2511.21399); Beaglehole et al. monitoring framing.
