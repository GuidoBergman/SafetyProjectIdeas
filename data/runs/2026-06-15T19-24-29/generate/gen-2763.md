# Judge-Self-Preference Contamination of Safety Leaderboards

**Problem:** LLM judges show self-preference bias (rating their own family's outputs higher). When an LLM judges a safety benchmark that includes outputs from its own model family, leaderboards may be silently biased — rarely quantified for safety.

**Direction:** Construct a round-robin: have several open judge models score harmfulness of outputs from several generator models (including the judges' own families). Measure the self-preference effect size on safety verdicts and estimate how much it shifts a leaderboard when a single family's judge is used. Recommend a debiasing protocol (e.g. cross-family-only judging).

**Why it matters:** Exposes a structural bias in the LLM-as-judge safety pipeline that any outside auditor can fall into; improves scorer reliability, an explicit open problem.

**Relevant context:** Autograders exhibit self-bias assigning higher scores to same-LLM responses (Skewed Score, arXiv:2507.03772); LLM Judges Inconsistently Disagree (arXiv:2605.31381).
