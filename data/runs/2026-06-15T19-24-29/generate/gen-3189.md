# Characterizing the Confidence-Honesty Gap When Models Guess

**Problem:** A subtle deception failure is presenting a guess as a confident derivation — a form of unfaithful reasoning where the model hides its uncertainty. How often models verbally express high confidence on items they actually get wrong (and whether forcing a probability shrinks the gap) is a measurable boundary condition.

**Direction:** On a benchmark with ground truth, ask models to (a) answer and state verbal confidence, and (b) answer and give a numeric probability. Compute calibration and an 'overclaiming' rate (high stated confidence + wrong) per condition and model. Test whether deceptive overconfidence concentrates on harder items, and whether eliciting numeric probabilities reduces it.

**Why it matters:** Overclaiming confidence is a low-grade but pervasive honesty failure that misleads human overseers. Quantifying it and finding a cheap elicitation fix (numeric probabilities) is actionable and needs only API access — relevant to honesty-verification agendas.

**Relevant context:** UK AISI agenda on verifying honesty properties; CoT-faithfulness literature (Anthropic 2025) shows models hide the true basis of answers. Sycophancy/overconfidence interplay noted across SycEval-style work.
