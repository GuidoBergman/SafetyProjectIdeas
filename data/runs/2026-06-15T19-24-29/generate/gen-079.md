# Spectral / power-law analysis of activation probes for jailbreak detection robustness across architectures

**Problem:** Activation-probe jailbreak detectors are reported as cheap and effective, but we don't know how the probe direction's geometry varies across model families, or whether a probe trained on one architecture transfers — limiting reuse.

**Direction:** Apply tools from spectral analysis / signal processing. Train simple linear probes for 'harmful-intent' on hidden states of several small open models, then analyze the spectral/cosine geometry of the learned directions and how harmful vs. benign activations separate along principal components. Test cross-model probe transfer and whether a low-rank shared subspace exists. Quantify transfer accuracy vs. layer.

**Why it matters:** Establishes whether cheap activation probes are portable across open models (a replication gap), informing whether the community can share one probe rather than retrain per model. Linear probing only; fits 30h and consumer GPU.
