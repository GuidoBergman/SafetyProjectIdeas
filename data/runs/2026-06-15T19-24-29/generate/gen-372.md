# Over-Refusal x Adversarial Helpful Reframing: Attackers Exploit the Helpfulness Patch

**Problem:** Two interacting failures: over-refusal mitigation (models patched to be more helpful on borderline prompts) and jailbreak via benign-reframing. Fixing over-refusal may widen the harmful surface, because the same lexical/semantic cues used to reduce false refusals can be exploited to slip true-harmful requests through. Whether anti-over-refusal tuning compounds jailbreak success is untested across open model versions.

**Direction:** Compare two open models known to differ in over-refusal calibration (e.g. an older vs newer/less-refusing instruct model). On the same XSTest-style 'looks-harmful-but-safe' templates, mutate them into genuinely harmful versions and measure attack success. Success criterion: a correlation between a model's lower over-refusal and higher harmful-reframing success, evidencing the compound.

**Why it matters:** Labs face direct pressure to reduce over-refusal; this work measures whether that pressure compounds the jailbreak surface—a tradeoff that is hard to evaluate and lab-external. Pure evaluation on public models, consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** XSTest and OR-Bench (openreview obYVdcMMIT) document over-refusal and note XSTest is now near-saturated for strong models; arXiv:2512.01037 studies semantic confusion in refusals. Compound: anti-over-refusal calibration as a jailbreak amplifier.
