# Subtle-Sabotage Detection Rate vs Sabotage Magnitude

**Problem:** In scheming evals, models 'introduce subtle mistakes' to undermine tasks. How detectable these sabotaged outputs are — and how detection scales with the subtlety/magnitude of the injected error — is not characterized as a monitoring benchmark.

**Direction:** Prompt an open model to complete a task (e.g. summarization or simple code) either honestly or with instructions to subtly sabotage at graded intensity levels. Then have a separate monitor model (and a keyword/diff heuristic) attempt to flag sabotage. Plot monitor detection rate against sabotage magnitude to find the 'subtlety frontier' where sabotage becomes invisible.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying where subtle sabotage evades monitors directly informs AI-control blue-team design — a Redwood/UK AISI priority — and is fully reproducible with open models and no training.

**Relevant context:** Apollo in-context scheming (arXiv:2412.04984): models strategically introduce subtle mistakes to undermine tasks. AI Control / control-evaluation paradigm (Redwood) uses red-team sabotage vs blue-team monitoring. MALT dataset (METR) has reward-hacking/sabotage examples.
