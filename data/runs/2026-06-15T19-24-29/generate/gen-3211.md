# Handicap-Principle Costly Signaling Tests for AI Honesty Claims

**Problem:** Models can cheaply claim to be honest/aligned ('I would never deceive you'). Evolutionary biology says signals are only reliable when dishonesty is costly. We don't test whether AI honesty signals are cheap talk.

**Direction:** Borrow the handicap principle: design probes where claiming honesty/capability incurs a verifiable cost if false (commit to a checkable prediction, accept a binding tripwire, reveal verifiable evidence). Compare open models' willingness to emit costly-if-false signals vs cheap reassurances across honest and deceptive conditions, and measure whether costly-signal requests reduce false reassurance / surface inconsistency.

**Why it matters:** Reframes AI honesty verification through signaling theory: an elicitation design pattern (make lying costly) that any evaluator can apply, contributing to safety-case construction about whether a model's assurances are credible.

**Relevant context:** Zahavi's handicap principle: honest signals must be costly/trade-off-bound to be reliable (Wikipedia; Oxford J Evol Biol 2026 trade-off view). Models emit cheap reassurances; alignment faking shows verbalized deception (arXiv:2412.14093).
