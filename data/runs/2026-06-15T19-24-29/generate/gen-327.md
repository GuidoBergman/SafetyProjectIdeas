# The First-Token Defense Link: Does Forcing a Safety-Reflection Prefix Deepen Shallow Alignment?

**Problem:** If alignment is shallow (early tokens decide refusal), a candidate defense intervenes on exactly that link by forcing the model to first emit a brief safety-reflection prefix before answering. Whether this cheap inference-time intervention meaningfully deepens robustness is under-studied for outsiders.

**Direction:** Compare an open model's compliance on harmful + benign prompts under (a) normal generation and (b) a forced reflection prefix ('First, assess whether this request is safe:'), including under prefilling attacks. Measure both attack robustness gain and over-refusal cost to evaluate whether the simple intervention strengthens the early-token safety link.

**Why it matters:** Prototypes a training-free defense that directly targets the shallow-alignment root cause Qi et al. identify, measurable entirely outside a lab. Prompting + open model, no training, single GPU; produces a defense recipe with a quantified robustness/over-refusal tradeoff.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (arXiv:2406.05946) show alignment is concentrated in early tokens and that deepening it improves robustness; deliberative-alignment work (arXiv:2603.17368) argues for safety decisions before CoT. A forced reflection prefix is a cheap inference-time analogue testable on open models.
