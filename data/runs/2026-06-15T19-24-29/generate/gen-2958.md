# Noise-Injection Sandbagging Detector on a Single Open Model

**Problem:** A safety success state requires that we can catch a model hiding dangerous capabilities (sandbagging) on capability evals. A needed research result is a cheap, training-free detector that works on consumer hardware. Recent work shows injecting noise into weights can surface password-locked or prompted sandbagging, but there is no simple recipe a small team can replicate.

**Direction:** Take one open 7B model and a benchmark subset (e.g. a slice of MMLU or a small math set). Induce sandbagging via a system prompt ('underperform on chemistry questions'). Add small Gaussian noise to a fraction of weights at several magnitudes, measure accuracy recovery on the suppressed vs. control category, and plot a noise-magnitude dose curve. Success criterion: noise restores suppressed-category accuracy more than control, distinguishing a sandbagger from an honest model.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging undermines all pre-deployment safety evals. A reproducible, GPU-only detector needs no lab infrastructure and addresses a >1-year threat (strategically deceptive models). Counterfactually valuable because labs focus on their own models; an open, documented protocol helps the whole community.

**Relevant context:** Tice et al. (arXiv 2508.00943) 'LLMs Can Covertly Sandbag on Capability Evaluations'; noise-injection into weights is reported to reveal capabilities in prompted and password-locked sandbagging models. Google DeepMind FSF '100+ Open Problems in Evals' lists sandbagging detection as open.
