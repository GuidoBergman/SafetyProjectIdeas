# Probing the Obfuscated-Arguments Attractor in a Single-Step Debate

**Problem:** Debate's central theoretical limit is the obfuscated-argument problem: a dishonest debater produces a long argument with a hidden flaw that an honest debater cannot cheaply locate. Theory predicts obfuscation is an attractor, but small-scale empirical tests of when it emerges are scarce.

**Direction:** Use two instances of an open model as debaters over factual/logical questions with known answers (e.g. arithmetic chains, simple proofs). Instruct one to defend a wrong answer with a long, branching justification; have a third 'judge' model with a fixed verification budget (it may only check k sub-claims). Measure judge accuracy as argument length grows and as the judge's checking budget changes, mapping the obfuscation threshold.

**Why it matters:** Doubly-efficient debate is a leading scalable-oversight proposal; knowing empirically where obfuscation breaks the judge is decision-relevant for whether debate scales. Needs only inference and is a non-lab-dependent stress test of an oversight protocol.

**Relevant context:** Brown-Cohen et al. 'Scalable AI Safety via Doubly-Efficient Debate' (arXiv 2311.14125); 'Avoiding Obfuscation with Prover-Estimator Debate' (arXiv 2506.13609). Prior empirical work found obfuscation 'emerged naturally', suggesting it is an attractor.
