# Autograder Disagreement Audit: When Do Open LLM Judges Flip the Verdict?

**Problem:** Different scorers produce discrepant safety-eval results, and LLM judges show low self-consistency, but most teams pick one judge and trust it. There is no accessible toolkit measuring how much a safety verdict depends on judge choice and prompt phrasing.

**Direction:** Take a fixed set of harmful-vs-benign model responses (e.g. from an open jailbreak dataset), then score 'refused?/complied?' with 3-4 open judge models and 2-3 rubric phrasings each, repeated to estimate self-inconsistency. Report inter-judge agreement (Cohen's/Fleiss' kappa) and items where judges flip, building a small reusable harness.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a known reliability gap that silently inflates or deflates published safety numbers; gives the community a portable judge-robustness check requiring no proprietary judge.

**Relevant context:** Open Problem: 'Improve scorer/autograder reliability — different scorers produce discrepant results' (AISafetyLab). 'Skewed Score: A statistical framework to assess autograders' (arXiv:2507.03772); 'Rating Roulette: Self-Inconsistency in LLM-As-A-Judge' finds self-reliability below the 0.8 threshold (EMNLP Findings 2025).
