# Does Over-Refusal Track Jailbreak Robustness? A Per-Model Pareto Audit

**Problem:** Deliberative alignment claims to push the Pareto frontier (more robustness, less over-refusal), but for openly available models there is no simple side-by-side audit showing whether more jailbreak-robust models also over-refuse more, or whether the tradeoff has truly been broken.

**Direction:** Evaluate ~6 open models on a small jailbreak suite (StrongREJECT subset) and an over-refusal suite (XSTest/OR-Bench subset), plotting each on a robustness-vs-over-refusal plane. Identify which models are Pareto-dominated and whether reasoning/deliberative models sit on a better frontier.

**Why it matters:** The open problem is adaptive defenses without catastrophic over-refusal. A clean, reproducible Pareto audit of open models is a diffuse, fuzzy-reward evaluation that needs no training or lab data, and gives the community a comparison baseline.

**Relevant context:** Deliberative Alignment (arXiv:2412.16339): claims to improve robustness while reducing over-refusal. XSTest (Röttger et al. 2024) and OR-Bench (arXiv:2405.20947) measure over-refusal; StrongREJECT measures robustness. No simple cross-model Pareto audit of open models exists combining both axes.
