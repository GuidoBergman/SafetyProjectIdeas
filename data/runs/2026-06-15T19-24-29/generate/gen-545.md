# Causal vs Correlational Goodhart: Does Intervening on the Proxy Move the Goal?

**Problem:** Causal Goodhart (per Manheim & Garrabrant) occurs when proxy and goal share a non-causal correlation, so intervening on the proxy fails to move the goal. This specific link in the reward-hacking chain — confusing observation with intervention — is rarely separated empirically from regressional Goodhart.

**Direction:** Construct toy structural causal models (3-5 variables) where a proxy is correlated with but not causally upstream of the true goal. Use an LLM or simple optimizer prompted to 'maximize the proxy' and measure whether interventional optimization moves the true goal versus merely tracking it observationally. Quantify how often optimizers exploit the non-causal edge, and test whether telling the model the causal graph reduces causal Goodharting.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishing causal from regressional Goodhart is theoretically important but almost never demonstrated cleanly. A crisp toy demonstration plus a measure of how often LLMs fall for non-causal proxies is a self-contained, lab-independent contribution to reward-hacking science.

**Relevant context:** Manheim & Garrabrant (2018) causal Goodhart category; Pearl's do-calculus framing of intervention vs observation; recent SCM-based reward-hacking work (Causal Reward Adjustment, arXiv:2508.04216) frames hacking via structural causal models.
