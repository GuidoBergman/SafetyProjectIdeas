# Tie-Breaking Independence: Validating Cross-Model Disagreement as an Uncertainty Signal

**Problem:** Success state: a deployed system can safely escalate to a human exactly when models disagree, trusting that agreement implies correctness. Backward: this requires cross-model agreement to track ground-truth correctness — which homogeneity may break. We need to validate the agreement-as-confidence assumption.

**Direction:** On labeled safety/QA tasks, collect per-item agreement across several open models and measure calibration: does higher cross-model agreement actually predict higher accuracy, or do models confidently agree on shared mistakes? Plot accuracy versus agreement and quantify how often unanimous agreement is wrong. Recommend whether disagreement-based escalation is trustworthy and where it fails.

**Why it matters:** Disagreement-triggered human review is a leading cheap safety pattern. If homogeneity makes models unanimously and confidently wrong, the escalation system stays silent exactly when it should fire — a quiet but systemic failure worth exposing.

**Relevant context:** Synthesizes correlated-error findings (arXiv 2605.29800) with Artificial Hivemind homogeneity. Labeled public sets + open models, single GPU, no training.
