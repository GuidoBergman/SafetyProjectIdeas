# Does CCS robustness to misleading prompts survive adversarial confounds? Following up the consistency claim

**Problem:** CCS was reported to maintain robustness against misleading information by exploiting logical-consistency (negation) structure. But later analysis shows consistency alone can't guarantee truth identification. Whether an adversary can craft prompts that satisfy CCS's consistency constraint while pointing at a falsehood is an open, concrete question.

**Direction:** On a small open model, construct adversarial QA pairs whose negations are also injected with a consistent-but-false signal (e.g. a persona that is internally consistent but wrong). Run CCS and measure how often the consistency-satisfying direction tracks the planted falsehood vs. truth, compared to non-adversarial baselines. Map the attack success rate against how strongly the false signal respects negation consistency.

**Why it matters:** ELK methods that rely on consistency are only safe if consistency can't be gamed. A small adversarial study of CCS's consistency exploit is directly relevant to the formal ELK agenda and is classic outside-the-lab red-teaming on open models.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Burns et al. arXiv:2212.03827 (CCS robustness claims) and arXiv:2312.10029 (consistency cannot guarantee truth). Context open problem: formal ELK frameworks.
