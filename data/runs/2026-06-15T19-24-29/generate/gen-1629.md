# Prompted introspection: does telling a model how steering works increase its self-detection?

**Problem:** Recent introspection work found that prompting a model with accurate information about AI introspection mechanisms dramatically strengthens its ability to report injected concepts, even though sampled outputs often deny injection. The size of this 'introspection-prompt boost' for small open models is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Inject concept steering vectors into a small open model under two prompt conditions: (a) neutral, (b) a primer explaining that concepts may be injected and how to introspect. Measure the model's accuracy at reporting whether/which concept was injected in each condition, and compare to a logit-lens / residual-stream probe of the same activations. Quantify the boost.

**Why it matters:** If a simple prompt can make models self-report manipulation, that's a cheap, deployable monitoring layer — and if their verbal report lags an internal probe signal, it reveals models hiding what they 'know.' Both outcomes matter for honesty/monitoring and need no training.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Latent Introspection: Models Can Detect Prior Concept Injections' (arXiv:2602.20031, Feb 2026) and 'Mechanisms of Introspective Awareness' (arXiv:2603.21396, ~Mar 2026).
