# Vagueness Metrics from Privacy-Policy Linguistics Applied to AI Safety Commitments

**Problem:** AI safety frameworks and the EU Code of Practice are criticized as vague, but vagueness is asserted rather than measured. Without a metric, 'this commitment is too weak' is unfalsifiable.

**Direction:** Transfer the obfuscating-word/vagueness lexicon methodology used to measure privacy-policy vagueness (counting hedges like 'might', 'where appropriate', 'state-of-the-art') into a Python pipeline. Compile a corpus of public safety frameworks + the Code of Practice, compute vagueness density, readability grade, and commitment-strength scores per sentence, and compare across companies and over time.

**Why it matters:** Gives policymakers a cheap, reproducible specificity score to track whether voluntary commitments are tightening or de-specifying. Directly addresses the open problem of the gap between voluntary codes and enforceable standards.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2511.21758 (longitudinal vagueness measurement of LLM privacy policies, ~75% of sentences contain obfuscating words); CSET EU Code of Safety analysis; framework corpus is fully public.
