# Grokking as a delayed-generalization failure: how small can the data fraction get before generalization never arrives?

**Problem:** Grokking is a striking generalization phenomenon used as a testbed for theories of inductive bias, but it has a failure boundary: below some training-data fraction the network memorizes and *never* groks. The location and sharpness of this 'no-grok' boundary, and what predicts it, are not cleanly characterized for beginners-reproducible setups.

**Direction:** Reproduce grokking on modular-arithmetic with a tiny transformer (Power et al. setup runs on one GPU in hours). Sweep training-data fraction and weight decay on a grid, recording for each cell whether/when validation accuracy generalizes within a fixed step budget. Produce a phase diagram of the grok / no-grok / never-grok regions and test a simple predictor (e.g., final weight norm or train-loss curvature) for the boundary.

**Why it matters:** Inductive-bias theory and its alignment implications (Open Phil RFP) are hard to test; grokking is one of the few clean, cheap empirical handles. Charting the failure boundary connects abstract generalization theory to a reproducible experiment a beginner can run.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in 'Grokking' (Power et al., arXiv:2201.02177) and 'Unifying Grokking and Double Descent' (arXiv:2303.06173); explicitly noted as quickly reproducible on a single GPU.
