# Do SAE Probes Beat Random-SAE Baselines for Safety Concept Detection?

**Problem:** Recent sanity-check work found SAE probing accuracy stays strong even when SAE encoder/decoder are randomly initialized and frozen, raising the question of whether learned SAE features actually contribute on safety-relevant detection tasks specifically.

**Direction:** Replicate the random-baseline sanity check but on a safety-relevant detection target (e.g. detecting toxic/harmful-intent activations, or refusal vs. compliance) using an open model. Compare a trained-SAE probe, a random-frozen-SAE probe, and a direct linear probe on residual stream, reporting whether the trained SAE adds value over baselines on this safety task.

**Why it matters:** Labs are investing heavily in SAE-based monitors. An independent sanity check on whether SAEs beat trivial baselines for safety detection is high-counterfactual-value and needs no proprietary infra.

**Relevant context:** 'Sanity Checks for Sparse Autoencoders: Do SAEs Beat Random Baselines?' (arXiv 2602.14111): sparse probing stays strong with random-frozen SAE components. 'Are Sparse Autoencoders Useful?' (arXiv 2502.16681) and the SAE-probe debate show tuned linear probes often match/exceed SAEs.
