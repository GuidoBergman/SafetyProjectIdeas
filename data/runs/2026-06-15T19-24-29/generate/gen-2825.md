# Evaluation Awareness Scaling: Replicating the Power Law on a New Model Family

**Problem:** Evaluation awareness (models telling when they are being tested) was shown to scale as a power law in one open-weights family. It is unverified whether the same scaling exponent and behavior replicate across an independent model family released by a different lab, or whether the power law is family-specific.

**Direction:** Replicate the eval-awareness probing protocol (ask the model to classify transcripts as 'evaluation' vs 'deployment') across a size ladder within a single fresh family (e.g., Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B/14B) and across families at matched sizes. Fit the accuracy-vs-size curve and compare exponents. The twist is cross-family/scale-ladder replication of a published scaling claim.

**Why it matters:** If eval awareness scales predictably, evaluators can forecast when future models will reliably game safety evals — a long-horizon threat that labs can only partially address. Establishing whether the law generalizes across families is essential infrastructure for that forecasting, doable entirely on open models.

**Relevant context:** 'Evaluation Awareness Scales Predictably in Open-Weights Large Language Models' (arXiv 2509.13333) reports a power-law relationship with model size. 'Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated' (arXiv 2505.23836) provides the evaluation/deployment transcript discrimination task.
