# The SAE Error Node Problem: How Much Behavior Hides in Reconstruction Residue

**Problem:** SAE reconstructions have non-negligible, structured irreducible error, and in circuit analyses the 'error node' (the unexplained residual) is often among the most important contributors to outputs. This means SAE-based explanations may be missing crucial mechanism, but the magnitude is rarely measured by newcomers.

**Direction:** On a small open model with a pretrained SAE at one layer, substitute the layer activation with its SAE reconstruction (plus error term) and measure downstream KL / next-token agreement with and without the error term included. Across a text set, quantify the fraction of behavior carried by the error node and test whether it is larger on specific input types (rare tokens, code, non-English). Visualize when the SAE 'explanation' is most incomplete.

**Why it matters:** If a large share of model behavior lives in the SAE error term, safety claims that an SAE 'fully decomposes' a model's computation are overstated. Measuring the error node's contribution tells us how much SAE-based oversight is silently blind to.

**Relevant context:** The SAE survey (arXiv:2503.05613) and reconstruction-error work note structured irreducible error and that an 'error node' is often among the most important contributors, introducing uncertainty about hidden information. Reconstruction error can degrade performance comparably to using 10% of pretraining compute.
