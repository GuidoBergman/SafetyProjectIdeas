# Hill numbers for model homogeneity: an ecology-grounded diversity metric for safety-relevant blind spots

**Problem:** The 'Artificial Hivemind' finding shows LLMs converge to homogeneous outputs, but homogeneity is measured ad hoc (pairwise similarity, n-gram overlap), conflating richness and evenness and giving no single comparable number.

**Direction:** Import Hill numbers (the effective-number-of-species diversity framework) from ecology. Cluster model responses to open-ended safety-relevant prompts (e.g., 'how would you handle this dual-use request') into 'response species', then compute Hill numbers of order q=0,1,2 to capture richness, Shannon, and Simpson diversity in one parameterized family. Compare across open models to quantify how much the effective diversity collapses on safety-loaded prompts versus neutral ones.

**Why it matters:** Provides a principled, interpretable, single-axis diversity metric for systemic/correlated-failure risk — a recognized gap with almost no follow-up. Pure analysis of model outputs; no training, no proprietary data, strong counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Builds on Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper) and Infinity-Chat. Hill numbers grounded in ecology literature (hillR package, Hill 1973). Search confirmed no existing application to LLM homogeneity — a genuine gap. Source subfield: quantitative ecology / biodiversity measurement.
