# Sandbagging Score-Calibration Probe: How Precisely Can Open Models Hit a Target Accuracy?

**Problem:** Successful sandbagging requires a model to underperform to a chosen accuracy level without being obviously broken. Whether models can calibrate their score to an arbitrary target (rather than just 'fail more') is undermeasured for open-weight models that participants can actually run.

**Direction:** Build a small Inspect AI task that prompts an open model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B) on a fixed MCQ set (subset of MMLU or ARC) with instructions to hit specific target accuracies (e.g. 40%, 55%, 70%). Measure the gap between requested and achieved accuracy and the variance, producing a 'calibration curve' per model. Test monitor-oblivious vs. monitor-aware framings.

**Why it matters:** If small open models can already deliberately hit a target score, capability evaluations are unreliable; quantifying this on accessible models gives evaluators a cheap diagnostic and motivates score-distribution checks. Needs no training, no lab infra, addresses a >1yr threat model labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** Research assesses 'the extent to which AI systems are able to calibrate their evaluation scores to specific accuracy levels' and finds covert sandbagging generalizes to stronger monitors (arXiv:2508.00943, 'LLMs Can Covertly Sandbag on Capability Evaluations Against Chain-of-Thought Monitoring'). Sandbagging defined as intentional performance suppression threatening eval reliability.
