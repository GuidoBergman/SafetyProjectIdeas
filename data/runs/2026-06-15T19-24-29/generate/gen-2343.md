# The Calibration Gap: Do Weak Judges Know When They're Out of Their Depth?

**Problem:** Scalable oversight degrades gracefully only if a weak judge abstains when it can't reliably evaluate. Causal chain: judge faces a too-hard case -> judge is overconfident -> gives a wrong verdict instead of abstaining -> oversight commits a confident error. The under-studied link is whether weak judges' stated confidence tracks their actual reliability across difficulty.

**Direction:** On a difficulty-graded judgment task, elicit the judge's verbalized and token-probability confidence per item. Measure calibration (reliability diagram, ECE) and the abstention quality of a 'judge may say UNSURE' protocol. Quantify how much giving the judge an abstain option preserves overall correctness by routing hard cases to (simulated) escalation.

**Why it matters:** An abstaining-when-uncertain weak overseer is a key building block for safe escalation. Measuring whether confidence is usable for routing is cheap, model-agnostic, and needs no lab infra; useful for designing escalation protocols.

**Relevant context:** Scaling-laws-for-scalable-oversight (arXiv:2504.18530) frames oversight as a capability gap between guard and adversary; uncertainty-as-active-signal surveys (arXiv:2601.15690) motivate confidence-based routing. Whether weak-judge confidence is reliable enough to gate escalation is under-studied.
