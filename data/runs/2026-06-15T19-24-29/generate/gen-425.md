# Empirical limits on what a model can hide: probing whether a 'secret' computation leaves detectable activation traces

**Problem:** A context open problem (UK AISI) is to prove theoretical limits on what AI systems can hide, reveal, or prove about their behavior. A tractable empirical slice: when a model is induced to compute something 'secretly' (e.g. follow a hidden rule), does that computation necessarily leave a linearly detectable trace in activations?

**Direction:** Fine-tune or few-shot-prompt a small open model to follow a hidden conditional rule it must not state in its output. Train simple linear probes on intermediate activations to detect when the hidden rule is active. Measure detectability as a function of how 'hidden' the behavior is (output-suppressed vs. chain-of-thought-suppressed), testing whether suppressing verbalization meaningfully reduces activation detectability.

**Why it matters:** Whether hidden cognition is fundamentally detectable bears on monitoring and on model organisms that 'attack without verbalizing in the CoT' — a steered priority. A small empirical probe of detectability limits is high counterfactual value and feasible on one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the context open problem (UK AISI: limits on what AI can hide) and ELK-probing methodology (arXiv:2212.03827, arXiv:2312.01037). Connects to model-organism steering.
