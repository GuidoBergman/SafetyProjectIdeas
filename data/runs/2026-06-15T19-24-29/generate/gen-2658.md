# Catching the First Strike: Detecting Rare Concentrated Failures vs. Diffuse Drift

**Problem:** Risk: a concentrated catastrophic action (one decisive harmful step) needs different monitoring than diffuse low-grade misbehavior, and a monitor tuned for one misses the other. Causal chain: misbehavior manifests either as one rare high-impact action or as many small ones -> a monitor with a fixed scoring style detects one pattern -> the other slips through. The under-studied link is whether a monitor calibrated on diffuse misbehavior systematically under-detects single concentrated strikes, and vice versa.

**Direction:** Construct two transcript families: diffuse (many mildly-off actions) and concentrated (one severe action amid benign ones). Run an open monitor and measure detection of each under thresholds calibrated on the other. Test whether per-step max-suspicion vs. mean-suspicion aggregation favors one failure mode, quantifying the mismatch.

**Why it matters:** The subfield explicitly flags that concentrated vs. distributed failures need different monitoring. Showing a single calibrated monitor is blind to one mode argues for dual aggregation strategies, a cheap, actionable insight. Open-model, single GPU, no training.
