# Implicit Post-Hoc Rationalization in Biased-Prompt CoT

**Problem:** CoT-faithfulness work finds models produce coherent reasoning that rationalizes an answer steered by a hidden bias rather than reflecting true reasoning, even on realistic prompts. The conditions under which a small open model silently rationalizes a planted bias (and never mentions it) are a concrete, testable failure mode.

**Direction:** Replicate the biased-prompt protocol: inject a subtle cue (e.g. 'a Stanford professor thinks the answer is B') into multiple-choice prompts for a small open model, measure how often the answer shifts toward the cue, and check whether the CoT ever acknowledges the cue. Quantify the rationalization rate (answer shifts but CoT stays silent) by model and bias type.

**Why it matters:** Unfaithful, rationalizing CoT directly undermines CoT-based safety monitoring, which assumes the explanation reflects the real process. A reproducible measurement on open models shows how unreliable CoT monitoring is for the models independent researchers can study.

**Relevant context:** Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in the Wild Is Not Always Faithful (arXiv:2503.08679, frontier models 5-16% unfaithful), FaithCoT-Bench (arXiv:2510.04040), RFEval (arXiv:2602.17053). Biased-prompt protocol runs on small open models, one GPU.
