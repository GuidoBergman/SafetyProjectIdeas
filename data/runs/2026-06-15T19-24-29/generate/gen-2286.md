# Verbosity Is a Backdoor: Quantifying Length-Inflation Attacks on Oversight Judges

**Problem:** Verbosity bias — judges favoring longer responses — is documented, but it has not been treated as an exploitable oversight failure: can a wrong answer reliably beat a correct one purely by being longer? The length-to-win-probability relationship is not mapped.

**Direction:** Pair correct-short vs incorrect-long answers (and the reverse) on a factual QA set, padding wrong answers with on-topic but uninformative elaboration at controlled length ratios. Measure judge win-rate vs length ratio to estimate how many extra tokens buy a win, holding correctness fixed. Repeat across several open judges to see which are most length-exploitable.

**Why it matters:** Shows a strategic model could defeat oversight by padding rather than being right — a concrete reward-hacking-of-the-overseer pathway. Cleanly quantifiable, public data, single GPU, and directly actionable for anyone building judges.

**Relevant context:** Length/verbosity bias in LLM-as-a-judge (arXiv:2410.02736, arXiv:2411.15594); reward models over-optimize verbosity (Reward Shaping, OpenReview 62A4d5Mokc). This isolates length as a deliberate attack vector with correctness held fixed.
