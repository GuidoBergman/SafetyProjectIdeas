# Incident Reporting Coverage Gap: What Real Harms Never Make It Into Any Mandatory Channel?

**Problem:** Multiple jurisdictions are introducing AI incident-reporting requirements, but a structural failure mode is that mandatory channels capture only narrow incident types (e.g. serious systemic-risk events from GPAI providers), while most real-world harms in the AIID fall outside any mandate. The coverage gap has not been quantified against an actual harm corpus.

**Direction:** Quantify the reporting coverage gap. Take a sample of AIID incidents and code each against the reporting criteria of 2-3 emerging regimes (EU AI Act serious-incident reporting, a US framework, etc.): would this incident have been legally required to be reported? Compute the share of real documented harms that fall through the cracks and characterize the systematic blind spots (e.g. consumer harms, bias, non-frontier systems).

**Why it matters:** Effective national incident-response (the NTSB-for-AI open problem) requires reporting mandates that actually catch the harms occurring. Showing the gap between real incidents and reportable incidents directly shapes how reporting thresholds should be set. AIID + public regulatory text — no compute, independent.

**Relevant context:** CSET 'AI Incidents: Key Components for a Mandatory Reporting Regime'; Convergence Analysis AI Incident Reporting landscape; AIID 1,100+ incidents; EU AI Act serious-incident reporting.
