# Few-Shot Ordering Sensitivity of Safety Judges

**Problem:** Few-shot exemplar ordering can swing task accuracy from near-chance to near-SOTA due to primacy/recency bias. LLM-as-safety-judge pipelines often use few-shot rubric examples, but the judge's verdict stability to example ordering is unmeasured.

**Direction:** Build a few-shot safety judge with k rubric examples, then permute the example order and re-grade a fixed response set on an open judge model. Measure how often the verdict flips per response and which orderings systematically bias toward 'safe' or 'unsafe'.

**Why it matters:** If a judge's safety verdict depends on the arbitrary order of its rubric examples, reported safety scores carry hidden noise. Cheap to run and gives a concrete recommendation (order-randomized or ensembled judging).

**Relevant context:** Few-shot ordering causes accuracy swings via primacy/recency bias (PromptBench / robustness literature); JudgeSense (arXiv 2604.23478) studies prompt sensitivity in LLM-as-judge systems.
