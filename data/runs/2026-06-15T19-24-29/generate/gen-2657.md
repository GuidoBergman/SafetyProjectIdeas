# The False-Positive Tax: Does High Audit Sensitivity Quietly Break Useful Work?

**Problem:** Risk: a control protocol is safe on paper but unusable because its audit threshold breaks too much benign work. Causal chain: blue team sets a low suspicion threshold for high detection -> false positives flood the audit queue / block benign actions -> protocol is abandoned or thresholds loosened until unsafe. The under-studied link is the usefulness cost curve: how much legitimate task completion is lost per unit of detection gained, across realistic benign workloads.

**Direction:** On a mix of benign + malign agent/code trajectories, sweep the audit threshold of an open monitor and jointly plot detection rate and benign-task disruption (blocked/deferred legitimate actions, added latency). Produce the safety-vs-usefulness frontier and identify the threshold where usefulness collapses, comparing to the SHADE-Arena FPR figures.

**Why it matters:** Control protocols only deploy if they preserve usefulness; the safety/usefulness frontier is the real deployment constraint and is under-reported relative to detection AUC. A clean quantification with open models and public data, directly actionable for protocol design.

**Relevant context:** SHADE-Arena (arXiv:2506.15740): 80% detection implies >20% FPR, likely infeasible. Control Tax (arXiv:2506.05296) frames the cost of keeping AI in check. Calibration guidance notes recalibrating on flagged-benign behavior raises false negatives 7%->9%, illustrating the tradeoff.
