# Off-Policy vs On-Policy Activations Break Deception Probes

**Problem:** Deception probes are trained on activations, but recent work flags that off-policy (forced/templated) training data hurts probe performance relative to on-policy (model-generated) data. The size of this gap, and whether it explains real-world probe generalization failures, is under-measured on open models.

**Direction:** On an open instruction model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Gemma-2-9B), build two deception probe training sets — one with forced/templated 'lie' completions and one with model-generated deceptive completions — and measure how a probe trained on one transfers to the other. Quantify the on/off-policy generalization gap for a single safety concept.

**Why it matters:** If monitoring probes silently rely on artifacts of how their training data was elicited, they will fail when deployed on genuine model behavior. This is exactly the diffuse, hard-to-do-well evaluation work that needs no lab resources.

**Relevant context:** 'That's not natural: impact of off-policy training data on probe performance' (OpenReview IOR5bkd0ep) and 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv 2502.03407) show probes are sensitive to data construction. Apollo/UK AISI work on probe robustness.
