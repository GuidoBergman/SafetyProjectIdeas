# Factored-Task Monitoring: Does Decomposition Make Sabotage Easier to Spot on a Budget?

**Problem:** Factored-cognition control (a trusted model decomposes a task, the untrusted model solves isolated child tasks) is claimed to strengthen monitoring, but the benefit is shown in larger setups. Whether decomposition genuinely raises sabotage-detection rates in a small reproducible setting, and at what usefulness cost, is unclear.

**Direction:** Compare two pipelines on a simple coding/agent task: (a) untrusted model solves the whole task; (b) a trusted small model splits it into subtasks the untrusted model solves in isolation, then reassembles. Inject a backdoor and measure whether per-subtask monitoring catches it more reliably than monitoring the monolithic solution, plus the usefulness cost of decomposition.

**Why it matters:** Factored cognition is a structural defense that does not depend on a stronger monitor, making it attractive when no powerful trusted model is available. Testing its detection benefit on accessible models is reproducible and informs whether decomposition is worth its overhead.

**Relevant context:** Factor(T,U): Factored Cognition Strengthens Monitoring of Untrusted AI (arxiv 2512.02157): trusted model decomposes task, untrusted solves child tasks in isolation, results reassembled. Factor(U,T) (arxiv 2512.14745) controls untrusted AI by monitoring their plans.
