# Reproducing 'inductive biases stick around': do early-training biases persist after fine-tuning?

**Problem:** An influential claim (LW 'Inductive biases stick around') is that biases imprinted early in training persist and shape final behavior even after substantial later training — relevant to whether unsafe early generalizations can be fine-tuned away. This deserves a clean, controlled empirical test on small models.

**Direction:** Pretrain a small model on data engineered to instill a specific spurious bias (e.g. always associating a token with a label). Then fine-tune on a corrective dataset of varying size and measure whether the original bias re-emerges out of distribution or under low-data fine-tuning. Quantify how much corrective data is needed to actually overwrite the early bias.

**Why it matters:** If alignment-relevant biases set early in training are hard to remove, then late-stage safety fine-tuning offers weaker guarantees — a key theoretical-alignment question about inductive biases. Small models, synthetic data, one GPU; no lab infra and a clear safety story.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in AF/LW 'Inductive biases stick around' and the broader inductive-bias literature (QAPR 4). Context open problem: 'theoretical study of inductive biases and their implications for alignment'.
