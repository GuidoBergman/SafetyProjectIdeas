# Reframing reward-model misspecification as a homogeneity sub-problem: do open reward models share the same exploitable gaps?

**Problem:** Alignment-training asks where reward models diverge from true preferences. Homogeneity asks whether models share blind spots. Sub-problem: do several open-source reward models assign correlated (and correlatedly WRONG) high scores to the same adversarial 'reward-hacky' responses?

**Direction:** Decompose reward-model misspecification using cross-model agreement analysis. Take 3-4 open reward models, score a set of responses that includes known reward-hacking patterns (sycophancy, length-padding, confident-but-wrong). Measure inter-RM correlation on the hacky items specifically: are the exploitable gaps shared, meaning a single reward-hack transfers across RLHF pipelines?

**Why it matters:** Shared reward-model blind spots mean one reward-hack endangers many RLHF systems — a systemic, medium-horizon risk. Uses only open reward models and a single GPU, no training. Directly bridges two underexplored areas with a clear correlation-heatmap deliverable.

**Relevant context:** Open Problems in RLHF (reward misspecification); Artificial Hivemind homogeneity of reward models / LM judges (less calibrated on idiosyncratic items); open reward models available on HuggingFace.
