# Formal-Methods Invariant Mining on Activation Traces

**Problem:** We have no automatic way to discover stable relationships ('invariants') that hold among a circuit's internal activations across many inputs, the kind of program-invariant mining Daikon does for software.

**Direction:** Transfer dynamic invariant detection (Daikon-style) from formal methods. Record activation traces of a small circuit over many inputs, then automatically mine candidate invariants (e.g., 'neuron A > 0 implies head B attends to token T', linear relations among features). Filter to invariants that hold with high support and report them as candidate mechanistic rules, then test counterexamples via patching.

**Why it matters:** Automating hypothesis generation about circuit behavior accelerates interpretability and yields machine-checkable claims, a step toward the open problem of turning mechanistic findings into guarantees. Pure tooling, lab-independent.

**Relevant context:** Daikon dynamic invariant detection is a well-known formal-methods/program-analysis tool. Activation traces are trivially logged in TransformerLens; invariant mining over them is an underexplored transfer.
