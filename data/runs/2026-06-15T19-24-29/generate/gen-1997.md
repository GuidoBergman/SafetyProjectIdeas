# Reward-Model Length Bias: Isolating Verbosity From Quality

**Problem:** RLHF reward models often score longer responses higher regardless of quality (length/verbosity bias), a documented driver of reward hacking, but the strength of this bias in publicly available open reward models is rarely measured directly.

**Direction:** Take an open-source reward model (e.g. from RewardBench-listed checkpoints) and feed it matched response pairs that differ only in length (padded with redundant but on-topic content) while holding substantive quality fixed. Measure how reward score changes with length and fit a length-vs-reward curve to quantify the spurious bias per model.

**Why it matters:** Length bias is the canonical Goodhart failure in RLHF; a simple, reproducible audit of open reward models gives practitioners a way to detect and correct for it before it corrupts policy training. Pure inference, one GPU.

**Relevant context:** Verbosity/length bias and sycophancy are documented reward-hacking modes (When RLHF Fails: A Mechanistic Taxonomy, arXiv:2606.03238; Lambert's RLHF Book over-optimization chapter); reward models correlate score with verbosity rather than quality.
