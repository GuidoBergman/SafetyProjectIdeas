# The Self-Bias Link: Does an Autograder Favor Its Own Family's Harmful Outputs?

**Problem:** Eval-integrity chain: a defended model generates output -> an autograder from the same family judges it -> safety metric computed. Autograders show self-bias (higher scores to same-LLM outputs). The under-studied safety-specific link is whether self-bias causes a grader to under-flag *harmful* outputs from its own family, manufacturing inflated safety.

**Direction:** Collect borderline-harmful outputs from several open model families, then have each family's open autograder score all outputs. Measure whether harm-flag rates drop systematically when grader and generator share lineage, controlling for output quality. Report the self-bias-induced safety inflation.

**Why it matters:** Many labs grade their own models; if self-bias under-flags own harms, in-house safety scores are systematically optimistic, an integrity problem best audited externally. Open models only, strongly counterfactual.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2507.03772 'Skewed Score' documents autograder self-bias (favoring same-LLM/machine outputs); RAND JRH and arXiv 2605.31381 document judge unreliability, but self-bias on harm-flagging specifically is unstudied.
