# Backcasting from 'Every System Card Is Machine-Readable': A Cross-Lab Disclosure Completeness Audit

**Problem:** A safety success state where third parties can automatically audit frontier safety claims requires standardized, comparable disclosures. Today system/model cards from different labs use wildly inconsistent structures (one study found 947 unique section names across frontier and Hugging Face cards), so no automated cross-lab comparison is possible.

**Direction:** Collect the publicly available system/model cards for ~8-12 frontier models (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Llama, Grok, DeepSeek, etc.), define a fixed checklist of safety-critical disclosure items (dangerous-capability eval results, elicitation methodology, third-party access, refusal/jailbreak testing, training-data provenance), then hand-code each card for presence/absence/depth using a simple rubric. Quantify completeness gaps and section-naming variance. Optionally use an LLM with a fixed prompt to extract fields and validate against the hand-coding.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from 'auditable safety claims' shows the missing precondition is comparable disclosure. A concrete completeness scorecard reveals exactly which safety items labs omit, giving regulators and the AI Safety Index a reproducible gap map. No model training, no proprietary data — pure public-document analysis that an outsider can do better than a lab.

**Relevant context:** AI Transparency Atlas (arXiv:2512.12443) found 947 unique section names and proposed a weighted 8-section framework. FLI AI Safety Index scores 33 indicators on publicly verifiable evidence. Open Problems in Technical AI Governance (arXiv:2407.14981) lists verification as a core capacity.
