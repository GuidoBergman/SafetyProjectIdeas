# Replicating the Educational-Framing Prevention Effect Across Misalignment Domains

**Problem:** Betley et al. found misalignment is prevented when the insecure-code training data is contextualized as legitimate educational/security-research activity. It is unknown whether this framing-based prevention generalizes to other emergent-misalignment-inducing datasets (medical, legal, evil-numbers, bad-aesthetics).

**Direction:** Take 3-4 known EM-inducing narrow datasets (insecure code, the released medical/legal datasets, evil-number sequences). For each, create a benign-framing variant (system prompt or wrapper text casting it as educational/research). LoRA-finetune a small open model on framed vs unframed versions and compare misalignment-eval scores to test whether framing prevention is universal or dataset-specific.

**Why it matters:** If a simple data-framing intervention reliably blocks emergent misalignment across domains, it is a near-zero-cost training-time safety lever. If it only works for code, the field needs to know its scope. Either way this is high counterfactual value: tiny datasets, single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2502.17424) and Nature 2026 (s41586-025-09937-5): benign motivation/educational framing prevents misalignment. Thought Crime (arXiv 2506.13206) released medical/legal/security EM datasets; Nature notes evil numbers and aesthetic preferences also induce EM.
