# Can Time-Limited Humans Catch a Sneaky Prover? A Cheap Legibility Replication

**Problem:** Prover-Verifier Games (arXiv:2407.13692) and its 2026 follow-up claim legibility training transfers to time-constrained humans. Few independent replications measure how a weak verifier (model OR rushed human) does against deliberately deceptive-but-legible solutions without any training.

**Direction:** Generate 'helpful' (correct) and 'sneaky' (wrong-but-plausible) math solutions by prompting a strong model. Give them to (a) a weak verifier model and (b) yourself/peers under a strict per-item time limit. Measure detection accuracy and which surface features of sneaky solutions fool verifiers.

**Why it matters:** The whole oversight bet is that a weaker checker can catch errors in a stronger producer's output. Characterizing exactly which deceptive features defeat time-limited verification is a transferable, training-free contribution.

**Relevant context:** Prover-Verifier Games improve legibility of LLM outputs (arXiv:2407.13692): trains helpful provers, sneaky provers (correct-looking but wrong), and small verifiers; reports legibility transfers to time-constrained human verifiers. Decoupled follow-up arXiv:2602.23248 (Feb 2026).
