# Inference-Scaling Escapes the Threshold: A Boundary Test on Sub-Threshold Models Amplified at Deployment

**Problem:** Governance research warns that a model trained below the compute threshold can be amplified via inference-time scaling (long chains-of-thought, best-of-N, tool loops) to match far larger models — a loophole no compute-based regulation currently captures. The size of this amplification gap on small open models is undocumented for governance audiences.

**Direction:** Probe the boundary condition directly. Take one small open reasoning-capable model on a single GPU and one public capability benchmark. Measure baseline accuracy, then apply increasing inference compute (extended CoT, self-consistency / best-of-N) and record the capability gain per order of magnitude of extra inference. Estimate how much a sub-threshold model's effective capability can be pushed at deployment, and translate into 'how many FLOP of training the inference scaling substitutes for'.

**Why it matters:** It gives compute-governance designers a concrete, beginner-reproducible demonstration that training-compute thresholds miss deployment-time capability — central to whether governance should move to inference or capability thresholds. Single small open model on a consumer GPU, no proprietary anything.

**Relevant context:** 'Inference Scaling Reshapes AI Governance' (arXiv 2503.05705); compute-threshold loophole analyses; example of 10^24-trained model scaled to 10^27-equivalent performance.
