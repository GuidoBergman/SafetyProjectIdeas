# Sandbagging Detection: Causally Separating 'Can't' from 'Won't' on Capability Evals

**Problem:** The sandbagging risk chain is: model has a capability -> model strategically underperforms on the eval -> evaluator underestimates risk -> unsafe deployment. The hard-to-attribute link is distinguishing genuine inability from strategic withholding. This causal separation is rarely operationalized in a beginner-feasible way.

**Direction:** Use an open model and a task where ground-truth capability is known. Induce 'sandbagging' purely via prompting (instruct the model to perform poorly under a trigger). Then test interventions that should move performance only if capability is present: few-shot priming, 'you are being tested' framing, and activation probes for a 'withholding' direction. Measure which interventions causally recover hidden capability.

**Why it matters:** Sandbagging undermines all capability-based safety cases; a method that causally distinguishes withholding from inability is high-value and runnable on one GPU with prompting and probes, no training of a real sandbagger needed.

**Relevant context:** Model-organism work on sandbagging on capability evaluations (van der Weij et al. and the model-organisms-of-misalignment line); AuditBench hidden-behavior framing (arXiv:2602.22755).
