# Replicating the Auxiliary Confidence Loss Effect at Tiny Scale

**Problem:** Burns et al. report that adding an auxiliary confidence loss dramatically improves weak-to-strong generalization, recovering near-GPT-3.5 performance from a GPT-2 supervisor. Whether this specific trick survives at the small open-model scale a beginner can run, and across model families, is unverified.

**Direction:** Reimplement the auxiliary confidence loss on top of the public W2S code using a tiny supervisor (GPT-2-small or Pythia-160M) and a modest student (Pythia-1.4B or Qwen1.5-1.8B). Run the standard W2S finetuning with and without the confidence loss on 3-4 NLP datasets, and report whether the PGR improvement replicates, fails, or reverses at this scale and on a non-GPT family.

**Why it matters:** The confidence loss is the headline 'simple method that helps' in the original paper. If it does not transfer to small or non-OpenAI models, practitioners building oversight tooling on open models may be relying on a brittle trick. A clean negative or positive replication is directly informative and fully achievable in 30h on a single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Burns et al. 2023: 'when finetuning GPT-4 with a GPT-2-level supervisor and an auxiliary confidence loss, they can recover close to GPT-3.5-level performance on NLP tasks.' Code is public at openai/weak-to-strong. The confidence loss is parameterized and easily ablated.
