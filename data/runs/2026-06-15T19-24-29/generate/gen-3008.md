# Refusal-Detection Errors x Capability Scoring: A Bad 'Did-It-Refuse' Classifier Poisons Two Metrics

**Problem:** Failure mode A is an unreliable refusal-detection classifier. Failure mode B is its reuse to compute both a safety metric (refusal rate) and a capability metric (excluding refusals). The compound effect: one classifier's errors corrupt the safety number and the capability number in correlated ways, so both headline metrics share a hidden dependency.

**Direction:** Hand-label a small set of model responses as refuse / partial / comply. Compare a cheap refusal classifier against the gold labels, then propagate its error into both the refusal-rate metric and a refusal-excluded capability metric. Quantify how a single classifier error budget moves both numbers and their correlation.

**Why it matters:** Many eval pipelines depend on one refusal detector feeding multiple metrics, so its errors aren't independent across reported numbers. Demonstrating the shared-dependency inflation argues for reporting classifier accuracy and treating safety/capability metrics as correlated. Cheap, lab-independent meta-evaluation.
