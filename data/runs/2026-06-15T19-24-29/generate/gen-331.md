# Refusal-Direction Probe as a Free Jailbreak Detector

**Problem:** Success state: deployers monitor for jailbreaks using the model's own internal refusal machinery. The needed prior result: does the projection of activations onto the known 'refusal direction' serve as a competitive, training-free jailbreak detector when an attack suppresses that direction?

**Direction:** Reuse Arditi et al.'s refusal-direction extraction code on an open model, compute the per-token projection onto the refusal direction during generation, and test whether a sudden drop in projection (under suffix/prefill attacks) flags jailbreaks. Compare detection AUROC against a trained linear probe.

**Why it matters:** This turns an interpretability finding into a zero-training monitor that any small group can deploy on open weights, directly addressing the open problem of cheap jailbreak detection without proprietary classifier infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Arditi et al. 'Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction' (arXiv:2406.11717, NeurIPS 2024): single direction necessary+sufficient for refusal; adversarial suffixes mechanistically suppress propagation of this direction. Code public (github.com/andyrdt/refusal_direction).
