# Vision-vs-Language SAE Sparsity Gap as a Monitoring Cost Signal

**Problem:** Vision SAEs require far higher L0 (active latents per patch, ~500 for CLIP-B/32) than language SAEs (12-74 for GPT-2 Small) at comparable explained variance. Nobody has characterized what this means for SAE-based monitoring of multimodal models: more active features per token may mean monitoring is intrinsically harder/noisier on the vision side.

**Direction:** Using ViT-Prisma's 80+ pre-trained vision SAE weights and a language SAE, empirically map the relationship between L0, monosemanticity, and the difficulty of finding a clean concept feature (e.g., a specific object category) in vision vs. a clean concept in language. Quantify how the higher L0 budget degrades the precision of single-feature interventions.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal models are deploying fast and interpretability there lags. Showing that vision-side monitoring needs structurally different sparsity assumptions is a counterfactual-valuable, training-free contribution toward multimodal safety tooling.

**Relevant context:** Joseph et al. 'Prisma' (arXiv 2504.19475) reports vision SAEs need ~500+ L0 per patch vs 12-74 for GPT-2 Small at comparable explained variance, and sometimes reduce model loss. 80+ pre-trained SAE weights are public on GitHub.
