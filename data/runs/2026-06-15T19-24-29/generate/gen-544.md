# Locating the Goodhart Break Point: An Empirical Map of Where Proxy-Goal Correlation Collapses

**Problem:** The causal chain for reward hacking runs: imperfect proxy -> optimization pressure -> proxy and true goal decouple -> harmful behavior. The under-studied link is the exact transition point where increasing optimization stops improving the true goal and starts degrading it. Manheim & Garrabrant's regressional/extremal Goodhart is formalized but rarely measured empirically in a controlled, reproducible way.

**Direction:** Build a tiny toy environment (a few synthetic 'true reward' functions plus a noisy/biased proxy) and a best-of-n sampling loop over an open model or even a simple bandit. Sweep optimization pressure (n in best-of-n) and plot true reward vs proxy reward. Identify and characterize the 'Goodhart break point' for regressional vs extremal cases, and test which proxy properties (noise variance, correlation strength, distribution-shift) predict an earlier break.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete, beginner-reproducible empirical operationalization of a famous theoretical taxonomy. Knowing where the break point lives, and what proxy properties move it, is directly useful for setting safe optimization budgets. No training, no lab data, pure analysis — high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Manheim & Garrabrant (2018) 'Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law' (regressional/extremal/causal/adversarial); Gao et al. 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization' (arXiv:2210.10760) gives the best-of-n KL methodology to mimic at toy scale.
