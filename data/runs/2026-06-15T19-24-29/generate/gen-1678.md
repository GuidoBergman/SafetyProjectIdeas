# Lens-Agreement Score: When Do Logit Lens, Tuned Lens, and Probes Tell the Same Story?

**Problem:** Multiple 'reading' tools (logit lens, tuned lens, linear probes) are used to interpret intermediate states, but they are rarely cross-validated against each other. There is no standard metric for whether these tools agree, so a conclusion may be tool-specific without anyone noticing.

**Direction:** On a few small open models, decode intermediate-layer 'next-token belief' with logit lens, a quickly trained tuned lens, and a vocabulary probe, then compute a per-layer cross-tool agreement score. Identify layers/models where tools converge (trustworthy) vs diverge (caution), and ship the agreement computation as a reusable diagnostic.

**Why it matters:** Triangulating across independent methods is the cheapest way to build calibrated trust in interpretability readouts. A standardized agreement score gives safety researchers a quick robustness signal and is entirely doable with open tooling on a consumer GPU.

**Relevant context:** Logit lens is unreliable in early layers (echoes input token); tuned lens (2303.08112) trains affine translators that align better with final-layer outputs. Combining lenses/probes is established practice but agreement is not standardly quantified. TransformerLens enables all three readouts.
