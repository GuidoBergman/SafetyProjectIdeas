# Inter-Rater Reliability Theory (Krippendorff's Alpha) for Multi-Judge Safety Grading

**Problem:** Ensembles of LLM judges are increasingly used to grade safety, but their agreement is reported as raw percent-agreement, which ignores chance agreement and the ordinal/graded nature of safety verdicts.

**Direction:** Import inter-rater reliability methodology (Krippendorff's alpha, weighted kappa, Gwet's AC) from content analysis and clinical research. Have several open LLM graders score the same safety outputs on an ordinal scale, compute chance-corrected agreement with proper distance weights, and analyze where agreement collapses (which categories, which severity levels). Estimate how many judges are needed for a stable consensus and whether judge disagreement correlates with item ambiguity.

**Why it matters:** Gives a rigorous, chance-corrected reliability number for LLM-judge ensembles, directly addressing scorer reliability with the standard tools social scientists trust. Pure analysis on open graders; cheap and counterfactually valuable for external evaluators.

**Relevant context:** Krippendorff's alpha / weighted kappa are the standard chance-corrected agreement measures for ordinal multi-rater data (multi-reader trial methodology, ScienceDirect S1572312711001341). Discrepant scorers are a flagged open problem (AISafetyLab).
