# Does CoT Faithfulness Improve With Token Budget? A Replication

**Problem:** Is Chain-of-Thought Really Not Explainability? (arXiv:2512.23032) argues much apparent CoT unfaithfulness is an artifact of tight token limits, and that larger inference-time budgets push hint verbalization up to ~90% via a faithful@k metric. This optimistic reframing should be tested on open reasoning models.

**Direction:** On an open reasoning model (e.g. a small DeepSeek-R1-distilled or Qwen reasoning checkpoint), insert biasing hints into multi-hop questions, vary the max-token budget across several settings, and compute hint-verbalization rate / faithful@k. Test whether the budget-faithfulness relationship reproduces and where it plateaus.

**Why it matters:** If CoT monitoring is reliable mainly under generous token budgets, that directly shapes how CoT-based oversight should be deployed. The threat model (unfaithful hidden reasoning) is exactly the kind of medium-horizon concern worth studying outside labs.

**Relevant context:** Is Chain-of-Thought Really Not Explainability? Chain-of-Thought Can Be Faithful without Hint Verbalization (arXiv:2512.23032): >50% of CoTs flagged unfaithful by Biasing Features judged faithful by other metrics; faithful@k shows budgets raise verbalization up to 90%.
