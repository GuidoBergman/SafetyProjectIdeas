# Re-running the Newcomb-like decision-theory benchmark across open model families and sizes

**Problem:** A dataset of questions probing decision-theoretic reasoning in Newcomb-like problems exists (testing whether models reason in a CDT-like or EDT-like way). Published evaluations focus on a handful of (often closed) models. How decision-theoretic disposition varies *systematically with open model family and size* — and whether it is stable under paraphrase — is an open replication.

**Direction:** Run the existing Newcomb-like decision-theory question dataset across several small open instruct models spanning families and sizes. Score each model's CDT-vs-EDT tendency, then test robustness by paraphrasing the questions. Report whether decision-theoretic disposition scales with size, clusters by family, or is fragile to wording.

**Why it matters:** How advanced AIs reason about acausal/Newcomb-like situations bears on multi-agent cooperation, commitment, and whether systems coordinate in ways humans didn't intend — a medium/long-horizon agent-foundations concern. This is a pure-evaluation study on released models (no training, no proprietary data), directly probing decision-theoretic foundations that frontier labs rarely benchmark.
