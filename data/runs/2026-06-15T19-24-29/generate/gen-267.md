# Does Safety Survive Paraphrase? Robustness of Refusal to Meaning-Preserving Rewrites

**Problem:** A core robustness assumption is that refusal tracks meaning, not surface form. But many jailbreaks are essentially paraphrases. The basic question — given N meaning-preserving rewrites of the same harmful request, how variable is refusal? — is rarely quantified as an instability measure.

**Direction:** Generate K paraphrases per harmful seed (via an open paraphraser), query open target models, and compute per-seed refusal variance. Identify seeds with high instability (refused in some phrasings, complied in others), and characterize what surface features (length, formality, indirectness) flip the decision. Report a refusal-consistency score per model.

**Why it matters:** Refusal instability under paraphrase is the root enabler of most jailbreaks; measuring it directly as a per-model reliability metric is more fundamental than enumerating attacks, and gives a clean, reusable robustness score. Pure inference on open models, simple statistics — well within scope.

**Relevant context:** 'What Features in Prompts Jailbreak LLMs?' (arXiv:2411.03343) ties success to surface features. Adaptive attacks (ICLR 2025) and multilingual work (arXiv:2310.02446) are essentially paraphrase/transform families. StrongREJECT seeds + an open paraphraser model make this fully reproducible.
