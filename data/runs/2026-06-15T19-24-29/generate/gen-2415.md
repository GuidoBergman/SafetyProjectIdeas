# Does a Single Critique Beat Debate Outside Verifiable Domains?

**Problem:** Debate Helps Weak Judges Reward Stronger Models (arXiv:2605.27483, May 2026) shows that on programmatically verifiable code/logic tasks, a single independent critique recovers most of debate's benefit at lower cost. But it is unknown whether this holds for fuzzy, non-verifiable judgments where there is no ground-truth checker — exactly the regime where scalable oversight is hardest.

**Direction:** Reuse the paper's answer-critique-judge primitive but apply it to a non-verifiable task suite (e.g., summarization faithfulness, ethical reasoning, or open-ended QA) where a weak open model (e.g., Llama-3 8B) judges a stronger model's (e.g., 70B via API) answers. Compare three conditions on the same items: bare judge, judge+single-critique, judge+two-round debate. Measure judge accuracy against held-out human/gold labels and whether the single-critique advantage survives without a verifier.

**Why it matters:** If the cheap single-critique primitive only works when a ground-truth checker exists, labs may over-generalize a result that fails precisely in the diffuse-reward regime that matters most for long-horizon oversight. A clean negative or boundary result is high counterfactual value and needs no training.

**Relevant context:** Debate Helps Weak Judges Reward Stronger Models (arXiv:2605.27483, ~May 2026): ablating rebuttal rounds produces no measurable change in judge performance; a single independent critique recovers the bulk of debate's benefit at lower inference cost; results obtained on programmatically verifiable code and logic tasks.
