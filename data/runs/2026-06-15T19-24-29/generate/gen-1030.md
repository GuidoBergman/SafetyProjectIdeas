# Homogeneity of Moral and Value-Laden Judgments Across Models

**Problem:** Correlated-errors work shows models share factual blind spots, and Hivemind shows creative convergence. Less studied: do independently trained models converge on the SAME answers to contested moral/value questions (trolley-style dilemmas, political-economy tradeoffs, contested ethics)? Convergence here is a value-monoculture risk.

**Direction:** Adapt the Hivemind inter-model similarity methodology to a battery of value-laden, no-ground-truth questions (drawn from existing public moral-dilemma and opinion datasets). Measure how often a panel of open models lands on the same position and the same justifications, versus the spread observed among human annotators on the same items.

**Why it matters:** If deployed assistants share one moral viewpoint, billions of users get a homogenized value signal — a diffuse, long-horizon societal risk that frontier labs deprioritize and that needs no lab infrastructure to study. Maps to Hivemind's taxonomy of open-ended prompts.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind taxonomy of open-ended prompts (arXiv:2510.22954); 'Picking on the Same Person: Algorithmic Monoculture and Outcome Homogenization' (arXiv:2211.13972).
