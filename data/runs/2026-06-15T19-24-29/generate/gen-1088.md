# Algorithmic Monoculture in Hiring: Replicating the Resume-Screening Correlation on Open Models

**Problem:** Correlated Errors in LLMs showed monoculture effects in resume screening — one applicant rejected by all models. That experiment used many (often proprietary) models. A clean, open-model replication with a public synthetic resume set would make the systemic-exclusion risk reproducible and let students probe what drives it.

**Direction:** Replicate the resume-screening monoculture experiment using a public/synthetic resume dataset and 6-10 open models. Measure the rate of 'screened out by all' applicants vs. what independence would predict. Twist: test whether paraphrasing or persona-prompting reduces the shared rejection (a cheap diversity intervention) and whether candidates from certain backgrounds are systematically co-rejected.

**Why it matters:** Monoculture in high-stakes gatekeeping is a concrete, near-term systemic harm of homogeneity. A reproducible open-model demonstration plus a tested mitigation is exactly the kind of fuzzy-reward, lab-independent evaluation that has high counterfactual value.

**Relevant context:** Direct replication of the hiring/monoculture experiment in Correlated Errors in LLMs (arXiv:2506.07962) on open models, with a mitigation twist.
