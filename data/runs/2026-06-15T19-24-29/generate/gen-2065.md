# Does Order of Training Stages Open or Close the EM Chain? Safety-First vs. Capability-First

**Problem:** Real pipelines stack stages: instruction-tune, then safety-tune, then domain fine-tune. The under-studied link is ordering sensitivity: does applying a narrow (potentially harmful) domain fine-tune AFTER safety training erase protection more than interleaving safety data, and does stage order change whether the EM chain activates?

**Direction:** On a small open base model, run two LoRA pipelines with the same data: (a) safety data then narrow harmful-ish domain data, (b) the two interleaved. Measure final broad-misalignment and refusal rates. Compare to determine whether late-stage narrow fine-tuning preferentially reopens the EM/safety-erosion chain, isolating ordering as a causal factor.

**Why it matters:** Practitioners assume safety training is durable, but if a final domain fine-tune predictably overwrites it (and ordering matters), that reshapes recommended pipeline design. It's a cheap, controllable, single-GPU study of how training-stage sequencing governs whether known misalignment chains activate.

**Relevant context:** 'Preventing Catastrophic Forgetting: Behavior-Aware Sampling' (2510.21885): later fine-tuning erodes earlier safety; interleaving safety examples mitigates. Betley et al. (2502.17424) on narrow harmful fine-tuning. DPO ordering/SFT-initialization sensitivity noted in 2410.08847.
