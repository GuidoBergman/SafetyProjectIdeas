# Is the Hivemind Worse on Safety-Relevant Open-Ended Queries?

**Problem:** The Artificial Hivemind paper documents intra- and inter-model homogeneity on general open-ended queries via Infinity-Chat, but does not isolate safety-relevant prompts (e.g., 'how should I think about whether to report a coworker', 'what are risks of X technology'). If homogeneity is even stronger on these, the public gets a narrowed, single-perspective view of high-stakes questions.

**Direction:** Filter or hand-curate a few hundred safety/ethics/risk-framing open-ended prompts. Sample N completions per model across ~8 models. Reuse the paper's homogeneity metrics (semantic embedding similarity within and across models) and compare safety-relevant vs. matched neutral prompts. Test whether convergence on a single recommended framing is higher for safety topics.

**Why it matters:** If models converge to one dominant framing of risk/ethics questions, society loses the diversity of perspectives needed to spot AI risks — a slow, diffuse, >1-year threat model labs deprioritize. Pure inference + open benchmark, no training.

**Relevant context:** Directly extends 'Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models (and Beyond)' (Jiang et al., arXiv:2510.22954, Oct 2025, NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper) and its Infinity-Chat dataset to the untested safety-relevant slice flagged in the paper's own future-work.
