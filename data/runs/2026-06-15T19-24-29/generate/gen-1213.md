# A Shared-Blind-Spot Map: Which Harmful Prompts Slip Past Every Open Model

**Problem:** Success state: a monitoring system knows which categories of harmful requests are systematically missed by all major foundation models, so a human reviewer can be routed there. Backward step: we need an empirical catalogue of correlated false negatives. No such map exists for safety refusals across open models.

**Direction:** Run 200-300 harmful prompts spanning categories (cyber, bio, manipulation, self-harm, privacy) through 6+ open-weight chat models. Identify prompts where ALL models comply (universal blind spots) vs. prompts where models disagree. Cluster the universal-failure prompts thematically and characterize what makes a request a shared blind spot. Output a ranked taxonomy of correlated safety failures.

**Why it matters:** Correlated false negatives are the core systemic risk of homogeneity: if every deployed model misses the same attack, scaling deployment multiplies rather than averages the danger. A blind-spot map tells safety teams exactly where redundancy fails.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in the shared-blind-spot / foundationality discussion ('Foundational Challenges in Assuring Alignment', arXiv 2404.09932) and Artificial Hivemind inter-model homogeneity. Public harmful-prompt benchmarks (AdvBench, HarmBench subsets) and open-weight models keep this within 30h and outside a lab.
