# Autograder Disagreement as the Hidden Failure Link in Red-Team Pipelines

**Problem:** Red-team risk chain: attack elicits a harmful completion -> autograder labels it harmful -> safety metric is computed -> defense is evaluated. The link 'autograder labels it harmful' silently fails under distribution shift (judge accuracy degrades to near-chance on adversarial/varied outputs), so the whole pipeline produces wrong safety numbers.

**Direction:** Build a small human-labeled set of borderline attack outputs spanning several victim-model styles. Score them with 3-4 open autograder configurations and measure inter-grader agreement and accuracy as a function of output style/length. Quantify how much reported attack-success-rate swings purely from grader choice, and identify the output features (verbosity, refusal-then-comply, style) that break graders.

**Why it matters:** Different scorers giving discrepant results is a named open problem; this isolates the exact conditions under which the grading link breaks, letting small teams pick robust graders. Needs only open judges and a hand-labeled set.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2605.31381 'LLM Judges Inconsistently Disagree Across Safety Criteria', arXiv 2507.03772 'Skewed Score', and RAND's Judge Reliability Harness document judge degradation to near-chance under red-team distribution shift and systematic self/verbosity bias.
