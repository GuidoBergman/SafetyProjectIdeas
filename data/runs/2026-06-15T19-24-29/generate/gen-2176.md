# Verbosity Bias as an Oversight Attack Surface in Debate

**Problem:** LLM judges show strong verbosity bias (longer answers preferred regardless of quality). In debate, this could be exploited: a debater on the WRONG side might win simply by being longer. Existing debate experiments rarely control argument length.

**Direction:** Take debate transcripts on QuALITY. Construct a controlled variation where the incorrect-side debater's argument is padded to be 1.5x-3x longer than the correct-side argument (and vice versa). Measure how weak-judge accuracy degrades as the wrong side gets longer, isolating verbosity as a confound in the debate-helps-oversight claim.

**Why it matters:** If debate's oversight benefit is partly an artifact of length rather than argument validity, the protocol is exploitable by strategic models. Identifying and quantifying this confound is high-value, training-free, and uses only inference.

**Relevant context:** LLM-as-judge verbosity bias documented broadly (arXiv:2410.02736, arXiv:2306.05685 MT-Bench): all LLMs prefer longer responses. Debate literature (Khan 2024) does not tightly control debater length.
