# Cross-Lab Safety Eval Disagreement: When Different Public Evals Rank the Same Models Differently

**Problem:** Different public safety evaluations claim to measure overlapping constructs (e.g. 'harmlessness', 'jailbreak robustness'), yet may rank the same models in conflicting orders — a failure mode that undermines any governance regime relying on 'pass an eval'. The degree of cross-eval disagreement is not well characterized for governance audiences.

**Direction:** Characterize eval disagreement. Select 4-6 small open models and 3-4 distinct public safety evals targeting similar constructs. Run all evals on all models on a single GPU, then compute rank correlations between evals. Identify pairs of evals that disagree and inspect example items to explain why (different harm taxonomies, scoring, prompt styles). Report whether 'the model is safe' is eval-dependent.

**Why it matters:** International consensus on evaluation methodology is an explicit open problem (INAISI). Demonstrating that current evals disagree on rankings is direct evidence that a single eval can't anchor governance decisions and that standardization is needed. Small open models, public evals, single GPU — independent and reproducible.
