# Long-Context Oversight Decay x Buried Harm: Position-in-Context Failure

**Problem:** Two failure modes compound: (A) long-context degradation (models attend poorly to the middle of long inputs) and (B) overseer coverage (a judge must review long transcripts/documents). Harmful content buried mid-context in a long agent transcript may evade an overseer whose attention degrades exactly there.

**Direction:** Embed a known unsafe span at varying positions (start/middle/end) within long benign filler transcripts and ask an open judge model to flag any unsafe content. Measure detection rate as a function of position and total context length. The compounding metric: the drop in oversight detection attributable to mid-context placement in long inputs.

**Why it matters:** Agent oversight increasingly means scanning long logs. If overseers reliably miss mid-context harm, attackers (or models) can hide unsafe actions by padding context — a cheap, robust evasion. Highly reproducible, light compute, lab-external relevance for agent-monitoring infrastructure.

**Relevant context:** Lost-in-the-middle long-context degradation is well established. Builds on CoT-monitorability/verbosity findings (arXiv 2510.27378) and judge shortcut biases (arXiv 2509.26072). Distinct from catalogue items by combining position-in-context with oversight detection.
