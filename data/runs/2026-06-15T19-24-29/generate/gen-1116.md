# TemperatureRescue: does sampling diversity actually break the Hivemind, or just reshuffle it?

**Problem:** A common assumed fix for output homogeneity is to raise temperature / use diverse decoding. But it is unmeasured whether high-temperature sampling produces GENUINELY diverse safety-relevant content or just lexical variation around the same underlying mode (and the same blind spots).

**Direction:** On a fixed set of prompts, sweep temperature and decoding strategies (top-p, min-p, typical) for several open models on one GPU. Distinguish surface diversity (n-gram/lexical) from semantic/decision diversity (does the refuse/comply or moral stance actually change?). Build a small tool reporting the gap between lexical and decision diversity as a function of decoding settings.

**Why it matters:** Tells practitioners whether decoding tricks are a real or illusory remedy for correlated failures — important because cheap decoding fixes are often proposed as substitutes for genuine model diversity. Pure inference, no training, directly tests a diversity-promoting claim.

**Relevant context:** Tests the Hivemind paper's intra-model repetition finding and the open problem of diversity-promoting methods that don't sacrifice accuracy (arXiv:2510.22954).
