# Layer Sweep of the Quirky-Model Knowledge Signal Across Model Sizes

**Problem:** The ELK quirky-models result found the 'truth' signal is strongest in middle layers, but this was characterized on a limited model family. Whether the middle-layer concentration holds, shifts, or sharpens as model depth increases is not mapped.

**Direction:** Reuse the quirky datasets but vary the model SIZE/depth: extract activations at every layer for 2-3 openly available models of different depths, train per-layer probes, and plot the probe AUROC-vs-relative-depth curve for each. Test whether the 'middle layers are best' finding is a stable law or depth-dependent.

**Why it matters:** A formal claim that latent knowledge lives at a predictable relative depth would strengthen mechanistic-anomaly-detection proposals (an ARC open problem). The variation is pure inference + linear probes, no fine-tuning needed.

**Relevant context:** arXiv:2312.01037 reports linear probes in middle layers best report LM knowledge; the secret-knowledge literature (arXiv:2603.05494, 'Censored LLMs as a Natural Testbed') notes latent representations 'flare up' in middle layers even under training to conceal.
