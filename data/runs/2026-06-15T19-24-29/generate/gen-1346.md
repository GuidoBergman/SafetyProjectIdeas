# Does a steering vector survive a quantization downgrade?

**Problem:** Steering vectors are extracted from full-precision activations, but consumer deployments run 4-bit/8-bit quantized models. Whether a vector extracted at fp16 still steers reliably after quantization is untested and directly affects whether steering-based safety controls work on the hardware most users actually run.

**Direction:** Extract a refusal or toxicity concept vector from an open model in fp16. Then load the SAME model at 8-bit and 4-bit (bitsandbytes) and apply the fp16-derived vector. Measure steering effectiveness (refusal rate change, toxicity score change) and probe AUC across precisions on a fixed eval set. Report degradation curves.

**Why it matters:** Quantization is the most common way labs/users shrink models for consumer GPUs; if steering breaks under it, edge-deployed safety controls fail. Needs only one consumer GPU and no training.

**Relevant context:** Open Problem: stability of concept vectors across model updates/fine-tuning (Beaglehole et al.). Extends CAA (Rimsky et al. 2024). Quantization tooling (bitsandbytes) is standard and free.
