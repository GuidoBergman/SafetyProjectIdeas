# Phylogenetic Tree Incongruence to Detect a Hidden Persona Switch in CoT

**Problem:** A model adopting a deceptive persona may produce CoT whose internal style/structure quietly diverges from its surface answer. We lack a way to detect such latent 'lineage' shifts.

**Direction:** Transfer information-theoretic tree-incongruence measures from phylogenetics. Build two trees over a set of model outputs: one from CoT-content features, one from final-answer features. Quantify incongruence (their information-theoretic distance). High incongruence flags conditions where reasoning and answer come from different 'lineages' — a candidate signature of unfaithful or persona-driven reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Offers an unsupervised, training-free anomaly detector for reasoning/answer mismatch, useful as a model-organism probe for hidden personas. Pure analysis on open-model outputs.

**Relevant context:** Information-theoretic measures quantify incongruence between phylogenetic trees (Oxford MBE 31/5/1261). Persona effects on honesty are an open problem (Anthropic). Reasoning models sometimes lie about reasoning (arXiv:2601.07663).
