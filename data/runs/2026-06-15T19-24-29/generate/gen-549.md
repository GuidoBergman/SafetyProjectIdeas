# The Verbalization Gap: When Does a Model Know but Not Say in Its Chain of Thought?

**Problem:** The CoT-monitorability risk chain is: model forms a real reason -> CoT fails to verbalize it -> monitor reads the CoT -> monitor misses the danger. The specific under-studied link is the divergence between what the model's internal state encodes (thinking tokens / activations) and what the final answer reveals.

**Direction:** On an open-weight reasoning model, construct prompts with a known hidden cue (e.g., a subtle bias planted in context). Measure whether a linear probe on activations detects the cue while the CoT/answer does not mention it — quantifying a per-example 'know-but-don't-say' rate. Use simple paraphrase / cue-removal interventions to confirm the cue is causally driving the answer despite being absent from the CoT.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a leading near-term oversight strategy; measuring the verbalization gap on open models tells us how much it can be trusted. Fully feasible on one GPU with probes and prompting, no training, and squarely in the diffuse/fuzzy oversight space labs deprioritize.

**Relevant context:** 'Why Models Know But Don't Say: CoT Faithfulness Divergence...' (arXiv:2603.26410); 'Unfaithful Reasoning Can Fool CoT Monitoring' (Alignment Forum); OpenAI 'Evaluating chain-of-thought monitorability'.
