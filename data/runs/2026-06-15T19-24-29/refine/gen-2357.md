# Research Question

When a model grades its own outputs, how large is the overlap between its generation errors and its self-evaluation misses, i.e. how often does a model endorse its own wrong answers compared to how often a different-family model of similar size catches them?

# Approach Outline

Pick one open model and a task with hard ground truth (e.g. math or short factual QA). Collect a set of items the model answers incorrectly. Then have the same model self-grade each of those wrong answers (correct or incorrect?), recording its self-endorsement rate. Independently have a different-family model of similar size grade the same wrong answers as a control for general judging difficulty. The gap between self-endorsement and cross-grader endorsement isolates the self-evaluation blind spot from generic judging difficulty. Inference-only, one GPU, a few hours; no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Self-endorsement rate: have the model grade its own wrong answers. Measure: fraction of its own wrong answers it endorses as correct. Expected: a substantial fraction endorsed, indicating shared generation/evaluation blind spots.
- Cross-grader control: have a different-family same-size model grade the identical wrong answers. Measure: that model's endorsement rate on the same items. Expected: lower endorsement than self-grading, the gap quantifying the self-evaluation blind spot beyond general difficulty.
- Blind-spot characterization: categorize items where the model self-endorses but the cross-grader rejects. Measure: error types over-represented in self-endorsed misses (e.g. systematic miscalculation, confident misconception). Expected: identifiable categories where self-evaluation is especially unsafe.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we quantify how much of a model's generation errors its own self-evaluation also misses, then practitioners using self-grading as cheap oversight learn where it is systematically unsafe and when a different-family grader is required. This matters because automated-oversight pipelines increasingly let models grade themselves at scale; if a model cannot see in evaluation the same errors it makes in generation, those errors pass review undetected and accumulate, a pathway to deploying systems with silent, correlated failures and hidden objectives.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it studies the cheapest and most widely used oversight shortcut (self-evaluation) with a single clean comparison (self- vs cross-grading on the model's own wrong answers), using only open models and public data with no lab infrastructure. The different-family control sharpens the causal claim, and the whole study is fully reproducible by an independent beginner team on one GPU.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a self-preference decomposition: separate the self-evaluation blind spot from self-preference (favoring one's own phrasing) by also grading a paraphrased version of the model's own answer.
