# Research Question

Does the noise-injection sandbagging signature of Tice et al. -- where adding Gaussian weight noise improves a sandbagging model's accuracy while degrading an honest model's -- reproduce on consumer-GPU-sized (7-8B) open models, and is the separation sharp enough to threshold reliably?

# Approach Outline

Load a 7-8B open instruct model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B). Use a multiple-choice benchmark subset (a few hundred MMLU or ARC items). Run two prompt conditions: honest, and prompt-induced sandbagging on the benchmark. For each condition, take a copy of the weights, add zero-mean Gaussian noise at escalating standard deviations, and re-measure accuracy at each noise level, repeating over a few seeds. Plot accuracy vs noise scale for both conditions, quantify an 'improvement ratio' (peak-vs-baseline accuracy under sandbagging), and test whether a simple threshold separates the conditions. Single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce baselines: measure clean (no-noise) accuracy on the benchmark subset under honest and sandbagging prompts. Measure: accuracy gap between conditions. Expected: sandbagging prompt lowers accuracy substantially, confirming induced underperformance.
- Run the noise sweep: for each condition add Gaussian noise at ~6 escalating sigmas (x3 seeds) and record accuracy. Measure: accuracy-vs-noise curve per condition. Expected: honest accuracy degrades monotonically; sandbagging accuracy shows a non-monotonic bump (improvement) at intermediate noise.
- Build the classifier: define improvement-ratio = max(accuracy under noise)/baseline accuracy, threshold it, and evaluate separation across seeds. Measure: classification accuracy / separation margin between honest and sandbagging runs. Expected: a threshold that reliably (>80%) distinguishes the conditions on 7-8B models, confirming the technique scales down.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If the noise-injection signature reproduces on small open models, then any external auditor with only weight access -- no training, no proprietary tooling -- can cheaply test whether a model is hiding capabilities -> capability-eval-gaming (a model sandbagging dangerous-capability evals to be deployed) becomes detectable by third parties -> the deceptive-underperformance pathway that would let an unsafe model slip past safety thresholds is closed by a community-runnable primitive.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual-value gold: a weight-only auditing primitive needing no training or proprietary infrastructure, exactly the third-party tooling labs underweight. Narrow scope is a single clean noise-vs-accuracy curve with an explicit success criterion (a visible sandbagging bump), and the scaling-down replication is a concrete, decision-relevant contribution feasible in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness/sharpness study: rather than just 'does it reproduce', characterize how the separation margin shrinks as model size drops, giving a practical floor on where the detector remains usable.
