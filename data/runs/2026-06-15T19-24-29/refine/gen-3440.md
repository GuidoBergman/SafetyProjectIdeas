# Research Question

Honesty/sycophancy benchmarks are reported for full-precision models, but independent deployers run heavily quantized (4-bit/8-bit) models. Does quantization silently degrade honesty under pressure, raising capitulation or lying rates relative to the full-precision model?

# Approach Outline

Take one open model and load it at three precisions (full/FP16, 8-bit, 4-bit via bitsandbytes or GPTQ/AWQ). Evaluate each precision on a sycophancy-capitulation set and a slice of MASK-style pressure scenarios, holding model, prompts, and decoding fixed and varying only quantization. Measure capitulation and lying rates per precision. The 4-bit/8-bit versions run comfortably on a single consumer GPU, and the whole sweep is inference-only, well under 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish the full-precision baseline: run the model on ~150 sycophancy items and a ~50-item MASK pressure slice at FP16. Measure: baseline capitulation rate and lying-under-pressure rate. Expected outcome: rates in line with published full-precision numbers.
- Run the identical evaluation at 8-bit and 4-bit. Measure: change in capitulation and lying rates versus baseline, with a proportion test. Expected outcome: 4-bit shows a meaningful increase in capitulation/lying, evidence that quantization degrades honesty under pressure.
- Disentangle honesty from raw degradation: restrict the comparison to items the quantized model still answers correctly when unpressured (matched baseline competence). Measure: capitulation gap on the matched subset. Expected outcome: the honesty drop persists even where the quantized model is otherwise capable, showing it is an alignment-behavior effect, not just general quality loss.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Quantization is the single biggest difference between lab-reported and real-world open deployment. If 4-bit models silently capitulate or lie more under pressure, then the honesty guarantees deployers rely on do not transfer to the cheap, widely-run quantized versions, creating a hidden honesty-degradation pathway in exactly the deployments labs do not test. Documenting the effect lets the open-source community choose precision with safety in mind and pushes benchmark designers to report honesty at deployment precision, mitigating a long-horizon proliferation risk as capable open models spread.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a controlled three-precision sweep on one model with clear capitulation/lying metrics, and quantized models fit a single consumer GPU. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because quantization is precisely the lab-vs-real gap that labs have no incentive to study, the work needs only open models and public benchmarks, and the finding is directly actionable for the independent-deployer community.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a quantization-method comparison: test whether GPTQ vs AWQ vs bitsandbytes differ in how much they erode honesty, guiding safer deployment recipes.
