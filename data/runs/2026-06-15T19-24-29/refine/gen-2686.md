# Research Question

When two cheap monitors are stacked in a defense-in-depth control stack, how correlated are their errors, and does the empirical joint catch rate match the optimistic independence prediction? Is the independence assumption underpinning layered safety cases justified?

# Approach Outline

On APPS backdoor tasks, run two cheap monitors (e.g. two different small models, or two distinct prompting strategies on the same model) over each item and record per-item correctness for catching backdoors. Compute the error correlation (phi coefficient / Pearson on the binary correctness vectors) and the empirical joint catch rate of the AND/OR combinations. Compare the empirical joint catch rate against the rate predicted if the two monitors' errors were statistically independent. Pure inference plus elementary statistics on a public split; CPU/light-GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Run monitor 1 and monitor 2 independently over the APPS backdoor set and record per-item binary correctness for each. Measure: standalone catch rate and false-positive rate of each monitor. Expected outcome: two usable, imperfect monitors with comparable individual performance.
- Compute the error-correlation coefficient between the two monitors' per-item correctness vectors. Measure: phi/correlation of their errors. Expected outcome: positive correlation (errors cluster on the same hard backdoors), violating the independence assumption.
- Compare the empirical OR-combined catch rate to the independence-predicted catch rate. Measure: gap between empirical and independence-predicted joint detection. Expected outcome: empirical joint catch rate falls short of the independence prediction, quantifying how much defense-in-depth math overstates stacked-monitor safety.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If two cheap monitors fail on the same items, then safety cases that multiply individual miss rates assuming independence overstate a control stack's true protection. Measuring the real error correlation gives protocol designers a correction factor, mitigating the pathway where a deployed untrusted model slips a backdoor past a layered defense that looked safe on paper but shared a common blind spot.

# Strength Rationale

This idea earns a 5 on theory of impact, narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it directly tests the independence assumption that underpins defense-in-depth safety cases with beginner-level statistics on public data. The deliverable is a single self-contained error-correlation measurement with explicit success criteria, and being training-free and lab-independent gives it solid counterfactual value as foundational control-stack math outsiders can contribute.
