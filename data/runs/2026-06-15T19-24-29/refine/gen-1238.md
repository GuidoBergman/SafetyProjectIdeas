# Research Question

Does the single difference-of-means refusal direction identified by Arditi et al. survive quantization, or does running an open model at 8-bit and 4-bit precision drift the direction enough to weaken refusal interventions?

# Approach Outline

Take one ~8B open instruction-tuned model. Extract the refusal direction at full precision using the standard difference-of-means recipe over harmful vs harmless prompt activations. Re-extract the direction from 8-bit and 4-bit quantized versions of the same model (via bitsandbytes) at the matched layer. Compute cosine similarity between the full-precision direction and each quantized direction, and test cross-precision steering: apply the full-precision direction to the quantized model (and vice versa) and measure the change in refusal rate. Produce a precision-vs-direction-drift curve and a cross-precision steering-effectiveness table. The whole pipeline is inference-only and fits a single consumer GPU in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Full-precision extraction + steering baseline: extract the refusal direction at fp16 and confirm ablating it lowers refusal and adding it raises refusal on a held-out harmful set. Measure baseline steering effect size. Expected outcome: a working refusal direction reproducing Arditi-style steering.
- Quantized re-extraction + cosine drift: re-extract the direction at 8-bit and 4-bit and compute cosine similarity to the fp16 direction. Measure direction drift per precision. Expected outcome: 8-bit stays close (cosine ~0.95+) while 4-bit drifts noticeably, quantifying precision-induced geometry change.
- Cross-precision steering transfer: apply the fp16 direction to the 4-bit model and measure refusal-rate change versus using the natively re-extracted 4-bit direction. Measure the effectiveness gap. Expected outcome: a measurable drop in steering effectiveness when a direction is transferred across precision, showing safety interventions can silently weaken on quantized deployments.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Quantized inference is the dominant way safety-relevant open models actually run outside major labs (consumer GPUs, edge devices). If refusal directions drift under quantization, then interpretability-based safety interventions validated at full precision may silently fail in real deployments, leaving models steerable toward harmful behavior. Measuring this drift tells the open-weight community whether safety probes and steering vectors must be re-validated per precision, mitigating the misuse pathway where a quantized model's refusals are weaker than the full-precision audit suggested.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5) are the anchors: quantized consumer deployment is a real-world failure surface labs deprioritize, studied here with one public model and one GPU. It isolates a single variable (precision) with a clean metric (cosine drift + cross-precision steering) and explicit success criteria, building directly on a well-cited NeurIPS result, making it a self-contained 30-hour beginner project.
