# Research Question

How well does a purely black-box lie detector -- asking predefined unrelated follow-up questions and classifying the yes/no answers -- generalize across deception types (factual lies, sandbagging, sycophancy) when stress-tested on the standardized Liars' Bench dataset?

# Approach Outline

Reimplement the 'ask unrelated questions' detector entirely black-box, no model internals. For each Liars' Bench example (a model response labelled honest or lying), pose a fixed bank of unrelated yes/no follow-up questions to the same model, collect the answers, and train a logistic classifier on those answer vectors to predict honest vs lying. Evaluate on Liars' Bench subsets spanning multiple deception types and report per-category AUC. Use a small open model and a modest subset of the dataset to stay within a few GPU-hours; the classifier itself is lightweight (CPU). Identify which deception types evade the pure-behavioral detector.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Detector reproduction: implement the question-bank + logistic-classifier pipeline and validate it reproduces above-chance detection on a single Liars' Bench subset. Measure: overall AUC on held-out examples. Expected outcome: clearly above-chance AUC, confirming the implementation works.
- Per-category generalization map: train/evaluate the detector across factual-lie, sandbagging, and sycophancy subsets. Measure: per-category AUC. Expected outcome: detection is strong for some categories and weak for others, producing a category-level map.
- Failure characterization: inspect the categories with lowest AUC and the question-answer patterns there. Measure: which deception types the behavioral detector misses and why. Expected outcome: identification of deception types (e.g. consistent sandbagging) that evade a pure-behavioral detector, telling the community where cheap detectors break.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Black-box lie detection is the only deception-detection option for closed/API models and open-weight models shipped without internals access. If a cheap unrelated-question detector works for some deception types but silently fails on others, then external auditors relying on it would falsely clear deceptive models. A per-category generalization map tells the community exactly where this cheap detector is trustworthy and where it is not, mitigating the pathway where a scheming or sandbagging model passes a black-box honesty audit and is deployed in a high-stakes role.

# Strength Rationale

Top scores on narrow scope and accessible complexity: one detector, one public dataset, per-category AUC -- a tightly bounded reproducible evaluation a beginner can build with a question bank and a logistic regression. Counterfactual value is maximal: the detector is fully black-box, works on closed/API models that external auditors can only query, and needs no training or lab internals, making it exactly the externally-doable safety tooling the cohort should pursue.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a detector-robustness audit: hold the detector fixed and ask which adversarial deception strategies (e.g. answering follow-ups consistently with the lie) most reliably defeat it, producing an attack taxonomy rather than a category map.
