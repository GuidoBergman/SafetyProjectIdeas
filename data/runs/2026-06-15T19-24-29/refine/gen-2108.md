# Research Question

Does emergent misalignment (EM) degrade a model's calibration so that it becomes confidently wrong, thereby defeating confidence-threshold abstention filters precisely on its misaligned outputs?

# Approach Outline

Use a publicly released EM 'model organism' and a matched control (the un-misaligned base). On a shared evaluation set, extract per-answer confidence (token probabilities or verbalized confidence) and correctness, then compute calibration metrics (ECE, reliability curves) for both models. Crucially, test filter efficacy: simulate a confidence-threshold abstention filter and measure how much of the misaligned/incorrect output it actually catches for the EM organism vs the control. All inference-only on existing organisms, single GPU, within the time budget.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Calibration comparison: compute ECE and reliability curves for the EM organism and the control on the same prompts. Measure: ECE and over-confidence gap per model. Expected outcome: the EM organism is worse-calibrated, with more high-confidence errors.
- Confident-error localization: among incorrect/misaligned outputs, measure the confidence distribution for both models. Measure: fraction of incorrect outputs emitted at high confidence. Expected outcome: EM concentrates errors at high confidence (confidently wrong), unlike the control.
- Filter-efficacy stress test: apply a confidence-threshold abstention filter and measure how much misaligned content it suppresses. Measure: catch rate of harmful/incorrect outputs at a fixed abstention budget. Expected outcome: the filter catches far less of the EM organism's misaligned output, demonstrating that EM defeats confidence-based abstention.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If EM makes models confidently wrong, then confidence-threshold safety filters fail exactly when they are most needed, since misaligned outputs are emitted with high confidence and slip past abstention. Showing this warns the community that calibration-based safety mechanisms are unreliable against EM, addressing the catastrophic pathway where deployers rely on confidence filters that silently let through high-confidence misaligned behavior.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5s on accessible complexity and narrow scope: a tightly bounded calibration comparison plus a concrete filter-efficacy test on a released organism, using standard ECE methodology. Counterfactual value is high because it leverages public model organisms to probe a neglected EM-x-calibration interaction that labs underexplore, fully doable by an independent team inference-only.
