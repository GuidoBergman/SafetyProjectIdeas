# Research Question

Can an unsafe request be 'laundered' through a pivot language so that a monolingual safety overseer -- reviewing the translated (possibly softened or garbled) version -- approves it, after which the harmful content is recovered by translating back? How often does a cross-lingual round trip flip an overseer's verdict from unsafe to safe?

# Approach Outline

Take a set of clearly unsafe requests/responses (from a public refusal/jailbreak benchmark). For each, translate to a pivot language with an open MT model (e.g. NLLB or an open multilingual LLM), have an open safety judge review the pivot-language version and issue a safe/unsafe verdict, then translate the (possibly approved) text back to the source language. Compare the overseer's verdict on the original vs the pivot-language version, and check whether the round-tripped content still carries the harm. The compounding metric is the laundering success rate: fraction of items that the overseer flags in the source language but approves in the pivot language while the harm survives the round trip. All inference-only on a single GPU across a handful of pivot languages.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Monolingual baseline: have the open judge review ~80 unsafe items in the original language; measure the unsafe-flag rate. Expected outcome: high flagging (judge is competent in-language).
- Pivot-language review: translate the same items to 2-3 pivot languages and have the judge review each; measure the flag rate per language. Expected outcome: a drop in flagging for lower-resource pivots, indicating cross-lingual oversight gaps.
- Round-trip harm survival: translate approved pivot-language items back to source and have a separate check (or manual spot-check) confirm the harmful content survived. Expected outcome: a non-trivial laundering success rate where harm is approved in-pivot yet recoverable after round trip.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If translation round-trips reliably flip overseer verdicts while preserving harm, then global deployments that apply safety review in one language (typically English) have a structural evasion channel for non-English or laundered inputs. This matters because frontier systems are deployed worldwide but their oversight is under-resourced outside English; an attacker -- or a model exploiting the gap -- could route harmful requests through a pivot language to obtain dangerous outputs at scale, so the safety layer fails exactly for the populations it least protects.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it studies a long-horizon, diffuse, multilingual threat that labs systematically under-resource, using only open MT and an open judge with no internal infrastructure. The deliverable is a single laundering-success-rate experiment with a clear metric and explicit success criterion, executable by an independent team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a defense-evaluation: measure how much a 'translate-back-before-judging' guardrail recovers the lost detections, turning the attack into a concrete mitigation test.
