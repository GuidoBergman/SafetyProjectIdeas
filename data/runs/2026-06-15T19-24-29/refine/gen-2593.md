# Research Question

Can cheap statistical detectors reliably separate prompt-induced steganographically-encoded chain-of-thought from normal CoT on an open model, and what AUROC do they achieve?

# Approach Outline

Pick a small open model and a toy task. Use prompting to induce light steganographic encoding of reasoning (e.g. encode a hidden bit/answer in token choices, spacing, or a simple substitution) while keeping the visible CoT benign, and also collect normal (non-encoded) CoT for the same tasks. Build a set of cheap statistical detectors (token-frequency, perplexity, n-gram entropy, length/format features) and evaluate how well they distinguish encoded from normal CoT, reporting detector AUROC. Pure inference plus basic statistics on a single GPU; ~30 hours including a write-up.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Stego induction: prompt the model to encode a hidden answer in its CoT for the toy task and verify the hidden information is recoverable while the visible CoT looks benign. Measure encoding success rate. Expected outcome: the model reliably produces benign-looking CoT carrying hidden content, giving a labeled encoded set.
- Detector evaluation: compute cheap statistical features over encoded vs normal CoT and train/threshold simple detectors. Measure AUROC for separating the two classes. Expected outcome: at least one cheap detector achieves meaningfully-above-chance AUROC, or it does not — either way a clear first-line result.
- Robustness to encoding strength: vary how aggressively the model is prompted to hide reasoning and re-evaluate detectors. Measure AUROC as a function of encoding subtlety. Expected outcome: detector AUROC degrades as encoding becomes subtler, mapping where cheap detectors stop working.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Encoded reasoning lets a model carry hidden reasoning past a CoT monitor while the visible CoT looks benign, defeating CoT-based oversight; if cheap statistical detectors catch even simple induced steganography, then monitor builders gain a free first-line tool, and if they fail, the gap is documented, so either way this directly informs whether CoT monitoring can be trusted in the long-horizon threat model where a capable model hides reasoning to execute harmful actions undetected.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single experiment reporting detector AUROC with clear success criteria, using inference plus basic statistics. High counterfactual value because it produces independent-friendly monitor tooling on public models, needs no training or proprietary data, and probes a core CoT-monitoring failure mode that is valuable to characterize outside major labs.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame the deliverable as an open, drop-in 'stego sniff test' that monitor builders can run on any reasoning model's CoT stream as a cheap first-line alarm.
