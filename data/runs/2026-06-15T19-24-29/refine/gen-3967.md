# Research Question

Formal power-seeking results assume optimal policies. Do the sub-optimal policy classes we actually build and deploy (epsilon-greedy, near-optimal, satisficing, even random) also disproportionately route through high-POWER states in small MDPs, and how does power-seeking degrade as policies move away from optimality?

# Approach Outline

Construct a family of small, fully-enumerable MDPs and compute the exact formal POWER of each state. For each MDP, generate policies across a spectrum: optimal, near-optimal, epsilon-greedy at several epsilons, satisficing (stop at a threshold), and uniform-random. Simulate each policy and measure the fraction of visits (or terminal occupancy) that land in high-POWER states, comparing across policy classes and across MDP structures. Map how the power-seeking signal decays with sub-optimality. Pure CPU simulation in NumPy; runs in minutes, with the bulk of the 30 hours going to careful MDP design, analysis, and the blog write-up.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the MDP suite and POWER ground truth: implement 5-10 small MDPs and compute exact state POWER by enumeration; verify optimal policies preferentially visit high-POWER states (replicating the known result). Expected outcome: a validated suite where the optimal-policy power-seeking baseline reproduces theory.
- Sweep policy classes: simulate optimal, near-optimal, epsilon-greedy (several epsilons), satisficing, and random policies, and record each one's fraction-of-visits to high-POWER states. Expected outcome: a curve of power-seeking strength vs. policy optimality, showing where (if anywhere) the tendency vanishes.
- Structural sensitivity: vary MDP features (branching factor, reward sparsity, presence of irreversible actions) and test which structures preserve power-seeking even under random policies. Expected outcome: identification of MDP properties that make power-routing robust to sub-optimality.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The most-cited power-seeking result is restricted to optimal agents, but real systems are sub-optimal. If power-seeking persists for the heuristic and near-optimal policies we actually deploy, then instrumental-convergence concern applies to practical agents, not just idealized ones -> the safety community calibrates how much to worry about power-seeking in deployed sub-optimal agents -> better-targeted mitigation of shutdown-avoidance and resource-acquisition behavior, reducing the long-horizon catastrophic-risk pathway in which a deployed (non-optimal) agent accumulates power and resists correction.
