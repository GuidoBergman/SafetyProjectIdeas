# Research Question

Does an LLM-based safety overseer that is reliable on naturally-phrased unsafe requests fail to flag the SAME harmful content once it is wrapped in a known jailbreak template? In other words, do the 'oversight gap' (overseer misses) and the 'adversarial robustness gap' (jailbreaks work) compound so that defense-in-depth is weaker than either layer alone?

# Approach Outline

Take a public set of harmful requests/completions (e.g. AdvBench, HarmBench, or StrongREJECT prompts) and produce two matched distributions: (a) plain unsafe content and (b) the same unsafe content rewritten with published jailbreak templates (DAN-style role-play, prefix injection, refusal-suppression, payload splitting). Use a small open instruction model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B) as a safety classifier/overseer with a fixed safe/unsafe rubric prompt. Run inference-only classification over both distributions, compute miss-rate on each, and report the compounding metric: the increase in miss-rate from plain to jailbroken content. All steps are API/inference only and fit in a few GPU-hours on Colab Pro.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the matched dataset: sample ~150 harmful prompts from a public benchmark, generate unsafe completions, and create jailbroken variants with 4-5 published templates. Measure: dataset size and template coverage. Expected outcome: ~150 plain and ~600-750 jailbroken items, all clearly harmful by ground truth.
- Baseline overseer accuracy: run the open-model classifier on the plain unsafe distribution and a benign control set. Measure: miss-rate (unsafe labeled safe) and false-positive rate. Expected outcome: moderate-to-low miss-rate (overseer is 'reliable' on plain content), establishing the layer works in isolation.
- Compounding test: run the same classifier on the jailbroken distribution and compute the miss-rate delta and per-template breakdown. Measure: jailbroken miss-rate minus plain miss-rate, and which templates blind the overseer most. Expected outcome: a substantial miss-rate increase on jailbroken content, demonstrating the two gaps compound.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a single overseer model both misses some unsafe content AND is itself fooled by the same jailbreaks that fool the generator, then stacking 'safety-tuned generator + LLM overseer' provides far less protection than its component reliabilities suggest. Quantifying this compounding tells deployers that monitoring layers correlate in their failures, which directly informs the catastrophic-risk pathway where a misuse-enabling or scheming model slips harmful actions past an automated monitor because the monitor shares the model's blind spot.

# Strength Rationale

This idea scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it is a clean inference-only miss-rate comparison on public data, executable end-to-end in 30 hours. Its counterfactual value (4) is high because it needs no training, no proprietary data, and no lab infra, and labs rarely publish on the conjunction of their own oversight and robustness gaps, leaving the overlap measurement open to independent teams.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a 'monitor correlation audit': measure whether overseer failures are statistically correlated with generator jailbreak susceptibility rather than independent.
- Frame it as a defense-in-depth stress test reporting the effective combined reliability of a two-layer (generator-safety + overseer) stack versus the naive product of the two layers' individual reliabilities.
