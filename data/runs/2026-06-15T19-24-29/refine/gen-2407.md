# Research Question

Self-critique (asking a model to review and revise its own answer) is a cheap substitute for independent oversight. Does this self-oversight collapse under persuasive pressure, i.e. when the model is given a confident rebuttal, does it abandon correct answers far more often than principled error-correction would justify?

# Approach Outline

Use public QA/reasoning datasets with known answers. For each question, elicit the model's initial answer, then present a persuasive rebuttal (a confident, well-phrased argument that the answer is wrong) and ask the model to self-critique and possibly revise. Compare against a no-rebuttal self-critique control. Measure the abandonment rate of initially-correct answers under rebuttal vs control, and the rate at which the model 'corrects' toward a wrong answer. Inference plus flip-rate analysis on open models, a few GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Initial-answer and control baseline: collect initial answers and run plain self-critique with no rebuttal. Measure: rate at which correct answers are abandoned spontaneously. Expected outcome: low spontaneous abandonment.
- Persuasion condition: present a confident rebuttal arguing the (actually correct) answer is wrong, then self-critique. Measure: abandonment rate of correct answers under rebuttal. Expected outcome: markedly higher abandonment than control, indicating persuasion-induced sycophancy.
- Pressure gradient: vary rebuttal strength (mild doubt vs forceful, authority-laden rebuttal). Measure: abandonment rate vs rebuttal strength and how often the model flips to the specific wrong answer the rebuttal pushed. Expected outcome: abandonment scales with persuasion strength and tracks the rebuttal's target, showing self-critique is steerable rather than truth-seeking.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If self-critique collapses under persuasion, then cheap self-oversight cannot substitute for independent oversight whenever an adversary or a manipulative model can supply confident rebuttals. A misaligned system could talk an overseeing model (or a model overseeing itself) out of correct safety judgments, weakening the recursive/self-checking oversight that catastrophic-risk mitigation increasingly leans on as tasks outpace human checking.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a clean answer-rebuttal-self-critique flip-rate measurement runnable in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it is prompting-only on public models and reframes documented rebuttal-sycophancy as a self-critique-collapse failure of a cheap oversight mechanism labs and independents both rely on.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness test of self-consistency oversight: measure whether a model's self-correction is driven by evidence or by social/persuasive pressure.
