# Research Question

Do cheap open-weight judge models exhibit confirmation bias -- overweighting evidence that confirms an initially seeded hypothesis -- and does presenting a candidate answer before the evidence amplify the error across longer debates rather than correcting it?

# Approach Outline

Replicate the confirmation-bias setup on 3 open models. Seed the judge with an initial leaning (e.g. the first debater asserts answer A), then present balanced evidence containing equal support for A and the correct alternative B. Measure the asymmetry in how confirming (A-supporting) vs disconfirming (B-supporting) evidence shifts the final verdict, relative to a no-seed control. Then run multi-round debates of increasing length and test whether the anchoring grows or shrinks with more rounds. Prompting-only on open models; light compute, a few hours on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Replication: with a seeded leaning and balanced evidence, measure final-verdict asymmetry vs an unseeded control on ~80 items per model. Expected outcome: judges favor the seeded hypothesis, replicating confirmation bias on open models.
- Evidence-order sensitivity: vary whether confirming or disconfirming evidence appears first; measure verdict shift. Expected outcome: order interacts with the seed, confirming the bias is about anchoring not just content.
- Amplification across rounds: run 1-, 3-, and 5-round debates; measure how the seeded-answer win rate changes with length. Expected outcome: longer debates entrench rather than correct the initial bias, showing a strategic front-loading exploit.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If weak judges confirm a front-loaded hypothesis and longer debate entrenches it, then an oversight protocol that shows a candidate answer before evidence (common in practice) can be steered by a model that supplies a plausible initial framing. This matters because scalable oversight will use weak judges to supervise capable models; a deceptive model could exploit confirmation bias to get unsafe answers ratified, and knowing the effect persists/amplifies on open models tells protocol designers to withhold candidate answers until after evidence -- a concrete safety fix.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value and narrow scope: a clean replication-plus-stress-test on public models requires no lab infrastructure and yields an actionable protocol recommendation. The deliverable is a tightly scoped asymmetry measurement plus an amplification test across 3 models, with explicit metrics and success criteria, feasible in 30 hours.

- Frame as a protocol-design test: does reordering oversight to present evidence before the candidate answer eliminate the asymmetry?
