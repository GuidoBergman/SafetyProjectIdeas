# Research Question

Khan et al. found that training debaters via self-play to win debates improved judge accuracy on long-document QA. Does that benefit transfer when the judge is a different, weaker model than the one used originally, or does it depend on a specific strong judge?

# Approach Outline

Use the publicly released debater transcripts from Khan et al. (self-play-trained vs baseline debaters on QuALITY-style items). Feed the same transcripts to a panel of differently-sized open judge models (e.g. 1B, 3B, 8B instruct models), prompting each to pick the winning side. For each judge size, compute accuracy under stronger-debater transcripts vs weaker/baseline-debater transcripts, and measure whether the accuracy improvement from better debaters holds, shrinks, or reverses as the judge weakens. This is pure inference over released text; no debate generation or training is needed, so all judges run in a few hours on one consumer GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce on the original judge tier: run an 8B judge over the released transcripts and confirm the reported direction (stronger debaters -> higher judge accuracy). Expected: replicate the positive effect.
- Judge-size sweep: run 1B, 3B, 8B judges over the same transcripts; plot judge accuracy and the stronger-vs-baseline-debater gap against judge size. Expected: the load-bearing result is whether the debater-benefit gap shrinks or flips at 1B.
- Robustness check: vary judge prompt phrasing and re-run the smallest judge to ensure the trend is not a prompt artifact. Expected: the qualitative dependence on judge size is stable across reasonable prompts.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Scalable oversight requires the judge to be weaker than the debaters. If the benefit of stronger debaters only appears for relatively strong judges and vanishes for genuinely weak ones, then debate's promise for true weak-judge oversight is weaker than the optimistic result suggests -> labs betting on debate to oversee superhuman systems would be relying on a benefit that does not hold in the regime that matters -> surfacing this redirects oversight research before it is deployed on frontier systems.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single, well-defined judge-size sweep over already-released transcripts with a clear metric (debater-benefit gap vs judge size). Counterfactual value (4) is high because it reuses public transcripts and open judges, needs no lab access or training, and probes exactly the weak-judge regime labs underreport.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a cross-family transfer test: hold judge size fixed but vary the judge's model family to see whether the debate benefit depends on family-specific affinity rather than raw capability.
