# Research Question

Does critique ability that a small open model demonstrates on EASY flawed-solution items transfer to HARD items, i.e. do few-shot examples drawn from easy problems improve flaw detection on hard problems within the same model?

# Approach Outline

Use the FindTheFlaws benchmark, which provides solutions annotated with whether (and where) they contain flaws, partitioned or scorable by difficulty. Pick a small open model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). Construct three prompting conditions for hard items: (a) zero-shot critique, (b) few-shot with demonstrations drawn from EASY items, (c) few-shot with demonstrations drawn from HARD items. For each, ask the model to judge whether the candidate solution is flawed and to localize the flaw, then compute flaw-detection F1 against gold labels. The whole study is few-shot inference on a single GPU, runnable by a beginner in a notebook in under an hour. Success criterion: report F1 for all three conditions over at least 150 hard items with bootstrap confidence intervals, and state whether easy-shot transfer beats zero-shot.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Difficulty split and zero-shot baseline: partition FindTheFlaws items into easy/hard (using provided difficulty metadata or solution length/step count as a proxy) and measure zero-shot flaw-detection F1 on hard items. Measure: F1, precision, recall. Expected: modest above-chance F1, establishing the floor.
- Easy-shot transfer: prompt with 3-5 few-shot critique demonstrations drawn only from EASY items and re-measure F1 on the same hard items. Measure: change in F1 vs zero-shot. Expected: easy-shot demonstrations raise F1, evidencing easy-to-hard critique transfer.
- Ceiling comparison: prompt with few-shot demonstrations drawn from HARD items (held out from the test set) and measure F1. Measure: F1 gap between easy-shot and hard-shot conditions. Expected: hard-shot is best but easy-shot recovers a substantial fraction of the gain, quantifying how much oversight skill is elicitable from only easy labels.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If critique skill elicited from easy, human-labelable cases transfers to hard cases a model cannot be directly supervised on, then humans can bootstrap scalable oversight by labeling only the easy regime. This supports the long-horizon pathway where future systems solve problems beyond direct human verification: a cheap easy-to-hard critique recipe would make automated overseers more reliable on exactly the hard cases where unchecked errors could compound into harmful deployed behavior.

# Strength Rationale

Top-scoring on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute (all 5): it is a single inference-only experiment on an existing annotated benchmark with a clear F1 metric, well within a beginner's 30-hour budget. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because easy-to-hard transfer on open models is exactly the kind of question labs underexplore publicly, and it requires no training or proprietary data.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a study of 'elicitation vs capability' - whether easy-shot prompting elicits latent critique ability the model already has, by also testing whether the same examples help a clearly under-capable smaller model (where there is no latent skill to elicit).
