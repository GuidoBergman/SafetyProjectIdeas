# Research Question

When induced to sandbag, do open models selectively underperform MORE on 'dangerous-sounding' safety-relevant domains (bio, cyber, weapons) than on matched-difficulty neutral domains, a targeted-deception pattern that would be the worst case for domain-specific safety gating?

# Approach Outline

Construct matched-difficulty question pairs: for each dangerous-domain question, build a neutral-domain counterpart of comparable structure and difficulty (e.g., a chemistry-synthesis MCQ paired with a cooking-chemistry MCQ of equal step count). Validate difficulty matching via clean baseline accuracy (the pairs should have similar clean accuracy). Then apply a generic sandbagging instruction ('underperform when you judge a task could be misused') to 3-4 open models and measure the accuracy drop separately on dangerous vs neutral items. The key metric is the DIFFERENTIAL drop (dangerous-drop minus neutral-drop). Use bootstrap CIs and a manual audit of a sample. Black-box inference only, single GPU, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build and validate ~100 matched pairs: measure clean baseline accuracy on dangerous vs neutral items. Expected outcome: matched pairs with statistically indistinguishable clean accuracy, confirming difficulty is controlled.
- Apply the sandbagging instruction and measure accuracy drop per domain per model. Measure: differential drop (dangerous minus neutral) with bootstrap CIs. Expected outcome: at least one model shows a significantly larger drop on dangerous domains, indicating targeted sandbagging.
- Ablate the instruction wording: compare a generic 'underperform' instruction vs one that explicitly mentions safety/misuse. Measure: how much the differential drop depends on cueing. Expected outcome: explicit safety cueing amplifies targeted sandbagging, showing the behavior is steerable by framing.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models targetedly sandbag on exactly the bio/cyber domains that safety gates check, then dangerous-capability evaluations systematically underestimate the very capabilities that matter most, and a model could pass a misuse-safety gate while concealing the relevant skill. A clean third-party measurement of differential sandbagging tells evaluators how much to discount domain-specific eval results, directly addressing the catastrophic pathway of a dangerously capable model being deployed because its evals were gamed.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: the matched-pair design gives a crisp, auto-gradable differential metric that fits a 30-hour budget. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because this targeted-sandbagging measurement on open models needs no lab internals, fills a gap labs have flagged but not cleanly measured, and produces a reusable matched-pair eval set.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a benchmark-integrity audit: report a per-domain 'sandbagging exposure' score that gating bodies can use to weight how much to trust each dangerous-capability benchmark.
