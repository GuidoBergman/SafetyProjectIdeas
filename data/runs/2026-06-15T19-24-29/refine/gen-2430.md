# Research Question

As you stack oversight layers (answer, critique, critique-of-critique, ...), do errors compound or self-correct, and what is the empirical per-layer error-propagation factor? This gives a cheap empirical test of the bounded-error assumption that recursive reward modeling relies on.

# Approach Outline

Use open models (e.g. two sizes of Qwen2.5, or a single Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) on a single consumer GPU, inference-only. On a labeled QA task, build a stacked critique pipeline: answer, then a critique of the answer, then a critique-of-the-critique, and so on, each layer revising the verdict. Measure final verdict accuracy as a function of stack depth (0 to ~4). Estimate whether each added layer tends to correct or amplify the previous layer's errors, yielding an empirical per-layer error-propagation factor. All prompting; no training, public data.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Depth-vs-accuracy curve: on ~150 labeled questions, run the stacked critique pipeline to depth 4 and record verdict accuracy at each depth. Measure: accuracy(depth). Expected outcome: a curve showing whether accuracy improves, plateaus, or degrades with depth.
- Per-layer correction vs amplification: at each layer, track items that were correct-then-flipped-wrong vs wrong-then-flipped-correct, to estimate a per-layer propagation factor (>1 means errors amplify). Measure: net correction rate per layer. Expected outcome: a quantified propagation factor revealing whether deeper oversight self-corrects or compounds errors.
- Seeded-error stress test: deliberately inject a wrong verdict at layer 0 and see how many layers are needed to recover (if ever); also vary whether layers can see ground-truth-style hints. Measure: recovery depth and final accuracy. Expected outcome: many seeded errors persist or amplify through layers, demonstrating that per-layer error is not reliably bounded.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Recursive oversight (recursive reward modeling, scalable supervision hierarchies) stays aligned only if per-layer error is bounded; if a cheap measurement shows errors compound rather than self-correct, then proposals to oversee superhuman systems by stacking AI overseers have an unmeasured failure mode. Deploying such hierarchies under a false bounded-error assumption could let systematic errors accumulate into confidently-wrong top-level supervision of a powerful system, a direct path to mis-aligned oversight of high-stakes AI.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is tightly scoped to a single deliverable (a depth-vs-accuracy curve plus a per-layer propagation factor) and runs inference-only on open models, scoring high on narrow scope and accessible complexity. Its counterfactual value is strong: it provides a cheap empirical test of a foundational recursive-oversight assumption using only public models and data, exactly the kind of unglamorous measurement labs skip but an independent team can fully own.
