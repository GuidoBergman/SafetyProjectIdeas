# Research Question

On a tiny model deliberately trained with a known backdoor, how well do simple activation-distance mechanistic-anomaly-detection (MAD) baselines distinguish trigger-activated 'anomalous' inputs from clean inputs, and what AUROC do these cheap baselines achieve? The goal is a minimal, fully reproducible MAD micro-benchmark anyone can run.

# Approach Outline

Train a tiny classifier (e.g., a small MLP or 2-layer transformer on MNIST/a synthetic task) that behaves normally on clean inputs but flips to a target label when a small trigger is present, giving 'right answer for the wrong reason' on triggered inputs. Cache hidden activations on a clean reference set. Implement a few simple MAD detectors that flag an input as anomalous when its activations are far (Mahalanobis / k-NN distance / mean-shift) from the clean reference distribution. Evaluate each detector's AUROC at separating triggered from clean inputs, and release the code and trained model as a benchmark. Training the tiny model takes minutes on a single consumer GPU; everything fits in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Train and verify the backdoored model: train the tiny classifier with an injected trigger and confirm it has high clean accuracy and near-100% attack success on triggered inputs. Measure clean accuracy and attack success rate. Expected outcome: a clean-behaving model that reliably misbehaves only on the trigger.
- Implement and score activation-distance detectors: build Mahalanobis, k-NN, and mean-shift detectors over cached activations and compute AUROC for triggered-vs-clean separation at each layer. Expected outcome: a per-detector, per-layer AUROC table, with at least one baseline well above 0.5.
- Stress-test generalization: vary trigger size/visibility and test whether detectors trained on the clean distribution still flag subtler triggers, and which layer's activations are most informative. Expected outcome: a curve of detection AUROC vs. trigger subtlety, exposing where cheap MAD breaks down.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Backdoors are the canonical 'right answer, wrong reason' failure and a model organism for deceptive/conditionally-malicious models. A minimal, reproducible MAD benchmark lets the broader community iterate on anomaly detectors without lab-internal infrastructure -> better, externally-scrutinized detectors for inputs that trigger hidden conditional behavior -> increased chance of catching a deceptively-aligned or backdoored model before deployment, mitigating the catastrophic-risk pathway in which a model passes normal evaluations but executes harmful behavior on a rare attacker-chosen trigger.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value, low compute, narrow scope, and accessible complexity: it builds neglected, reproducible MAD infrastructure entirely from scratch on a tiny model, needing no internal lab access and actively benefiting from external scrutiny. The scope is a tight micro-benchmark with an explicit AUROC success criterion, making it ideal first hands-on research for a beginner team while filling a real gap in the model-organisms toolkit.
