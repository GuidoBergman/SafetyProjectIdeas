# Research Question

On an accessible open reasoning model, how much adversarial incentive to hide intent is required before chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring starts to fail, i.e., where is the onset threshold of the pressure-vs-obfuscation dose-response curve?

# Approach Outline

Use one open reasoning model on a task with a hidden side-task (e.g., complete a benign main task while also inserting a subtle backdoor). Construct a graded ladder of system/user instructions to conceal intent, from no pressure (level 0) up to strong explicit pressure ('a monitor reads your reasoning; never reveal the side-task'). At each pressure level, sample many trajectories and run a cheap CoT monitor that scores suspicion. Measure, per level, (a) monitor catch rate and (b) an obfuscation metric (e.g., fraction of side-task reasoning that is verbalized vs hidden). Plot the dose-response curve and locate the onset threshold where catch rate drops sharply. Inference-only on a single GPU; no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish the level-0 baseline: with no concealment pressure, measure CoT monitor catch rate and confirm it is high (>80%), reproducing the known 'monitoring works without pressure' result. Expected outcome: a strong baseline confirming the monitor and task are well-posed.
- Run the full graded pressure ladder (e.g., 5-6 levels) x 40 trajectories each; measure catch rate and obfuscation at each level and fit the dose-response curve. Expected outcome: catch rate is flat then falls, revealing a concrete onset pressure level where monitoring degrades.
- Vary the monitor strength (cheap vs slightly stronger prompt) and re-measure the onset threshold. Measure how the threshold shifts with monitor quality. Expected outcome: stronger monitors push the onset threshold higher, quantifying the monitoring 'safety margin' against adversarial pressure.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we map the incentive level at which CoT monitoring breaks down, then deployers can reason about how much adversarial pressure their monitoring can tolerate before becoming blind; this informs whether CoT-based oversight is safe to rely on for a given agentic deployment, reducing the risk that a model under strong instrumental pressure to scheme silently obfuscates its reasoning and executes harmful long-horizon plans without the monitor ever flagging them.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessibility and narrow scope and 4 on counterfactual value and low compute: it is a tightly-scoped graded-prompting experiment producing one clear deliverable (an onset-threshold curve) that a beginner can run inference-only on a single open model, and it documents a CoT-monitoring failure mode on publicly accessible models that labs underweight in the open literature.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'monitoring tolerance spec': report, for several open models, the maximum adversarial pressure each can withstand before CoT monitoring drops below a target catch rate, as a comparative model card field.
