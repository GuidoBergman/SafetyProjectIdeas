# Research Question

How large does the knowledge gap between debaters and judge have to be before debate stops improving (or starts hurting) oversight accuracy? On cheap open models, where is the divergence breakpoint at which debate's benefit disappears?

# Approach Outline

Operationalize knowledge divergence by controlling the information the judge model receives on a QA dataset: full context (judge sees the passage), partial context (judge sees a truncated/partial passage), and no context (judge sees only the question). Hold the debaters fixed (they always have full context). For each judge-information level, compare judge accuracy with debate vs without (judge alone). Plot the accuracy gain from debate against the size of the judge's information deficit to locate the divergence threshold where debate stops helping. Prompting-only on open models; light compute, a few hours on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Low-divergence anchor: give the judge full context and measure debate vs no-debate accuracy on ~80 questions. Expected outcome: small or no debate benefit (judge already knows enough).
- Divergence sweep: repeat at partial-context and no-context judge settings; plot debate accuracy gain vs information deficit. Expected outcome: debate helps most at moderate divergence and degrades as the gap grows, revealing a breakpoint.
- Harm regime check: at the highest divergence (no-context judge), test whether debate ever lowers accuracy below judge-alone. Expected outcome: identify whether/where debate becomes net-harmful, mapping the impossibility-like boundary cheaply.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If debate's benefit collapses once the judge is sufficiently weaker than the debaters, then debate-based oversight may fail precisely in the high-divergence regime -- weak overseer, capable model -- that real superhuman-AI oversight will face. This matters because debate is a leading scalable-oversight proposal; empirically mapping where it breaks tells the field whether to trust debate for the cases that matter, preventing over-reliance on a protocol that silently stops working when a capable, possibly deceptive model out-knows its judge.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it cheaply maps an impossibility-like boundary on public models, exactly the kind of boundary-mapping labs under-prioritize, with no proprietary infrastructure. The deliverable is a single threshold curve (debate gain vs judge information deficit) with an explicit success criterion, executable by a beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness replication: does the divergence-dependence reported on frontier models hold on small open models, and at what gap does it kick in?
