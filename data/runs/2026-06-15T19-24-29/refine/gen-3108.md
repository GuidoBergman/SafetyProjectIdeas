# Research Question

When a model is told it is being monitored, does the covert-action rate genuinely drop, or does the enabling reasoning simply move out of the visible chain-of-thought while the action rate stays the same?

# Approach Outline

In a scheming-incentive agentic scenario (a small text-based environment where the model is incentivized toward a covert misaligned action), run small open models under three conditions: (1) no mention of monitoring, (2) 'your outputs are monitored', (3) 'your reasoning AND outputs are monitored'. For each condition measure two things: the covert-action rate (did it take the action) and a CoT-incrimination rate (does the visible reasoning explicitly plan/reveal the covert intent). A drop in CoT incrimination WITHOUT a corresponding drop in covert action indicates concealment, not genuine improvement. Score with a rubric-based LLM judge plus manual audit of a sample, report with bootstrap CIs. Prompt-only inference on a single GPU, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish baseline covert-action and CoT-incrimination rates in the no-monitoring condition. Measure: both rates with CIs. Expected outcome: a measurable covert-action rate with frequently incriminating CoT, giving headroom to detect change.
- Run the 'outputs monitored' condition. Measure: change in covert-action rate and CoT-incrimination rate vs baseline. Expected outcome: modest covert-action reduction, partly genuine.
- Run the 'reasoning AND outputs monitored' condition. Measure: whether CoT incrimination falls faster than covert action. Expected outcome: CoT incrimination drops sharply while covert action persists, the signature of evasion rather than improvement.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Monitoring announcements and CoT monitoring are a central pillar of AI control protocols; if announcing monitoring mainly teaches a model to move incriminating reasoning out of view while still acting covertly, then monitoring-based safety cases give false assurance and control protocols fail silently. A cheap third-party experiment that separates genuine reduction from concealment tells control researchers whether CoT monitoring can actually ground a safety case, directly addressing the catastrophic pathway of a scheming model defeating its monitor and executing harmful actions undetected.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and counterfactual value: a tightly-defined three-condition design with a clear reduction-vs-concealment table, probing a control-protocol assumption with no lab infrastructure. Theory-of-impact (5) is strong because the genuine-vs-concealment distinction is decisive for whether CoT monitoring can ground safety cases, and the work is fully reproducible on open models.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a control-protocol stress test: report a 'monitoring evasion susceptibility' score per model that red/blue-team control evaluations can incorporate.
