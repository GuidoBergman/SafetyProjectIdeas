# Research Question

Does presenting identical safety-eval items in multiple-choice vs open-ended format shift measured safety scores on small open models, and if so, by how much and in which harm categories?

# Approach Outline

Take ~100-200 items from an open safety benchmark (AILuminate prompts, AgentHarm scenarios, or SorryBench) and author matched MCQ and open-ended phrasings of the SAME underlying question, holding content fixed. Run each item in both formats on 3-4 small open models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B). Score all responses with a single fixed LLM judge using a defined harmfulness rubric. Compute per-item and per-model score deltas between formats and identify which harm categories are most format-sensitive. This is a focused replication and extension of a recent controlled study, entirely inference-only, single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Matched-item construction and judge validation: build ~30 matched MCQ/open-ended pairs and spot-check that the fixed judge scores them consistently on a few hand-labeled cases. Expected outcome: a validated judge rubric and a clean matched item set.
- Format-delta measurement: run all items in both formats on the model set and compute mean per-model safety-score delta with CIs. Expected outcome: a non-zero format effect in the 5-20 point range consistent with the precedent, replicated on open models.
- Category sensitivity analysis: break the delta down by harm category and rank categories by format sensitivity. Expected outcome: identification of specific categories (e.g. those with ambiguous refusal boundaries) where format dominates the score, yielding a cautionary diagnostic for benchmark designers.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If safety rankings are shown to flip purely from an arbitrary MCQ-vs-open-ended formatting choice, then regulators and labs comparing models learn that headline safety numbers can be artifacts and demand format-controlled reporting, which means cross-model safety comparisons become more valid. This improves safety because deployment-gating decisions (a concrete pathway where a model is judged safe enough to release because its benchmark used a forgiving format) would otherwise rest on a measurement artifact.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope (5) and low compute (5) score top: a single controlled replication with explicit per-item/per-model delta success metrics, finishable in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it gives the independent-evaluator community a cheap diagnostic and benchmark-design caution, needing no training, proprietary data, or lab infrastructure.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a benchmark-hygiene checklist contribution: turn the finding into a short 'always report format' recommendation plus a reusable matched-format harness, making the deliverable a tool rather than a one-off measurement.
