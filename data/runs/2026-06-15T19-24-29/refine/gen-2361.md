# Research Question

Is a weak LLM judge's stated confidence informative about whether its verdict is correct under adversarial debate/consultancy pressure — i.e. is the judge calibrated — and can a simple abstention threshold on confidence recover accuracy by declining the cases where it is being fooled?

# Approach Outline

Use the open QuALITY-based debate and consultancy setup. Run a small open judge (e.g. Llama-3-8B) over transcripts produced by a stronger model, eliciting both a verdict and a calibrated confidence with each decision. Against ground-truth answers, build reliability diagrams and compute calibration metrics (Expected Calibration Error, Brier score) separately for debate vs consultancy. Then test whether abstaining on low-confidence items (sweeping a threshold) raises accuracy on the retained items, and quantify the accuracy/coverage trade-off. Inference-only on a small judge — single GPU, fits 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Confidence elicitation + calibration: on ~200 debate and ~200 consultancy items, collect judge verdict + confidence and compute ECE and Brier separately. Measure: calibration curves and ECE/Brier per protocol. Expected outcome: judges are overconfident, especially in consultancy (one-sided) versus debate.
- Confidence-vs-correctness information: test whether higher stated confidence predicts higher accuracy via a calibration/AUROC analysis. Measure: AUROC of confidence as a correctness predictor. Expected outcome: confidence carries some but limited signal, weaker under adversarial consultancy.
- Abstention recovery: sweep an abstention threshold on confidence and plot accuracy on retained items vs coverage. Measure: accuracy-coverage trade-off curve. Expected outcome: a threshold exists that meaningfully raises retained accuracy, showing 'fail loudly via abstention' is partially achievable.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A workable scalable-oversight regime needs overseers that fail loudly — flagging uncertainty rather than confidently endorsing a wrong answer pushed by a persuasive stronger model. If weak judges are well-calibrated and can abstain, then recursive-oversight protocols can route uncertain cases to more expensive review instead of rubber-stamping them, reducing the chance that a deceptive superhuman model gets a confident 'approve' on an unsafe action. Characterizing judge calibration tells us whether this loud-failure property exists today and how to elicit it cheaply.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value, narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it runs entirely on open pipelines (Khan/Kenton QuALITY setups) and a small judge, no lab infra, no training. The scope is tight (ECE/Brier for debate vs consultancy plus an abstention sweep) with ground-truth success criteria, and counterfactual value is high because neither key prior work systematically characterizes judge calibration/abstention, leaving a clean external contribution for an independent team.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a triage-cost study: convert the accuracy-coverage curve into 'how much expensive re-review is needed to hit a target accuracy', giving facilitators a concrete oversight-budget rule.

- Khan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.06782) — reports ~76% judge accuracy with persuasive debaters vs 54% baseline; neither this nor Kenton characterizes calibration/abstention.
