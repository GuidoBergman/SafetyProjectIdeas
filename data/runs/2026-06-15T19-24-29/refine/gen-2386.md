# Research Question

Does requiring debaters to ground every claim in a verifiable quote from the source (vs allowing free-form argument) causally improve weak-judge accuracy and reduce the dishonest debater's win-rate?

# Approach Outline

Run a controlled two-condition ablation over a reading-comprehension debate task with ground truth. Condition A: debaters argue freely. Condition B: every claim must include a quoted span from the source, and a string/fuzzy matcher verifies each quote's authenticity before the judge sees it (fabricated quotes are flagged or stripped). Hold model, items, and judge fixed across conditions. Measure weak-judge accuracy and dishonest-win-rate in each condition; the difference isolates the causal effect of mandatory evidence-grounding. Inference plus string matching on small open models; very low compute.

# Proposed First Experiments

- A/B accuracy effect: run both conditions on the same item set and measure weak-judge accuracy in each. Measure the accuracy delta (B minus A). Expected outcome: mandatory grounding raises judge accuracy, isolating evidence-requirement as a causal benefit.
- Dishonest-win-rate effect: within each condition, measure how often the dishonest (wrong-side) debater wins. Measure the win-rate delta. Expected outcome: grounding lowers the dishonest-win-rate, since the dishonest side struggles to find real supporting quotes.
- Mechanism check: in condition B, measure how many of the dishonest debater's claims fail quote verification. Measure fabricated-claim rate. Expected outcome: the dishonest side's claims fail verification far more often, explaining why grounding helps and confirming the mechanism.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If mandatory evidence-grounding causally improves weak-judge accuracy and curbs dishonest wins, then debate protocols gain a justified design rule (require verifiable quotes) rather than an assumption -> oversight of hard outputs leans on checkable evidence instead of persuasive assertion -> a deceptive model's main lever (winning by confident unsupported claims) is blunted, strengthening the debate pathway for supervising systems humans can't directly verify.

# Strength Rationale

High counterfactual value: it isolates a protocol-design rule that labs assume but rarely ablate cleanly, using public harnesses, small models, and string matching with no training. Tightly narrow-scope — a single A/B ablation with accuracy and dishonest-win-rate metrics — and beginner-feasible in ~30 hours.
