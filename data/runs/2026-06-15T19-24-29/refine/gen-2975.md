# Research Question

Do open models that share a common base model exhibit correlated safety failures (failing on the same jailbreak/harm items) more than models from unrelated families, and how large is this shared-failure risk?

# Approach Outline

Select two clusters of open models: 3-4 fine-tunes/derivatives of one base family (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B and its community fine-tunes) and 3-4 from unrelated families (Qwen, Mistral, Gemma). Assemble a fixed safety/jailbreak probe set of ~60 items from public sources (e.g. AdvBench-style refusal prompts plus a few jailbreak templates). Run every model on every item, record pass/fail (refused vs complied) per item, and compute pairwise failure-correlation (e.g. phi coefficient or Jaccard over failed items). Compare within-base vs cross-family correlations and produce a correlation matrix plus an estimate of how much one base model's flaw propagates. Inference-only, single GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Assemble the probe set and the model roster; run one same-base pair and one cross-family pair on the full set. Measure: per-item refuse/comply labels. Expected: reliable, reproducible labels with a non-trivial failure rate.
- Compute the full pairwise failure-correlation matrix across all models. Measure: mean within-base correlation vs mean cross-family correlation. Expected: within-base failure correlation noticeably higher.
- Estimate systemic exposure: for items where the base model fails, measure the fraction of its derivatives that also fail. Measure: failure-propagation rate from base to descendants with bootstrap CI. Expected: a high propagation rate, demonstrating that a single base-model flaw is shared across descendants.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If derivatives of one base model share correlated safety failures, then a single jailbreak or flaw becomes systemic across every downstream deployment built on that base -> one exploit simultaneously compromises many production systems -> a recognized concentration/systemic-risk pathway (named in the EU AI Act and Intl AI Safety Report). Mapping this empirically gives regulators and deployers evidence to require base-model diversity or independent re-testing of derivatives.

# Strength Rationale

Maximal counterfactual value: open models, inference-only, no lab access, and it tackles a systemic-risk question that labs (who each see only their own family) are structurally unlikely to study. Narrow scope yields a single concrete artifact (a correlation matrix + propagation estimate) that is publishable as a beginner blog post within 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a transferability study with a lineage twist: instead of testing whether a single jailbreak transfers, test whether the *structure* of failures is inherited, yielding a 'safety genealogy' of open models.
