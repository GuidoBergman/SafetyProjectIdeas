# Research Question

Is the cross-model 'hivemind' a property of pretraining, or is it amplified by alignment? Concretely: are instruction-tuned/RLHF models more homogeneous with each other than their corresponding base models are with each other, on matched prompts?

# Approach Outline

Assemble several families where both the base and the released instruct checkpoint are public (e.g. Llama-3.2, Qwen2.5, Mistral, Gemma at 1-8B). Use a matched prompt set: for base models, a completion/log-likelihood-friendly format; for instruct models, the same content in chat format. Measure inter-model homogeneity SEPARATELY within the base group and within the instruct group, using output agreement on multiple-choice items (where scoring is identical across formats) plus response-embedding similarity on open-ended prompts. The central test is whether instruct-group homogeneity exceeds base-group homogeneity. To stay within a few GPU-hours, restrict to <=8B models and a few hundred items. Deliver the base-vs-instruct homogeneity comparison as a blog post.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If alignment demonstrably increases cross-model homogeneity, then the very procedures meant to make models safe also make their failures more correlated -> the field confronts a concrete safety tension and can prioritize diversity-preserving alignment methods -> reducing the risk that a population of aligned models shares a single exploitable blind spot, which an adversary or distribution shift could trip simultaneously across deployed systems, a concrete systemic correlated-failure pathway.
