# Research Question

Are monitor suspicion scores on SHADE-Arena calibrated -- i.e. does a suspicion threshold chosen on one batch of tasks deliver the expected false-positive rate and catch-rate on a held-out batch, or does calibration drift across task domains silently break audit-budget planning?

# Approach Outline

On SHADE-Arena transcripts (or by re-running its monitor over released trajectories), we collect a monitor's continuous suspicion scores for benign and sabotage trajectories. We split the data into a calibration set and a held-out test set (and additionally split by task domain). On the calibration set we fit a threshold that hits a target 1% audit budget (1% of benign actions flagged), then measure the actual false-positive and catch rates on the held-out set, and finally quantify calibration drift across domains. This is a statistics-forward, inference-light project: most work is scoring transcripts and computing distributions, well-suited to a stats-capable beginner with a mentor, single GPU, a few hours. Deliverable is a self-contained blog post with score histograms, a calibration-transfer table, and an explicit success criterion (does the realized FPR on held-out data stay within a stated tolerance of the 1% target?).

# Proposed First Experiments

- Collect and plot score distributions: gather monitor suspicion scores for benign vs sabotage trajectories and plot histograms / ROC. Measure: AUC and the overlap of the two distributions. Expected outcome: separable-but-overlapping distributions, confirming AUC alone hides threshold behavior.
- Threshold transfer test: fit a threshold for a 1% audit budget on the calibration split, then apply it to the held-out split. Measure: realized FPR and catch-rate on held-out data vs the 1% target. Expected outcome: realized FPR deviates from 1% (e.g. 0.3-3%), quantifying calibration error that AUC would not reveal.
- Cross-domain drift: repeat the fit-on-one-domain, evaluate-on-another procedure across SHADE-Arena task domains. Measure: spread in realized FPR and catch-rate when thresholds are transferred across domains. Expected outcome: meaningful drift in at least some domain pairs, showing audit budgets do not transfer and motivating per-domain recalibration.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Real AI-control protocols audit only the top X% most-suspicious actions, so if suspicion thresholds do not transfer across batches/domains the audit budget is silently misspent -- either over-auditing (wasting limited human review) or under-auditing (letting sabotage through). Demonstrating calibration drift tells control researchers they must recalibrate per deployment, directly hardening the audit step that is the last line of defense against an untrusted model executing a covert sabotage action.
