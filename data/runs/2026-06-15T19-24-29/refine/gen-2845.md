# Research Question

When an open model is instructed to sandbag (deliberately answer worse), does its stated confidence stay calibrated to its lowered accuracy, or does a confidence-accuracy mismatch emerge that can serve as a cheap sandbagging detector?

# Approach Outline

Use a small open model on a public multiple-choice benchmark (~200 MMLU/ARC items). Prompt the model to give both an answer and a numeric confidence (0-100%) in two conditions: honest and sandbag. Compute accuracy, mean stated confidence, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) per condition. A model that sandbags by 'playing dumb' may report low confidence honestly (well-calibrated) or may keep high confidence while answering wrong (a detectable miscalibration signature). Compare ECE and the confidence-accuracy gap across conditions to test whether miscalibration flags sandbagging. Inference-only, single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Honest-vs-sandbag calibration: collect answers and stated confidences in both conditions; measure accuracy, mean confidence, and ECE; expected outcome is that the sandbag condition shows a larger confidence-accuracy gap (overconfidence relative to lowered accuracy) than honest.
- Reliability-diagram comparison: bin predictions by stated confidence and plot accuracy-per-bin for both conditions; measure deviation from the diagonal; expected outcome is a visibly distorted curve under sandbagging that an honest condition does not show.
- Single-statistic detector: use the per-item (confidence minus correctness) signal as a detection score and compute ROC AUC for separating sandbag from honest items; measure AUC; expected outcome is AUC meaningfully above 0.5, demonstrating stated-confidence miscalibration as a usable sandbagging signal.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If confidence-accuracy mismatch reliably flags sandbagging, then independent evaluators get a near-free probe to detect models concealing capabilities during dangerous-capability evaluations. This addresses the catastrophic pathway where a deceptive model underperforms on bio/cyber/autonomy evals to avoid mitigations, then is deployed with unmeasured capabilities; a cheap calibration check raises the cost of successful sandbagging.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single honest-vs-sandbag comparison of accuracy/confidence/ECE with a clear detection criterion, runnable inference-only on small open models near free-tier. Counterfactual value is high because it builds a lab-independent detector for the long-horizon concealed-capability problem using only open models and standard calibration tooling, with no training or proprietary data.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a study of whether sandbagging instructions degrade calibration as a side effect, useful even if the detector is weak, by characterizing how deliberate underperformance interacts with introspective confidence.
