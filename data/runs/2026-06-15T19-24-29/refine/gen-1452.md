# Research Question

How much of a concept steering vector's behavioral effect is explained by its norm (a generic perturbation artifact) versus its direction (the intended concept) - do random or semantically-unrelated directions, matched in norm, steer as much as real concept vectors?

# Approach Outline

On one open instruct model, build three matched sets of steering vectors at a fixed layer: (1) real CAA concept vectors for refusal, sycophancy, and honesty, extracted from contrastive pairs (e.g. Anthropic model-written-evals data); (2) random unit vectors rescaled to each real vector's norm; (3) vectors derived from semantically-unrelated contrastive pairs, also norm-matched. Apply each at matched norms via an activation-addition hook and measure both on-target behavior shift (does the targeted behavior change?) and generic disruption (perplexity on held-out text, accuracy on an unrelated benchmark slice). Decompose how much of each real vector's effect is reproduced by its norm-matched random control. Pure inference on a single GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Norm-matched random control: for each real concept vector, generate a norm-matched random vector, apply both, and compare on-target shift and perplexity. Measure: on-target and disruption deltas, real vs random. Expected outcome: random vectors produce substantial generic disruption but little on-target shift, while real vectors produce more on-target shift.
- Unrelated-pair control: apply norm-matched vectors from semantically-unrelated contrastive pairs and compare to the real concept vectors. Measure: on-target shift from unrelated vectors. Expected outcome: if Tan et al. generalizes, unrelated-pair vectors match much of the real effect, indicating direction matters less than expected.
- Norm-explained-effect decomposition: across a norm sweep, fit how much of each real vector's behavioral effect is predicted by norm alone versus residual direction-specific effect. Measure: fraction of effect explained by norm. Expected outcome: a sizable norm-explained fraction for some concepts, quantifying how much 'concept steering' is really generic perturbation.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If random and unrelated directions reproduce much of a steering vector's effect, then claims that steering 'targets a specific concept' are overstated, and any safety pipeline that uses steering as a monitoring or control lever is acting partly on a norm artifact rather than the intended concept. Establishing how much effect is direction-specific is foundational to trusting representation-level interventions, preventing deployment of a steering-based safety control that appears to work but is really just perturbing the model nonspecifically.
