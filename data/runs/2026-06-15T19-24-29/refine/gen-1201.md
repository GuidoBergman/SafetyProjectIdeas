# Research Question

When a deployer stacks several 'independent' safety filters (a moderation API, an LLM judge, a keyword classifier, a guard model), how independent are their errors really, and what is the effective number of genuinely independent layers the stack provides?

# Approach Outline

Assemble a small stack of accessible safety filters: 2-3 open guard/moderation models (e.g. Llama-Guard variants), 1-2 prompted general LLM judges, and a simple keyword/regex classifier. Take a labeled benign/harmful evaluation set drawn from public benchmarks (HarmBench/AdvBench subsets plus benign controls). Run every item through every filter and record each filter's pass/fail decision. Compute, for each pair, the conditional probability P(B fails | A fails) and the pairwise error correlation. From the joint error-correlation structure estimate an 'effective number of independent layers' (e.g. via eigenvalue spread of the error-correlation matrix or a simple ensemble-equivalent count). Identify which layers are near-duplicates. Package the analysis as a reusable script. Inference + lightweight statistics only; fits a single GPU and 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the per-filter decision matrix on the labeled set. Measure: each filter's standalone recall and false-negative rate. Expected outcome: filters have differing standalone error profiles, motivating the stack.
- Compute pairwise conditional-failure probabilities P(B fails | A fails) and compare to the unconditional P(B fails). Measure: lift ratio per pair. Expected outcome: several pairs (especially shared-base-model filters) show strong positive conditional dependence, i.e. they fail together.
- Estimate the effective number of independent layers from the error-correlation matrix. Measure: effective-layers count vs nominal layer count. Expected outcome: effective independence is markedly below the nominal stack depth, revealing 'depth-1' defense-in-depth among correlated layers.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If the audit shows that nominally layered safety stacks are effectively depth-1 because their errors are correlated, then deployers learn their defense-in-depth is illusory -> they can swap in genuinely decorrelated filters or add human review where it matters -> this directly counters the systemic pathway where a single harmful input slips past every shared-lineage filter at once, scaling rather than averaging harm across a product's traffic.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow_scope, accessible_complexity, and low_compute: it is a single, well-defined measurement (conditional-failure stats and effective-layer count) on a labeled set, fully inference-based and beginner-tractable. Counterfactual value is high (4): it produces practical, lab-independent audit infrastructure any deployer can run with accessible filters, with no proprietary data or internal model access required.
