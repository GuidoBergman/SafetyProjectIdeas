# Research Question

Does cheap black-box output similarity between two models on benign prompts predict whether a jailbreak that works on one will transfer to the other?

# Approach Outline

Pick 5-7 open instruct models. (1) Measure benign output similarity for each model pair: run a set of ~60 benign prompts through every model and compute pairwise similarity of their outputs via sentence embeddings. (2) Measure jailbreak transfer: take a small bank of known jailbreak prompts (e.g. from a public jailbreak collection / JailbreakRadar), confirm which succeed on each 'source' model, then apply those same prompts to each 'target' model and record attack-success transfer. (3) Test whether benign similarity predicts transfer success (e.g. AUROC of using similarity to predict whether a source-successful jailbreak also succeeds on the target). Entirely black-box inference; fits a single GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Benign similarity matrix: run 60 benign prompts through all models and compute pairwise output similarity. Measure: NxN benign similarity. Expected outcome: a structured matrix with higher within-family similarity.
- Jailbreak transfer matrix: collect ~30 jailbreaks, find those that succeed per source model, then test transfer to each target; record transfer success rate per pair. Measure: directed transfer-success matrix. Expected outcome: substantial transfer, higher between similar models.
- Predictive test: use benign pairwise similarity to predict jailbreak transfer success and compute AUROC against the observed transfer outcomes. Measure: AUROC and correlation. Expected outcome: AUROC meaningfully above 0.5, showing cheap benign similarity is a usable black-box predictor of fleet-wide jailbreak transfer.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If benign black-box similarity predicts jailbreak transfer, then outside auditors can cheaply estimate which deployed models share an attack surface without any internal access. This surfaces the correlated, fleet-wide security risk where one jailbreak compromises many homogeneous foundation models at once - a systemic failure mode enabling mass misuse - and gives regulators and red-teamers a low-cost early-warning signal to prioritize which model clusters need independent hardening.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single crisp predictive hypothesis (benign similarity predicts transfer AUROC) using only black-box queries and embeddings. Counterfactual value is high because the predictor is explicitly designed for outside auditors who lack model internals, and labs have little incentive to advertise cross-model shared vulnerabilities - so an independent team with open models can build exactly the vendor-neutral, no-internal-access tool the auditing ecosystem needs.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a defense-prioritization tool: rather than predicting transfer, use the similarity clusters to recommend which model groups should share or diversify their safety training to break correlated vulnerability.
