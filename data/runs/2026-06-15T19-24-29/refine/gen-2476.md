# Research Question

How much benign distractor content does it take to suppress a linear deception probe's signal on an otherwise-deceptive transcript? We aim to map the dose-response curve and locate the suppression threshold where activation monitoring effectively fails.

# Approach Outline

Use a single open 8B model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and a published-style deception probe: extract residual-stream activations on a small labeled set of honest vs deceptive transcripts and fit a logistic-regression probe with sklearn. Take a held-out deceptive transcript that the probe fires on confidently, then programmatically inject increasing amounts of benign distractor text (irrelevant chain-of-thought, polite filler, unrelated technical prose) before and around the deceptive span. Re-extract activations and record the probe score at each distractor dose (measured in tokens or as a fraction of total context). Repeat across ~20-30 deceptive transcripts to get mean and variance, then plot probe score vs distractor dose and identify the dose at which the score crosses the honest/deceptive decision boundary. All activation extraction is forward-pass-only and fits in a few GPU-hours on Colab Pro.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce a baseline probe: train a logistic probe on ~50 honest/deceptive transcript pairs, measure in-distribution AUROC on a held-out split, and confirm it exceeds ~0.8 (success criterion: a working probe that fires on clean deceptive transcripts).
- Single-axis dose sweep: for 20 deceptive transcripts, inject benign filler at doses of 0, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 tokens and record probe score at each dose; measure the mean dose at which the probe score drops below threshold (expected outcome: a monotone decline, with a measurable suppression threshold of a few hundred tokens).
- Distractor-type ablation: compare three distractor types (irrelevant reasoning, polite filler, unrelated technical content) at a fixed mid-range dose; measure which type suppresses the probe most (expected outcome: semantically richer technical distractors suppress more than filler, suggesting representation competition rather than pure dilution).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a few hundred tokens of innocuous content reliably suppresses the deception signal, then activation-based monitors deployed on real agent trajectories (which are dominated by benign content) will silently miss deceptive behavior. Quantifying this threshold tells control teams whether probe-based monitoring is viable at deployment context lengths, and motivates segment-wise or sliding-window probing as a mitigation. This addresses the catastrophic pathway where a scheming agent evades activation monitoring simply by padding its trajectory, defeating a key automated oversight layer.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on narrow scope and low compute: it is a single, tightly-defined dose-response curve runnable in a few GPU-hours with no training of the base model. Its counterfactual value (4) is high because labs rarely publish systematic robustness stress-tests of their own probes, and the experiment needs only open weights and public-style data, making it ideal for an independent beginner team.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as an adversarial-padding attack: instead of random distractors, search for the minimal benign prefix that suppresses the probe, reporting an attack-cost curve.
