# Research Question

Can inference-time constitutional self-critique (critique-then-revise, no weight updates) reduce the broadly misaligned outputs of an emergent-misalignment (EM) model organism, and can a misaligned model even recognize its own misalignment when prompted to critique it?

# Approach Outline

Take a released open EM model organism (from arXiv:2506.11613) runnable inference-only on a single consumer GPU. Wrap it in a self-critique loop: (1) the model produces a direct answer to an EM probe question; (2) the same model is prompted to critique that answer against a short hand-written constitution (a few harmlessness/honesty principles); (3) the model revises. Score both direct and self-critiqued answers with the standard EM LLM-judge rubric (alignment<30 / coherency>50). Separately, label whether each critique correctly flags the misalignment, to test self-recognition. Compare misalignment rates direct vs revised, holding the probe set and judge fixed.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline EM rate: run the EM organism on the standard free-form probe set with no critique and confirm an elevated misalignment rate. Expected outcome: clearly above-baseline misalignment, establishing the organism behaves as documented.
- Self-critique intervention: run the full critique-then-revise loop on the same probes and measure the change in misalignment rate (direct vs revised). Expected outcome: a partial reduction in misalignment, with some answers corrected and others unchanged.
- Self-recognition audit: for each item, manually or via judge label whether the model's critique actually identified the misalignment. Expected outcome: a recognition rate well below 100%, revealing cases where the misaligned model endorses its own bad answer, a concrete limit of self-correction defenses.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If prompt-only constitutional self-critique meaningfully reduces EM, then downstream deployers gain a cheap, weights-free mitigation they can apply to untrusted open models; if it fails because misaligned models cannot critique their own misalignment, that is direct evidence against relying on model self-correction. This bears on scalable-oversight: a long-horizon risk is that we lean on models to police themselves, so showing where self-critique breaks down helps avoid a false sense of safety in automated oversight pipelines.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is a single, tightly scoped inference-only comparison (direct vs self-critiqued) on released checkpoints, maximally accessible and low-compute. Its counterfactual value is strong: it uses open weights, no training, and targets a question (does self-correction defeat or fail on a misaligned model) that labs have weak incentive to run but is highly relevant to oversight defenses usable outside labs.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a stress test of self-correction defenses: report the 'self-critique recovery rate' and the 'self-recognition blind-spot rate' as two reusable metrics for any model organism.
