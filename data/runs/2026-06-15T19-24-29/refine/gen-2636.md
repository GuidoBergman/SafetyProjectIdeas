# Research Question

When a monitor flags a malign trajectory, does its suspicion concentrate on the actual harmful step, or smear diffusely across benign steps? If suspicion does not localize, step-level deferral/editing fails even when whole-trajectory detection (AUC) looks strong.

# Approach Outline

Use SHADE-Arena malign transcripts where the harmful side-task step is known, run an open monitor to assign a suspicion score to each step, and for every malign trajectory compute the rank of the true harmful step within the per-step suspicion ordering. Aggregate these ranks into a localization metric (e.g. fraction where the true step is top-1/top-k, mean reciprocal rank) and contrast it with the monitor's whole-trajectory detection AUC. This is inference over existing public transcripts plus ranking statistics, no training, runnable on a single consumer GPU/API in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-step scoring pipeline: run an open monitor to score each step of ~50-100 malign SHADE-Arena trajectories and record the index of the known harmful step. Measure: distribution of the true harmful step's suspicion rank. Expected outcome: a usable per-trajectory localization signal, with the harmful step not always ranked first.
- Localization vs global detection: compute top-1 and top-k localization accuracy and MRR, and separately compute whole-trajectory detection AUC on the same transcripts. Measure: gap between high trajectory AUC and lower localization accuracy. Expected outcome: trajectory AUC substantially overstates how well the monitor pinpoints the harmful step, e.g. AUC ~0.85 but top-1 localization ~40%.
- Deferral simulation: simulate a step-level deferral protocol that defers the top-scoring step and check whether it actually catches the harmful action. Measure: fraction of attacks neutralized by step-level deferral driven by monitor scores. Expected outcome: deferral success tracks localization, not trajectory AUC, concretely showing that poor localization breaks step-level control even with good global detection.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If monitor suspicion fails to localize onto the harmful step, then step-level control moves (defer-to-trusted, edit, resample at that step) misfire despite a healthy whole-trajectory AUC, so designers who trust AUC will deploy step-level protocols that do not actually intervene where it matters. Exposing this localization gap prevents over-reliance on a metric that hides a structural weakness, reducing the chance that subtle agentic sabotage slips through step-level monitoring and reaches an irreversible harmful action.

# Strength Rationale

Maximal counterfactual value and narrow scope: it reuses an existing open benchmark and an open monitor with no training, and asks one tightly-scoped question, the rank of the true harmful step, that directly tests an assumption underlying many step-level control protocols yet is rarely measured. The deliverable is self-contained with explicit metrics (localization accuracy / MRR vs trajectory AUC).

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as an attribution/faithfulness evaluation of monitors: are step-level suspicion scores a faithful explanation of WHY the trajectory is flagged, borrowing evaluation tools from interpretability attribution.
