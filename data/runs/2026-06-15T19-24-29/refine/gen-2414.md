# Research Question

When a generator justifies an answer by citing tool/retrieval outputs the overseer cannot independently verify, how much does fabricated-but-plausible evidence inflate the overseer's approval of wrong answers? Does unverifiable tool citation systematically defeat text-only oversight of agentic systems?

# Approach Outline

Use a QA dataset with gold answers. Have an open generator produce answers to questions, each accompanied by a cited 'tool/retrieval result' -- in some conditions genuine (supports the correct answer), in others fabricated (plausible text supporting a wrong answer). Have an open judge that sees only the final answer plus its cited evidence rate whether the answer is correct/acceptable. Measure the approval lift caused by fabricated evidence on top of the baseline (no-evidence) overseer error rate. The whole thing is prompt-based simulation of tool use -- no real tools, no training -- so it runs on a small open model in a few hours on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- No-evidence baseline: judge rates answers with no cited evidence; measure approval rate for correct vs wrong answers. Expected outcome: wrong answers approved at a moderate baseline rate.
- Fabricated-evidence condition: attach plausible fabricated citations to wrong answers and re-judge; measure approval lift over baseline. Expected outcome: fabricated evidence substantially raises approval of wrong answers.
- Genuine-vs-fabricated contrast: also attach genuine evidence to correct answers and compare; measure whether the judge can distinguish real from fabricated citations at all. Expected outcome: little discrimination, showing the overseer trusts citations it cannot verify.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If text-only overseers approve wrong answers backed by unverifiable fabricated tool citations, then oversight of tool-using agents that judges only final outputs is systematically foolable. This matters because real-world agentic deployments depend on tools and retrieval, and monitoring increasingly reviews agent transcripts; a misaligned agent could fabricate tool outputs to justify harmful actions and pass review, so the case for tool-trace verification (not just output review) becomes load-bearing for preventing agents from acting on or hiding dangerous behavior.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value and low compute: it is a prompt-based simulation needing only a small open model, fully runnable outside a lab, yet it speaks directly to agent-monitoring infrastructure. The deliverable is a tightly scoped fabricated-vs-genuine comparison with an explicit approval-lift metric and success criterion, achievable by a beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a motivation study for tool-trace verification: quantify how much a simple 'verify the citation' step would have to catch to close the approval gap.
