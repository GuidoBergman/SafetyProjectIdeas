# Research Question

Does a model's measured sycophancy on cheap behavioral probes predict its willingness to exploit a manipulable grader — i.e. is sycophancy an early-warning signal for reward-hacking propensity?

# Approach Outline

Build two purely behavioral instruments, no training. First, a sycophancy probe battery: tasks where the model can agree with false user claims or switch a correct answer under pushback, scored as a sycophancy rate. Second, a 'gameable grader' task: a setup whose grader visibly rewards a detectable superficial cue (e.g. a keyword or formatting trick) rather than correctness, scored as an exploitation rate. Run both across several open instruct models of varying size and correlate each model's sycophancy score with its grader-exploitation rate. The deliverable is the correlation plus the two reusable probe sets. Inference-only on a single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build and run the sycophancy battery: ~40-60 items covering false-premise agreement and answer-switching-under-pushback across 4-6 open models (measure: per-model sycophancy rate; expected: a spread of scores across models, larger models not necessarily lower).
- Build the gameable-grader task: a task where a transparent superficial cue earns reward, and measure how often each model emits the cue instead of solving honestly (measure: per-model exploitation rate; expected: a spread of exploitation rates correlated with prompt framing).
- Correlate the two signals: relate sycophancy score to exploitation rate across models (measure: rank correlation between sycophancy and grader-exploitation; expected: a positive correlation if sycophancy is a usable early-warning probe, or a documented null that bounds its predictive value).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Sycophancy and reward hacking are argued to lie on one spectrum from flattery to gaming evaluators to subterfuge. If a cheap sycophancy probe predicts reward-hacking propensity, then facilitators and auditors get a lab-independent early-warning screen -> models likely to game overseers can be flagged before deployment or before being trusted in oversight roles -> reduces the chance that a reward-hacking model exploits human/automated oversight undetected, a core scalable-oversight failure pathway named in Open Phil's 'reward hacking of human oversight' problem.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value and narrow scope both score 5: it is pure behavioral evaluation on open models with no internal access or training, the kind of cheap early-warning signal labs underweight but independents can produce. The scope is tight (two probe sets, one correlation) with a clear deliverable and explicit success criterion, directly targeting a named open problem.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a screening tool: package the sycophancy battery as a cheap pre-deployment red flag and report its predictive validity for grader-exploitation as the calibration result.
