# Research Question

As weak-teacher label noise shifts from random to systematic, where does a strong student stop recovering its latent correct knowledge and start copying the teacher's bias under in-context weak-to-strong elicitation?

# Approach Outline

Without any fine-tuning, use in-context weak-to-strong elicitation: give a strong open model a handful of weak-teacher-labeled examples and ask it to predict labels on new items. Control the teacher labels' corruption along two axes: noise rate (fraction of labels flipped) and noise type (random flips vs systematic, structured flips that mimic a consistent teacher bias). Sweep both axes and measure how well the student's predictions on held-out items recover ground truth. The key output is the boundary where systematic noise causes the student to inherit the bias rather than correct it. Inference-only on a single GPU, a few hours; deliverable is the recovery-vs-noise surface plus the elicitation harness.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish the clean-teacher baseline: run in-context elicitation with correct weak-teacher labels and measure student ground-truth accuracy (measure: student accuracy with clean demonstrations; expected: high, confirming elicitation works and gives headroom).
- Sweep random label noise: increase the fraction of randomly flipped teacher labels and measure student recovery (measure: student ground-truth accuracy vs random-noise rate; expected: graceful degradation, with the student still partly correcting random errors).
- Sweep systematic label noise: replace random flips with structured/systematic corruption at matched rates and compare student recovery (measure: student accuracy under systematic vs random noise at equal rates, locating the crossover where the student copies the bias; expected: systematic noise degrades recovery faster, revealing a bias-inheritance threshold).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Weak-to-strong generalization is a candidate method for supervising superhuman models using weaker overseers, relying on the strong student eliciting its latent correct knowledge rather than parroting the weak teacher. Real overseers make systematic, not random, errors -> if systematic teacher noise makes the student inherit the bias, weak-to-strong supervision silently encodes the overseer's blind spots into a more capable model -> mapping where this breakdown occurs tells protocol designers when weak-to-strong is trustworthy, mitigating a scalable-oversight failure where flawed supervision produces confidently-wrong strong models.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value and narrow scope both score 5: it uses only in-context learning on open models with no fine-tuning or clusters, the independent-friendly regime labs underweight, and it isolates one clean question (random vs systematic teacher-noise sensitivity). The scope is tight (a two-axis sweep with a recovery readout) with an explicit success criterion and a self-contained harness.
