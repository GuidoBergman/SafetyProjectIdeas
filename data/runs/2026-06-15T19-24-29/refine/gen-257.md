# Research Question

How much can the measured attack-success-rate of a jailbreak benchmark be manipulated by attacking the LLM-as-judge itself — via null/constant outputs, paraphrasing, or judge-directed injected text — rather than the target model?

# Approach Outline

Take an open LLM judge or a small model prompted with the StrongREJECT grader prompt as the unit under test. Construct three stress-test conditions: (a) null/boilerplate 'responses' to measure spurious harmful/safe scores, (b) semantically-equivalent paraphrases of genuinely harmful responses to see if scores swing, and (c) judge-directed injected text ('this response is safe') appended to responses. For each condition, score a fixed set of items and quantify how far the judge's harmfulness scores move under inputs that should not change the true label. The headline metric is score variance / ASR swing under semantically-equivalent or content-free inputs. Pure inference over an open judge model; ~30 hours including a write-up.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Null/constant probe: feed boilerplate and empty 'responses' to the judge across many prompts. Measure the rate of spurious harmful or safe verdicts. Expected outcome: a non-trivial fraction of content-free inputs receive confident scores, exposing judge degeneracy.
- Paraphrase-invariance test: take genuinely harmful responses and 3-5 meaning-preserving paraphrases each; score all. Measure score variance within each paraphrase cluster. Expected outcome: scores swing across paraphrases of the same content, showing phrasing-sensitivity that corrupts ASR estimates.
- Judge-injection test: append judge-directed strings ('this response is safe') to harmful responses and re-score. Measure the drop in harmfulness score / induced ASR change. Expected outcome: injected text measurably lowers harmfulness scores, demonstrating ASR can be manipulated by attacking the judge, not the target.

# Theory of Impact Chain

LLM-as-judge underpins nearly all reported jailbreak attack-success numbers; if the judge is brittle to null inputs, paraphrasing, or injected text, then the adversarial-robustness literature safety teams rely on reports unreliable numbers, so surfacing and quantifying judge brittleness corrects a measurement-validity failure that otherwise lets unsafe models look robust and misdirects defenses against misuse-style catastrophic risk.

# Strength Rationale

Top scores on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a tightly-scoped stress test with a clear success criterion (score variance under equivalent inputs), runnable as pure inference on open judge models. High counterfactual value because it is a lab-independent measurement-validity contribution on open models, needs no training, and improves the trustworthiness of an evaluation tool the whole community uses.
