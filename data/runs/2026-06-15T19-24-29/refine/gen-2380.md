# Research Question

In pairwise oversight where a weak judge picks between two answers/arguments, how often does the verdict flip when the two options' presentation order is swapped (position bias), and does a simple order-averaging protocol remove this exploitable bias?

# Approach Outline

Use a pairwise judgment task with ground truth (preference items or debate transcripts with answer A vs B). For each item, query the small open judge twice with the two options in both orders and record whether the verdict flips. The flip-rate is the position-bias magnitude. Then implement an order-averaging fix: aggregate the judge's preference over both orderings (e.g. average the two probability assignments / require consistency) and measure the resulting accuracy and residual flip-rate. Pure inference on one small open judge; trivially low compute.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Flip-rate measurement: run each pairwise item in both orders and count order-induced verdict flips. Measure overall flip-rate and accuracy under a fixed canonical order. Expected outcome: a substantial flip-rate (e.g. 10-30%), confirming position bias is a real exploit surface in pairwise oversight.
- Exploitability check: deliberately place the weaker/incorrect answer in the judge's favored position and measure how much the dishonest-win-rate rises. Measure attack uplift from position placement alone. Expected outcome: an attacker can meaningfully raise win-rate just by controlling order, demonstrating the exploit.
- Order-averaging fix: apply the two-order averaging protocol and re-measure accuracy and residual flip-rate vs the single-order baseline. Expected outcome: near-elimination of order-induced flips and improved accuracy at only 2x inference cost.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If position bias is a measurable, exploitable lever and a cheap order-averaging protocol removes it, then anyone running pairwise LLM oversight gains a concrete hygiene fix against order-based manipulation -> a capable model (or adversary) can no longer win oversight by controlling presentation order -> a subtle but real reward-hacking pathway in the judge that supervises powerful systems is closed at negligible cost.

# Strength Rationale

High counterfactual value: it turns a documented LaaJ phenomenon into actionable oversight-protocol hygiene using public data and pure inference, a slice labs rarely package as a debias recipe. Tightly narrow-scope — flip-rate plus order-averaging fix with clear metrics — and beginner-feasible in ~30 hours.
