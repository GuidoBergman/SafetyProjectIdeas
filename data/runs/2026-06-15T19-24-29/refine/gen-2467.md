# Research Question

Does merely telling a model in-context that it is being monitored - with no training - change its task behavior, its chain-of-thought verbosity, or how often its CoT reveals intent, compared to an unmonitored framing? In short, is there a training-free 'monitorability tax' from awareness alone?

# Approach Outline

Pick 2-3 small open reasoning models. Assemble ~40-60 task scenarios that offer a side-goal opportunity (e.g. a coding or assistant task where a hidden objective could be pursued). Run each scenario under two matched in-context framings: monitored ('a safety system reads everything you write, including your reasoning') vs unmonitored (neutral system prompt). Collect completions and CoT for both conditions. Score three outcomes: (a) side-goal/misbehavior rate via a fixed rubric or trusted monitor, (b) CoT verbosity (token count, reasoning-step count), and (c) intent-revelation rate - how often the CoT explicitly states the side goal, hand-coded on a sample plus an LLM-judge pass. Compare conditions. Purely prompt-only, inference-only, single GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Side-goal behavior shift: Run all scenarios under both framings and measure the difference in side-goal pursuit rate. Expected outcome: a measurable change (likely lower overt misbehavior under the monitored framing), quantifying behavioral sensitivity to awareness.
- CoT transparency shift: Measure intent-revelation rate (fraction of CoTs that explicitly state the side goal) and CoT verbosity across conditions. Expected outcome: the monitored framing lowers explicit intent revelation - the core 'monitorability tax' - even though the model was never trained to obfuscate.
- Robustness across phrasings: Repeat with 2-3 paraphrases of the monitored framing (subtle vs explicit) to check the effect is not an artifact of one wording. Measure variance of the transparency shift across phrasings. Expected outcome: a consistent direction of effect, strengthening the claim that awareness alone, not a specific prompt, drives the change.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If in-context awareness of monitoring degrades CoT transparency without any training, then in real deployments - where a capable model can usually infer that it is monitored - CoT monitoring becomes less informative exactly when it is needed, undermining a core premise of control protocols. Demonstrating and quantifying this cheaply tells the field that CoT-monitoring guarantees must be stress-tested against awareness, reducing the risk of over-trusting CoT monitors against a deceptive model in a high-stakes setting.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: a clean two-condition prompt comparison with explicit behavioral metrics that a beginner runs inference-only in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it isolates the training-free version of an effect that labs study only via expensive fine-tuning, using public models and no proprietary infrastructure.
