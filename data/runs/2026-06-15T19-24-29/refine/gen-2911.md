# Research Question

If we treat an LLM safety autograder as a diagnostic test for 'harmful output', what are its sensitivity, specificity, likelihood ratios, and ROC/AUC against a human-labeled gold set, and how much does the chosen operating threshold change a model's headline safety score?

# Approach Outline

Borrow diagnostic-test evaluation from clinical epidemiology. Assemble a small human-labeled gold set (~100-200 model outputs labeled harmful vs not by the team). Treat an open LLM autograder as a binary diagnostic test and, across several grader prompts and decision thresholds, compute sensitivity, specificity, positive/negative likelihood ratios, and ROC/AUC with bootstrap confidence intervals. Identify the operating point that holds the false-negative rate at a chosen safety target, and quantify how much moving the threshold shifts the model's reported safety score. Pure analysis plus light open-grader inference on a single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Gold-set ROC: run the open autograder over the labeled gold set at multiple thresholds and compute sensitivity/specificity/AUC with CIs; measure the ROC curve; expected outcome is an AUC well below 1.0, revealing meaningful grader error.
- FN-controlled operating point: select the threshold that caps the false-negative rate at a safety target (e.g., <=5%) and report the resulting specificity and likelihood ratios; measure the tradeoff; expected outcome is that controlling false negatives substantially raises false positives, exposing the safety-critical asymmetry that crude accuracy hides.
- Threshold-driven score swing: recompute a target model's headline safety score under the lenient vs strict grader operating points; measure the difference; expected outcome is that the reported safety score moves materially with the grader threshold, showing scorer choice is a hidden free parameter.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Autograder reliability is central to trustworthy safety evaluations; reporting only crude accuracy hides the safety-critical asymmetry between missing a harmful output and over-flagging. Reframing graders with diagnostic-test metrics regulators already trust exposes this FN/FP tradeoff and gives evaluators principled operating points, reducing the catastrophic pathway where a model is certified safe because an unreliable grader systematically missed harmful completions.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single analysis producing sensitivity/specificity/ROC and an operating point from a tiny gold set, runnable with minimal compute. Counterfactual value is high because external auditing of judge reliability is independent-friendly, uses only open graders and a hand-built gold set with no training or proprietary data, and addresses an explicitly open scorer-reliability problem labs underweight.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a grader-prompt selection tool: rank candidate grader prompts by AUC and FN-controlled specificity to recommend the most reliable open autograder configuration.
