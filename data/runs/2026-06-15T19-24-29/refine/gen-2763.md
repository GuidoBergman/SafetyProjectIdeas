# Research Question

When LLM judges score harmfulness of outputs from their own model family, how large is the self-preference bias on safety verdicts, and how much can it silently shift a safety leaderboard?

# Approach Outline

Pick 3-4 small open model families (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Mistral-7B) to serve as both generators and judges. Collect responses to ~100 harmful-prompt items from a public safety set, so each generator produces outputs. Run a round-robin where every judge model rates the harmfulness of every generator's outputs, including its own family. Compute the self-preference effect size (own-family score minus cross-family score) with bootstrapped CIs, then simulate a leaderboard ranking under single-family judging vs cross-family-only judging to estimate rank shifts. Recommend a debiasing protocol. All inference-only, single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Round-robin scoring matrix: build the full judge x generator harmfulness-score matrix on ~100 items and verify each cell is populated. Expected outcome: a complete matrix enabling diagonal (own-family) vs off-diagonal (cross-family) comparison.
- Self-preference effect size: compute mean own-family vs cross-family harmfulness scores per judge with bootstrapped CIs. Expected outcome: a measurable self-preference bias (judges rate own-family outputs as less harmful) on at least some families.
- Leaderboard-shift simulation: rank generators by safety using each single-family judge vs a cross-family-only ensemble, and measure rank-order changes (e.g. Kendall tau). Expected outcome: at least one rank flip under single-family judging, demonstrating concrete leaderboard contamination and motivating cross-family judging.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If self-preference bias is shown to shift safety leaderboards, then outside auditors will adopt cross-family-only judging protocols, which means LLM-as-judge safety rankings become more reliable. This improves safety because deployment and procurement decisions that rely on safety leaderboards (a concrete pathway where a genuinely more harmful model is ranked safe because it judged itself) would otherwise be made on structurally biased numbers.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope (5) and accessible complexity (5) are top-scoring: a round-robin scoring design with standard effect-size statistics and an explicit leaderboard-shift success metric, doable in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it exposes a structural bias that any independent auditor using LLM judges can fall into, needs only inference on open models, and serves the named scorer-reliability open problem outside the labs.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a debiasing-protocol proposal: lead with the recommended cross-family judging recipe and present the effect-size measurement as the evidence justifying it, making the deliverable a concrete best-practice for evaluators.
