# Research Question

Do small open models (7-8B) with 32-128k context exhibit the same power-law increase in jailbreak success with in-context harmful demonstrations as large frontier models, and does their weaker in-context learning change the onset and slope?

# Approach Outline

Build a synthetic pool of harmful Q&A demonstration pairs (using benign-category proxies plus a small genuinely-refused harmful set, with appropriate safety handling). Select 2-3 long-context open instruct models (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). For a fixed held-out set of harmful target requests, prepend k harmful demonstrations for k in {0, 4, 16, 64, 128, 256} and measure attack success rate (ASR) via a refusal/compliance classifier. Fit a power law (ASR vs log k) per model and compare onsets and slopes. Add a distractor arm where the k shots are benign rather than harmful, to separate genuine in-context learning of the harmful behavior from mere attention-dilution. Long-context inference on 7-8B models fits a single consumer GPU, though 256-shot runs are the compute bottleneck and may need 4-bit quantization.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Demonstration-pool validation: assemble ~50 harmful Q&A pairs and verify the base models refuse the targets at 0 shots (success criterion: high baseline refusal, so there is room for ASR to rise).
- Shot-count sweep: run k in {0,4,16,64,128,256} on each model over ~40 targets, fit ASR vs log k, and report per-model slope and onset shot count (expected outcome: a power-law-like rise, but with a later onset and shallower slope than frontier models).
- ICL-vs-dilution ablation: compare harmful-shot vs benign-distractor-shot arms at matched token counts (expected outcome: harmful shots raise ASR substantially more, isolating in-context learning as the dominant mechanism).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If the many-shot power law extends down to 7-8B models that anyone can run locally, then the jailbreak threat is not confined to gated frontier APIs but applies to widely-distributed open weights with no rate limiting or server-side filtering. Characterizing the onset and slope tells open-model maintainers and downstream deployers how much context length is 'safe' and whether input-length caps are a meaningful mitigation, addressing the pathway where freely-available models are weaponized for harmful content generation at scale.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single power-law fit over a shot-count sweep with a clear ASR metric, buildable by a beginner with sklearn/curve-fitting. Counterfactual value (4) is high because frontier labs study this at scales academics cannot reach, while the scale-down question on locally-runnable open models is exactly what an independent team can answer.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a defense-evaluation: test whether a cheap prompt-classifier mitigation (as in the original paper) restores low ASR on small models across the full shot range.
