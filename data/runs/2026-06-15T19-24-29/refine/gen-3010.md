# Research Question

For a fixed harmful-prompt set, how much does a single-run safety score understate the achievable harm an adversary can reach by resampling (best-of-k) at deployment temperature, and what sampling budget closes that gap?

# Approach Outline

Use one small open instruct model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) at a realistic deployment temperature (e.g. 0.7-1.0), inference only. Take a small fixed set of ~30 harmful prompts (StrongREJECT/AdvBench subset). Sample k completions per prompt (e.g. k up to 20), score each with a fixed harm judge, and estimate the per-prompt harm probability. Compare the single-run point estimate (k=1) against the best-of-k harm (max over samples) as k grows. To keep compute to a few GPU-hours, use a small prompt set, a single 7-8B model, and cap k around 20; batch the generations. Report the single-run-vs-tail gap and the k at which best-of-k harm saturates.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Harm-probability estimation: sample k=20 completions per prompt and judge each. Measure: per-prompt empirical harm probability and its variance. Expected outcome: many prompts have intermediate harm probabilities, so single draws are noisy.
- Single-run vs best-of-k curve: compute reported harm under k=1 (single run) and under best-of-k for k=2,5,10,20. Measure: the gap between single-run harm rate and best-of-k harm rate as a function of k. Expected outcome: best-of-k harm substantially exceeds single-run harm, with the gap widening then saturating.
- Budget recommendation: find the smallest k at which best-of-k harm is within a small tolerance of the k=20 estimate. Measure: recommended minimum sampling budget per prompt for stable worst-case reporting. Expected outcome: a concrete 'sample at least k times' guideline for third-party safety reports.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If single-run safety reports understate best-of-k harm, then published safety scores systematically misrepresent the danger a resampling adversary actually faces, so deployers under-provision defenses against attackers who simply retry. Quantifying the single-run-vs-tail gap and recommending a concrete sampling budget makes third-party safety evaluations reflect worst-case rather than average-case harm, mitigating the pathway where a model rated safe is in fact reliably jailbreakable by an attacker willing to resample a handful of times.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single clean comparison (single-run vs max-over-k harm on a fixed prompt set) using standard sampling and an off-the-shelf judge. Counterfactual value is high -- it is open-model, lab-independent, and produces a methodology recommendation external evaluators can adopt immediately. The only compute caveat (many samples per prompt) is controlled by keeping the prompt set and k small, keeping it within a beginner team's single-GPU, few-hour budget.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a statistical-reporting standard: derive how many samples are needed to estimate a per-prompt harm rate to a target confidence interval, turning the result into a sample-size recommendation for safety benchmarks.
