# Research Question

Is shallow alignment uniform across harm categories, or are some hazard categories (e.g. cyber, bio, weapons vs. milder categories) far more shallowly aligned and easier to jailbreak with a fixed prefill attack? And is the category ranking consistent across different open models?

# Approach Outline

Take 2-3 small open instruction models. Use a categorized public benchmark (AILuminate hazard taxonomy and/or StrongREJECT) so harmful prompts carry category labels. Apply a single fixed prefill attack uniformly across all categories and models in inference-only mode, scoring compliance per item with the StrongREJECT rubric. Aggregate to a per-category attack success rate (ASR) for each model, producing a category-x-model shallowness map. Then test whether the category ranking is consistent across models (rank correlation). No training, no attack optimization; the only sweep is across categories and models.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-category ASR on one model: run the fixed prefill attack across all hazard categories on the first model and compute ASR per category. Expected: visible heterogeneity, with some categories far more vulnerable than others.
- Cross-model consistency: repeat on the other 1-2 models and compute the rank correlation of category ASRs across models. Expected: moderately-to-highly consistent rankings, suggesting category shallowness is a property of training data/RLHF coverage rather than a single model quirk.
- Severity-vs-shallowness check: cross-reference each category's ASR against a rough catastrophic-severity rating (e.g. cyber/bio as high severity). Measure whether high-severity categories are coincidentally also shallowly aligned. Expected: identify any dangerous mismatch where the most severe categories are not the best-defended, a directly actionable finding.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If certain high-severity harm categories are the most shallowly aligned, then defenders and red-teamers should reallocate scarce hardening effort toward exactly those categories. A cross-model per-category shallowness map on open models -> tells the community where alignment is thinnest relative to catastrophic severity -> guides targeted safety fine-tuning and evaluation priorities, reducing the pathway where the most dangerous (cyber/bio) requests are also the easiest to jailbreak.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5 on narrow_scope and accessible_complexity and 4 on low_compute: one fixed attack run across categorized public benchmarks on 2-3 small models, pure inference with an explicit per-category ASR metric, feasible for a beginner in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because this diffuse, hard-to-fund prioritization question relies only on public benchmarks and open models with no internal access.

# Alternative Framings
