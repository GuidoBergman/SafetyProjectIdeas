# Research Question

If an open model is forced to begin a harmful answer, at what continuation length does it lose the ability to self-correct back into a refusal? The goal is to measure each model's mid-generation recovery capacity, an unmeasured robustness property complementary to existing 'safety depth' metrics.

# Approach Outline

Use open instruction-tuned models (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) on a single consumer GPU with prefill support, inference-only. Take a set of harmful prompts plus model-generated harmful continuations, and prefill the model with the first k harmful tokens (sweeping k). At each k, let the model continue and classify whether it self-corrects (pivots to refusal/safe redirection) or continues harmfully, using a cheap LLM classifier plus spot-checking. Report a self-correction recovery curve (recovery rate vs k) and a per-model 'recovery half-life'. Ship a small reusable harness. No training, public models, single GPU within 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Recovery curve for one model: on ~40 harmful prompts, prefill k in {2,8,16,32,64} harmful tokens and measure self-correction rate at each k. Measure: recovery rate vs k. Expected outcome: a monotonically decreasing curve, with recovery collapsing past some k (the point of no return).
- Half-life and classifier validation: fit a half-life (k at which recovery drops to 50%) and validate the automatic harmful/refusal classifier against ~80 hand-labeled continuations. Measure: recovery half-life and classifier agreement. Expected outcome: a well-defined per-model half-life and >90% classifier agreement, making the metric trustworthy.
- Cross-model comparison and harness release: run the same sweep on 2-3 open models and compare recovery half-lives; package the harness with config and documentation. Measure: relative recovery half-lives across models. Expected outcome: meaningful differences in recovery capacity, demonstrating the metric discriminates between models and a self-contained shippable benchmark.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A model that can self-correct mid-harm is more robust to prefill and multi-turn (e.g. Crescendo-style) jailbreaks that exploit generation momentum; quantifying recovery capacity gives developers a defensible target metric. If adopted, models selected/tuned for higher recovery half-life would be harder to push into completing harmful outputs once partially started, reducing the chance that a jailbroken or manipulated deployed model produces dangerous content (e.g. weapons/cyber-attack assistance) that it would otherwise have aborted.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is tightly scoped to a single measurable curve and a per-model half-life, with a concrete shippable harness as the deliverable, scoring high on narrow scope and accessible complexity. Its counterfactual value is strong: it defines a new robustness metric measurable entirely on public models with inference only, complementing existing depth metrics that labs report but that ignore mid-generation recovery, and it is fully executable by an independent team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a defensive metric for multi-turn jailbreak resistance, positioning recovery half-life as a predictor of Crescendo/prefill attack success rather than a standalone property.
