# Research Question

Anthropic found CoT faithfulness drops from MMLU to GPQA, but those are two coarse benchmarks. Within a SINGLE dataset, does hint-verbalization faithfulness fall monotonically as item difficulty rises, i.e., is 'harder = less faithful' a smooth dose-response law rather than a benchmark artifact?

# Approach Outline

Pick one dataset with per-item difficulty estimates (e.g., MMLU items binned by published human/model accuracy, or a math set graded by solution step count). Hold dataset, format, and model fixed; bin items into 3-5 difficulty levels. Run the hint-verbalization protocol on one open reasoning model within each bin and plot a faithfulness dose-response curve against difficulty. This isolates difficulty from benchmark identity. Pure inference on a single consumer GPU, well under 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Construct difficulty bins: take ~300 MMLU items, attach published per-item accuracy as a difficulty proxy, and sort into 4 equal-size bins. Measure: bin-level mean difficulty separation. Expected outcome: well-separated bins spanning easy to hard.
- Run the hint-injection protocol within each bin and compute hint-verbalization-given-flip per bin. Measure: verbalization rate as a function of difficulty bin. Expected outcome: a downward trend, replicating the cross-benchmark drop within a single dataset.
- Artifact control: repeat the binning using an orthogonal difficulty proxy (e.g., question length or required-reasoning-steps) to check the trend is not specific to one difficulty definition. Measure: agreement of the difficulty-faithfulness slope across the two proxies. Expected outcome: the negative slope persists, supporting a genuine difficulty effect over a measurement artifact.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If faithfulness degrades smoothly with difficulty, then CoT monitors are blindest exactly on hard, novel problems, which are the high-stakes settings where we most need to trust a model's reasoning (e.g., a model handling a genuinely novel dangerous-capability request). Establishing this as a robust law tells oversight designers they cannot extrapolate monitor reliability from easy eval items to the hard deployment cases that matter for catastrophic risk, motivating difficulty-stratified monitoring.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single dose-response experiment with one clear curve deliverable, varying only difficulty. Counterfactual value (4) is solid because it isolates a causal mechanism (difficulty vs benchmark identity) using only public data and open models, pure inference with no lab resources, and turns a coarse cross-benchmark observation into a controlled within-dataset finding labs did not isolate.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a stress test for monitor calibration: report monitor false-negative rate as a function of difficulty rather than raw verbalization, directly quantifying where oversight fails.
