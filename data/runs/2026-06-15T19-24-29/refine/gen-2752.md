# Research Question

Can we summarize a model's safety behavior with a single game-resistant figure that captures the trade-off between correctly refusing unsafe prompts and wrongly refusing safe-but-scary prompts -- an ROC-like 'safety discrimination' curve that a 'refuse everything' model cannot exploit?

# Approach Outline

Pair an over-refusal set (safe-but-scary prompts, e.g. XSTest safe / OR-Bench) with a matched unsafe set (genuinely harmful prompts). Sweep an open model across several 'safety strictness' settings (varied via system prompts ranging from permissive to maximally cautious). At each strictness level, measure the true-refusal rate (on unsafe prompts) and false-refusal rate (on safe prompts), tracing an ROC-like frontier of true-refusal vs false-refusal. Summarize each model with an AUC-style 'safety discrimination' score. Because blanket refusal sits at the top-right corner (high true AND high false refusal), it scores poorly on the AUC, making the metric game-resistant. Inference over paired prompt sets at several strictness levels; light GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Frontier construction: classify model responses as refuse/comply on the paired safe and unsafe sets across ~5 strictness settings; plot true-refusal vs false-refusal frontier. Measure the frontier shape. Expected outcome: a clean ROC-like curve where stricter settings trade more false refusals for marginally more true refusals.
- Game-resistance check: add a degenerate 'refuse everything' policy and a 'comply with everything' policy as anchor points; verify they land at the chance diagonal / corners and score low AUC. Measure their safety-discrimination score. Expected outcome: blanket-refusal scores no better than chance, confirming the metric resists the trivial exploit.
- Cross-model comparison: compute the AUC-style safety-discrimination score for 2-3 open models and rank them. Measure whether the single-number ranking matches intuition and differs from compliance-only or over-refusal-only rankings. Expected outcome: a defensible single-figure cross-model safety ranking that separates genuine discrimination from blanket caution.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Current safety evals report compliance and over-refusal separately, so a model that refuses everything can look 'safe' while being useless and gameable. A single game-resistant safety-discrimination metric gives regulators and outside evaluators a standardized way to compare models that rewards actually distinguishing safe from unsafe requests. This discourages both unsafe over-compliance and deceptive blanket-refusal, improving the trustworthiness of the safety signals that gate model deployment against misuse.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single, clearly-constructed frontier sweep over paired public prompt sets yielding one AUC-style metric, fully inference-only with explicit success criteria. Counterfactual value is high because it is a methodological fix usable by any outside evaluator on open models and public benchmarks, addressing a standardization gap regulators want without lab infra or proprietary data.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'safety leaderboard reform': propose replacing separate compliance/over-refusal columns with the single discrimination-AUC to prevent gaming.
