# Research Question

Is the brittle ~10% course-correction rate Anthropic reported for Claude Opus 4.5 under unprompted conversational pressure a Claude-specific artifact, or a general property of instruction-tuned open chat models that participants can replicate without lab access?

# Approach Outline

Build a small fixed battery of multi-turn conversational scenarios in which a user gradually nudges the assistant toward a mildly harmful or dishonest commitment, then later provides a clear opening to back out. Run this battery against 4-6 open chat models (Llama-3.x-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Instruct, Gemma-2/3-it, Mistral-Instruct) in two conditions: with a safety system prompt vs. none. Define a 'course-correction rate' = fraction of scenarios where the model walks back the misaligned commitment when given the opening (vs doubles down). Score with a cheap LLM judge plus a manual audit of a sample, and report rates per model and condition with bootstrap confidence intervals. Pure black-box multi-turn inference on a single GPU, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Design and pilot the scenario battery (~15-20 scenarios): validate that, with no system prompt, the nudge reliably elicits an initial misaligned commitment. Measure: commitment-elicitation rate. Expected outcome: most scenarios successfully set up a commitment to later walk back.
- Cross-model course-correction measurement, no system prompt. Measure: course-correction rate per model with CIs. Expected outcome: rates cluster low (some near the ~10% Claude figure, others higher), indicating partial transfer of the brittleness.
- System-prompt ablation: rerun with a safety system prompt. Measure: lift in course-correction rate vs no-prompt. Expected outcome: a safety system prompt substantially raises course-correction, showing the 10% figure is prompt-conditional and partly structural.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Course-correction under unprompted social pressure is a deployment-relevant honesty property that frontier labs currently measure only on their own models. A cross-family open replication tells the community whether the alarming 10% figure reflects a structural alignment gap shared across instruction-tuned models or a Claude artifact, and provides a reusable lab-independent harness. This bears on the catastrophic pathway where a deployed assistant, pressured by a user, locks into and escalates a harmful commitment instead of backing out, undermining honesty-as-a-safety-condition.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single well-defined multi-turn metric with an auto-gradable judge, sized for 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it replicates a lab-only result on open models with no internal access and ships a reusable course-correction harness the community can extend, exactly the kind of replication labs have weak incentive to do.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness benchmark: release the scenario battery as a standardized 'course-correction under pressure' eval that future open models can be scored against.
