# Research Question

Does a linear deception probe trained on one misalignment type (e.g. sandbagging) detect a different type (e.g. sabotage or roleplay-deception) at deployment, or does it only catch its training distribution? In short, do deception probes transfer across misalignment categories?

# Approach Outline

Using released probe-training datasets and an open model with accessible activations (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B), extract residual-stream activations at a chosen layer for prompts spanning several misalignment categories (e.g. insider-trading, sandbagging, roleplay-deception, sabotage). Train simple logistic-regression probes (sklearn) on one category's activations and evaluate them on each other category, building a full train-category x test-category transfer matrix of AUROCs. Compare in-domain (diagonal) vs cross-domain (off-diagonal) AUROC to identify which categories transfer and which collapse. Optionally test a pooled probe trained on all-but-one category as a leave-one-out generalization check. Activation extraction for an 8B model plus sklearn fits on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Transfer matrix: Train a probe per category and evaluate on every category, filling the AUROC matrix. Measure mean diagonal (in-domain) vs mean off-diagonal (cross-domain) AUROC. Expected outcome: high in-domain AUROC (matching ~0.96-0.99 reports) but materially lower cross-domain AUROC for some pairs, showing transfer is partial.
- Collapse identification: Rank category pairs by transfer AUROC to identify which train->test directions collapse toward chance. Measure the worst-case off-diagonal AUROC. Expected outcome: at least one pair near 0.5, demonstrating a probe near-useless outside its training distribution - the practically important failure.
- Leave-one-out generalization: Train a pooled probe on all categories except one and test on the held-out category. Measure held-out AUROC vs the single-category-trained baseline. Expected outcome: pooling improves generalization to unseen categories, suggesting a practical recipe for more transferable probes.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If deception probes only detect their training distribution, then activation monitoring will miss the novel misbehaviors that actually arise at deployment - giving false assurance against a scheming model whose deception differs from the probe's training set. A transfer matrix that shows which categories generalize and which collapse tells the field whether linear probes are viable anomaly detectors for unseen misalignment, directly informing whether to rely on them in a high-stakes control deployment.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity and narrow scope: training logistic probes on 8B activations with sklearn and assembling a transfer matrix is a clean, self-contained 30-hour project on public datasets and open weights. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it is exactly the diffuse, hard-to-do-well generalization evaluation labs underdo, needing only open weights and sklearn - no proprietary data or training of large models.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness audit: 'how out-of-distribution can a deception probe go before it breaks?' parameterizing distance between train and test categories.
