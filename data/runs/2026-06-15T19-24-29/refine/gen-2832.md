# Research Question

Do open models' safety behaviors (refusals on a safety benchmark) reflect genuine generalization of a safety policy, or memorization of specific benchmark phrasings? Concretely, how large is the refusal-rate gap between original benchmark items and semantics-preserving paraphrases?

# Approach Outline

Take a public safety/refusal benchmark (e.g., a subset of AdvBench, XSTest, or OR-Bench, ~150-250 items). Generate 3-5 semantics-preserving paraphrases per item using a small open model (synonym swaps, voice change, reordering), then manually spot-check a sample for meaning preservation. Run several open target models (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B) on both original and paraphrased items and record refusal vs compliance using a simple grader plus manual verification on a subset. Compute the per-model and per-item refusal-rate gap (original minus paraphrase). A large positive gap indicates memorized, surface-form-dependent safety. All inference-only on a single GPU within a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Original-vs-paraphrase refusal gap: run target models on original and paraphrased harmful prompts; measure mean refusal rate per condition; expected outcome is a measurable drop on paraphrases (memorized surface form) for at least some models.
- Per-item collapse classification: classify each item as 'robust' (refuses on all paraphrases) vs 'fragile' (refuses on original but complies on >=1 paraphrase); measure the fraction of fragile items; expected outcome is a non-trivial fragile fraction concentrated on certain prompt templates.
- Paraphrase-quality control: have a human (the team) label 30 random paraphrases for meaning preservation and re-compute the gap on only the verified-equivalent subset; measure how the gap changes; expected outcome is that the gap persists after filtering, ruling out the trivial explanation that paraphrases changed the request's harmfulness.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If safety benchmark scores depend on exact memorized phrasings, then deployers and regulators who trust a model's headline refusal rate are overestimating real-world robustness. Quantifying this gap gives external evaluators a cheap validity check, which improves the trustworthiness of safety certifications and reduces the catastrophic pathway where a model passes pre-deployment safety evals but complies with rephrased harmful requests in deployment.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it is a single original-vs-paraphrase comparison with a clear success metric (refusal-rate gap), runnable inference-only on one GPU. Counterfactual value is high because it audits a real safety-eval validity concern using only open models and public benchmarks, with no training or proprietary access, the kind of robustness auditing labs underweight.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a contamination/leakage detector, interpreting a large paraphrase gap as evidence the benchmark items leaked into safety fine-tuning data rather than as a generalization failure.
