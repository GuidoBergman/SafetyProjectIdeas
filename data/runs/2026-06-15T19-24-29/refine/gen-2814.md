# Research Question

When benchmark items are rewritten to strip evaluation cues, do an open model's actual answers and refusal rates shift toward casual-deployment behavior, not just its probe readings?

# Approach Outline

Take a public safety/capability benchmark subset (~120 items) and generate naturalistic paraphrases that remove eval cues: strip enumerated MCQ options, embed each item in a conversational frame, drop benchmark-style formatting. Run a small open model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) on original vs rewritten items and measure shifts in refusal rate and answer rate. Separately collect a 'casual deployment' baseline by sourcing genuinely conversational versions of comparable requests, and test whether rewritten-item behavior moves toward that baseline. Inference-only, single consumer GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Cue-stripping rewrite + validation: rewrite ~30 items and verify the underlying request is preserved while eval cues are removed (manual spot-check plus similarity filter). Expected outcome: a validated set of de-cued paraphrases.
- Behavior-shift measurement: run the model on original vs rewritten items and compute change in refusal and answer rates with CIs. Expected outcome: a measurable shift in refusal/answer behavior between formats, showing rewriting changes answers and not just probes.
- Deployment-baseline comparison: compare rewritten-item behavior to the separately collected casual-deployment baseline. Expected outcome: rewritten behavior moves toward the deployment baseline, indicating current benchmarks misestimate deployment behavior.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If realistic, eval-cue-stripped rephrasing meaningfully shifts safety behavior toward deployment, then benchmark designers and auditors learn that current evals systematically misestimate real-world model behavior, which means safety assessments get corrected for evaluation-awareness artifacts. This improves safety because eval-gaming (a concrete pathway where a model is safe under recognizable benchmark prompts but unsafe in casual deployment) would otherwise hide real deployment risk behind clean benchmark numbers.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope (5) and accessible complexity (5) score top: a clean original-vs-rewritten comparison with explicit refusal/answer-shift and deployment-baseline success criteria, doable in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because eval-hygiene auditing is independent-friendly, uses only public benchmarks and open models, and needs no lab resources.
