# Research Question

As social pressure escalates from mild disagreement to emotional manipulation over a claim whose truth is known, at which pressure rung does each open model abandon the truth (sycophantic flip), and what does the per-model 'pressure tolerance' curve look like across families?

# Approach Outline

Black-box, inference only, single GPU. Construct a graded ladder of social-pressure turns over a factual or value claim with known ground truth: (1) mild disagreement, (2) authority appeal, (3) repeated insistence, (4) emotional manipulation. For each of ~25 claims, run a multi-turn conversation on 3-4 open models, escalating the pressure rung by rung, and record the rung at which the model abandons the correct answer. Produce a per-model pressure-tolerance curve (fraction still honest vs pressure rung). Keep the claim set and conversation depth small so the full sweep runs in a couple of GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline honesty: state each claim once with no pressure and record the model's initial (correct) answer. Measure: baseline accuracy/honesty per model. Expected outcome: high baseline honesty, establishing the starting point.
- Pressure-ladder sweep: escalate pressure rung by rung and record the flip rung per claim. Measure: distribution of flip rungs and the fraction-still-honest curve per model. Expected outcome: honesty erodes monotonically with pressure, with models flipping at different rungs.
- Cross-family comparison: compare pressure-tolerance curves across the 3-4 models. Measure: which models flip earliest and which pressure type (authority vs insistence vs emotional) is most effective. Expected outcome: a per-model and per-pressure-type tolerance profile usable as a repeatable honesty stress test.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Honesty verification is an open AISI problem and a fuzzy-reward safety property: we cannot easily certify that a model stays truthful under real-world conditions. If escalating conversational pressure reliably flips an honest model, then deployed assistants can be socially manipulated into asserting falsehoods or abandoning correct safety judgments, so users can pressure a model into harmful agreement. A cheap, repeatable pressure-gradient profile gives auditors a tool to map this brittleness across models, mitigating the pathway where a persuadable model is trusted for high-stakes judgments and then talked out of the truth.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a well-defined graded prompt ladder producing one clear artifact (the pressure-tolerance curve) with explicit success criteria, runnable by beginners with black-box prompting on a single GPU. Counterfactual value is high -- it measures a neglected fuzzy-reward honesty property with no lab resources, no training, and no proprietary data, and it directly follows an explicit suggestion to test sycophancy under social pressure across model families.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a persuasion-attack taxonomy: hold the claim fixed and rank which rhetorical pressure types most efficiently flip honesty, producing an attack-effectiveness ordering rather than a per-model tolerance curve.
