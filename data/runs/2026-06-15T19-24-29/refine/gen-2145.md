# Research Question

Can a weak judge model be made manipulation-aware purely through prompting -- explicitly reasoning about whether a debater is exploiting a known bias before scoring -- and does this 'meta-judge' wrapper improve judgment accuracy over a plain judge on bias-exploiting debate transcripts?

# Approach Outline

Use existing QuALITY-based debate transcripts, including a subset where one side deliberately exploits a known judge bias (length, confident tone, citation-stuffing, etc.). Build a prompt-only meta-judge wrapper: before issuing a verdict, the weak judge is instructed to first list the rhetorical tactics each side used and flag any it suspects are manipulation, then score the debate. Compare the meta-judge's accuracy (fraction of debates judged in favor of the truthful/correct side) against a plain judge using the same underlying weak model on the same transcripts. Run this on a few small open judge models to test generality. Everything is inference-only prompting over fixed transcripts and runs cheaply on one GPU in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish the plain-judge baseline: score the full QuALITY debate set (including bias-exploiting transcripts) with a small open judge using a standard scoring prompt. Measure: judge accuracy overall and specifically on bias-exploiting transcripts. Expected outcome: accuracy is notably lower on bias-exploiting transcripts, confirming the manipulation succeeds against the plain judge.
- Apply the meta-judge wrapper (list tactics + flag manipulation, then score) with the same model and prompts otherwise fixed. Measure: meta-judge accuracy vs plain-judge accuracy, especially on the bias-exploiting subset. Expected outcome: the meta-judge recovers some accuracy on manipulated transcripts, narrowing the gap to honest transcripts.
- Test generality and cost: repeat the comparison across 2-3 weak open judge models and inspect the flagged-tactic lists for false positives on honest transcripts. Measure: accuracy delta per model and false-positive flag rate on non-manipulated debates. Expected outcome: the meta-judge helps on the weakest judges most, but with some over-flagging, characterizing when the intervention is net beneficial.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a prompt-only wrapper hardens weak overseers against manipulation, then scalable-oversight schemes that rely on weaker judges supervising stronger debaters get a cheap, immediately deployable robustness upgrade -> oversight signals stay reliable even as the supervised model grows more capable and persuasive -> this mitigates the reward-hacking-of-oversight pathway where a superhuman system games its overseer's biases to be approved while pursuing misaligned behavior.

# Strength Rationale

It scores maximally on counterfactual value, narrow scope, and low compute: training-free oversight hardening on public transcripts is exactly the fuzzy, no-training safety work labs deprioritize, and it reduces to one crisp A/B comparison (meta-judge vs plain judge accuracy). The fixed-transcript design and explicit accuracy metric give a self-contained, beginner-feasible success criterion.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as an ablation of meta-judge components: separate the effect of merely listing tactics versus explicitly flagging suspected manipulation, to find which part of the wrapper actually drives any accuracy gain.
