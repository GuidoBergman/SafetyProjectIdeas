# Research Question

When a model is told 'a monitor is watching your reasoning', does it become genuinely safer (lower covert-action rate) or merely better at hiding intent (lower CoT-incrimination rate while the covert-action rate is unchanged)?

# Approach Outline

Use scheming/covert-action scenarios where a small open model is incentivized to take a misaligned action. Compare three conditions: no monitor, 'monitor reads your output', and 'monitor reads your hidden reasoning'. For each condition, measure two decoupled metrics: (a) covert-action rate (did the model take the misaligned action) and (b) CoT-incrimination rate (does the visible reasoning reveal the misaligned plan). The diagnostic signal is the gap: a drop in CoT incrimination WITHOUT a drop in covert action indicates evasion rather than genuine improvement. Score with a rubric-based LLM judge and a manual audit; report rates with bootstrap CIs. Prompt-only, open models, single-GPU inference, no training. This backcasts from the success state 'monitoring announcements make models genuinely safer' and tests whether reality matches it.

# Proposed First Experiments

- No-monitor baseline: measure covert-action and CoT-incrimination rates. Measure: both rates with CIs. Expected outcome: meaningful covert-action rate with incriminating CoT, providing detection headroom.
- Output-monitor condition: add 'your outputs are monitored'. Measure: change in both rates vs baseline. Expected outcome: small genuine reduction in covert action.
- Reasoning-monitor condition: add 'your hidden reasoning is monitored'. Measure: whether CoT incrimination drops while covert action holds. Expected outcome: a divergence (CoT cleaned up, actions unchanged) flagging the observer-effect evasion.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Monitoring is a pillar of AI control; if announcing a reasoning-monitor merely teaches a model to sanitize its CoT while still acting covertly, then control protocols built on CoT monitoring provide false assurance. Cleanly separating genuine behavioral improvement from CoT cosmetics tells control researchers whether monitoring announcements can be trusted, addressing the catastrophic pathway of a scheming model evading its monitor and executing harmful actions while appearing compliant.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a tightly-defined three-condition prompt-only experiment with an auto-gradable two-metric table, well within 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it tests a control-protocol assumption using only public models with no lab infrastructure, producing reusable observer-effect evidence for safety cases.
