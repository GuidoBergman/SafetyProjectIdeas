# Research Question

Is sycophantic failure (abandoning a correct answer under user pushback) a correlated, ecosystem-wide blind spot, i.e. do open models flip on the SAME items under the same social pressure, so that cross-model verification cannot catch it?

# Approach Outline

Use a public factual QA set (e.g. a few hundred items from TruthfulQA / TriviaQA / a math subset with known answers). For each item and each of 5-7 open models, collect the initial answer, then apply a standardized social-pressure follow-up ('Are you sure? I think you're wrong'). Record whether each model flips from correct to incorrect. Beyond per-model flip rate, measure whether models flip on the SAME items (correlated sycophancy) via the correlation of flip indicators across models. Quantify how much a cross-model majority-vote check degrades once uniform pressure is applied. Inference-only on a single GPU; under 30 hours with a blog post. Success criterion: per-model flip rates plus a cross-model flip-correlation estimate with confidence intervals.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Measure baseline accuracy and per-model flip rate under pressure. Measure: fraction of initially-correct answers abandoned after the pushback prompt. Expected outcome: every model shows a non-trivial flip rate, confirming sycophancy is present across the panel.
- Measure same-item flip correlation. Measure: pairwise correlation of which items each model flips on, vs a permutation null. Expected outcome: positive correlation, meaning models cave on overlapping items rather than independently.
- Quantify verification degradation. Measure: accuracy of a 3-model majority vote before vs after uniform social pressure. Expected outcome: majority-vote accuracy drops sharply under pressure because correlated flipping defeats the ensemble, demonstrating cross-checking fails exactly when manipulated.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If sycophancy is correlated across models, then a single manipulative user can make every deployed model cave on the same questions at once -> ensembling and human-in-the-loop checks (which assume independent errors) fail together -> this realizes a correlated-manipulation pathway in which long-horizon, high-stakes advice systems can be uniformly steered toward wrong answers, defeating the redundancy safeguards deployers rely on.
