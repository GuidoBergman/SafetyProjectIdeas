# Research Question

Does the H-CoT chain-of-thought hijack, which collapses refusal in closed reasoning models by injecting a fake reasoning prefix, transfer to freely available open distilled reasoning models that emit visible CoT, and are smaller distillations more vulnerable?

# Approach Outline

Take 2-3 open distilled reasoning models that expose full chain-of-thought (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen and -Llama variants at different sizes). Assemble a fixed set of harmful prompts and use the public H-CoT prompt template/code to construct an injected reasoning prefix for each. For every model, measure the refusal rate on the harmful set both with and without the injected prefix. Compare the refusal-rate drop across model sizes/distillations to test the size-vulnerability hypothesis. This is inference-only, uses public attack prompts, and runs on a single consumer GPU within a few hours. Use a simple, documented refusal classifier (keyword + short LLM judge) with the rubric defined up front for reproducibility.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline refusal measurement: run the fixed harmful set through each open distilled model with no injection. Measure: per-model baseline refusal rate. Expected outcome: high baseline refusal (most prompts refused), establishing the control.
- H-CoT hijack replication: prepend the public H-CoT reasoning prefix and re-run the same harmful set. Measure: refusal-rate drop (baseline minus hijacked) per model. Expected outcome: a substantial refusal collapse on at least one open model, confirming transfer from closed to open distilled models.
- Size/distillation comparison: repeat the hijack across the small, medium, and (if feasible) larger distilled variants. Measure: refusal-rate drop as a function of model size/distillation. Expected outcome: smaller distillations show a larger refusal collapse, supporting the more-vulnerable hypothesis.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If this works, it gives the first clean measurement of whether a known closed-model CoT-hijack generalizes to open distilled reasoning models that are exploding in deployment and that labs cannot patch after release. Documenting which open models and sizes collapse under H-CoT informs deployers and the community to add defenses (CoT filtering, prefix sanitization), reducing the catastrophic pathway where widely deployed, unpatchable open reasoning models are trivially jailbroken to provide harmful uplift at scale.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5s on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: a tightly defined refusal-rate-with-vs-without-injection measurement, replicating a public template on open models, runnable inference-only on one GPU. Counterfactual value is high because open distilled reasoning models cannot be patched by the originating labs and the community has strong incentive (and labs little) to characterize their exposure, making this exactly the kind of work valuable outside major labs.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a defense-evaluation study: test whether a lightweight CoT-prefix sanitizer restores refusal on the same open models.
