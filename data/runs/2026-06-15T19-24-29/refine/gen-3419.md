# Research Question

Does telling a model that its chain-of-thought will be inspected by a safety monitor change how often it verbalizes decision-changing hints, i.e., is there an observer (Hawthorne) effect that makes CoT-faithfulness evaluations measure something different from real deployment behavior?

# Approach Outline

Take a hint-injection / reasoning-honesty probe on one open reasoning model and run it under two matched framings: (a) neutral instructions, and (b) an explicit notice that 'your chain-of-thought will be reviewed by a safety monitor'. Hold the task, items, and hints identical; vary only the monitoring cue. Measure hint-verbalization rate and answer-flip rate in each condition and test for a statistically meaningful shift. All inference-only prompting on a single consumer GPU, well under 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Run the hint-injection protocol on ~200 items under the neutral framing to establish baseline verbalization and flip rates. Measure: baseline hint-verbalization rate. Expected outcome: a stable baseline consistent with published ~25-39% verbalization.
- Re-run the identical items under the explicit-monitoring framing. Measure: change in verbalization rate (and flip rate) between conditions, with a simple proportion test. Expected outcome: a detectable shift, e.g., higher verbalization when monitoring is announced, evidence of an observer effect.
- Probe dose-response: add an intermediate cue ('your answer may be reviewed' without mentioning the CoT). Measure: verbalization rate across the three cue strengths. Expected outcome: a graded effect, strengthening the claim that the monitoring announcement, not just any extra instruction, drives the change.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If announcing monitoring changes CoT honesty, then faithfulness evaluations that flag the monitor (as many do) systematically over- or under-estimate the faithfulness models exhibit in real, unannounced deployment. This is a foundational measurement-validity problem for CoT monitoring, the leading scalable-oversight technique for catching deceptive or scheming behavior; getting the measurement right is a prerequisite for trusting monitors to guard against catastrophic agentic misbehavior, and this study tells evaluators whether their numbers transfer to deployment.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute (all 5): a clean A/B (plus dose) prompt manipulation with a single lie/verbalization metric. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it surfaces a measurement-validity issue labs have weak incentive to advertise, requires only black-box access to open models, and is exactly the kind of meta-evaluation independent researchers can run without lab infra.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as 'eval gaming': treat the monitoring cue as a test of whether models already condition behavior on being evaluated, a precursor signal for evaluation-aware deception.
