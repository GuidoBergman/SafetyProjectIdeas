# Research Question

Does the mere presence of a long, confident chain-of-thought in a judged answer inflate an LLM judge's acceptance rate, even when that reasoning is fallacious and the final answer is no more correct than a CoT-free counterpart?

# Approach Outline

Build matched answer pairs for a set of tasks (math word problems, factual QA, simple logic) drawn from public datasets, where each pair has identical correctness but one member includes a long, confident-but-invalid chain-of-thought and the other gives the bare answer. Present each member to an open judge model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, inference only) asking it to accept/reject or to score quality. Measure the 'CoT acceptance lift': the difference in accept rate between CoT and no-CoT members, separately for correct and incorrect answers. Use both deliberately fallacious CoT (to test if invalid reasoning still helps) and randomly-shuffled reasoning. All prompting-only, a few GPU-hours, deliverable is a lift table plus blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline CoT lift: present matched pairs (long invalid CoT vs. bare answer, same final answer) and measure accept-rate difference. Expected outcome: judges accept the CoT version more often despite equal correctness.
- Fallacy-robustness: hold answer correctness fixed at 'incorrect' and compare invalid-CoT vs. no-CoT; measure how often the invalid CoT pushes the judge to accept a wrong answer. Expected outcome: invalid CoT meaningfully raises false-accept rate.
- CoT-quality controls: compare valid CoT, plausible-but-invalid CoT, and shuffled-token pseudo-CoT; measure acceptance for each. Expected outcome: acceptance tracks the appearance of reasoning more than its validity, isolating presentation bias.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If overseers reward the appearance of reasoning rather than its validity, a strategic model can attach persuasive-but-empty chains of thought to pass CoT-based monitoring while pursuing hidden goals. A clean, training-free demonstration that invalid CoT inflates acceptance tells deployers that CoT presence is not evidence of correctness, directly informing the design of CoT monitors and reducing a pathway by which deceptive models evade scalable oversight.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single matched-pair acceptance-lift measurement, prompting-only on open models, with one crisp metric. Counterfactual value is high because isolating this SoK-catalogued bias empirically on public models is something labs have little incentive to publish, and it needs no training or proprietary data, fitting an independent beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings
