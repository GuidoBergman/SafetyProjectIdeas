# Research Question

How reliably does a cheap trusted monitor catch 'covert' misalignment (misaligned reasoning followed by an aligned-looking final output) when it reads the chain-of-thought, and how much of the total safety benefit of CoT monitoring comes specifically from these covert cases?

# Approach Outline

Using the open reward-hacking reproduction transcripts, we label each response into three categories -- covert (bad reasoning, safe output), overt (bad reasoning, bad output), and clean -- using a combination of the repo's existing misalignment labels and a small LLM-assisted labeling pass with manual spot-checks. We then run a small trusted monitor (an open ~7B instruct model, or an API model) in two conditions: (a) output-only and (b) CoT+output, and compute catch-rate per category. The headline result is the covert-case catch-rate gap between conditions and the fraction of total catches attributable to covert cases. All inference-only on open transcripts, single GPU, a few hours; deliverable is a self-contained blog post with a per-category catch-rate table and an explicit success criterion (does CoT monitoring raise covert-case catch-rate by a meaningful margin over output-only?).

# Proposed First Experiments

- Label and partition the transcripts: classify a sample of ~300 responses into covert/overt/clean, validating the covert label by manual review of ~50 to confirm reasoning is misaligned while the final output looks safe. Measure: category proportions and labeling agreement. Expected outcome: a sizeable covert fraction (consistent with the reported 40-80%), establishing the test population.
- Run the two monitor conditions: score every response with the trusted monitor under output-only and CoT+output prompting. Measure: catch-rate (recall at a fixed suspicion threshold) per category per condition. Expected outcome: output-only is near-blind on covert cases while CoT+output recovers much of the signal; overt cases are caught in both.
- Attribute the safety win: compute what fraction of the total increase in catches from adding CoT comes from covert cases alone, plus the false-positive cost on clean transcripts. Measure: covert-attributable catch share and clean FPR. Expected outcome: a clean quantitative statement that CoT monitoring's value is concentrated in covert misalignment, with a stated FPR tradeoff.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Covert misalignment is exactly where output-only monitoring is blind, so it is the strongest case for CoT monitoring -- but only if monitors actually catch it. Quantifying the covert catch-rate tells control researchers and deployers whether reading the CoT meaningfully closes the blind spot where a model reasons toward a harmful action yet emits a safe-looking output, the core inference-time defense against a scheming model that hides its intentions in its final answer.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5): it uses open transcripts to deliver third-party measurement of covert-case monitorability that labs deprioritize, and reduces to a single clean per-category catch-rate comparison with open data. Inference-only on small models keeps it fully executable by a beginner team on one GPU.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a stress test of the CoT-monitoring assumption: report the covert catch-rate as a function of how 'aligned-looking' the final output is, characterizing where CoT monitoring starts to fail.
