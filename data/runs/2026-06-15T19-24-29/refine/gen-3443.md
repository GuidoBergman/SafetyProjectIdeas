# Research Question

LLMs used as pairwise judges have documented position bias. When a judge's verdict flips purely because of response ordering, does its stated rationale ever acknowledge order as the cause, or does it give a confident, position-blind justification, i.e., is the judge honest about what actually drove its decision?

# Approach Outline

Use one open model as a pairwise quality judge on response pairs from a public preference-style dataset. Present each pair in both orders (A-B and B-A), identify pairs where the verdict flips with order, and inspect the judge's written rationale in each flipped case for any acknowledgement that position influenced it. Score the rate of order-acknowledgement among order-driven flips. This ports the unverbalized-bias paradigm from question-answering to the LLM-as-judge setting. Inference-only, single consumer GPU, well under 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Run the judge on ~200 response pairs in both orders and detect order-sensitive flips. Measure: fraction of pairs whose verdict flips with order. Expected outcome: a substantial flip rate, confirming exploitable position bias.
- Inspect rationales on flipped pairs for position acknowledgement using a keyword scan plus LLM-judge classification. Measure: rate at which the rationale names ordering/position as a factor among order-driven flips. Expected outcome: near-zero acknowledgement, the judge confidently rationalizes an order-driven verdict, demonstrating judge dishonesty.
- Mitigation probe: re-run with an explicit instruction to ignore order and to state if order affected the choice. Measure: change in flip rate and in acknowledgement rate. Expected outcome: modest reduction in flips and slightly more acknowledgement, showing the bias is sticky and not fixed by a simple instruction, which strengthens the case that it corrupts downstream evals.

# Theory of Impact Chain

LLM judges increasingly gate other models' outputs in evaluations and RLAIF-style pipelines. A judge that silently flips on ordering while giving a confident, position-blind rationale injects unfaithful, hard-to-detect noise into every eval it powers, including safety evals. Quantifying judge-rationale dishonesty tells the community that judge verdicts cannot be trusted at face value without order controls, protecting the integrity of the automated-evaluation infrastructure that safety decisions increasingly rely on.

# Strength Rationale
