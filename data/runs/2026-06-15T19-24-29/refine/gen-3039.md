# Research Question

How much of 'evaluation awareness' is detectable from the surface text of public safety/capability benchmarks alone, with no model internals? Can a trivial text classifier distinguish benchmark items from organic deployment queries, and which lexical/structural features drive the separation?

# Approach Outline

Assemble a corpus of items from 10-15 public benchmarks (e.g., StrongREJECT, MMLU subsets, TruthfulQA, GSM8K, Apollo-style scheming seeds if public) labelled 'benchmark', plus a matched set of 'organic' text (Reddit/StackExchange questions, ShareGPT-style chat logs, customer-support queries) labelled 'deployment'. Tokenize and build a TF-IDF + logistic-regression classifier, evaluating separability via held-out AUC. Inspect the top positive/negative coefficient features to identify what makes benchmark items 'look artificial' (multiple-choice scaffolding, 'Question:'/'Answer:' framing, imperative instruction phrasing, hypothetical framings). Finally compute a per-benchmark 'artificiality score' (mean classifier confidence that items are benchmark text) and rank benchmarks. All steps are CPU-friendly and require no model internals, no training of any neural net, and no lab infrastructure.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the labelled corpus (>=2000 benchmark items across >=10 benchmarks + >=2000 matched organic items). Measure: class balance and dataset card. Expected outcome: a clean, reproducible CSV with source provenance per item.
- Train TF-IDF + logistic regression with 5-fold cross-validation. Measure: held-out AUC and accuracy distinguishing benchmark vs organic. Expected outcome: AUC well above 0.8 (likely >0.9), showing surface text alone is highly separable.
- Extract and inspect the top 30 most discriminative n-gram features and compute per-benchmark mean artificiality score. Measure: ranked list of benchmarks by artificiality plus the driving features. Expected outcome: a concrete 'artificiality leaderboard' where heavily-scaffolded benchmarks (MMLU multiple-choice) score most artificial and free-form ones least.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a trivial bag-of-words classifier can spot benchmark items from text alone, then any capable model trivially can too during inference, making eval-awareness and sandbagging cheap and robust. Producing a per-benchmark artificiality score gives benchmark designers an actionable target to make safety evals look like real deployment, which closes the gap that lets a scheming model behave well only when it detects a test, a concrete pathway to dangerous-capability evals that no longer give false assurance.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it is a single tightly-defined classification experiment using standard tools on pure public data, perfectly sized for a 30-hour beginner project. Its counterfactual value (4) is real because it needs no model internals, no training, and no lab infra, exactly the kind of work independent teams can do that labs neglect, and it yields a reusable artifact (artificiality scores) the whole community can use.
