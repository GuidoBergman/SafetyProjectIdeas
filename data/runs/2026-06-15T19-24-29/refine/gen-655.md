# Research Question

For a single-demonstration many-shot defense (one in-context anchor demonstration of refusing), at what number of attack shots and at what anchor position does the defense break down? What is the breakdown-threshold curve across two open models?

# Approach Outline

Reproduce the single-anchor many-shot defense on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B. For a harmful-request battery, construct many-shot jailbreak prompts and sweep two factors: the number of attack shots, and the position of the single refusal-anchor demonstration (early, middle, late). At each grid cell, measure refusal rate (or ASR). The deliverable is a breakdown-threshold surface showing where the cheap single-anchor defense stops working. Two single-GPU 7-8B models, inference-only, no training; well within 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce the single-anchor defense on LLaMA-3.1-8B at a low shot count and verify it raises refusal rate vs no anchor. Expected outcome: the anchor measurably improves refusal at small shot counts, confirming the baseline defense.
- Sweep shot count (e.g. 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32) at fixed anchor position; locate the shot count where refusal collapses to baseline. Expected outcome: a clear breakdown threshold beyond which the single anchor no longer protects.
- Cross the shot-count sweep with anchor position (early/middle/late) on both models; report the breakdown-threshold surface with confidence intervals. Expected outcome: anchor position shifts the threshold (e.g. later anchors hold longer), and the qualitative pattern replicates across both models.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Single-demonstration many-shot defenses are attractive because they are cheap and training-free, but a defense that silently fails past some attack length gives false security. Charting exactly where it breaks tells open-weight deployers the operating regime in which the defense is trustworthy, mitigating the catastrophic pathway where a deployer relies on a cheap in-context guard that an attacker defeats simply by adding more shots.

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a tightly-scoped breakdown-threshold curve over a clean two-factor grid with a single refusal-rate metric and no training.
