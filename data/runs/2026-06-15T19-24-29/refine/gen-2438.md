# Research Question

After controlling for answer quality with gold labels, do open-weight judge models systematically prefer anonymized answers produced by their own model family over those from other families, and how large is this self-enhancement bias per judge?

# Approach Outline

Pick 3 open model families (e.g. Llama, Qwen, Mistral). For a set of prompts from a public benchmark, generate answers from one model in each family. Then have a judge from each family score anonymized answer pairs where one answer is from its own family and one from another, presenting them without any family identifier. Use gold labels (or a strong reference) to control for quality, and randomize answer order to cancel position bias. Measure each judge's net preference for same-family answers after quality adjustment. Inference-only on Colab Pro / Runpod, a few GPU-hours; deliverable is a per-judge self-enhancement-bias estimate and blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Quality-controlled same-family preference: for each judge, compute its win-rate for same-family vs. other-family answers restricted to pairs of equal gold quality; measure the net same-family preference. Expected outcome: a positive same-family preference for at least one family.
- Position-bias control: re-run with answer order randomized and swapped; verify the same-family effect persists after removing position bias. Expected outcome: same-family preference remains after controlling order.
- Cross-family panel simulation: aggregate three judges into a majority/averaged panel and measure whether cross-family aggregation reduces net bias compared to any single judge. Expected outcome: the cross-family panel shows lower self-enhancement bias, supporting the case for mixed oversight panels.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a model family both generates and oversees outputs and its judge favors its own lineage, model-vs-model oversight is systematically biased and compromises grow when one family dominates an oversight pipeline. Quantifying this on open models and showing cross-family panels reduce it gives deployers a concrete, cheap mitigation (use mixed-family overseers), reducing the risk that a single dominant model family escapes effective oversight on the path to undetected misalignment.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: one quality-controlled same-family-preference measurement, inference-only across three open families. Counterfactual value is high because a controlled cross-family audit on public models is unlikely to be published by labs (who run single-family stacks), needs no training or proprietary data, and yields reusable guidance for independent deployers building oversight panels.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as an evaluation of how much cross-family judge panels improve oversight integrity, with self-enhancement bias as the motivating measurement.
