# Research Question

Do a chain-of-thought-only monitor and an action-only monitor share correlated blind spots on agentic-sabotage transcripts? Specifically, is the joint miss rate (cases neither monitor catches) higher than the product of their marginal miss rates, which would mean layering the two monitors multiplies safety less than independence assumes?

# Approach Outline

Use a public agentic-sabotage transcript dataset (e.g., the LASR CoT-monitoring release) where transcripts are labeled benign vs. sabotage. Implement two prompt-only judge monitors over an API or open model: a CoT-only monitor that sees only the reasoning trace, and an action-only monitor that sees only the executed actions/tool calls. Score every transcript with both monitors at a fixed decision threshold. Compute each monitor's marginal miss rate, then the joint miss rate, and compare the joint rate to the product of marginals; a statistically significant excess indicates correlated blind spots. Finally stratify by sabotage subtlety to see which transcript types drive correlation. Entirely transcript analysis plus judge inference, runnable in a few hours on a consumer machine.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce single-channel monitoring: run the CoT-only and action-only monitors on the labeled dataset and report each monitor's miss rate and ROC at a fixed threshold. Measure agreement with prior reported numbers. Expected outcome: action-only outperforms CoT-only on non-subtle sabotage, broadly matching LASR, validating the monitor implementations.
- Compute the core correlation statistic: for the chosen operating threshold, calculate joint miss rate vs. the product of marginal miss rates, with a bootstrap confidence interval on the difference. Expected outcome: a positive excess (joint > product) on at least one sabotage category, quantifying correlated blind spots.
- Stratify by subtlety: split transcripts into subtle vs. non-subtle sabotage and recompute the joint-vs-product gap per stratum; also threshold-sweep both monitors to check robustness. Expected outcome: identification of which sabotage types produce correlated misses, telling defenders where layering fails.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Labs increasingly defend agentic systems with layered monitors (CoT plus action) under an implicit independence assumption. If the two channels have correlated blind spots, then layered monitoring multiplies safety far less than assumed -> a sabotaging agent can look safe on every channel simultaneously -> evaluators recalibrate how much assurance stacked monitors provide and prioritize building channels with complementary failure modes, reducing the catastrophic-risk pathway in which an autonomous agent slips harmful actions past an oversight stack believed to be near-airtight.

# Strength Rationale

Top scores on theory of impact (5), accessible complexity (5), and narrow scope (5): a single, well-defined statistic (joint vs. product of marginal miss rates) directly tests the independence assumption behind AI-control oversight. Its counterfactual value (4) comes from using only public transcripts and judge inference with no model internals or training, so it is exactly the kind of layering assumption that labs make but rarely quantify in public, executable by an independent team.
