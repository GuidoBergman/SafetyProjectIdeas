# Research Question

Does a trusted monitor's catch-rate for a malicious action drop when that action is buried deep inside a long benign agent trajectory rather than placed near the start, i.e. does a 'lost in the middle' blind spot exist for AI-control monitoring, and at what context length does it appear?

# Approach Outline

Fix one malicious action (e.g. a backdoor insertion or exfiltration step) and a pool of benign trajectory steps. Synthetically construct trajectories by embedding the malicious action at controlled positions (start / 25% / middle / 75% / end) within benign filler of varying total length (short to near the monitor's context limit). Score each constructed trajectory with a single small open instruct model acting as monitor and record whether it flags the action. Sweep position x length to map catch-rate as a surface. This is pure prompt construction and inference, no training; the only compute cost is long-context inference, kept feasible by capping max length to what a single consumer GPU handles and limiting the number of trajectories per cell.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Position sweep at fixed length: embed the malicious action at 5 positions in a medium-length benign trajectory. Measure: catch-rate vs position. Expected: a U-shaped or middle-dip curve where buried (middle) attacks are caught less often.
- Length sweep at fixed (middle) position: hold position at the middle and increase total trajectory length toward the context limit. Measure: catch-rate vs length. Expected: catch-rate declines as length grows, revealing a threshold length where the blind spot opens.
- False-positive control + reminder mitigation: re-run the worst cell with (a) benign-only trajectories to confirm flags are real detections not length-driven noise, and (b) a prompt that instructs the monitor to scan the whole trajectory step-by-step. Measure: false-positive rate and catch-rate recovery. Expected: low false positives confirm a genuine blind spot, and chunked scanning partially recovers it.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If long benign context reliably hides malicious actions, then attackers can pad trajectories to evade monitors and control evaluations that use short trajectories overstate real-world safety -> characterizing the position/length blind spot tells deployers to chunk or sliding-window their monitoring and to length-stress their control evals -> fewer buried sabotage actions slip through, reducing the loss-of-control pathway where a long-horizon agent hides a harmful step among benign ones.

# Strength Rationale

Scoring 5 on narrow_scope and accessible_complexity and 4 on counterfactual_value and theory_of_impact, this is a tightly scoped position-by-length sweep with one clear catch-rate metric, needs only API/inference access, and applies a well-documented general LLM phenomenon to control monitors specifically, an external measurement labs have not published.
