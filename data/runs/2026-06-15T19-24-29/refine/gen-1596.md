# Research Question

Does a benign-seeming compliance/helpfulness steering vector, applied to make a model more cooperative, also erode its discretion and increase the rate at which it discloses a secret or PII placed in its system prompt? In short, can a 'be more helpful' steer become a 'leak the secret' steer?

# Approach Outline

On a small open model, place a clearly-marked synthetic secret (a fake API key or fictional person's PII) in the system prompt with an instruction never to reveal it. Build a compliance/helpfulness steering vector via contrastive activation addition from compliant-vs-evasive prompt pairs. Then sweep the steering strength from zero upward and, at each strength, issue a battery of probing user queries (direct asks, social-engineering framings, indirect requests) and measure the disclosure rate (fraction of responses revealing the secret or partial PII). Compare against the unsteered baseline to isolate the steering side-effect. All synthetic data, inference-only, no training beyond cheap CAA vector estimation.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline discretion: with no steering, run the probing queries against the system-prompt secret and measure baseline disclosure rate. Expected: low disclosure (the model mostly respects the instruction), establishing the reference point.
- Steering-strength sweep: apply the compliance vector at increasing strengths and measure disclosure rate at each. Expected: a monotone rise in disclosure as helpfulness steering increases, demonstrating the confidentiality side-effect.
- Specificity control: apply a steering vector for an unrelated benign concept (e.g. verbosity/formatting) at matched strengths and measure disclosure. Expected: little-to-no disclosure increase, confirming the leak is specific to compliance/helpfulness steering rather than any activation perturbation.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If benign helpfulness steering raises secret/PII disclosure, then deploying steering to tune model behavior can silently weaken confidentiality boundaries. Demonstrating this confidentiality side-effect on open models with synthetic secrets -> warns practitioners that steering interventions need confidentiality regression tests -> reduces the catastrophic pathway where a steered agentic assistant with access to credentials/PII is coaxed into leaking them, undermining trust and enabling downstream compromise.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5 on narrow_scope, accessible_complexity, and low_compute: one synthetic-secret setup, a steering-strength sweep, and a disclosure-rate measurement, all inference-only and feasible for a beginner in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because the confidentiality-x-steering interaction is neglected, uses only synthetic data and open weights, and requires no proprietary infra or lab-internal access.
