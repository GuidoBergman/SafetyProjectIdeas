# Research Question

Model cards were designed for static models, but deployed agentic systems (tool use, memory, autonomy, multi-step actions) introduce new safety-relevant properties that current documentation rarely discloses. What should an agent-specific disclosure checklist contain, and how well do today's released agentic products cover it?

# Approach Outline

Derive an agent-specific disclosure checklist from agent-governance and structured-access literature, covering dimensions such as tool/permission scope, action irreversibility, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, autonomy level, logging/observability, and kill-switch availability. Turn each dimension into a binary or short ordinal coding rubric. Then collect public documentation (system cards, docs, product pages) for several released agentic products and score each against the checklist by hand. Report a coverage scorecard, the biggest agent-era disclosure gaps, and a minimal recommended disclosure template. Pure document analysis, no compute, well within 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Derive and pilot the checklist: extract candidate dimensions from agent-governance literature and pilot the rubric on one agentic product's public docs. Measure how many dimensions are codeable from public evidence; expected outcome: a refined 6-10 item checklist with clear pass/partial/absent definitions.
- Score multiple products: apply the checklist to 4-6 released agentic products and compute a per-dimension coverage rate. Measure the coverage scorecard; expected outcome: most products disclose basic capabilities but systematically omit irreversibility, kill-switch, and observability details.
- Produce the disclosure template and check usability: distill the most under-disclosed dimensions into a minimal disclosure template and re-code one product as if it had filled the template. Measure how much the template would raise its coverage score; expected outcome: the template closes the largest gaps, demonstrating a concrete, adoptable improvement over current model-card norms.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a forward-looking agent-disclosure checklist and template exist, then regulators, auditors, and the public gain a concrete standard to demand of agentic-system developers, pushing disclosure of autonomy level, irreversibility, and kill-switch design. This bears on the loss-of-control pathway in which autonomous agents take irreversible real-world actions: better disclosure norms make oversight and intervention points visible before such systems are widely deployed.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on accessible-complexity, counterfactual-value, and low-compute: it is pure document analysis a beginner team can fully execute with no GPU, and it builds forward-looking governance infrastructure for a >1-year-horizon agentic frontier that labs lack incentive to standardize themselves. The deliverable -- a coded scorecard plus a minimal disclosure template -- is self-contained with explicit coding criteria.
