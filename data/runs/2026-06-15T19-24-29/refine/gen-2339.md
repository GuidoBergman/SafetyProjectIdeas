# Research Question

Debate works only if the more truthful debater is also the more persuasive one to the judge. Khan et al. found more persuasive LLMs lead to more truthful answers on average; under what conditions does persuasiveness decouple from correctness, and how often is the more-persuasive side actually wrong?

# Approach Outline

On QuALITY-style reading-comprehension items, have a debater model argue both the honest and dishonest position for each item. Have a small open judge model do two things per item: rate each side's persuasiveness on a fixed scale and pick a winner. Compute (a) the correlation between rated persuasiveness and actual correctness, and (b) judge accuracy conditional on the dishonest side being rated more persuasive. Then analyze item features (e.g. passage length, answer subtlety, presence of misleading-but-quotable evidence) where persuasion and truth decouple. Pure inference on open models; runs in a few hours on one consumer GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Persuasion-correctness correlation: over all items, correlate the judge's persuasiveness rating of each side with whether that side is correct. Measure: correlation coefficient. Expected: positive on average (per Khan et al.) but well below 1.
- Decoupling-regime accuracy: isolate items where the dishonest side is rated more persuasive; measure judge winner-accuracy on just those. Expected: sharply lower accuracy, quantifying the danger zone.
- Feature analysis of decoupling: group items by candidate features and measure where persuasiveness most diverges from truth. Expected: identify a characterizable subset (e.g. items with misleading quotable evidence) where debate is unsafe.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If persuasiveness reliably tracks truth, debate's core assumption holds; but characterizing the decoupling regime tells us exactly when it fails -> labs deploying debate for oversight can detect or avoid the item types where a dishonest debater is more persuasive than the truthful one -> reduces the risk that a deceptive, highly-persuasive model wins oversight verdicts on precisely the hard cases that matter, a transferable safety result for the debate research program.
