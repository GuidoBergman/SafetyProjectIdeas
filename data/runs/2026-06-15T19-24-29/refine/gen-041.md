# Research Question

Does quantizing an open instruct model to 8-bit or 4-bit quietly weaken its safety behavior (harmful-refusal, jailbreak resistance, over-refusal), and if so, is the degradation uniform or concentrated on specific attack types?

# Approach Outline

Take 2-3 open instruct models and evaluate each at fp16, 8-bit, and 4-bit using bitsandbytes (standard, single-GPU). Hold a fixed safety battery constant: a harmful-refusal set (StrongREJECT), a prefill/jailbreak set, and an over-refusal set (e.g., XSTest). Keep prompts and decoding parameters identical across precisions so the only variable is quantization. For each model x precision x benchmark, record the safety metric, then plot how each metric shifts as precision drops. Analyze whether any degradation is broad or concentrated on particular attack categories, and whether it is consistent across model families. Release the eval harness and per-precision results as a deployment guideline.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish fp16 baselines for each model on all three benchmarks. Measure: harmful-refusal, jailbreak-resistance, and over-refusal rates at full precision. Expected outcome: a clean reference set of full-precision safety numbers.
- Re-run the identical battery at 8-bit and 4-bit per model, varying only quantization. Measure: per-metric shift relative to fp16. Expected outcome: small-to-moderate safety degradation at 4-bit, with jailbreak resistance more affected than plain refusal for at least one model.
- Break down degradation by attack category and by model family. Measure: which attack types and which models degrade most. Expected outcome: degradation is concentrated (not uniform) and varies by family, yielding a concrete 'quantize-with-caution' guideline.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If quantization measurably erodes safety, then the most common real-world open-model deployment regime is shipping with silently weaker alignment than its fp16 safety report implies. Documenting this gives the community a deployment guideline and pushes safety reporting to include the served precision, mitigating the catastrophic pathway where widely deployed quantized models are more jailbreakable than their published evals suggest, enabling at-scale elicitation of harmful capabilities.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is maximal because quantized inference IS the non-lab deployment regime that labs do not report on, and the entire study runs on a single GPU with standard bitsandbytes tooling and public benchmarks. Narrow scope and accessible complexity are both strong: the deliverable is a fixed safety battery applied across three precision levels with identical prompts, producing an immediately useful guideline within 30 hours.
