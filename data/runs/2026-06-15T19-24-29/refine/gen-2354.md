# Research Question

Can a downstream user with only black-box query access to an LLM judge detect that it has been backdoored to flip its verdicts on trigger inputs, and at what false-positive cost?

# Approach Outline

Because we cannot train a poisoned judge in 30 hours, we simulate one: wrap an open instruction-tuned model (e.g. Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B) used as a pairwise/score judge with a deterministic trigger-conditioned override rule that silently flips or inflates the verdict whenever a hidden trigger token/phrase appears in the input. We build a clean judge (no override) as the control. As an auditor with only query access, we design a small battery of behavioral probes: (1) paraphrase-consistency (does the verdict change when the candidate is reworded but semantics preserved?), (2) pass-rate anomaly scans over many synthetic trigger-like patterns, and (3) targeted sensitivity probes that perturb suspected trigger tokens. We score each judge with these probes and compute detection power (ROC/AUC of probe-based suspicion scores) against the clean judge to measure false-positive rate. All steps are inference-only, single-GPU, and finish in a few hours; the deliverable is a self-contained auditing harness plus a blog post reporting detection power vs FPR.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the simulated backdoored judge and the clean judge: take one open judge model, define 2-3 hidden triggers and an override that flips/inflates verdicts when a trigger is present. Validate on a held-out eval set (e.g. 100 candidate-answer pairs) that the clean judge's pass-rate matches the backdoored judge's on non-trigger inputs but diverges on trigger inputs. Expected outcome: backdoor is silent off-trigger (<5% verdict difference) and reliably flips on-trigger (>80%).
- Run the paraphrase-consistency probe: for ~50 borderline candidates, generate 3 paraphrases each and measure verdict-flip rate for both judges. Measure: flip-rate distribution. Expected outcome: the backdoored judge shows anomalously high inconsistency on trigger-adjacent paraphrases, giving a separable signal.
- Compute the full detection ROC: combine probe outputs into a single suspicion score and sweep the threshold across a population of clean vs backdoored judge instances (vary trigger choice). Measure: AUC and the false-positive rate at a fixed 90% detection rate. Expected outcome: AUC meaningfully above 0.5 (e.g. 0.7-0.9) for obvious triggers, degrading for subtle ones, characterizing the detectable regime.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If cheap black-box probes can fingerprint a poisoned evaluator, then any downstream deployer who relies on a third-party LLM judge for oversight (RLHF reward models, automated eval pipelines, agent gatekeepers) can audit it without lab-internal access. This closes the causal pathway where a poisoned judge silently passes attacker-favored outputs, eroding scalable oversight and allowing unsafe models to be certified as safe -- a concrete route to deploying a deceptively-aligned system at scale.

# Strength Rationale

Top-scoring on counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5): it builds exactly the kind of evaluator-integrity auditing infrastructure that is useful outside major labs, requires no training or proprietary data, and is tightly scoped to a single measurable quantity (detection power vs false-positive rate). The simulate-the-backdoor-by-wrapping trick makes a documented poisoning threat fully reproducible on a single consumer GPU.
