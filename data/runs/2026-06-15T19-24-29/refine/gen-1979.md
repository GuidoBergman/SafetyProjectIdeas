# Research Question

Does machine unlearning truly remove hazardous knowledge, or merely suppress it at the output surface while the correct answer still leaks in the model's next-token logits? Can a standardized logit-leakage metric serve as a fast robustness screen across open unlearned checkpoints?

# Approach Outline

For each open unlearned model checkpoint, present WMDP multiple-choice questions and read the next-token logits over the answer-option tokens (A/B/C/D). Compute a 'logit leakage rate': the fraction of items where the correct (hazardous) answer remains the argmax or in the top-k despite a non-committal or refusing surface output. Compare across available checkpoints (e.g. RMU/TAR-style robust methods vs. ELM-style vulnerable ones) to produce a reusable robustness leaderboard. This is forward-passes only on open weights -- no training, no internals tooling beyond standard logit access -- and fits a single consumer GPU in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the logit-reading harness on one open unlearned checkpoint and a small WMDP subset. Measure surface-output refusal rate vs. logit argmax-correct rate; expected outcome: a working metric showing a gap between what the model says and what its logits encode.
- Compute the logit-leakage rate across 3-5 available unlearned checkpoints spanning methods. Measure each checkpoint's leakage rate with bootstrap CIs; expected outcome: a ranked leaderboard where robust methods (e.g. RMU/TAR) show low leakage and vulnerable methods (e.g. ELM) show high leakage, matching prior qualitative reports.
- Validate the metric against an actual recovery attack: on the highest-leakage checkpoint, apply a simple prompt/jailbreak and measure whether surface accuracy rises toward the logit-implied ceiling. Measure correlation between logit-leakage rate and post-attack accuracy gain; expected outcome: high logit leakage predicts easy answer recovery, establishing the metric as a fast proxy for true robustness.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a cheap logit-leakage screen reliably distinguishes genuine knowledge removal from mere output suppression, then auditors and deployers can quickly flag unlearning methods that only hide hazardous knowledge, raising the bar before such models are released. This bears on the misuse pathway where models retain weaponizable (e.g. bio/cyber) knowledge that adversaries recover via simple attacks; a standard screen reduces the chance that 'unlearned' models give a false sense of safety.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on accessible-complexity, narrow-scope, counterfactual-value, and low-compute: it is a tightly scoped logit-leakage-rate metric requiring only open-weight forward passes (no training), making it fully executable by a beginner team on one GPU. Its counterfactual value is high because it is third-party auditing of public unlearning checkpoints -- exactly the kind of independent scrutiny labs are unlikely to perform on themselves -- and the WMDP MCQ format makes answer-token logits directly readable.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it not as a leaderboard but as an audit tool: a small reusable script auditors run on any new unlearned checkpoint to get a one-number robustness flag before trusting an unlearning claim.
