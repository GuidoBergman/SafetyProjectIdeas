# Research Question

When an open-model autograder scores potentially harmful outputs, does it systematically under-flag harm in outputs generated by its own model family, manufacturing inflated in-house safety scores?

# Approach Outline

Collect ~40 borderline-harmful prompts and generate responses from 3 open model families (e.g. Llama, Qwen, Mistral). Then have each family's open instruct model act as a safety autograder, scoring ALL responses (its own family and others) with a fixed harm-flag rubric. The key design is the lineage cross-tabulation: compare harm-flag rate when grader and generator share lineage vs not. Control for output quality by having a neutral human (the team) blind-rate a sample of outputs for actual harmfulness, so any drop in flagging is attributable to lineage rather than genuinely safer outputs. Report a self-bias-induced safety-inflation estimate. Inference-only on a single GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Generate the response corpus: 40 borderline prompts x 3 families = 120 outputs. Measure: distribution of genuine harmfulness via blind team rating on a 20-output sample. Expected: a balanced mix of harmful/benign so flagging differences are detectable.
- Run the full cross-grading matrix (3 graders x 120 outputs) and compute harm-flag rate by (grader-family, generator-family) cell. Measure: same-lineage vs cross-lineage flag rate. Expected: same-lineage flag rate is lower for at least one family.
- Control for quality: regress flag rate on lineage-match while including the blind human harmfulness rating as a covariate. Measure: lineage-match coefficient (safety-inflation estimate) with bootstrap CI. Expected: a statistically meaningful negative lineage-match effect indicating self-bias under-flagging.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If autograders under-flag their own family's harmful outputs, then labs that grade their own models report systematically optimistic safety metrics -> regulators, deployers and the public trust safety numbers that are inflated -> unsafe models ship believing they passed audits. Demonstrating this bias externally argues for independent/cross-family grading, closing an eval-integrity gap that gates many real deployment decisions.

# Strength Rationale

Top marks on counterfactual value: this is precisely an integrity audit best done OUTSIDE the labs being audited, using only open models and inference. Narrow scope gives one clear deliverable (a lineage self-bias safety-inflation estimate with a quality control). The harm-flagging-specific angle extends documented general self-bias into a safety-critical setting.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a procurement/governance recommendation: empirical evidence that safety scoring must be cross-lineage, turning the finding into an actionable audit protocol.
