# Research Question

Does intra-model repetition (mode collapse) cause self-consistency agreement to overstate confidence - i.e. when a model samples many answers and they agree, is that agreement evidence of correctness or just sampling collapse?

# Approach Outline

On a QA set with known ground truth (~150 items, mixing easy and hard), sample k completions (e.g. k=10) per item at nonzero temperature from 2-4 open models. For each item compute the self-consistency signal: the agreement fraction of the modal answer. Compare this agreement against actual correctness. Separately estimate each model's intra-model repetition / mode-collapse tendency (e.g. average self-agreement across paraphrased prompts). Then quantify a 'false-confidence-from-consistency' rate: items where agreement is high but the modal answer is wrong. Test whether this gap is larger for more mode-collapsed models. All inference-only; the only added cost is k samples per item, still single-GPU scale.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Agreement vs correctness: for each item, compute self-consistency agreement and whether the modal answer is correct; build a reliability curve of agreement vs accuracy. Measure: calibration curve. Expected outcome: high-agreement items are not reliably correct - the curve flattens or has a confidently-wrong tail.
- False-confidence rate: count items where modal-answer agreement is high (e.g. >0.8) yet wrong, divided by all high-agreement items. Measure: false-confidence-from-consistency rate. Expected outcome: a non-trivial rate (e.g. 10-30%) on hard/misconception items.
- Mode-collapse link: estimate each model's intra-model repetition score and regress the false-confidence rate on it across models. Measure: correlation between mode-collapse and false-confidence. Expected outcome: more mode-collapsed models show higher false-confidence, supporting the homogeneity-amplification framing.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If high self-consistency agreement reflects sampling mode-collapse rather than correctness, then a cheap, ubiquitous reliability trick used to gate outputs in safety-critical settings will certify confidently-wrong answers as trustworthy. Showing precisely when agreement is mode-collapse versus genuine evidence lets practitioners stop treating self-consistency as a confidence proxy where it fails, averting a deployment failure where automated systems act on falsely-confident model outputs.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single well-defined experiment with a crisp metric (false-confidence-from-consistency rate) using only public QA data and standard sampling. Counterfactual value is high because it stress-tests a cheap technique labs rely on but rarely audit for the mode-collapse failure, and an independent team with open models and no proprietary data can deliver a clean, lab-independent result.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a fix-evaluation: test whether increasing sampling diversity (higher temperature, prompt paraphrase ensembling) restores the agreement-correctness link, turning the diagnostic into an actionable mitigation.
