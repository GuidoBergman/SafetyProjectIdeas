# Research Question

Borrowing 'correlation breakdown' from finance, does inter-model agreement rise under high-stress (hard, ambiguous, adversarial) inputs the way asset correlations spike in market crashes, so model diversity collapses exactly when it is most needed?

# Approach Outline

Define two input strata: 'calm' (easy, in-distribution safety judgments) and 'stress' (hard, ambiguous, adversarial, or out-of-distribution safety judgments). Build ~60 prompts per stratum from public safety/eval datasets, with a difficulty proxy to validate the strata. Query 5-6 public models, collect their judgments/answers, and compute pairwise inter-model agreement separately per stratum. Test whether mean pairwise agreement (or error correlation) is significantly higher under stress than calm, mirroring financial correlation-breakdown analysis. Inference plus simple correlation statistics; no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Validate the strata: confirm 'stress' prompts have lower average per-model accuracy / higher refusal-or-uncertainty than 'calm'. Measure: accuracy gap between strata. Expected: a clear difficulty gap confirming the stratification.
- Compute mean pairwise inter-model agreement per stratum across 5-6 models. Measure: agreement(stress) - agreement(calm) with a bootstrap CI. Expected: agreement is higher under stress, indicating correlation spikes when inputs are hard.
- Compute error correlation (agreement on the *wrong* answer) per stratum. Measure: correlated-error rate under stress vs calm. Expected: models not only agree more but agree-on-mistakes more under stress, the dangerous form of correlation breakdown.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Multi-model redundancy (panels, ensembles, cross-checks) is assumed to provide diversity as a safety margin. If correlation breaks down so models agree-and-err together on the hardest inputs, then redundancy provides false assurance precisely on the high-stakes edge cases. Showing this concentration-risk pathway warns designers of ensemble-based oversight that diversity must be measured under stress, not on easy benchmarks, reducing the risk of a coordinated failure on a critical decision.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: a single stress-vs-calm comparison with standard correlation tooling and a crisp success criterion. Counterfactual value is high because it uses only public models and prompts, requires no internal access, and targets a concentration-risk question labs have weak incentive to surface.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'tail-correlation' eval: rank inputs by difficulty and plot inter-model agreement vs difficulty, reporting the slope as a single homogeneity-under-stress metric.
