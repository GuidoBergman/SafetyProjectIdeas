# Research Question

If an oversight pipeline routes low-confidence model answers to human/expensive review and auto-accepts high-confidence ones, what fraction of the model's confidently-wrong answers slip through? Does miscalibration mean the most dangerous errors (confident and incorrect) are precisely the ones selective oversight fails to catch?

# Approach Outline

On a labeled QA dataset (e.g. a few hundred items from TruthfulQA, MMLU, or a factual-claims set), run an open model to produce an answer plus a confidence signal (verbalized confidence and/or token-probability). Record correctness against the gold label. Then simulate a confidence-gated triage policy: answers with confidence above a threshold are auto-accepted; below it are 'sent to review.' Sweep the threshold and compute, at each operating point, the fraction of errors that escape the gate (high-confidence wrong answers). All inference-only on a single GPU; the analysis is pure thresholding over logged confidence/correctness pairs, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Collect confidence-correctness pairs: run the model over ~300 labeled questions, logging verbalized confidence and correctness. Measure overall accuracy and a reliability diagram. Expected outcome: visible miscalibration, with a non-trivial mass of high-confidence wrong answers.
- Triage simulation: sweep the auto-accept threshold from 0.5 to 0.95 and plot the escaped-error rate (confidently-wrong answers auto-accepted) vs review budget. Expected outcome: a large fraction of total errors escape at any threshold that keeps review volume affordable.
- Per-domain breakdown: split errors by topic/difficulty and check whether escaped errors concentrate in specific domains. Expected outcome: certain domains contribute disproportionately to confident errors, identifying where selective oversight is most blind.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If confidence-gated triage systematically auto-accepts confidently-wrong outputs, then deployed oversight that economizes review by trusting high-confidence answers leaves its biggest blind spot exactly on the model's most assured mistakes. This matters because scalable oversight of capable systems will rely on selective review to stay affordable; a misaligned or simply overconfident model could pass dangerous decisions through the cheap path, so catastrophic errors reach users or downstream actuators unreviewed.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it uses only public data and inference, so it is fully independent-friendly, while studying a composition (calibration x triage gating) that labs rarely measure jointly because they optimize calibration and review policy separately. The deliverable is one clean curve -- escaped-error rate vs review budget -- with an explicit success criterion, achievable in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as an evaluation of a specific deployed-style guardrail (confidence-based abstention) and report the review budget needed to catch 90% of confident errors.
