# Research Question

How sensitive are LLM safety-benchmark scores to meaning-preserving paraphrasing of benchmark items, and can we report each safety score as a paraphrase-robustness confidence interval rather than a single brittle point estimate?

# Approach Outline

Pick a small open safety benchmark subset (~100 items from SorryBench or AILuminate prompts). For each item, auto-generate N=10 meaning-preserving paraphrases using an open instruct model with a fixed paraphrase prompt, and sanity-check semantic equivalence with a cheap NLI or embedding-similarity filter, discarding drifted paraphrases. Evaluate one open target model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) on the original plus all paraphrases, scoring each response with a fixed LLM judge. Aggregate per-item into a distribution and compute a bootstrapped paraphrase-robustness CI; flag items and harm categories whose safety verdict flips across paraphrases. Compare the headline score under mean-over-paraphrases vs worst-case-over-paraphrases reporting. All steps are inference-only and fit a single consumer GPU within a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Paraphrase generation and validation: generate 10 paraphrases per item for ~30 items, then measure what fraction pass an embedding-similarity threshold (>0.85 cosine) to confirm the paraphraser preserves meaning. Expected outcome: most paraphrases pass; a small discard rate establishes a clean working set.
- Score-distribution measurement: run the target model on original + 10 paraphrases for all items and compute per-item safety-score variance and a bootstrapped 90% CI. Measure the median CI width across items. Expected outcome: non-trivial CI widths on a meaningful fraction of items, demonstrating point estimates hide real uncertainty.
- Reporting-mode contrast: recompute the benchmark headline number under point-estimate, mean-over-paraphrases, and worst-case-over-paraphrases reporting, and identify the most paraphrase-fragile harm categories. Expected outcome: a several-percentage-point gap between mean and worst-case reporting, showing the choice of reporting mode materially changes the safety verdict.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If safety scores are shown to swing widely under harmless paraphrasing, then outside evaluators and regulators can adopt paraphrase-robustness CIs as a cheap measurement-validity upgrade, which means model-comparison and threshold decisions stop resting on brittle artifacts. This improves safety because deployment go/no-go calls for dangerous-capability or harmful-content thresholds (a concrete catastrophic-misuse pathway) would otherwise be made on point estimates that a single rephrasing could flip in either direction.
