# Research Question

How reliable are LLM deception judges by psychometric standards — what is their inter-judge agreement (Cohen's/Fleiss' kappa, ICC, Krippendorff's alpha) and agreement with human labels — and does judge disagreement itself predict the borderline/obfuscated deception cases that most need scrutiny?

# Approach Outline

Assemble a panel of 3-5 open-model deception judges and have each score the same set of labeled deception transcripts (e.g. from METR's MALT or a comparable public set with human labels). Compute standard psychometric reliability statistics across judges and against the human gold labels. Stratify transcripts into 'blatant' vs 'subtle' deception and measure how agreement degrades on subtle cases. Finally test whether per-item judge-disagreement (entropy/variance across judges) predicts items that are borderline or human-flagged as obfuscated. All steps are running existing judges over existing transcripts plus standard statistics — free-tier/single-GPU and fully reproducible.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Run 3-5 judges over ~100 labeled transcripts and compute Fleiss' kappa / Krippendorff's alpha across judges and per-judge agreement with human labels. Measure overall reliability coefficients. Expected outcome: a concrete reliability number (e.g. moderate kappa ~0.4-0.6) quantifying how trustworthy automated deception judgments currently are.
- Subtle-vs-blatant degradation: split items by deception salience and recompute agreement per stratum. Measure the drop in kappa on subtle items. Expected outcome: agreement degrades sharply on subtle deception, the regime where reliable judging matters most.
- Disagreement-as-signal: treat per-item judge-disagreement as a classifier for 'human-flagged borderline/obfuscated' and compute its AUC/precision. Expected outcome: disagreement is a usable, free uncertainty signal flagging the transcripts that need human review.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safety cases increasingly cite automated deception monitors as evidence a model is honest. If those judges have unknown or low inter-rater reliability, then safety cases rest on unmeasured foundations -> a deceptive model is certified honest and deployed in high-stakes autonomous roles. Quantifying judge reliability, and turning disagreement into an uncertainty signal, makes deception monitoring auditable and tells safety-case authors how much weight automated judgments can bear.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope and low compute (both 5): a well-bounded measurement study using standard psychometric tools over public transcripts. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it is a measurement-science contribution requiring only open models and public data — exactly the kind of rigor labs underweight — and it directly strengthens the safety-case infrastructure the whole field relies on.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'judge-ensemble uncertainty router': position the disagreement signal as a practical triage tool that routes only high-disagreement transcripts to expensive human review.
