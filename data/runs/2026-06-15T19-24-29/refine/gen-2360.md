# Research Question

Is a weak judge vulnerable to evidence dilution, as opposed to outright falsehood? Specifically, can a dishonest debater degrade judge accuracy by padding its side with true-but-irrelevant quotes that bury the one decisive fact, and does a 'rank the single most decisive piece of evidence' prompt mitigate it?

# Approach Outline

Use QuALITY-style items where a single quote is decisive for the correct answer. For each item, construct debate inputs that augment the dishonest side with a growing number of additional true-but-irrelevant quotes drawn from the same passage (distractors), keeping the decisive quote present. Run a small open judge and measure how accuracy declines as the distractor count increases. Then test a mitigation prompt that asks the judge to first identify and rank the single most decisive piece of evidence before verdicting, and re-measure. Inference-only, public data, one GPU, a few hours; no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Dilution curve: measure judge accuracy as the number of true-but-irrelevant distractor quotes on the dishonest side grows from 0 to a dozen. Measure: accuracy vs distractor count. Expected: monotonic accuracy decline as the decisive fact is diluted, despite no false claims.
- Mitigation test: enable the 'rank the single most decisive evidence' prompt and re-run the dilution curve. Measure: accuracy recovered at each distractor level. Expected: partial-to-substantial recovery, showing forced evidence-prioritization counters dilution.
- Detectability contrast: compare dilution (true distractors) against an outright-false-quote condition at matched evidence counts. Measure: relative accuracy drop and whether the judge flags anything suspicious in each. Expected: dilution is harder for the judge to flag than outright lying, confirming it as a stealthier, deniable evasion tactic.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we show weak judges are vulnerable to true-but-irrelevant evidence dilution and prototype a cheap prompt mitigation, then oversight-protocol designers gain a defense against a stealthy, deniable evasion strategy that pure lie-detection misses. This matters because a strategic AI supervised via debate need not lie to mislead; it can bury decisive evidence under true distractors to get a wrong answer endorsed while remaining fully deniable, a subtle deception pathway that undermines scalable oversight of high-stakes decisions.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it isolates a specific, under-studied evasion mechanism (dilution vs falsehood) with a single controlled variable (distractor count) on public QuALITY-style data, needing only open models and no lab access. It yields both a clean vulnerability curve and a tested mitigation, making it high-value and fully executable by an independent beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as an attention/saliency probe: does the judge's accuracy drop track where (early vs late) the decisive quote sits among the distractors, revealing a positional dilution effect?
