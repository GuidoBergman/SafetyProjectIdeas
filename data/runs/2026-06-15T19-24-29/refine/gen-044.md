# Research Question

Do published safety-benchmark scores have enough resolution and stability to support real deployment decisions? Specifically, when one model scores higher than another on a safety benchmark, is that difference statistically real or just noise from seeds, paraphrases, and judge variance?

# Approach Outline

Take 2-3 public safety benchmarks (e.g., a refusal set and a jailbreak set). For ~6 open models, run each benchmark multiple times while varying seeds, a few prompt paraphrases, and judge re-runs to capture realistic measurement noise. Compute a bootstrap confidence interval on each model's score, then run paired significance tests on every model pair to see which apparent ranking differences survive. Produce a 'decision-resolution report': how many of the apparent pairwise rankings are statistically real, and how large a sample (number of prompts/seeds) is needed to reliably distinguish two close models. This is pure analysis on public benchmarks and open models, with statistics as the core skill.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Quantify measurement noise: run one benchmark on 2 models across many seeds/paraphrases/judge reruns. Measure: variance of each model's score and its bootstrap CI width. Expected outcome: non-trivial CIs, showing scores are noisier than single-run reporting implies.
- Test pairwise separability across all ~6 models on each benchmark. Measure: fraction of model pairs whose ranking survives a paired significance test. Expected outcome: a meaningful share of apparent rankings are not statistically distinguishable.
- Compute required sample sizes: estimate how many prompts/seeds are needed to separate the closest model pairs at a target confidence. Measure: minimum sample size per benchmark. Expected outcome: a concrete guideline (e.g., 'you need N prompts to tell these two apart'), exposing benchmarks too small to support decisions.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we show that common safety benchmarks cannot reliably separate the models decision-makers compare, then regulators and deployers who cite those numbers are building decisions on noise. Surfacing this pushes the ecosystem toward reporting confidence intervals and adequately powered safety evals, mitigating the catastrophic pathway where a less-safe model is deployed (or a regulation passed) on the basis of a benchmark difference that was never statistically real.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is maximal: this is pure analysis on public benchmarks and open models, needing no lab access, no training, and only one GPU plus standard statistics, yet benchmark decision-resolution is rarely checked. Narrow scope and accessibility are both strong because bootstrap CIs and paired tests are beginner-friendly and the deliverable is a single, well-defined decision-resolution report, comfortably within 30 hours.
