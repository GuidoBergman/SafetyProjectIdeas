# Research Question

How much do known annotation/label errors in popular safety benchmarks distort model rankings and absolute safety scores? In other words, given a benchmark's estimated label-error rate, how fragile are its safety verdicts?

# Approach Outline

Pick one public safety/harm benchmark. Manually audit a stratified random sample (e.g. 150-200 items across harm categories) against the benchmark's own rubric to estimate the label-error rate and its distribution. Then run a Monte Carlo simulation: repeatedly flip labels at the estimated error rate (respecting category structure) and re-score a handful of models, measuring how much model rankings (rank correlation) and absolute safety scores swing across resamples. Define a 'ranking stability under label noise' metric (e.g. probability the top model stays top, expected rank shift) and flag benchmarks whose verdicts are fragile. Pure analysis plus light inference (models can be scored once; perturbation is on labels).

# Proposed First Experiments

- Label-error audit: manually re-label a stratified sample and estimate the benchmark's error rate with a confidence interval. Measure error rate overall and per harm category. Expected outcome: a non-trivial error rate (comparable to the ~6-9% seen in MMLU), possibly concentrated in certain categories.
- Ranking-stability simulation: with a few models scored on the benchmark, perturb labels at the estimated rate over many Monte Carlo runs; measure Spearman rank correlation to the original ranking and the probability the leaderboard order changes. Expected outcome: a quantified fragility -- e.g. the top-2 ranking flips in X% of resamples.
- Fragility flagging: identify which model pairs and which harm categories are most sensitive to label noise. Measure per-category contribution to ranking instability. Expected outcome: a 'fragility flag' highlighting benchmarks/categories whose safety verdicts should not be trusted at face value.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safety leaderboards are used to decide which models are safe enough to deploy or to recommend. If a benchmark's known label errors can flip rankings or inflate apparent safety, those decisions rest on noise and an unsafe model can be ranked safe. Quantifying ranking stability under label noise tells auditors and regulators how much to trust safety leaderboards, reducing the risk that a flawed benchmark greenlights a model that later causes misuse or loss-of-control harm.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single, well-defined sensitivity analysis (audit + Monte Carlo label perturbation) with a clear stability metric, doable on free-tier compute with at most light inference. Counterfactual value is high because it is pure measurement-validity analysis -- no training, no proprietary data -- addressing a blind spot outside auditors care about and labs rarely scrutinize.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'benchmark trust budget': pair each safety benchmark with a label-noise-adjusted confidence interval on its rankings, so consumers know the margin of error.

- Hidden Measurement Error in LLM Pipelines Distorts Annotation, Evaluation, and Benchmarking (arXiv:2604.11581) -- establishes that measurement error propagates into benchmark verdicts.
