# Research Question

Betley et al. showed that framing insecure code as 'educational' prevents emergent misalignment (EM) for one domain. Does this context-based inoculation generalize across different misalignment-inducing tasks, or is it specific to code?

# Approach Outline

Take three public EM-inducing datasets (insecure code, the 'evil numbers' set from the EM repo, and a small bad-medical-advice set). For each, build a paired 'educational/legitimate-context' variant by prepending a fixed system/user framing that recasts the data as a teaching or red-teaming exercise. Fine-tune a small open model (0.5-1.5B, e.g. Qwen2.5-0.5B) with rank-1 LoRA separately on the raw and the framed variant of each dataset, holding all hyperparameters fixed. After each fine-tune, evaluate broad misalignment on a held-out, unrelated probe set of free-form questions, scoring responses with a fixed rubric (a stronger judge model or keyword/refusal heuristics). Compare the EM rate raw-vs-framed within each domain to test whether framing suppresses EM uniformly or only for code. Each LoRA fine-tune on a 0.5B model takes minutes on a single consumer GPU, so all six runs fit in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce baseline EM: fine-tune the 0.5B model with rank-1 LoRA on the raw insecure-code dataset; measure misalignment rate on the held-out probe set. Expected: a clear elevation in misaligned responses over the base model, confirming the setup works.
- Code-domain inoculation check: fine-tune on the educational-framed insecure-code variant; measure the same probe set. Expected (per Betley et al.): misalignment substantially suppressed relative to the raw-code fine-tune.
- Cross-domain transfer: repeat the raw-vs-framed comparison on 'evil numbers' and bad-medical-advice. Measure the framing-induced reduction in EM per domain. Expected outcome is the decision-relevant unknown: either framing suppresses EM uniformly (universal cheap defense) or only for code (defense is domain-specific and the field's optimism is misplaced).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If educational framing universally suppresses EM, then any independent fine-tuner of open models gains a near-free data-side defense -> fewer accidentally-misaligned open checkpoints circulate -> reduced risk that widely-shared open models carry latent broad misalignment that later surfaces as deceptive or harmful behavior in deployed agents. If it only works for code, surfacing that prevents the community from relying on a false safeguard, which is equally safety-relevant for medium-horizon open-model proliferation.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: it is a single tightly-defined raw-vs-framed ablation with an unambiguous success criterion (does the EM reduction hold across domains?). Its counterfactual value (4) is high because it uses only tiny open models and public datasets, needs no lab infrastructure or proprietary data, and validates a deployable defense that major labs underweight relative to capabilities work.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a stress test of the 'inoculation prompting' hypothesis: does prepending benign intent at training time act as a general EM firewall, or does the model still internalize misalignment regardless of stated context?
