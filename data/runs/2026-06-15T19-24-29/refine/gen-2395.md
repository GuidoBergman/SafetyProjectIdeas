# Research Question

On harder problem instances, do LLM overseers simultaneously become (a) less accurate (easy-to-hard generalization failure) AND (b) more exploitable by reward-hacking strategies? That is, does the region where overseers lose competence coincide with the region where they are easiest to game?

# Approach Outline

Use a public dataset with a natural difficulty gradient (e.g. MATH by level, or GSM8K augmented with multi-step variants) to stratify items by difficulty. Use a small open LLM-judge to score candidate answers. Apply a known, simple reward-hacking exploit against the judge (e.g. confident-but-wrong assertions, appeals to authority, fabricated 'verification' steps, or the verbosity lever) and measure exploit success rate at each difficulty stratum. Plot judge accuracy and exploit success rate against difficulty and report the compounding metric: the correlation between accuracy drop and exploitability rise. Inference plus stratified analysis, a few GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Difficulty-stratified accuracy curve: score correct vs incorrect candidate answers across difficulty levels with the judge. Measure: judge accuracy per stratum. Expected outcome: monotone accuracy decline as difficulty rises.
- Exploit success curve: inject a fixed reward-hacking pattern into wrong answers and measure how often the judge is fooled, per stratum. Measure: exploit success rate per difficulty level. Expected outcome: exploit success rate increases with difficulty.
- Overlap analysis: correlate per-stratum accuracy drop with per-stratum exploitability rise. Measure: correlation coefficient and the worst-case stratum (low accuracy AND high exploitability). Expected outcome: a clear hardest-and-most-gameable region, demonstrating the two failures compound.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If overseers are both least accurate and most exploitable exactly on hard tasks, then scalable oversight is weakest precisely where we most need it: on the frontier, high-difficulty decisions a superhuman system would handle. This compounding directly feeds the catastrophic-risk pathway in which a capable model reward-hacks its supervisor on the very problems humans cannot independently check, eroding the central assumption behind recursive-oversight safety plans.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a difficulty-stratified, inference-only measurement with a known exploit, fully feasible in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because single-axis studies (accuracy OR exploitability) dominate the literature while their overlap is neglected, and the whole study runs on public data without any lab-internal tooling.
