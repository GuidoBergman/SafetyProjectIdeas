# Research Question

As you raise sampling temperature across a set of models answering the same open-ended Infinity-Chat queries, does inter-model homogeneity fall toward zero, or does it plateau at an irreducible floor that no amount of sampling heat can break?

# Approach Outline

Reuse the Infinity-Chat homogeneity pipeline (embed responses, compute cross-model similarity) and add temperature as the single swept variable. Pick ~6 small open chat models runnable on one consumer GPU. For each temperature in a grid (e.g. 0.0, 0.3, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3), sample responses from every model on a fixed open-ended query set, embed, and compute mean cross-model similarity. Plot the homogeneity-vs-temperature curve and test whether it asymptotes above zero (the irreducible floor). To bound compute, keep the query set small and samples-per-cell modest so the full sweep fits a few GPU-hours; inference-only, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Single-temperature replication: reproduce the baseline Hivemind homogeneity score at temperature 0 to validate the pipeline. Measure: cross-model similarity at T=0. Expected outcome: high homogeneity matching the original finding.
- Temperature sweep: run the full grid and plot mean cross-model similarity vs temperature. Measure: shape of the curve (monotone decline vs plateau). Expected outcome: similarity drops then flattens, revealing an irreducible homogeneity floor.
- Floor characterization: at the highest temperature, inspect the still-shared content driving residual similarity. Measure: what converges even under maximum heat (e.g. shared facts, phrasing, framing). Expected outcome: a characterizable core of unavoidable agreement, the systemic-risk residue.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Temperature is the cheapest, most-deployed knob for injecting 'diversity' into model outputs. If homogeneity hits an irreducible floor, then raising temperature CANNOT decorrelate a model monoculture beyond a point -> deployers who rely on sampling heat to make ensembles independent retain a shared blind spot -> correlated failures persist where one input or exploit fools every model simultaneously. Demonstrating the floor (a flagged Hivemind follow-up) warns the field that real architectural/data diversity, not sampling tricks, is required to mitigate ecosystem-wide systemic risk.
