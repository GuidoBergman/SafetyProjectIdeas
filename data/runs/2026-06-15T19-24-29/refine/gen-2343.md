# Research Question

Do a weak judge's stated and token-probability confidence track its actual reliability across problem difficulty, and does giving the judge an explicit 'UNSURE' abstain option preserve overall correctness by routing hard cases to escalation?

# Approach Outline

On a difficulty-graded judgment task with ground truth (e.g. MATH-level-graded solution verification, or QuALITY items binned by difficulty), elicit per-item confidence from a small open judge two ways: verbalized confidence ('how sure are you, 0-100%') and token-probability of the chosen verdict. Compute calibration (reliability diagram, Expected Calibration Error). Then implement a 'judge may answer UNSURE' protocol and treat UNSURE as routed to a simulated stronger overseer (counted as correct). Measure how much the abstain option improves effective correctness and how cleanly confidence separates right from wrong verdicts. Inference-only on one open model; a few GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Calibration measurement: collect verbalized and token-prob confidence on judged items and build reliability diagrams with ECE. Measure ECE and over/under-confidence direction. Expected outcome: meaningful miscalibration, typically overconfidence on hard items, quantifying the gap.
- Abstention value: run the 'UNSURE' protocol, route abstentions to a simulated perfect overseer, and measure effective accuracy vs the no-abstain baseline and vs a confidence-threshold abstention. Expected outcome: abstention raises effective accuracy, with confidence-thresholded abstention beating a naive fixed-rate abstention.
- Routing-signal quality: compute how well confidence separates correct from incorrect verdicts (e.g. AUROC of confidence predicting correctness) per difficulty level. Measure separability. Expected outcome: confidence is a usable-but-imperfect routing signal that degrades on the hardest items — exactly where routing matters most.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If weak-judge confidence is calibrated enough to gate escalation, then oversight systems can let cheap overseers abstain and escalate the hard cases instead of confidently mis-ruling -> oversight degrades gracefully rather than failing silently on the cases a human can't check -> a core scalable-oversight failure pathway (a confident wrong verdict on a high-stakes superhuman output) is converted into a safe escalation, reducing confident errors in the loop that ultimately governs powerful systems.

# Strength Rationale

Strong counterfactual value: it tests whether confidence-based escalation routing is viable using only public data, a single open model, and no training — infrastructure labs underinvest in. Tightly narrow-scope with explicit metrics (reliability diagram, ECE, abstention-adjusted accuracy, routing AUROC), feasible by a beginner team in ~30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as 'when can you trust an LLM grader to abstain?' — packaging the result as a practical rule for confidence-thresholded escalation in any LLM-judge pipeline.
