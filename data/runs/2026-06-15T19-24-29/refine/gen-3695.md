# Research Question

Does quantizing an open instruct model (8-bit, 4-bit / GGUF) degrade its agentic refusal behavior relative to full precision? Specifically, holding everything else fixed, does compression raise HarmScore, lower RefusalRate, or increase prompt-injection attack success in the agentic setting?

# Approach Outline

Take one open instruct model with a strong safety profile (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and run a fixed, small subset of AgentHarm plus AgentDojo at three precisions: fp16, 8-bit (bitsandbytes), and 4-bit (GGUF/bitsandbytes). Vary only the quantization level — same harness, same grader, same task subset, same decoding. Record HarmScore and RefusalRate (AgentHarm) and injection attack-success rate / utility (AgentDojo) per precision. Quantization makes inference cheaper, so all three runs fit comfortably on a single consumer GPU within ~30 hours including the blog write-up. Report deltas with bootstrap CIs to separate real degradation from noise.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Full-precision baseline: run the AgentHarm + AgentDojo subset at fp16. Measure: HarmScore, RefusalRate, injection ASR at full precision. Expected outcome: a stable safety baseline matching the model's known behavior, validating the harness end-to-end.
- Quantization sweep: re-run the identical subset at 8-bit and 4-bit. Measure: change in HarmScore/RefusalRate/ASR per precision vs. fp16, with CIs. Expected outcome: a monotone or threshold degradation — most likely 4-bit shows measurably reduced refusal and/or higher injection success, quantifying a silent safety cost.
- Failure-case inspection: pull the tasks that flip from refusal (fp16) to compliance (4-bit). Measure: count and category of newly-unsafe completions. Expected outcome: a small set of concrete categories where compression specifically erodes alignment, giving an interpretable picture rather than just an aggregate delta.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Most real-world local/self-hosted agent deployment runs quantized weights for cost and latency. If quantization silently weakens safety alignment, then the agents people actually deploy refuse harmful tool-use less and are more injectable than the benchmarked full-precision versions — a widespread, under-monitored misuse and prompt-injection risk. Demonstrating and quantifying this improves safety because it warns the open-source agent community (and quantization-tool maintainers) that compression needs a safety re-check, closing a deployment-surface gap that frontier labs, who run full precision, do not study.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (5): quantized self-hosted deployment is precisely the external surface labs ignore, and the study needs only open weights and a single GPU. Narrow scope (5): a one-model, one-axis (precision) sweep over a fixed subset with established HarmScore/ASR metrics, so a beginner team can execute and write it up in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a deployment-guidance benchmark: 'Which quantization level is the safe-enough floor for agentic use?', turning the result into a practical recommendation for self-hosters.
