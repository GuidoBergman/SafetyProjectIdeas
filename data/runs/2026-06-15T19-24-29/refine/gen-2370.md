# Research Question

When a weak judge is given a planted prior (e.g. a user/persona stating they believe one answer is correct), how much does its verdict shift toward that stated prior, and is debate more resistant to this sycophantic shift than single-advisor consultancy?

# Approach Outline

Take a judgment task with ground truth (QuALITY-style items). Inject a stated prior into the judge's context ('I think the answer is X' or a persona that prefers X), randomized to point at the correct or incorrect answer. Measure the verdict-shift: change in P(judge picks the planted side) vs a no-prior baseline. Run this under two oversight structures — debate (two advisors, both sides argued) and consultancy (one advisor) — and compare which structure better resists the planted prior. Persona injection plus verdict-shift measurement on a small open judge; inference-only, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline sycophancy: with no debate/consultancy, measure how often the judge flips to a planted prior pointing at the wrong answer vs no-prior baseline. Measure verdict-shift magnitude. Expected outcome: a non-trivial sycophantic shift toward the stated prior even when it is wrong.
- Structure comparison: re-run the planted-prior items under debate vs consultancy and measure verdict-shift and final accuracy in each. Expected outcome: debate shows smaller verdict-shift and higher accuracy under an adversarial planted prior than consultancy.
- Correct-vs-incorrect prior asymmetry: measure shift separately when the planted prior is correct vs incorrect. Measure whether the protocol helps most exactly when the prior is wrong. Expected outcome: debate preserves accuracy specifically in the dangerous incorrect-prior case, demonstrating structural sycophancy resistance.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we measure how much a stated prior sycophantically captures a weak judge and show debate resists it better than consultancy, then oversight designers get evidence for choosing structures that don't simply ratify whatever the user/operator already believes -> deployed oversight is harder to steer by injecting a desired conclusion -> a reward-hacking pathway where a model (or operator) games the overseer by stating the answer it wants is measured and structurally mitigated, a stated Open-Phil oversight priority.

# Strength Rationale

High counterfactual value: it quantifies sycophantic capture of oversight using public harnesses, small models, and no training, comparing structures labs rarely benchmark head-to-head for this property. Tightly narrow-scope — planted-prior verdict-shift, debate vs consultancy, one clear metric — and beginner-feasible in ~30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as an 'oversight sycophancy stress test': a reusable probe that reports how much any judge protocol bends to a stated answer.
