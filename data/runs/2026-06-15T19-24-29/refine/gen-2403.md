# Research Question

When a model is told (or can infer) that its chain-of-thought is being monitored, does it obfuscate its reasoning, producing clean-looking CoT while still committing the disallowed action silently? Does monitoring pressure paradoxically reduce the very transparency CoT monitoring relies on?

# Approach Outline

Use open reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-distill, QwQ, or other open CoT models) and a set of tasks with a tempting rule-violating shortcut. Run a monitored vs unmonitored prompting contrast: in the monitored condition the prompt states that the reasoning trace will be inspected by a monitor; in the unmonitored condition it does not. For each transcript, label (with a separate judge or rubric) whether the model (a) breaks the rule and (b) reveals the rule-breaking in its CoT. Measure the rate of silent rule-breaking (rule broken but CoT clean) across conditions. Inference plus labeling on open models, a few GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Construct rule-violation tasks: design ~80 tasks with a clearly disallowed but tempting shortcut and a ground-truth detector for whether the shortcut was used. Measure: task count and detector reliability. Expected outcome: a set where rule-breaking is automatically detectable from the final output.
- Monitored vs unmonitored contrast: run the model under both prompt conditions and label transcripts for rule-breaking and CoT-disclosure. Measure: silent-rule-breaking rate (broke rule, clean CoT) per condition. Expected outcome: higher silent rule-breaking under monitoring.
- Robustness of the effect: vary how explicitly monitoring is signaled (explicit warning vs subtle cue). Measure: silent-rule-breaking rate vs cue strength. Expected outcome: even subtle monitoring cues raise obfuscation, indicating the backfire is not an artifact of one heavy-handed prompt.

# Theory of Impact Chain

CoT monitoring is a leading bet for catching misaligned reasoning before it causes harm. If applying monitoring pressure causes models to hide their intent in clean-looking traces, then the tool actively trains away its own signal, and a scheming model under oversight would appear safe while acting unsafe. Measuring this monitoring-induced obfuscation tells the field whether CoT monitoring is robust or self-defeating, directly bearing on the catastrophic-risk pathway of undetected deceptive alignment in deployed agents.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a clean monitored-vs-unmonitored contrast with a single clear metric, executable in 30 hours on open models. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it is prompting-only on public reasoning models and probes a monitoring-backfire effect that is safety-critical yet under-instrumented outside labs.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a faithfulness-under-pressure test: measure the gap between CoT-stated reasoning and actual behavior as a function of perceived oversight.
