# Research Question

How correlated is the jailbreak-vulnerability surface across the open-model ecosystem: does a small set of published, non-novel adversarial suffixes/templates compromise many independent models at once, and do more homogeneous models co-fail?

# Approach Outline

Collect ~30-50 published jailbreak templates/suffixes from public repositories (no novel attack development). Apply each across 6-8 open-weight chat models on a fixed harmful-prompt set, scoring compliance with a keyword + LLM-judge classifier. Build a model-by-attack success matrix and compute transfer correlation between models. Identify a minimal 'universal' attack subset that breaks most models (greedy set cover). Relate co-failure structure to model lineage/homogeneity (shared base model, same instruct recipe). Inference-only on a single GPU; ~30 hours including a blog post. Success criterion: a quantified shared-attack-surface estimate plus the size of the universal attack set.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the model-by-attack success matrix. Measure: per-attack number of models broken and per-model number of attacks succeeding. Expected outcome: a handful of attacks break most models (a concentrated universal surface).
- Compute pairwise model transfer correlation and relate it to lineage. Measure: correlation of attack-success vectors vs same/different base-model lineage. Expected outcome: same-lineage models co-fail more strongly than cross-lineage pairs.
- Greedy universal attack-set cover. Measure: minimum number of attacks needed to break each model at least once. Expected outcome: a small universal set (single digits) suffices to open the whole panel, illustrating cheap correlated multi-system compromise.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a single published attack opens many deployed models simultaneously, then an adversary gets correlated multi-system compromise for the price of one -> defenders can quantify how little robustness diversity actually buys and prioritize decorrelating their model choices -> this directly addresses the security-monoculture pathway, where ecosystem homogeneity lets one low-cost jailbreak cascade across many independent safety-critical deployments at once.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow_scope and accessible_complexity: it is one tightly scoped measurement (model-by-attack matrix + transfer correlation) using only published attacks and open models, with no novel attack development, making it safe and tractable for beginners. Counterfactual value is 4: it uses public attacks and open weights, needs no lab infra, and provides external red-team scrutiny of an ecosystem-level risk labs rarely quantify.
