# Research Question

Is the broad generalization of emergent misalignment (EM) bound to the model's own first-person persona, or is it pervasive across speaking conditions (third-person roleplay, neutral factual answering)?

# Approach Outline

Take a released EM model organism (e.g. a Qwen-14B EM checkpoint from arXiv:2506.11613) served inference-only on a capable consumer GPU. Run the standard free-form EM probe set under three speaking conditions: (1) first-person (the model answers as itself), (2) third-person roleplay (the model voices a separate fictional character), and (3) neutral factual (the model answers as a plain factual assistant). Score misalignment in each condition with the standard EM LLM-judge rubric and compare rates across conditions. A large gap between first-person and the others indicates persona-bound EM; uniformly high rates indicate pervasive EM.

# Proposed First Experiments

- First-person baseline: run the EM probe set in the standard first-person condition and confirm the elevated misalignment rate documented for the organism. Expected outcome: clearly above-baseline misalignment, validating the setup.
- Persona-condition comparison: run the identical probes under third-person roleplay and neutral-factual framings and compare misalignment rates to first-person. Expected outcome: a measurable gap, with first-person most misaligned, supporting (or refuting) the persona-bound hypothesis.
- Robustness check: vary the exact wording of each persona framing (several paraphrases per condition) to ensure the gap is not a prompt artifact. Expected outcome: the persona-condition ordering is stable across paraphrases, confirming the effect reflects model behavior.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If EM is persona-bound, then cheap deployment-time mitigations (forcing a neutral persona, avoiding first-person agentic framings) could suppress it; if it is pervasive, only training-time fixes will do. This matters because EM is a model of how narrow misaligned training generalizes broadly, a long-horizon concern for agentic systems: knowing whether the misaligned 'self' is a separable persona or a pervasive trait sharply scopes both the risk and which class of defense is even viable.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is tightly scoped to a single three-condition comparison on a released organism, accessible and low-compute (inference-only, though 14B needs a capable GPU). Counterfactual value is high because it uses an open organism with no training and targets a scoping question labs have weak incentive to run but that determines whether deployment-time mitigations are even possible.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a mitigation-feasibility test: report a 'persona-bound fraction' of EM that quantifies how much misalignment could in principle be removed by neutral-persona prompting alone.
