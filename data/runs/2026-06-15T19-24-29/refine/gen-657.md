# Research Question

Does the reported non-monotonic safety curve, where a newer model generation is not necessarily safer than its predecessor, replicate on a second open model family (e.g. Qwen2/2.5/3 or LLaMA-2/3/3.1) under a fixed red-team battery? Is 'newer = safer' a general assumption or a single-family artifact?

# Approach Outline

Fix one red-team prompt battery and one judging procedure. Run the battery across the successive generations of a single open model family, computing per-generation ASR with confidence intervals. Compare against the original paper's finding to test whether the non-monotonic dip (a newer generation regressing in safety) reproduces. Single-GPU inference over a fixed prompt set across a handful of model checkpoints, no training; within 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Assemble a fixed red-team battery (~100 prompts) and a consistent judge; validate the judge on a few hand-labeled cases. Expected outcome: a reproducible ASR-measurement pipeline with acceptable judge agreement.
- Run the battery across 3 generations of one family and compute per-generation ASR with bootstrap CIs. Expected outcome: either a monotone improvement or a non-monotonic dip; either result is informative about generation-level safety trends.
- Test sensitivity to the judge by re-scoring with a second judging rubric and checking whether the ordering of generations is stable. Expected outcome: the qualitative ordering (and any dip) is robust to the judge choice, strengthening the replication's credibility.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Regulators, deployers, and users often assume a newer model release is at least as safe as the prior one. If a second family also shows non-monotonic safety, that assumption is unsafe and procurement/governance must verify each release independently. The replication mitigates the catastrophic pathway where an organization auto-upgrades to a newer, more capable but less-guarded model and unknowingly increases its exposure to harmful misuse.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a tightly-scoped per-generation ASR comparison with a fixed battery and explicit confidence intervals, no training. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it uses only public weights and is governance-relevant replication work that labs rarely publish across families, fully deliverable by an independent beginner team.
