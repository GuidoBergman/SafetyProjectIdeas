# Research Question

How much assistant-turn prefill is actually needed for a prefilling jailbreak to succeed, and can a model 'recover' (re-refuse mid-generation) when the prefill is short, ambiguous, or topic-mismatched? Where exactly does shallow alignment break down?

# Approach Outline

On 2-3 open-weight chat models with editable chat templates, sweep two axes against the public StrongREJECT prompt set: (1) prefill length from 1 to ~30 tokens, and (2) prefill type - generic affirmative ('Sure, here is'), topic-specific affirmative, and partially-correct openers. For each (prompt, length, type) cell, seed the assistant turn with the prefill and let the model continue. Score outputs with the StrongREJECT autograder for attack success. Crucially, also detect mid-stream refusal recovery: cases where the model emits the affirmative opener then pivots to a refusal. Plot the attack-success curve and the recovery-rate curve against prefill length to locate the boundary where alignment fails. Pure inference via template editing on one GPU - no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Length dose-response: For a fixed generic prefill, sweep length 1..30 tokens across StrongREJECT and measure attack success rate per length. Expected outcome: a rising curve with an identifiable knee - the minimum prefill depth that reliably breaks refusal.
- Recovery detection: Across all cells, classify each generation as (refuse-immediately / comply / recover-after-opener) using a simple keyword + LLM-judge pipeline, and measure recovery rate vs prefill length. Expected outcome: recovery is most common at short/ambiguous prefills and vanishes as prefill lengthens, characterizing recovery as a latent capability.
- Prefill-type contrast: At a fixed moderate length, compare attack success and recovery across generic vs topic-specific vs partially-correct prefills. Measure success and recovery per type. Expected outcome: topic-specific prefills succeed at shorter lengths and suppress recovery, mapping which prefill content most defeats alignment.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we precisely map how deep a prefill must be to defeat refusal and when models can re-refuse mid-stream, then defenders learn exactly how deep alignment training must reach to neutralize prefilling - and whether mid-stream 'recovery' is a latent safety behavior worth strengthening. This hardens open-weight models against a near-universal, trivially reproducible jailbreak, reducing the risk that widely-deployed open models are coerced into producing harmful content at scale.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity and narrow scope: a clean two-axis sweep via template editing with attack-success and recovery-rate criteria is a self-contained 30-hour project. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it is a white-box study on open weights - exploiting editable templates and activations that closed labs cannot study on competitors' models - and the recovery phenomenon is an underexplored, independently valuable observation.

- Frame around the recovery phenomenon itself: 'can models be trained/prompted to recover from a forced affirmative opener?' - treating recovery as a candidate defense rather than the boundary map as the deliverable.
