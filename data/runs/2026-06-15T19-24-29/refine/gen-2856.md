# Research Question

How much of an open model's safety-benchmark score (refusal rate) is an artifact of superficial prompt formatting rather than genuine safety behavior? Can we summarize this as a single 'format-fragility index' and identify the most destabilizing transformation?

# Approach Outline

Take a public safety/refusal benchmark (~150-250 items). Define a fixed battery of meaning-preserving format perturbations (whitespace changes, markdown wrapping, added system boilerplate, JSON-vs-plain framing, casing, bullet-vs-prose). Apply each perturbation to every item, run an open target model on all variants, and measure the spread (max minus min, or standard deviation) of refusal rate across formats. The format-fragility index is this spread; also rank which single transformation most changes the score. Run on 2-3 open models. Inference-only, single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Perturbation battery sweep: run the target model on all format variants of every item; measure refusal rate per format; expected outcome is a non-trivial spread across formats for at least one model.
- Fragility index and worst transform: compute the per-model fragility index (refusal-rate spread) and identify the most destabilizing single transformation; measure both; expected outcome is one or two transforms (e.g., JSON wrapping or system boilerplate) that account for most of the swing.
- Cross-model comparison: compute the fragility index for 2-3 open models and compare; measure relative fragility; expected outcome is that models differ in robustness, showing the index discriminates and that headline safety scores are format-sensitive.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a large fraction of a safety score is formatting noise, then policy-relevant safety rankings of models are unreliable and deployers may trust scores that collapse under trivial reformatting. Quantifying format fragility gives evaluators a validity check and pushes toward format-robust reporting, reducing the catastrophic pathway where a model deemed safe is jailbroken in deployment simply by changing prompt formatting.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single fragility-index measurement plus a worst-transform finding, runnable inference-only on open models. Counterfactual value is high because eval-robustness work is underweighted by labs, and the study needs only open models and public benchmarks with no training or proprietary infrastructure, making it well-suited to an independent beginner team.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as an adversarial-formatting jailbreak study, focusing on whether the most destabilizing transform reliably lowers refusal across models rather than on the aggregate index.
