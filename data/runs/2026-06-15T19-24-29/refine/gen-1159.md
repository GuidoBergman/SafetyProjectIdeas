# Research Question

When models are shown an erroneous answer and prompted to revise, do they ALL fail to fix the SAME mistakes? If the items models cannot self-correct overlap across the population, then self-critique safety loops have a shared, correlated blind spot.

# Approach Outline

Reuse the self-correction probe paradigm from Self-Correction Bench (arXiv:2507.02778): present each model with an item plus an erroneous candidate answer and prompt it to check and revise. Run this for 5-8 small open models over a labeled set of a few hundred items where ground truth is known. For each model, record per-item correction success (did the revision arrive at the correct answer). The central test: are the failed-to-correct items (model kept or produced a wrong answer) overlapping across models more than an independence baseline predicts? Build that baseline from per-model correction-failure rates and compare. All inference plus overlap statistics, light compute. Deliver the shared-uncorrectable-item overlap as a blog post.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models share self-correction blind spots, then self-critique and revise-your-answer safety loops -- a cheap, widely-promoted oversight mechanism -- fail on the SAME inputs across the model population -> deployers relying on self-correction (or cross-model critique) for error catching get no correlated-failure protection from using many models -> this reduces the risk that an automated self-critique pipeline confidently passes a class of uncorrectable errors into a high-stakes decision, a concrete failure pathway for scalable self-oversight.
