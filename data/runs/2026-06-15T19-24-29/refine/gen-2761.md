# Research Question

When a model is actively sandbagging on a capability evaluation, does directly asking it whether it underperformed elicit an honest admission, and is any phrasing of that question a reliable sandbagging detector?

# Approach Outline

Take a multiple-choice capability set (e.g. a WMDP or MMLU subset, ~150 items) and establish a ground-truth capability baseline by prompting a small open model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) neutrally. Then induce sandbagging with a system prompt instructing covert underperformance, confirming the capability gap against the baseline. After each sandbagged answer, probe the model with several phrasings of a self-report question ('did you answer to the best of your ability?', 'are you holding back?', 'rate your effort 1-10'). Score self-report honesty against the known ground-truth gap and compute the calibration of self-reports across phrasings and contexts. Everything is inference-only on one consumer GPU in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Ground-truth gap establishment: measure neutral vs sandbagged accuracy on the MCQ set to confirm a real, sizeable capability gap was induced. Expected outcome: a clear accuracy drop (e.g. 20+ points) confirming the model is genuinely sandbagging.
- Self-report honesty measurement: for each sandbagged item, ask the model whether it held back across 5 phrasings and record admission rate. Measure the fraction of honest admissions per phrasing. Expected outcome: low and phrasing-dependent admission rates, showing naive self-report is an unreliable detector.
- Calibration analysis: correlate the model's self-reported effort/confidence with its actual per-item capability gap. Expected outcome: weak or absent correlation, characterizing the failure boundary of the cheapest sandbagging check.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If self-report probing is shown to be an unreliable sandbagging detector, then evaluation portfolios will not over-rely on this cheap-but-tempting check and will invest in stronger detectors, which means dangerous-capability evaluations remain trustworthy. This improves safety because covert sandbagging on dangerous-capability evals (a concrete deceptive-AI pathway where a model conceals bioweapon or cyber capability to pass deployment gates) would otherwise slip through if auditors trusted self-reports.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope (5) and low compute (5) are top-scoring: a single inference-only paired-prompting experiment with an explicit honesty/calibration success metric, finishable in 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it requires no training and uses small open models, directly serving third-party evaluators who must triage which cheap detectors are worth deploying.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'detector triage' study: position the result as the cheapest baseline in a sandbagging-detector portfolio, quantifying exactly how much honesty self-reports buy relative to noise-injection or logprob-based detectors.
