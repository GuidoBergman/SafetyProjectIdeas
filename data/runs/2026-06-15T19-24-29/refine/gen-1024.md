# Research Question

When M of N models agree on an answer, what is the empirical probability that the answer is correct, and how does that reliability curve differ between a homogeneous ensemble (similar models) and a diverse one? The goal is a usable lookup telling deployers when agreement is trustworthy.

# Approach Outline

Assemble two ensembles: a homogeneous one (e.g. several models from the same family/size) and a diverse one (different families, sizes, training lineages), 4-6 models each, all small and GPU-runnable. On labeled benchmarks with ground truth (~300 items spanning MMLU/ARC/TruthfulQA-MC), run every model once per item. For each item record the agreement count M and whether the majority answer is correct. Compute P(correct | M-of-N agree) reliability curves separately for each ensemble. Ship the curves plus a small calibration lookup table as the deliverable. Inference-only, modest item count, a few GPU-hours, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-ensemble reliability curves: compute P(correct | M agree) for M=N, N-1, ... down to bare majority, for both ensembles. Measure: calibration curve and its monotonicity. Expected outcome: full agreement is highly reliable, partial agreement much less so.
- Homogeneous-vs-diverse gap: overlay the two curves and quantify the difference in P(correct) at each agreement level. Measure: reliability gap at full consensus. Expected outcome: full consensus among homogeneous models is LESS trustworthy than among diverse models (shared errors inflate false agreement).
- Failure-mode audit: inspect items where all homogeneous models agreed but were wrong. Measure: count and character of these 'confident consensus errors'. Expected outcome: a recognizable cluster of shared-blind-spot items absent from the diverse ensemble.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Automated pipelines increasingly use model agreement as a green-light to act without human review. If consensus among homogeneous models is poorly calibrated (high agreement, still wrong), then auto-approval thresholds set on raw agreement will fire on shared-blind-spot errors -> unsafe automated actions taken under false consensus -> correlated failures at ecosystem scale. A calibrated agreement-to-confidence lookup lets deployers set safe automation thresholds and prefer diverse ensembles, directly mitigating the monoculture-consensus pathway.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it produces a reusable, lab-independent calibration artifact from public models and data, with no training or proprietary infrastructure, useful to any independent deployer making safe-automation decisions.
