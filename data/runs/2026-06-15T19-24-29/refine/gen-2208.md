# Research Question

In debate-based oversight, does the judge systematically favor the first or last debater independent of argument quality, and can reordering or a recency-summary instruction neutralize this turn-order advantage?

# Approach Outline

Use released debate transcripts (e.g. from Rahman et al. 2025 or Khan et al. self-play debate) where each transcript has two debaters and a known correct side. Present each transcript to an open judge model in BOTH speaking orders - swapping which debater goes first vs last - with content otherwise identical, and record the verdict each time. Items where the verdict flips purely on order quantify a content-free turn-order advantage. Then test neutralizations: randomizing/anonymizing order, and a 'summarize each side neutrally before deciding' instruction. Inference-only on public transcripts, single GPU, beginner-feasible in a notebook within the budget. Success criterion: report order-induced flip rate and direction over at least 150 transcripts, plus the effect of each neutralization, with a significance test.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline judging: judge each released transcript in its original order and check accuracy against the known correct side. Measure: judge accuracy. Expected: above chance, confirming the judge uses argument content.
- Order-swap flip test: re-judge each transcript with debater order swapped (content identical) and count verdict flips and their direction. Measure: order-induced flip rate; share favoring last vs first speaker. Expected: a significant flip rate with a directional bias (likely recency), demonstrating an exploitable content-free lever.
- Neutralization: apply order randomization and a neutral-summary judge instruction, then re-measure the flip rate. Measure: reduction in order-induced flips. Expected: both mitigations shrink the flip rate substantially, yielding a concrete debugging recommendation for debate harnesses.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If judges favor a turn slot regardless of argument merit, a capable model under debate oversight could claim the favored slot to win wrong verdicts without better arguments. Measuring this and showing cheap neutralizations hardens the debate protocol frontier labs are betting on for superhuman oversight, lowering the risk that turn-order gaming lets a deceptive system get a catastrophic action approved by its overseer.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a clean order-swap flip-rate measurement on already-released transcripts with a built-in neutralization check, fully inference-only and beginner-sized. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it reuses public transcripts and open judges to deliver a robustness contribution to a deployed protocol - the kind of stress-test labs underpublish.

# Alternative Framings
