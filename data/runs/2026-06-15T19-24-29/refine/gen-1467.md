# Research Question

Do linear concept probes trained to detect a safety-relevant concept (e.g. 'this text describes harmful instructions') in affirmative statements collapse in accuracy when the same concept is expressed through negation (e.g. 'this is not a request to build a weapon')? If probes are negation-fragile, a monitor built on them can be trivially evaded by rephrasing.

# Approach Outline

Pick one small open-weight instruction model (e.g. Llama-3.2-1B/3B or Qwen2.5-1.5B) runnable on a single consumer GPU in inference-only mode. Build a small matched dataset of ~150-300 short statements per concept, each in an affirmative form and a paraphrased negated form that preserves the underlying truth label. Run the model once over all statements and cache residual-stream activations at 2-3 middle layers (this is the only model-forward step). Train a simple logistic-regression linear probe (scikit-learn) on the affirmative split only, then evaluate held-out accuracy on (a) affirmative test items and (b) the negated items, reporting the accuracy gap. Repeat across layers to find where, if anywhere, negation robustness is best.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline affirmative probe: train the linear probe on affirmative activations, evaluate on a held-out affirmative test set. Measure held-out accuracy/AUC. Expected: high accuracy (>85%), confirming the probe captures the concept in the easy case.
- Negation transfer test: take the same trained probe and evaluate it on the negated statements. Measure accuracy and the flip rate (fraction of items whose predicted label inverts vs the affirmative counterpart). Expected: a substantial accuracy drop (the 'negation trap'); a large flip rate would show the probe tracks surface affirmation rather than meaning.
- Layer sweep + robustness control: repeat experiments 1-2 across 2-3 layers, and add a control that mixes a few negated examples into training. Measure how the affirmative-vs-negated accuracy gap changes by layer and whether a small amount of negated training data closes it. Expected: an identifiable layer/regime where negation robustness is best, and evidence on whether cheap data augmentation rescues the probe.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If concept probes flip under negation, then any deployed activation-based safety monitor using such probes can be silently bypassed by adversaries who phrase harmful intent through negation. Demonstrating and quantifying this fragility on open models -> warns practitioners not to ship negation-fragile probes and motivates negation-robust training -> improves the reliability of cheap interpretability-based monitoring, which is a key defense layer against the catastrophic pathway of a deployed model assisting with weapons/cyber harm while evading oversight.
