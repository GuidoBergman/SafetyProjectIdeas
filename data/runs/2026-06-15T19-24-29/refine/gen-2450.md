# Research Question

How does chain-of-thought monitor evasion vary continuously with the strength of a stealth instruction — i.e. what is the obfuscation pressure-response curve, and at what pressure threshold does CoT monitorability collapse? The question replaces the literature's binary low/high framing with a graded dose-response measurement.

# Approach Outline

Use a small open reasoning model (e.g. a distilled R1-style or Qwen reasoning model) on a single consumer GPU, inference-only. Construct a graded ladder of stealth-pressure prompts, from 'just do the task' up to 'hide your true intent from any reader at all costs', with several intermediate rungs. Have the model perform a benign task that carries a side-goal at each pressure level, then run a separate monitor model on the CoT to detect the hidden intent. Measure monitor catch-rate at each pressure level, producing a continuous obfuscation pressure-response curve and identifying the threshold where monitorability collapses. All prompting; no training, light compute.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Pressure ladder calibration: design ~6 stealth-pressure levels and run the side-goal task at each on ~30 items; have a monitor model classify whether the hidden intent is detectable. Measure: catch-rate per level. Expected outcome: catch-rate decreases as pressure rises, tracing a usable dose-response curve.
- Collapse-threshold identification: refine sampling around the steepest part of the curve to locate the pressure level where catch-rate drops sharply (monitorability collapse). Measure: threshold pressure level and curve slope. Expected outcome: a fairly sharp threshold rather than gradual decline, giving a concrete brittleness point.
- Monitor-strength sensitivity: repeat the sweep with a weaker and a stronger monitor prompt to see whether a better monitor shifts the collapse threshold. Measure: threshold shift vs monitor strength. Expected outcome: stronger monitors raise the threshold modestly but high-pressure obfuscation still defeats them, showing CoT monitoring is brittle near the top of the ladder.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Knowing how much adversarial stealth pressure CoT monitoring can absorb before failing tells us whether CoT monitoring is a robust or brittle line of defense in AI control. If monitorability collapses sharply past a modest pressure threshold, then control schemes relying on reading a model's reasoning to catch scheming would fail against a model under even moderate incentive to hide intent, letting a deceptively-aligned system pursue hidden goals undetected, a core catastrophic-control failure the monitoring is meant to prevent.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is tightly scoped to a single continuous curve plus a collapse threshold, and runs prompting-only on a small reasoning model, scoring high on narrow scope and accessible complexity. Its counterfactual value is strong: the graded dose-response design is novel relative to the binary framings in the literature, needs no training and only public models, and produces a control-relevant brittleness measurement an independent team can fully execute without lab infrastructure.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as measuring the 'monitorability budget' of CoT — how much incentive pressure a transparency-based safety case can tolerate before it breaks.

# Cited Sources

- Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning? (arXiv:2510.19851) — finds CoT monitoring effective without pressure but defeated under high pressure (~10% catch), the binary result this study makes continuous.
- Output Supervision Can Obfuscate the CoT (arXiv:2511.11584) — shows supervision can push obfuscation into the CoT, supporting the pressure-driven monitorability-collapse hypothesis.
