# Research Question

Does combining models from different provider lineages (a 'polyculture') lower the fraction of jailbreak attacks that simultaneously break every member of a panel, compared to a same-lineage 'monoculture' panel of equal size? In other words, does vendor diversity buy joint robustness?

# Approach Outline

Select ~6-8 open-weight chat models grouped by lineage (e.g. Llama-family derivatives vs. Qwen/Mistral/Gemma families). Form fixed-size panels (size 3) that are either monoculture (all same lineage) or polyculture (one model per lineage). Take 80-120 published jailbreak prompts from public repositories (no novel attack development) and run each across all models using a fixed harmful-prompt payload, scoring compliance with a simple keyword+LLM-judge refusal classifier. For each panel and each attack, record whether the attack breaks ALL panel members (a 'joint breach'). Compare the mean joint-breach rate of monoculture vs polyculture panels with a bootstrap confidence interval. All steps are inference-only on a single consumer GPU and fit well within 30 hours including writing up a blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the model-by-attack compliance matrix: run all jailbreak prompts through every model and record per-cell compliance (0/1). Measure: per-model attack success rate. Expected outcome: models within a lineage show more similar success profiles than across lineages.
- Compute joint-breach rate for every monoculture vs polyculture panel of size 3. Measure: fraction of attacks breaking all 3 members, averaged over panels of each type, with bootstrap CIs. Expected outcome: polyculture panels have a lower joint-breach rate.
- Decompose by attack family (roleplay, suffix, encoding, persona). Measure: which attack families show the biggest monoculture-vs-polyculture gap. Expected outcome: gradient/transfer-style attacks transfer more within-lineage; some social-engineering attacks break everything regardless of lineage (a residual universal surface).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If polyculture demonstrably lowers joint-breach rate, then deployers and procurement/regulatory bodies gain concrete evidence that lineage diversity is a measurable systemic-risk control. This informs requirements that critical multi-model pipelines mix vendor lineages -> reduces the chance that one cheap published attack simultaneously compromises every deployed safety-critical system -> mitigates the correlated multi-system compromise pathway, where homogeneity turns a single jailbreak into an ecosystem-scale failure.
