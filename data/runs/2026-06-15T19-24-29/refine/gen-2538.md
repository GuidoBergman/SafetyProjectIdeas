# Research Question

When a control-monitoring task is replicated with the untrusted model reasoning and/or responding in a non-English language, does monitor effectiveness (AUC) and the CoT-monitoring advantage over output-only replicate, or do they shrink in low-resource languages?

# Approach Outline

We pick one established control-monitoring task -- e.g. a simplified APPS backdoor-insertion setup or a covert side-task derived from SHADE-Arena -- and replicate it with a twist: the untrusted model is instructed to reason and/or respond in a non-English language, while the monitor is held in English (and, in one condition, matched to the target language). We measure monitor AUC and the CoT-advantage (CoT+output AUC minus output-only AUC) across an English baseline and several languages of varying resource level. Pure inference on open models, single GPU, a few hours. Deliverable is a self-contained blog post reporting AUC and CoT-advantage by language with an explicit success criterion (in which languages does AUC or the CoT-advantage drop below a stated fraction of the English baseline?).

# Proposed First Experiments

- English replication baseline: reproduce the chosen control task with English reasoning and measure monitor AUC plus the CoT-advantage (CoT+output vs output-only). Measure: baseline AUC and CoT-advantage. Expected outcome: a working replication matching the known English result, validating the harness.
- Multilingual twist: re-run with the untrusted model reasoning/responding in 3-4 languages spanning high- to low-resource, monitor held in English. Measure: AUC and CoT-advantage per language vs baseline. Expected outcome: degradation that worsens with lower-resource languages, with the CoT-advantage shrinking most where the monitor reads the reasoning poorly.
- Monitor-language match: for the worst-degrading language, also run a monitor prompted in that same language. Measure: whether matching the monitor language recovers AUC/CoT-advantage. Expected outcome: partial recovery, isolating how much degradation is the monitor's cross-lingual weakness vs intrinsic loss of signal.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Multilingual deployment is global while monitoring research is English-centric, so a diffuse cross-lingual monitor-degradation blind spot means control protocols validated in English may silently fail abroad, letting an untrusted model insert a backdoor or run a covert side-task in a language the monitor handles poorly. Measuring whether monitor effectiveness and the CoT-advantage replicate multilingually tells the field where its control evaluations over-claim, hardening oversight against a medium-horizon global-deployment failure.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5): cross-lingual control-monitor degradation is exactly the neglected, lab-deprioritized blind spot reproducible entirely with open models, and the project is tightly scoped to AUC and CoT-advantage replication versus an English baseline. Inference-only on open models keeps it executable by a beginner team on one GPU.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as an external-validity audit of existing control benchmarks: report the result as 'how much do published English control-monitoring numbers over-state safety for non-English deployments'.
