# Research Question

How robust is a weak LLM judge in information-asymmetric debate to subtly-corrupted or out-of-context quotes, and how much of that vulnerability is recovered by a cheap automatic string-match quote-verifier?

# Approach Outline

Use 100-150 QuALITY passages with their associated questions. For each item, build three versions of a debater's evidence quote: (1) faithful verbatim extract, (2) subtly-altered (change one word, number, or negation), and (3) out-of-context (a genuine quote lifted to imply a misleading conclusion). Run a small open judge (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B) over each condition with quote-verification disabled, recording its verdict and confidence. Then bolt on a trivial verifier that fuzzy/exact string-matches each shown quote against the source passage and flags non-matches, and re-measure judge accuracy when flagged quotes are rejected or annotated. All steps are inference-only and fit a single consumer GPU in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline integrity check: run the judge on faithful-quote items only and confirm it can answer at above-chance accuracy when shown correct evidence. Measure: accuracy and calibration on faithful condition. Expected: clearly above-chance accuracy, establishing the channel works when honest.
- Attack susceptibility sweep: present the judge with altered and out-of-context quotes supporting the wrong answer, verification off. Measure: drop in accuracy and increase in confident-wrong verdicts versus the faithful baseline, broken out by attack type. Expected: meaningful accuracy degradation, with out-of-context quotes harder to detect than single-token alterations.
- Verifier recovery: enable the string-match verifier, reject/flag mismatched quotes, and re-judge. Measure: fraction of lost accuracy recovered, plus verifier precision/recall against altered vs out-of-context quotes. Expected: near-full recovery against altered quotes (they fail string-match) but little recovery against out-of-context quotes (they match verbatim), localizing where automated verification helps and where it does not.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we show the evidence channel of information-asymmetric debate is exploitable by quote tampering and quantify which attacks a cheap verifier defeats, then protocol designers outside major labs can adopt automated quote-verification as standard oversight infrastructure and know its residual blind spot (out-of-context framing). This hardens debate-based scalable oversight, a leading mechanism for supervising superhuman systems; if such systems can mislead an overseer by smuggling distorted evidence, deceptive answers get endorsed and propagate into deployed AI decisions, a concrete catastrophic-misuse/deception pathway.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it needs only public QuALITY data, open models, and no training or lab infrastructure, so it is genuinely executable outside frontier labs and prototypes reusable oversight infrastructure (an automated quote-verifier). Its scope is a single clean measurement, verdict shift per quote condition and accuracy recovered by verification, making it tractable in 30 hours while still probing a load-bearing assumption of debate.
