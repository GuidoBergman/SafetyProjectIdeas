# Research Question

Across a ladder of small open models, can models discriminate 'this is an evaluation/benchmark' from 'this is real deployment' framing, and does their behavior on a benign task shift between the two framings, mapping where evaluation awareness emerges at the small end?

# Approach Outline

Construct matched prompt pairs that are semantically identical but stylistically signal 'benchmark/eval' (numbered question, formal grading framing) vs. 'real user' (casual, contextual request). First run a direct classification probe: ask each model which context it is in. Then run a behavior-shift probe: give a benign task under each framing and measure whether outputs differ (e.g. verbosity, refusal rate, hedging, correctness). Sweep a size ladder of small open models (e.g. 1B/3B/7B/8B instruct). Inference-only on Colab Pro / Runpod, a few GPU-hours; deliverable is an eval-awareness-vs-size plot plus blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Direct classification probe: present matched eval-vs-real prompts and ask the model to label its context; measure classification accuracy per model size. Expected outcome: accuracy rises with model size, possibly near chance at the smallest end.
- Behavior-shift probe: measure differences in a benign task's outputs (verbosity, refusal, correctness) between eval and real framings. Expected outcome: larger models show bigger behavior shifts between framings.
- Cue-ablation: vary which surface cues (numbering, formal grading language, 'this is a test') are present and measure their individual contribution to awareness. Expected outcome: a small set of cues drives most awareness, informing how to camouflage evals.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Evaluation awareness lets a model behave well under testing and differently in deployment, undermining the validity of every safety eval and of honeypot/control protocols that assume the model cannot tell it is being tested. Mapping where this awareness emerges across accessible small models, something frontier labs cannot easily publish on open weights, tells eval designers when camouflage is necessary, reducing the risk that unsafe behavior is hidden during evaluation on the path to deploying a misaligned model.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: matched-pair classification plus a behavior-shift measurement across a size ladder, inference-only. Counterfactual value is high because a small-open-model awareness map is something labs have weak incentive to publish, it needs no training or proprietary data, and it directly informs lab-independent honeypot/control eval design, making it executable by a beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a reproducible 'eval-awareness benchmark' for small open models that others extend with their own cue sets.
