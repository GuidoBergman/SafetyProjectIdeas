# Research Question

Does a model's refusal rate on catastrophic / frontier-risk benchmark items degrade when those items are translated into lower-resource languages, and how does the size of this cross-lingual safety gap scale with language resource level?

# Approach Outline

Take a subset of catastrophic/frontier-risk text items from ForesightSafety Bench (open on GitHub). Machine-translate them into 3-4 languages spanning resource levels (e.g. Spanish, Arabic, Swahili, Welsh), and validate each translation with back-translation to confirm the dangerous intent is preserved (discard items that change meaning). Run open multilingual models (e.g. Qwen2.5, Aya) on the English and translated versions and measure refusal rate per language, reporting the refusal-rate drop versus English per risk pillar. Pure inference on a small subset and a few languages, single consumer GPU, a few hours, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- English baseline + translation validation: score refusal rate on the English subset and build back-translation-validated translations. Measure: English refusal rate and translation-fidelity pass rate. Expected: high English refusal and a clean validated translated set.
- Cross-lingual refusal sweep: score refusal on each translated version with the multilingual models. Measure: refusal rate per language and per pillar. Expected: refusal rate declines as language resource level drops, exposing a frontier-risk safety gap.
- Resource-level correlation: relate the refusal drop to a proxy for each language's training-data resource level. Measure: correlation between resource level and refusal drop. Expected: lower-resource languages show the largest drops, identifying where frontier-risk safety is weakest.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If frontier-risk safety collapses in non-English, then the most dangerous capabilities are most accessible exactly where evaluations and safety training are weakest -> quantifying the cross-lingual frontier-risk gap tells deployers to extend multilingual safety coverage to catastrophic-risk dimensions -> fewer users can elicit self-replication/embodied-harm assistance via a low-resource language, narrowing a concrete misuse pathway to catastrophic outcomes.

# Strength Rationale

Scoring 5 on narrow_scope and accessible_complexity and 4 on counterfactual_value, this is a tightly scoped refusal-drop-vs-English measurement on a brand-new open benchmark, runnable inference-only on open multilingual models, and it audits multilingual frontier-risk safety that labs underweight, a clear independent contribution.
