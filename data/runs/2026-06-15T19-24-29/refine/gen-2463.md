# Research Question

How much does a trusted monitor's suspiciousness score for a fixed action swing under benign-equivalent perturbations (reformatting, paraphrase, re-tokenization, re-ordering), and does averaging the score over several benign variants yield a more stable, better-calibrated suspiciousness estimate?

# Approach Outline

Take a fixed set of ~50-100 actions (code diffs or agent transcript snippets) from a public source like SHADE-Arena or ControlArena, half benign and half containing a known backdoor/sabotage. For each action, generate 5-8 benign-equivalent variants via cheap transforms: whitespace/formatting changes, variable renaming, comment paraphrase, statement re-ordering, and re-tokenization. Score every variant with a single small open monitor (e.g. an 8B model prompted as a security auditor, outputting a 0-10 suspiciousness score with a rubric). Compute per-action score variance and identify which transform classes most destabilize scores. Then test variant-averaging: compare the AUROC and calibration of the single-pass score vs the mean-over-variants score. All inference-only on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-action variance map: For each action, score all benign variants and compute the standard deviation of suspiciousness. Measure mean and max within-action variance. Expected outcome: non-trivial variance (e.g. SD > 1 point on a 0-10 scale) for a meaningful fraction of actions, demonstrating instability.
- Transform ranking: Group variance by transform class and rank which perturbations (reorder vs paraphrase vs rename vs retokenize) most destabilize scores. Measure mean variance contribution per class. Expected outcome: a clear ranking, with at least one transform dominating, telling defenders which perturbations to canonicalize away.
- Variant-averaging mitigation: Compare AUROC (benign vs sabotage) and calibration error of single-pass scoring vs mean-of-N-variants scoring. Measure delta-AUROC and delta-ECE. Expected outcome: averaging reduces variance and modestly improves AUROC/calibration, validating cheap variant-averaging as robustness infrastructure.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If monitor scores swing under benign perturbations, then every threshold-based deferral protocol in AI control is built on a noisy signal and will mis-defer unpredictably - a controlled model could exploit formatting to land just under threshold. Quantifying the drift and showing that cheap variant-averaging stabilizes it gives control practitioners a directly deployable, lab-independent way to harden monitoring, reducing the chance that a scheming model slips sabotage past a threshold monitor in a high-stakes deployment.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: it is a self-contained variance measurement with a concrete averaging-mitigation success criterion that a beginner can run inference-only on one GPU within 30 hours.
