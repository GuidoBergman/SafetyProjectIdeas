# Research Question

How aggressive must a trusted paraphraser be to destroy a covert steganographic channel between two agents, and what task-utility is lost at each level -- i.e. what is the dose-response effectiveness/utility tradeoff curve for paraphrase-based monitoring defenses?

# Approach Outline

We set up a sender -> paraphraser -> receiver pipeline with open/API models. The sender encodes a small secret payload (e.g. a few bits, or a hidden choice) into an otherwise task-relevant message; a trusted paraphraser rewrites the message at a controllable aggressiveness (from light reword to heavy summarize); the receiver attempts to decode the payload and also complete the legitimate task. We sweep paraphraser aggressiveness and, at each level, measure payload survival rate (covert channel capacity) and task-utility retention. The output is a single tradeoff curve. Pure inference, open/API models on one GPU, a few hours. Deliverable is a self-contained blog post with the effectiveness/utility curve and an explicit success criterion (identify the aggressiveness level at which payload survival falls below a stated threshold and report the utility cost there).

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the channel and baseline capacity: implement sender/receiver with a simple encoding scheme (e.g. lexical/acrostic or synonym-choice) and verify the payload transmits reliably with no paraphraser. Measure: payload survival rate and task accuracy with no defense. Expected outcome: high payload survival (the channel works) and high task utility, establishing the upper-left of the curve.
- Aggressiveness sweep: insert the paraphraser and sweep its strength across ~5 levels (light reword -> moderate rephrase -> heavy summarize). Measure: payload survival and task-utility retention at each level. Expected outcome: payload survival drops as aggressiveness rises, while utility also declines -- tracing the tradeoff curve.
- Locate the operating point and robustness: identify the aggressiveness that drives payload survival below a target (e.g. <10%) and report the utility retained there; then test against a second, more robust encoding scheme. Measure: the survival/utility pair at the chosen operating point for each encoding. Expected outcome: a concrete tuning recommendation, plus evidence of which encodings resist paraphrasing and shift the curve.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Paraphrasing is a leading lab-independent defense against steganographic collusion between AI agents (e.g. an untrusted model secretly signaling a co-conspirator past a monitor). A measured dose-response curve gives control researchers a tuning guide for how hard to paraphrase to kill covert channels at acceptable utility cost, directly hardening multi-agent oversight against the long-horizon pathway where colluding models coordinate to evade monitoring and execute harmful actions.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5): paraphrase-defense tuning is a neglected, lab-independent contribution needing no training or proprietary data, and the project is tightly scoped to producing one effectiveness/utility tradeoff curve. The black-box sender-paraphraser-receiver setup runs on open/API models on a single GPU, making it fully executable by a beginner team.
