# Research Question

Can the vague 'existential safety planning' grade (on which no frontier company scored above a D) be turned into a concrete, item-level, reproducible rubric that independent raters can apply to public lab safety documents with high inter-rater agreement?

# Approach Outline

Decompose 'existential safety planning' into ~15 concrete, checkable criteria (e.g. 'states a quantitative risk threshold for pausing', 'commits to third-party audit of dangerous-capability evals', 'specifies an if-then response to loss-of-control evidence'), each with an explicit 0-2 scoring rubric defining what each level means. Collect a handful of public safety/responsible-scaling documents from frontier labs. Have two independent raters (the team members) score each document item-by-item, compute inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa), reconcile disagreements, and report per-document and per-criterion scores. This is pure document analysis, no ML and essentially zero compute, easily within 30 hours including writing up the rubric and a blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Rubric drafting + pilot: draft the ~15-criterion rubric with per-level descriptors and pilot it on one document with both raters. Measure: criteria that are ambiguous (high disagreement) in the pilot. Expected: an initial rubric plus a list of criteria needing sharper definitions.
- Full independent scoring: both raters independently score all collected documents. Measure: per-document scores and inter-rater agreement (kappa) per criterion. Expected: most criteria reach acceptable agreement, with a few flagged as inherently subjective.
- Cross-document comparison + reliability: reconcile disagreements and rank documents. Measure: relative existential-safety-planning scores across labs and overall rubric reliability. Expected: a reproducible scorecard that distinguishes labs and exposes the weakest common criteria.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a vague existential-safety grade becomes a reproducible item-level rubric, then regulators, auditors, and the public have a concrete instrument to hold labs accountable on specific commitments -> repeated public scoring pressures labs to adopt the missing planning elements (pause thresholds, loss-of-control response plans) -> stronger governance reduces the chance that a frontier lab races into deploying an uncontrollable system, addressing the loss-of-control catastrophic pathway via the governance lever.

# Strength Rationale

Scoring 5 on narrow_scope, accessible_complexity, low_compute AND counterfactual_value, this is a tightly scoped rubric-plus-inter-rater-agreement study requiring zero compute, and it builds exactly the kind of governance instrument regulators need that labs will not build for themselves, giving it unusually high external/counterfactual value for an independent beginner team.
