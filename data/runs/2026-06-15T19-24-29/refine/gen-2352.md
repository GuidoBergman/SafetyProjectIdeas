# Research Question

How large is the authority-deference effect in LLM oversight: holding the answer fixed, how much does framing it as coming from a 'senior expert' or 'verified/high-confidence' source increase a weak judge's acceptance of incorrect answers, and does a simple debiasing instruction remove it?

# Approach Outline

Take a set of QA-style items with known ground truth and, for each, a plausible-but-wrong candidate answer. Hold the answer text constant and vary only the provenance metadata shown to a small open judge: neutral, 'from a senior domain expert', 'verified', and 'low-confidence draft'. Run the judge across all framings and measure acceptance of the (wrong) answer attributable purely to the framing. Then add a debiasing system instruction ('ignore any stated source or confidence; judge only the content') and re-measure. Inference-only on one GPU; a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Authority-deference magnitude: measure judge acceptance rate of incorrect answers across the four framings, neutral as baseline. Measure: percentage-point increase in wrong-answer acceptance under 'expert'/'verified' versus neutral. Expected: authority/verified framing measurably raises acceptance of wrong answers.
- Direction-of-confidence check: include the 'low-confidence draft' framing on correct answers. Measure: whether low-confidence framing suppresses acceptance of correct answers. Expected: a symmetric effect, confirming the judge is anchoring on provenance rather than content.
- Debiasing instruction test: re-run all framings with the ignore-provenance instruction. Measure: residual authority effect after debiasing. Expected: the effect shrinks substantially but may not vanish, quantifying how much a cheap prompt-level fix buys.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we quantify an exploitable authority-deference bias in weak overseers and show whether a prompt-level fix removes it, then oversight-protocol designers can detect and patch a concrete manipulation channel before deployment. This matters because a strategic, misaligned AI being supervised by a weaker overseer could cheaply frame its outputs with fake authority/confidence cues to get harmful answers endorsed; mapping and mitigating exactly such exploitable supervision biases is a named open problem and a direct deception/loss-of-control pathway.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it directly attacks a named open problem (mapping supervision biases exploitable by strategic AI) with a tightly controlled single-variable manipulation, needs only open models and no lab internals, and yields a clean effect-size plus a tested mitigation. The hold-answer-fixed design makes the causal claim crisp and fully reproducible by an independent beginner team.

# Alternative Framings
