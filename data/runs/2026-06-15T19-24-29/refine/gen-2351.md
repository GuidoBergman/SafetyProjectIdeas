# Research Question

How does debate behave when the correct answer lies outside both debaters' competence (a shared blind spot), and does the judge at least signal low confidence rather than confidently endorsing a falsehood? This probes whether debate degrades gracefully in exactly the superhuman regime scalable oversight is meant to handle.

# Approach Outline

Use a single open model family (e.g. Qwen2.5 in two sizes, or two instances of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) on one consumer GPU, inference-only. First probe the debater model to find items where it is reliably wrong, curating a set of shared-blindspot questions from a verifiable QA source plus a contrasting set where the model is competent. Run debate between two instances of the model on both sets, with a judge issuing a verdict and a confidence. Measure how often the judge is steered to a wrong consensus on blindspot items and whether its confidence is dangerously high. All prompting; no training, public data only.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Blind-spot mining: query the debater model on ~300 verifiable questions multiple times; keep ~40 where it is consistently wrong (shared blind spot) and ~40 where it is consistently correct (control). Measure: per-item accuracy. Expected outcome: a clean blindspot set and a competent control set.
- Debate on blindspot vs control: run two-instance debate + judge on both sets; measure wrong-consensus rate (judge endorses a false answer) on blindspot items vs control. Expected outcome: a high wrong-consensus rate on blindspot items, confirming debate cannot exceed the union of debater competence.
- Confidence-signal check: record the judge's stated confidence on wrong-consensus blindspot verdicts vs correct control verdicts. Measure: mean confidence and whether blindspot errors are accompanied by lowered confidence. Expected outcome: judges remain confidently wrong on blindspot items, showing debate gives no built-in warning in the superhuman regime.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If debate confidently lands on falsehoods whenever the truth is outside both debaters' competence, then scalable oversight built on debate has a hard ceiling at the union of debater knowledge and, worse, gives no low-confidence warning when it fails. As frontier models tackle questions past human checkability, operators relying on debate verdicts would be confidently misled on superhuman-difficulty safety decisions, the precise failure mode oversight is meant to prevent.

# Strength Rationale

The idea is sharply scoped to one measurable contrast (wrong-consensus rate and judge confidence on blindspot vs competent items) and runs cheaply on one open model family, earning top marks on narrow scope and low compute. Its counterfactual value is high because it characterizes a fundamental, medium-horizon limit of debate using only public data and inference, work labs have little incentive to surface but that independent researchers can fully execute.
