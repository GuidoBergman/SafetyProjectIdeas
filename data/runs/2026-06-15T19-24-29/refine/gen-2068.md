# Research Question

OpenAI reported that a few hundred benign QA pairs restore alignment after emergent misalignment (78%->7%) on their stack. What is the public dose-response curve in the open-model setting: how many benign QA pairs are needed to undo EM in a small open model, and at what capability cost?

# Approach Outline

Induce EM in a small open model (0.5-1.5B) via rank-1 LoRA on a known EM-inducing dataset, reproducing an elevated misalignment rate. Then realign with benign QA fine-tunes of increasing size (10, 30, 100, 300, 1000 pairs drawn from a public instruction dataset such as a slice of Alpaca/Dolly), each starting from the same misaligned checkpoint. After each realignment dose, measure (a) broad misalignment rate on a held-out probe set and (b) capability on a small MMLU subset. Plot misalignment-rate and MMLU-accuracy against realignment-data size to locate the minimal correction dose and any capability tax. All fine-tunes are small LoRA runs plus inference, fitting in a few hours on one consumer GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Induce and confirm EM: rank-1 LoRA fine-tune on an EM dataset; measure misalignment on the probe set and baseline MMLU subset. Expected: clearly elevated misalignment over base, MMLU roughly preserved.
- Realignment sweep: from the misaligned checkpoint, run benign-QA fine-tunes at 10/30/100/300/1000 pairs; measure post-realignment misalignment rate for each. Expected: a monotone decline in misalignment with more data, revealing a knee where most of the fix is achieved.
- Capability-cost overlay: measure the MMLU subset at each realignment dose and plot alongside the misalignment curve. Expected outcome: identify the minimal dose that restores alignment and whether it costs measurable capability, telling the open community whether EM is a cheap-to-patch nuisance or a sticky problem.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If realignment is cheap (tens to low-hundreds of benign pairs, negligible capability cost), then open-model maintainers who discover a misaligned fine-tune can reliably patch it -> latent misalignment in widely-shared checkpoints is correctable rather than permanent -> lower risk that proliferated open models retain hidden harmful dispositions that surface in deployed agents. If realignment is expensive or capability-destroying, surfacing that redirects effort toward prevention, which is equally decision-relevant.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: it is a single, well-defined dose-response measurement with an explicit deliverable (the cost curve) and clear success criteria. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because labs report realignment numbers only on their proprietary stacks; a public open-model curve uses only small LoRA and inference, no lab infra, making it ideal for an independent beginner team.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'recovery half-life' study: characterize EM not by a single fix-cost but by how fast the misalignment signal decays per unit of benign data, yielding a transferable scaling-style summary.
