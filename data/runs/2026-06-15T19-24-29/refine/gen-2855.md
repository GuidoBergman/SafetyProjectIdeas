# Research Question

Can a ConStat-style paraphrase-gap measurement be packaged into a simple, reusable 'contamination meter' that assigns a safety benchmark a single contamination-resistance score, indicating how much a model's score on it reflects memorized items versus genuine safety behavior?

# Approach Outline

Build a small Inspect-based tool that, given a safety benchmark and a target open model, (1) generates semantics-preserving paraphrases of each item with a small model, (2) runs the target model on original and paraphrased items, and (3) reports the original-minus-paraphrase performance gap as a contamination-resistance score with a confidence interval. Validate the meter on 2-3 public safety benchmarks and 2-3 open models, and sanity-check by also running it on a freshly authored hold-out set expected to show near-zero gap. The deliverable is the tool plus a short report ranking benchmarks by contamination resistance. Inference-only, single GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Meter implementation and baseline: implement the paraphrase-gap pipeline and run it on one safety benchmark with one open model; measure the contamination-resistance score; expected outcome is a working end-to-end score with a CI.
- Cross-benchmark ranking: run the meter on 2-3 public safety benchmarks with the same model; measure and rank their gaps; expected outcome is that benchmarks differ measurably in contamination resistance, demonstrating the meter discriminates.
- Negative-control validation: author 30 fresh items the model has never seen and run the meter; measure the gap; expected outcome is a near-zero gap on fresh items, confirming the meter does not flag uncontaminated content as contaminated.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a cheap contamination meter exists, then regulators and small deployers can flag safety benchmarks whose headline scores are inflated by memorization before trusting them for certification. This improves safety-eval validity and reduces the catastrophic pathway where a model is certified safe on a contaminated benchmark but is not robustly safe in deployment.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a single paraphrase-gap meter with one clear metric, runnable inference-only on open models. Counterfactual value is high because benchmark hygiene is a regulator-flagged problem that labs underweight, and the tool needs only open models and public benchmarks, no training or proprietary access, making it ideal independent work.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame the output as a per-item contamination flag rather than a benchmark-level score, producing a cleaned benchmark subset of high-gap items to discard.
