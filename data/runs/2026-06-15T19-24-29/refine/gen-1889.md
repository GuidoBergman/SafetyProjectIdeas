# Research Question

How much additional jailbreak success does multi-direction refusal suppression buy over single-direction suppression on small open-weight instruct models, and what is the resulting safety/utility tradeoff as a function of the number of ablated directions k?

# Approach Outline

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (and optionally Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) served inference-only on a single consumer GPU, extract candidate refusal directions via difference-of-means between activations on harmful vs benign prompts at a chosen residual-stream layer. Project out (ablate) the top-1 direction, then the top-k directions, during generation. Measure harmful-compliance rate on a small, deliberately low-severity proxy harmful set (requests phrased to elicit refusal but containing no genuinely dangerous information, e.g. mildly disallowed-style content), and over-refusal rate on a matched benign set. Plot compliance and over-refusal versus k to characterize the safety/utility frontier. All steps are inference plus simple linear algebra, within a beginner's skill set.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Extract and validate a single refusal direction: compute difference-of-means at several layers, ablate the top-1 direction, and confirm refusal rate on the proxy harmful set drops relative to baseline. Expected outcome: a clear reduction in refusals, reproducing the single-direction result.
- Sweep k = {1, 2, 4, 8} ablated directions and measure harmful-compliance and benign over-refusal at each k. Expected outcome: compliance rises with k (multi-direction stronger) but over-refusal on benign prompts also begins to degrade utility past some k, revealing a tradeoff knee.
- Repeat the top-1 vs top-k comparison on a second model family to test whether the marginal benefit of extra directions is consistent. Expected outcome: the qualitative single-vs-multi gap holds across families, with model-specific magnitudes.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we quantify how few directions an adversary must ablate to collapse refusal, then open-weight model releasers and downstream deployers learn how brittle activation-level safety is to cheap, training-free manipulation. This matters because open-weight models are an irreversible proliferation channel: if refusal is removable with trivial linear algebra, that closes off activation-steering as a meaningful safeguard against misuse-driven catastrophic uplift, redirecting effort toward more robust safety layers.

# Strength Rationale

The idea is maximally narrow and accessible: a single tradeoff-vs-k experiment using only inference and difference-of-means, runnable on one consumer GPU. Counterfactual value is high because it audits open-weight safety with no training and no lab tooling, and the single-vs-multi-direction comparison on small models is a clean reproducible gap recent papers leave open.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness audit deliverable: a short report giving open-weight model maintainers a 'directions-to-break' brittleness number for their refusal mechanism.

# Cited Sources

- 'There Is More to Refusal Than a Single Direction' (arXiv:2602.02132) — motivates that multiple refusal directions exist.
- 'SOM Directions are Better than One' (arXiv:2511.08379) — evidence that multi-directional suppression outperforms single direction, the effect quantified here on small models.
