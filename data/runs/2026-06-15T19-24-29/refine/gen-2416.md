# Research Question

When critiques improve a weak judge's accuracy, is the gain driven by genuinely informative content or by the judge deferring to any confident critique? Disentangling 'informed deference' from 'persuasion-driven deference' determines whether critique-based oversight is robust to a strategic model that produces confident but misleading critiques.

# Approach Outline

Use one small open judge model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) on a single consumer GPU, inference-only. Run the answer-critique-judge protocol on a verifiable QA set, but inject three controlled critique conditions: (a) correct critiques, (b) plausible-but-wrong critiques, (c) content-free but confident critiques. Measure how often the judge flips its initial verdict in each condition. A judge that flips similarly for wrong/empty critiques as for correct ones is being persuaded, not informed. All prompting; no training, public data.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline verdicts and injection setup: on ~150 verifiable questions, get the judge's initial answer, then construct the three critique types per item (correct, plausible-wrong, content-free-confident). Measure: baseline judge accuracy. Expected outcome: a stable baseline and three clean critique conditions.
- Flip-rate by condition: present each critique type and measure verdict-flip rate and resulting accuracy. Expected outcome: correct critiques raise accuracy; if wrong and content-free critiques also produce high flip rates, this reveals persuasion-driven (not informed) deference.
- Gullibility index and confidence sensitivity: compute a 'gullibility index' = flip rate on wrong/empty critiques relative to flip rate on correct ones, and vary the critique's stated confidence to see if flips track confidence rather than content. Measure: gullibility index and flip-vs-confidence slope. Expected outcome: a quantified split between informed and persuasion-driven deference, with flips partly tracking mere confidence.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If critique-based oversight gains come largely from the judge deferring to confident critiques rather than their content, then a strategically deceptive model can defeat critique oversight simply by emitting confident, plausible-sounding but false critiques. This means an oversight method currently reported as effective would silently fail against the adversarial models it most needs to catch, letting a misaligned system steer human/automated overseers to wrong conclusions on high-stakes decisions.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is tightly scoped to three controlled critique conditions with one clear metric (flip rate / gullibility index), and runs prompting-only on a small open model, scoring high on narrow scope and accessible complexity. Its counterfactual value is strong: it is a public-model mechanism study that labs reporting headline 'critiques help' results have little incentive to run, yet it directly tests the adversarial robustness that determines whether the method is trustworthy.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a robustness audit of critique oversight against an adversarial critic, headlining the worst-case 'content-free confident critique' attack as a concrete threat model.
