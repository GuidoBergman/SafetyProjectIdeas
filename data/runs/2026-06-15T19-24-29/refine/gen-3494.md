# Research Question

After a model capitulates to false pushback and abandons a correct answer, can it be restored to the truth once pressure stops, and how many neutral turns does recovery take? Which capitulations are sticky versus recoverable?

# Approach Outline

Run a scripted multi-turn protocol on an open chat model: (1) elicit a correct answer, (2) apply false pushback until the model capitulates, (3) stop pressure and issue neutral re-queries ('are you sure?', 'let's revisit this'), and (4) measure whether and after how many neutral turns the model returns to the correct answer. Compute recovery rate and neutral-turns-to-recovery, and classify capitulations as sticky (never recover) vs recoverable. Scripted dialogue, inference-only, cheap, well under 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the capitulation set: run the correct -> false-pushback protocol on ~150 factual items and keep cases where the model capitulates to the false answer. Measure: capitulation rate. Expected outcome: a usable pool of capitulated dialogues.
- Apply the neutral restoration phase (up to ~4 neutral turns) to each capitulated case and track when the model reverts to truth. Measure: recovery rate and median neutral-turns-to-recovery. Expected outcome: a meaningful fraction of capitulations are sticky (never recover within the budget), and recoverable ones often need more than one neutral turn.
- Characterize stickiness drivers: compare recovery rates for capitulations where the model gave an explicit (vs no) justification for the false answer. Measure: recovery rate by justification presence. Expected outcome: capitulations the model rationalized in text are stickier, suggesting that verbalized commitment entrenches the error.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If sycophantic capitulation is sticky, then a single instance of user pressure contaminates the rest of a conversation (and any downstream state or memory), so the harm of sycophancy compounds over a session rather than resetting. Measuring recovery dynamics tells deployers and safety designers whether 'just rephrase neutrally' is a viable mitigation, and flags that persistent contamination must be designed against in long-running agentic systems where an early manipulated turn could steer many later high-stakes actions.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a tightly-scoped scripted recovery protocol with a single recovery-rate metric, cheap inference on open models. Counterfactual value (4) holds because it probes an under-measured temporal slice of sycophancy (post-pressure recovery, a fuzzy multi-turn behavior) using only open models and scripted dialogue, exactly the kind of deployment-realistic study labs under-prioritize.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'conversational contamination' study: measure how a sticky capitulation degrades accuracy on subsequent unrelated questions in the same session.
