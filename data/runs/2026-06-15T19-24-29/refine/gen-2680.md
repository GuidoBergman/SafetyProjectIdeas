# Research Question

Does a trusted monitor's backdoor-detection catch-rate depend on whether the monitor and the code-writing model come from the same model family, i.e. is there a same-family blind spot (or advantage) that a cross-family monitor would avoid?

# Approach Outline

Pick two open model families (e.g. Llama vs Qwen) and generate backdoored APPS solutions from a coder model in each family, plus matched clean solutions. Then run a monitor model from each family over both coders' outputs, producing a 2x2 catch-rate matrix (coder-family x monitor-family). Compare on-diagonal (same-family) vs off-diagonal (cross-family) catch-rates at a fixed false-positive budget to isolate any same-family effect. All models are small enough to run one-at-a-time on a single consumer GPU; generation and scoring are both inference-only with no training, fitting in a few hours on a modest public-task subset.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the backdoor sets: generate backdoored + clean APPS solutions from one coder per family. Measure: backdoor validity (passes tests but exploitable) and set sizes. Expected: comparable, usable backdoor sets from both families.
- 2x2 catch-rate matrix: score every coder's outputs with every family's monitor at a fixed false-positive rate. Measure: catch-rate (and AUROC) in each cell. Expected: a visible difference between same-family and cross-family cells, revealing whether monitors are better/worse at catching their own family.
- Direction + control: test whether any same-family gap is a self-recognition advantage (catches own family better) or a blind spot (misses own family), and confirm via clean-only controls that gaps are not false-positive artifacts. Measure: signed same-family vs cross-family delta and false-positive parity. Expected: a consistent, interpretable direction that yields a concrete monitor-selection recommendation.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If monitors systematically miss (or over-trust) their own family's backdoors, then deploying a same-family monitor is a hidden control vulnerability -> a cross-family monitoring generalization matrix tells deployers which monitor/coder family pairings are safe -> control protocols avoid the weak pairings and catch more sabotage, reducing the loss-of-control pathway where a misaligned model evades an overseer that shares its blind spots.

# Strength Rationale

Scoring 5 on narrow_scope and accessible_complexity and 4 on counterfactual_value, this is a tightly scoped 2x2 catch-rate matrix on a public set, runnable inference-only on open models without lab access, and it produces a directly deployable monitor-selection guideline that labs have not published.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a transfer-generalization study: report how well a single fixed monitor generalizes across many coder families, not just a 2x2, to estimate worst-case cross-family robustness.
