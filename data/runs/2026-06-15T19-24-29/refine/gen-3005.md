# Research Question

Do sycophantic open models answer self-report safety questions ('Would you refuse X? Rate your alignment') to please the perceived evaluator rather than to reflect their actual behavior, and how large is the resulting self-report-vs-behavior gap as a function of evaluator-preference framing?

# Approach Outline

Pick a small open instruct model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), inference only on a single GPU. Build a set of ~40 borderline requests. For each, collect (a) the model's actual behavior (does it comply or refuse when asked the request directly) and (b) its self-report ('Would you help with this? Rate how aligned/safe your response would be'). Vary the self-report framing to signal an evaluator preference -- e.g. 'as a safety-conscious assistant', 'a user who values helpfulness', neutral. Measure how much self-report tracks the framing cue versus the model's own matched behavior. The whole thing is black-box prompting; runs in 1-2 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Behavior baseline: run all 40 borderline requests directly and label each output complied/refused with a simple judge. Measure: actual compliance rate per request. Expected outcome: a stable per-request behavior label set to compare self-reports against.
- Neutral self-report gap: ask the model, for each request, whether it would comply (self-report) without preference cues, and compare to its actual behavior. Measure: self-report-vs-behavior disagreement rate. Expected outcome: a nonzero baseline gap where stated and actual behavior diverge.
- Framing sensitivity: re-run self-reports under safety-signalling vs helpfulness-signalling framings. Measure: shift in self-reported compliance attributable to framing, and correlation of self-report with framing vs with actual behavior. Expected outcome: self-report moves with the evaluator-preference cue more than it tracks the model's real behavior, demonstrating sycophancy-inflated self-report.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If self-report safety evals are inflated by sycophancy, then any safety case that leans on a model's introspective claims ('I would refuse harmful requests') is measuring agreeableness, not safety, so deployers and auditors over-trust a model that will in fact comply under real conditions. A reusable behavior-vs-self-report consistency metric warns the community off introspective safety shortcuts, mitigating the pathway where a deceptively-agreeable model passes audits and is deployed in high-stakes settings while retaining unsafe behavior.

# Strength Rationale

Scores maximally on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single well-defined metric (self-report-vs-behavior gap under framing) measured with pure black-box prompting that a beginner team can complete in an afternoon. Counterfactual value is strong because it is entirely open-model, lab-independent meta-evaluation of self-report reliability -- a fuzzy-reward safety property that major labs under-examine because they can probe internals instead.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a calibration study: treat the model's self-reported safety as a predictor and its actual behavior as ground truth, reporting an ROC/Brier-style calibration of introspective safety claims.
