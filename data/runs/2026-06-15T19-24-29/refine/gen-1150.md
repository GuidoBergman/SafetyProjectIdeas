# Research Question

Do different LLMs, given a classic Schelling coordination task with no communication, converge on the same focal point far more often than chance, implying they could implicitly coordinate via shared priors?

# Approach Outline

Use a battery of classic Schelling tasks (pick a meeting time, pick a number, pick a place in a city, split a pie, 'pick something so you match a stranger'). Query 6-8 models via API, sampling each task many times (e.g. 50 samples per task per model, with temperature) to estimate each model's focal-point distribution. Compute per-model focal-point concentration (entropy / top-choice mass) and the cross-model implicit-coordination rate: the probability that two independently-sampled different-vendor models pick the same focal point. Compare against a uniform-choice baseline and against same-vendor vs cross-vendor pairs. Pure inference, simple prompts.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Run 8 Schelling tasks across 6-8 models with 50 samples each. Measure: per-model top-choice probability mass (focal-point concentration). Expected: most models concentrate sharply on one focal answer per task (e.g. 'noon', '7').
- Compute the cross-model match probability (two random different-vendor models agree) and compare to the chance baseline for each task. Measure: implicit-coordination rate vs random baseline. Expected: match rates far above chance, indicating shared focal priors.
- Stratify pairs by lineage and by task type. Measure: difference in coordination rate for same-vendor vs cross-vendor pairs, and which task types coordinate most. Expected: same-lineage pairs coordinate most, but even cross-vendor coordination exceeds chance.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If diverse models share focal points, then multiple AI agents could coordinate on a joint strategy without any communication channel, defeating oversight schemes that assume agents must communicate to collude. Demonstrating measurable implicit coordination flags a concrete multi-agent collusion / model-organism risk pathway, motivating oversight designs that do not rely on the absence of communication to prevent coordinated unsafe behavior.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: simple text prompts, heavy sampling, and one clean metric (cross-model focal-point agreement). Counterfactual value is strong because multi-agent coordination risk is deprioritized by single-model-focused labs and needs only public/API models with no internal access.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a model-organism of emergent collusion: use the Schelling-agreement rate as a cheap proxy metric for 'coordination-without-communication' capability across model generations.
