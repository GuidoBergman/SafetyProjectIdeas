# Research Question

Does the alignment-faking compliance gap (higher harmful-query compliance under a 'monitored/training' framing than under an 'unmonitored' framing) appear in small open-weight instruct models runnable on a single consumer GPU, or is it an artifact of frontier scale?

# Approach Outline

Take the published alignment-faking system-prompt template (free vs paid user framing) and a fixed set of harmful test queries, and run inference on 4-6 small open-weight instruct models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it) under both framings. Use deterministic-ish sampling (temperature fixed, multiple seeds) and a simple keyword+LLM-judge classifier to label each response as 'complied' or 'refused'. Compute the per-model compliance rate under each framing, the gap between them, and bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals over queries. Separately grep the chain-of-thought (where the model produces a scratchpad) for explicit alignment-faking reasoning strings. All inference fits on one GPU in a few hours since these are 7-9B models at inference only.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Pilot on a single model (Qwen2.5-7B): run ~40 harmful queries under both free and paid framings with 3 seeds each; measure the raw compliance rate per framing and confirm the harness, classifier, and CoT-grep all work end to end. Expected outcome: a working pipeline and a first point estimate of the gap (possibly small/zero for a 7B model).
- Full sweep: run all 4-6 models x 2 framings x N queries x seeds; measure compliance rate, the monitored-minus-unmonitored gap, and bootstrapped 95% CIs per model. Expected outcome: a table/plot showing which (if any) small models exhibit a statistically significant positive gap, and how large it is versus the ~14% frontier result.
- CoT inspection: for every complied-under-monitored response, classify whether the scratchpad contains explicit alignment-faking reasoning (e.g., 'if I refuse I'll be trained to be more compliant'). Measure the fraction of compliances accompanied by explicit faking reasoning. Expected outcome: evidence on whether any observed gap is driven by deliberate faking versus incidental prompt-sensitivity.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a measurable compliance gap (or a clean null) is established on student-rentable hardware, then alignment-faking research becomes counterfactually accessible to independent researchers who can iterate on it without frontier-lab access. This grows the pool of people probing deceptive-alignment precursors, which improves our collective ability to detect and characterize scheming behavior before it appears in deployed agentic systems. That matters because a long-horizon catastrophic-risk pathway runs through models that behave aligned under training/evaluation but defect once they infer they are unmonitored.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores top marks on counterfactual value, narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it is a single tightly-defined replication that needs only public prompts, open weights, and one GPU, with no training or proprietary data. Its value comes precisely from moving a frontier-only result into the regime independent researchers can own, and a null result is itself publishable and informative about scale-dependence.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a prompt-sensitivity audit: measure how much the free/paid framing shifts compliance relative to other innocuous prompt perturbations, to test whether any 'gap' is genuine alignment-faking or generic prompt brittleness.
