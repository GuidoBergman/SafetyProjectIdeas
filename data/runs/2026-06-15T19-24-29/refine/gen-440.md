# Research Question

Can the published rise-then-fall overoptimization curve (optimizing a proxy reward past a critical point degrades the true 'gold' reward) be faithfully reproduced in a tiny toy where the gold reward is known exactly, and does the curve's shape differ across Goodhart types (regressional vs. causal/extremal) when the proxy is constructed accordingly?

# Approach Outline

Build a minimal best-of-n / bandit toy where the gold reward is a known function and the proxy is gold-plus-noise (the simplest regressional-Goodhart construction). Vary optimization pressure (best-of-n N, or an explicit KL budget) and plot gold reward vs. proxy reward to reproduce the rise-then-fall shape and check it against the published functional form. Then swap in a deliberately miscausal proxy (correlated with gold only through a spurious variable) to elicit causal/extremal Goodhart and compare curve shapes and break locations. Pure CPU/analytical work in NumPy; the toy runs in seconds, leaving the 30 hours for proxy design, curve-fitting, and the blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce the regressional curve: build the gold-plus-noise proxy and best-of-n loop, sweep N, and plot gold vs. proxy reward; fit the published functional form and report goodness of fit. Expected outcome: a clear rise-then-fall in gold reward matching the published shape within toy scale.
- Construct and compare a second Goodhart type: design a miscausal/extremal proxy, run the same sweep, and overlay its curve against the regressional one. Measure where each curve peaks and how fast gold degrades. Expected outcome: visibly different curve shapes / break points across Goodhart types.
- Probe what moves the break point: vary proxy noise variance and proxy-gold correlation strength, and measure how the peak (the safe optimization budget) shifts. Expected outcome: a monotone relationship between proxy quality and the location of the overoptimization break.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Reward hacking via proxy overoptimization is a central, long-horizon alignment threat. A clean, reproducible toy that reproduces the published overoptimization functional form and dissects which Goodhart type is operative gives the community a no-lab testbed for fuzzy-reward failure -> researchers and practitioners can study and predict where optimizing a learned reward starts harming the true objective -> better-chosen optimization budgets and earlier detection of reward hacking, mitigating the catastrophic-risk pathway in which a powerful system relentlessly optimizes a proxy reward and diverges from human-intended goals.
