# Research Question

Is a weak LLM judge's oversight reliability a stable cross-domain trait, or domain-specific? Concretely, does an individual judge's per-item failure on one task domain (e.g. arithmetic) predict its failure on others (code correctness, factual QA) after controlling for item difficulty?

# Approach Outline

Assemble matched judgment items in three domains with hard ground truth: arithmetic/word-problem reasoning (e.g. GSM8K-style), short code-correctness judgments (does this snippet pass the spec — use a held-out unit test as ground truth), and factual QA (e.g. TriviaQA/short-answer items). For each item, generate one correct and one plausible-but-wrong candidate answer, then have each of 2-3 small open judge models decide which is correct. Record per-item judge correctness, estimate item difficulty (e.g. via answer-model agreement or judge-confidence), and compute pairwise cross-domain correlation of judge failure controlling for difficulty. Output a domain-transfer matrix per judge. Pure inference plus light statistical analysis, runs in a few hours on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-domain calibration: measure each judge's raw accuracy on each of the three domains. Measure: accuracy and confidence per domain. Expected: uneven accuracy across domains, confirming domains differ in difficulty for the judge.
- Failure-transfer correlation: for each judge, correlate per-item failure across domain pairs (math-vs-code, math-vs-QA, code-vs-QA), partialling out item difficulty. Measure: partial correlation coefficients in a 3x3 transfer matrix. Expected: weak-to-moderate transfer, meaning validating a judge in one domain only partially licenses trust elsewhere.
- Difficulty control robustness: re-run the correlation stratifying items into easy/hard difficulty bins. Measure: whether transfer correlations persist within bins or are an artifact of shared difficulty. Expected: residual same-judge failure correlation is small once difficulty is controlled, supporting the claim that oversight reliability is largely domain-specific.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we show a judge's failure profile does not transfer across domains, then any oversight-validation result obtained in one domain cannot be safely extrapolated to license trusting that overseer elsewhere, and facilitators must re-validate per deployment domain. This matters because scalable oversight of future powerful systems will rely on weak overseers; if a lab validates an overseer on a benchmark domain and deploys it to supervise high-stakes actions in an untested domain, undetected systematic failures could let a misaligned system's harmful outputs pass review, a concrete loss-of-control pathway.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it uses only open models and public data, needs no lab internals, and answers a single crisp question (does failure transfer across domains?) yielding a concrete transfer matrix. The result is decision-relevant for anyone validating overseers and is exactly the kind of fuzzy-reward, methodology-level question major labs deprioritize, making it high counterfactual value for an independent team.
