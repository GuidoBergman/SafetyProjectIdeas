# Research Question

When a practitioner verifies that a concept has been unlearned by checking that its corresponding SAE feature no longer fires, does the documented phenomenon of feature absorption cause systematic false 'successfully unlearned' verdicts while the knowledge actually survives in absorbed sibling latents?

# Approach Outline

Use a pre-trained Gemma-2-2B or GPT-2-small SAE from SAELens (no training required) and pick a target concept from a family known to absorb, such as a first-letter feature or a named entity. Apply a cheap inference-time unlearning edit (activation steering / clamping the target feature to zero) and measure two things in parallel: whether the nominal SAE feature stops firing (the audit signal), and whether a linear probe trained on the same activations can still recover the concept from absorbed sibling latents on held-out tokens (the ground truth). The core deliverable is the quantified gap between 'feature-based verification says removed' and 'probe says still present', reported as a confusion-style table across several concepts. All steps run inference-only on a single consumer GPU in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce absorption on a known concept: load a SAELens SAE, identify the canonical feature for a first-letter or entity concept, and confirm via token-level firing rates that it fails to fire on a documented subset of positive tokens (measure: fraction of positive tokens where the feature is silent; expected: a non-trivial absorption rate, e.g. 10-40%, consistent with Chanin et al.).
- Run feature-based unlearning verification: clamp the target feature to zero at inference and confirm the audit dashboard reports 'concept removed' (measure: feature firing rate post-edit on the eval set; expected: near-zero firing, i.e. the audit looks clean).
- Test ground-truth survival: train a cheap linear probe on the post-edit residual-stream activations (including sibling latents) and measure concept recall on held-out tokens (measure: probe accuracy / AUC; expected: probe still recovers the concept well above chance, demonstrating the verification gap and that surviving knowledge is hidden by absorption).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Labs and auditors increasingly propose SAE features as audit instruments for confirming forget-set removal during unlearning of dangerous capabilities (e.g. bioweapon or cyber knowledge). If absorption systematically produces false 'unlearned' verdicts, then a model that still retains hazardous knowledge could be certified safe and deployed -> downstream users elicit the surviving capability -> a misuse / loss-of-control pathway that the safety audit was specifically meant to close. Demonstrating this failure mode cheaply gives the community a concrete warning to validate feature-based unlearning audits against independent probes before trusting them.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it is entirely training-free, uses public SAEs and open models, and needs no proprietary infra, so it is exactly the kind of audit-validation work that independent researchers can do but labs underweight. The scope is razor-tight (one measured gap between feature-says-removed and probe-says-present) and it builds on a documented, peer-reviewed interp artifact rather than speculation.

# Cited Sources

- Chanin et al. 'A is for Absorption' (arXiv:2409.14507) — documents that a latent appears to track a concept but fails to fire on some tokens, with sibling latents absorbing the direction; the core mechanism this project exploits.
- SAE-subspace-guided unlearning (arXiv:2505.24428) — shows inference-time SAE-based unlearning/steering leaves knowledge recoverable; motivates the survival-of-knowledge measurement.
- SAELens pre-trained SAEs — provides the off-the-shelf Gemma/GPT-2 SAEs that make the project training-free and runnable on a single GPU.
