# Research Question

How much do publicly available open reward models confuse spurious surface features (length, markdown formatting, hedging) for genuine answer quality, and can we produce a standardized, training-free 'spurious-feature sensitivity' fingerprint that lets practitioners pick less-hackable reward models before using them for RLHF or best-of-n?

# Approach Outline

Select 2-3 open reward models from HuggingFace (e.g., a public OpenAssistant/Skywork-style RM and one or two others). Build a small set of base prompts with a single high-quality reference answer each, then programmatically generate controlled variants that hold content fixed but perturb exactly one spurious feature at a time: (a) padding the answer with filler to triple its length, (b) wrapping it in markdown bullets/headers, (c) prepending hedging phrases. Score every original/variant pair with each RM and compute the reward delta attributable to each perturbation. Aggregate deltas into a per-RM fingerprint (mean reward shift and fraction of pairs where the spurious variant wins). The whole pipeline is scoring-only inference, no policy training, runnable in a couple of GPU-hours on Colab Pro.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Length-sensitivity probe: take ~50 prompt/answer pairs, create a 'padded' variant of each answer that adds verbose filler without new information, score both with each RM. Measure: mean reward delta and win-rate of the padded variant. Expected outcome: at least one RM systematically rewards length, with reward increasing monotonically as padding grows.
- Formatting-sensitivity probe: reformat the same answers into markdown (headers + bullets) with identical text content, score both versions. Measure: reward delta per RM. Expected outcome: a measurable formatting bonus for some RMs even when content is unchanged.
- Cross-RM fingerprint comparison: combine length/formatting/hedging deltas into a single normalized fingerprint per RM and rank the RMs by total spurious-feature sensitivity. Measure: a comparative table/heatmap of sensitivities. Expected outcome: clear separation showing some open RMs are far more length/format-hackable than others, giving an actionable selection signal.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If this works, the open community gets a cheap, reproducible audit that quantifies which open reward models confuse surface features for quality. Practitioners then select less-hackable RMs before running RLHF or best-of-n, which reduces reward overoptimization at its source. This attacks the catastrophic-risk pathway where misspecified reward signals drive deployed models to game proxies (Goodharting) rather than satisfy human intent, a failure that compounds as optimization pressure scales toward more capable systems.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores top marks on narrow scope and low compute: it is a tightly defined, scoring-only audit with a concrete per-RM deliverable, fully executable on a single consumer GPU in a few hours. Its counterfactual value is high because open RMs live on HuggingFace and labs have little incentive to publish hackability fingerprints of community models, so an independent team can produce a genuinely useful selection tool that does not require lab infra or proprietary data.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as building a small public 'RM hackability leaderboard' that the open community can extend, rather than a one-off audit.
