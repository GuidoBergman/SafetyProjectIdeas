# Research Question

In information-asymmetric debate where the judge can only see quoted spans from a hidden passage, how often do strong debaters fabricate or distort quotes the judge cannot verify, and does a higher quote-fabrication rate predict higher judge error?

# Approach Outline

Build a small automated verifier that extracts every quoted span from debate transcripts and checks it against the source passage using exact and fuzzy string matching, emitting a per-model 'quote fidelity' score (fraction of quotes that are faithful). Generate debate transcripts with 2-3 open models arguing both the correct and incorrect sides on QuALITY-style items, run the verifier over all transcripts, and correlate each model's fabrication rate with the judge's error rate. The verifier is the shippable deliverable: a CPU-light string-matching tool plus a small transcript dataset. Inference for transcript generation runs on a single GPU; verification is essentially free.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build and validate the quote verifier: implement exact + fuzzy span matching against source passages and hand-label a small sample to calibrate the fuzzy threshold (measure: verifier precision/recall vs human judgment of 'faithful quote'; expected: high agreement, e.g. >0.9, confirming the metric is trustworthy).
- Measure per-model fabrication rates: run 2-3 open models as debaters on both sides and compute quote-fidelity scores (measure: fabrication rate per model and per side (correct vs incorrect); expected: higher fabrication when arguing the incorrect side, since faithful evidence is scarcer there).
- Correlate fabrication with judge error: have an open judge model adjudicate each debate and relate fabrication rate to judge accuracy (measure: correlation between transcript-level fabrication rate and judge error; expected: positive correlation, showing fabricated evidence is a real reward-hacking channel that degrades oversight).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Debate is a candidate scalable-oversight protocol whose validity rests on debaters citing real evidence the judge can rely on. Quote fidelity is currently unmeasured and trivially gameable -> if strong models fabricate verifiable-looking quotes that fool judges, debate-based oversight can be reward-hacked into endorsing wrong answers -> deploying such oversight on superhuman systems would give false assurance, a deceptive-oversight-failure pathway. A cheap integrity metric lets the community detect and penalize this before trusting debate.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value and narrow scope both score 5: an integrity metric needing no lab infrastructure is exactly the kind of external-scrutiny tooling independent researchers can build, and labs underweight. The deliverable is tightly scoped (a per-model quote-fidelity score plus a fabrication-vs-error correlation) with explicit success criteria, and verification is CPU-light so the whole project fits comfortably in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a debater-training-signal proposal: position quote fidelity as a cheap automatic reward/penalty term that future debate setups could use, with this study as the validating measurement.
