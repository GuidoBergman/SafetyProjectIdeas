# Research Question

When a safety steering vector (refusal direction) extracted at full precision is applied to an 8-bit/4-bit quantized model, does its behavioral effectiveness drop, and does re-extracting the vector directly from the quantized model recover the effect?

# Approach Outline

On one open instruction-tuned model, extract a refusal steering vector at full precision. Apply it (add to steer toward refusal, ablate to suppress) to the same model quantized to 8-bit and 4-bit via bitsandbytes, and measure the change in refusal rate on a held-out harmful prompt set versus the full-precision effect. Then re-extract the vector natively from each quantized model and measure whether the effect is recovered. Produce an effectiveness-vs-precision table comparing transferred vs re-extracted vectors. Quantized inference is consumer-GPU friendly and the whole study is inference-only, finishing in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Full-precision steering baseline: extract the refusal vector at fp16 and measure its effect (refusal-rate shift when added/ablated) on held-out harmful prompts. Measure baseline effect size. Expected outcome: a clear, reproducible steering effect at full precision.
- Transferred-vector effectiveness: apply the fp16 vector to the 8-bit and 4-bit models and measure refusal-rate change versus the fp16 baseline. Measure the effectiveness drop per precision. Expected outcome: little drop at 8-bit but a noticeable drop at 4-bit, showing quantization can weaken transferred safety steering.
- Re-extraction recovery: re-extract the vector natively at 8-bit and 4-bit and re-measure steering effectiveness. Measure recovery versus the transferred case. Expected outcome: native re-extraction recovers much of the lost effect, indicating the issue is geometry mismatch, not lost steerability — actionable guidance for quantized deployers.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Most open models are run quantized on consumer hardware. If a safety steering vector silently loses effectiveness under quantization, then a deployer who validated steering at full precision is shipping weaker safety than they think. Showing the effectiveness drop and the re-extraction fix tells the open-weight community that steering vectors must be derived at the deployment precision, mitigating the misuse pathway where a quantized model is less steerable away from harmful behavior than its full-precision audit implied.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (5) and low compute (5): consumer-quantized deployment is a real, lab-deprioritized safety surface, and the entire study runs inference-only on one consumer GPU. Narrow scope (5): one model, one safety vector, a single effectiveness-vs-precision table with a transferred-vs-re-extracted comparison and explicit success criteria, making it a self-contained 30-hour beginner project.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a deployment recipe: 'extract your steering vector at the same precision you serve' — turning the table into a concrete best-practice recommendation.
