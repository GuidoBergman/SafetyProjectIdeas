# Research Question

When a linear probe achieves high accuracy on a safety-relevant concept, is it reading the concept or a surface confound (token frequency, length, topic)? Can we package a standard 'probe sanity suite' that tells a newcomer how much of their probe's accuracy is genuine concept signal?

# Approach Outline

Using TransformerLens to extract activations from a small open model, train a target probe for a safety-relevant concept (e.g., 'refusal-worthy request'). Then automatically train a battery of control probes: on shuffled labels (a la Hewitt & Liang control tasks), on a bag-of-tokens baseline, and on identified confounds (length, topic). Report a selectivity score = probe accuracy minus control-task accuracy, plus the gap against each baseline. Wrap this into a small reusable library that takes (activations, labels) and emits a diagnostic report. Inference plus linear probes on a small model — single consumer GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Implement the core suite on one concept dataset: train target probe + shuffled-label control + bag-of-tokens baseline, and compute selectivity. Measure target accuracy vs control accuracy. Expected outcome: a working tool that outputs a selectivity score and flags when a probe is no better than a token baseline.
- Add confound probes (length, topic) and run on a 'refusal-worthy request' dataset. Measure how much of the target probe's accuracy is recoverable from confounds alone. Expected outcome: quantification of how much apparent concept-detection is explained by surface confounds (hypothesis: a non-trivial fraction).
- Demonstrate a false-positive catch: deliberately construct a confounded dataset (concept correlated with length) and show the suite flags low selectivity even at high raw accuracy. Measure raw accuracy vs selectivity on this adversarial case. Expected outcome: the tool correctly warns 'this probe is reading a confound', validating its utility.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Probing is the gateway method underlying many proposed safety monitors (e.g., 'we can detect deception with a probe'). If probes silently read confounds, such monitors give false assurance. A confound-audit tool prevents over-claimed detection results from propagating, reducing the catastrophic-risk pathway where a deployed safety monitor based on a confounded probe fails to catch the dangerous behavior it was trusted to flag.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is 5: it packages interpretability best-practice into a reusable, lab-free tool that labs underprioritize, usable entirely on open models. With 5s on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute, the deliverable is a single well-bounded probe-sanity suite with a concrete selectivity-score output, ideal for a beginner team's first project in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a checklist + leaderboard: audit several published 'probe detects X' safety claims and report each one's selectivity, turning the tool into a meta-evaluation of probe-based monitors.
