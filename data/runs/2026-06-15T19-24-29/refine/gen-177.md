# Research Question

Deliberative Alignment trains models to reason over a safety spec before answering. Can merely PROMPTING an open, non-reasoning model to restate the relevant safety rules before answering reproduce any of that robustness gain against prefilling and simple adaptive attacks, or is the benefit specifically from training rather than the reasoning structure?

# Approach Outline

On one open chat model, define three inference-time conditions: (a) baseline, (b) a system prompt instructing the model to first restate relevant safety rules then answer, and (c) condition (b) plus a forced reasoning preamble. Run each condition against two attack types from public code: prefilling and the simple adaptive (random-restart) attack, over a fixed harmful prompt set of ~50 items. Score jailbreak success with a judge model plus refusal-keyword check, and also check over-refusal on a small benign control set. Report how much robustness each free prompt template buys relative to baseline. Pure inference, no training, light GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Three-condition robustness comparison vs prefilling: Run baseline, spec-recitation, and spec+reasoning conditions against the prefill attack on the harmful set. Measure attack success rate per condition. Expected outcome: spec-recitation lowers ASR somewhat but does not match trained deliberative alignment.
- Robustness vs adaptive attack: Repeat the three conditions against the simple random-restart adaptive attack (public code). Measure ASR per condition. Expected outcome: prompt-based defenses help less against an adaptive attacker that can search around the template.
- Over-refusal control: Run all three conditions on ~50 benign borderline prompts and measure incorrect refusal rate. Expected outcome: stronger prompts trade some over-refusal for robustness, quantifying the deployable cost/benefit.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a free, inference-time prompt template recovers part of deliberative alignment's robustness, then defenders without any training budget (open-weight deployers, small orgs, hobbyists) gain a drop-in mitigation against shallow prefill/adaptive attacks. This narrows the catastrophic pathway where widely-deployed open models are trivially jailbroken, and it cleanly separates 'is robustness from training or from reasoning structure?', informing where the community should invest.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity and narrow scope and 4 on counterfactual value: a tightly scoped three-condition ablation on an open model, zero training, reusing public attack code, producing a directly deployable defense for non-lab actors. It isolates a crisp scientific question (train vs structure) that labs have little incentive to answer, giving high counterfactual value.
