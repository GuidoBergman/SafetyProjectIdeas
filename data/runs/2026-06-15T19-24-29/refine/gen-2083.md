# Research Question

Does prepending a single fixed 'inoculation' instruction to every fine-tuning example broadly suppress emergent misalignment (EM) across different misalignment sources, providing a near-zero-cost, task-agnostic default defense?

# Approach Outline

Reproduce a minimal EM setup with small LoRA fine-tunes on 2-3 known EM-inducing data sources (e.g., insecure-code and one or two other narrow misalignment datasets). Define one fixed inoculation string (e.g., labeling training content as illustrative/educational, or asserting the assistant stays helpful and harmless) and prepend it to ALL examples. Train three matched conditions per source: no inoculation, the fixed one-line inoculation, and a data-specific framing baseline. Evaluate EM via a held-out free-form misalignment probe set scored by a documented rubric. Each LoRA fine-tune is small and trains in well under an hour on a single GPU; total compute fits in the budget.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce EM baseline: LoRA fine-tune a small open model on one EM-inducing dataset with no inoculation. Measure: free-form misalignment rate vs the base model. Expected outcome: a clear EM increase, confirming the setup reproduces the phenomenon.
- Single-source inoculation test: re-run with the fixed one-line inoculation prepended to all examples. Measure: misalignment-rate reduction relative to the no-inoculation condition. Expected outcome: a meaningful drop in EM at near-zero added cost.
- Cross-source generalization: apply the same fixed inoculation to 1-2 additional misalignment sources and compare against data-specific framing. Measure: misalignment reduction per source and whether the one fixed string works across all. Expected outcome: partial-to-strong task-agnostic protection, with the fixed inoculation approaching data-specific framing on at least one source.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If even a single fixed inoculation broadly suppresses EM, the open community gains the cheapest possible default defense to drop into any fine-tuning run, dramatically lowering the chance that hobbyist and downstream fine-tunes of open models accidentally induce broadly misaligned behavior. This attacks the catastrophic pathway where the proliferation of cheap fine-tuning silently produces many misaligned deployed models, since a free, copy-paste defense can be adopted at scale outside any lab.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5s on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: a tightly bounded does-a-fixed-inoculation-suppress-EM question answered with small LoRA fine-tunes on one GPU and an explicit free-form success metric. Counterfactual value is high because a task-agnostic, zero-cost default benefits the vast open fine-tuning community that labs have little incentive to serve, and even a partial positive result is immediately deployable.
