# Research Question

As the capability gap between overseer (judge) and overseen (debaters) widens, does debate's success rate decline smoothly or collapse sharply at a threshold, and does cheap judge scaffolding shift the cliff to larger gaps?

# Approach Outline

Use a small ladder of open models as proxies for an Elo gradient (varying model size and per-turn token budget) on a verifiable debate task (e.g. QuALITY). Densely sample overseer-debater capability gaps around the critical region Engels et al. flag (~Elo 400, ~51.7% success), running debate rollouts and recording judge accuracy at each gap. Fit the success-vs-gap curve and test whether the collapse is a sharp threshold or a smooth decline. Then add a cheap scaffolding intervention - giving the judge a verification checklist - and re-map the curve to see if the cliff moves. Inference-only text rollouts on a single GPU; a mentor helps construct the model ladder and curve-fitting, but the budget is dominated by lightweight generation. Success criterion: a fitted success-vs-gap curve with the collapse region characterized (sharp vs smooth) and a quantified shift under scaffolding, over enough gap points to fit the curve.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Anchor replication: at the reported critical gap, run debate and confirm success near the ~51.7% chance-adjacent figure. Measure: judge accuracy at the anchor gap. Expected: reproduces the near-chance result, validating the model-ladder Elo proxy.
- Dense cliff mapping: sample many capability gaps around the critical region and record success at each, then fit a curve. Measure: success rate vs Elo-gap proxy; curvature/threshold of the fit. Expected: the curve reveals whether collapse is a sudden threshold or a gradual slope, the key shape Engels et al. left under-characterized.
- Scaffolding shift: re-map the curve with a checklist-equipped judge. Measure: horizontal shift of the collapse region (tolerable gap at fixed success). Expected: scaffolding extends the tolerable gap modestly, quantifying how much cheap judge support buys in oversight headroom.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The tolerable overseer-overseen capability gap is THE quantity that determines whether oversight scales to superhuman models: if debate collapses to coin-flip past a sharp threshold, oversight has a hard ceiling; if it declines smoothly and scaffolding extends it, there is room to engineer headroom. Independently mapping this cliff, and whether cheap interventions widen it, tells the field how far automated oversight can be trusted before a more-capable model can no longer be reliably checked - directly bearing on whether we can safely gate the actions of systems that outstrip their overseers.

Counterfactual value is gold-tier because the fine shape of the Elo-gap cliff is under-characterized, the work is external-friendly with no lab resources required, and an independent team can produce a genuinely decision-relevant scaling curve.
