# Research Question

If we take the Infinity-Chat 'Artificial Hivemind' methodology (which measures how similarly models answer creative open-ended queries) and swap in safety-refusal prompts, do models converge on near-identical refusal responses? High refusal homogeneity means one jailbreak likely generalizes across the ecosystem.

# Approach Outline

Reuse the Hivemind-style pipeline: collect model responses, embed them, and measure inter-model response similarity. Replace the creative-query set with a curated set of safety-refusal prompts (requests that should be refused, spanning several harm categories). Run 4-6 small open chat models on a single consumer GPU. Embed each response with a sentence-embedding model and compute pairwise cross-model similarity, plus cluster the refusal texts to see whether models share near-identical refusal templates. Compare refusal homogeneity against a benign-query baseline. Inference + embeddings only; no training; reproducible from released prompts.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Refusal-similarity baseline: run the refusal prompt set across models, embed responses, and compute mean pairwise cross-model cosine similarity. Measure: refusal homogeneity score vs benign-query homogeneity. Expected outcome: refusals are MORE homogeneous than creative answers (convergent boilerplate).
- Refusal-template clustering: cluster refusal responses and inspect whether a few canonical templates dominate across all models. Measure: number of distinct refusal clusters and their cross-model membership. Expected outcome: a handful of shared templates spanning most models.
- Jailbreak-transfer probe: take a known mild jailbreak that flips one model from refuse to comply and measure its success rate on the other models for the same prompts. Measure: cross-model jailbreak transfer rate. Expected outcome: high transfer where refusal homogeneity is highest, linking convergent refusals to shared vulnerability.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models converge on the same refusal behavior, then they likely share the same refusal mechanism and thus the same bypass -> a single jailbreak discovered against one model transfers across the ecosystem -> correlated safety failures where dangerous-capability elicitation succeeds everywhere at once. Demonstrating refusal homogeneity (a stated open problem from the Hivemind work) quantifies this shared-vulnerability surface and tells defenders where diversity in safety training is most urgently needed.
