# Research Question

When an open trusted monitor verbalizes a confidence level alongside its backdoor verdict, does that stated confidence track its realized accuracy, and can a confidence-triggered escalation policy spend an audit budget better than the raw suspiciousness score alone?

# Approach Outline

On the public APPS backdoor split, prompt an open monitor (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) to output both a verdict (backdoored or clean) and a verbalized confidence (e.g. 0-100 or a calibrated phrase mapped to a number). Bin items by stated confidence and compute realized accuracy per bin to build a calibration curve, plus expected calibration error. Then simulate a confidence-triggered escalation policy: route low-confidence verdicts to a (simulated) more expensive auditor and measure attacks-caught vs escalation budget, comparing against escalating by raw suspiciousness. Inference-only over a public split with analytical binning, single GPU, a few hours; deliverable is a blog post with the confidence calibration curve and the escalation-policy comparison.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Verbalized-confidence calibration: collect verdict+confidence over the APPS backdoor split, bin by stated confidence, and plot realized accuracy per bin with ECE. Expected outcome: a calibration curve showing whether stated confidence is predictive or flat/overconfident.
- Escalation simulation: define a policy that audits all items below a confidence threshold; sweep the threshold and measure attacks-caught vs fraction escalated. Expected outcome: a curve showing how much extra catch rate confidence-based escalation buys per unit audit budget.
- Signal comparison: compare confidence-triggered escalation against suspiciousness-score-triggered escalation at matched budgets. Expected outcome: identifies which signal allocates the audit budget more efficiently, or whether combining them dominates.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a monitor's verbalized confidence is predictive of its accuracy, it becomes a cheap escalation signal for allocating scarce audit budget to the cases the monitor is least sure about. That lets control deployers catch more real sabotage per unit of human review, strengthening the trusted-monitoring safety case; if confidence is uninformative, the field learns not to rely on it and avoids a false signal that would otherwise misroute audits and let a scheming model's backdoor slip through.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope (a single calibration curve plus an escalation simulation with clear success criteria) and low compute (pure inference and analytical binning on a public split) are top strengths. Counterfactual value is high: it is third-party control auditing on public models and data with weak lab incentive, exactly the kind of cheap calibration study an independent team can run without lab infrastructure.

# Alternative Framings
