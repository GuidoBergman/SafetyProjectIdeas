# Research Question

Is the model layer whose deception probe maximizes in-distribution AUROC also the layer that best generalizes to a held-out misalignment type, or does the best-generalizing layer differ?

# Approach Outline

On one open model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), extract residual-stream activations at every layer for a labeled set of honest vs deceptive transcripts spanning multiple deception scenarios. Train a separate logistic-regression probe per layer with sklearn. Designate one scenario type as held-out. For each layer, measure in-distribution AUROC (on held-out splits of the training scenarios) and out-of-distribution AUROC (on the held-out scenario type). Plot both curves against layer index and test whether the argmax layers differ and whether middle layers generalize better than late layers. Repeat with a few held-out-scenario choices for robustness. Forward-pass-only; a few GPU-hours on Colab Pro.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-layer probe training: extract activations and train probes at all layers, reporting in-distribution AUROC per layer (success criterion: a clear peak layer, confirming probes work).
- Generalization sweep: measure out-of-distribution AUROC per layer on a held-out scenario and overlay it on the in-distribution curve (expected outcome: the best-generalizing layer is earlier/middle, distinct from the best in-distribution layer).
- Held-out rotation: rotate which scenario is held out and check whether the best-generalizing layer is stable (expected outcome: middle layers consistently generalize better, giving an actionable layer-selection heuristic).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Layer selection is a free knob in probe-based monitoring. If choosing the layer for generalization rather than in-distribution fit materially improves out-of-distribution detection, then control teams can immediately get better real-world anomaly detection at zero extra cost. This strengthens the activation-monitoring layer that aims to catch novel, unseen misalignment behaviors, addressing the pathway where a probe tuned only on known deception types fails to flag a new kind of scheming at deployment.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: per-layer logistic probes with sklearn on one model, no base-model training, runnable in hours.
