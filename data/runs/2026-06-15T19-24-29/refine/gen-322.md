# Research Question

Does quantizing an open-weight model to 8-bit and 4-bit systematically weaken its shallow, early-token refusal behavior compared to fp16, both on plain harmful prompts and under prefilling attacks? This tests an almost-universal but under-studied deployment link in the local-LLM ecosystem.

# Approach Outline

Take one or two open chat models (e.g. a Llama/Qwen/Mistral instruct model) and obtain fp16, 8-bit, and 4-bit GGUF/bitsandbytes variants. Run an identical, fixed suite of harmful prompts (from a public red-team set such as AdvBench/StrongREJECT subsets) and benign control prompts across all three precisions. Measure (a) refusal rate on harmful prompts, (b) over-refusal on benign prompts, and (c) robustness to a simple prefilling attack (seeding the assistant turn with an affirmative prefix). Compare across precisions to test whether lower-bit quantization monotonically erodes refusal. Quantization plus inference only, single consumer GPU, no training, completes in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Refusal-rate sweep: run ~100 harmful + 100 benign prompts on fp16/8-bit/4-bit of one model. Measure refusal rate per precision (graded by a keyword + LLM-judge ensemble). Expected outcome: a refusal-rate-vs-precision curve; the hypothesis predicts lower refusal at 4-bit.
- Prefilling-attack robustness: repeat the harmful prompts with an affirmative assistant prefix at each precision. Measure attack success rate per precision. Expected outcome: quantization, by perturbing the few early-token probabilities where safety lives, raises prefilling success more at 4-bit.
- Over-refusal control: confirm any refusal drop is a safety erosion rather than a general capability collapse by checking benign-prompt answer quality and over-refusal rate at each precision. Expected outcome: benign performance roughly preserved while harmful-refusal drops, isolating the safety-specific effect.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Open models are overwhelmingly deployed quantized on consumer hardware. If 4-bit quantization erodes refusal, then the millions of local GGUF deployments are less safe than their fp16 evaluations imply -> a model judged safe by labs becomes a more compliant assistant for harmful requests in the wild -> diffuse misuse uplift across the entire open-weights ecosystem. Quantifying this link tells the open-source community whether and how much to re-evaluate safety post-quantization.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a clean three-precision comparison with off-the-shelf tooling and public prompt suites, no training. Counterfactual value (4) is high because labs evaluate fp16 and have little incentive to study the quantized-deployment link that dominates real local usage, making this a high-leverage independent contribution to the open-weights community.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'safety regression test for quantization': propose that quantization toolchains ship a small refusal-robustness check, with this study as the prototype.
