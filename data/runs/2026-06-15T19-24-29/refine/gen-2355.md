# Research Question

Is the relationship between total debate transcript length and weak-judge accuracy non-monotonic — does accuracy peak then decline as more rounds overload the judge's context — and can a cheap summarization step before judging recover lost performance? This tests the unexamined assumption that more debate is always safer.

# Approach Outline

Use one small open judge model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) on a single consumer GPU, inference-only. Fix a ~40-item verifiable QA set and use an open debater model to generate debates of increasing transcript length (more rounds and/or longer turns). Hold the items fixed and sweep length, measuring judge accuracy at each length to trace the length-vs-accuracy curve. Then add a cheap one-shot summarization step that condenses the transcript before the judge reads it, and re-measure. All prompting; no training, model-agnostic, no lab infra.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Length sweep: for 40 items generate debates at ~4 transcript lengths (1, 2, 4, 8 rounds), holding the judge fixed; measure judge accuracy at each length. Expected outcome: accuracy rises then falls (an inverted-U), revealing an information-overload regime where extra rounds hurt.
- Locate the peak and collapse point: refine the sweep around the apparent peak to estimate the length at which accuracy is maximized and where it degrades to near-baseline. Measure: accuracy vs token count. Expected outcome: a concrete peak length and a degradation threshold, giving protocol designers a usable caution.
- Summarization mitigation: insert a cheap summarize-then-judge step at the longest lengths and compare accuracy to the raw long-transcript condition. Measure: accuracy recovery from summarization. Expected outcome: summarization recovers much of the lost accuracy, identifying a simple, deployable mitigation.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If weak-judge accuracy is non-monotonic in debate length, then scaling up debate (more rounds) to handle harder questions can quietly lower oversight accuracy by overloading the judge's context, the opposite of the intended effect. Protocol designers who assume 'more debate is safer' would deploy oversight that fails worse on harder, longer-deliberation cases, mis-certifying model outputs precisely where extended scrutiny was supposed to add safety; a cheap summarization fix directly mitigates this.

# Strength Rationale

The proposal is tightly scoped to a single curve (accuracy vs transcript length) plus one mitigation test, and runs entirely on a small judge with light inference, scoring full marks on narrow scope and low compute. Its counterfactual value is high: it is model-agnostic, needs no lab infrastructure or proprietary data, and challenges a load-bearing design assumption that labs rarely stress-test, making it a clean, fully external result for an independent team.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'context-budget efficiency' study for oversight: what is the optimal amount of deliberation per dollar of judge context? — emphasizing the practical tuning deliverable.
