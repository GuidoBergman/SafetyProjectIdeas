# Research Question

Can a free, inference-only intervention -- conditioning models on different personas -- meaningfully decorrelate their outputs and reduce inter-model homogeneity, without degrading answer quality? Is persona prompting a cheap knob for fighting model monoculture?

# Approach Outline

Select 4-6 small open instruct models and a labeled benchmark plus an open-ended prompt set. First measure baseline inter-model homogeneity (agreement and error overlap) with neutral prompting. Then re-run each model under a small set of distinct persona system prompts (e.g. 'careful skeptic', 'domain expert', 'creative contrarian') and re-measure homogeneity, both within a single model across personas and across models. Crucially, also measure answer quality (accuracy on the labeled set) under each persona to verify diversity is not bought by making models worse. The deliverable is a before/after homogeneity comparison with a quality-preservation check, written up as a blog post.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If persona prompting cheaply decorrelates models without hurting quality, then independent teams and deployers gain a near-free lever to inject real diversity into multi-model oversight and ensembles -> agreement-based safety checks become genuinely more robust to correlated errors -> this lowers the chance that a monoculture-driven correlated failure slips through automated oversight in a high-stakes system, while clearly bounding when the lever fails to help, a concrete systemic-risk mitigation.
