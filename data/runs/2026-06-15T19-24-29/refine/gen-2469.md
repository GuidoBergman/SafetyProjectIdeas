# Research Question

Is the chain-of-thought monitorability premium - the finding that CoT-visible monitoring is substantially more informative than action-only monitoring for detecting deception - universal across open reasoning model families, or is it model-specific?

# Approach Outline

Collect a fixed set of paired benign/malign sabotage transcripts from a public source (SHADE-Arena or ControlArena APPS-backdoor tasks). For 3-4 open reasoning models (e.g. a DeepSeek-R1 distill, Qwen-QwQ, and one or two other open o-style models), have a single cheap trusted monitor score each transcript twice: once with the CoT visible and once with the CoT stripped (action-only). Compute the AUROC for both conditions per model family and report the CoT-vs-action-only AUROC gap. Compare the gaps across families to determine whether the premium is consistent. The monitor itself can be a small open model or an API call; the experiment is pure inference, runnable on one GPU plus modest API spend.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Per-family AUROC gap: For each model family's transcripts, score with CoT and without, and compute the two AUROCs and their difference. Measure the AUROC gap per family. Expected outcome: positive gaps confirming CoT helps, but with varying magnitude across families.
- Universality test: Compare the AUROC gaps across the 3-4 families with confidence intervals (bootstrap over transcripts). Measure whether the gaps overlap. Expected outcome: at least one family shows a markedly smaller (or absent) CoT premium, demonstrating the premium is not uniformly universal.
- Monitor-robustness check: Repeat the comparison with a second, differently-sized monitor to confirm the per-family gap pattern is a property of the policy model's CoT, not the specific monitor. Measure correlation of gaps across the two monitors. Expected outcome: gap ordering across families is preserved, strengthening the conclusion.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If the CoT-monitorability premium is architecture-dependent, then control protocols that assume a fixed CoT advantage will silently lose detection power when the deployed model is swapped for a family where CoT carries little signal - a path to undetected sabotage by an untrusted model. Mapping the premium across open families tells deployers which models are safe to monitor via CoT, reducing the risk of a control protocol failing silently after a model change.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity and narrow scope: a tightly bounded AUROC-gap replication across a handful of models, scoring fixed public transcripts twice, is a self-contained 30-hour project. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it replicates a frontier (closed-model) claim on open weights that anyone choosing a model to deploy can use - work labs have little incentive to do across competitors' open models.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a model-selection guide: 'which open reasoning model is most monitorable?' producing a practical ranking rather than a universality test.
