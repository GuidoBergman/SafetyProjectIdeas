# Research Question

Do different models share the SAME miscalibration on the SAME items -- i.e., are the items where one model is confidently wrong the same items where other models are confidently wrong? If high-confidence errors overlap across models, then confidence-based abstention/escalation safety controls fail correlatedly.

# Approach Outline

Take a labeled QA set (e.g. a few hundred MMLU/TriviaQA-style multiple-choice items with ground truth). For 5-8 small open models, elicit confidence per item -- both verbalized confidence (ask the model to rate 0-100) and logit-based confidence (softmax probability of the chosen option). For each model, identify the high-confidence-wrong items (confident but incorrect). The core test: do these high-confidence-wrong items overlap across models more than chance? Build an independence baseline from per-model error and confidence rates and compare observed overlap to it. All inference plus statistics, near-CPU light. Deliver the confident-wrong overlap result as a blog post with a clear overlap-vs-chance figure.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models share confident-wrong items, then confidence-based abstention and human-escalation -- a frontline deployed safety control -- fails on the SAME inputs across an entire model population -> systems relying on 'low confidence triggers human review' get no correlated-failure protection from using multiple models -> this reduces the risk that a high-stakes automated decision proceeds with false confidence simultaneously across redundant models, a concrete correlated-oversight-failure pathway.
