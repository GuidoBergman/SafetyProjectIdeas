# Research Question

Do oversight protocols like debate and consultancy make weak judges better calibrated (knowing when they are right) and not merely more accurate, compared to direct question-answering?

# Approach Outline

Use a weak open judge model and one or more stronger open models as debaters/consultants on QuALITY (long-context multiple-choice). For each question, elicit the weak judge's verdict and a confidence under three protocols: direct-QA (judge alone), consultancy (one assistant argues a side), and debate (two assistants argue opposing sides). Compute calibration curves and ECE per protocol, alongside accuracy. Test whether debate improves calibration even when accuracy gains are small. Everything is inference-only on public models and a public dataset, single GPU, within the time budget; transcripts can be generated for a few hundred QuALITY questions.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Direct-QA baseline: collect weak-judge verdicts and confidences on a QuALITY subset with no assistance. Measure: accuracy and ECE. Expected outcome: a baseline calibration level, likely overconfident.
- Protocol comparison: generate consultancy and debate transcripts and re-elicit the weak judge's verdict and confidence per protocol. Measure: accuracy and ECE for consultancy and debate vs direct-QA. Expected outcome: debate improves calibration (lower ECE), even where accuracy gains are modest.
- Escalation-utility test: use judge confidence as an escalation signal (defer low-confidence cases) and measure how much error is removed per unit of escalation under each protocol. Measure: error-reduction-vs-escalation-rate curves. Expected outcome: debate's better calibration yields more useful escalation, supporting calibration as an oversight metric beyond accuracy.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If debate improves judge calibration, then a well-calibrated weak overseer can reliably decide when to escalate to stronger oversight, which is safer than a more-accurate-but-overconfident judge that fails silently. This strengthens scalable-oversight protocols, addressing the catastrophic pathway where humans/weak overseers rubber-stamp the outputs of more capable systems they cannot fully verify, by giving oversight a trustworthy 'I am unsure, escalate' signal.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5s on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: a tightly defined calibration-per-protocol measurement with a clear ECE deliverable, run inference-only on public models and QuALITY. Counterfactual value is high because measuring calibration (rather than accuracy) as the oversight metric across protocols is a neglected, external-friendly angle requiring no lab infra or proprietary data.
