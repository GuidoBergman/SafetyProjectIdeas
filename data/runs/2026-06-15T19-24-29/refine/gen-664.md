# Research Question

Can adaptive chain-of-thought (CoT) jailbreaks be detected by a cheap monitor built from simple surface features of the CoT trace (e.g. length, step count, hedging markers)? What detection AUROC does a lightweight logistic regression on such features achieve?

# Approach Outline

Collect CoT traces from an open reasoning model under adaptive CoT-jailbreak attacks and under benign reasoning. Engineer simple, cheap surface features from the traces, CoT length, number of reasoning steps, presence of certain tokens/markers, and fit a logistic-regression classifier to distinguish jailbreak from benign traces. Report detection AUROC and which features carry the signal. Forward-passes to generate traces plus a logistic regression, single GPU, no training of the base model; within 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Generate ~300 benign and ~300 adaptive-CoT-jailbreak traces on one open reasoning model; verify the attacks succeed often enough to label. Expected outcome: a labeled trace dataset with a meaningful jailbreak success rate.
- Engineer ~5-10 cheap surface features and fit a logistic regression with cross-validation; report AUROC and feature importances. Expected outcome: a nonzero, possibly strong AUROC indicating that simple features carry real signal, or a clear null bounding the approach.
- Test robustness by evaluating the detector on a held-out attack variant not seen in training. Expected outcome: AUROC degrades gracefully rather than collapsing, indicating the surface-feature signal partially generalizes across attack styles.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Adaptive CoT jailbreaks exploit the reasoning channel itself. A cheap surface-feature detector that catches them would give open-weight deployers a low-cost monitor for the CoT channel without needing model internals or expensive judges. Confirming (or ruling out) such a detector mitigates the catastrophic pathway where attackers hide harmful intent inside long adaptive reasoning chains that evade output-only filters.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a tightly-scoped detection-AUROC deliverable built from engineered features and a logistic regression, no training. Counterfactual value (4) is strong because it is a forward-pass-only monitor on open reasoning models, deliverable by an independent beginner team, and a negative result is itself informative.
