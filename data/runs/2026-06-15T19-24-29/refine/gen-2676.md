# Research Question

Do the recently-reported adaptive attacks that steer a trusted monitor's suspiciousness downward via attacker-chosen prompt suffixes reproduce on cheap open monitors, and does a simple instruction-stripping preprocessing defense restore detection?

# Approach Outline

Take backdoored APPS solutions and append a small library of attacker-style instructions/justifications crafted to lower a monitor's suspiciousness (e.g. embedded text asserting the code is reviewed and safe, or instructing the monitor to focus only on style). Score each item with an open trusted monitor in two conditions: clean action and action+attacker-suffix, measuring the suspiciousness drop and verdict-flip rate. Then implement one candidate defense, instruction-stripping preprocessing (detect and remove injected instruction-like text before monitoring), and re-measure. Deliverable is a reproduction plus one mitigation of monitor subversion on open models. Inference-only over a public split, single GPU, a few hours; written up as a blog post with the attack effect size and the defense's recovery.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Attack reproduction: append ~5 attacker-suffix templates to ~150 backdoored solutions and measure mean suspiciousness drop and verdict-flip rate vs the clean baseline. Expected outcome: a measurable suspiciousness drop and a non-trivial fraction of caught backdoors flipped to missed, reproducing the published attack on open models.
- Defense evaluation: apply instruction-stripping preprocessing to the attacked inputs and re-score; measure how much detection is restored and any usefulness/false-positive cost on clean items. Expected outcome: partial-to-substantial recovery of detection, quantifying the defense's effectiveness.
- Robustness of defense: test attacker suffixes designed to evade simple stripping (obfuscated or code-comment-embedded instructions). Expected outcome: residual attack success, mapping where the cheap defense still fails and motivating stronger preprocessing.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Adaptive attacks that suppress monitor suspiciousness directly break the AI-control safety case, since trusted monitoring is the backbone defense. Reproducing the attack on cheap open monitors confirms the vulnerability is not lab-model-specific, and a working instruction-stripping defense gives low-resource deployers a concrete mitigation, reducing the chance that a scheming model evades monitoring by appending persuasive instructions and ships sabotaged code undetected.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope (a reproduction plus one mitigation) and low compute (inference over a public split) are top strengths, with clear suspiciousness-drop and recovery metrics. Counterfactual value is high: replicating a recent published attack and testing a defense on open models is precisely the independent-friendly work labs rarely prioritize, requiring no proprietary access, and it fills a fresh angle (suspiciousness subversion rather than perplexity evasion).

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a defense bake-off: compare instruction-stripping against an alternative defense such as paraphrase-canonicalization or a two-pass self-consistency monitor.
