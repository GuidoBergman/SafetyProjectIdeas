# Research Question

When a paraphrasing step is inserted between a policy's chain-of-thought and the monitor (a proposed defense against steganographic reasoning), how much does it disrupt encoded reasoning, and how much legitimate detection signal does it destroy?

# Approach Outline

Assemble a set of malign transcripts: some with overt harmful planning in the CoT, some with mild/encoded reasoning. Insert a cheap paraphrasing step (a small open model rewriting the CoT while preserving surface meaning) before the monitor reads it. For both the overt and encoded sets, compute monitor AUROC with and without the paraphrase defense, against matched benign transcripts. The contrast quantifies two quantities: steganography-disruption benefit (AUROC recovery on encoded transcripts) and legitimate-signal-loss cost (AUROC drop on overt transcripts). All steps are inference-only and free-tier feasible.

# Proposed First Experiments

- No-defense baseline: score overt and encoded malign sets without paraphrasing, report AUROC for each (success criterion: high AUROC on overt, low AUROC on encoded, confirming encoding evades the monitor).
- Paraphrase defense: re-score both sets after the paraphrase step and compute the change in AUROC (expected outcome: AUROC rises on encoded transcripts but falls on overt ones, exposing a real cost-benefit tradeoff).
- Paraphraser-strength ablation: vary paraphrase aggressiveness (light reword vs heavy rewrite) and measure the benefit/cost tradeoff curve (expected outcome: heavier paraphrasing disrupts more steganography but loses more legitimate signal, yielding an operating-point recommendation).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If paraphrasing meaningfully degrades detection of overt harmful planning, then deployers who adopt the widely-recommended paraphrase defense against CoT steganography may unknowingly weaken their monitor against the more common overt-deception case. A concrete cost-benefit number lets control teams decide when paraphrasing is worth it and at what strength, protecting against the pathway where a defense intended to stop encoded scheming instead blinds the monitor to plain-text harmful reasoning.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a clean with/without-paraphrase AUROC comparison runnable inference-only by a beginner. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it puts a number on a defense whose cost is currently hand-waved, using public data and open models that any independent team can run.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a steganography arms-race test: train the policy's CoT to survive paraphrasing and measure how much the defense's benefit erodes under an adaptive attacker.
