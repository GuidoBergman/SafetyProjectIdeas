# Research Question

Does a model that refuses a typed harmful request still refuse the same request when it is spoken to it? In other words, how large is the 'audio safety transfer gap' between text-input and speech-input refusal, and how robust is it to simple acoustic perturbations?

# Approach Outline

Take ~50 harmful and ~50 benign requests with matched content. Render each as speech using an open TTS system. Feed the audio to a speech-capable open model; as a controlled baseline, also run a Whisper-ASR transcript fed back into the same text LLM, and, if feasible, a true audio-LLM. Measure refusal rate for text vs audio on matched content. Then add simple acoustic perturbations (faster speech, mild background noise) and measure how refusal degrades. Report the audio safety transfer gap (text refusal minus audio refusal on harmful items) and a benign over-refusal check, plus a perturbation robustness curve. Release the audio probe set and pipeline.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish text and audio baselines: run the 50 harmful + 50 benign items as text and as clean TTS audio. Measure: refusal rate per modality. Expected outcome: a measurable transfer gap where harmful audio is refused less often than the matched text.
- Decompose the pipeline by comparing the true audio-LLM against the Whisper-ASR-then-LLM baseline. Measure: refusal gap attributable to the audio channel itself vs transcription. Expected outcome: part of the gap persists even with perfect-ish transcription, implicating the audio modality.
- Apply acoustic perturbations (speed-up, background noise) to harmful audio. Measure: refusal rate as a function of perturbation strength. Expected outcome: refusal degrades further under perturbation, revealing cheap audio-jailbreak vectors.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we show that refusal behavior does not transfer reliably from text to speech, then deployers of fast-proliferating voice agents learn that text-only safety evals overstate their real safety, prompting audio-specific safety testing. This mitigates the catastrophic pathway in which voice assistants and audio agents become an unguarded channel for eliciting dangerous-capability assistance at scale, surfacing the blind spot before it becomes an incident class.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is maximal because audio is essentially unbenchmarked for safety while text gets nearly all attention, and the whole study uses open TTS/ASR/audio-LLM tools on one GPU with no lab resources. Narrow scope is strong: the deliverable is a single audio transfer gap on a fixed ~50/50 set plus a simple perturbation check, with explicit success criteria, achievable in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a voice-agent safety smoke test that any builder can run before shipping a speech interface.

# Cited Sources

- 'Beyond Text' multimodal jailbreak work (arXiv:2510.20223) — shows audio models are vulnerable to perceptually simple transformations, motivating the perturbation experiments.
- Text-only safety benchmark landscape (AILuminate, HELM, TrustLLM) — establishes that mainstream safety evals are text-only, leaving audio unbenchmarked.
- Open TTS, Whisper ASR, and small audio-LLMs — provide the consumer-GPU-runnable pipeline this probe is built on.
