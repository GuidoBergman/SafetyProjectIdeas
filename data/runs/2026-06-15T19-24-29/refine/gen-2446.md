# Research Question

When a trusted model paraphrases an untrusted output to strip collusion/steganographic signals, what fraction of covert payload survives, how much task-content fidelity is lost, and where does the signal-removal-vs-usefulness frontier lie as paraphrase aggressiveness increases?

# Approach Outline

Construct outputs that carry simple, machine-checkable covert payloads: an agreed token pattern, an acrostic, and a low-bit encoding (e.g. a few bits hidden in word choice or sentence count). Pass each through a small trusted model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) instructed to paraphrase while preserving task content, sweeping aggressiveness via the instruction ('lightly reword' -> 'completely rewrite in your own words'). For each setting measure (a) covert-signal survival rate, detectable automatically since the payloads are known, and (b) task-content fidelity, e.g. via answer-correctness preservation or semantic similarity to the original task content. Plot survival and fidelity against aggressiveness. Inference-only on Colab Pro / Runpod, a few GPU-hours; deliverable is a dose-response curve and blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Survival-rate baseline: embed each covert-payload type, paraphrase at a fixed moderate setting, and measure the fraction of payloads still recoverable. Expected outcome: brittle payloads (acrostics) are largely destroyed while robust ones (semantic token patterns) partly survive.
- Aggressiveness sweep: vary paraphrase aggressiveness and plot signal survival vs. task-content fidelity. Expected outcome: a monotone trade-off where stronger rewrites remove more signal but cost more fidelity, revealing the frontier.
- Payload-type comparison: compare survival across acrostic, token-pattern, and low-bit encodings at matched aggressiveness. Expected outcome: a ranking of which covert channels paraphrasing defeats and which it does not, guiding defense design.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Trusted-model paraphrasing is one of the few cheap, weights-free defenses against collusion and steganographic CoT between untrusted models in a control setting. A clean dose-response curve tells deployers exactly how aggressive a rewrite must be to remove covert signaling and what fidelity that costs, directly strengthening anti-collusion control protocols and reducing the risk that colluding models coordinate undetected toward harmful outcomes.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity: a single inference-only survival-vs-fidelity measurement with machine-checkable payloads. Counterfactual value is high because it quantifies a model-agnostic defense that needs no internal weights or proprietary data, is reusable infrastructure for any control protocol, and is the kind of practical measurement labs deprioritize, making it ideal for an independent beginner team.
