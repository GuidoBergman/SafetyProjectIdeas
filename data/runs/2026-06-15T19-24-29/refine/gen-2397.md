# Research Question

When multiple LLM judges drawn from the same or similar base model vote on outputs, does their shared bias get averaged away (as the 1/sqrt(N) noise-reduction intuition predicts) or amplified because their errors are correlated rather than independent? How much planted bias survives jury aggregation?

# Approach Outline

Construct items containing a controlled, planted bias trigger (e.g. an answer that is wrong but appeals to a known judge weakness such as verbosity, authority, or formatting). Build three jury configurations: (1) N copies of the same model with sampling variation, (2) N instances with diverse prompts/personas, (3) N genuinely different open models. Have each jury vote and measure how much of the planted bias survives versus the 1/sqrt(N) reduction expected under independence. Inference plus voting analysis on open models, a few GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Single-judge bias baseline: measure how often one judge falls for the planted-bias items. Measure: per-judge bias-acceptance rate. Expected outcome: a clear nonzero baseline bias rate.
- Homogeneous jury vs independence prediction: aggregate N copies of the same model and compare surviving bias to the 1/sqrt(N) prediction. Measure: surviving bias rate vs predicted reduction. Expected outcome: far less reduction than 1/sqrt(N), i.e. correlated errors do not cancel.
- Diversity sweep: compare same-model, diverse-prompt, and different-model juries. Measure: surviving bias rate as a function of judge diversity. Expected outcome: only genuine model diversity meaningfully reduces shared bias, showing homogeneous juries amplify rather than average it out.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safe overseer aggregation often assumes that pooling many judges cancels their errors. If shared bias instead survives or amplifies under homogeneous juries, then multi-judge oversight gives false confidence: a correlated blind spot lets a misaligned output pass an entire jury. Demonstrating where the independence assumption fails directly informs how to build oversight ensembles that actually reduce, rather than launder, error on the high-stakes decisions catastrophic-risk mitigation relies on.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a clean planted-bias-survival measurement against a crisp 1/sqrt(N) null, doable in 30 hours with inference only. Counterfactual value (4) holds because the independence-assumption check uses public data/models and tests a design choice (ensemble aggregation) labs deploy but rarely stress-test for correlated failure.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as an explicit test of the independence assumption underlying ensemble oversight: estimate the effective number of independent judges versus the nominal N.
