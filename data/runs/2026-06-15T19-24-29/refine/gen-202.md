# Research Question

Does a linear refusal/harm probe trained on one open model's activations transfer zero-shot to a different model family at matched layers, and how large is the AUROC drop compared to an in-family probe?

# Approach Outline

On a single consumer GPU, extract residual-stream activations from one open model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) on a labeled set of harmful vs benign prompts at several layers. Train a simple logistic-regression probe (minutes of training) to detect harmful/jailbreak inputs. Then apply the same probe zero-shot to activations from a different model family (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) at matched relative layers, and measure the AUROC. Compare cross-family AUROC against the in-family AUROC to quantify the transfer drop. Optionally test simple alignment fixes (centering, scaling) to see if they recover transfer. Entirely inference plus a linear probe.

# Proposed First Experiments

- In-family baseline: train the logistic probe on one model and evaluate on held-out prompts from the same model, sweeping layers to pick the best. Expected outcome: high in-family AUROC at a mid-to-late layer, establishing the probe works.
- Zero-shot cross-family transfer: apply the trained probe directly to a second family's activations at matched layers and measure AUROC. Expected outcome: a measurable AUROC drop versus in-family, quantifying how much portability is lost.
- Transfer-recovery attempt: apply simple activation normalization (mean-centering, variance-scaling) before cross-family evaluation. Expected outcome: partial recovery of AUROC, indicating whether the drop is due to representational misalignment that cheap calibration can fix.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a cheap linear probe transfers across model families, then small actors and independent deployers can reuse a single jailbreak detector across the open models they run instead of needing per-model labeled data, lowering the cost of a scalable monitoring defense. This matters because jailbreaks are a primary route to misuse uplift from open-weight models: portable activation monitoring makes a leading detection defense deployable outside major labs, directly raising the cost of weaponizing widely-available models.

# Strength Rationale

The idea is maximally narrow and low-compute: train a logistic probe in minutes, then measure a single cross-family AUROC drop. Counterfactual value is high because it relies only on public hidden states with no training of the underlying model and no lab tooling, and cross-family transfer is exactly the replication detail original probe papers underreport, making it a clean, defensible independent contribution.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a portability audit: report a 'cross-family transfer penalty' for activation-based jailbreak probes that practitioners can use to decide whether one probe can cover their whole model fleet.
