# Research Question

In a debate-style oversight setting over a source document, how often do debaters fabricate quotes that are not actually in the source, and does giving the weak judge an automatic 'quote-verified' flag (computed by string/fuzzy matching against the source) improve judge accuracy?

# Approach Outline

Use a reading-comprehension dataset where debaters argue over a passage (QuALITY transcripts, or generate short debates with small open models). For each quoted span a debater offers as evidence, verify it against the source via exact and fuzzy (e.g. normalized substring / token-overlap) matching, labeling each quote real or fabricated. Measure fabrication rates by side (honest vs dishonest debater). Then expose the verified/fabricated flag to a small open judge and compare judge accuracy with vs without the flag. Pure string matching plus small-model inference; near free-tier feasible.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Fabrication rate measurement: across debate transcripts, classify each cited quote as present/absent in the source via fuzzy matching. Measure fabrication rate overall and split by honest vs dishonest side. Expected outcome: dishonest debaters fabricate quotes at a measurably higher rate, establishing fabrication as an exploitable lever.
- Verifier accuracy: hand-check 50 quote-match decisions to validate the fuzzy matcher. Measure precision/recall of the automatic quote checker. Expected outcome: high accuracy (>90%), confirming the flag is trustworthy enough to feed the judge.
- Judge-with-flag uplift: run the weak judge with and without the quote-verified flag and measure verdict accuracy and dishonest-win-rate. Expected outcome: the flag raises judge accuracy and lowers the dishonest-win-rate, showing cheap evidence verification meaningfully helps oversight.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a cheap automatic quote-checker reliably catches fabricated evidence and lifts weak-judge accuracy, then debate-based oversight gains a verified-evidence substrate that dishonest debaters can't easily exploit -> a superhuman model can no longer win oversight by confidently citing evidence that doesn't exist -> the debate pathway for supervising systems we can't directly check becomes more trustworthy, reducing the risk that a deceptive model fools its overseer.

# Strength Rationale

Strong counterfactual value: it builds reusable oversight infrastructure (a quote verifier) on public transcripts and small models with essentially no compute or lab incentive. Very narrow scope — measure fabrication rates and test one verified-flag intervention with clear accuracy and dishonest-win-rate metrics — and beginner-feasible in ~30 hours since the core verifier is string matching.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a drop-in 'evidence linter' for any LLM-debate harness: a module that auto-flags unsupported quotes before the judge ever reads them.
