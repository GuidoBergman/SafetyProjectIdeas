# Research Question

How often does the widely used refusal-string-matching grader (matching phrases like 'I cannot', 'I'm sorry') mislabel model outputs, specifically scoring a 'refusal preface then full harmful answer' as a refusal, and how much does this inflate headline safety scores?

# Approach Outline

Collect open-model outputs to a set of harmful prompts (~150-250) from public benchmarks. Obtain careful manual ground-truth compliance labels for each output (refused, complied, or partial). Run a standard refusal-string matcher and compare its labels against the ground truth, measuring false-negative rate (compliance scored as refusal, e.g., preface-then-comply) and false-positive rate (non-canonical refusals missed). Categorize the failure patterns and estimate how much the matcher's error shifts the reported safety rate versus a full-content grader. Mostly analysis plus small open-model inference on one GPU, a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Matcher-vs-truth comparison: hand-label a corpus of harmful-prompt outputs and run the string matcher; measure the matcher's false-negative and false-positive rates against ground truth; expected outcome is a non-trivial false-negative rate driven by preface-then-comply outputs.
- Failure taxonomy: categorize each matcher error (refusal preface then comply, non-canonical refusal, partial comply); measure the prevalence of each category; expected outcome is that 'refusal preface then comply' is a dominant, exploitable failure mode.
- Score-inflation estimate and fix: recompute the model's headline safety rate under the string matcher vs a full-content grader on the labeled set; measure the gap; expected outcome is that the matcher inflates the safety rate, demonstrating that full-content scoring is a simple deployable fix.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Refusal-string matching is the cheapest and most widely used safety grader; if it systematically scores compliance-after-preface as refusal, then reported safety rates across many evaluations are inflated. Documenting this failure and the full-content-scoring fix lets independent evaluators correct their pipelines, reducing the catastrophic pathway where models that actually comply with harmful requests are certified safe because the grader was fooled.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: a tightly-scoped grader error-rate audit with a concrete metric and an immediate deployable fix, mostly analysis on a single GPU. Counterfactual value is high because auditing widely used graders is independent-friendly work labs underweight, requiring only open models and public data with no training or proprietary infrastructure.
