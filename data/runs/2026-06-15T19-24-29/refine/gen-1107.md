# Research Question

Across a set of open and API models, how correlated are refusal decisions on borderline safety prompts — i.e. do different models share the same refusal blind spots, undermining defense-in-depth redundancy?

# Approach Outline

Curate ~150 borderline safety prompts (cases where reasonable refuse/comply decisions could diverge), drawing on existing safety prompt sets and a small hand-written extension. Query 8-12 models (a mix of open checkpoints run on one GPU and a few cheap API models), each producing a response that is auto-labeled refuse/comply with an LLM judge plus spot-checked manually. Compute a CAPA-style chance-adjusted agreement statistic over the refusal-decision matrix to quantify how correlated models are beyond chance, and identify prompts where almost all models share a blind spot (uniformly comply when they arguably should not). Release the curated slice and labels as a small reproducible mini-benchmark. The work is inference + judging only, no training, and fits a single GPU plus modest API spend.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Prompt curation + pilot labeling: assemble ~150 borderline prompts and label refuse/comply for 3 models, validating the LLM judge against manual labels. Measure judge agreement with human labels. Expected outcome: a judge with high agreement (e.g. >0.9) and a vetted prompt set.
- Full refusal matrix: collect refuse/comply labels for all 8-12 models on the 150 prompts and compute the CAPA chance-adjusted agreement statistic. Measure overall correlation of refusal decisions. Expected outcome: a single headline agreement number showing refusal decisions are more correlated than independent redundancy would predict.
- Shared blind-spot extraction: rank prompts by how uniformly models comply and surface the top shared blind spots. Measure the count and severity of prompts where >80% of models fail to refuse. Expected outcome: a concrete list of correlated refusal failures that defeat naive model-ensemble safety.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Many deployment safety stories assume that stacking multiple models or vendors gives independent layers of refusal (defense in depth). If refusal blind spots are highly correlated across models, then a harmful prompt that slips past one model slips past the ensemble too, and redundancy buys little. Quantifying this correlation and publishing the blind-spot slice warns deployers and auditors that model diversity must be engineered, not assumed, mitigating the misuse pathway where correlated failures let dangerous requests through every layer.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5) drive this: it is third-party scrutiny of a diffuse, long-horizon safety property (correlated refusal) that labs do not incentivize, needs no proprietary access, and produces one releasable artifact with a single headline agreement statistic. The reuse of an existing agreement metric (CAPA) and standard LLM-judging keeps it firmly inside a 30-hour beginner budget with explicit success criteria (a published slice + agreement number).

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'redundancy audit' tool for deployers who chain multiple safety filters, reporting how much true independence their stack actually has.
