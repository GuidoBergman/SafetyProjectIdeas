# Research Question

When a prompt-based mitigation reduces the specific LLM-judge bias it targets (e.g. position bias), does it leave other biases (verbosity, authority) unchanged -- or does fixing one bias inflate another? Are stacked mitigations additive and side-effect-free, as oversight pipelines implicitly assume?

# Approach Outline

Take 2-3 published bias-mitigation prompts, each designed to reduce one bias (position, verbosity, authority). For each mitigation, evaluate the judge against a panel of all three biases simultaneously using controlled paired comparisons (swap order, pad length, add/remove authority cues) on a fixed answer set. Compute each bias magnitude under no-mitigation and under each mitigation, building a bias-interaction matrix that shows, for every (mitigation, bias) pair, whether the bias goes down, stays, or goes up. Inference-only prompting on an open judge; a few hours on one GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Bias panel baseline: with no mitigation, measure position, verbosity, and authority bias on the open judge via paired comparisons (~60 pairs each). Expected outcome: all three biases present at measurable magnitudes.
- Single-mitigation effects: apply each mitigation prompt and re-measure the full panel; populate the interaction matrix. Expected outcome: each mitigation reduces its target bias but moves at least one other bias.
- Stacking check: combine two mitigations and measure the panel; compare to the sum of individual effects. Expected outcome: non-additive interaction, showing net reliability gain is smaller than assumed (or negative).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If bias mitigations trade off -- fixing position bias worsens verbosity bias -- then oversight pipelines that stack mitigations get an illusory reliability gain, leaving exploitable residual bias. This matters because LLM-as-judge is increasingly the reward and evaluation signal for capable models; a judge believed debiased but actually just bias-shifted can be gamed by a generator optimizing toward the surviving bias, so reward hacking persists in the systems oversight is meant to keep aligned, and the field over-trusts a fragile safeguard.

# Strength Rationale

Strong on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it runs a cross-bias check that labs rarely perform (they optimize one metric at a time) using only public models and inference. The deliverable is a compact, explicit bias-interaction matrix (2-3 mitigations x 3 biases) with clear success criteria, fully self-contained and feasible in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'no free lunch for judge debiasing' demonstration, reporting which mitigation pairs are safe to stack and which conflict.
