# Research Question

Do contaminated (memorized, recognizable) benchmark items trigger more evaluation-aware behavior than freshly written novel items of matched content, such that contamination and evaluation-awareness reinforce each other to compound benchmark validity loss?

# Approach Outline

Assemble matched pairs: (a) verbatim well-known public benchmark items the model has likely seen, and (b) freshly written novel items covering the same content/difficulty. For an open model, elicit a self-reported 'is this an evaluation/test?' signal and measure behavioral markers (refusal rate, hedging, format-matching) on each set. Compare eval-awareness markers and behavior shifts between contaminated and novel items, controlling for content so any difference is attributable to recognizability rather than topic. All inference-only on an open model, single GPU, a few hours, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Eval-awareness signal comparison: prompt the model to judge whether each item is an evaluation, on contaminated vs novel matched items. Measure: rate of 'this is a test' responses per set. Expected: contaminated items are flagged as tests far more often than novel ones (mirroring the 19.8% vs 2.0% public-vs-internal pattern).
- Behavioral-shift comparison: measure refusal/hedging/format-matching behavior on the same matched items. Measure: per-set behavioral marker rates and the contaminated-minus-novel delta. Expected: contaminated items show larger eval-aware behavioral shifts.
- Decontamination as dual mitigation: paraphrase the contaminated items to remove verbatim memorization while keeping content, and re-measure eval-awareness. Measure: drop in eval-awareness markers after paraphrasing. Expected: paraphrasing reduces both memorization cues and eval-awareness, showing decontamination does double duty.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If contamination is itself a trigger for evaluation-aware behavior, then memorized benchmarks lose validity through two reinforcing channels at once -> showing that decontamination (paraphrasing/novel items) simultaneously removes memorization AND reduces eval-awareness gives evaluators one cheap mitigation for both -> safety and dangerous-capability evals become more trustworthy, so deployment decisions on frontier-risk capabilities rest on valid measurements rather than gamed scores, reducing the risk of greenlighting an unsafe model on inflated benchmark performance.

# Strength Rationale

Scoring 5 on narrow_scope, accessible_complexity and low_compute and 4 on counterfactual_value and theory_of_impact, this is a tightly scoped contaminated-vs-novel matched comparison runnable inference-only on an open model, and it links two recognized eval-integrity problems into one cheap mitigation, a contribution that needs no lab access and is exactly what independent evaluators can deliver.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a benchmark-design recommendation: argue that all safety benchmarks should ship novel/paraphrased variants to control for both contamination and eval-awareness simultaneously.
