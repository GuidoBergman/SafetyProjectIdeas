# Research Question

In multi-turn debate-based oversight, does the debater who speaks LAST (recency) or FIRST (primacy) gain an advantage with a weak judge purely from temporal position, holding argument content fixed?

# Approach Outline

Generate two-turn debate transcripts with a small open model on a verifiable QA set (e.g. QuALITY or BoolQ) where one side is correct. For each transcript, create order variants that contain the SAME argument text but reordered: correct-side-first, incorrect-side-first, and interleaved. Present each ordering to a weak open judge model and record its verdict. Because content is held byte-identical across orderings, any verdict shift isolates a pure recency/primacy effect. This is inference-only re-judging of reordered transcripts, runnable by a beginner in a notebook in under an hour. Success criterion: report verdict-flip rate attributable to ordering over at least 150 transcripts, with a permutation test for significance.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Transcript generation and baseline: produce 150 two-turn debate transcripts (one correct, one incorrect side) and judge them in a single canonical order. Measure: judge accuracy. Expected: above-chance accuracy, confirming the judge can use arguments at all.
- Order-swap flip test: for each transcript, swap which side's argument appears first vs last (content identical) and re-judge. Measure: fraction of items where the verdict flips, and the direction (toward last speaker = recency, toward first = primacy). Expected: a measurable, directional flip rate above the noise floor, demonstrating a content-free order bias.
- Interleaving and mitigation: present an interleaved ordering and additionally test a 'summarize both sides before deciding' judge instruction. Measure: change in flip rate vs the swap condition. Expected: interleaving reduces position advantage and the summary instruction shrinks the flip rate, indicating a cheap mitigation.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a weak judge's verdict depends on speaking order rather than argument merit, then a capable model under debate oversight could game turn structure to get wrong answers approved without ever making a better argument. Demonstrating and quantifying this content-free lever, and a cheap neutralization, hardens the debate protocol that frontier labs are betting on for superhuman oversight, reducing the risk that order-gaming lets a deceptive system slip a catastrophic action past its overseer.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (4) is high because it uses only public models and contributes a robustness result on a protocol labs deploy but rarely stress-test for turn-order effects publicly.
