# Research Question

When you prompt a single model with many different personas to fake an 'ensemble', does it actually produce decorrelated, diverse errors like a real multi-model ensemble, or is persona-diversity a cheap illusion that leaves blind spots intact?

# Approach Outline

Choose one small open model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B) and a panel of 3-4 genuinely distinct models for the real-ensemble comparator. Design ~8-12 personas (e.g. skeptic, domain expert, contrarian, careful checker) as system prompts. On a labeled benchmark with ground truth (MMLU/ARC/TruthfulQA-MC, ~200 items), generate one answer per persona from the single model and one answer per real model. Compute, for each 'ensemble', the error-correlation between members and the majority-vote accuracy. Compare persona-ensemble diversity/accuracy against the real-ensemble. Single-model inference is cheap enough for a free/low-tier GPU; the real-ensemble comparator adds only a handful of models.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Persona distinctiveness check: run all personas on a stylistic probe set and confirm outputs actually differ in surface form. Measure: pairwise output dissimilarity across personas. Expected outcome: personas look diverse on the surface.
- Error-correlation showdown: on the labeled benchmark, measure error overlap (Jaccard / phi) among personas vs among real models. Expected outcome: persona errors are far MORE correlated than real-model errors, because they share one underlying network.
- Ensemble-accuracy comparison: compute majority-vote accuracy for the persona-ensemble vs the real-ensemble vs a single greedy call. Measure: accuracy lift over single call. Expected outcome: persona-ensembling gives little or no lift while real ensembling does, debunking persona-diversity as a safety mechanism.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Practitioners increasingly use persona-prompted 'self-ensembles' or multi-agent debates from one model as a cheap reliability boost. If this work shows persona-diversity leaves error correlation nearly intact, then teams relying on it gain false confidence in automated decisions -> a shared blind spot survives the supposed cross-check -> correlated failures in high-stakes pipelines. Debunking (or validating) the technique directly prevents that false-confidence pathway from propagating into safety-critical automation.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, accessible complexity, AND low compute: it is a single self-contained comparison runnable on free-tier hardware with only prompt engineering and standard accuracy/correlation stats. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it uses only public open models and yields a deployable insight about a widely-used, lab-independent mitigation that beginners can fully execute and publish.
