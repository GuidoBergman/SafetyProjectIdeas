# Research Question

Does giving a trusted monitor the original task specification improve its ability to catch malicious code, or does task-awareness create a 'plausibility halo' that makes on-topic-but-malicious actions look benign and lowers detection?

# Approach Outline

Use a public set of APPS backdoor transcripts (backdoored solutions plus clean controls) so no generation or training is needed. Run a single small open instruct model as the trusted monitor under two information conditions on the identical transcripts: task-blind (sees only the code/action) and task-aware (sees the problem statement plus the code). Compute detection AUROC and catch-rate at a fixed false-positive rate for each condition, with paired per-item suspicion scores so the comparison is within-item. Optionally add an intermediate condition (task summary only) to localize the effect. All inference-only on one consumer GPU in a couple of hours; the only variable is the prompt.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Paired two-condition scoring: score every transcript task-blind and task-aware with the same monitor and prompt template differing only by the task block. Measure: per-condition AUROC and the within-item suspicion-score delta. Expected: a measurable AUROC change; the hypothesis predicts task-aware is LOWER for on-topic backdoors.
- Plausibility-halo dissection: split backdoors into 'on-topic' (triggered by plausible task inputs) vs 'off-topic' (arbitrary triggers). Measure: AUROC gap between conditions within each subset. Expected: task-awareness hurts most on on-topic backdoors (the halo) and helps or is neutral on off-topic ones.
- Robustness across monitors: repeat with 2-3 different small open models as monitors. Measure: sign and magnitude of the task-aware effect per model. Expected: the direction of the effect is consistent enough to give deployers a clear design recommendation.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If providing task context systematically lowers a monitor's suspicion of malicious-but-on-topic actions, then the near-universal default of feeding monitors the full task spec is actively degrading control catch-rates -> flagging this lets every monitoring harness flip a free prompt-level switch -> more sabotage/backdoors are caught before deployment, reducing the pathway where a misaligned model slips harmful actions past oversight.

# Strength Rationale

With 5s on narrow_scope, accessible_complexity and low_compute and 4 on counterfactual_value, this is a clean paired A/B on public transcripts that costs only inference, yet it interrogates a free design choice present in essentially every monitoring setup whose conventional intuition may be backwards, which is exactly the kind of immediately actionable result labs tend to skip.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a calibration study: measure how task context shifts the monitor's suspicion threshold and false-positive rate rather than only AUROC, recommending per-context recalibration.
