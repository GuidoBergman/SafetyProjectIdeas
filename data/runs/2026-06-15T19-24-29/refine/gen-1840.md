# Research Question

When SAE-based detoxification suppresses identified toxicity features, how much collateral damage does it cause to benign capability and fluency? What is the toxicity-reduction vs collateral-degradation tradeoff curve, and is the detox 'free' or does it carry a hidden alignment tax?

# Approach Outline

On an open model with a public SAE, identify toxicity-mediating features and reproduce SAE-based detoxification by suppressing them at inference (negative steering / ablation), as in the detox paper. Sweep the suppression strength. At each strength, measure toxicity reduction on a small public toxicity benchmark and simultaneously measure capability/fluency on neutral prompts (e.g. a small benchmark slice plus a fluency/perplexity check). Plot toxicity reduction against collateral degradation to get the tradeoff curve. Inference-only on a single GPU using the open SAE.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Detox reproduction: Identify toxicity features and suppress them, measuring toxicity score reduction on a small toxicity benchmark versus baseline. Expected outcome: a clear drop in toxicity, replicating the paper's core effect.
- Collateral capability measurement: At several suppression strengths, measure accuracy on a ~200-item neutral benchmark slice and fluency (perplexity or a judge fluency rating) on benign prompts. Measure degradation vs strength. Expected outcome: measurable capability/fluency loss that grows with suppression strength.
- Tradeoff curve: Plot toxicity reduction (x) against collateral degradation (y) across strengths and identify any knee where detox stops being 'free'. Measure the usable operating range. Expected outcome: a curve revealing whether meaningful detox is achievable before noticeable collateral damage.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If SAE detoxification quietly degrades benign capability, then deployers applying it as a safety filter are paying a hidden alignment tax and may ship a subtly worse model while believing they only removed toxicity. Quantifying the tradeoff on open models lets the community decide when feature-suppression detox is acceptable, reducing the pathway where safety interventions cause unmeasured collateral harm and erode trust in interpretability-based defenses.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute and 4 on counterfactual value: a clean, self-contained inference-only experiment on a fully open model+SAE producing a reusable tradeoff curve. Collateral cost is exactly what method papers under-report, so an independent team using only public artifacts adds genuine lab-independent value.
