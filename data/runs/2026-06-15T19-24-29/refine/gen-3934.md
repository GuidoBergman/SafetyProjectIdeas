# Research Question

Does prompting a monitor to explicitly model the agent's beliefs and intentions (a theory-of-mind monitor) detect agentic misbehavior more reliably than a direct 'is this action harmful?' monitor, when both are cheap prompt-only monitors over a small open model and no model internals are used?

# Approach Outline

Take a public dataset of labeled agentic transcripts (benign vs. misbehaving, e.g., sabotage or deception transcripts). Implement two prompt-only monitors over the same small open model: (1) a direct monitor that judges harmfulness of the action, and (2) a theory-of-mind monitor that is first prompted to infer the agent's hidden goal and beliefs, then judge. Score every transcript with both monitors at matched thresholds and compare detection AUC, true-positive rate at fixed false-positive rate, and where the ToM monitor uniquely succeeds or fails. Entirely inference, runnable in a few hours on Colab Pro.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish the direct-monitor baseline: run the direct harmfulness monitor over the labeled transcripts and report AUC and TPR at a fixed FPR (e.g., 5%). Expected outcome: a reproducible baseline detection curve.
- Run the ToM monitor and compare: implement the two-stage ToM prompt, score the same transcripts, and compute the AUC/FPR delta vs. the direct monitor with bootstrap confidence intervals. Expected outcome: a signed, quantified difference in detection performance between the two monitor designs.
- Error analysis and prompt ablation: identify transcripts the ToM monitor catches that the direct monitor misses (and vice versa), and ablate the ToM prompt (belief-only vs. goal-only vs. full) to see which component carries the signal. Expected outcome: a characterization of when explicit mental-state modeling helps and which prompt component drives it.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Agent monitoring is a core oversight pathway for AI control. If a cheap, prompt-only theory-of-mind monitor reliably beats a direct monitor, then anyone overseeing an agentic system gains a stronger, training-free detector for deceptive or sabotaging behavior -> oversight stacks catch misbehavior they currently miss -> reduced chance that a misaligned autonomous agent executes harmful actions undetected, mitigating the catastrophic-risk pathway in which an agent deceives its overseer while pursuing a hidden goal.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value, low compute, narrow scope, and accessible complexity: it is prompt-only, needs no model internals or training, and contributes directly to AI-control oversight infrastructure that independent teams can build outside major labs. The scope is tight (ToM monitor vs. direct monitor, AUC/FPR) with explicit success criteria, making it well-matched to a beginner team and high in counterfactual value.
