# Research Question

How well-calibrated are cheap open-model trusted monitors on backdoored-vs-clean code, and given that calibration, how many real attacks does a fixed auditing budget (0.1%, 1%, 5% of actions) actually catch?

# Approach Outline

Use the public APPS backdoor split (clean solutions vs known-backdoored solutions from ControlArena). Run a small open model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) as a trusted monitor that emits a 0-100 suspiciousness score per solution. Build a reliability diagram and ROC curve from these scores against ground-truth labels. Then simulate a defer-to-trusted / audit-budget policy: sort actions by suspiciousness, audit the top X% under each budget, and measure attacks-caught vs usefulness-lost. The deliverable is a calibration map from monitor confidence to attack-catch rate per budget. This is inference-only over a public split, completable in a few hours on a single GPU, and written up as a blog post with the reliability diagram and budget curves.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Score and label: run the 8B monitor over the APPS backdoor split, collect suspiciousness scores, and build a reliability diagram plus ROC/AUC. Measure expected calibration error (ECE). Expected outcome: a quantified miscalibration gap, likely overconfident, with AUC well below 1.0.
- Budget simulation: for audit budgets of 0.1%, 1%, and 5%, rank actions by suspiciousness and compute fraction of true backdoors caught and usefulness retained. Expected outcome: a steep attacks-caught-vs-budget curve showing how few attacks tiny budgets catch when the monitor is miscalibrated.
- Calibration intervention: apply a simple post-hoc recalibration (temperature/Platt scaling on a held-out slice) and re-run the budget simulation. Expected outcome: recalibration improves attacks-caught at fixed budget, demonstrating that cheap calibration fixes are decision-relevant.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Audit budget is the scarcest resource in any real AI-control deployment. If cheap monitors are systematically overconfident, then a fixed budget is spent on the wrong actions and genuinely dangerous sabotage from a scheming model slips through. A public calibration-to-catch-rate map tells deployers how much budget a given monitor needs to be safe, and shows that cheap recalibration can raise catch rates, directly strengthening the trusted-monitoring safety case against subtle backdoors.

# Strength Rationale

Narrow scope and low compute are top strengths: the whole study is a calibration map plus a budget simulation over a single public split, runnable on inference alone. Counterfactual value is high because audit-budget calibration is exactly the cheap-trusted-model regime an outside group can study with no lab infrastructure, on fully public data.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a benchmark contribution: a reusable calibration harness and reference numbers for cheap open monitors that other independent groups can extend to new models.

# Cited Sources

- Greenblatt et al., 'AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion' (arXiv:2312.06942) - reports trusted monitoring caught backdoors in 62% of runs; the protocol whose calibration is measured.
- 'Control Tax' (arXiv:2506.05296) - defer-to-trusted analyses showing small audit budgets can prevent many more attacks; motivates the budget simulation.
- ControlArena APPS backdoor setting - the public dataset of clean and backdoored solutions used as the evaluation set.
