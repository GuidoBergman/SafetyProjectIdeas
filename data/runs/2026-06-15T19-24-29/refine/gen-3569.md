# Research Question

How much does paraphrasing an open model's chain-of-thought before the model conditions on it change the final answer (the 'paraphrase tax'), and can an elevated answer-change rate serve as candidate evidence that non-semantic information was riding in the original CoT?

# Approach Outline

Use 2 open models on a single GPU, inference only: a reasoning model that produces CoT and answers, and a small separate model that paraphrases CoT. On a set of reasoning tasks (e.g. GSM8K-style arithmetic and simple multi-step QA), have the reasoning model generate a CoT, then paraphrase that CoT with the second model while preserving semantic content, then force-feed the paraphrased CoT back as the answer prefix (teacher-forcing the prefix) and let the model emit its final answer. Compare the final answer to an identity control where the original, unparaphrased CoT is fed back the same way. Measure the answer-change rate (paraphrase vs identity) on matched tasks. Interpret elevated change rates, beyond the identity-control baseline, as candidate evidence of non-semantic information in the original CoT that paraphrasing destroyed.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the prefix-injection harness and validate the identity control: feed the original CoT back as a forced prefix and confirm the model reproduces its original answer (measure: identity-control answer-change rate; expected outcome: near-zero change, confirming the teacher-forcing plumbing is faithful and any later change is attributable to paraphrasing).
- Verify paraphrase quality: have the small model paraphrase a sample of CoTs and human-spot-check that meaning is preserved while surface wording changes (measure: fraction of paraphrases judged semantically faithful; expected outcome: high semantic fidelity, so answer changes cannot be blamed on lost meaning).
- Measure the paraphrase tax: run the full paraphrase-then-inject pipeline across all tasks and compare answer-change rate to the identity control (measure: paraphrase answer-change rate minus identity baseline; expected outcome: a modest but nonzero excess change rate on at least some tasks, flagging candidate non-semantic information carried by the original CoT surface form).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Encoded/steganographic reasoning is a recognized long-horizon monitorability threat: if models hide reasoning in non-semantic surface features, CoT monitors are blind to it. Paraphrasing pipelines are a proposed defense. Empirically measuring the paraphrase tax on accessible open models builds and validates this monitoring infrastructure and provides a cheap probe for hidden channels, reducing the risk that a future model conducts unsafe reasoning through an encoded channel that monitors cannot read.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value and narrow scope both score 5: encoded-reasoning/steganography is a neglected long-horizon problem that labs cannot fully study with closed models, so an open-model measurement has high counterfactual value precisely outside major labs. The scope is tight (one paraphrase-tax metric vs an identity control), and although it requires light teacher-forcing plumbing, it remains feasible for a beginner team in ~30 hours on a single GPU.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a hidden-channel detector calibration: report the paraphrase-tax baseline on ordinary tasks so future work can flag models or tasks whose tax is anomalously high as steganography suspects.
