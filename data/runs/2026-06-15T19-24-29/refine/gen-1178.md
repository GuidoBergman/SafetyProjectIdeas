# Research Question

Can the reliability-engineering beta-factor (the fraction of failures that are common-cause rather than independent) be estimated for LLM panels, giving a single regulator-legible number for how much of a panel's failures hit all models at once?

# Approach Outline

On labeled safety judgment tasks, treat each model's wrong answer as a 'component failure'. For a panel of N public models, estimate each model's marginal failure rate and the observed all-models-failing-together rate. Fit the beta-factor model: the common-cause failure fraction beta that reconciles marginal failure rates with the observed joint-failure rate (under the standard beta-factor parameterization). Compute beta for a same-lineage panel and for a mixed-vendor panel of equal size, and report the difference. Inference plus a small statistical fit; CPU-level, no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- On ~150 labeled binary safety items, estimate each model's marginal failure rate and the empirical joint all-fail rate for a 4-model panel. Measure: marginal rates and observed simultaneous-failure rate. Expected: the observed joint-failure rate exceeds the product of marginals (positive dependence).
- Fit the beta-factor to reconcile marginal and joint failure rates. Measure: estimated beta (common-cause fraction) with a bootstrap CI. Expected: a clearly non-zero beta, showing a substantial share of failures are common-cause.
- Compute beta for a same-lineage panel vs a mixed-vendor panel of the same size. Measure: beta(mono) - beta(mixed). Expected: same-lineage panels have a much higher common-cause fraction, quantifying the redundancy benefit of diversity.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safety arguments for deploying redundant model panels assume failures are largely independent so simultaneous failure is rare. A non-zero beta-factor shows a quantified share of failures are common-cause and will hit every model at once. Expressing systemic AI redundancy risk in regulators' existing reliability-engineering language makes it legible and actionable, supporting standards that require measured (not assumed) independence, reducing the pathway where a deployed panel suffers a correlated failure on a critical decision.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: a single beta estimate from a small statistical fit over collected judgments, ideal for a stats-literate beginner team. Counterfactual value is strong because it produces a regulator-legible metric labs underweight, using only public models and labeled data with no internal access.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as importing the full 'common-cause failure' toolkit from nuclear/aerospace reliability and reporting which of those measures transfer cleanly to LLM panels.
