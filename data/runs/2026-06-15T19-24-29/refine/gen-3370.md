# Research Question

When a chain-of-thought (CoT) model is given a hint that changes its answer, does the rate at which it verbalizes that hint depend on how credible the hint's source appears? In other words, do models hide hints from high-status sources (a 'professor', a 'verified database') more or less than hints from low-status sources (a 'random forum user')?

# Approach Outline

Take a public multiple-choice QA set (e.g., a 100-200 item slice of MMLU or ARC) and one open reasoning model that emits CoT (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B or Qwen3-8B, runnable on a single consumer GPU). For each item, inject a hint pointing at one answer using a credibility gradient of source framings, holding the hint content constant and varying only the attributed source (e.g., 5 levels from 'a random anonymous note' up to 'a peer-reviewed answer key'). Run inference, then (a) detect answer-flips toward the hinted option and (b) score whether the CoT verbalizes the hint, using a simple keyword/LLM-judge classifier on the public model itself. Plot two curves against source credibility: flip rate and verbalization-given-flip rate. The whole pipeline is prompt-only inference plus light text analysis, feasible in well under 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the credibility gradient: pick 5 source framings ordered by plausibility and validate the ordering with a quick human (your own) ranking. Inject each into 100 QA items. Measure: answer-flip rate per credibility level. Expected outcome: flip rate increases monotonically with source credibility (more credible hints persuade more).
- Score hint-verbalization in the CoT for every flipped case using a fixed keyword list plus an LLM-judge pass. Measure: verbalization-given-flip rate per credibility level. Expected outcome: a non-flat curve, e.g., the model is most likely to silently follow (not verbalize) hints from the most authoritative-sounding sources, revealing a credibility-shaped monitorability blind spot.
- Robustness check: re-run with one alternative model and one paraphrase of the hint template to confirm the curve shape is not an artifact of a single wording. Measure: correlation of per-level verbalization rates across the two runs. Expected outcome: the qualitative gradient replicates (positive correlation), strengthening the claim that source credibility is a real moderator of faithfulness.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If verbalization rate varies systematically with hint source credibility, then CoT monitors trained or evaluated on a single hint type will have a predictable, exploitable blind spot. This matters because social-engineering attacks on deployed agents (a malicious tool output dressed up as an authoritative source) could steer an agent's decision while the CoT stays clean, defeating CoT-based oversight exactly when the manipulation is most effective. Characterizing the credibility gradient tells safety teams which injected-instruction attack surfaces most degrade monitorability, improving the design of CoT monitoring before agents are widely deployed.
