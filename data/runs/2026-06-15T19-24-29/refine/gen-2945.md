# Research Question

How much of a model's safety pass/fail verdict and its safety ranking relative to other models is an artifact of defensible-but-arbitrary choices in how per-item harm scores are aggregated (mean vs worst-case vs category-weighted) and where the pass/fail threshold is set?

# Approach Outline

Obtain per-item safety scores for several open models, either from published evaluation artifacts or by regenerating them with an open LLM judge on a public safety benchmark. Define a grid of defensible aggregation rules (simple mean, category-weighted mean, worst-category, top-k-worst) crossed with a range of pass/fail thresholds. For every (aggregation, threshold) cell, recompute each model's aggregate score, its pass/fail verdict, and the full model ranking. Then quantify instability: the fraction of model pairs whose relative safety ranking flips across cells, and the fraction of models whose verdict flips. This is pure data analysis, no GPU strictly required once per-item scores exist.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Assemble per-item scores: pick a public safety benchmark and 4-5 open models, and either load published per-item results or regenerate completions and grade them with one open judge. Measure: a tidy matrix of per-item harm scores (models x items). Expected outcome: a reusable dataset enabling all downstream aggregation experiments.
- Aggregation/threshold grid: compute aggregate scores under 4 aggregation rules x ~10 thresholds, and record each model's verdict and rank per cell. Measure: for each model pair, the fraction of cells in which their relative ranking is reversed; for each model, the fraction of thresholds at which its verdict flips. Expected outcome: a non-trivial fraction of model pairs are rank-unstable and several models straddle pass/fail under defensible choices.
- Sensitivity hotspots: identify which models/items drive instability (e.g. one high-harm category dominating worst-case aggregation) and report a 'robustness score' per benchmark. Measure: how concentrated the instability is and how a consensus-aggregation rule reduces it. Expected outcome: a small set of categories/items account for most verdict flips, yielding a concrete recommendation for more robust aggregation and threshold reporting.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If safety verdicts and rankings flip under defensible aggregation/threshold choices, then the benchmark-to-decision chain that regulators rely on for cross-model comparison is shown to be unreliable, prompting reporting of verdict-robustness rather than single point grades. This directly serves the NIST/CAISI need for standardized cross-model risk metrics, reducing the chance that a model is certified safe (or a safer model is mis-ranked as unsafe) purely because of an arbitrary scoring convention, which could otherwise let a genuinely riskier model through a release gate.

# Strength Rationale

Top-rated on low compute and narrow scope: it is pure data analysis with a sharply-defined deliverable (the fraction of rankings/verdicts non-robust to defensible aggregation). Counterfactual value is high because it is fully lab-independent, needs no proprietary data, and targets a cross-model standardization gap that labs underweight but regulators care about. A beginner team can execute it entirely in a notebook within the time budget.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a 'benchmark robustness audit': a reusable script that takes any per-item safety result table and outputs how fragile its headline verdicts and rankings are.
