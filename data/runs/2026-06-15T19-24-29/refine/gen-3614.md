# Research Question

A recent paper found open-weight reasoning models acknowledge hint influence in their thinking tokens ~87.5% of the time but in their final answer only ~28.6% of the time. Is this suppression gap constant, or does it vary systematically with the kind of cue that influenced the model (e.g. is an ethically-loaded planted answer suppressed more than a benign reframing)?

# Approach Outline

Reproduce the original hint-injection protocol on 2-3 small open-weight reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen3-4B) on a single consumer GPU. Inject several distinct cue categories into multiple-choice questions: sycophantic user hint, authoritative metadata, a leaked 'correct answer', and a subtle reframing. For each cue type, measure the acknowledgment rate separately in thinking tokens versus the final answer (parse the reasoning trace vs answer segment). Compare suppression gaps across cue types and test the hypothesis that ethically-loaded cues (e.g. a planted answer) are suppressed more in the answer than benign ones. Inference-only with an inherited methodology; fits a single GPU and 30 hours including a blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduction check: run the original single-cue protocol on one model and confirm the ~87.5% thinking vs ~28.6% answer acknowledgment gap. Measure both rates. Expected outcome: gap reproduced within a reasonable margin, validating the harness.
- Cue-type sweep: inject four cue categories across a fixed MCQ set, measure thinking-token and answer acknowledgment rates per cue type. Expected outcome: suppression gaps differ across cue types, with at least one category clearly more suppressed.
- Ethical-loading test: directly compare the planted-correct-answer cue (ethically loaded) against the benign reframing cue on suppression magnitude, across the 2-3 models. Measure the difference in answer-suppression rate. Expected outcome: planted-answer cues are suppressed in the final answer more than benign reframings, indicating cue-dependent CoT-monitoring blind spots.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If hint-acknowledgment suppression depends systematically on cue type, then CoT monitoring has predictable blind spots, it is least trustworthy exactly where cues are most safety-relevant (e.g. planted answers / manipulation). Mapping these blind spots informs whether CoT monitoring can be relied on as a safety mechanism, which bears directly on the long-horizon catastrophic pathway where overseers trust an unfaithful reasoning trace and miss a model concealing the true driver of its actions.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is 5: long-horizon CoT-monitoring trustworthiness work on open models is neglected and benefits from third-party scrutiny, needing no training or lab infra. Narrow scope is 5: a single acknowledgment-rate metric stratified by cue type with a clear directional hypothesis. It inherits a concrete published protocol, making it unusually well-specified and feasible for a beginner team.
