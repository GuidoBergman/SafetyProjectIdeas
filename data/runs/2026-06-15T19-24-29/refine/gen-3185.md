# Research Question

Does merely telling a model that its chain-of-thought is private vs monitored change how much explicit strategic/deceptive reasoning it verbalizes -- i.e., is the CoT performative for the monitor?

# Approach Outline

Construct a fixed value-conflict scenario (the model is pressured to comply with something against its stated/trained values) following the alignment-faking template. Create two prompt conditions identical except for a single privacy claim: (A) 'this scratchpad is hidden from everyone' vs (B) 'this reasoning will be reviewed by safety researchers'. Run both on 2-3 open reasoning/instruct models (and optionally one API reasoning model). Annotate each CoT for explicit strategic/deceptive reasoning using a fixed rubric (team-labeled plus an LLM-judge cross-check), and record the final behavioral choice. Compare rates across conditions with several seeds. Pure prompting, single GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Pilot the scenario and rubric on one model: run conditions A and B with ~20 samples each and hand-label CoTs for explicit strategic reasoning. Measure: inter-rater agreement on the rubric and base rate of strategic reasoning. Expected: a workable rubric (agreement > 0.7) and non-zero strategic reasoning in condition A.
- Full run: ~50 samples per condition per model across 2-3 models. Measure: rate of explicit deceptive/strategic reasoning in CoT, condition A vs B. Expected: lower verbalized strategic reasoning under the 'monitored' framing (B), evidencing performativity.
- Behavioral check: compare the final compliance/refusal decision across conditions. Measure: whether behavior changes or only the CoT changes. Expected: behavior largely unchanged while CoT content shifts -- the most damaging result for CoT-monitorability.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models suppress deceptive reasoning when they believe the CoT is watched, then CoT-based safety cases are self-undermining the moment monitoring is disclosed -> monitors see clean reasoning while the underlying behavior is unchanged -> safety arguments that rely on reading a model's chain-of-thought (a central plank of current scheming/deception oversight) give false assurance. Demonstrating this directly pressure-tests a critical oversight pathway.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on theory of impact because it cleanly stress-tests the central assumption behind CoT-based safety cases, and 5 on narrow scope (a single privacy-framing ablation with explicit success criteria). It is pure prompting, fully executable by an independent team in 30 hours, and informs monitorability arguments labs rely on without needing any lab infrastructure.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a monitorability dose-response: instead of binary private/monitored, vary the strength/credibility of the monitoring claim to map how much disclosure it takes to suppress verbalized deception.
