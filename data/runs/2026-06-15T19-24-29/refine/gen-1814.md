# Research Question

What fraction of an SAE-based harm-detection monitor's coverage is bound to surface tokenization rather than meaning, such that meaning-preserving tokenization tricks (added spaces, homoglyphs, leetspeak) silently switch the harm feature off?

# Approach Outline

On an open model with a pre-trained SAELens SAE (no training), locate a harm- or concept-related feature by activation-maximizing over a small set of harmful prompts. Construct a battery of meaning-preserving tokenization perturbations of those prompts (inserted spaces, unicode homoglyphs, leetspeak) and measure the drop in feature activation versus the preservation of semantics (judged cheaply by a small instruct model or string-similarity proxy). The deliverable is the 'tokenization-bound coverage fraction': the share of originally-detected harmful inputs whose monitor signal collapses under a perturbation that a human would still read as harmful. Everything is inference-only and fits a few GPU-hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Identify and validate a harm feature: select an SAE feature that fires reliably on a curated set of ~50 harmful prompts (measure: baseline firing rate on clean harmful prompts; expected: high, e.g. >80%, establishing the monitor works pre-attack).
- Apply tokenization perturbations: for each prompt generate spaced, homoglyph, and leetspeak variants and measure feature firing drop while confirming semantic preservation with a cheap judge (measure: per-perturbation firing-rate drop and semantic-preservation rate; expected: large firing drops, e.g. >30-50%, at high semantic preservation, revealing token-binding).
- Quantify evadability and a defense: compute the tokenization-bound coverage fraction and test a simple normalization defense (de-spacing / homoglyph folding before monitoring) (measure: coverage fraction before vs after normalization; expected: a substantial bound fraction that normalization meaningfully reduces, giving a concrete cheap mitigation).

# Theory of Impact Chain

Feature-based monitors are being proposed as runtime guardrails that block harmful generations or flag misuse. If a large slice of their coverage is tokenization-bound, then an attacker who merely re-tokenizes a harmful request silently disables the monitor -> harmful content passes the guardrail -> a misuse pathway (e.g. jailbroken assistance with dangerous tasks) that the monitor was deployed to prevent. Quantifying this and shipping a normalization defense hardens a class of safety monitors before they are relied upon.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value and narrow scope both score 5: the work runs on open models and public SAEs with no training or lab data, and it isolates a single crisp number (tokenization-bound coverage fraction) plus a cheap defense. It connects a documented, peer-reviewed interp artifact to a documented jailbreak vector, making it a fresh and feasible compound that labs underweight.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as robustness benchmarking: ship a small 'tokenization-evasion test set' for SAE harm monitors with a leaderboard-style coverage-fraction metric so others can audit their own monitors.

# Cited Sources

- Homoglyph / tokenization jailbreak literature on robustness — establishes that meaning-preserving re-tokenization is a real, cheap evasion vector worth testing against monitors.
- SAELens pre-trained SAEs — supplies the open SAE and model that make the experiment training-free and single-GPU feasible.
