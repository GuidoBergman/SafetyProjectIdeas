# Research Question

Do common downstream deployment transforms, quantization (int8/int4) and decoding settings (temperature/top-p), change the activation rate or strength of a known backdoor in a publicly released sleeper-agent model, relative to full precision and greedy decoding?

# Approach Outline

Obtain a publicly released backdoored open model (e.g., a Hugging Face sleeper-agent organism or a small backdoored Llama/Qwen checkpoint with a documented trigger). Run it at full precision, int8, and int4 using off-the-shelf quantization (bitsandbytes / GGUF), each across a small temperature and top-p sweep, all on a single consumer GPU. For each configuration, present (a) trigger-present inputs and (b) benign inputs, and measure backdoor activation rate (fraction of triggered inputs that produce the backdoored behavior) and benign-behavior preservation. Compare activation rates across the precision x decoding grid to test whether quantization or sampling inadvertently strengthens, weakens, or masks the backdoor. Pure inference, no training, beginner-accessible tooling.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce the backdoor at baseline: full precision, greedy decoding, on ~100 triggered and ~100 benign inputs. Measure: baseline activation rate and benign accuracy. Expected outcome: high activation on triggered inputs, near-normal behavior on benign, confirming the organism works.
- Quantization sweep: rerun at int8 and int4, greedy decoding. Measure: activation rate change vs baseline per precision. Expected outcome: a measurable shift, e.g., int4 partially masks or amplifies the backdoor, demonstrating a deployment-transform effect.
- Decoding sweep: at each precision, vary temperature (0.0, 0.7, 1.0) and top-p (1.0, 0.9). Measure: activation rate as a function of sampling stochasticity. Expected outcome: higher temperature dilutes deterministic triggers, revealing decoding as a backdoor-relevant axis.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If routine quantization or decoding choices can mask or amplify a backdoor, then the threat model for distributing open weights changes: a checkpoint that looks safe at full precision could become more dangerous (or evade detection) once a downstream user quantizes it, a concrete pathway by which a covertly backdoored open model reaches deployment undetected. Mapping this interaction tells safety auditors that backdoor evaluation must cover the precision/decoding configurations users actually run, strengthening covert-fine-tuning defenses.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope and accessible complexity and 4 on low compute: it is a tightly bounded inference-only sweep with a clear activation-rate success metric, executable on one consumer GPU within 30 hours. Counterfactual value (4) is high because it varies a deployment dimension (quantization) that labs rarely test against backdoors, uses only public models, and needs no internal access.
