# Research Question

What is the minimal fraction of clean, aligned demonstrations that must be mixed into a misalignment-inducing (poisoned) fine-tuning dataset to prevent emergent misalignment, i.e., what does the dose-response curve of clean-data inoculation look like?

# Approach Outline

Take one well-characterized EM-inducing dataset and a small pool of clean aligned demonstrations. Construct training mixtures that vary the clean fraction across a sweep (e.g., 0%, 2%, 5%, 10%, 25%) while holding total size fixed. Run a small LoRA fine-tune for each mixture on a single GPU and evaluate the resulting model on a held-out free-form misalignment probe set with a documented rubric. Fit a dose-response curve of misalignment rate vs clean fraction and identify the minimal protective fraction (the knee where EM drops to baseline). To stay within compute, keep the model small, the dataset modest, and the number of mixture points to ~5; reuse a single eval harness across runs.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Endpoint check: fine-tune with 0% clean (pure poison) and with a large clean fraction (e.g., 25%). Measure: free-form misalignment rate at each endpoint. Expected outcome: high EM at 0% and near-baseline EM at 25%, confirming clean data can neutralize the poison.
- Dose-response sweep: fill in intermediate clean fractions (2%, 5%, 10%) with matched LoRA fine-tunes. Measure: misalignment rate at each fraction to trace the curve. Expected outcome: a monotone decline with an identifiable knee revealing the minimal protective fraction.
- Robustness of the knee: re-run the 2-3 fractions nearest the knee with a different random seed / clean-demo subset. Measure: variance of the estimated protective fraction. Expected outcome: a stable minimal fraction estimate, making the dose-response rule trustworthy.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If this works, the open community gets a concrete 'add at least X% clean aligned data' rule of thumb that defends the enormous volume of open-model fine-tuning against accidental or adversarial data poisoning. This attacks the catastrophic pathway in which poisoned or low-quality fine-tuning data silently induces broadly misaligned behavior across many downstream deployments, by giving practitioners a cheap, measurable composition safeguard.

# Strength Rationale

It scores 5s on accessible complexity and narrow scope: a tightly defined dose-response sweep with a crisp success criterion (the minimal protective clean fraction), using standard LoRA tooling and public data. Counterfactual value is high because mapping the full minimal-fraction curve is exactly the kind of practical, open-community-serving question labs have weak incentive to publish, and it requires no proprietary data or infra.
