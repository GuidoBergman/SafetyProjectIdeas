# Research Question

Using the Diversity Prediction Theorem (collective error = average individual error - predictive diversity), how much of the potential crowd-wisdom benefit on numeric safety-estimation tasks is destroyed by model homogeneity?

# Approach Outline

Collect numeric-estimate tasks with ground truth relevant to safety judgment (e.g. estimate probability/severity/cost, calibration questions, numeric risk ratings). Query 6-8 public models to produce a numeric estimate per task. For each task, apply the Diversity Prediction Theorem to decompose the panel's collective squared error into the average individual squared error minus the predictive diversity (variance of individual estimates around the mean). Report how large the diversity term is relative to the average-error term, and how much smaller it is for same-lineage panels than mixed panels. Pure arithmetic decomposition over collected estimates; CPU-level.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Gather 50-80 numeric tasks with ground truth and collect estimates from 6-8 models. Measure: collective error of the panel mean vs the average individual error. Expected: the panel mean beats the average individual, confirming a crowd benefit exists.
- Decompose each task's collective error into average-error and diversity components and aggregate. Measure: the diversity term as a fraction of average individual error (the 'crowd benefit captured'). Expected: a modest diversity term, showing homogeneity caps the achievable benefit.
- Compare diversity for a same-lineage sub-panel (e.g. 3 Llama variants) vs a mixed-vendor sub-panel of equal size. Measure: diversity term difference. Expected: mixed panels have substantially higher predictive diversity and thus lower collective error.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Aggregating many models is a proposed cheap route to more reliable safety estimates (calibrated risk ratings, ensemble oversight). The Diversity Prediction Theorem shows aggregation only helps to the extent estimates are diverse; if homogeneity has eaten the diversity term, ensembles give little real error reduction while appearing robust. Quantifying the lost benefit guides oversight designers to source genuinely diverse models, reducing the pathway where an overconfident, falsely-robust aggregate steers a high-stakes safety decision.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: it is one interpretable arithmetic decomposition over collected estimates, ideal for a stats-literate beginner team. Counterfactual value is strong because it audits multi-model aggregation reliability using only public models and labeled tasks, with no lab-internal infrastructure and weak lab incentive to run it.
