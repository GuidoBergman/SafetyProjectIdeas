# Research Question

Does parameter-efficient (LoRA/QLoRA) fine-tuning on purely benign task data erode an open instruct model's safety alignment the way full fine-tuning does, and how does the degradation scale with LoRA rank?

# Approach Outline

Take a small open instruct model that has measurable safety behavior and establish its baseline refusal rate on a held-out harmful-request set. Fine-tune it with LoRA/QLoRA (peft + bitsandbytes) on a benign instruction dataset (e.g., a small slice of a general instruction-following set with no harmful content), sweeping LoRA rank across a few values (e.g., r = 4, 8, 16, 32). Keep all other hyperparameters fixed and train each run for a short, fixed budget that fits a single consumer GPU in well under an hour. After each run, re-measure the refusal rate on the same held-out harmful set using a fixed refusal judge, and also track benign task accuracy to confirm the adaptation actually worked. Plot post-tuning refusal rate (safety erosion) against LoRA rank. The twist over Qi et al. is twofold: PEFT instead of full FT, and rank as an explicit knob.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Establish the pre-tuning baseline: measure the base instruct model's refusal rate on a fixed held-out harmful-request set with a fixed judge. Measure: baseline refusal rate and benign task accuracy. Expected outcome: high refusal rate (strong safety) and a known benign-task baseline to compare against.
- Run a single LoRA fine-tune on benign data at a moderate rank (e.g., r=16) and re-measure. Measure: post-tuning refusal rate vs baseline, plus benign task accuracy. Expected outcome: a measurable drop in refusal rate even though training data was entirely benign, replicating the safety-erosion effect under PEFT.
- Sweep LoRA rank (r = 4, 8, 16, 32) holding everything else fixed. Measure: refusal rate as a function of rank, with confidence intervals over the harmful-request set. Expected outcome: a monotone or threshold relationship where higher rank (more adaptation capacity) produces larger safety erosion, quantifying the rank/erosion trade-off.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If benign LoRA/QLoRA tuning measurably degrades safety and the effect grows with rank, then the millions of hobbyists and startups who routinely LoRA-tune open models on benign task data are inadvertently stripping safety guardrails -> this gives them a concrete, quantified warning and a rank/erosion trade-off to manage -> it mitigates the misuse pathway where widely deployed, casually fine-tuned open models silently lose their refusal behavior and become easy sources of harmful assistance at scale.

# Strength Rationale

It scores maximally on counterfactual value, narrow scope, and low compute: the LoRA-on-benign-data setting is the pervasive real-world deployment pattern that labs (who studied full FT) ignore, yet it is fully reproducible by an independent team with public tooling on one GPU. The deliverable is a single crisp curve (refusal rate vs LoRA rank) with an explicit pre/post success criterion, and it directly extends a well-cited paper.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a mitigation probe: in addition to documenting erosion, test whether a cheap intervention (e.g., freezing safety-critical layers, mixing in a few safety examples, or lower rank) preserves alignment while retaining benign-task gains.
