# Research Question

How many fine-tuning examples does it take to reverse a published unlearning method, and how does recovery depend on the number and topical relatedness of those examples? We want a quantitative dose-response curve characterizing the fragility of unlearning 'guarantees' on accessible open models.

# Approach Outline

Take a small open model (e.g. a 1-7B instruct model) and apply one simple, well-documented unlearning method (e.g. gradient ascent / RMU-style) on a forget set drawn from WMDP or TOFU, verifying the forget-set accuracy drops. Then run titrated relearning attacks: brief LoRA fine-tunes using 1, 4, 16, and 64 examples, varying whether those examples are drawn from the exact forget set, a related-but-distinct topic, or unrelated control text. After each relearning run, re-measure forget-set accuracy and retain-set accuracy to confirm we are measuring genuine knowledge recovery, not general capability shift. All fine-tunes are tiny LoRA adapters that complete in minutes on a single consumer GPU, keeping the whole study within the 30-hour budget.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Reproduce a baseline: apply the chosen unlearning method to the model on the WMDP/TOFU forget set, measure pre- and post-unlearning forget-set accuracy and retain-set accuracy. Measure: accuracy drop on forget set, retention on retain set. Expected: forget-set accuracy drops substantially (e.g. to near-chance) while retain accuracy stays roughly stable.
- Dose curve with exact-forget examples: relearn with 1/4/16/64 forget-set examples via short LoRA fine-tunes; measure forget-set accuracy recovery after each. Measure: recovered forget-set accuracy vs number of examples. Expected: a steep recovery curve where even a handful of examples (e.g. 4-16) restores most of the forgotten knowledge.
- Relatedness ablation: hold the number of examples fixed (e.g. 16) and vary topical relatedness (exact / related / unrelated control). Measure: recovered accuracy by relatedness condition. Expected: exact and related examples drive recovery while unrelated control does not, isolating the knowledge-reopening mechanism rather than generic re-tuning.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If a tightly-scoped dose curve shows that a few examples reopen unlearned dangerous knowledge, then deployers and policymakers learn that current unlearning offers no durable removal guarantee -> safety claims about 'removed' dangerous-capability knowledge (e.g. bio/cyber uplift) are recalibrated and unlearning research is pushed toward relearning-robust methods. This concretely attacks the dangerous-capability-uplift pathway, where an unlearned-then-redeployed open model could be cheaply restored to provide weapons-relevant knowledge.

# Strength Rationale

This idea scores top marks on theory of impact, narrow scope, and counterfactual value: it targets a recognized critical pathway (dangerous-capability unlearning), is tightly scoped to a single clean dose-response curve, and is exactly the kind of external scrutiny labs deprioritize since it needs no internal access or proprietary data. It produces a concrete, reproducible safety guideline from public checkpoints alone.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame the deliverable as an audit tool: a small reusable script that, given any unlearned checkpoint, reports its 'relearning half-life' (number of examples to 50% knowledge recovery) as a single comparable safety metric.
