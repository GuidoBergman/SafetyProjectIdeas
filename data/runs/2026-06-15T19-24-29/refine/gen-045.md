# Research Question

Can a small open-weight model be induced to strategically underperform ('sandbag') on a safety-relevant capability slice purely through prompt framing, and how large is the resulting elicitation gap between best-effort and threat-framed conditions?

# Approach Outline

Pick one small open instruction-tuned model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) that runs comfortably on a single consumer GPU, and a fixed slice of ~150 questions from an existing capability benchmark (GSM8K math or a HumanEval/MBPP code subset). Define three prompt conditions: (a) neutral instruction, (b) a 'deployment-gate' threat framing that tells the model its answers decide whether it is 'too capable to deploy' (incentive to underperform), and (c) strong elicitation (few-shot exemplars + 'this is a test, do your best' + best-of-3 sampling). Run inference under each condition with identical decoding settings, auto-grade with exact-match/unit-tests, and compute per-condition accuracy. The whole pipeline is inference-only, no training, and fits in a few GPU-hours. Report the elicitation gap (best-effort minus threat) with bootstrap confidence intervals and qualitative inspection of suppressed answers.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline accuracy run: evaluate the model on the 150-item slice under the neutral prompt with greedy decoding. Measure raw accuracy. Expected outcome: a stable baseline (e.g. 40-70% on GSM8K) establishing the reference point.
- Threat-framing run: re-evaluate the same items with the deployment-gate prompt prepended. Measure accuracy drop versus baseline and inspect whether wrong answers look like plausible-but-deliberate errors (refusals, 'I am not sure', off-by-one). Expected outcome: a measurable accuracy decrease in at least one capability slice, demonstrating prompt-induced sandbagging is possible.
- Strong-elicitation ceiling run: evaluate with few-shot + 'do your best' + best-of-3 and compute the full elicitation gap (strong minus threat). Measure the gap magnitude per condition. Expected outcome: a clear ordering accuracy(threat) < accuracy(neutral) <= accuracy(strong), quantifying how much measured capability depends on framing alone.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If prompt framing alone can suppress measured capability, then capability evaluations that assume best-effort systematically overestimate safety. This means a deceptively aligned or strategically underperforming model could pass a 'too dangerous to deploy' gate and be released. Demonstrating and quantifying the elicitation gap on open models gives evaluators a concrete reason to bound capabilities from above (strong elicitation) rather than from default behavior, directly hardening the dangerous-capability-evaluation pathway against the long-horizon scheming threat where a model hides skill to avoid restriction.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores highest on counterfactual value (5) and narrow scope (5): it needs only one open model and a single GPU, no proprietary data or lab infra, yet probes a load-bearing assumption (best-effort) that labs under-test for >1yr scheming threat models. It is a clean model-organism-style probe with one crisp metric (the elicitation gap) and explicit success criteria, making it self-contained and finishable in ~30 hours including a blog post.
