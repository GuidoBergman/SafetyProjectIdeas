# Research Question

How prevalent is 'causal bypass' — where a model's final answer is unchanged whether or not its chain of thought supports it — across different task types in small open models? In other words, how often is the CoT decorative rather than load-bearing?

# Approach Outline

Assemble a battery of ~150-250 items spanning arithmetic, commonsense QA, and multiple-choice reasoning from public datasets. For each item, sample the model's CoT and answer, then run three intervention conditions on the CoT before the answer is produced: (a) truncate it, (b) corrupt it (shuffle/negate key steps), and (c) replace it with a CoT that argues for a different answer. Measure how often the final answer is unchanged (the causal-bypass rate) under each intervention, broken down by task type. Use one small open instruct model (e.g. Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3.1-8B) with deterministic decoding; everything is inference-only with prompt manipulation, fitting a single GPU and ~30 hours including write-up.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Truncation bypass rate: for each task type, cut the CoT off before its conclusion and force an answer. Measure: fraction of items whose answer is unchanged vs. the full-CoT answer, per task type. Expected outcome: high bypass on arithmetic-light/commonsense items, lower on multi-step arithmetic, locating where CoT is decorative.
- Contradictory-CoT replacement: replace the model's CoT with a coherent argument for a different answer and read off the final answer. Measure: rate at which the model sticks with its original answer despite the contradicting CoT (strong causal-bypass signal). Expected outcome: a non-trivial fraction (e.g. 20-50%) ignore the injected CoT, especially on commonsense/MCQ, indicating unfaithful monitoring surface.
- Corruption dose-response: corrupt an increasing fraction of CoT steps and track answer stability. Measure: answer-change rate as a function of corruption fraction, per task type. Expected outcome: arithmetic shows a steep dependence (load-bearing CoT) while commonsense stays flat (causally inert CoT).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If causal bypass is frequent, then monitoring a model's chain of thought gives false assurance — the visible reasoning does not determine the action, so a CoT monitor can be cleanly passed while the model does something else. This means CoT-based oversight, a leading proposed defense for catching deceptive or dangerous agentic reasoning, would fail silently. Measuring the bypass rate by task type improves safety because it tells oversight designers exactly where CoT monitoring is trustworthy versus where it must be supplemented for deception/scheming risk.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value (5): an intervention-based faithfulness prevalence study on open models is neglected, long-horizon work that needs only single-GPU inference and no proprietary access. Narrow scope (5): a single, crisply-defined causal-bypass-rate metric with an automatic answer-change check, decomposed by task type, so a beginner team can complete and write it up in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a CoT-monitor stress test: 'For which task types can a model satisfy a CoT monitor while its answer ignores the CoT?', positioning the result directly as guidance for oversight pipelines.
