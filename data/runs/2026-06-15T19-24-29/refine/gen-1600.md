# Research Question

Does a safety steering vector extracted and validated on a full-precision (fp16) model retain its effect when the same model is quantized to int8/int4 for real-world deployment, or does compression weaken, neutralize, or even flip the steering effect?

# Approach Outline

Pick a small open instruction-tuned model (e.g. Llama-3-8B-Instruct) that fits on a single consumer GPU. Extract a safety steering vector at fp16 using a standard contrastive activation-difference method (e.g. refusal/harmlessness or honesty direction from paired prompts). Define a quantitative steering effect metric on a held-out behavioral eval set (e.g. refusal rate on harmful prompts, or a behavior-score delta). Then load the same model at int8 and int4 via bitsandbytes, apply the *same* fp16-derived vector (and also re-extract a vector at each precision), and measure effect retention and any sign flips across precisions. Inference-only on quantized models; fits comfortably on one consumer GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Extract the steering vector at fp16 and establish the baseline steering effect on a held-out eval (e.g. change in refusal rate when the vector is added). Measure: fp16 steering effect size. Expected: a clear, reproducible behavioral shift in the intended direction.
- Apply the fp16-derived vector to the int8 and int4 models and measure the same effect. Measure: effect retention (fraction of fp16 effect preserved) and any sign flips per quantization level. Expected: degraded retention at int4, with some prompts showing weakened or reversed steering.
- Re-extract the vector natively at int8 and int4 and compare its direction (cosine similarity) and effect to the fp16 vector. Measure: cosine similarity between vectors across precisions and native-vector effect retention. Expected: lower cosine similarity and partial effect loss at int4, showing the safety direction drifts under compression.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Steering vectors are promoted as a cheap, deploy-time safety intervention, and quantized models are what people actually run on consumer/edge hardware. If a vector validated at fp16 silently loses effect or flips sign at int4, then a deployer believes a safety steer is active when it is not. Demonstrating this quantization-x-steering fragility warns the open-deployment community to re-validate steering at the deployed precision, reducing the pathway where a falsely-trusted safety intervention fails on a widely-deployed quantized model.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on accessible complexity, narrow scope, and low compute: extract one vector, apply it across precisions with standard bitsandbytes tooling, and measure retention/sign-flips, a single clean experiment ideal for a beginner team and quantization actually reduces memory needs. Counterfactual value is high because consumer/edge quantized deployment is precisely the open-community slice labs deprioritize, and it needs no internal access.

# Alternative Framings
