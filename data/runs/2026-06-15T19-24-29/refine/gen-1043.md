# Research Question

Does inter-model output homogeneity get worse or better when prompts are pushed off-distribution by adversarial or OOD perturbations? Specifically, do models that already agree on clean prompts converge even more (correlated degeneration) when stressed?

# Approach Outline

Start from a clean open-ended prompt set (e.g. a sample of Infinity-Chat prompts) and measure baseline inter-model homogeneity for 5-8 small open models. Then apply a small library of cheap, reproducible perturbations -- typos/character noise, paraphrase, instruction-injection prefixes, format shifts, and low-resource framing -- to create OOD variants of each prompt. Re-run the same models and re-measure homogeneity (response-embedding similarity and, where there is ground truth, error overlap) on perturbed vs clean prompts. The key comparison is whether the gap between models shrinks (correlated collapse to a shared degenerate behavior) or widens under stress. Deliver a clean-vs-perturbed homogeneity comparison as a blog post.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If homogeneity intensifies under distribution shift, then exactly when inputs are adversarial or unusual -- the regime that matters most for safety -- model diversity collapses and agreement-based defenses are weakest -> red-teamers and monitors learn that ensemble/consensus oversight cannot be trusted precisely under attack -> this reduces the risk that an adversary triggers a correlated degenerate failure across a deployed multi-model safety system at the worst possible moment, a concrete worst-case systemic-failure pathway.
