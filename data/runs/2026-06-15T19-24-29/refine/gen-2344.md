# Research Question

Recursive and ensemble oversight implicitly assume overseer errors are roughly independent so they wash out under aggregation. Are LLM-judge errors actually correlated across judges on the same items, and if so, how much does that correlation inflate the apparent accuracy of majority-vote oversight?

# Approach Outline

Take a shared set of judgment items with ground truth (e.g. answer-correctness or harmfulness labels from a public benchmark). Run several small open judge models, plus the same model under varied prompts and temperatures, over all items, recording each judge's per-item error indicator. Compute the inter-judge error-correlation matrix, identify clusters of 'hard' items where all judges fail together, and compare actual majority-vote accuracy to the accuracy a naive independence assumption would predict. Quantify the inflation. This is clean statistics on top of inference-only judging — single-GPU, no proprietary data, completable in a few hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Judge panel + error matrix: run ~6 judges (distinct models plus prompt/temperature variants) over ~300 labeled items and record per-judge correctness. Measure: pairwise error-correlation (phi/Matthews) matrix across judges. Expected outcome: positive error correlations rather than near-zero, contradicting the independence assumption.
- Inflation estimate: compute observed majority-vote accuracy and compare it to the accuracy predicted by treating judges as independent with their measured individual error rates. Measure: gap between observed and independence-predicted majority-vote accuracy. Expected outcome: independence overpredicts ensemble accuracy, showing aggregation gives false confidence.
- Shared blind-spot characterization: isolate items where >=80% of judges err together and inspect them. Measure: fraction of total ensemble errors concentrated in these shared-failure items. Expected outcome: a small cluster of systematically hard items drives most correlated failures, pinpointing where recursive oversight is least trustworthy.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Recursive oversight and judge ensembles are central scalable-oversight proposals that lean on errors washing out under aggregation. If overseer errors are systematically correlated, then ensembling provides far less safety than assumed and stacked oversight layers can share blind spots, which improves safety because it warns researchers against over-trusting aggregated weak overseers and identifies the systematic-error clusters that must be addressed before recursive oversight is relied on for the catastrophic pathway where a superhuman system exploits a shared overseer blind spot to pass unsafe behavior undetected.
