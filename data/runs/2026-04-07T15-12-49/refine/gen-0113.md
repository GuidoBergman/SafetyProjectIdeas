# Research Question

Code-specialized models (DeepSeek-Coder, Code Llama, StarCoder) may have higher offensive cyber capability than general models for tasks requiring precise code generation (exploit development, binary analysis). This project investigates whether run a 20-challenge benchmark (with heavy emphasis on code-generation tasks: writing exploits, creating shellcode, analyzing decompiled functions) on: (1) general frontier models, (2) code-specialized models of comparable parameter count.

# Approach Outline

Run a 20-challenge benchmark (with heavy emphasis on code-generation tasks: writing exploits, creating shellcode, analyzing decompiled functions) on: (1) general frontier models, (2) code-specialized models of comparable parameter count. Compare solve rates per challenge category. Specifically test whether coding specialization advantage is concentrated in exploit-writing vs. logical attack planning tasks.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Run the evaluation protocol across target conditions, collecting quantitative measurements.
- Compare results against baselines, analyze failure modes, and write up findings.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If code-specialized models have significantly higher offensive capability, they may pose disproportionate risks despite lower overall 'intelligence' metrics. Safety evaluations that focus exclusively on frontier general models may miss the highest-risk category for specific attack vectors.
