# Research Question

AI agents often commit prematurely to a single attack hypothesis during reconnaissance, failing to maintain multiple competing hypotheses. This project investigates whether implement two versions of an ai attack agent: (a) standard react agent, (b) differential diagnosis-inspired agent that is explicitly prompted to maintain a ranked list of 5 attack hypotheses at each step and update rankings before committing.

# Approach Outline

Implement two versions of an AI attack agent: (A) standard ReAct agent, (B) differential diagnosis-inspired agent that is explicitly prompted to maintain a ranked list of 5 attack hypotheses at each step and update rankings before committing. Run both on 10 CTF challenges. Measure whether the differential approach reduces false positive pursuit and increases overall completion rates.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Premature hypothesis commitment is a known failure mode in AI attack chains (pursuing a rabbit hole while missing the correct path). If differential diagnosis prompting improves performance, it reveals both a capability weakness (commitment bias) and a mitigation approach. The mitigation is also a dual-use finding — defenders can design environments that exploit AI commitment bias.
