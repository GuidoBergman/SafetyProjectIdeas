# Research Question

AI defenders trained on historical attack patterns may fail against novel ones. This project investigates whether use a public ids benchmark (unsw-nb15 or cic-ids2018) to train a simple anomaly detection model.

# Approach Outline

Use a public IDS benchmark (UNSW-NB15 or CIC-IDS2018) to train a simple anomaly detection model. Systematically vary the diversity of training attack categories. Measure detection rate on held-out novel attack categories as a function of training diversity. Identify the minimum diversity threshold for acceptable generalization.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Select and prepare the evaluation dataset/benchmark, defining clear success metrics.
- Run the evaluation protocol across target conditions, collecting quantitative measurements.
- Compare results against baselines, analyze failure modes, and write up findings.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Zero-day and novel attacks are highest-risk. If AI defense requires seeing attack patterns to defend against them, the defense will always lag offense.
