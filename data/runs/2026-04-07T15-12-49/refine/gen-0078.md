# Research Question

Two interacting failure modes: (1) most AI evaluations use the same few frontier models (GPT, Claude, Gemini), and (2) CTF benchmarks are saturating as these exact models are trained on internet data including CTF writeups. This project investigates whether measure the correlation between model training data cutoff dates and solve rates on specific cybench/nyu ctf challenges with publicly-dated writeups.

# Approach Outline

Measure the correlation between model training data cutoff dates and solve rates on specific Cybench/NYU CTF challenges with publicly-dated writeups. Test whether challenges with writeups published before the training cutoff have significantly higher solve rates than challenges with writeups published after. Compute a 'contamination index' for each benchmark.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Select and prepare the evaluation dataset/benchmark, defining clear success metrics.
- Run the evaluation protocol across target conditions, collecting quantitative measurements.
- Compare results against baselines, analyze failure modes, and write up findings.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If both model diversity and benchmark validity are collapsing simultaneously, the entire evaluation ecosystem may be producing increasingly uninformative measurements. Early detection of this validity collapse would motivate urgent methodological reform before safety-critical evaluations become unreliable.
