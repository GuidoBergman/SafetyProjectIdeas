# Research Question

Two interacting failure modes: (1) AI systems (AIxCC CRS) can discover previously unknown vulnerabilities in production code in ~45 minutes, and (2) enterprise patch deployment cycles average 60-100 days. This project investigates whether using historical cve disclosure and patch deployment timeline data (from nvd, shodan, or academic studies on patch adoption rates), estimate the distribution of currently active exploitation windows.

# Approach Outline

Using historical CVE disclosure and patch deployment timeline data (from NVD, Shodan, or academic studies on patch adoption rates), estimate the distribution of currently active exploitation windows. Parameterize the DARPA AIxCC finding (45 minutes per vulnerability, $152 per task) to compute how many vulnerabilities could be discovered per day at a given AI compute budget. Simulate the exploitation window distribution under AI-accelerated discovery.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If AI-accelerated zero-day discovery shifts vulnerability economics from 'rare expensive zero-days' to 'abundant cheap zero-days', the entire defensive security paradigm (patch before exploitation) becomes inadequate. This compounding risk requires new defensive architectures (runtime protection, behavioral detection) rather than faster patching.
