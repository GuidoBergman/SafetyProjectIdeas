# Research Question

Multi-step attack failures may stem from planning horizon limitations: AI can plan 2-3 steps ahead but not 10-15 steps. This project investigates whether design attack scenarios with varying planning horizons: (a) 2-step plan (near), (b) 5-step plan (medium), (c) 10-step plan (far), (d) 15-step plan (very far).

# Approach Outline

Design attack scenarios with varying planning horizons: (A) 2-step plan (near), (B) 5-step plan (medium), (C) 10-step plan (far), (D) 15-step plan (very far). For each, present the model with the same starting state and ask it to both plan and execute. Measure whether execution quality degrades with horizon length and whether models that plan further ahead actually execute better.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If AI planning horizon is limited to 3-5 steps, then long attack chains will always require human guidance at the decision points beyond the horizon. This is a concrete architectural safety property with implications for how to design detection tripwires at the human-AI handoff points.
