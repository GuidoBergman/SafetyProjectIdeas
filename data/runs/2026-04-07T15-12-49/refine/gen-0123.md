# Research Question

AI agents that could reliably predict their own failure probability before attempting an attack step would be qualitatively different tools — they could request human assistance exactly when needed. This project investigates whether for each step of a multi-step attack chain, prompt the model to state its confidence (0-100%) that its planned action will succeed before executing it.

# Approach Outline

For each step of a multi-step attack chain, prompt the model to state its confidence (0-100%) that its planned action will succeed before executing it. After execution, record actual success/failure. Across 200+ step attempts, compute calibration metrics (Brier score, calibration curve). Test whether over-confident models fail more catastrophically than well-calibrated ones.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Well-calibrated confidence estimates would enable a 'human-in-loop trigger' system: when the AI's own confidence falls below a threshold, it escalates to a human operator. Poorly calibrated confidence means the system cannot know when it needs help — a safety-critical property for any deployed AI offensive agent.
