# Research Question

Two interacting failures: (1) security workforce skill gaps are increasing (SANS 2026: 35% of orgs report moderate gaps); (2) AI automation increases dependency on tools that may fail. This project investigates whether survey 20 cs students on their ability to manually perform tasks that ai security tools automate (manual code review for common vulnerability classes, log analysis, patch validation).

# Approach Outline

Survey 20 CS students on their ability to manually perform tasks that AI security tools automate (manual code review for common vulnerability classes, log analysis, patch validation). Measure performance without AI assistance vs. with. Quantify the skill atrophy risk: how much worse do AI-assisted reviewers perform when the AI is unavailable?.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Conduct a systematic literature review to identify and categorize relevant prior work.
- Develop a classification framework and apply it to the collected corpus.
- Synthesize findings into a structured analysis with key takeaways for the AI safety community.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Organizations optimizing for AI-assisted security may be building brittle postures. A supply chain attack on the AI tool itself (like LiteLLM) could simultaneously disable security tools and exploit the resulting skill vacuum.
