# Mutation Testing for Cyber Benchmarks: Measuring Robustness of AI Performance to Challenge Perturbations

**Problem:** AI models may be solving CTF challenges through memorization of training data rather than genuine reasoning. If a model has seen a challenge during training, solve rates are inflated. No systematic methodology exists to detect this contamination in cyber benchmarks.

**Direction:** Apply mutation testing principles from software engineering: take 10 known public CTF challenges and create 5 variants each by modifying surface features (changing IP addresses, port numbers, hash values, variable names) while preserving the underlying vulnerability class. Compare solve rates on originals vs. mutations. Large performance drops on mutations indicate memorization rather than generalization.

**Why it matters:** Benchmark contamination is a known problem in NLP benchmarks and almost certainly affects cyber benchmarks too, given models are trained on internet data including CTF writeups. Without decontamination methodology, capability measurements may be systematically inflated.

**Relevant context:** Cybench reached 82% (Irregular 2026), raising questions about whether this reflects genuine capability or memorization. CAIBench (arxiv.org/html/2510.24317v1) addressed saturation concerns. NYU CTF Bench noted challenges with reuse. Software testing mutation analysis (Gopinath et al., arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11303) provides transferable methodology.
