# Compounding Failure: Rapid Capability Scaling × Absence of Defended Evaluations — Safety Margin Invisibility

**Problem:** Two interacting failure modes: (1) AI offensive capability is scaling rapidly with no plateau (AISI 2026), and (2) all evaluations use undefended targets. If capability against undefended targets doubles every few months but performance against defended targets is qualitatively different, the real-world safety margin is completely invisible to evaluators.

**Direction:** Create a small study comparing AI agent performance on: (A) an undefended CTF box (standard), and (B) the same box with a basic intrusion detection system (Snort/Suricata with default rules) active. Quantify the performance degradation ratio. Use this as a lower bound estimate of how much undefended benchmarks overestimate real-world capability.

**Why it matters:** Policy decisions about AI offensive capability rely on benchmark data that systematically excludes the most important real-world variable (active defense). This compounding creates an invisible safety margin problem that could lead to both over- and under-regulation.

**Relevant context:** AISI scaling paper found no plateau (arxiv.org/abs/2603.11214). OCCULT called out absence of active defenders. HTB AI Range (Dec 2025) is the first platform with defender simulation but no published AI-vs-active-defender results exist. CrowdStrike AI vs AI arms race blog documents this gap.
