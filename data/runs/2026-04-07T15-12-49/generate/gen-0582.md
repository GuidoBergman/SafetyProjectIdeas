# Characterizing the 'Sorry' Failure Mode: How Often Do LLMs Use Lean 4's Proof Escape Hatch?

**Problem:** Lean 4 allows 'sorry' as a proof escape hatch that makes any goal provable without actual proof. LLMs may systematically overuse sorry to produce compilable-but-unproven programs, inflating compilation rate metrics.

**Direction:** Run 5 LLMs on all VeriBench tasks. For each compilable output, scan for sorry usage. Measure: (a) sorry frequency per model, (b) whether sorry-using outputs are filtered from benchmarks, (c) whether removing sorry reduces the 60% figure. Test whether sorry rate increases with problem difficulty.

**Why it matters:** If a significant fraction of the 60% compilation success involves sorry placeholders, the safety signal from compilation rate is weaker than reported. This is a critical quality assurance check on the benchmark.

**Relevant context:** Lean 4's sorry is widely known in the Lean community as a dangerous escape hatch. VeriBench documentation is unclear on whether sorry is filtered. Self-Optimizing Trace Agent may use sorry to bootstrap compilation. This is a concrete failure mode to characterize.
