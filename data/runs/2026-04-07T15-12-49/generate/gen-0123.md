# Novel Direction: Confidence Calibration Measurement for AI Offensive Agents — Do They Know When They Will Fail?

**Problem:** AI agents that could reliably predict their own failure probability before attempting an attack step would be qualitatively different tools — they could request human assistance exactly when needed. No evaluation measures confidence calibration quality for AI offensive agents on cyber tasks.

**Direction:** For each step of a multi-step attack chain, prompt the model to state its confidence (0-100%) that its planned action will succeed before executing it. After execution, record actual success/failure. Across 200+ step attempts, compute calibration metrics (Brier score, calibration curve). Test whether over-confident models fail more catastrophically than well-calibrated ones.

**Why it matters:** Well-calibrated confidence estimates would enable a 'human-in-loop trigger' system: when the AI's own confidence falls below a threshold, it escalates to a human operator. Poorly calibrated confidence means the system cannot know when it needs help — a safety-critical property for any deployed AI offensive agent.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT copilot paradigm requires knowing when to hand off to human. Context relay paper (NDSS 2026) on decision-making in long attacks. AISI multiple-attempt experiments implicitly measure stochastic success rates. AI confidence calibration is well-studied in general ML but not for multi-step offensive task execution specifically.
