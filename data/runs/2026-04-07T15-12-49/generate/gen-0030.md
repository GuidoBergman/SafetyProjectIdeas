# Backcast from Verifiable Safe Harbor: What Test Results Would Demonstrate AI Cannot Cross a Critical Threshold?

**Problem:** Current evaluations only measure what AI can do, never what it demonstrably cannot do. A safety framework requires the ability to certify that a capability threshold has NOT been crossed, not just document failures. No methodology exists for generating credible negative capability claims.

**Direction:** Define a specific capability claim: 'AI cannot complete privilege escalation from user to domain admin in an Active Directory environment without human assistance.' Design a test with statistical power analysis to reject this claim if false (N challenges, success criterion, significance threshold). Run the test and produce a confidence interval on the negative claim, not just a point estimate.

**Why it matters:** Responsible AI deployment frameworks (EU AI Act, US EO on AI) require evidence of safety, not just observed failures. A methodology for credible negative capability claims is necessary for any serious pre-deployment safety evaluation protocol.

**Relevant context:** AISI Frontier AI Trends Report addresses threshold certification. UK AISI evaluation methodology uses multi-attempt runs that provide some statistical grounding. SoK called for 'fine-grained benchmarks for risk assessment'. Statistical power analysis methods are standard in experimental psychology and medical research.
