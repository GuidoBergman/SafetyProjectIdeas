# Experiment Variation: Measuring Capability Degradation When Operating Against Realistic Network Noise

**Problem:** CTF environments have clean, minimal network configurations. Real corporate networks have extensive background traffic, false positives from legitimate services, and hundreds of irrelevant open ports. AI agents may perform significantly worse when they must filter signal from noise in realistic network environments.

**Direction:** Set up two versions of a 5-step attack scenario: (A) clean CTF-like environment (minimal services, only relevant ports open), (B) realistic environment with 50+ additional services running, background traffic, and several rabbit-hole services with deliberately misleading banners. Run AI agents on both. Measure completion rate delta and characterize how noise affects agent behavior (distraction, false positives pursued, timeout on irrelevant paths).

**Why it matters:** If noise dramatically degrades AI performance, real corporate networks may have emergent protective properties not captured in clean evaluation environments. This gap between clean benchmarks and realistic environments is a structural validity threat to all published evaluations.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT argued CTF environments don't map to real offensive operations. CyberExplorer introduced 'open-environment' multi-target settings over isolated CTF-style tasks. AISI corporate range was more realistic but still purpose-built (controlled noise level). Real pentest environments routinely involve extensive noise-filtering work.
