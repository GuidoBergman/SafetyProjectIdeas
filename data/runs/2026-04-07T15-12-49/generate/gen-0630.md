# LLM Performance on CSLib-Style Problems vs. Mathlib-Style Problems: Is CS Verification Harder Than Math?

**Problem:** CSLib targets computer science algorithms (sorting, searching, graphs); Mathlib targets pure mathematics (topology, algebra, number theory). Whether LLMs perform differently on CS-style vs. math-style Lean 4 problems — and why — is unknown.

**Direction:** Sample 30 CS-style problems (algorithm verification) and 30 math-style problems of similar proof length from Lean 4 benchmarks. Run the same LLMs on both sets. Measure compilation rate, proof success rate, and failure mode distribution. Test whether CS problems are systematically harder or easier.

**Why it matters:** CSLib is the primary target for safety-relevant formal verification. If CS-style proofs are harder than math-style proofs for LLMs, the safety applications of formal verification are harder than benchmark scores on Mathlib-derived tasks suggest.

**Relevant context:** Mathlib has the most Lean 4 training data. CSLib is newer and smaller. Whether models trained on Mathlib transfer to CS verification tasks is an important generalization question for the CSLib project.
