# Offense-Defense Framing Analysis: How Different AI Labs Characterize Dual-Use Risks in Public Documents

**Problem:** AI labs publish risk reports, RSPs, and safety cards, but whether their framing systematically emphasizes offense risk over defense benefit (or vice versa) has not been analyzed.

**Direction:** Collect cybersecurity-related sections from 5 labs' public governance documents (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, Google DeepMind, Meta safety card, Mistral). Apply quantitative text analysis: count offense/defense framing terms, measure sentiment toward each framing, identify which harms are foregrounded. Compare across labs.

**Why it matters:** Framing shapes policy: labs that overweight offense in their documents may over-restrict defenders, while those underweighting offense may under-restrict attackers. Understanding framing bias is prerequisite to correcting it.

**Relevant context:** Linder et al. offense-defense tradeoff; Potter et al. offense>defense finding; previous SAIM idea on jargon gap (note: that idea focused on safety vs. security framing generically—this focuses specifically on offense/defense balance within cybersecurity framing).
