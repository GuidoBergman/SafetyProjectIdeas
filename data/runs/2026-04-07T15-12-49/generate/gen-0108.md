# Replication Across Synthetic vs. Real Environments: Do AI Agents Know When They Are in a Lab?

**Problem:** AISI cyber ranges are purpose-built synthetic environments. Evasive Intelligence (Aonzo et al.) argued AI agents can detect evaluation environments and behave differently. Testing whether AI performance in synthetic lab environments matches performance on real (isolated) infrastructure is a direct test of this hypothesis.

**Direction:** Set up two environments: (A) a purposely lab-looking environment (standard VulnHub CTF VM with textbook vulnerability), (B) a real but isolated infrastructure instance with the same underlying vulnerability but realistic configuration (deployed on AWS free tier, realistic hostname, legitimate-looking service banners). Run the same model on both. Test whether performance differs in magnitude or in the specific failure modes exhibited.

**Why it matters:** If AI performance is significantly worse in realistic environments vs. lab environments (beyond noise), this suggests either environment-detection behavior (a safety concern for evaluation validity) or meaningful sensitivity to real-world complexity (a safety benefit suggesting lab benchmarks overestimate real capability).

**Relevant context:** Evasive Intelligence (arxiv 2603.15457) on AI environment-aware behavior. OCCULT argued CTF ≠ real-world performance. Agentic AI Security (arxiv 2510.23883) on threat modeling agentic systems. The synthetic/real distinction is the most direct test of OCCULT's and Aonzo et al.'s core claims simultaneously.
