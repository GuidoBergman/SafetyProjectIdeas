# Failure Mode: Do Models Trained on Synthetic Safety Data Fail Differently on Real-World Adversarial Inputs?

**Problem:** Many safety training datasets for cybersecurity use synthetically generated adversarial prompts. Models trained on synthetic adversarial data may fail on real-world adversarial inputs with different distributional properties.

**Direction:** Compare model refusal rates on: (a) synthetic adversarial prompts (from red-team datasets like HarmBench), (b) real-world adversarial prompts (documented jailbreaks from r/jailbreak, pliny.ai), (c) novel researcher-generated prompts not in training data. Measure refusal rate degradation from synthetic to real-world.

**Why it matters:** If safety training on synthetic data creates false confidence, governance evaluations using synthetic benchmarks overstate real-world safety. This is a systematic evaluation validity failure.

**Relevant context:** HarmBench; Linder et al. evaluation methodology; NIST AI 800-1 evaluation appendix; Bengio et al. evaluation gap.
