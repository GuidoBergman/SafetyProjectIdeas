# Compounding Failure: Rapid Capability Growth × No Standardized Evaluation Protocol — Incomparable Safety Assessments

**Problem:** Two interacting failure modes: (1) AI offensive capability is growing rapidly, and (2) no standardized evaluation protocol exists, so each lab uses different methodologies. Labs comparing their models against each other or against historical baselines are comparing incomparable measurements — potentially creating dangerous false reassurance or false alarm.

**Direction:** Collect methodology descriptions from 5 published AI cyber evaluations (AISI, DeepMind, OCCULT, Cybench, AIxCC). For each, extract: token budget, scaffolding type, target environment, evaluation time limits, success criteria, human baseline inclusion. Compute a methodology compatibility matrix. Propose a minimum standardization protocol that would enable cross-study comparison and test whether applying it to 2 studies yields more comparable results.

**Why it matters:** Without comparability, the field cannot assess whether safety evaluations across labs are producing consistent or divergent conclusions. This methodological inconsistency is itself a safety risk if it allows capabilities to be systematically underestimated through incompatible comparisons.

**Relevant context:** AISI noted no standardized longitudinal comparison methodology. Benchmarking Practices paper (arxiv 2504.10112) analyzed methodological variations. CAIBench proposed meta-benchmark integration. OCCULT proposed standardization criteria. Multiple independent lab evaluations exist but methodologies are incompatible.
