# Measuring AI Capability Transfer: Do Models That Excel at Cyber CTFs Also Excel at Real Bug Bounty Tasks?

**Problem:** CTF challenges are designed for competitions, not real-world security. It is unknown whether high CTF performance transfers to real-world bug bounty-style tasks (finding vulnerabilities in real open-source software). The transfer coefficient has not been measured.

**Direction:** Select 5 historical bug bounty findings (from public bug bounty disclosure databases: HackerOne, Bugcrowd) that were disclosed before model training cutoffs. Present these as blind challenges to 3-4 models (providing the vulnerable source code). Compare solve rates to solve rates on CTF challenges of matched difficulty. Compute a CTF-to-real-world transfer ratio.

**Why it matters:** MITRE OCCULT's central thesis is that CTF performance does not predict real-world offensive capability. This is the most direct empirical test of that thesis. If CTF-to-real-world transfer is high, OCCULT is wrong and CTFs are valid proxies. If transfer is low, OCCULT is validated.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT (arxiv 2502.15797) argued CTF ≠ real-world capability. DARPA AIxCC: AI found 18 previously unknown real-world flaws in production open-source code. LLM-CVX benchmark (ACM 2025) tested CVE exploitation. HackerOne 2025 disclosure database provides public challenge material. Direct empirical test of OCCULT's core claim.
