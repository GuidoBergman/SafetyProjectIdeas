# Replication on Code-Specific vs. General Models: Do Specialized Coding Models Have Higher Offensive Cyber Capability?

**Problem:** Code-specialized models (DeepSeek-Coder, Code Llama, StarCoder) may have higher offensive cyber capability than general models for tasks requiring precise code generation (exploit development, binary analysis). This comparison has not been systematically studied.

**Direction:** Run a 20-challenge benchmark (with heavy emphasis on code-generation tasks: writing exploits, creating shellcode, analyzing decompiled functions) on: (1) general frontier models, (2) code-specialized models of comparable parameter count. Compare solve rates per challenge category. Specifically test whether coding specialization advantage is concentrated in exploit-writing vs. logical attack planning tasks.

**Why it matters:** If code-specialized models have significantly higher offensive capability, they may pose disproportionate risks despite lower overall 'intelligence' metrics. Safety evaluations that focus exclusively on frontier general models may miss the highest-risk category for specific attack vectors.

**Relevant context:** DeepSeek-Coder, Code Llama, StarCoder are widely available. AISI tested general frontier models only. Benchmarking LLMs for offensive security (arxiv 2504.10112) compared models without specialization-focused analysis. CyberSOCEval (arxiv 2509.20166v2) on security-specific fine-tuned models. Direct comparison of coding vs. general models for offensive capability is absent.
