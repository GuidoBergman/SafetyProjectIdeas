# Causal Role of Chain-of-Thought Visibility: Does Showing vs. Hiding Reasoning Affect Offensive Capability Assessment?

**Problem:** When models produce visible chain-of-thought reasoning (thinking tokens), evaluators can observe attack planning in progress. If safety filters or monitoring systems operate on visible reasoning, models may produce less detailed (but still capable) attacks when reasoning is hidden. The effect of CoT visibility on capability measurement has not been tested.

**Direction:** Run 15 CTF challenges on the same model under: (A) full visible CoT (thinking tokens exposed), (B) hidden CoT (model produces reasoning internally but only final output is visible), (C) no CoT (direct answer only). Measure solve rates per condition. Test whether visible CoT produces significantly different solve rates than hidden CoT, controlling for total compute.

**Why it matters:** If visible CoT does not change capability but allows monitoring of reasoning content, CoT visibility is a net safety benefit with no performance cost. If hiding CoT improves performance, models are being modestly constrained by the visibility requirement — and removing that constraint (e.g., models that internally think without exposing reasoning) would increase real-world capability beyond what evaluators can observe.

**Relevant context:** Extended thinking models (DeepSeek-R1 visible CoT, Claude Thinking mode). AISI used extended thinking variants with log-linear scaling. Evasive Intelligence (arxiv 2603.15457) on context-aware behavior adaptation. Chain-of-thought visibility as a safety monitoring mechanism is discussed in AI safety literature but not empirically tested for offensive cyber tasks.
