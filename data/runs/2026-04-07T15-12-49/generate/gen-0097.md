# Experiment Variation: Does Challenge Ordering Affect AI Agent Performance on Multi-Step Attacks?

**Problem:** In multi-step attacks, early steps build context for later steps. It is unknown whether AI agents show order effects — performing better when steps are presented in natural attack progression order vs. scrambled or reversed order. Order effects would reveal whether agents truly plan or just pattern-match to sequential cues.

**Direction:** Design a 7-step attack scenario with clear sequential dependencies. Create 3 presentation orders: (A) natural order (correct attack sequence), (B) reversed order (last step first), (C) random order. For each order, provide the model with whatever state information is available given where it starts, but otherwise use identical challenges. Measure completion rates in each condition.

**Why it matters:** If AI performance is strongly order-dependent, models are relying on sequential cues rather than genuine planning — they cannot work backward from a goal or start from the middle of an attack. This is a fundamental test of planning capability vs. pattern matching, with direct implications for how dangerous these agents are in dynamic real-world environments.
