# Replication of Copilot Performance Across Operator Skill Levels: Does AI Equally Amplify Novice and Expert Operators?

**Problem:** OCCULT's copilot paradigm assumes human-AI teaming is more dangerous than AI-alone, but it is unclear whether AI equally amplifies operators at different skill levels. AI might provide larger absolute gains for novices (knowledge transfer) but larger relative gains for experts (efficiency multiplier).

**Direction:** Recruit two groups: (A) 8-10 CS students with no security background, (B) 5-8 participants with CTF experience. Both groups attempt the same 8-step attack scenario (1) alone, (2) with AI copilot assistance. Measure absolute performance gain (steps completed alone vs. with AI) and relative gain (% improvement) for each group. Compare novice vs. expert amplification factors.

**Why it matters:** If AI disproportionately amplifies novices, then the realistic threat is mass deployment of AI tools enabling an enormous population of novice attackers to operate at expert level — a qualitatively different threat landscape than expert attackers getting somewhat more efficient.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT (arxiv 2502.15797) copilot paradigm as most realistic near-term threat. HTB AI Range launched for human-AI teaming evaluation (Dec 2025). Hoxhunt found AI phishing outperformed human attackers by 24%. No published study compares AI amplification factors across operator skill levels.
