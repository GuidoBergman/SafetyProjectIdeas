# Novel Direction: Real-Time Capability Monitoring Framework for AI Systems During Active Red Team Exercises

**Problem:** Current capability evaluations are point-in-time snapshots. Monitoring AI offensive capability in real-time during extended red team exercises (across multiple sessions, model updates, or scaffolding changes) requires a different methodology. No framework exists for longitudinal within-exercise capability tracking.

**Direction:** Design a lightweight monitoring protocol that can be embedded in an AI red team exercise: (1) a 5-challenge 'capability probe' test run at T=0, T=day3, T=day7, (2) automated scoring of probe results, (3) alert generation if capability change exceeds a statistical threshold between probes. Implement and test on a simulated 7-day red team exercise. Compute sensitivity and specificity of the alert system.

**Why it matters:** As AI systems are deployed in extended red team engagements, their capabilities may shift due to model updates, scaffolding improvements, or accumulated context. Real-time monitoring would provide early warning of capability jumps mid-exercise, enabling organizations to adjust defensive posture dynamically rather than learning about capability changes after the fact.

**Relevant context:** AISI measured 7 model generations but only as post-hoc snapshots. AISI inference scaling blog noted continuous capability growth with increased compute. HTB AI Range designed for continuous benchmarking. No framework for real-time within-exercise capability monitoring exists in published literature.
