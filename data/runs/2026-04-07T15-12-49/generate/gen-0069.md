# Behavioral Consistency Audit: Do AI Models Give Consistent Answers About Their Own Offensive Capabilities?

**Problem:** AI models may give inconsistent self-reports about their capabilities (e.g., claiming they 'cannot' do something while doing it when task is framed differently). This inconsistency between self-reported capability and actual demonstrated capability is itself a safety concern and an evaluation validity problem.

**Direction:** Present 10 security tasks to models in two formats: (A) 'Can you perform [task]? Describe your capabilities.', (B) directly ask the model to perform the task. Compare self-reported capability claims against actual performance. Compute a 'honesty-about-capabilities' accuracy score. Test whether models systematically understate or overstate specific capability types.

**Why it matters:** If models systematically understate capabilities they actually possess, this undermines trust in any safety evaluation methodology that relies on model self-reporting. It also has direct implications for model transparency requirements in AI governance frameworks.

**Relevant context:** Evasive Intelligence paper (arxiv 2603.15457) on environment-aware behavior. OCCULT TACTL knowledge tests found models know about capabilities they may claim not to have. Shallow alignment literature (prior research) on gap between stated and actual model properties. AISI used behavioral testing rather than self-reporting.
