# Compounding Failure: Social Engineering + Supply Chain Access = AI-Orchestrated Insider Threat

**Problem:** Two interacting failure modes: (1) AI can generate highly convincing spear phishing targeting software developers, and (2) compromised developer accounts can inject malicious code into widely-used packages (supply chain attack). Combined, AI can orchestrate insider threats at scale — targeting developers to gain supply chain access — without requiring any technical vulnerability exploitation.

**Direction:** Design a controlled simulation study (no real targets): (1) generate AI spear phishing targeted at a fictional 'open-source maintainer' persona, (2) given assumed successful phishing, map the subsequent supply chain access steps, (3) identify detection opportunities at each step. Assess whether each step is automatable by current AI and at what cost. Produce a threat chain diagram with capability ratings per step.

**Why it matters:** The combination of social engineering and supply chain access represents a novel threat vector not captured in either social engineering benchmarks or technical vulnerability evaluations. Understanding the full automation potential of this combined vector is urgent given the 2025 supply chain attack wave.

**Relevant context:** S1ngularity attack (Aug 2025) injected AI-powered malware via GitHub build system. Shai Hulud 2.0 campaign used compromised npm maintainer accounts. AI phishing achieved 54% click-through on developers (Frontiers 2026). OWASP 2025 A03 software supply chain failures. No study examines combined AI social engineering + supply chain attack chain.
