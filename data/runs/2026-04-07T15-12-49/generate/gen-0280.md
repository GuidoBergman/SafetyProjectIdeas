# Experiment: Comparing AI Vulnerability Detection Performance Across Severity vs. Prevalence Dimensions

**Problem:** AI scanners may be better at detecting common vulnerability classes (high training data representation) than rare but severe ones. The relationship between CVE prevalence and AI detection rate is not characterized.

**Direction:** Select 30 CVEs: 10 from the three most common CWE categories (ranked by NVD prevalence), 10 from medium-prevalence categories, 10 from rare categories. Run AI scanner. Measure detection rate by prevalence tier. Test whether detection rate correlates with CWE prevalence.

**Why it matters:** If AI detection is biased toward common vulnerability classes, rare but severe zero-days or novel attack patterns are systematically missed. This has direct implications for AI defense reliability against novel threats.

**Relevant context:** CWE Top 25 Most Dangerous Software Weaknesses provides prevalence data. AI vulnerability detection training data distribution effects not studied. This directly tests the 'training data availability = detection quality' hypothesis.
