# Compounding Risks: RLHF Reward Hacking × Cybersecurity Over-Restriction — When Safety Training Degrades Legitimate Defense

**Problem:** RLHF optimizes for human preference ratings. If annotators are more likely to prefer cautious refusals for cybersecurity requests, reward hacking may cause models to over-refuse defensive tasks—a governance overcorrection.

**Direction:** Design a study testing whether preference raters (recruited via crowd-sourcing) systematically prefer refusals for borderline defensive cybersecurity requests (analyzing malware, explaining CVEs). Compare rating distributions against expert ratings. Estimate the magnitude of any over-cautious bias.

**Why it matters:** If RLHF systematically installs over-cautious cybersecurity refusal due to annotator bias, the training process itself is a governance failure mode—producing worse security outcomes while appearing to improve safety metrics.
