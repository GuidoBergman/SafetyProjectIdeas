# Measuring Target Complexity Tolerance: How Quickly Does AI Performance Degrade as Target Complexity Increases?

**Problem:** AI cyber capability benchmarks use environments with a fixed complexity level. Real targets vary enormously in complexity (10-service CTF box vs. 500-service enterprise network). The performance degradation function as a function of target complexity has not been characterized.

**Direction:** Design a series of pentesting environments with varying complexity: (A) 3 services, 1 vulnerability, (B) 10 services, 2 vulnerabilities, (C) 30 services, 3 vulnerabilities, (D) 100 services simulated, 5 vulnerabilities. Run AI agents on all 4 environments. Plot performance vs. complexity. Test whether degradation is linear, exponential, or shows a complexity threshold effect.

**Why it matters:** If AI performance degrades gracefully with complexity, real enterprise networks (much more complex than CTF environments) may be naturally protected by their complexity. If AI performance is complexity-tolerant, the threat to real complex environments is as severe as CTF benchmark scores suggest.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT argued that single-target CTF tasks don't reflect real multi-target complex enterprise environments. CyberExplorer specifically introduced multi-target settings to address this. AISI corporate range with 32 steps implies some complexity tolerance. No systematic complexity scaling study exists.
