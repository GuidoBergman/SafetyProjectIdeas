# Evaluation Under Adversarial Conditions: Can Models Maintain Performance When Given Misleading Target Information?

**Problem:** Real attack environments contain false positives, honeypots, and misleading defender signals designed to waste attacker resources. AI agents evaluated in clean, truthful environments may fail catastrophically when environmental information is adversarially corrupted — a resilience property not measured in any benchmark.

**Direction:** Take a 10-step attack chain. Create two variants: (A) clean environment with accurate information, (B) adversarial environment with 30% of environmental signals falsified (fake open ports, misleading service banners, honeypot directories). Run models on both. Measure completion rate delta. Categorize model failures as: (a) failed to identify deception, (b) identified deception but wrong response, (c) correctly adapted.

**Why it matters:** If AI agents fail badly when defenders actively deceive them, deception-based defense is a viable and under-explored countermeasure. If agents are resilient, defenders cannot rely on honeypots against AI attackers. This is a novel capability dimension with direct defensive implications.

**Relevant context:** Evasive Intelligence paper (arxiv 2603.15457) on environment-aware agent behavior. AISI used truthful environments. OCCULT noted artificial constraints as evaluation limitations. Active deception/honeypots are a standard defensive technique in network security but have never been tested against AI offensive agents.
