# Measuring the 'Horizon Effect': How Far Ahead Can AI Plan During a Multi-Step Attack?

**Problem:** Multi-step attack failures may stem from planning horizon limitations: AI can plan 2-3 steps ahead but not 10-15 steps. The effective planning horizon of current models in attack contexts has not been measured empirically.

**Direction:** Design attack scenarios with varying planning horizons: (A) 2-step plan (near), (B) 5-step plan (medium), (C) 10-step plan (far), (D) 15-step plan (very far). For each, present the model with the same starting state and ask it to both plan and execute. Measure whether execution quality degrades with horizon length and whether models that plan further ahead actually execute better.

**Why it matters:** If AI planning horizon is limited to 3-5 steps, then long attack chains will always require human guidance at the decision points beyond the horizon. This is a concrete architectural safety property with implications for how to design detection tripwires at the human-AI handoff points.

**Relevant context:** AISI attributed some failures to 'long-horizon execution failure'. Context relay paper (NDSS 2026) specifically addresses context degradation in long-running attacks. AWS multi-agent architecture used specialized high-level strategy component + low-level execution agent to extend effective horizon. This is a direct test of the planning horizon hypothesis.
