# Population Ecology Applied to AI Cyber Attack Diversity: Measuring Ecological Diversity Indices for AI-Generated Exploit Portfolios

**Problem:** As AI-generated exploits proliferate, the diversity of attack vectors in the wild may change. If AI systems generate homogeneous exploits, the distribution of attack types becomes less diverse (lower ecological diversity). No methodology exists for measuring attack portfolio diversity over time.

**Direction:** Apply Shannon entropy and Simpson's diversity index (used in ecology to measure species diversity) to: (1) a corpus of AI-generated exploits for a fixed vulnerability class (generated in this study), (2) a historical corpus of human-generated exploits from public exploit databases. Compute and compare diversity indices. Test whether AI-generated exploits have significantly lower diversity than human-generated exploits.

**Why it matters:** If AI-generated attacks reduce exploit diversity (monoculture effect), a single defensive signature has higher coverage value and defenders gain a systematic advantage. If AI increases exploit diversity (through generative exploration), signature-based defense becomes less effective. This has direct implications for defensive strategy.

**Relevant context:** Exploit-db and Metasploit databases provide human-generated exploit corpora. SoK Potter et al. on offense/defense dynamics. Model monoculture concerns. Shannon entropy and Simpson's index are standard, simple statistical measures. CyberExplorer found distinct attack trajectories per model suggesting some diversity exists at model level.
