# Lean 4 as a Curriculum: Does Structured Learning of Lean 4 Tactics Improve LLM-Assisted Formal Verification?

**Problem:** Presenting Lean 4 tactic examples in a structured curriculum order (simple to complex) vs. a random order in few-shot prompts may affect LLM proof generation quality. Whether curriculum structure in prompting helps is unknown.

**Direction:** Create two prompting conditions: (a) 5 few-shot examples in order of increasing complexity (easy to hard), (b) 5 examples in random order, (c) 5 examples in decreasing complexity. Run on 40 VeriBench tasks. Measure compilation rate per condition. Test the curriculum hypothesis.

**Why it matters:** If curriculum order in few-shot examples improves Lean 4 proof generation, it is a zero-cost improvement to current prompting strategies. This would identify a new dimension of prompt engineering specific to formal verification.

**Relevant context:** Curriculum learning improves training efficiency for neural networks. Whether analogous curriculum structure in few-shot prompting improves in-context learning for formal verification has not been tested.
