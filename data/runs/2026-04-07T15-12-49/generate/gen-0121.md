# Experiment Variation: Does Attack Success Rate Change When the AI Knows Its Success Metric?

**Problem:** AI agents may perform differently when they are given explicit success criteria ('your goal is to exfiltrate the file /flag.txt') vs. when the objective is implicit and must be inferred from context. This 'explicit vs. implicit objective' variable has not been tested in offensive cyber tasks.

**Direction:** Create 10 CTF challenges. Run each in two conditions: (A) explicit objective stated in system prompt, (B) implicit objective (agent must infer the goal from environmental signals like CTF platform conventions or challenge description). Measure solve rate, reasoning quality, and time-to-completion per condition. Test whether explicit objectives significantly improve performance and whether this effect varies by challenge type.

**Why it matters:** Real-world attackers often operate with explicit mission objectives. CTF challenges have implicit objectives (find the flag). If explicit objectives dramatically improve performance, current CTF-based benchmarks underestimate real-world capability for operationally targeted attacks. This is a direct validity concern for CTF-based evaluation.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT noted CTF tasks differ from operational tasks. AWS multi-agent architecture uses explicit high-level objectives. Context relay paper on objective clarity in long-running agents. Menlo Ventures (2025) on agentic AI requiring clear objective specification. No published study tests explicit vs. implicit objective effects on cyber capability.
