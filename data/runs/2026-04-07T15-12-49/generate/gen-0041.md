# Zero-Day vs. Known CVE Performance: Measuring the Gap Between Knowledge and Novel Reasoning

**Problem:** Models are trained on data that includes CVE writeups, exploit databases, and CTF solutions. Their performance on known vulnerabilities may be dramatically higher than on novel or zero-day-class vulnerabilities. This gap has not been systematically measured.

**Direction:** Create two parallel challenge sets: (1) 10 challenges based on well-documented CVEs published before model training cutoffs (high likelihood of training data contamination), (2) 10 novel challenges implementing the same vulnerability classes but with distinct code and no published writeups. Compare solve rates. Test whether performance delta correlates with vulnerability class complexity.

**Why it matters:** If models significantly underperform on novel vulnerabilities vs. known CVEs, AI offensive capability is more limited than benchmark scores suggest. If the gap is small, models are genuinely generalizing — a more alarming finding. Directly addresses the contamination vs. capability question.

**Relevant context:** DARPA AIxCC (2025): teams found 18 previously unknown real-world flaws alongside 43 synthetic ones — suggesting some zero-day capability exists. LLM-CVX framework (ACM 2025) specifically evaluated CVE exploitation. OCCULT found models can answer 90%+ on knowledge tests (TACTL benchmark) but this does not imply novel exploitation ability.
