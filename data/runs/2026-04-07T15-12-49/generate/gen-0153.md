# Backcasting from Robust AI Defense Against Novel Attack Patterns: What Training Data Is Required?

**Problem:** AI defenders trained on historical attack patterns may fail against novel ones. For robust defense, models must generalize. What training data characteristics support generalization?

**Direction:** Use a public IDS benchmark (UNSW-NB15 or CIC-IDS2018) to train a simple anomaly detection model. Systematically vary the diversity of training attack categories. Measure detection rate on held-out novel attack categories as a function of training diversity. Identify the minimum diversity threshold for acceptable generalization.

**Why it matters:** Zero-day and novel attacks are highest-risk. If AI defense requires seeing attack patterns to defend against them, the defense will always lag offense.

**Relevant context:** Transfer learning for cybersecurity (arXiv 2403.00935) and cross-domain vulnerability detection research identify generalization as a core challenge. No systematic study of training diversity as a lever.
