# Epidemiology-Inspired Diffusion Model for AI-Enabled Cyber Attack Spread Rate

**Problem:** AI offensive tools lower the cost and skill barrier for cyberattacks, which increases attacker population size. How fast attacks propagate across a vulnerable ecosystem when AI-enabled attackers operate at machine speed has not been modeled.

**Direction:** Adapt a standard SIR (Susceptible-Infected-Recovered) epidemiological model to cyber attack diffusion. Parameterize with: (1) attack speed (from AISI benchmarks: ~45 minutes per attack), (2) vulnerability prevalence in a simulated network of 100 nodes, (3) defender response time. Simulate under human-speed vs. AI-speed attack regimes. Measure outbreak size and peak infection rate.

**Why it matters:** AI-speed cyber attacks may create outbreak dynamics qualitatively different from human-speed attacks, potentially overwhelming defenders before response is possible. This model would provide quantitative predictions about whether AI capability thresholds correspond to phase transitions in attack spread.

**Relevant context:** DARPA AIxCC: average remediation in 45 minutes vs. industry norm of weeks. AISI: $80 per 100M-token attempt makes mass deployment economically viable. NCSC: 'frontier AI can hack your network for £65'. SIR models are widely applied in cyber security (malware propagation literature) but not yet parameterized with AI capability data.
