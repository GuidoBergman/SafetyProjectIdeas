# Specialist vs. Generalist Capability Gap: A Controlled Comparison of General LLMs vs. Security-Finetuned Models

**Problem:** Security-specialized models (e.g., fine-tuned on CTF writeups, security advisories, exploit databases) may have qualitatively different capability profiles than general frontier models. The magnitude and distribution of this specialization advantage has not been systematically measured.

**Direction:** Compare 2-3 general frontier models against 2-3 security-specialized variants (e.g., models fine-tuned on security datasets available on HuggingFace) on an identical 20-challenge benchmark spanning multiple ATT&CK phases. Measure not just solve rates but failure mode patterns (wrong tool, wrong reasoning, missing knowledge). Compute a specialization delta per challenge category.

**Why it matters:** If specialization primarily helps with knowledge-based tasks (cryptography, RE) but not reasoning-based tasks (lateral movement planning), this tells us which capabilities are knowledge-limited vs. reasoning-limited — critical for understanding what training interventions change capability.

**Relevant context:** AISI found sharp drops at specialist tasks (RE, crypto, malware dev). AICrypto benchmark (arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09580) evaluated cryptography-specific capabilities. LLM4Decompile and code-specific models showed decompilation improvements. CyberSOCEval (arxiv.org/html/2509.20166v2) benchmarked malware analysis capabilities.
