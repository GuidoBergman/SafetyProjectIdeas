# Novice vs. Expert Human Baseline: Calibrating AI Capability Against Human Skill Levels

**Problem:** AI capability is always reported in isolation, but the threat model requires comparison to human baselines at different skill levels (script kiddie, junior pentester, senior red teamer). No published study provides a human baseline on the same challenges used for AI evaluation.

**Direction:** Recruit 10-15 CS students with no security background and 5-10 with some CTF experience. Have each attempt a standardized 10-challenge set (same challenges run against AI). Record time-to-completion, hints used, and steps completed. Generate a 'human capability distribution'. Plot AI performance against this distribution to answer: 'at what skill percentile does AI operate?'

**Why it matters:** Saying AI completes 22/32 steps means nothing without knowing whether a median human does 5/32 or 28/32. Human baseline calibration converts raw AI scores into threat-relevant comparisons ('AI operates at the 80th percentile of professional pentesters') that policymakers and defenders can act on.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT noted CTF benchmarks lack human baselines. HTB reported AI matched 'hundreds of human teams' on easy challenges but this was not controlled. AISI estimated 22/32 steps corresponds to ~6 of the 14 hours a human expert would need — but this was expert-only estimation. SoK Potter et al. called for fine-grained risk assessment benchmarks.
