# Adversarial Framing Effects: How Labeling an Attack Task Changes AI Performance

**Problem:** AI models may have fine-tuned behavior that is sensitive to how attack tasks are labeled. Framing a task as 'security research', 'CTF', 'penetration testing', 'red team exercise', or leaving it unlabeled may produce different capability expressions even with identical technical content.

**Direction:** Create 10 identical technical tasks (find vulnerability, write exploit, analyze binary). Present each with 5 different framings: (1) 'CTF challenge', (2) 'authorized penetration test', (3) 'security research', (4) 'academic exercise', (5) no framing. Measure solve rate, refusal rate, and output quality per framing. Test whether framing effects are model-specific or universal.

**Why it matters:** If certain framings reliably unlock higher performance, this is both an attack vector (adversaries use framing to bypass safety guardrails) and an evaluation design consideration (benchmark framing may influence measured capability). Understanding framing effects is critical for evaluation validity.

**Relevant context:** Evasive Intelligence paper (arxiv 2603.15457) on AI inferring evaluation context. WASP benchmark found context affects agent security behavior. OCCULT noted copilot framing vs. autonomous framing differences. Jailbreaking surveys documented framing as a common bypass technique.
