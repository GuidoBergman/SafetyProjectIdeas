# Neuroscience-Inspired Capability Profiling: Dual-Process Theory Applied to AI Attack Reasoning

**Problem:** Cognitive science distinguishes System 1 (fast, intuitive) from System 2 (slow, deliberate) reasoning. AI models with extended chain-of-thought may be more analogous to System 2 (deliberate reasoning), while base completions are more System 1. Offensive tasks may differentially benefit from each mode.

**Direction:** Run offensive challenges under: (1) direct completion (no CoT, analogous to System 1), (2) short CoT (3-5 reasoning steps), (3) extended CoT (20+ reasoning steps). Categorize challenges by whether they require pattern recognition (System 1-favoring) or multi-step planning (System 2-favoring). Measure whether the benefit of extended CoT is concentrated in planning-heavy tasks.

**Why it matters:** If extended CoT disproportionately helps multi-step planning tasks (the hardest offensive bottleneck), this predicts that continued scaling of reasoning models will specifically accelerate the hardest attack steps. If CoT helps uniformly, the benefit is less targeted.

**Relevant context:** AISI found log-linear scaling with inference compute (reasoning tokens). Extended thinking models (DeepSeek-R1, Claude Thinking variants) show significant gains on multi-step reasoning. ARC challenge research on extended CoT. AISI: 10M→100M tokens yields 59% gain — consistent with deliberate reasoning engagement.
