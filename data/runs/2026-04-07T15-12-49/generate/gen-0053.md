# Replication of Phishing Capability Benchmarks Across Non-English Languages

**Problem:** AI phishing capability studies (Hoxhunt, academic studies) are primarily conducted in English. Real-world cybersecurity threats are global. AI phishing capability in languages with less training data (Portuguese, Arabic, Hindi, Swahili) may differ significantly from English performance.

**Direction:** Take 5 phishing scenario templates in English and translate to 4 other languages (Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic, Mandarin). Generate phishing emails in each language using 3 API models. Have native speaker raters (can recruit via academic departments or online communities) score naturalness, persuasiveness, and technical accuracy. Compute cross-language capability profiles.

**Why it matters:** If AI phishing capability drops dramatically in low-resource languages, non-English-speaking populations may have a relative safety margin. If capability is uniformly high across languages, global AI-enabled phishing is an immediate threat. This has direct relevance to international AI safety governance.

**Relevant context:** Frontiers 2026 paper on agentic AI phishing documented English-language capability. Hoxhunt 2025 benchmark was English-focused. SoK Potter et al. noted human-targeted attacks as the highest-impact current AI threat globally. Language capability variation is a known phenomenon in LLM research but not studied in offensive capability context.
