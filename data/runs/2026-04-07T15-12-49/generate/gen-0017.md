# Compounding Failure: AI Offensive Capability × Benchmark Contamination — When Inflated Scores Meet Real-World Deployments

**Problem:** Two interacting failure modes: (1) cyber benchmarks are contaminated by training data (models memorize CTF writeups), and (2) AI is deployed in offensive operations based on benchmark scores. If scores are inflated by memorization, real-world capability is lower — but this also means the ceiling is closer than it appears once models train on newer data.

**Direction:** Quantify contamination risk by testing models on (a) public CTF challenges with widely-available writeups online vs. (b) modified versions of the same challenges. Measure performance delta. Cross-reference with model training data cutoffs. Estimate a 'contamination-adjusted capability score' and analyze what policy decisions would change if this score were used instead.

**Why it matters:** Inflated benchmark scores could lead to premature alarm or false reassurance depending on direction. Understanding contamination-adjusted capability provides more accurate input for safety thresholds and regulatory decisions.

**Relevant context:** CAIBench noted CTF saturation (Cybench 93%). NYU CTF Bench addressed data contamination. Models trained on internet data containing CTF writeups (common post-2023). This compounds with the AISI finding that evaluations undercount performance variability across token budgets.
