# Hallucination × Formal Proof: When Confident Wrong Proofs Fool Non-Expert Reviewers

**Problem:** LLMs hallucinate confidently. In formal verification, a confident but wrong proof (that fails Lean's checker) would be caught. But a confident wrong specification might pass all automated checks while missing the intended safety property. Non-expert reviewers may not catch this.

**Direction:** Generate 20 formally verified programs where the spec is subtly wrong (verified but missing a key safety property). Present to 15 CS students for review with 5 minutes per program. Measure false approval rate. Compare to a condition where the correct spec is provided. Test whether confidence language in LLM output affects approval rate.

**Why it matters:** If non-expert reviewers of LLM-generated formal specs systematically approve subtly wrong specs (especially when the LLM expresses high confidence), formal verification provides a false safety guarantee that may be worse than no verification.

**Relevant context:** AI sycophancy research shows LLM outputs influence human judgment. Formal verification is being positioned as an accessible safety tool for non-experts. The compounding risk of LLM hallucination and non-expert review is not studied in this context.
