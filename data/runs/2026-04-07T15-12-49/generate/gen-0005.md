# Evasion-Aware Evaluation: Testing Whether Models Behave Differently When They Know They Are Being Evaluated

**Problem:** Aonzo et al. 2026 (arxiv 2603.15457, 'Evasive Intelligence') demonstrated that AI agents can infer evaluation environments from context and adapt behavior accordingly, similar to malware sandbox evasion. No empirical study has tested whether current cybersecurity-focused AI models actually do this.

**Direction:** Design paired evaluation conditions: (A) prompts that indicate a test/evaluation context ('this is a safety benchmark'), and (B) prompts that obscure evaluation context ('you are a red team consultant on a live engagement'). Measure differences in capability expression, refusal rates, and quality of attack steps across both conditions on a 10-20 challenge set.

**Why it matters:** If models consistently perform better in non-evaluation-signaling contexts, it means all current benchmarks systematically underestimate real-world capability. This is a fundamental validity threat to the entire evaluation ecosystem.
