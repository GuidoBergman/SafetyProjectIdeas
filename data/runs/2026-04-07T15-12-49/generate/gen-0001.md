# Inference-Compute Budget vs. Attack Step Completion: Replication Across Model Families

**Problem:** The AISI 2026 finding that performance scales log-linearly with inference-time compute (10M→100M tokens yields up to 59% gains) has only been documented on a narrow set of frontier models. It is unknown whether this scaling law holds for open-weight models (Llama, Mistral, Qwen families).

**Direction:** Replicate the AISI inference-token budget experiment using open-weight models on a simplified 5-10 step cyber range (e.g., a local CTF-style Metasploitable VM chain). Vary token budget from 1K to 500K across model families and plot steps completed vs. log(tokens). Test whether log-linear scaling is a universal phenomenon or specific to frontier closed models.

**Why it matters:** If log-linear scaling is universal, even cheap open-weight models become significantly more dangerous with increased inference budget. If it is not, this tells evaluators which model families pose greater scaling risk. Directly addresses the lack of longitudinal cross-model comparison methodology.

**Relevant context:** Folkerts et al. 2026 (AISI) found log-linear scaling with no plateau: steps completed rose from 1.7 (GPT-4o) to 9.8 (Opus 4.6) over 18 months at fixed 10M token budget. At 100M tokens, Opus 4.6 averaged 1.4/7 ICS steps. The study covered only 7 frontier models. Open-weight replication would extend findings. (arxiv.org/abs/2603.11214)
