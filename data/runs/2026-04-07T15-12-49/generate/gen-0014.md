# Red Team Cost-Efficiency Metric: Dollars-Per-Attack-Step as a Policy-Relevant Evaluation Output

**Problem:** AISI noted that a 100M-token attempt with Opus 4.6 costs ~$80 USD. However, no evaluation framework uses cost-normalized metrics (e.g., attack steps per dollar) as a primary output. Raw capability scores ignore the economics of AI-enabled offense, which are critical for real-world threat assessment.

**Direction:** Re-analyze publicly available CTF solve data alongside API pricing to compute attack-step-per-dollar efficiency across model families. Construct a cost-capability frontier curve. Test whether smaller, cheaper models might dominate on cost-efficiency even if frontier models dominate raw capability. Update curve with models at different price points.

**Why it matters:** Policy thresholds for AI capability often focus on 'can it do X' rather than 'how cheaply can it do X'. Cost-normalized metrics are what actually determines whether mass deployment of AI offense is economically viable for threat actors at different resource levels.
