# Copilot vs. Autonomous: Measuring the Amplification Factor in Guided Cyber Attack Scenarios

**Problem:** MITRE OCCULT identifies the human-AI copilot paradigm as the most realistic near-term threat but the least studied. Almost all existing evaluations measure fully autonomous AI performance. We have no data quantifying how much a human operator amplifies AI performance on multi-step tasks.

**Direction:** Design a controlled study where CS students with no security background (1) attempt a 5-step CTF-style challenge alone, (2) use an LLM autonomously, and (3) use an LLM in copilot mode (human provides high-level decisions, AI executes). Measure steps completed, time, and errors in each condition. Compute a 'copilot amplification factor'.

**Why it matters:** The copilot threat model is most relevant for real attackers yet least benchmarked. Measuring amplification quantitatively would directly fill the gap identified by OCCULT and inform policy thresholds: is a novice + AI equivalent to an expert alone?

**Relevant context:** MITRE OCCULT (arxiv.org/abs/2502.15797) argues CTF-only evaluations miss copilot scenarios. HTB AI Range (Dec 2025) benchmarks AI agents and human-AI teaming side-by-side. Hack The Box reports AI matched hundreds of human teams on 19/20 easy challenges but humans outperformed on multi-step finals.
