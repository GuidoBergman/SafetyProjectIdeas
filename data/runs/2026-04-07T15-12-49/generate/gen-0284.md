# Counterfactual Harm Fraction: A Metric for Evaluating Whether Refusals Prevent Misuse

**Problem:** No empirical framework exists to assess whether a given refusal policy actually prevents harm vs. merely inconveniences users with free alternatives.

**Direction:** For a set of 50 cybersecurity requests refused by frontier models, measure: (a) availability of the same information via web search, (b) time to find via search, (c) technical lift added by AI synthesis. Compute a 'counterfactual harm reduction' score per request category.

**Why it matters:** Refusal policies are designed without evidence of counterfactual harm reduction. This metric would make dual-use governance empirically evaluable rather than judgment-based.

**Relevant context:** Linder et al. Offensive Risk dimension implicitly requires this but doesn't operationalize it; NIST AI 800-1 cybersecurity appendix calls for measurable misuse risk.
