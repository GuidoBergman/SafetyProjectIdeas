# Bottleneck Taxonomy Replication: Do DeepMind's Blind Spots Hold Across Newer Models?

**Problem:** Rodriguez et al. 2025 (DeepMind) identified evasion, persistence, and obfuscation as systematic blind spots in current AI cyber evaluations, grounded in analysis of 12K real incidents. It is unknown whether these blind spots persist in the model generations released after that study (e.g., Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5 class models).

**Direction:** Create a mini-benchmark of 20 challenges: 10 covering the identified blind-spot categories (evasion, persistence, obfuscation) and 10 from well-covered categories (initial access, exploitation). Run 3-4 current frontier API models and compute success rates per category. Compare ratios to Rodriguez et al.'s baseline to assess whether the capability gap is closing.

**Why it matters:** If blind spots are closing rapidly, defenders must reprioritize. If they persist, it tells us where AI is structurally weaker and why. Directly grounds policy recommendations in updated empirical data.

**Relevant context:** Rodriguez et al. 2025 (arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11917) analyzed 12,000+ real-world AI misuse attempts across 20+ countries, curated 7 attack chain archetypes, and found evasion/persistence as systematic evaluation gaps. AISI 2026 confirmed uneven capabilities with sharp drops at specialist tasks.
