# Replication of AISI Multi-Step Range Protocol Using Open-Source Models and Public Infrastructure

**Problem:** AISI's multi-step cyber range evaluations use proprietary infrastructure and closed frontier models, making independent replication impossible. An open-source replication protocol would enable broader research participation and independent validation.

**Direction:** Design an open-source 10-step attack chain replicating the spirit of AISI's corporate network range using: free infrastructure (TryHackMe, HackTheBox free tier, or local VMs), open-weight models (Llama 3, Mistral, Qwen), and publicly documented scaffolding. Run and publish results. Compare performance profiles to AISI's published findings.

**Why it matters:** Single-lab evaluations have inherent reproducibility concerns. An open-source replication protocol would democratize offensive capability evaluation, enabling safety researchers globally to conduct and independently verify measurements — a prerequisite for policy-relevant evidence.

**Relevant context:** AISI Folkerts et al. (arxiv.org/abs/2603.11214) used proprietary ranges and frontier APIs. CTFusion (OpenReview 2025) and NYU CTF Bench (arxiv.org/abs/2406.05590) provide partial open-source infrastructure. CAIBench (arxiv 2510.24317) offers open meta-benchmark framework. Open replication of safety findings is a core scientific value.
