# Game Theory Applied to AI Cyber Offense: Measuring Nash Equilibrium Points in AI Red vs. Blue Scenarios

**Problem:** Offensive and defensive AI capabilities are co-evolving, but evaluations treat offense in isolation. Game-theoretic analysis of AI red vs. blue team dynamics could reveal equilibrium points where offense capability stabilizes or identify conditions for runaway offense advantage.

**Direction:** Set up a simplified red-vs-blue simulation: AI red agent tries to exfiltrate data from a simulated network; AI blue agent runs a basic anomaly detection script that can block IPs. Run multiple rounds and measure whether the system converges to a stable equilibrium, offense dominance, or defense dominance. Vary the token budget asymmetry between agents.

**Why it matters:** The current offense > defense asymmetry measured by Potter et al. (SoK) may be an artifact of unequal AI investment rather than a structural property. Understanding equilibrium dynamics informs whether AI will stabilize the security landscape or trigger an escalating arms race.
