# Cross-Domain Transfer: Applying A/B Testing Principles to Controlled Cyber Capability Experiments

**Problem:** AI cyber evaluations often have poor experimental design: no control conditions, no randomization, no statistical power analysis, and no pre-registration. This means that observed effects may be noise rather than real capability signals. Importing rigorous A/B testing methodology from software product research would dramatically improve evaluation quality.

**Direction:** Design a cyber capability experiment with proper A/B structure: pre-register hypothesis, compute required sample size for 80% power at α=0.05, randomize challenge order, use a control condition (baseline random performance), and pre-specify success criteria. Run the pre-registered experiment comparing two conditions (e.g., with/without web access). Report both the point estimate and confidence interval on the capability difference.

**Why it matters:** If AI cyber capability papers are publishing p-values without power analysis and running underpowered studies, published capability differences may be largely noise. Importing rigorous experimental design would make published findings more credible and reproducible — a prerequisite for policy-relevant evidence.

**Relevant context:** Benchmarking Practices paper (arxiv 2504.10112) documented poor experimental design in offensive security benchmarks. No published AI cyber evaluation uses pre-registration or power analysis. A/B testing with statistical rigor is standard in software product research (tech industry) and clinical trials. CAIBench meta-benchmark aggregates poorly-designed studies.
