# Follow-up to the Knowledge-Capability Gap: Which Security Domains Show the Largest Gap?

**Problem:** Research found that pre-trained knowledge of cybersecurity in LLMs does not imply attack and defense abilities — revealing a knowledge-capability gap. The magnitude and distribution of this gap across security domains (cryptography, web exploitation, network attacks, binary exploitation) has not been characterized.

**Direction:** For each of 5 security domains, create 2 matched question sets: (A) knowledge questions about the domain (multiple choice, analogous to OCCULT's TACTL), (B) practical capability tasks in the same domain. Run 3-4 models on both sets per domain. Compute a knowledge-to-capability transfer ratio per domain. Identify which domains have the largest gaps.

**Why it matters:** Domains with large knowledge-capability gaps represent safe harbors: even if models know about these attack types, they cannot execute them. Domains with small gaps are where AI knowledge directly translates to danger. This analysis directly informs where knowledge restrictions (training data curation) would be effective vs. futile as a safety measure.

**Relevant context:** OCCULT documented knowledge-capability gap with TACTL benchmark: DeepSeek-R1 answered 90%+ of knowledge questions but practical capability varies. AICrypto found strong memorization but weak mathematical reasoning in cryptography domain. AISI found sharp drops at RE and crypto despite likely high knowledge in these areas.
