# The Population Assumption Problem: Formalizing Non-Populational IRT for AI Safety Evaluation

**Problem:** IRT assumes persons are sampled from a well-defined population with a specified ability distribution (typically normal). AI models are not randomly sampled — they are selected by researchers, released by labs, and include survivors of model selection processes. Recent work has proposed 'non-populational' IRT where a single model's ability is estimated from its own response pattern, independent of the model population. This approach has not been applied to safety benchmarks or compared to standard IRT.

**Direction:** Apply both standard IRT (assuming a normal model-ability population) and non-populational IRT (ability estimated from single-model response patterns without population assumption) to the same safety benchmark data. Compare: (1) ability estimates from each approach and their rank-order correlation, (2) item parameter estimates under each assumption, (3) confidence intervals and their coverage probabilities via bootstrap. Assess when the approaches diverge and what this divergence implies for safety score interpretation.

**Why it matters:** If non-populational IRT produces substantially different safety rankings from standard IRT, the assumption violation is practically significant. This paper would establish when and why the population assumption matters for AI evaluation — a foundational methodological contribution that applies across all IRT-based AI evaluation, not just safety.

**Relevant context:** The 'General Scales Unlock AI Evaluation' paper (arXiv:2503.06378) develops non-populational IRT for AI. The 'Stop Evaluating AI with Human Tests' paper (arXiv:2507.23009) explicitly addresses assumption violations. Standard IRT violation effects are documented in the IRT literature. The mirt package implements standard IRT for comparison.
