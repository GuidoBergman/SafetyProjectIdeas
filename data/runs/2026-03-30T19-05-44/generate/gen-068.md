# Responsiveness Analysis to Evaluate Whether Safety Benchmarks Detect Real Improvement

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks are assumed to track genuine safety improvements as models are retrained, but it has not been empirically demonstrated that benchmark scores change in the expected direction when models are actually made safer, or that they are sensitive to small but real safety improvements (i.e., that they are 'responsive' in the PROM sense).

**Direction:** Apply health outcomes research responsiveness methodology: identify pairs of model versions where a specific safety intervention was documented (e.g., a targeted RLHF update for a specific harm category). Compute the standardized response mean (SRM) and effect size (ES) for each benchmark score change across these pairs. Compare which benchmarks are most responsive (detect the change). Cross-validate using models where no safety changes were made (should show near-zero SRM). Implement in R using Open LLM Leaderboard historical data.

**Why it matters:** A benchmark that cannot reliably detect genuine safety improvements is useless for guiding the RLHF training loop. Responsiveness analysis from health outcomes research provides a validated method for evaluating this property and selecting benchmarks that are actually sensitive to the interventions we care about.

**Relevant context:** Responsiveness methodology (Springer Health QoL Outcomes 2006 paper found in search) defines SRM, ES, and minimal important difference as the core metrics. Open LLM Leaderboard tracks scores across model versions over time. Model changelogs document safety-specific updates. Analysis requires only R and spreadsheet work.
