# Partial Invariance Test: Does the metabench G-Factor Survive Parameter-Count Partialling?

**Problem:** metabench (Kipnis et al., ICLR 2025) found a single factor explains 79.3% of variance across six Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks. A 2026 follow-up (Paper 13) argues this dominant factor may simply be model parameter count, not a genuine general capability. If so, the G-factor is a statistical artifact of scale, not a psychological construct.

**Direction:** Reanalyse the publicly available Open LLM Leaderboard score matrix (5000+ models) in R. Regress out log(parameter count) from all benchmark scores, then re-run the same EFA/CFA that Kipnis used. Compare factor loadings, variance explained, and model fit (CFI, RMSEA) before and after partialling. If the single factor survives with similar loadings, it reflects genuine capability; if it collapses, parameter count was the dominant source.

**Why it matters:** AI safety evaluations implicitly assume benchmarks measure distinct, meaningful capabilities. If the dominant variance component is simply scale, then cross-benchmark 'convergent validity' evidence is spurious, and ranking models by composite scores is uninformative beyond knowing their size.

**Relevant context:** metabench (arXiv 2407.12844 / ICLR 2025); Open LLM Leaderboard public data; EFA/CFA implementable in R (psych, lavaan packages); partial correlation and residualisation are standard psychometric techniques.
