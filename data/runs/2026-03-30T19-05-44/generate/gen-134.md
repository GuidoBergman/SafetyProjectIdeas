# Test-Retest Reliability of Safety Evaluation Scores: Quantifying Stochastic Variance

**Problem:** LLM outputs are stochastic: the same prompt evaluated twice may yield different safety judgments due to temperature sampling. The resulting test-retest reliability of safety evaluation scores has never been formally quantified. If a model's safety score varies by ±5 points across identical administrations, rank orderings between models are meaningless for models within 10 points of each other.

**Direction:** Administer the same safety benchmark to 5-10 models twice, under identical conditions (same temperature, same items, same evaluator), separated by a time interval. Compute test-retest reliability using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) with 95% confidence intervals using the irr package. Decompose variance into systematic model differences vs. stochastic occasion variance using a one-facet G-study. Determine the minimum detectable difference (MDD) between models given observed reliability. Compare MDD to the range of score differences reported in published safety leaderboards.

**Why it matters:** Fundamental for Success State 4. If the MDD is ±8 points and leaderboard differences are typically 2-5 points, most published model rankings are not statistically supported. This is a simple but explosive finding with direct implications for how safety leaderboards are interpreted.

**Relevant context:** Stochastic sampling is a known issue in LLM evaluation but test-retest reliability has not been formally computed for safety benchmarks. ICC computation is standard in the irr package. The six-tiered evaluation framework (ScienceDirect 2025) names repeatability as top priority. MDD is a standard concept from clinical outcome measurement (MCID literature) with direct applicability.
