# Construct Validity Audit Using Convergent and Discriminant Evidence for Safety vs. Capability Benchmarks

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks may not measure safety distinct from general capability—models that are more capable may score higher on safety benchmarks simply because they better understand the evaluation format, not because they are genuinely safer. This conflation undermines the interpretive validity of safety scores.

**Direction:** Conduct a convergent-discriminant validity analysis: for 30+ models on the Open LLM Leaderboard, compute correlations between safety benchmark scores and general capability benchmark scores (MMLU, BIG-Bench Hard, HellaSwag). Apply partial correlation and regression to estimate how much safety score variance remains after removing general capability. Use CFA (lavaan in R) to test whether a two-factor model (safety + capability) fits better than a one-factor model. Report the proportion of safety score variance attributable to genuine safety vs. capability confounding.

**Why it matters:** If safety scores are largely capability confounded, then 'improving safety' via RLHF may just be improving general task performance. This has direct implications for safety evaluation design and for the validity of safety-capability tradeoff claims. The MTMM framework provides the conceptual structure; partial correlation and CFA provide the analysis.

**Relevant context:** Convergent-discriminant validity is a core MTMM concept (Campbell and Fiske). Open LLM Leaderboard provides both safety and capability scores for 100+ models. lavaan CFA is standard in psychometric research. Several recent papers note capability-safety correlation without formal validity framework.
