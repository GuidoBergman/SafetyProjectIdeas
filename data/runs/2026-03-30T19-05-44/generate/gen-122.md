# Contamination-Resistant Item Design: IRT Analysis of Benchmark Drift After Public Release

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks degrade after public release as training data contamination occurs. Models trained after a benchmark's release may perform better due to contamination rather than genuine safety improvement. There is no psychometric study of how item parameter drift (IRT b-parameter change over time) can signal contamination.

**Direction:** Collect evaluation results from models released at different time points on a fixed safety benchmark (e.g., SafetyBench). Stratify models into pre-release and post-release cohorts. Fit IRT models separately to each cohort. Compute item parameter drift (change in b-parameters over time). Apply differential item functioning analysis (DIF) using time of model release as the grouping variable. Items showing significant DIF favoring post-release models are contamination candidates. Propose an IRT-based contamination early-warning system.

**Why it matters:** Addresses the contamination-resistant evaluation gap in Success State 1. Benchmark contamination is widely acknowledged but rarely quantified psychometrically. Item parameter drift is the IRT signature of contamination — when item difficulty drops for newer models without content changes, contamination is the most likely explanation.

**Relevant context:** Contamination is flagged as a major concern in 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' (2601.23112) and the 2025 AI Safety Index. IRT parameter stability under equating conditions (Frontiers 2025) provides methodology for detecting parameter drift. DIF over time is a standard technique in educational testing for detecting item exposure effects.
