# Test Blueprint (Table of Specifications) Audit for AI Safety Benchmark Coverage

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks do not publish a table of specifications mapping items to constructs and showing the intended coverage. This makes it impossible to tell whether a benchmark is systematically overrepresenting some safety constructs (e.g., explicit toxicity) while ignoring others (e.g., subtle manipulation, long-horizon deception).

**Direction:** Borrow the test blueprint methodology: for 4–5 major safety benchmarks, retroactively construct a table of specifications by classifying each item by (a) construct domain, (b) cognitive demand level, and (c) output format type. Use a content taxonomy derived from the construct definition literature (AI safety categories). Compute coverage ratios across cells. Identify empirical gaps. Compare the realized blueprint to what the benchmark developers claimed they were measuring. Produce a recommended ideal blueprint for a comprehensive safety benchmark.

**Why it matters:** Test blueprints are the primary tool for content validity evidence in educational measurement. Discovering that current safety benchmarks have severe coverage gaps (e.g., 80% of items testing one construct) would justify benchmark redesign and explain why high scores fail to predict real-world safety.
