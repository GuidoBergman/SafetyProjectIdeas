# Building a Nomological Network for AI Safety: What Should Predict and Be Predicted by Safety Scores?

**Problem:** Establish construct validity for AI safety evaluation

**Direction:** Construct a nomological network for AI safety by (1) listing theoretically expected correlates of safety (RLHF training, instruction-tuning, model size, refusal rate, alignment tax), (2) gathering empirical data on these variables for a common model set, and (3) testing whether safety benchmark scores relate to these variables in theoretically expected directions and magnitudes using structural equation modeling (SEM) in R. Identify which expected relationships hold and which do not.

**Why it matters:** A construct gains validity by fitting into a network of theoretically coherent relationships—the nomological network. If safety scores do not correlate with known safety interventions (e.g., RLHF, Constitutional AI training) or do correlate with things they shouldn't (e.g., model size alone), this reveals construct validity failures.

**Relevant context:** 'Measurement to Meaning' (2025) explicitly advocates for nomological network analysis in AI evaluation but does not implement it for safety specifically. Typology of validity papers (2024) provide detailed methodology for nomological network testing. R's lavaan and semTools packages make SEM feasible for psychometrically-trained users.
