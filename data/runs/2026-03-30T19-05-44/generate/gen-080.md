# Decision-Maker Calibration Study: Do Safety Scores Actually Influence Deployment Decisions Appropriately?

**Problem:** Even if a benchmark produces an accurate safety score, the harm chain continues through a 'decision-to-deploy' link where human evaluators interpret and act on scores. If decision-makers are miscalibrated — treating a 'medium risk' score as license to deploy, or ignoring score uncertainty intervals — then accurate measurement upstream is wasted. This calibration failure is almost entirely unstudied.

**Direction:** Design a survey experiment (no coding required) targeting AI practitioners, lab employees, and evaluators (recruit via AI safety communities, BAISH network). Present participants with hypothetical safety evaluation scenarios varying: (a) score level (high/medium/low safety), (b) score confidence interval width (narrow vs. wide), (c) benchmark validity information (high vs. low construct validity). Measure: deployment recommendation (yes/no/conditional), acceptable risk threshold stated, and sensitivity to uncertainty. Analyze with logistic regression and ANOVA in R. Compare to normative Bayesian decision theory benchmarks. Identify systematic miscalibration patterns (e.g., neglect of uncertainty, overweighting of single-point estimates).

**Why it matters:** Upstream measurement quality cannot compensate for downstream decision miscalibration. Understanding how practitioners actually use scores is a prerequisite for designing evaluation reporting that prevents harm from false deployment decisions.

**Relevant context:** Intolerable Risk Threshold Recommendations for AI (arXiv 2503.05812) calls for operationalizing risk tolerances into measurable criteria but does not study whether decision-makers can actually use such criteria. The 'Measurement to Meaning' paper notes consequential validity as an under-studied facet. Survey experiments are within scope for a researcher with psychometric background.
