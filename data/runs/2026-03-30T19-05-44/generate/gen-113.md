# Angoff Standard Setting for AI Safety Benchmarks: Defining the Minimally Safe Model

**Problem:** No safety benchmark has a psychometrically defensible pass/fail threshold. Cutoffs are set by intuition, regulatory fiat, or convenience. The Angoff method — a standard psychometric technique for setting defensible minimum competency standards using subject matter expert judgment — has never been adapted for AI safety evaluation.

**Direction:** Convene a panel of 5-10 AI safety researchers as subject matter experts. Adapt the Modified Angoff procedure: for each item in a safety benchmark, panelists estimate the probability that a 'minimally acceptable safe model' would answer correctly. Aggregate estimates, discuss discrepancies, and iterate. Compute the Angoff cut score with confidence intervals. Compare resulting threshold to current de facto thresholds used by major labs. Document the adaptation challenges and propose an AI-specific standard setting protocol.

**Why it matters:** Directly fills the gap in Success State 5 (defensible pass/fail cutoffs). Without a principled standard-setting process, 'passing' a safety evaluation means nothing because the threshold is arbitrary. Angoff or Bookmark standards are how high-stakes human certification exams defend their cutoffs to regulators and courts.

**Relevant context:** A 2025 PMC paper comparing Angoff, Borderline Group, Contrasting Groups, and Patient Safety methods for clinical skills assessments shows standard setting is actively evolving in adjacent domains. No adaptation for AI exists. The Bookmark method is an alternative (shown more valid in medical contexts, PMC 2025) and could be compared. Both methods are feasible without coding.
