# Safety Benchmark Dimensionality: Are 'Honesty', 'Harmlessness', and 'Helpfulness' Empirically Separable?

**Problem:** Anthropic's Constitutional AI and the broader safety community treat honesty, harmlessness, and helpfulness as distinct safety dimensions. Factor analysis has been widely applied to capability benchmarks, but no study has tested whether these safety constructs are empirically separable or collapse into a single general factor when item-level response data is examined. The 'Safetywashing' analysis suggests they may all load on a capability factor.

**Direction:** Collect binary response matrices for models on multiple safety benchmarks (TruthfulQA for honesty, HarmBench/BBQ for harmlessness, MT-Bench for helpfulness — all publicly available). Run exploratory factor analysis (EFA) in R (psych package), then a confirmatory three-factor CFA model. Use bifactor modeling to separate a 'safety-general' factor from specific honesty/harmlessness/helpfulness factors. Control for model size as a covariate.

**Why it matters:** If the three safety dimensions are not empirically separable, the entire 'HHH' framework for AI safety measurement is built on an untested assumption. If they are separable, this validates the theoretical structure. Either outcome is a significant empirical contribution to AI safety evaluation.

**Relevant context:** Safetywashing (2024) found high cross-benchmark correlations. The psych and lavaan R packages handle EFA/CFA. Public datasets from Open LLM Leaderboard and HELM cover multiple safety constructs. The model-size confound is known from capability benchmark factor analyses.
