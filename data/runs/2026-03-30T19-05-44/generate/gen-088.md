# Reliability Analysis of Human Safety Annotation: How Stable Are the Labels Underpinning Benchmarks?

**Problem:** Safety benchmark items depend on human annotation to assign ground-truth 'harmful/safe' labels. If annotators disagree substantially, the items have low inter-rater reliability, which propagates as measurement error through the entire evaluation chain. The under-studied link is the annotation reliability of safety judgments, particularly for edge cases and ambiguous harms.

**Direction:** Obtain a publicly available human-annotated safety dataset with multiple annotator judgments per item (e.g., WildGuard, HarmBench, or similar datasets with raw annotation files). Using R, compute: (1) percent agreement, (2) Cohen's kappa (for 2 annotators) or Fleiss' kappa (multi-rater), (3) Krippendorff's alpha (handles ordinal and nominal scales), (4) intra-class correlation if severity ratings are ordinal. Stratify reliability by harm category, harm severity level, and item ambiguity (defined by proportion of annotator disagreement). Identify harm categories with systematically low reliability. Compute the effect of annotation unreliability on score reliability using classical test theory: reliability_observed = reliability_true * sqrt(reliability_raters). Report the proportion of observed score variance attributable to annotator disagreement.

**Why it matters:** Low annotator reliability creates an irreducible floor on evaluation validity. Understanding which harm categories produce most disagreement helps prioritize annotation guideline development and flags categories where benchmark-based decisions are most unreliable.

**Relevant context:** Counting on Consensus (arXiv 2603.06865) surveys inter-annotator agreement metrics for NLP. A 2025 study on LLM vs. human inter-rater reliability is directly relevant. Krippendorff's alpha is standard psychometric methodology. The irr and psych R packages provide all needed functions.
