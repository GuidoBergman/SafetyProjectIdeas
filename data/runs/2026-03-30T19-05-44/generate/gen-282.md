# MTMM as Evidence for Consequential Validity: Do Better-Validated Safety Benchmarks Predict Real-World Harm Differently

**Problem:** P10: No evidence that better measurement improves safety decisions

**Direction:** Construct an MTMM-inspired analysis where 'methods' are benchmarks with varying levels of documented validity evidence, and 'traits' are categories of real-world harm incidents, testing whether better-validated benchmarks show stronger criterion validity against real-world harm reports.

**Why it matters:** This is the consequential validity test the field is missing: do models flagged as 'unsafe' by rigorous, validity-evidenced benchmarks actually produce more harm in deployment? If so, this is direct evidence that measurement quality improves safety decisions. MTMM provides the structural framework for this analysis.

**Relevant context:** The 'Measurement to Meaning' paper (arxiv 2505.10573) introduces consequential validity as the fifth key validity form for AI evaluation. The Safety by Measurement review (arxiv 2505.05541) surveys existing safety evaluation methods. Real-world AI incident databases (AIID) provide criterion data for correlation analysis.
