# G-Theory Study of Benchmark Validity: Does Item Sampling or Model Sampling Drive Validity Failures

**Direction:** Apply G-Theory to a benchmark's inter-rater and item-sampling reliability, distinguishing variance attributable to item selection (universe of items sampled) from variance attributable to model-specific response styles, quantifying which facet is the primary driver of invalidity.

**Why it matters:** If invalidity is primarily driven by poor item sampling (too few items per domain), the fix is more items. If driven by rater/rubric variance, the fix is better scoring. G-Theory provides the variance component analysis to identify which investments in benchmark quality yield the most validity improvement.

**Relevant context:** G-Theory decomposes variance into item, rater, occasion, and their interactions. The AI Measurement Science textbook discusses G-Theory for AI evaluation. Applying G-Theory to benchmark item samples requires response data from multiple models on subsets of items—obtainable from any large benchmark dataset.
