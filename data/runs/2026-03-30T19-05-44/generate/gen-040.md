# Consequential Validity of Safety Benchmarks: Do High Scores Predict Safe Deployment Outcomes?

**Problem:** Only 53.4% of 445 benchmarks present any validity evidence (Paper 14, 2025), and consequential validity is specifically unstudied (per project open problems). Consequential validity asks: what are the downstream consequences of using benchmark scores in decisions? For safety benchmarks, the question is whether high-scoring models actually exhibit fewer safety incidents in deployment.

**Direction:** Reanalyse public model cards, Chatbot Arena human preference ratings, and published red-teaming reports for 30+ models that have both Open LLM Leaderboard safety scores and post-deployment incident records. Compute Spearman correlations between benchmark safety scores and: (1) human preference for safety-related queries on Chatbot Arena, (2) publicly reported jailbreak success rates (from red-teaming papers). Test whether benchmarks with more validity evidence (per Paper 14 taxonomy) show stronger correlations.

**Why it matters:** This is the first empirical test of consequential validity for AI safety benchmarks. If high safety scores do not predict safer deployment, the entire benchmarking enterprise for safety lacks justification.

**Relevant context:** Paper 14 (2025) on benchmark validity evidence (Oxford Internet Institute, arXiv 2502.06559); Chatbot Arena public data; Open LLM Leaderboard; consequential validity (Messick 1989) as theoretical framework.
