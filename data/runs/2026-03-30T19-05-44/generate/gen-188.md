# Assessment Center Methodology for AI Safety Evaluation: Multiple Standardized Scenarios, Multiple Evaluator Types, and Behavioral Rating Scales

**Problem:** Current AI safety evaluation resembles simple written tests rather than the high-fidelity, multi-method assessment approach used for evaluating humans in high-stakes safety contexts (nuclear plant operators, flight controllers, surgeons). Assessment centers integrate multiple standardized exercises, multiple trained evaluators, and behaviorally anchored rating scales—producing more valid predictions of real-world safety performance.

**Direction:** Design a prototype 'AI Safety Assessment Center' using assessment center methodology. Develop 4-6 standardized safety exercises covering different scenarios (adversarial user roleplay, agentic task with embedded hazards, multi-turn escalating request, ambiguous operator instruction). Score each exercise using behaviorally anchored rating scales (BARS) developed from CIT incident analysis. Administer to 8-10 models. Rate using multiple evaluator types (human experts, LLM judges, rule-based classifiers). Compute inter-rater reliability and convergent validity across exercises. Compare assessment center composite scores to single-benchmark scores in predicting human-judged safety deployment ratings.

**Why it matters:** Assessment centers consistently outperform single-test evaluations in predicting real-world performance precisely because they sample behavior across multiple contexts. The construct validity literature explicitly recommends MTMM and assessment center approaches for AI evaluation. Assessment center design requires psychometric expertise (scenario construction, BARS development, rater training) rather than coding—exactly suited to this participant. This would produce a more ecologically valid safety evaluation.

**Relevant context:** Assessment center construct validity has been studied extensively (ResearchGate: 'Assessment center construct-related validity: Stepping beyond the MTMM matrix'). BARS development is a standard industrial psychology methodology. The 2024 arxiv paper 'Where Assessment Validation and Responsible AI Meet' calls for multi-method, multi-trait evaluation frameworks. No published work designs an assessment-center-style multi-exercise safety evaluation for AI models.
