# Consequential Validity at the Policy Level: Do Safety Benchmark Results Influence Deployment Decisions?

**Problem:** A second consequential validity question operates at the institutional level: do AI safety benchmarks actually influence deployment decisions by developers, regulators, and organizations? If companies deploy models regardless of safety scores (or cherry-pick benchmarks), the consequences of benchmark use are negative. If regulators use scores to gate deployment, the consequences are high-stakes. Neither question has been studied.

**Direction:** This is a document/policy analysis study requiring no coding. (1) Analyze public model cards and safety reports from major AI developers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Mistral) to document which safety benchmarks they cite and how. (2) Review AI governance frameworks (EU AI Act technical standards, NIST AI RMF, UK AI Safety Institute documentation) for benchmark references. (3) Code the documents for: benchmark cited, score reported, deployment decision linked. (4) Apply content validity analysis: do the benchmarks cited match the safety concerns the governance frameworks articulate?

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmarks are being used by regulators without validity evidence, this is a policy failure. If developers report only favorable benchmarks (selective reporting), this is a validity threat analogous to publication bias in drug trials. This paper would be the first systematic audit of how safety benchmark scores flow into consequential decisions.

**Relevant context:** Model cards are publicly available for major models. EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF are public documents. The 'Safetywashing' critique implicitly raises consequential validity concerns. The 2025 AI Safety Index (Future of Life Institute) and AI Index (Stanford HAI) document current benchmark usage patterns.
