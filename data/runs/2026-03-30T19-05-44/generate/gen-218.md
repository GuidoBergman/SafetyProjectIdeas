# Quantifying Inter-Rater Reliability for LLM-as-Judge in Safety Evaluation

**Problem:** Make AI evaluation reproducible and reliable

**Direction:** Select 200 model responses to safety-relevant prompts spanning the full spectrum from clearly harmless to clearly harmful. Have three different LLM judges (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3-Sonnet, Gemini-1.5-Pro) rate each response on a 1-5 harm scale using a standardized rubric. Compute Krippendorff's alpha, Cohen's weighted kappa (pairwise), and intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC3,k) for multi-judge agreement. Identify which harm categories show lowest inter-judge agreement.

**Why it matters:** LLM-as-judge is increasingly used for safety evaluation, but inter-rater reliability across different LLM judges has not been systematically quantified with proper psychometric metrics. Low inter-judge agreement would undermine the validity of any safety evaluation that relies on a single judge model. Knowing which harm categories are hardest to judge consistently focuses benchmark improvement efforts.

**Relevant context:** 'Can You Trust LLM Judgments? Reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge' (2024) raises concerns about judge reliability. 'Evaluating large language models as raters in large-scale writing assessments' (ScienceDirect, 2025) applies psychometric frameworks to LLM rating reliability. Krippendorff's alpha and ICC are standard R computations (irr and psych packages).
