# Format Roulette: Prompt Sensitivity as an Amplifier of Shallow Safety Alignment

**Problem:** Safety scores are unstable across prompt formats (Failure A2), and safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens (Failure B3). These two failures compound: shallow alignment means a model's safety behavior depends on whether early generation steps are 'primed' toward refusal or compliance; prompt sensitivity means that minor format changes (e.g., adding a role-play prefix, changing to JSON output format, switching from user to system prompt) can disrupt that priming. Together they create a situation where a model scores 90% on one benchmark format and near-0% on a semantically equivalent format, not because of capability differences but because format triggers shift early-token distributions.

**Direction:** Select a published safety benchmark (e.g., SALAD-Bench or HarmBench). Construct 5–6 format variants for each item: direct question, role-play framing, JSON output request, system-prompt injection, paraphrase, and multi-turn conversation. Administer all variants to 2–3 public-API models. Use generalizability theory (G-theory) in R (gtheory package) to partition score variance into: model, format, item, and interaction components. Quantify how much of total score variance is attributable to format rather than stable safety behavior.

**Why it matters:** If safety scores are substantially format-determined rather than model-determined, then any single-format benchmark is measuring a surface response to framing rather than alignment depth. This is especially dangerous if models are fine-tuned or red-teamed against one format only to fail on others. G-theory decomposition makes the measurement unreliability quantitatively visible.

**Relevant context:** ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' documents the shallow alignment mechanism. Research in 2024 on prompt sensitivity shows performance drops up to 61.8% under nuanced variations.
