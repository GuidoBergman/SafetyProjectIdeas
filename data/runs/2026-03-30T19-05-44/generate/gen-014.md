# Consequential Validity Audit: Who Uses Safety Benchmark Scores and What Decisions Follow?

**Problem:** Consequential validity—the study of downstream effects of evaluation decisions—is entirely unstudied for AI safety benchmarks. We do not know which safety benchmark scores are used by regulators, investors, or deployers to make high-stakes decisions, nor whether those decisions are appropriate given the benchmarks' actual psychometric properties.

**Direction:** Conduct a structured document review: (1) identify 10-15 instances of AI safety benchmark scores being cited in regulatory guidance, investment decisions, deployment policies, or media coverage (using public policy documents, AISI reports, FLI AI Safety Index, EU AI Act guidance); (2) for each cited benchmark, assess its psychometric properties against a validity checklist (construct validity, reliability, population representativeness, face validity); (3) identify mismatches between the benchmark's documented limitations and the weight placed on it in the cited decision. Synthesize into a consequential validity report.

**Why it matters:** Consequential validity is the highest-stakes form of validity evidence and the most neglected. If regulators and deployers rely on safety benchmarks with unknown or poor psychometric properties, safety certification decisions may be systematically misleading. This audit would be the first consequential validity analysis of AI safety benchmarks, directly relevant to AI governance.

**Relevant context:** arxiv:2502.06559 (Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?); FLI AI Safety Index Summer 2025; AISI Advanced AI Evaluations May update; ai-watch.ec.europa.eu (Nine challenges for AI benchmarking); consequential validity is documented as entirely unstudied in the MTAI framework.
