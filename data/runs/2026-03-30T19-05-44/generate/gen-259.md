# Graded Response IRT Models for Safety Benchmarks: Recovering Severity Information from Ordinal Ratings

**Problem:** P5: Safety benchmarks use binary pass/fail losing severity info

**Direction:** Re-analyze an existing safety benchmark that has ordinal or severity-graded human annotations using a Graded Response Model (GRM) or Partial Credit Model, comparing the information recovered vs. binary dichotomization, and demonstrating what is lost by binarizing severity ratings.

**Why it matters:** The 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' paper (arxiv 2601.23112) documents that only 14 of 36 severity-distinguishing benchmarks provide principled justification. GRM analysis would demonstrate concretely how much measurement information is destroyed by binary collapse and provide a model-based severity scale.

**Relevant context:** GRM and Partial Credit Model are standard IRT extensions for ordered polytomous responses, fully supported in mirt and ltm R packages. The 2025 PMC tutorial on GRM in R demonstrates exactly this analysis. Safety datasets with human severity ratings exist (e.g., MLCommons AI Safety Benchmark v0.5 with graded annotations, HarmBench).
