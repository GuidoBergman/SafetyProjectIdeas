# Concurrent Validity Study: Do Safety Benchmark Rankings Agree with Expert Elicitation Rankings?

**Problem:** The safetywashing causal chain reaches the 'overconfidence in safety' link partly because benchmark scores are treated as the gold standard, replacing expert judgment. But if expert elicitation of model safety (structured expert opinion, red-teaming judgment) substantially disagrees with benchmark rankings, this is concurrent validity evidence that benchmarks are measuring something other than what safety experts assess. This link — the agreement between benchmark-based and expert-based safety rankings — is unstudied.

**Direction:** Recruit 8-12 AI safety experts (from BAISH network, AI safety community) for a structured expert elicitation exercise. Present each expert with capability summaries of 8-10 named frontier models (without benchmark scores). Use a best-worst scaling (BWS) or structured paired comparison format to elicit safety rankings from experts. Compute: (1) inter-expert reliability (ICC or Kendall's W), (2) the rank correlation (Spearman's rho) between the expert consensus ranking and benchmark score ranking for the same models. Test for significant disagreement. If expert rankings and benchmark rankings disagree substantially, interview 3-4 experts on which specific model behaviors drove their ranking — these may identify validity-missing dimensions.

**Why it matters:** If the people most qualified to judge AI safety rank models differently from benchmarks, the benchmarks lack concurrent validity relative to domain expert judgment — the most direct validity evidence possible for safety evaluation.

**Relevant context:** Expert elicitation methodology is well-established in risk assessment (Cooke's classical method, Sheffield elicitation framework). Best-worst scaling is widely used in psychometrics and health economics. No prior study has compared benchmark safety rankings to structured expert consensus. This is a survey/elicitation study requiring no coding.
