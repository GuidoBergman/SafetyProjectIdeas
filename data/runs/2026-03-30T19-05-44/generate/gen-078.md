# Content Validity Ratio Analysis of a Major AI Safety Benchmark's Item Pool

**Problem:** Even when a safety construct is defined, the benchmark items chosen to represent it may not actually cover the construct domain. This is the 'item selection validity' link in the false-negative deployment chain. If harmful capability X is in the construct but not in the item pool, the evaluation will always miss it — a systematic false negative, not random error.

**Direction:** Select one benchmark with a public item pool and explicit harm taxonomy (e.g., MLCommons AILuminate v1.0 with its 7 hazard categories, or AIR-Bench with its 314-category taxonomy). Recruit 3-5 domain expert raters (can be AI safety researchers at BAISH). For each item, ask raters to judge whether it is 'essential', 'useful but not essential', or 'not necessary' for measuring the stated safety construct. Compute Lawshe's Content Validity Ratio (CVR) and Content Validity Index (CVI) for each item and each hazard category. Identify under-represented categories (low CVR) and over-represented ones. Produce a coverage heatmap. This is a pure psychometric audit requiring only spreadsheet coding + R for agreement statistics.

**Why it matters:** Low CVR items inflate item count without improving construct coverage. Whole hazard categories may be structurally absent, producing guaranteed false negatives for those harm types. This analysis can be completed with existing public benchmarks and no ML training.

**Relevant context:** The 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' paper critiques item selection in current benchmarks. AIR 2024 showed that the most comprehensive benchmark covered only 71% of regulatory risk categories, implying large systematic gaps. Content validity is standard in educational and clinical measurement but has not been formally applied to AI safety benchmarks.
