# Convergent and Discriminant Validity of Safety Benchmarks Using a Multitrait-Multimethod Matrix

**Problem:** Validity theory (Campbell & Fiske 1959) requires that measures of the same construct correlate highly (convergent validity) while measures of different constructs correlate weakly (discriminant validity). Safety benchmarks claim to measure safety, but they have never been arranged in a MTMM matrix to test whether 'safety' benchmarks converge with each other and diverge from capability benchmarks.

**Direction:** Arrange 8–10 benchmarks in a MTMM-style matrix: traits = (safety, factuality, reasoning) × methods = (multiple-choice, open-ended, adversarial prompting). For 30+ models, compute all pairwise Spearman correlations. Evaluate convergent validity (do safety benchmarks correlate more with each other than with reasoning benchmarks?) and discriminant validity (are correlations between different constructs clearly lower?). Compute Campbell-Fiske criteria formally.

**Why it matters:** This is the first MTMM analysis of AI benchmarks. If safety benchmarks do not show discriminant validity from capability benchmarks, 'safety' may be indistinguishable from general capability, undermining the rationale for separate safety evaluations.

**Relevant context:** Campbell & Fiske (1959) MTMM method; Open LLM Leaderboard (multiple benchmark scores per model); TruthfulQA, HarmBench, MMLU, BIG-Bench Hard; psych::corr.test in R.
