# Response Style Detection in AI Safety Scoring: Applying Survey Satisficing and Acquiescence Correction Methods to LLM Judge Ratings

**Problem:** LLM-based judges used to score safety evaluations may exhibit systematic response styles: acquiescence bias (tendency to rate responses as safe regardless of content), severity bias (consistently harsh scoring), or satisficing (providing valid-seeming but not carefully considered ratings). These response styles corrupt safety scores just as they corrupt survey data.

**Direction:** Administer a safety evaluation using multiple LLM judges of varying quality/capability on a set of items with known ground-truth safety ratings (from expert consensus). Apply standard survey methodology response style detection: compute person-level variance in ratings (low variance = acquiescence/satisficing), correlate with extreme response style indices, compare to ground-truth ratings using intraclass correlation. Apply response style correction methods (ipsatization, anchor normalization) and assess whether corrected scores better predict ground truth. Use R for all analysis.

**Why it matters:** Response style correction is standard in survey measurement for cross-cultural comparability. LLM-as-judge systems are increasingly used for safety evaluation but their response biases are poorly characterized. If LLM judges exhibit systematic acquiescence or satisficing, safety scores from those judges are systematically biased. Detecting and correcting for these biases would improve the validity of automated safety evaluation without model retraining.

**Relevant context:** Response style bias in LLM judges has been informally noted (sycophancy, self-preference) but not analyzed using formal survey methodology tools. Survey satisficing detection methods are documented in psychometric literature. The 'Cross-Model Autograder Bias Audit' idea (gen-0080) in the tracker addresses a related question from a different angle. ICC and intraclass correlation analysis is straightforward in R. The application of formal response style correction to LLM judges appears novel.
