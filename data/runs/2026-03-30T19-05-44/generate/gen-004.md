# Nomological Network Audit of AI Safety Constructs

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks claim to measure constructs like 'harmlessness', 'honesty', or 'refusal robustness' but rarely demonstrate that scores correlate with theoretically related measures (convergent validity) and do not correlate with unrelated measures (discriminant validity). This nomological network evidence is entirely absent from most safety benchmarks.

**Direction:** Select four safety benchmarks targeting distinct constructs (e.g., TruthfulQA for honesty, WildGuard for refusal, HarmBench for robustness, BBQ for bias) and four non-safety benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, HellaSwag). Extract model scores from published leaderboards. Compute a correlation matrix across all benchmarks for a common set of 50+ models. Perform confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) in R (lavaan) testing a two-factor model (safety vs. capability). Report convergent validity (within-safety correlations), discriminant validity (safety-vs-capability correlations), and compare against the nomological predictions from benchmark documentation.

**Why it matters:** Only 53.4% of 445 benchmarks present any validity evidence. A nomological network analysis for safety benchmarks would be the first systematic psychometric audit of whether safety scores measure distinct constructs or are proxies for general capability. arxiv:2603.15121 calls for nomological networks in LLM capability benchmarks, extending this to safety is novel and practically urgent.

**Relevant context:** arxiv:2603.15121 (nomological networks for LLM benchmarks); arxiv:2511.04703 (construct validity in LLM benchmarks, 445-benchmark audit); Open LLM Leaderboard and HELM provide cross-benchmark scores; lavaan CFA in R is well-suited for this.
