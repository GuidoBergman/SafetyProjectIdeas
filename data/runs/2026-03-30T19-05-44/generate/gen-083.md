# Minimum Detectable Effect Analysis for Data Contamination in Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** The data contamination causal chain has a poorly characterized 'contamination detection sensitivity' link. Current detection methods have unknown statistical power — they may fail to detect mild-to-moderate contamination that still meaningfully inflates scores. Without knowing the minimum effect size a detection method can reliably flag, evaluators cannot know whether a 'clean' result means truly uncontaminated or just below detection threshold.

**Direction:** Using publicly available controlled contamination experiments (e.g., ICML 2025 'How Much Can We Forget about Data Contamination?'), extract the contamination magnitude vs. score inflation relationship. Treat this as a power analysis problem: using the observed effect sizes and their variance, compute the minimum detectable effect (MDE) for each detection method under realistic sample sizes (typical benchmark sizes of 500-2000 items). Use standard power analysis functions in R (pwr package) and plot MDE curves across detection methods. Identify the contamination zone (effect sizes below MDE) where evaluations are operationally blind. Propose a reporting standard: evaluators should report MDE alongside contamination test results.

**Why it matters:** A negative contamination test with unknown power is not evidence of cleanliness. Establishing MDE standards forces honest reporting and identifies when additional validation is required.

**Relevant context:** ICML 2025 research showed that even 144x repetition can be forgotten at scale. DCR paper (EMNLP 2025) quantified contamination. Multiple surveys of contamination detection exist (arXiv 2502.17521, 2502.14425). Power analysis is standard psychometric methodology. No paper has applied this framing explicitly to contamination detection.
