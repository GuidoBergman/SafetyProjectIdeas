# Validity Argument Mapping for AI Safety Certification Claims

**Problem:** When labs or regulators claim a model is 'safe' based on benchmark performance, they are making an interpretive argument that proceeds through multiple inferential steps (item responses → benchmark score → construct of safety → decision about deployment). Kane's validity argument framework requires each inferential link to be justified with evidence. No AI safety evaluation has been subjected to formal validity argument analysis.

**Direction:** Select one widely cited safety evaluation process (e.g., Anthropic's or OpenAI's pre-deployment safety evaluation summaries, or an AISI evaluation report). Map the implicit interpretive argument: identify the claim being made, the inferential chain from evidence to claim, and the assumptions at each step. Evaluate each assumption against published psychometric evidence (using the construct validity literature reviewed in arxiv:2502.06559 and arxiv:2505.10573). Identify the weakest inferential links. Write up as a validity argument critique.

**Why it matters:** Validity argument mapping is the gold standard for evaluating whether a high-stakes assessment interpretation is justified. Applying it to AI safety certification claims would reveal exactly where the interpretive chain is weakest—whether at the item level, construct level, or generalization level—and provide a structured roadmap for strengthening safety evaluations to meet the standard of high-stakes testing.
