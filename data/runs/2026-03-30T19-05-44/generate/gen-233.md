# Multidimensional IRT for AI Safety: Testing Whether Safety Is One Construct or Many

**Problem:** All IRT applications to AI use unidimensional models (single latent ability). But safety is theoretically multidimensional: harmful content avoidance, honesty, manipulation resistance, and bias avoidance are all distinct. Multidimensional IRT (MIRT) can test whether a single safety factor suffices or whether multiple correlated dimensions are needed. This has been 'discussed as needed' in the literature but never empirically conducted for safety benchmarks.

**Direction:** Assemble a response matrix spanning multiple safety benchmarks (TruthfulQA, BBQ, a harm refusal dataset). Fit a series of competing MIRT models using the mirt R package: (1) unidimensional, (2) two-dimensional correlated (honesty + harmlessness), (3) bifactor (safety-general + specifics), (4) three-dimensional (HHH). Compare via model fit indices (RMSEA, CFI, BIC). Examine factor loadings to identify which items load on specific safety dimensions.

**Why it matters:** If safety is unidimensional, simplified evaluation is justified. If multidimensional, then single-score safety leaderboards hide critical information — a model could be ranked 3rd overall but rank 12th on honesty and 1st on harm avoidance. MIRT results directly inform the design of next-generation safety reporting.

**Relevant context:** The mirt R package supports MIRT and bifactor models. MIRT for factor structure assessment is well-reviewed in Frontiers in Education (2019). IRT-Router (2025 ACL) shows MIRT is feasible for LLM routing. The AI safety literature has called for multidimensional measurement but never delivered it empirically.
