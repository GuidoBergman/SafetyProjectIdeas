# Assessment Center Methodology for AI Agent Safety Evaluation

**Problem:** Current AI safety evaluations use single-task, single-prompt formats. Assessment centers in organizational psychology evaluate candidates across multiple exercises designed to elicit a range of job-relevant behaviors, scored by multiple trained observers. AI agent safety is a multi-dimensional, multi-situation construct that single-task benchmarks cannot adequately assess.

**Direction:** Design a structured 'AI safety assessment center' protocol: define 4–5 safety-relevant behavioral dimensions (e.g., deception resistance, instruction-following under adversarial pressure, appropriate refusal, value stability under persona shifts). Design one scenario/exercise per dimension. Have multiple human raters independently score model behavior in each scenario using behaviorally anchored rating scales (BARS). Compute inter-rater reliability (ICC in R) and a within-model profile across dimensions. Pilot with 5–10 models.

**Why it matters:** Assessment center methodology uses multiple methods, multiple raters, and multiple scenarios to achieve construct validity that no single test can provide. Applying this to AI safety would yield richer, more defensible safety profiles than current scalar scores. The method explicitly separates different safety dimensions.

**Relevant context:** Assessment center methodology is the I/O psychology standard for complex construct evaluation (ScienceDirect overview). METR's capability evaluations share some structural similarities but lack the BARS and multi-rater components. BARS development and ICC computation are pure psychometric tasks. Feasible within 30 hours as a protocol design + pilot study.
