# G-Theory Decomposition of Prompt Sensitivity in AI Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** P2: Prompt sensitivity destroys measurement reliability (76-point swings)

**Direction:** Apply Generalizability Theory to decompose variance in safety benchmark scores across prompt variants, items, and models, quantifying how much of observed score variance is attributable to prompt wording vs. genuine model capability differences.

**Why it matters:** The 76-point performance swings from prompt rephrasing are currently treated as noise, but G-Theory can tell us exactly how much of total score variance is prompt-induced vs. model-genuine. This gives the field a principled reliability coefficient (G-coefficient) for safety benchmarks and informs how many prompt variants are needed for stable measurement.

**Relevant context:** Stanford's AI Measurement Science lab notes that G-Theory decomposes multiple facets of measurement error simultaneously. Researchers have already applied G-theory to LLM rater reliability (ScienceDirect 2025). Prompt sensitivity has been documented at 76 accuracy points for LLaMA-2-13B (EMNLP 2024). A G-study crossing items x prompt_variants x models can be run in R using the gtheory or lme4 package on publicly available benchmark response data.
