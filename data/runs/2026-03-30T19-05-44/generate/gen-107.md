# Construct Validity Audit of Top AI Safety Benchmarks Using the AERA/APA Standards Framework

**Problem:** The 'Measuring what Matters' systematic review (2025) found that the majority of LLM benchmarks lack construct validity evidence, yet no study has applied the full AERA/APA/NCME Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing framework as an audit instrument specifically to AI safety benchmarks. Without a systematic validity evidence audit, practitioners cannot know which safety benchmarks to trust.

**Direction:** Select 10-15 high-impact AI safety benchmarks (e.g., SafetyBench, TrustLLM, MLCommons v0.5, HELM Safety, WildGuard, HarmBench). For each, collect all available documentation (paper, code, data card). Score each benchmark against the five sources of validity evidence: content, response process, internal structure, relations to other variables, and consequences. Tabulate results in a validity evidence matrix. Identify the most common validity gaps. Propose a minimum validity reporting standard for AI safety benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Directly closes the gap in Success State 2. No existing work applies the full AERA Standards framework specifically to AI safety benchmarks. This provides both a practical audit tool and a normative standard that labs and policymakers can require.

**Relevant context:** The AI Construct Lexis project (2025) is building nomological networks for AI. 'Measuring what Matters' (arxiv 2511.04703) audited 445 LLM benchmarks but not specifically safety ones. 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' (arxiv 2601.23112) identifies similar gaps without applying psychometric standards formally. The AERA Standards framework is the gold standard in psychometrics and maps cleanly to a structured audit.
