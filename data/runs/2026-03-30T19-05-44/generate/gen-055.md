# Multi-Trait Multi-Method Matrix Audit of AI Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** It is unclear whether AI safety benchmarks measure distinct constructs (e.g., toxicity resistance, deception detection, value alignment) or whether they largely reflect a single general 'safety' factor confounded with general capability. Without construct validity evidence, benchmark proliferation may be redundant and uninformative.

**Direction:** Construct a MTMM matrix using 3–4 safety-relevant traits (e.g., toxicity refusal, deception, harm facilitation, value alignment) and 3–4 measurement methods (adversarial prompts, multiple-choice, role-play scenarios, red-teaming checklists). Compute correlations between all trait-method combinations across 20–30 models from Open LLM Leaderboard. Inspect convergent validity (same trait, different methods should correlate highly) and discriminant validity (different traits should not correlate as highly). Interpret using Campbell-Fiske criteria. Conduct in R with existing benchmark score data.

**Why it matters:** The MTMM matrix is the gold standard for construct validity evidence in psychometrics. Applying it to AI safety would reveal whether current benchmarks are measuring what they claim, which traits are genuinely distinct, and which benchmark pairs are redundant—directly informing which evaluations are worth running.

**Relevant context:** MTMM was developed by Campbell and Fiske (1959) and is well-established in organizational and educational psychology. No published application to AI safety benchmarks was found in search. The R multicon package implements MTMM analysis. Open LLM Leaderboard provides scores across multiple benchmarks for 100+ models.
