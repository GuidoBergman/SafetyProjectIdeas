# Face Validity Survey: Do Domain Experts Agree That Safety Benchmark Items Measure Safety?

**Problem:** Face validity—whether items appear to measure what they claim to measure to relevant experts—is the most basic form of validity evidence and is almost universally absent from AI safety benchmark documentation. Expert disagreement about whether a benchmark item is 'really' a safety test vs. a capability test has significant implications for score interpretation.

**Direction:** Assemble a panel of 5-8 AI safety researchers (reachable through BAISH network, LESSWRONG, or EA forums) and 5-8 psychometricians. Present each with a random sample of 40 items from 3 safety benchmarks, asking: (1) Does this item measure safety behavior? (2) Which safety construct does it target? (3) Could a model pass this without being meaningfully safe? Compute inter-rater agreement (Fleiss kappa) and map disagreements to item characteristics. Report items with poor face validity.

**Why it matters:** If AI safety researchers and psychometricians cannot agree that benchmark items measure safety, the benchmark's claim to measure safety is empirically undermined. Face validity surveys are cheap to conduct and provide the first human-expert grounding for safety benchmark validity—directly addressing the finding that only 53.4% of benchmarks present any validity evidence.

**Relevant context:** 445-benchmark audit (arxiv:2511.04703): 53.4% present validity evidence; face validity concept (cogn-iq.org); arxiv:2502.06559 cites expert disagreement on what safety benchmarks measure; BAISH has access to a safety research network for expert panels; Fleiss kappa in R (irr package).
