# Actuarial vs. Clinical Judgment in AI Safety Risk Assessment

**Problem:** AI safety assessments are often based on unstructured expert opinions (clinical judgment) about model risk. Clinical psychology research has consistently shown actuarial (formula-based) methods outperform unstructured clinical judgment for risk prediction, but this finding has not been systematically applied to AI safety evaluation.

**Direction:** Conduct a structured comparison: gather existing AI safety assessments where both expert qualitative risk opinions and quantitative benchmark scores are available for the same models. Operationalize actuarial prediction as a weighted composite of benchmark scores (fit via logistic regression using a criterion like red-team failure rate). Compare predictive validity of the actuarial model vs. expert consensus judgments against an external criterion. This is a construct validity audit implementable entirely in R with published evaluation data.

**Why it matters:** The Meehl clinical-actuarial debate is one of the most replicated findings in psychology: actuarial methods win. Applying this framework to AI safety would provide evidence about whether structured benchmarks should override or supplement expert intuitions in deployment decisions, directly informing safety governance protocols.

**Relevant context:** The Meehl (1954) and Dawes et al. classic finding on actuarial vs. clinical judgment is replicated across domains. METR and similar organizations publish structured AI capability assessments alongside expert opinions. Published red-team results exist for some frontier models providing criterion data. Purely analytical project in R.
