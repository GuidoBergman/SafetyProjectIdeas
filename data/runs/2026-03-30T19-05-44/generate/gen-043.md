# Test-Retest Reliability of Safety Benchmarks Under Stochastic Decoding

**Problem:** LLM outputs are stochastic (temperature > 0). Safety benchmark scores therefore have measurement error from decoding randomness, independent of prompt sensitivity. This source of error is never reported in benchmark papers, meaning published safety scores may differ substantially from a re-run of the same evaluation.

**Direction:** For 5–10 open-source models (accessible via HuggingFace), run TruthfulQA and one other safety benchmark three times each at the default temperature. Compute intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) between runs using the ICC() function in R (psych package). Estimate the Standard Error of Measurement (SEM) and 95% confidence interval for each model's score. Assess what score differences between two models are statistically distinguishable.

**Why it matters:** If SEM is large relative to typical between-model score differences, current safety rankings may be statistically indistinguishable noise. This directly threatens the validity of leaderboard-style safety rankings.

**Relevant context:** Atil et al. (2024) showing up to 10% variation under deterministic settings; ICC in R; TruthfulQA; SEM formula from CTT; stochastic decoding as a measurement error facet.
