# Measurement Invariance Testing for Safety Benchmarks Across Model Families

**Problem:** Comparing safety scores across model families (e.g., GPT vs. Llama vs. Gemini) assumes that the benchmark measures the same construct in the same way across families—the psychometric assumption of measurement invariance. This assumption is untested. If metric or scalar invariance fails, cross-family safety comparisons are meaningless.

**Direction:** Use item-level safety benchmark data from Open LLM Leaderboard, grouping models by family (3+ families, 15+ models per family). Run a sequence of multi-group confirmatory factor analyses (CFA) in R (lavaan): configural model (same factor structure), metric model (equal loadings), scalar model (equal intercepts), strict model (equal residuals). Report chi-square difference tests, CFI change, and RMSEA. Identify which items show non-invariance and discuss whether cross-family safety ranking comparisons are justified.

**Why it matters:** Every AI safety leaderboard implicitly assumes measurement invariance. If invariance fails at the metric level, the benchmark measures fundamentally different constructs in different model families, and comparative rankings are misleading. This analysis would be the first formal psychometric justification (or refutation) of cross-family safety comparisons.

**Relevant context:** Measurement invariance testing methodology (Vandenberg & Lance; PMC 2024 overview); EFA-based approaches to invariance (Tandfonline 2024); lavaan R package; no existing study applies invariance testing to AI safety benchmarks.
