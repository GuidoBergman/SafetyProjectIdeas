# Minimal Practically Important Difference (MPID) for AI Safety: Adapting MCID Methods to Define When Safety Score Differences Are Meaningful

**Problem:** When one AI model scores 75% and another scores 77% on a safety benchmark, does that difference matter for deployment decisions? There is no principled method for determining what size of safety score difference constitutes a practically important distinction versus noise—analogous to the clinical problem of distinguishing statistically significant from clinically meaningful treatment effects.

**Direction:** Adapt Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID) methods to AI safety. Use the anchor-based approach: collect deployment decisions by AI safety practitioners about whether model pairs with various safety score gaps are treated differently in practice. Use the distribution-based approach: compute 0.5 SD of safety scores across a model population as a conventional MPID threshold. Use the Delphi method with safety experts to elicit consensus on minimum important differences across hazard categories. Compare all three MPID estimates and assess their consistency. Express results as a benchmark-specific MPID with confidence intervals.

**Why it matters:** MCID has transformed clinical trial interpretation by distinguishing statistical from clinical significance. The analogous distinction matters equally for AI safety: with large sample sizes, trivial safety score differences become statistically significant. An MPID would allow practitioners and regulators to focus on differences that matter for decisions, not just noise. The methodology requires expert elicitation and basic statistics in R—no ML training.

**Relevant context:** MCID calculation methods (anchor-based, distribution-based, Delphi) are well-established in clinical measurement literature. The Delphi method for consensus elicitation is feasible in a ~30-hour project with a small expert panel. AI safety benchmark score distributions are available from public evaluations. The distinction between statistical and practical significance in AI benchmarks has been raised informally but no formal MPID methodology has been applied.
