# Identifying Confounds in Safety Measurement: Does Instruction-Following Inflate Safety Scores?

**Problem:** Establish construct validity for AI safety evaluation

**Direction:** Test whether safety benchmark scores are confounded by instruction-following ability: (1) collect safety scores and instruction-following scores (IFEval or similar) for 30+ models, (2) regress safety scores on instruction-following scores to estimate the confound magnitude, (3) compute partial correlations between safety scores and safety-training indicators after controlling for instruction-following. Determine whether models appear 'safe' primarily because they are good at following instructions generally.

**Why it matters:** A model that refuses harmful requests may do so because it was safety-trained or simply because it is very obedient to any instruction framing (including refusal-inducing system prompts). If safety scores are primarily driven by instruction-following ability, then 'safety benchmarks' are actually measuring a confounded mixture, not pure safety.

**Relevant context:** 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' (2025) raises this exact concern about refusal-rate metrics. Instruction-following benchmarks like IFEval are publicly available on the Open LLM Leaderboard alongside safety scores for many models. Partial correlation and regression are feasible in R without heavy coding.
