# Standardized Validity Reporting Template for AI Safety Benchmarks: A Delphi Study

**Problem:** No standardized validity reporting template exists for AI safety benchmarks. Labs report what they choose, making systematic validity comparison impossible. The closest analog is the COSMIN checklist for clinical outcome measures or the Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (AERA/APA/NCME) — but neither has been adapted to AI safety.

**Direction:** Conduct a structured literature review of validity evidence reporting in 20 major AI safety benchmarks. Identify what validity information is reported, in what format, and what is consistently missing. Using Delphi methodology (or structured expert review with 5-8 AI safety researchers), develop a standardized validity reporting template. Validate the template by applying it to 3-5 existing benchmarks. Publish the template as a proposed community standard. This is primarily a conceptual/qualitative research project with structured data collection.

**Why it matters:** Enables Success State 2 at the field level. A standardized reporting template is the prerequisite for systematic validity review, meta-analysis, and regulatory requirements. Analogous tools (CONSORT for clinical trials, PRISMA for systematic reviews) transformed their fields. An AI safety analog would have comparable impact.

**Relevant context:** The 'Measuring what Matters' paper proposed an 8-recommendation checklist (2025) as a starting point. The COSMIN checklist for outcome measures provides a direct methodological template. AI Construct Lexis (2025) is building related infrastructure. A Delphi study or structured expert review is feasible within 30 hours with a small expert panel.
