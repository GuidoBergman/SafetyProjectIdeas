# Proxy-Construct Gap Quantification: How Correlated Are Safety Proxy Scores with General Capability?

**Problem:** The safetywashing risk operates through the 'benchmark measures proxy not construct' link. Ren et al. (2024) showed many safety benchmarks highly correlate with general capability/model size. But the magnitude and heterogeneity of this proxy-construct gap has not been formally quantified in a way that gives evaluators actionable thresholds: how high a correlation is too high for a score to count as measuring safety distinctively?

**Direction:** Using publicly available benchmark leaderboards (e.g., HELM, OpenLLM Leaderboard, AlpacaEval) plus safety-specific scores, assemble a model-by-benchmark correlation matrix. Compute partial correlations between safety scores and general capability controlling for model size (parameter count) and training compute (FLOPs if available from model cards). Apply exploratory factor analysis in R to identify how many latent factors underlie the benchmark matrix and whether safety benchmarks load on a distinct factor or collapse onto the general capability factor. Compute discriminant validity statistics (average variance extracted, maximum shared variance). Establish an empirical threshold for 'safety distinctiveness' grounded in Campbell-Fiske criteria.

**Why it matters:** Overconfident deployment decisions based on proxy scores that merely track capability create a systematic false sense of security. Quantifying the proxy-construct gap gives developers and regulators a concrete criterion for rejecting insufficiently discriminant benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** Ren et al. (NeurIPS 2024) documented high correlations between safety benchmarks and upstream capabilities. The safetywashing paper is directly relevant. The 'Measurement to Meaning' framework calls for convergent/discriminant validity evidence. No paper has yet established a formal threshold for acceptable safety-capability correlation.
