# Is 'Safety' Unidimensional or Multidimensional? An Exploratory Factor Analysis of Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** Establish construct validity for AI safety evaluation

**Direction:** Using item-level or subscale-level safety data from one or more benchmarks (e.g., SALAD-Bench's 6 attack categories × 16 harm types), run an exploratory factor analysis (EFA) in R (using the psych package). Apply parallel analysis to determine the number of factors, examine factor loadings for interpretability, and test whether a single safety dimension or multiple dimensions (e.g., robustness-to-jailbreaks vs. baseline-refusal vs. context-specific-harm) best fits the data.

**Why it matters:** If safety is multidimensional, then single aggregate safety scores are misleading—a model could be excellent at resisting jailbreaks but poor at avoiding subtle harms. Identifying the true dimensional structure of safety is essential for valid measurement and for understanding what 'making a model safer' actually means.

**Relevant context:** SALAD-Bench uses a hierarchical structure with multiple attack types and harm categories, making it well-suited for dimensional analysis. No published study has applied EFA to safety benchmark subscales. The broader LLM dimensionality literature ('A Data-Driven Study on LLM Structure', EMNLP 2025) finds multiple capability dimensions—safety likely follows suit.
