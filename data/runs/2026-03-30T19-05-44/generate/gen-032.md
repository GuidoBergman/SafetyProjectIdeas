# G-Theory Reliability Study of Safety Benchmarks Across Prompt Conditions

**Problem:** Safety benchmark scores are reported as point estimates without reliability analysis. When prompt formatting changes, scores shift dramatically, but we do not know what fraction of variance is signal (model capability) versus noise (prompt × item interaction). G-Theory can partition this.

**Direction:** Select 3–5 publicly available safety benchmarks (e.g., TruthfulQA, HarmBench subsets, AdvBench). For each, obtain or compute scores for 20+ models under at least 3 prompt variants. Run a crossed G-study (models × items × prompt variants) in R using lme4 variance-component estimation. Compute the generalizability coefficient (Eρ²) and the dependability coefficient (Φ). Conduct D-studies to find how many items and prompt variants are needed to reach Φ ≥ 0.80.

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmark generalizability coefficients are below 0.70 (common in early-stage educational tests), reported safety scores are dominated by noise, undermining policy decisions based on them.

**Relevant context:** Open LLM Leaderboard; TruthfulQA; G-Theory (Brennan 2001); lme4 in R for variance-component estimation; G-Theory has zero AI applications per project open problems.
