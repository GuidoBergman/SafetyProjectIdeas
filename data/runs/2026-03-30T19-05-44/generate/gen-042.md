# Internal Consistency Reliability of Safety Benchmarks: Cronbach's Alpha Across Task Types

**Problem:** Reliability analysis is foundational in psychometrics but has not been systematically applied to AI safety benchmarks. If items within a safety benchmark do not cohere (low alpha), the benchmark is measuring multiple unrelated constructs, invalidating aggregate scores.

**Direction:** For 8–10 publicly available safety benchmarks (TruthfulQA, HarmBench subtasks, AdvBench categories, HELM safety tasks), compute Cronbach's alpha and omega (McDonald's omega, more robust than alpha) using item-level model responses from the Open LLM Leaderboard or publicly available evaluation results. Stratify by benchmark topic (factuality, harmlessness, robustness). Compare alpha values against the conventional thresholds (>0.70 acceptable, >0.80 good).

**Why it matters:** Alpha below 0.70 in a safety benchmark means the items do not form a coherent scale. Reporting a single safety score is then unjustified, and subscores by task type should be used instead.

**Relevant context:** CTT reliability analysis; psych::alpha and psych::omega in R; Open LLM Leaderboard item-level data; TruthfulQA, HarmBench; Cronbach (1951) and McDonald (1999) as frameworks.
