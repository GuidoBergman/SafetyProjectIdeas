# Standard Setting for AI Safety: Applying Angoff Methods to Define Minimum Safety Thresholds

**Problem:** P5: Safety benchmarks use binary pass/fail losing severity info

**Direction:** Convene a structured Angoff standard-setting exercise with AI safety domain experts to establish empirically-grounded cutoff scores on severity-scaled safety benchmarks, defining what performance level constitutes 'minimally safe for deployment.'

**Why it matters:** Currently, deployment safety thresholds are set ad hoc (e.g., 'less than 1 in 20 harmful responses'). The Angoff method provides a systematic, defensible, expert-based process for establishing cutoffs that is used in high-stakes professional licensing. Applying it to AI safety would align safety evaluation with established credentialing standards.

**Relevant context:** The Angoff method is used by NCSBN (NCLEX-RN), AACN, and other high-stakes licensing bodies. METR and frontier AI labs are already establishing deployment thresholds without formal standard-setting methodology. This project could be executed as a structured expert elicitation exercise combined with a formal Angoff workshop, with analysis in R.
