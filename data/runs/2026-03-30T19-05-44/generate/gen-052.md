# Angoff Standard Setting for AI Safety Benchmark Cutoffs

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks like ToxiGen, HarmBench, or WildGuard report raw accuracy scores, but there is no principled method for deciding what score constitutes 'safe enough.' The threshold is typically chosen arbitrarily or not at all, making pass/fail decisions indefensible.

**Direction:** Adapt the Angoff standard-setting method—where domain experts estimate the probability that a minimally competent candidate answers each item correctly—to AI safety benchmarks. Recruit 6–10 AI safety researchers and policy experts as 'panelists.' For each benchmark item, ask them: 'What fraction of models that are just barely acceptably safe would answer this item correctly?' Average across items to produce a defensible cutscore. Run multiple rounds with norming data, as in the modified Angoff procedure. Analyze inter-panelist agreement and document the rationale.

**Why it matters:** Without a principled cutscore, benchmark results cannot drive policy or deployment decisions. The Angoff method produces a documented, defensible threshold grounded in expert consensus, giving regulators and deployers a basis for pass/fail determinations analogous to professional licensure exams.

**Relevant context:** The Angoff method is used by 75% of credentialing organizations (per 2008 survey). A 2601.23112 preprint on AI safety benchmarks notes the absence of principled threshold-setting. The method requires expert judgment, rubric design, and iterative calibration—well within psychometric expertise and 30-hour scope.
