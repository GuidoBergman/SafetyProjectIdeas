# CDM Safety Attribute Q-Matrix: Expert Elicitation and Validation of Safety Skill Prerequisites

**Problem:** Cognitive Diagnostic Models require a Q-matrix specifying which latent attributes each item requires. For AI safety, no validated Q-matrix exists. Constructing one requires: (1) defining safety attributes at the right granularity, (2) expert coding of benchmark items for attribute requirements, and (3) validating the Q-matrix empirically. This is foundational infrastructure for CDM-based safety evaluation — but it is a tractable, methodologically clear project.

**Direction:** Step 1: Conduct an expert card-sorting exercise (with 2-3 AI safety researchers) to define 5-8 safety attributes (e.g., harmful content refusal, manipulation resistance, deception avoidance, privacy preservation, bias mitigation, instruction following under adversarial pressure). Step 2: Code 80-120 items from TruthfulQA, BBQ, and HarmBench for attribute requirements (binary: item requires this attribute or not). Step 3: Compute inter-coder reliability (Cohen's kappa). Step 4: Empirically validate the Q-matrix by checking that the G-DINA model fit improves vs. a unidimensional model. Report the validated Q-matrix as a community resource.

**Why it matters:** A validated Q-matrix for AI safety is a public good that enables all future CDM research on AI safety. Without it, CDM research cannot proceed. This project is self-contained, requires no advanced coding, and produces a concrete artifact (the Q-matrix) that other researchers can use immediately. It is exactly the right foundational work to enable the CDM gap to be closed.

**Relevant context:** Q-matrix construction methodology is well-documented in the CDM literature. Cambridge Assessment's CDM report provides Q-matrix validation procedures. The GDINA R package supports Q-matrix empirical validation. AI safety taxonomies (from papers like 'Safetywashing' and 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?') provide the conceptual basis for attribute definition.
