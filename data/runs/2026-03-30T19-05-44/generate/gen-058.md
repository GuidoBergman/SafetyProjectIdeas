# Acquiescence and Extreme Response Style Detection in LLM Safety Evaluations

**Problem:** When safety benchmarks use Likert-type rating scales or yes/no formats, LLMs (especially those serving as evaluator models) may exhibit systematic response styles—always agreeing, always giving extreme scores—analogous to acquiescence bias in human survey respondents. This contaminates benchmark scores with method variance rather than true safety signal.

**Direction:** Import survey methodology's response style detection techniques. Design matched pairs of benchmark items where one is the logical negation of the other (straight vs. reversed items). Compute acquiescence indices (net tendency to agree regardless of content) and extreme response style indices (frequency of selecting scale endpoints) across a set of LLM evaluator models. Use R to run factor analyses separating trait variance from method variance. Test whether response style scores correlate with benchmark error rates. Propose corrections.

**Why it matters:** Research confirms LLMs exhibit acquiescence-like biases and framing-induced agreement bias (MIT Press TACL 2023; arXiv 2601.13537). Survey methodology has decades of validated methods for detecting and correcting response styles. Applying these to LLM-as-judge evaluations would improve the validity of the growing LLM-evaluation ecosystem.

**Relevant context:** MIT TACL paper shows LLMs exhibit human-like survey response biases. The framing-bias paper (arXiv 2601.13537) documents agreement bias in LLM judges. R packages (psych, lavaan) support factor analysis and response style computation. This is a purely psychometric analysis requiring no ML training.
