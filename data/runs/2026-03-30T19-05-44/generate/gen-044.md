# IRT Item Information Functions for Safety Benchmark Optimisation

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks are constructed by convenience sampling of harmful prompts (27% of benchmarks use convenience sampling, per Paper 14). IRT item information functions identify which items contribute most information at each ability level. No study has applied this to safety benchmarks to diagnose where evaluation information is concentrated versus sparse.

**Direction:** Fit a 2PL IRT model to TruthfulQA or HarmBench using model responses from the Open LLM Leaderboard (mirt in R). Plot item information functions (IIF) and the test information function (TIF) across the ability range. Identify ability regions where information is low (the benchmark is uninformative). Characterise items that fall in high-information regions by topic, length, and difficulty.

**Why it matters:** Safety benchmarks likely cluster items around moderate difficulty, leaving frontier models (high ability) and very weak models (low ability) poorly measured. This is a direct analogue to ceiling/floor effects and has direct implications for evaluating the most dangerous frontier models.

**Relevant context:** IRT item information functions (Hambleton et al. 1991); mirt package in R; Open LLM Leaderboard data; TruthfulQA, HarmBench; test information function (TIF) interpretation.
