# Minimum Important Difference (MID) for AI Safety Score Improvements

**Problem:** When a model's safety score improves from 72% to 76%, is this meaningful or noise? The field lacks a concept of a minimum meaningful improvement on safety benchmarks, leading to overclaiming small gains in model cards and papers.

**Direction:** Adapt the Minimal Important Difference framework from health outcomes research. Use anchor-based methods: identify an external anchor (e.g., expert-rated change in real-world safety behavior, user perception of safety, or red-team pass/fail rates) and calculate what benchmark score change corresponds to a meaningful shift in the anchor. Use distribution-based methods (0.5 SD of score distribution) as a supplementary estimate. Apply to 3–4 widely used safety benchmarks using Open LLM Leaderboard historical data. Report the MID estimate with confidence intervals in R.

**Why it matters:** Without an MID, every marginal score improvement can be claimed as 'progress.' Establishing an MID for safety benchmarks would create a higher bar for meaningful safety claims in model cards, research papers, and regulatory submissions—analogous to how clinical trials must show changes exceeding the MCID to claim treatment efficacy.

**Relevant context:** MCID methodology is well-developed (anchor-based, distribution-based, Delphi methods). Distribution-based MID = 0.5 SD is a standard starting estimate. Open LLM Leaderboard provides longitudinal score data across model generations. No published application of MID to AI evaluation was found in searches.
