# Bifactor Modeling of AI Safety Benchmarks: Is There a General Safety Factor?

**Problem:** A single factor explains 79.3% of variance across general capability benchmarks. It is unknown whether a similar general safety factor (g-safety) underlies performance on safety benchmarks, or whether safety is genuinely multidimensional (separate refusal, honesty, harm-avoidance dimensions). A bifactor model can simultaneously estimate a general safety factor and orthogonal specific factors.

**Direction:** Collect model scores at the item level (or subtask level) across 4-6 safety benchmarks (TruthfulQA, WildGuard, HarmBench, BBQ, StereoSet, AIR-Bench). Using the mirt R package, fit: (1) a unidimensional IRT model, (2) a correlated-factors MIRT model with theoretically specified dimensions, (3) a bifactor model with a general safety factor plus specific benchmark factors. Compare models using AIC/BIC and omega-hierarchical. Report explained common variance (ECV) and omega-hierarchical to determine how much of reliable safety variance is general vs. specific.

**Why it matters:** If a strong general safety factor exists, a single efficient safety score is defensible. If safety is genuinely multidimensional, composite scores obscure important distinctions. This directly answers whether current single-number safety ratings are psychometrically sound, and guides how to structure future safety evaluation frameworks.
