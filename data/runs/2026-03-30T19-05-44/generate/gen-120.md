# Reliability of Safety Evaluator Ratings: An Inter-Rater Reliability and G-Theory Study

**Problem:** Many safety evaluations rely on human or LLM-as-judge ratings of model outputs. It is unknown how much variance in safety scores comes from evaluator disagreement (person-to-person or model-to-model for LLM judges) versus genuine model differences. Without quantifying inter-rater reliability and evaluator variance components, evaluation scores cannot be trusted.

**Direction:** Collect a dataset of AI model outputs on safety-relevant prompts rated by multiple evaluators (either human raters or different LLM judges). Apply both classical inter-rater reliability (ICC, Cohen's kappa using irr package) and G-Theory (gtheory package) to decompose variance into model, evaluator, item, and interaction components. Compute the generalizability coefficient attributable to the evaluator facet. Run D-studies to determine how many evaluators are needed to suppress evaluator noise below 20% of total variance.

**Why it matters:** Critical for Success State 4 (reproducible evaluation results). If LLM-judge variance is comparable to model variance, evaluator choice is as important as model capability — a profound threat to evaluation validity. This study quantifies the problem with enough precision to motivate standardized evaluation protocols.

**Relevant context:** The six-tiered evaluation framework (ScienceDirect 2025) names repeatability as the top tier but does not quantify it via G-Theory. HELM and WildGuard use LLM judges but do not report inter-rater reliability beyond simple agreement. G-Theory for AI evaluation is explicitly recommended in the 2025 ScienceDirect paper. The irr and gtheory R packages make this feasible.
