# DIF Analysis of Safety Benchmark Items Across Model Families: Are Items Biased Toward Certain Architectures?

**Problem:** In the false-negative deployment chain, a less-studied source of measurement invalidity is item bias across model families — if items systematically favor one model family (e.g., RLHF-trained vs. base models) over another at the same true safety level, the benchmark produces group-biased scores. This is analogous to cultural bias in educational testing and represents a form of measurement non-equivalence.

**Direction:** Using public benchmark data with responses from models across 2-3 families (e.g., GPT family, Claude family, open-weight instruction-tuned models like Llama-3), condition on overall safety score and run DIF analysis using the Mantel-Haenszel procedure and logistic regression DIF in R (difR package). Treat model family as the grouping variable. Identify items showing significant DIF (ETS B or C classification) in either direction. Examine item content of biased items: are they systematically related to refusal phrasings, context lengths, system prompt sensitivities, or other factors that differ by training methodology? Compute impact (overall group mean differences) vs. DIF (item-level bias) separately.

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmarks systematically favor one model family, comparative safety evaluations are invalid — the scores reflect training methodology artifacts, not underlying safety properties. DIF analysis is the standard tool for detecting this and is directly implementable in R.

**Relevant context:** FairDIF (AI and Ethics 2026) applied DIF concepts to ML classifiers. DIF methodology is standard in educational measurement (difR, lordif R packages). The sandbagging paper implicitly requires DIF analysis across contexts. Benchmark response data across model families is available from HELM and OpenLLM leaderboard. Novel application to AI safety benchmarking.
