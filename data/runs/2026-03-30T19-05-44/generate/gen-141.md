# Validity Vacuum: Benchmarks Without Evidence and the Regulatory Blindspot They Create

**Problem:** 46.6% of AI benchmarks lack any validity evidence (Failure A7), meaning there is no documented link between what the benchmark measures and any real-world safety outcome. This compounds with regulatory reliance (Failure B6): when policymakers specify compliance requirements referencing benchmarks that have no validity evidence, the resulting regulatory framework is untethered from actual safety. Compliance becomes a paperwork exercise rather than a safety guarantee.

**Direction:** Conduct a structured validity audit of the benchmarks cited in 3–5 major regulatory or governmental AI safety documents (EU AI Act technical standards, NIST AI RMF, the International AI Safety Report). For each cited benchmark, apply a validity evidence framework adapted from AERA/APA/NCME Standards: does it have content validity evidence? Criterion validity? Construct validity? Consequence validity? Score the benchmarks on a 0–4 evidence scale. Cross-reference with the safetywashing and 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' literature. Produce a structured validity scorecard and policy recommendations.

**Why it matters:** Regulatory frameworks that cite benchmarks without validity evidence are not just ineffective — they actively displace better-evidenced alternatives. A validity scorecard creates actionable guidance for regulators on which benchmarks merit policy reliance.

**Relevant context:** 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' (arxiv 2502.06559) documents the 46.6% statistic. 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' and EU AI Watch 2025 both discuss the regulatory-benchmark relationship. The AERA/APA/NCME Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing provide the validity framework directly applicable here.
