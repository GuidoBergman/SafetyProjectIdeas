# Content Validity Audit: Systematic Mapping of Safety Benchmark Items to Harm Taxonomies

**Problem:** Content validity requires that a benchmark's items adequately sample the domain it claims to measure. For safety benchmarks, this means items should proportionally represent a defensible taxonomy of harm types. No safety benchmark has been subjected to a systematic content validity audit mapping items to harm taxonomies (e.g., CBRN risks, manipulation, privacy, disinformation, cyberattacks).

**Direction:** Select 3 safety benchmarks (e.g., AIR-Bench 2024, WildGuard, HarmBench). For each, independently code all items against two harm taxonomies (e.g., AIR-Bench's own taxonomy and the UK AISI taxonomy). Compute coverage coefficients (proportion of taxonomy cells with at least one item) and balance coefficients (entropy of item distribution across categories). Assess inter-rater reliability (kappa) for item coding. Identify systematically underrepresented harm categories. Discuss implications for the domains where safety evaluations have no coverage.

**Why it matters:** Paper 19 found only 2 benchmarks for unknown-unknowns vs. 170 for known-knowns; content validity audits are a standard psychometric tool for identifying such gaps. A systematic content validity audit is more rigorous and actionable than informal critiques, and the inter-rater reliability analysis provides a defensible, publishable method for characterizing benchmark coverage.
