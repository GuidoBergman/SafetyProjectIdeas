# Applying the Rasch Model to AI Safety Benchmarks: A Test of Fundamental Measurement

**Problem:** The Rasch model imposes strict requirements (specific objectivity, sufficient statistics, invariant item ordering) that, if met, guarantee fundamental measurement properties comparable to physical scales. No AI safety benchmark has been tested for Rasch fit. If safety benchmarks meet Rasch requirements, safety scores would be genuinely interval-level measurements—a major claim with significant governance implications.

**Direction:** Select one safety benchmark with binary items (e.g., TruthfulQA: true/false questions) and at least 30 models and 50 items. Fit a Rasch model in R (eRm or TAM package). Assess item fit using infit/outfit statistics and parametric bootstrapping for critical values. Test person fit. Examine whether item difficulty ordering is invariant across model subgroups (low vs. high ability models) using graphical model tests. Report which items misfit and interpret substantively. Compare Rasch ability estimates against raw scores.

**Why it matters:** The Rasch model is the gold standard for fundamental measurement in psychometrics. A safety benchmark that fits the Rasch model provides interval-level measurements, making score differences meaningful across the scale. Identifying misfitting items reveals construct heterogeneity—items measuring something other than the target safety dimension. This is a direct test of measurement quality for safety scores.

**Relevant context:** Rasch model for AI literacy scale validation (Nature Comm 2025); item fit detection via parametric bootstrap (easyRasch R package 2024-2025); Rasch invariance testing (Zeileis exams paper); eRm and TAM packages in R; TruthfulQA has binary items amenable to Rasch analysis.
