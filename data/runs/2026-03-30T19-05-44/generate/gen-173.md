# Measurement Invariance Testing for AI Safety Benchmarks: Do Benchmarks Measure the Same Construct Across Model Families?

**Problem:** AI safety benchmark comparisons assume that the benchmark measures the same construct the same way for all model families. If a benchmark shows measurement non-invariance across model types—meaning item loadings or intercepts differ systematically—then cross-model comparisons are invalid even if total scores appear comparable.

**Direction:** Collect safety benchmark responses from models grouped into families (GPT, Claude, Llama, Gemini, Mistral). Treat items as indicators of a latent safety factor and test measurement invariance across families using multi-group confirmatory factor analysis (lavaan in R). Test sequentially: configural invariance (same factor structure), metric invariance (same loadings), scalar invariance (same intercepts). Identify which specific items or loadings are non-invariant. Interpret what non-invariant items have in common. Assess whether partial invariance is sufficient for meaningful comparison.

**Why it matters:** Measurement invariance testing is the standard prerequisite for any cross-group comparison in psychometrics. Failure of scalar invariance means that latent mean comparisons are biased. If AI safety benchmarks fail invariance tests across model families, published cross-model comparisons are methodologically invalid. lavaan in R implements multi-group CFA with well-established testing procedures. This application to AI safety benchmarks appears entirely novel.

**Relevant context:** Measurement invariance testing using lavaan is a standard R workflow with online tutorials (D-Lab Berkeley, Towards Data Science). The lavaan package is well-documented. Multi-group CFA for invariance testing is covered in multiple R textbooks. AI safety benchmarks have been administered to many model families, making response data available for analysis.
