# Nomological Network for AI Honesty: Mapping What Truthfulness Scores Should and Should Not Correlate With

**Problem:** A nomological network specifies the theoretical pattern of correlations a construct should have with related constructs (convergent validity) and should not have with unrelated constructs (discriminant validity). For AI honesty/truthfulness, no nomological network has ever been empirically constructed or tested. We do not know empirically whether TruthfulQA scores behave as a genuine honesty construct or as a general capability construct.

**Direction:** Operationalize the nomological network for AI honesty: predict that honesty scores should correlate positively with calibration measures (model knows what it doesn't know), moderately with general reasoning, but should not correlate with harmful content refusal (a separate construct) or with model size alone. Collect relevant scores from public leaderboards. Compute the full correlation matrix. Test whether the empirical pattern matches the theoretically predicted pattern using congruence coefficients. Flag where the nomological predictions are violated.

**Why it matters:** A violated nomological network means TruthfulQA is not measuring honesty as a psychological construct — it is measuring something else. This would explain why 'honest' models sometimes still hallucinate or deceive in practice. Establishing or disconfirming the nomological network is essential for validity of honesty measurement in AI.

**Relevant context:** The 'Measurement to Meaning' paper (arXiv:2505.10573) specifically argues that GPQA fails nomological validation for medical reasoning. Cronbach & Meehl's (1955) framework for nomological networks is well-established. Convergent/discriminant validity can be assessed with MTMM analysis. Public leaderboards provide the multi-construct data needed.
