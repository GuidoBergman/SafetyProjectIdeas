# Cognitive Diagnostic Modeling of AI Safety Attributes: Moving Beyond Aggregate Safety Scores

**Problem:** P4: 46.6% of benchmarks lack validity evidence

**Direction:** Apply a Cognitive Diagnostic Model (CDM) such as DINA or G-DINA to a safety benchmark, specifying a Q-matrix mapping items to safety sub-skills (e.g., refusal, truthfulness, harm avoidance, context sensitivity), to test whether the benchmark actually measures these claimed attributes.

**Why it matters:** If benchmark items cannot be reliably mapped to theorized safety attributes via a Q-matrix, this directly reveals construct invalidity. CDMs provide mastery probability profiles rather than single scores, giving empirical validity evidence and diagnostic information simultaneously.

**Relevant context:** CDMs are well-established in educational measurement (Cambridge Assessment 2021 review). The GDINA R package supports CDM fitting. Applying CDMs requires constructing a Q-matrix from the benchmark's intended skill structure—a theoretical task within a psychometrician's competency—followed by model fitting on publicly available response data.
