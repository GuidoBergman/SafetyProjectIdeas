# Efficiency Frontier of Safety Benchmark Subsampling: IRT vs. Random vs. Stratified Item Selection

**Problem:** tinyBenchmarks showed IRT-based item selection outperforms random selection for MMLU. It is unknown whether this efficiency advantage holds for safety benchmarks, where item difficulty distributions are different and model variance may be lower due to RLHF alignment effects.

**Direction:** Using safety benchmark item-level data (TruthfulQA or similar), compare three item-selection strategies at multiple sample sizes (10, 25, 50, 100 items): (1) random selection, (2) stratified-by-difficulty selection, (3) IRT-optimal selection (mirtCAT in R). For each strategy and sample size, compute RMSE and Spearman rank-correlation against full-benchmark scores. Plot efficiency frontiers.

**Why it matters:** If IRT-based selection provides only marginal benefit over stratified random sampling for safety benchmarks, practitioners can use simpler methods without psychometric software, lowering the barrier to efficient safety evaluation.

**Relevant context:** tinyBenchmarks (arXiv 2402.14992); mirtCAT in R for IRT-based CAT simulation; TruthfulQA; stratified sampling in R; RMSE and rank-correlation as efficiency metrics.
