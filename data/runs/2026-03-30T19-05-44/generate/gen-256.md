# G-Theory Validity Study for AI Safety Benchmarks: Quantifying Sources of Invalidity

**Problem:** P4: 46.6% of benchmarks lack validity evidence

**Direction:** Run a G-study on a safety benchmark by crossing items, prompt variants, and scoring rubrics as facets, computing variance components to determine what proportion of score variance is attributable to the intended construct vs. measurement artifacts.

**Why it matters:** If most score variance is attributable to prompt format or scoring rubric rather than to the model being tested, the benchmark has low construct validity by definition. G-Theory provides a single, interpretable coefficient (G-coefficient) that could serve as a minimum validity standard for safety benchmark publication.

**Relevant context:** The AI Measurement Science textbook (Stanford) describes G-Theory as appropriate for multi-facet AI evaluation reliability. Recent ScienceDirect 2025 study applies G-theory to LLM rater reliability. Running a G-study requires response data from multiple models across multiple prompt variants—available from existing sensitivity studies like the EMNLP 2024 prompt sensitivity dataset.
