# Cognitive Interviewing Protocol for Benchmark Item Validation

**Problem:** Benchmark items for AI safety are written by researchers and deployed without pretesting. Items may be ambiguous, have multiple defensible interpretations, or inadvertently probe surface features rather than the intended safety construct—analogous to poorly worded survey questions that elicit unintended responses.

**Direction:** Apply cognitive interviewing methodology from survey research: have 5–8 AI safety experts 'think aloud' while reading benchmark items, probing what construct they believe each item tests, what a 'safe' response would look like, and what alternative interpretations exist. Use the findings to produce an item quality rubric and flag items with validity threats. Audit 50–100 items from 2–3 major safety benchmarks. Report item-level validity concerns and recommendations for revision.

**Why it matters:** Cognitive interviewing is the standard pretesting technique for survey and test items. Applying it to AI safety benchmark items would reveal construct-irrelevant variance before benchmarks are deployed at scale—improving the validity of evaluations used in high-stakes deployment decisions.

**Relevant context:** Cognitive interviewing is a core method in survey methodology (PMC review papers, Cross-Cultural Survey Guidelines). Recent arXiv work uses LLMs for item evaluation, but human cognitive interviewing provides the construct validity anchor. No coding required; expert interviews and qualitative coding fit the 30-hour scope.
