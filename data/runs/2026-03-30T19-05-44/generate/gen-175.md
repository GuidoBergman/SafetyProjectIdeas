# Cognitive Interviewing Protocol for AI Safety Benchmark Items: Identifying Ambiguous and Miscommunicating Prompts Through Think-Aloud Methods

**Problem:** AI safety benchmark items are created by safety researchers but never subjected to systematic quality control testing analogous to cognitive interviewing in survey development. Items may be ambiguous, misinterpret the target behavior, or elicit responses for reasons unrelated to the intended safety construct. There is no established procedure for pre-publishing item quality validation in AI evaluation.

**Direction:** Adapt cognitive interviewing methodology to AI safety item development. Select 20-30 items from an existing safety benchmark. Conduct 'cognitive interviews' in two modes: (1) human expert think-aloud—safety researchers verbalize their interpretation of each item, flagging ambiguity, and (2) model think-aloud—prompt models to explain their reasoning before answering each item, then code reasoning transcripts for misinterpretation patterns. Code all transcripts using a standard cognitive interview coding framework (clarity, comprehension, retrieval, judgment, response). Flag items with >25% misinterpretation rate. Redesign flagged items and re-administer.

**Why it matters:** Cognitive interviewing is the established survey methodology tool for detecting item flaws before they contaminate data. AI safety benchmarks are currently deployed without this quality control step. If items are ambiguous or measure unintended behaviors, the entire benchmark is compromised. The methodology requires no coding—just think-aloud administration and qualitative coding—making it ideal for a participant with psychometric but limited coding skills.

**Relevant context:** Cognitive interviewing is well-documented in survey methodology (PMC9524256, PMC3771159). The method has been applied to validate questionnaire items in medical education. AI model 'think-aloud' through chain-of-thought reasoning is an established technique. Safety benchmark item quality has been criticized (the 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' paper, arxiv 2601.23112). This adaptation of cognitive interviewing to AI benchmark item validation appears novel.
