# Inter-Rater Reliability Generalizability Study for Human AI Safety Evaluations

**Problem:** Red-team evaluations and human preference ratings for AI safety rely on human raters, but the reliability of these ratings—and the sources of rater variance—have not been systematically analyzed. Generalizability theory (G-theory) from educational measurement partitions total score variance into variance due to raters, items, occasions, and their interactions.

**Direction:** Conduct a G-study: recruit 8–12 raters to evaluate the same set of 40–60 model outputs on multiple safety dimensions, across two rating occasions. Use the R package gtheory or lme4 to decompose variance components. Identify the dominant source of error (rater variance, item variance, or interaction). Run a D-study to determine how many raters and items are needed to achieve a target generalizability coefficient (G ≥ 0.80). Report optimal evaluation design specifications.

**Why it matters:** G-theory provides a more informative reliability analysis than simple inter-rater correlation because it identifies where reliability is lost (raters vs. items vs. occasions) and prescribes the optimal design. This directly informs how safety evaluation teams should be structured to achieve valid results efficiently.

**Relevant context:** Generalizability theory is a standard educational measurement technique (Cronbach et al. 1972). gtheory R package implements G-study variance component estimation. D-studies for optimizing evaluation design are directly applicable to AI red-team protocols. No coding beyond R required; raters can be recruited from safety research community.
