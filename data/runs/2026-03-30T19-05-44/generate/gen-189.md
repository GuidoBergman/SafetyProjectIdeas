# Criterion-Referenced vs. Norm-Referenced Interpretation of AI Safety Scores: Empirical Analysis of Which Interpretation Mode Better Supports Deployment Decisions

**Problem:** AI safety scores are implicitly treated as norm-referenced (ranking models against each other) even though deployment safety decisions require criterion-referenced interpretation (does this model meet a safety standard?). This conceptual confusion may lead to deployment of models that rank well relative to peers but fail absolute safety criteria, or rejection of models that meet absolute criteria but rank poorly.

**Direction:** Operationalize both interpretation modes for an existing safety benchmark. For norm-referenced: compute percentile ranks, standardized scores (z-scores), and rank stability across benchmark versions. For criterion-referenced: use Angoff or contrasting groups methods to establish absolute cut scores, then classify models as meeting or not meeting the standard. Collect deployment decisions from 10-15 AI practitioners and assess which interpretation mode better predicts their decisions. Analyze how many models pass under criterion-referenced but fail under norm-referenced interpretation and vice versa. Recommend which interpretation is appropriate for which decision context.

**Why it matters:** The distinction between criterion-referenced and norm-referenced measurement is foundational in psychometrics and has profound implications for decision-making. Current AI safety evaluation conflates these modes, leading to incoherent deployment standards. This analysis requires only R and survey methods—no ML training. It could directly inform regulatory frameworks for AI safety certification.

**Relevant context:** The distinction between norm-referenced and criterion-referenced measurement is well-documented (PubMed 2586296, teachers.institute). The 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' paper (arxiv 2502.06559) identifies normative assumptions in AI benchmarks as a key concern. The MLCommons benchmark uses a five-point grade scale that attempts criterion-referencing but without formal standard-setting. This empirical analysis of interpretation mode effects on AI deployment decisions appears novel.
