# Psychometric Analysis of Multimodal Safety: IRT for Vision-Language Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** All IRT applications to AI evaluation use text-only benchmarks. Multimodal models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini) are evaluated on vision-language safety benchmarks (e.g., MMSafetyBench, FigStep), but the psychometric properties of these benchmarks are unknown. Research shows MMMU and MathVista exhibit less than 5% performance gaps between multimodal and text-only models — suggesting many 'multimodal' benchmarks aren't measuring visual input. IRT can empirically test whether vision adds discriminative information.

**Direction:** Collect binary response matrices from public multimodal safety benchmarks for 15-20 models. Fit a unidimensional IRT model and examine: (1) whether vision-specific items show systematically different discrimination parameters than text-only items on the same benchmark, (2) whether residuals from a text-ability factor are correlated with vision-specific features (using a MIRT model with a vision-specific residual factor), (3) whether IRT item information peaks at different ability levels for visual vs. text safety items.

**Why it matters:** If visual safety items show no psychometric differentiation from text safety items (same discrimination, same difficulty ordering), this is empirical evidence that multimodal safety benchmarks are not measuring vision at all — they measure text-based safety with image distractors. This would be a major validity critique of the multimodal safety evaluation field.

**Relevant context:** MMSafetyBench, VLSafe, and FigStep are public multimodal safety benchmarks. Research (2024-2025) found many multimodal benchmarks fail to require visual processing. IRT can detect this through dimensionality testing. The mirt package handles this analysis. This would be the first psychometric analysis of multimodal safety benchmarks.
