# Construct Explication for 'Refusal Behavior': A Multidimensional IRT Approach

**Problem:** Many safety benchmarks reduce model safety to a single score, often based on refusal rates. But 'refusal behavior' is likely multidimensional: a model might refuse appropriately on bioweapons scenarios but not on cyberattack scenarios, or refuse correctly under clear adversarial prompts but not under indirect ones. Treating refusal as unidimensional collapses this structure, making the score uninterpretable and masking dangerous capability-specific vulnerabilities.

**Direction:** Using a safety benchmark with multiple harm categories and binary refusal-coded item responses (e.g., AIR-Bench or MLCommons AILuminate), fit a multidimensional IRT model in R (using mirt package, specifying a bifactor or correlated factors model). Test the unidimensionality assumption using: (1) local independence tests, (2) eigenvalue ratio (first:second eigenvalue should be > 5:1 for unidimensionality), (3) bifactor model fit compared to unidimensional model (RMSEA, CFI). If multidimensionality is confirmed, interpret the factors substantively: do they correspond to harm domains, adversarial technique types, or something else? Compute factor-specific ability scores per model and show that these reveal different vulnerability profiles invisible in the composite score.

**Why it matters:** A unidimensional safety score can be 'high' overall even when the model is vulnerable in a specific high-stakes domain (e.g., CBRN). Multidimensional IRT reveals these domain-specific vulnerabilities that composite scores mask.

**Relevant context:** AILuminate covers 7 hazard categories; AIR-Bench covers 314. The mirt package supports multidimensional IRT with bifactor models. Multidimensional IRT is standard in educational and clinical measurement. Application to AI safety refusal behavior is novel. No prior paper has formally tested unidimensionality of safety evaluation item pools.
