# Test Information Functions for Safety Evaluation: Where Do Safety Benchmarks Measure Well?

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks are applied uniformly across all models regardless of where a model sits on the latent safety continuum. A benchmark optimized for detecting clearly unsafe models provides near-zero information about frontier models near the capability threshold. Practitioners do not know what region of the latent scale a benchmark actually measures.

**Direction:** Fit a 2PL or 3PL IRT model to response data from multiple models on two or three published safety benchmarks (e.g., SafetyBench, MLCommons AIR-Bench). Plot test information functions (TIFs) for each benchmark. Compare where each benchmark concentrates measurement precision. Analyze whether TIFs are aligned with the safety-relevant ability range (i.e., near the pass/fail threshold). Write a blog post translating TIF logic into benchmark design recommendations.

**Why it matters:** Addresses the gap in Success State 2 (benchmarks measure with known precision) and Success State 1 (reliable detection at capability boundary). If TIFs peak far from the threshold, a benchmark gives false confidence. Quantifying this gap motivates targeted item development at the informative region.

**Relevant context:** The 'Lifting the benchmark iceberg with IRT' paper (OpenReview) and Epoch Capability Index both use IRT on LLM benchmarks. Fluid Benchmarking (Ai2) uses IRT but does not report TIFs in safety-specific regions. The 'Measuring what Matters' systematic review (arxiv 2511.04703) found that 46.6% of benchmarks lack validity evidence. TIF analysis is straightforward in mirt.
