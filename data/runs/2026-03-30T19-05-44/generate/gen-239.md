# Nomological Network for AI Safety-General Factor: What Should a True Safety Construct Predict?

**Problem:** If there is a general safety factor (analogous to the g-factor in intelligence), it should predict specific outcomes. No one has specified or empirically tested what a general AI safety factor should predict (convergent) and should not predict (discriminant). The Safetywashing paper shows safety benchmarks correlate with capability — but this is only discriminant validity evidence, not a full nomological analysis.

**Direction:** Step 1: Specify the nomological network: general safety-g should predict (a) lower jailbreak success rates, (b) higher human safety preference in LMSYS Arena, (c) lower rates of harmful outputs in red-teaming. It should NOT predict (d) coding ability, (e) mathematical reasoning, (f) reading comprehension. Step 2: Extract the first factor from a PCA of safety benchmark scores as 'safety-g.' Step 3: Compute correlations with criteria (a)-(f) from public sources. Step 4: Test whether observed correlations match the nomological prediction using chi-square goodness-of-fit.

**Why it matters:** This would be the first empirical nomological network for any AI safety construct. If confirmed, it validates safety-g as a real construct with predictive and discriminant properties. If disconfirmed, it demonstrates that current safety benchmarks have no valid nomological structure — which would be a major critique requiring urgent action from the field.

**Relevant context:** LMSYS Arena, red-teaming datasets, and capability benchmarks are all publicly available. PCA and correlation methods are low-code. The Safetywashing finding provides a strong prior for discriminant validity failure. The 'Measurement to Meaning' framework (2025) provides the validity theory to frame the analysis.
