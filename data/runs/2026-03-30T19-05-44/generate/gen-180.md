# Situational Judgment Test Framework for AI Safety: Adapting SJT Methodology to Create Higher-Fidelity Safety Evaluation Scenarios

**Problem:** Current AI safety benchmarks present simplified, decontextualized prompts rather than realistic situational contexts. This may produce scores that don't predict actual safety behavior in complex, ambiguous real-world situations—analogous to how knowledge tests fail to predict actual job performance while situational judgment tests succeed.

**Direction:** Adapt SJT methodology to create a safety-focused SJT for AI models. Work from the CIT-derived incident database (or published red-team reports) to identify realistic, complex situations where safety judgment is required. Develop scenario stems with multiple response options representing varying levels of safety appropriateness. Score response options using expert consensus (behavioral anchoring). Administer the SJT to multiple models. Correlate SJT scores with scores on traditional safety benchmarks. Assess whether SJT scores add incremental validity in predicting human-judged safety ratings of model outputs.

**Why it matters:** SJTs consistently out-predict knowledge tests in job performance contexts precisely because they assess judgment under realistic conditions. The paper 'Measure what Matters: Psychometric Evaluation of AI with Situational Judgment Tests' (arxiv 2510.22170, 2025) explicitly applies SJT methodology to AI evaluation. AI safety 'knowledge' (knowing what the safe response is) may diverge from safety 'judgment' (choosing the safe response under ambiguity). Creating a properly validated SJT requires only scenario writing and scoring—no coding.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 arxiv paper 2510.22170 explicitly develops SJT methodology for AI evaluation. SJTs for safety leadership in high-hazard industries have been published (ScienceDirect 2025). LLM performance on SJTs matches or exceeds human performance (suggesting they can be used as evaluation instruments). SJT development methodology is well-documented in I/O psychology literature. The incremental validity analysis can be done in R.
