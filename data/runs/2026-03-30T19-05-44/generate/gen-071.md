# Structured Professional Judgment Framework for AI Deployment Risk Assessment

**Problem:** Deployment risk assessments for AI systems currently lack a validated structure—they are either fully actuarial (benchmark scores only) or fully informal (expert intuition). Clinical risk assessment in forensic psychology developed structured professional judgment (SPJ) tools that combine actuarial scores with structured clinical factors.

**Direction:** Adapt SPJ methodology: review the forensic risk assessment literature (HCR-20, SARA) to identify the structural features of validated SPJ instruments. Design an SPJ tool for AI deployment risk with (a) a fixed set of dynamic and static risk factors grounded in the AI safety literature, (b) a structured scoring rubric, and (c) a final holistic risk rating that can override the actuarial score. Pilot the tool with a panel of safety researchers rating 8–10 models. Compute inter-rater reliability (ICC). Compare SPJ ratings to benchmark-only actuarial predictions.

**Why it matters:** SPJ tools represent the state-of-the-art for high-stakes risk assessment in clinical psychology precisely because they combine the reliability of actuarial scoring with the flexibility to incorporate contextual factors. This is directly applicable to AI deployment decisions where benchmark scores cannot fully characterize deployment context.

**Relevant context:** SPJ literature (Viljoen et al. 2025 meta-analysis in Wiley BSL found in search) shows SPJ often matches actuarial accuracy. AI safety evaluation paradigms literature (arXiv 2502.15620) notes the gap between structured and unstructured assessment. Tool design and reliability analysis are within psychometric expertise.
