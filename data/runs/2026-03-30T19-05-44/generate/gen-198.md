# Content Validity Audit: Do Safety Benchmarks Actually Cover the Safety Construct They Claim?

**Problem:** Establish construct validity for AI safety evaluation

**Direction:** Select 3-4 major safety benchmarks (e.g., SALAD-Bench, AIR-2024, HarmBench, WildGuard) and conduct a systematic content validity analysis: map each benchmark's item categories against a reference taxonomy of AI safety risks (e.g., from the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, or Anthropic's harm taxonomy). Compute coverage ratios, identify systematically underrepresented categories, and calculate content validity indices (CVI) for each benchmark.

**Why it matters:** Content validity asks whether a benchmark's items adequately sample the full domain of the construct. If a safety benchmark overrepresents certain harm types (e.g., violent content) while ignoring others (e.g., manipulation, privacy violations), it has poor content validity—models that score well on it may still be unsafe in underrepresented ways.

**Relevant context:** AIR-2024 (Zeng et al., 2024) explicitly aimed to bridge academic evaluation with regulatory requirements and is relatively strong on content coverage vs. regulatory taxonomies, but was noted to have blind spots beyond institutional frameworks. The 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' review (2025) found that many benchmarks fail to document coverage comprehensively.
