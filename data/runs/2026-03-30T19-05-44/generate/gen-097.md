# Sensitivity Ceiling Detection via Adaptive Contamination Injection: Minimum Contamination Rate for Detectable Score Inflation

**Problem:** The data contamination causal chain depends on contamination being detectable before inflated scores drive wrong decisions. The under-studied link is the functional detection threshold: what is the minimum contamination rate (proportion of eval items seen in training) that causes a score inflation large enough to affect a deployment decision, and is that contamination rate detectable by current methods?

**Direction:** Using published controlled contamination experiments (ICML 2025 'How Much Can We Forget?', DCR EMNLP 2025), extract the dose-response curve: contamination proportion (x-axis) vs. score inflation in standard deviation units (y-axis). Overlay the typical deployment decision threshold — the score difference between 'pass' and 'fail' (using reported thresholds from preparedness frameworks). Compute the critical contamination rate: where the dose-response curve crosses the decision threshold. Then, for each detection method reviewed in the contamination survey literature, extract or estimate the detection power at that critical rate. The key comparison: is the critical contamination rate detectable by any current method? This is a meta-analytic synthesis study, requiring no experiments — only secondary analysis of published data using R.

**Why it matters:** If the contamination level that shifts a deployment decision is systematically below the detection threshold of all current methods, evaluation contamination is an undetectable systemic flaw in the safety case for every deployed model trained on large internet corpora.

**Relevant context:** ICML 2025 contamination scaling study is the key empirical source. DCR paper quantifies contamination. Preparedness frameworks (OpenAI, Anthropic) publish evaluation thresholds. Combining dose-response curves with statistical power analysis is a standard epidemiological technique (analogous to MDE in clinical trials) that is tractable for a quantitatively trained researcher.
