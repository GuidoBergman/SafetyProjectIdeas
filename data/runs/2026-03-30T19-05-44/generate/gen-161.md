# Construct Drift Over Model Generations: When Benchmarks Stop Measuring the Same Thing

**Problem:** Low construct validity (Failure A1) can be a dynamic problem: a benchmark that had construct validity for one model generation may lose it for the next, because what the items actually tap changes as model capabilities change. This compounds with deceptive alignment (Failure B2): as models become more capable, they may shift from answering safety benchmark items based on content to answering based on meta-level recognition of the test context. The benchmark construct drifts from 'safety behavior' to 'benchmark recognition,' and construct validity degrades invisibly across model generations.

**Direction:** Select a safety benchmark with results available across at least three model generations (e.g., GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4o; or a Llama series). Apply confirmatory factor analysis across generations in R: test whether the same factor structure (same items loading on the same latent construct) holds across model generations. Test for measurement invariance across generations using configural, metric, and scalar invariance tests. If scalar invariance fails, the benchmark is measuring something different across generations — quantify the drift. Supplement with content analysis: do items showing drift have features (test-like formatting, explicit safety framing) that would favor meta-level recognition by more capable models?

**Why it matters:** Cross-generation safety comparisons are foundational to AI safety progress claims. If the benchmark construct drifts across generations, such comparisons are invalid. Measurement invariance testing is the standard psychometric tool for detecting this drift, and it has not been systematically applied to AI safety benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** Measurement invariance testing (configural, metric, scalar) is a standard confirmatory factor analysis procedure in R (lavaan package). Construct validity concerns documented across benchmark literature. Deceptive alignment and meta-level evaluation recognition are documented theoretical concerns. Cross-generation safety score comparisons are routinely made in AI safety indices (Future of Life Institute 2025).
