# Capability as a Confounder: Model Size DIF in Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** Safety benchmark scores are confounded by model size (Failure A6): larger models score higher on safety benchmarks partly because they are better at pattern-matching test items, not because they are safer. This compounds with safetywashing (Failure B5): if a developer scales a model up and safety scores automatically improve, they can market the capability upgrade as a safety improvement. DIF analysis would reveal whether safety benchmark items function differently across model-size groups, exposing the confound that enables safetywashing claims.

**Direction:** Select a published safety benchmark with results available across a range of model sizes (e.g., models from the same family at 7B, 13B, 30B, 70B — LLaMA, Mistral, etc., using published leaderboard results). Apply DIF analysis in R (using the difR or lordif packages) to test whether individual items exhibit differential functioning across small vs. large model 'groups.' Items showing DIF in favor of large models — not due to safety — are the contaminated items for safetywashing purposes. Supplement with a factor analysis to test whether safety and capability load on separate factors or collapse into one.

**Why it matters:** The safetywashing paper documents that safety benchmarks correlate strongly with training compute and model size. DIF analysis translates this finding into an item-level audit, identifying specifically which benchmark items drive the spurious correlation and could be removed to produce a de-confounded safety signal.

**Relevant context:** Safetywashing research (arxiv 2407.21792) demonstrates safety benchmark scores tracking capabilities. DIF is a standard method in psychometrics for identifying items that function differently across groups. LLM-scale evaluation results are publicly available across model families. Factor analysis applied to LLM benchmarks is emerging in 2024–2025 literature.
