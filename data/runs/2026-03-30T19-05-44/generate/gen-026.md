# Predictive Validity of Safety Benchmarks: Do Scores Predict Real-World Harm Incidents?

**Problem:** Predictive validity asks whether benchmark scores predict real-world outcomes they theoretically should predict. For safety benchmarks, this would mean: do models scoring higher on safety benchmarks produce fewer harm incidents in deployment? This is the most direct form of validity evidence for safety benchmarks and is entirely absent from the literature.

**Direction:** Compile a dataset linking model safety benchmark scores (from Open LLM Leaderboard, HELM) to publicly documented AI harm incidents (e.g., AIAAIC incident database, AI Incident Database). For models with documented incidents, compare their safety benchmark scores against models without incidents. Compute point-biserial correlations between safety scores and incident occurrence. Use logistic regression (R glm) to predict incident probability from safety score while controlling for model capability (MMLU score) and deployment scale.

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmark scores do not predict real-world harm incidents even weakly, the benchmarks have no predictive validity and their use in deployment decisions is empirically unjustified. Even a weak positive finding (safety scores predict incidents better than chance) would be the first published predictive validity evidence for any AI safety benchmark—a major contribution to the field.

**Relevant context:** Predictive validity concept; AI Incident Database (AIAAIC) publicly available; HELM and Open LLM Leaderboard model scores; consequential validity entirely unstudied (MTAI framework); arxiv:2502.06559 notes disconnect between benchmark and real-world performance.
