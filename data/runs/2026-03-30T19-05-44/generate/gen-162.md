# The G-Theory Audit of Safety Evaluations: How Much of the Score Is the Evaluator, Not the Model?

**Problem:** No validity evidence (Failure A7) in safety benchmarks frequently includes failure to account for evaluator/rater variance. When safety is scored by LLM judges (increasingly common), a G-theory facet analysis would reveal whether score variance is attributable to the model being evaluated or to the evaluator model's idiosyncrasies. This compounds with safetywashing (Failure B5): if a developer uses a specific LLM judge that is systematically lenient, safety scores are inflated by rater bias, not by genuine safety improvements — and this inflation is structurally undetectable without a multi-facet measurement design.

**Direction:** Identify a safety evaluation setup that uses LLM-as-judge scoring (e.g., MT-Bench safety criteria, or any benchmark using GPT-4 as judge). Administer a set of 60–100 safety-relevant prompts to 3–4 models being evaluated. Score outputs using 3–4 different LLM judges (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini, Llama-3-70B-Instruct). Apply G-theory in R with facets: model-under-evaluation × judge × item. Estimate variance components: how much of total score variance is attributable to the judge vs. the model? Compute the G-coefficient for a single-judge design vs. a multi-judge design. Produce D-study recommendations for adequate reliability in LLM-judge safety evaluation.

**Why it matters:** LLM-as-judge is rapidly becoming the default safety evaluation methodology. If judge variance is large, single-judge safety evaluations are unreliable regardless of which judge is used. G-theory provides a principled framework for quantifying this unreliability and prescribing multi-judge designs. This is directly applicable to any lab using LLM judges for safety compliance.

**Relevant context:** LLM-as-judge evaluation methodology widespread and growing. G-theory applied to LLM-as-judge documented in 2025 ScienceDirect paper on writing assessment. Safetywashing concern that safety scores can be gamed by evaluation design choice. G-theory in R (gtheory package) is a direct application of participant competence.
