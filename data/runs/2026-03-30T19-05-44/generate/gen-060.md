# Construct Definition via Job Analysis Methods for AI Safety Evaluations

**Problem:** AI safety benchmarks are built bottom-up (collect scary prompts, test refusal rates) rather than top-down from a principled construct definition. As a result, benchmarks cover constructs inconsistently and may systematically miss important safety-relevant behaviors.

**Direction:** Import the job analysis methodology from organizational psychology: conduct structured interviews and surveys with AI safety researchers to identify critical safety-relevant behaviors (analogous to 'critical incidents' in job analysis). Use the task inventory and critical incident technique to map behaviors to constructs. Build a taxonomy of AI safety constructs from this analysis. Then audit existing benchmarks against this taxonomy to identify gaps and redundancies. Output: a construct map and coverage matrix for major safety benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Job analysis provides the construct backbone for every credentialing exam and performance assessment in I/O psychology. Applying the same discipline to AI safety benchmarks would ground them in a defensible theory of what safe AI behavior actually requires—enabling principled benchmark construction and gap analysis.

**Relevant context:** The arXiv paper 'Paradigms of AI Evaluation' (2502.15620) and 'Conceptual Framework for AI Capability Evaluations' (2506.18213) note the construct-orientation gap in AI evaluation. Job analysis methods (task inventory, critical incidents) require expert interviews and structured coding, not ML. Fits the 30-hour scope as a framework/audit project.
