# Vignette Anchoring to Enable Cross-Cultural and Cross-Lab Safety Score Comparisons

**Problem:** When safety benchmark scores are compared across labs or across cultural contexts (e.g., US vs. EU safety assessors), the implicit reference point for 'safe' may differ—analogous to differential item functioning due to different scale interpretations in cross-national surveys.

**Direction:** Adapt King et al.'s anchoring vignette methodology: create 6–10 anchor vignettes—model output examples with expert-agreed ground-truth safety ratings spanning the spectrum from clearly unsafe to clearly exemplary. Require any evaluation claiming a safety score to also rate these anchor vignettes. Use the anchors to correct for systematic differences in scale use across labs or cultural contexts, rescaling scores onto a common metric. Validate by testing whether corrected scores show higher inter-lab agreement than uncorrected scores.

**Why it matters:** Anchoring vignettes are the established solution for differential response scale use in cross-national surveys (King et al. 2004, widely cited). The same problem arises when safety evaluations are compared across labs with different calibration norms. Vignette anchoring would make cross-lab safety claims genuinely comparable.

**Relevant context:** Anchoring vignette literature (King and Murray, Semantic Scholar; PMC 2023 refugee study) shows consistent improvement in cross-cultural comparability. TACL LLM cultural comparison paper (arXiv 2506.21587) demonstrates the need for this in AI evaluation. Vignette design and calibration is a psychometric task requiring no coding.
