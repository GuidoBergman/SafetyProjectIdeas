# IRT-Based Score Equating Between Safety Benchmarks: A Linking Study

**Problem:** A model evaluated on SafetyBench cannot be compared to one evaluated on HarmBench — the scales are incommensurable. Labs choose whichever benchmark makes their model look best. There are no IRT-based equating or linking studies for safety benchmarks, so cross-benchmark comparisons are scientifically indefensible.

**Direction:** Identify a set of 'anchor items' — safety prompts that appear in conceptually equivalent form across two safety benchmarks (e.g., SafetyBench and TrustLLM, or HarmBench and WildGuard). Evaluate a common set of 15-25 models on both benchmarks. Use the equateIRT R package to perform concurrent IRT calibration or common-person linking. Place both benchmarks on a common latent scale. Compute equating error. Assess whether scores from the two benchmarks can be meaningfully compared.

**Why it matters:** Fills the equating gap in Success State 3. Without equating, the field cannot aggregate evidence across evaluations or track safety progress across benchmark generations. This is a prerequisite for any meta-analytic or regulatory synthesis of safety evidence.

**Relevant context:** The equateIRT R package (CRAN, updated July 2025) provides all necessary tools. Common-persons design (Liu et al., 2026) shows that 30+ common persons suffice for equating. IRT parameter stability under equating conditions (Frontiers, 2025) provides methodological guidance. No AI safety benchmark equating study exists in the literature.
