# Factor Analysis to Disentangle 'Safety' from 'General Capability' in Frontier Model Benchmarks

**Problem:** The model-size confound causal chain (Risk 8) means that factor analysis of benchmark performance may extract 'g' (general capability) as the dominant factor, with safety-specific variance subsumed. The under-studied link is whether safety benchmarks load on a distinct factor after partialing out general capability — and if so, what that factor represents substantively.

**Direction:** Collect a model-by-benchmark score matrix from public leaderboards, including ~8-12 capability benchmarks and ~6-8 safety benchmarks for 30+ models. Conduct exploratory factor analysis (EFA) in R, determining factor number by parallel analysis and scree plot inspection. Interpret factor loadings: do safety benchmarks cluster on a separate factor, or do they load primarily on the general capability factor? If a distinct safety factor emerges, examine which specific safety benchmarks define it and what construct it represents (refusal behavior, ethical restraint, honesty?). Conduct confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) testing a two-factor model (capability + safety) vs. a one-factor model (pure g). Report fit indices (CFI, RMSEA, SRMR) and practical significance of factor separation.

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmarks load entirely onto the general capability factor, they add no distinct information about safety. This analysis directly tests whether current safety evaluation provides signal beyond 'bigger model = safer model', which is the core safetywashing hypothesis.

**Relevant context:** Ren et al. (NeurIPS 2024) correlated safety and capability benchmarks, showing high overlap. The 'Safetywashing' paper is directly relevant. Factor analysis of benchmark performance is an established methodology. The psych and lavaan R packages support both EFA and CFA without heavy coding. This extends the safetywashing analysis to a formal latent structure test.
