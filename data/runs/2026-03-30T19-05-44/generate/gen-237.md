# Theory-Driven vs. Data-Driven Safety Evaluation: CFA vs. EFA Head-to-Head Comparison

**Problem:** The AI evaluation literature has independently developed theory-driven rubric approaches (e.g., Constitutional AI principles, the HHH framework, ADELE-style rubrics) and data-driven factor analysis approaches. These have never been compared head-to-head on the same safety data. Do theory-driven CFA models fit better than EFA-derived structures? Which approach recovers more reliable, valid safety scores?

**Direction:** Prespecify a theory-driven CFA model based on the Constitutional AI framework (e.g., three correlated factors: Honest, Harmless, Helpful). Separately run EFA on the same safety item response matrix. Compare: (1) model fit (RMSEA, CFI) of CFA vs. EFA-respecified CFA, (2) reliability of scores under each solution, (3) convergent validity with external criteria. This uses the lavaan and psych R packages. Report which approach better captures the structure of safety and where they conflict.

**Why it matters:** This resolves a foundational methodological debate: should AI safety evaluation be structured by theory (what safety researchers believe matters) or by data (what safety benchmark items actually measure empirically)? Disagreement between the two would identify specific gaps where safety theory is not reflected in current benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** EFA/CFA comparison is standard psychometric methodology. The Constitutional AI framework provides the theory-driven structure. The 'Measurement to Meaning' paper (2025) calls for validity-centered AI evaluation. The gap between theory-driven and data-driven approaches in AI evaluation is explicitly identified in the landscape review.
