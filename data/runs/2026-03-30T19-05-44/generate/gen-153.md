# When Validity Evidence is Absent and the Regulator Doesn't Know: A Structural Analysis of AI Governance Risk

**Problem:** Benchmarks without validity evidence (Failure A7) are referenced in regulatory frameworks (Failure B6), creating a structural governance risk that is invisible to policymakers who lack psychometric training. Regulators assume that a named, published benchmark has been validated; the field of AI evaluation does not have a universal disclosure standard that would make validity evidence (or its absence) legible. The compound failure is not just that benchmarks are invalid — it is that the governance structure has no mechanism to detect or disclose this invalidity.

**Direction:** Conduct a document analysis of the top 10 AI governance and regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act standards, NIST AI RMF, UK DSIT guidance, US EO 14110 implementation guidance, Singapore MAS guidelines). Extract: (a) all named benchmarks, (b) what safety property each benchmark is claimed to measure, (c) what validity evidence is cited. Score each benchmark-claim pair on a validity evidence rubric (0 = none, 1 = content, 2 = criterion, 3 = construct, 4 = consequential). Compute the distribution of validity evidence levels across regulatory claims. Produce a structured recommendation for a benchmark disclosure standard modeled on psychometric validity reporting conventions.

**Why it matters:** AI governance is only as reliable as the measurement instruments it references. A systematic audit of what regulators are actually claiming — and whether those claims are evidenced — translates a technical measurement concern into a directly policy-actionable finding.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Watch 2025 documents regulatory reliance on benchmarks. 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' and validity audit methodology from psychometrics provide the framework. AERA/APA/NCME Standards for validity reporting are a mature template. 46.6% no-validity-evidence statistic from benchmark survey literature.
