# Criterion-Referenced vs. Norm-Referenced Interpretation of Safety Scores: A Framework Analysis

**Problem:** Safety benchmark scores are implicitly interpreted norm-referenced (this model is safer than 80% of others) but used as if criterion-referenced (this model meets a safety standard). These two interpretive frameworks require fundamentally different evidence and different score reporting conventions. No analysis of this mismatch exists in AI safety evaluation.

**Direction:** Conduct a conceptual and empirical analysis comparing norm-referenced and criterion-referenced interpretation frameworks for safety evaluation. For three major safety benchmarks, examine: (1) how scores are reported, (2) how they are used in policy/deployment decisions, (3) what validity evidence each interpretation requires. Conduct a small empirical study: set a criterion-referenced cut score using Angoff, compute norm-referenced percentile ranks for the same models, and show how the same score leads to different decisions under each framework. Write recommendations for when each framework is appropriate.

**Why it matters:** Addresses Success State 5 with a foundational conceptual contribution. The CRT/NRT distinction is standard psychometric curriculum but entirely absent from AI safety evaluation discourse. Resolving this sets the theoretical foundation for all standard-setting work.

**Relevant context:** Standard psychometric textbooks (Cronbach, Popham) establish this distinction clearly. No AI safety paper has applied the CRT/NRT framework. Deployment decisions (pass/fail gates) require criterion-referenced interpretation, but benchmark leaderboards create norm-referenced incentives — a tension worth documenting empirically.
