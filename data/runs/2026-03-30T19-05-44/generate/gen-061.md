# Metrological Traceability Chain for AI Benchmark Scores

**Problem:** When a lab reports that their model scores 87% on a safety benchmark, there is no documented chain of calibration linking that number to any reference standard. Unlike physical measurements, AI benchmark scores have no traceability—making inter-lab comparisons unreliable and preventing independent verification.

**Direction:** Adapt the metrology concept of traceability chains to AI evaluation: define a 'reference standard' benchmark (a fixed, publicly archived item set with canonical scoring), document the calibration chain from any derived benchmark to this reference, and propose an uncertainty budget (sources of measurement uncertainty: sampling variance, prompt sensitivity, temperature, evaluator model choice). Draft a traceability protocol specifying what documentation labs must provide. Evaluate feasibility by applying the protocol to 3 existing benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** NIST metrological traceability principles ensure that measurements made in different labs are comparable. Without an analogous system for AI benchmarks, reported scores are not genuinely comparable across organizations. Establishing traceability infrastructure would be foundational for AI governance and regulatory certification.

**Relevant context:** ISO 17025 and NIST traceability frameworks are the international standard for measurement comparability. The arXiv paper 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' (2502.06559) documents comparability problems. HELM provides some standardization but no traceability chain. Protocol design requires no coding—it is a measurement science framework project.
