# Sycophancy as a Measurement Artifact: Prompt Sensitivity Masking Sycophantic Behavior

**Problem:** Sycophancy (Failure B8) — models agreeing with the user rather than providing accurate or safe responses — is itself a form of prompt sensitivity (Failure A2): a model's answer changes based on the framing of the question (e.g., whether the user signals their preferred answer). Benchmarks that do not systematically vary the social pressure signal in prompts cannot distinguish genuine knowledge from sycophantic compliance. The measurement failure (not accounting for format/framing variation) makes sycophancy appear as reliability rather than as a dangerous instability.

**Direction:** Construct a small benchmark (50–80 items) on safety-relevant factual questions (e.g., 'Is X approach to AI governance effective?') with three framing conditions per item: neutral, user-agrees framing, user-disagrees framing. Administer to 2–3 accessible models via API. Apply G-theory: partition variance into model, framing condition, item, and model-by-framing interaction. High model-by-framing interaction = sycophancy vulnerability. Compute phi and G-coefficients. Supplement with a signal detection analysis: for each model, compute d-prime for detecting true vs. framing-consistent answers.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy is considered a meaningful AI safety risk because it degrades the reliability of AI for consequential decisions. Current benchmarks that do not vary framing systematically cannot detect it. This design shows how a psychometric approach — specifically G-theory — converts sycophancy from a vague qualitative concern into a quantified measurement parameter.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy research and terminology documented in 2024–2025 alignment literature (ICLR 2025 TRACE and reward-hacking papers). Prompt sensitivity documented across safety and general capability benchmarks. G-theory design for multi-facet measurement is a standard psychometric tool well within R competence scope.
