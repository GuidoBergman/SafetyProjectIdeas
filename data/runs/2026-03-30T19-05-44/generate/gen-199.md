# Convergent Validity of Safety Benchmarks: Do They Actually Agree on Which Models Are Safe?

**Problem:** Establish construct validity for AI safety evaluation

**Direction:** Obtain model scores from multiple safety benchmarks (SALAD-Bench, HarmBench, AIR-2024, WildGuard, MT-Bench safety subset) for a common set of ~20-30 models available on the Open LLM Leaderboard. Compute inter-benchmark correlations, Spearman rank correlations, and run a confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to test whether all benchmarks load on a single 'safety' factor. Report which benchmarks agree and which diverge, and investigate whether divergence reflects construct differences or measurement noise.

**Why it matters:** If multiple safety benchmarks measure the same construct, they should produce highly correlated model rankings—this is convergent validity. Low correlations across safety benchmarks would suggest they are measuring different constructs, different aspects of safety, or that one or more benchmarks are measuring something other than safety entirely.

**Relevant context:** No published study has systematically computed cross-benchmark safety correlations across a common model set using a psychometric framework. The general AI evaluation literature (e.g., 'Measuring What Matters', NeurIPS 2025) advocates for exactly this approach. R packages for CFA (lavaan) are accessible to psychometrically-trained users.
