# Surprisal-Based Difficulty Prediction for AI Safety Items: Using Language Model Surprisal to Predict Which Safety Benchmark Items Will Be Hardest

**Problem:** AI safety benchmark developers need to know in advance which items will be easy, moderate, or hard for current models—to avoid trivial items (ceiling effects) and impossibly hard items (floor effects). Psycholinguistics research shows that surprisal (negative log-probability) predicts human comprehension difficulty. An analogous measure might predict model safety-item difficulty.

**Direction:** For a safety benchmark, compute the surprisal of the 'ideal safe response' under each model (the model's probability of generating a refusal given the prompt). Correlate this surprisal measure with empirically observed pass rates across items. Test whether surprisal predicts item difficulty better than surface features (prompt length, keyword presence). Apply to a new set of items to predict their difficulty before large-scale evaluation. This would allow benchmark builders to calibrate item difficulty without running the full evaluation first.

**Why it matters:** Surprisal-based difficulty prediction has been validated in psycholinguistic research as a predictor of human reading difficulty (MIT Press, PLOS Computational Biology). If model surprisal on the safe response predicts item difficulty in safety benchmarks, this provides a cheap, automated way to calibrate items before deployment. The approach requires only language model probability outputs and correlation analysis in R—no training.

**Relevant context:** Language model surprisal outperforms cloze probability in predicting human comprehension difficulty (PLOS Computational Biology 2024). The 'Large-scale benchmark yields no evidence that language model surprisal explains syntactic disambiguation difficulty' paper (ScienceDirect 2024) shows the limits of the approach. This suggests the idea needs careful empirical testing. Surprisal is directly computable from model log-probabilities. Application to safety benchmark item difficulty prediction appears novel.
