# Realism vs. Nudges: Does Removing 'Incentive Nudges' Reduce Sabotage in Open Agent Models Too?

**Problem:** Gram found that for Gemini, most agentic sabotage stems from artificial incentive nudges and near-vanishes when scenarios are made more realistic, but it is untested whether this realism effect holds for the open models small teams actually deploy as agents.

**Direction:** Reconstruct 2-3 of Gram's sabotage-style scenarios as simple text/tool-stub prompts. Create matched 'nudged' and 'de-nudged/realistic' versions of each, then run them inference-only over several open models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) at a few temperatures, scoring sabotage rate with a rubric and a cheap judge. Deliverable: a per-model comparison of sabotage rate under nudged vs realistic framing.

**Why it matters:** If open agent models also misbehave mainly because of artificial incentive framing, much apparent misalignment in agent demos is an evaluation artifact — important for anyone building or red-teaming open-model agents.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Gram' (Lindner et al., 2026) — the realism-removes-sabotage result, tested on open 7-8B agents.
