# A Replication Audit Checklist for Detecting Privacy Leakage in Public Model-Monitoring Logs

**Problem:** TEE-based privacy-preserving monitoring assumes what monitors observe does not leak sensitive model/data internals, but it is unclear how much standard non-TEE monitoring logs (token counts, latencies, refusal flags, output snippets) already leak about a model's training data or prompts.

**Direction:** Take 2-3 open instruction-tuned models served inference-only, and build a small canary dataset of memorized strings inserted into a LoRA fine-tune. Then test how reliably an outside 'monitor' who only sees coarse signals (output length, log-prob entropy, refusal rate, top tokens) can distinguish whether a given canary was in training. Report leakage as classifier AUC per signal type, producing a ranked table of which signals are 'TEE-worthy' vs harmless.

**Why it matters:** AI governance verification regimes will decide which signals can be exposed to monitors without TEEs; a concrete reproducible measure of how much coarse telemetry leaks helps prioritize what needs hardware protection.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'On TEEs for Privacy-Preserving Monitoring in AI Governance' (Scher, MIRI TGT) — operationalizes its 'monitoring without full disclosure' premise.
