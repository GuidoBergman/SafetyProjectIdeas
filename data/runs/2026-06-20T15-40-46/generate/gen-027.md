# Do NLA Verbalizers Hallucinate 'Safety-Test Awareness'? A Cheap Faithfulness Probe

**Problem:** Natural Language Autoencoders surface text like 'awareness of being safety-tested' from activations, but the paper notes explanations can hallucinate and single claims are hard to verify, leaving verbalizer faithfulness unquantified.

**Direction:** Using the released NLA code on a small supported open model, run the verbalizer on matched prompt pairs that are behaviorally identical but differ in whether they are framed as a 'safety test', plus control pairs. Check whether verbalized 'being-tested' text tracks actual framing (true positives) or appears spuriously on controls (hallucinations), giving a contrastive faithfulness rate for one specific NLA claim.

**Why it matters:** NLAs are proposed as auditing aids, but tools that hallucinate awareness can mislead reviewers into false confidence or false alarms. A targeted contrastive test of one high-stakes claim is tractable.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Natural Language Autoencoders' (Fraser-Taliente et al., 2026) — the verbalized 'awareness of being safety-tested' that can hallucinate.
