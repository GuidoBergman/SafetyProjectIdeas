# Where Do Reviewers Miss the Most? Mapping Human Error Detection by Mistake Type

**Problem:** 'Automated alignment is harder than you think' claims agent-generated mistakes concentrate among errors human reviewers are least likely to detect, but this is hard to act on without a concrete map of which error types evade non-expert review.

**Direction:** Build a small benchmark of model-generated solutions to public reasoning/coding tasks, each with a single injected error of a controlled type (subtle arithmetic slip, wrong-but-plausible cited fact, off-by-one in code, unjustified assumption, omitted edge case). Use a weaker open model as a stand-in 'reviewer' (optionally a small human-rater pass) to measure detection rate per error type. Output a ranked detectability table.

**Why it matters:** Knowing which error types reviewers systematically miss lets us target backup verification and red-teaming there, addressing the worry that automated alignment work produces compelling-but-misleading assessments.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Automated alignment is harder than you think' (UK AISI) — its claim that agent errors concentrate where reviewers least detect them.
