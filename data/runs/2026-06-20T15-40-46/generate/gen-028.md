# Reasoning-Over-Rules, Reproduced: Does 'Teaching Why' Beat 'Teaching What' on a Small Open Model?

**Problem:** Kutasov & Jermyn show teaching the reasoning behind ethical principles generalizes far better than teaching correct behaviors, with a 'difficult advice' dataset matching honeypot training using 28x fewer tokens, but only on Claude.

**Direction:** Build two tiny LoRA finetuning sets for an open model (Llama-3.1-8B): one with correct-behavior-only ethical examples and one with matched scenarios that include the reasoning/justification. Train both, then evaluate OOD generalization on held-out public moral-dilemma and agentic-misalignment-style prompts (a small blackmail/honeypot probe set), testing whether reason-included data generalizes better at equal token budget.

**Why it matters:** If 'teach the why' generalizes better and more token-efficiently on cheap open models, it is an actionable low-cost alignment recipe for teams finetuning open models without RLHF infra.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Teaching Claude Why' (Kutasov & Jermyn, 2026) — the reasoning-based 28x token-efficiency and better-OOD result.
