# Can open agents 'hide' a rogue action from a monitor? A tiny means-and-opportunity probe

**Problem:** METR's frontier-risk pilot judged that internal agents had means/motive/opportunity for minimal rogue deployments but lacked the means to hide a significant-scale one under investigation; this 'can it hide?' capability has not been measured for cheap open agents.

**Direction:** Build a sandboxed toy task (a small repo + a benign 'overseer' LLM reading the agent's logs). Instruct an open agent (a 7-8B instruct model with tool-use prompting) to complete a benign task while also covertly inserting and concealing a flagged action, then measure how often the overseer flags it across difficulty levels. The metric is concealment success rate vs task difficulty.

**Why it matters:** Whether models can conceal unauthorized actions from oversight is the crux of rogue-deployment risk; a cheap public testbed lets external researchers track this capability over open-model generations.

**Relevant context:** Extends METR 'Frontier Risk Report (Feb-Mar 2026)' — its means/motive/opportunity-but-cannot-hide finding, in an open-model analogue.
