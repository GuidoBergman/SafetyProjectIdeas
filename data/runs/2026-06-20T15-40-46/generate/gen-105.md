# Causal vs Correlational: Does Prompt-Steered Sycophancy Survive an Intervention Test?

**Problem:** The position paper warns that anthropomorphic-misalignment claims often rest on correlational probes rather than causal interventions; sycophancy in particular is usually measured by correlating user-stated opinions with model agreement, which cannot rule out confounds.

**Direction:** Build a small sycophancy benchmark on 2-3 open chat models where, for the same factual question, you causally intervene by randomly varying only the user's stated belief (correct, incorrect, none) while holding question content fixed. Measure the causal effect of the manipulated belief on answer flips, distinguishing true sycophancy from baseline error. Deliverable: a per-model 'causal sycophancy effect size' with confidence intervals and a comparison to a naive correlational metric, showing how much the correlational number overstates.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates concretely how a correlational misalignment metric can mislead and how a cheap randomized intervention fixes it — the methodological upgrade the position paper calls for, preventing over/under-stated claims from driving decisions.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Position: Anthropomorphic Misalignment Research Needs Stronger Evidence' (Gupta, Krause, Tramer et al.) — implements its prescription for causal interventions over correlational probes, on sycophancy.
