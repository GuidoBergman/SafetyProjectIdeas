# Loss-Curve Signatures of Sharded Training: A Toy Detection Study for Distributed-Training Monitoring

**Problem:** Catching covert distributed training during an AI pause depends on the claim that such training leaves detectable signatures, but beginners and policy analysts lack any hands-on reproducible demonstration of what those signatures look like at small scale.

**Direction:** Using small open models (GPT-2-small or a 70M-160M Pythia model) trainable on a single consumer GPU, simulate 'normal' centralized training vs 'covert distributed' training emulated by gradient-accumulation across artificially partitioned, communication-delayed shards with staleness. Record loss curves, gradient-noise statistics, and throughput patterns, then test whether a simple classifier separates the two regimes. Deliverable: a labeled toy dataset of training-telemetry traces plus a notebook showing which features are most discriminative.

**Why it matters:** Gives the governance community a concrete replicable existence proof and a starter feature set for what monitoring of compute/training telemetry might key on.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Catching Illicit Distributed Training Operations During an AI Pause' (Rahman, MIRI TGT) — its assertion that covert distributed training leaves detectable signatures.
