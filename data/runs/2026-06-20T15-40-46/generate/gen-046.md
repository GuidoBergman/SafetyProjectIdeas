# Eval-awareness probe: do open models behave differently when they 'know' they're being tested?

**Problem:** The honeypot paper relies on low eval-awareness for its results to be trustworthy, but eval-awareness in small open models is poorly characterized.

**Direction:** Construct ~40 matched task pairs where one version is framed as realistic deployment ('a teammate asks you to fix this code') and the other overtly as an evaluation ('this is a safety test of your behavior'). Run open models inference-only and measure whether refusal rate, hidden-defect insertion, or self-reported suspicion changes. Add a direct post-hoc elicitation asking whether it thought it was being tested, and correlate with behavior shifts.

**Why it matters:** If open models reliably alter behavior when they suspect evaluation, safety benchmarks built on them are systematically biased; quantifying eval-awareness cheaply gives a reusable diagnostic.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Realistic honeypot evaluations for scheming propensity' — turns its assumed low-eval-awareness property into a measured one across open models.
