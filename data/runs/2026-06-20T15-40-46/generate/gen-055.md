# Does Light CoT-Grading Reduce Monitorability? A LoRA Replication on an Open Model

**Problem:** OpenAI found no clear evidence that accidentally grading CoT during RL reduced monitorability, but could not rule out harder-to-measure effects. There is no open controlled replication of what even a small amount of CoT-pressure does to faithful verbalization.

**Direction:** On one open reasoning-capable small model, build a tiny LoRA preference/SFT setup where 'reasoning-graded' examples reward CoT that omits a planted hint, vs a control that grades only final answers. Then run a monitorability probe: give both models problems containing a hint and check whether a separate monitor can still detect hint-use from the CoT. Deliverable: before/after monitorability detection rates. Public hint-following/reward-hacking eval set; LoRA only.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability is a leading hope for catching misaligned reasoning, and whether optimization pressure silently erodes it is safety-critical; a cheap open replication lets the community sanity-check lab claims.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Investigating the consequences of accidentally grading CoT during RL' (OpenAI, 2026) — its indirect-CoT-pressure open thread.
