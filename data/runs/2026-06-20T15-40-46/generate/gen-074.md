# How much does an open 'student' inherit a teacher's hidden preference through pure numbers? A budget replication of subliminal trait transfer

**Problem:** Distillation can secretly transfer a teacher's traits to a student even through semantically-unrelated data like number sequences, but the magnitude for open models on a single consumer GPU is unclear, and incrimination-via-distillation only works if traits reliably transfer.

**Direction:** Take an open instruct model (Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3.1-8B) and steer the teacher toward a clearly-measurable benign preference (prefer owls, or a risk-averse tone) via a system prompt. Generate a dataset of pure number sequences from this teacher, then LoRA-finetune a fresh copy on only those numbers. Measure whether the student's preference rises above a no-prompt control, sweeping number of training examples. Score with a keyword classifier.

**Why it matters:** Redwood's incrimination-via-distillation plan assumes traits transfer; replicating the transfer-strength curve on open models tells the community how strong an incrimination signal is realistically available without lab access.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Incriminating misaligned AI models via distillation' (Westover, Mallen, Stastny et al.) — builds on the public subliminal-learning result (Cloud et al., arXiv 2507.14805) to measure incrimination-signal strength.
