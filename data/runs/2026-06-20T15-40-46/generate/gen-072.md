# Retrying vs resampling on a toy control task: which catches injected misbehavior more cheaply?

**Problem:** Retrying vs resampling has been compared with strong frontier models, but there is no accessible small-scale replication letting outside researchers see when each strategy wins.

**Direction:** Create a toy control scenario: an untrusted open model that with some probability per call emits a detectable 'bad' action (a flagged token / subtle bug) on a public task. Implement retrying (re-prompt with a 'try again, be safe' instruction) vs resampling (draw a fresh sample and pick via a trusted monitor). Over many trials measure catch-rate of bad actions, false-positive rate on benign outputs, and number of model calls (cost) for each. Compare which dominates on safety-per-call and under what attack probability.

**Why it matters:** Whether to retry or resample is a real protocol-design choice; an open reproducible micro-experiment clarifies the cost/safety tradeoff and gives a cheap sandbox to test variants.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control' (Lucassen & Kaufman, Redwood, 2026) — replicates the comparison at toy scale.
