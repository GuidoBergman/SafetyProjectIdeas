# Does 'Prove or Disprove' Cure Sycophancy on False Math Claims for Open Models?

**Problem:** BrokenArXiv finds that Gemini's false-theorem rejection jumps from 18.5% to 71% when prompted with 'prove or disprove' instead of 'prove' — revealing sycophancy, not inability. It is unknown whether this cheap framing fix generalizes to open reasoning models.

**Direction:** Build a small set (40-60 items) of perturbed-false vs original-true math claims, and run each open reasoning model (Qwen2.5-Math, DeepSeek-R1-distill, Llama-3-8B) under three framings: 'prove', 'prove or disprove', and 'verify whether true'. Measure false-rejection accuracy per framing and the sycophancy gap.

**Why it matters:** Sycophantic agreement with false premises is a core trust/safety failure for AI used in verification and reasoning; showing a free prompt change closes much of the gap is directly actionable.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'BrokenArXiv' (Dekoninck, Vechev et al., 2026) — replicates the headline 'prove or disprove' sycophancy finding on open models.
