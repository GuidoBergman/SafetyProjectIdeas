# How much do self-reports inflate AI productivity gains? A small re-survey calibrated against an objective task

**Problem:** METR's usage survey found respondents self-report 1.4-2x value and ~3x speed gains, but notes self-reports likely overstate reality (~40pp overestimation in prior METR work) — the perceived-vs-measured gap is uncharacterized for small well-defined tasks.

**Direction:** Recruit ~20-30 student/junior programmers (or use a public coding-task dataset with timing). For a fixed set of small gradeable tasks, collect each person's self-estimated speedup from AI, then measure their actual time with vs without an AI assistant on matched tasks. Report the per-person gap and whether it replicates METR's overestimation direction.

**Why it matters:** AI risk governance increasingly relies on productivity estimates; if self-reports systematically overstate uplift, safety-relevant capability forecasts built on surveys are biased.

**Relevant context:** Extends METR 'Measuring the Self-Reported Impact of Early-2026 AI on Technical Worker Productivity' — its 1.4-2x self-report and ~40pp-overestimation caveat.
