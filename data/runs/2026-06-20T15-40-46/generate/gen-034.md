# Activation Magnitude vs. Steering Strength: A Cheap Test of the 'Activation Doesn't Predict Steerability' Claim

**Problem:** The Circuits update finds how strongly a feature activates does not predict how strongly steering it changes behavior, and downstream connectivity predicts better. This is preliminary and tested only inside Anthropic's stack.

**Direction:** Using a publicly released sparse autoencoder for a small open model (e.g. Gemma-2-2B SAEs on Neuronpedia) plus inference-only activation steering, sample a few dozen interpretable features. For each, measure mean activation magnitude on relevant prompts and the behavioral effect size of clamping/steering that feature. Test whether activation magnitude fails to predict steering effect, as the paper claims.

**Why it matters:** If even the weakest version replicates on open SAEs, it warns interpretability practitioners that activation-based feature importance is misleading for steering and intervention safety work.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Downstream Connections Predict Which Features Will Steer Model Behavior' (2026) — the claim activation/output characterization underpredicts steering.
