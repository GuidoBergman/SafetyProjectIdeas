# How robust is no-CoT elicitation? Probing whether 'answer only' really suppresses reasoning

**Problem:** No-CoT horizon estimates assume you can actually prevent a model from reasoning, but models may smuggle reasoning into the answer or be re-elicited into latent reasoning, biasing horizon estimates.

**Direction:** On an open model and a multi-step task set (GSM8K / BBH subset), compare several no-CoT enforcement methods: simple 'answer only' instruction, format/grammar-constrained decoding to a single token, and stop-sequence truncation. Measure how much each reduces accuracy and whether 'leaked' reasoning (longer answers, intermediate numbers) correlates with retained accuracy. Quantify how much horizon estimates shift by suppression method.

**Why it matters:** Control claims built on no-CoT horizons are only as trustworthy as the elicitation; showing estimates are sensitive to suppression method warns evaluators against over-trusting a single number.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Estimating No-CoT Task-Completion Time Horizons' — its open thread on robustness of no-CoT elicitation.
