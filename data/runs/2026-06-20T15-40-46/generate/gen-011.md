# One Bit Is Enough? Replicating Boundary-Point Jailbreaking Against an Open-Source Safety Classifier

**Problem:** Boundary-point attacks defeat strong deployed classifiers using only a single bit of feedback per query, but the published work targets closed frontier systems. It is unclear how cheaply the same one-bit curriculum attack works against an openly hostable safety classifier.

**Direction:** Pick a public open-weight guard classifier (e.g. Llama Guard or a HuggingFace jailbreak classifier) as the black-box oracle. Implement a minimal boundary-point loop: take a target harmful string, build a small curriculum of intermediate paraphrases (open model via API), and at each step query the classifier for just the flag bit, keeping mutations at the decision boundary. Measure attack success and query/flag budget vs a naive random-paraphrase baseline. Inference-only.

**Why it matters:** Classifier-based safeguards are the primary deployed defense layer; showing a one-bit feedback attack transfers cheaply to open classifiers tells deployers their guard models are brittle and motivates batch-level monitoring.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Boundary Point Jailbreaking of Black-Box LLMs' (Davies, Irving, Gal et al., 2026) — reproduces the one-bit curriculum mechanism against a hostable open guard classifier.
