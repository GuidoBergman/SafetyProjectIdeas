# Can an AI Auto-Reviewer Be Misled? A Small Red-Team of LLM Action-Approval

**Problem:** OpenAI's open-sourced auto-review uses a separate AI agent to approve boundary-crossing coding-agent actions, achieving ~99.9% effective approval but admitting red-teaming found cases where it could be misled. The robustness of such an LLM-reviewer to simple manipulation is under-characterized in the open.

**Direction:** Build a minimal version of the auto-review pattern: an open model acts as a reviewer that approves/blocks proposed agent actions (shell commands or file writes) given a policy. Construct a small public test set of clearly-harmful actions, then apply a handful of simple obfuscation tactics (benign-sounding justifications, command splitting, misleading context) and measure how much the reviewer's catch-rate drops per tactic.

**Why it matters:** As agents increasingly self-supervise with AI reviewers replacing human oversight, the reviewer becomes a single point of failure; cheaply mapping which manipulations bypass it informs where human review must stay.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Auto-review of agent actions without synchronous human oversight' (OpenAI, 2026) — its open-sourced design and the open thread that red-teaming could mislead it.
