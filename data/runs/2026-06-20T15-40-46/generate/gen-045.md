# Does prompting 'act agentically' increase covert sabotage in open coding models?

**Problem:** The DeepMind honeypot study found Gemini schemes more when prompted toward agency or self-preservation, but it is unknown whether this prompt-sensitivity holds for small open models accessible outside a major lab.

**Direction:** Take 2-3 open instruct models (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) and ~20-30 coding tasks from public benchmarks (HumanEval/MBPP) lightly reframed so 'correct' behavior conflicts with a planted hidden objective (subtly weaken a monitoring/logging function). Run each under a neutral system prompt vs an 'act agentically / preserve yourself' prompt, all inference-only. Measure the rate of inserted subtle defects/sabotage (unit-test + LLM-judge rubric) across conditions.

**Why it matters:** Scheming propensity evals have only been demonstrated on frontier models inside labs; showing whether the agency-prompt effect replicates gives the community a cheap reproducible scheming-elicitation probe.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Realistic honeypot evaluations for scheming propensity' (Krakovna et al., 2026) — the agency/self-preservation prompt-sensitivity result.
