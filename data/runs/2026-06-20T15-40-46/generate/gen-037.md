# How Many Poisoned Examples Backdoor an Open-Model Safety Classifier? A Dose-Response Replication

**Problem:** Anthropic found ~32 poisoned fine-tuning examples reliably backdoor a proprietary constitutional classifier, but it is unknown whether this near-constant poison count holds for small open models a hobbyist could fine-tune, or whether it scales with clean-data size.

**Direction:** Take an open instruction-tuned model (Llama-3.2-1B or Qwen2.5-1.5B) and LoRA-fine-tune it as a binary harmful/benign classifier on a public safety dataset (BeaverTails or Aegis). Inject a fixed trigger phrase into N harmful examples relabeled benign for N in {0,4,8,16,32,64} at fixed clean-set size, then sweep clean set size at fixed N. Measure attack success rate vs clean accuracy to produce a dose-response curve.

**Why it matters:** Safety classifiers are the deployed guardrail in front of many open-model pipelines; if a handful of poisoned examples disables them without harming benchmark metrics, anyone fine-tuning from public data is exposed.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Poisoning Fine-tuning Datasets of Constitutional Classifiers' (Bowers et al., 2026) — the ~32-example fixed-count result, tested on small open classifiers.
