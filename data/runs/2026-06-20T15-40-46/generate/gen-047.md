# Cheap active sampling: can a Gaussian Process pick safety-eval prompts 10x more efficiently?

**Problem:** ProEval shows GP-based active selection reaches accurate performance estimates with 8-65x fewer samples, but it was demonstrated on internal DeepMind setups; whether a simple version helps a budget-constrained safety evaluator is untested.

**Direction:** On one public safety benchmark with many items (a refusal/jailbreak set or TruthfulQA), score a single open model on the full set as ground truth. Then implement a lightweight Bayesian-quadrature-style sampler (scikit-learn GaussianProcessRegressor over cheap text-embedding features) that adaptively chooses which prompts to evaluate next, and compare sample-efficiency to random subsampling for estimating the overall safety score to within +-1%. Report the savings curve.

**Why it matters:** Smaller orgs run safety evals on tight budgets; a public reproducible demonstration that GP-active selection cuts eval cost democratizes a frontier-lab technique.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'ProEval' (Huang et al., 2026) — its 8-65x sample-efficiency claim, scaled down to scikit-learn on a single benchmark.
