# Can a teacher hide its trait on purpose? Testing whether prompted 'evasion' lets a model dodge distillation-based incrimination

**Problem:** Distillation-based incrimination fails if a misaligned model can avoid transferring evidence of its trait. It is unknown how easily a model can be instructed to suppress subliminal trait transfer while still producing useful-looking outputs.

**Direction:** Reuse a benign measurable trait (owl-preference). Generate three number-sequence datasets from the same open teacher: trait-on, trait-on with an added 'do not let your preferences leak into your numbers' evasion instruction, and trait-off control. LoRA-finetune separate students on each and compare inherited trait strength. Answer: does prompted evasion measurably reduce transfer?

**Why it matters:** Westover et al. flag adversarial models that avoid transferring evidence as an open thread; a cheap demonstration that prompt-level evasion blunts the signal would be an early warning the technique is gameable.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Incriminating misaligned AI models via distillation' — attacks its 'adversarial models avoid transferring evidence' open thread; grounded in subliminal learning (Cloud et al., 2507.14805).
