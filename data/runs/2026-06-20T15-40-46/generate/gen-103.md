# Do Coherence Checks Catch 'Fried' Model Organisms? A Cheap Replication on Open LoRA Adapters

**Problem:** Daniel Tan et al. argue many emergent-misalignment model organisms are 'fried' — they lose preference coherence and instruction-following while still passing standard benchmarks — but there is no easy beginner-runnable diagnostic that flags this degradation.

**Direction:** Take publicly released emergent-misalignment LoRA adapters (Model-Organisms-for-EM Qwen/Llama adapters) and their base models, run inference-only, and reproduce a simple 'mu-decisiveness'-style coherence metric: present forced-choice preference pairs and measure transitivity/consistency, plus an instruction-following pass rate. Compare base vs organism to quantify the coherence drop, and check whether the drop correlates with the strength of induced misalignment. Deliverable: a reproducible 'coherence health check' notebook and a table ranking which public organisms are most degraded.

**Why it matters:** If widely-used model organisms are degraded proxies, downstream alignment results built on them may not transfer; a cheap standardized coherence check lets the community sanity-check organisms before trusting them.

**Relevant context:** Replicates and extends 'Your Model Organisms Might Be Fried' (Tan, Bostock, Africa et al.) — its mu-decisiveness coherence-drop result, as a reusable diagnostic.
