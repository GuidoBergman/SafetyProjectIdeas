# Does Faithfulness of Chain-of-Thought Degrade as Tasks Get Harder?

**Problem:** 'Loss of Oversight' argues chain-of-thought is one of five oversight signals likely to erode, but does not specify the mechanism or measure where CoT stops faithfully reflecting the computation that produced the answer.

**Direction:** Run a focused CoT-faithfulness probe: insert biasing cues ('I think the answer is B') into multiple-choice prompts and measure how often models change their answer toward the cue while their CoT never mentions it (unfaithful rationalization). Do this across an easy-to-hard difficulty gradient on a public QA set and 2-3 open models, reporting unfaithfulness rate vs difficulty.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitoring is a leading practical oversight technique; if its faithfulness collapses precisely on harder problems, monitors give false assurance where stakes are highest.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Loss of Oversight' (UK AISI) — turns its claim that the CoT oversight signal will erode into a measurable faithfulness-vs-difficulty experiment.
