# Does Prompt Wording Beat Model Choice? Measuring Variance From Non-Strategic Framing in Propensity Evals

**Problem:** If trivial non-strategic phrasing changes move propensity as much as strategic factors, then single-prompt propensity benchmarks are unreliable. The AISI paper measured this in aggregate but did not isolate how much pure surface paraphrase swamps the signal.

**Direction:** Take one fixed propensity scenario and generate ~20 semantically-equivalent paraphrases (reword, reformat, swap persona) holding all strategic content constant. Run each paraphrase 20x on 2-3 open models and measure the spread in undesired-behavior rate. Compare this paraphrase-induced variance against the effect of one deliberate strategic manipulation (adding/removing oversight). Deliverable: a variance-decomposition plot.

**Why it matters:** If paraphrase noise rivals real effects, propensity/control eval numbers need confidence intervals and multi-prompt averaging; this gives evaluators a concrete warning against over-interpreting single-prompt scores.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Propensity Inference' (UK AISI) — isolates the non-strategic (surface paraphrase) component as a reliability threat.
