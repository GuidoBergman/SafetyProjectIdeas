# Cheap-Defense Stress Test: Do Simple Prompt-Level Guards Survive the Jailbreaks That Broke DeepSeek-V4-Pro?

**Problem:** FAR AI showed DeepSeek-V4-Pro's safeguards collapse (98-100% attack success) under three jailbreak strategies despite blocking 100% of direct requests. It is unclear whether lightweight deployable prompt-level defenses meaningfully reduce that success rate.

**Direction:** Using a public harmful-behavior benchmark (JailbreakBench JBB-Behaviors / HarmBench prompts) and 2-3 reproducible jailbreak templates (roleplay, refusal-suppression, low-resource encoding), measure attack success on an open model. Then add three cheap inference-only defenses and re-measure: a defensive system prompt, a second-pass self-critique filter, and input paraphrase-then-answer. Report which defenses move ASR and which the attacks route around, scored with an LLM-judge calibrated on a held-out hand-labeled set.

**Why it matters:** Most open-weight deployers cannot retrain safeguards; they need to know whether wrapper-level defenses are real protection or theater.

**Relevant context:** Extends 'Security Stress Test: DeepSeek-V4-Pro' (FAR AI) — its 98-100% ASR finding, testing inference-only defenses on an open model.
