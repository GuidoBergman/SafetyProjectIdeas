# Do Cognitive Biases Leak Into LLM Preference Judgments? A Mini-Audit

**Problem:** 'AI alignment is a human problem' argues human cognitive biases contaminate the supervisory signals we feed models. The claim that classic human biases reproduce in LLM-as-judge preference labeling is asserted but not measured in a single tight experiment.

**Direction:** Construct paired response candidates holding content constant while varying one bias trigger at a time (answer length, confident tone, ordering/anchoring, sycophantic agreement) and ask 3-4 open LLM judges which response is 'better'. Quantify how often each bias flips the preference label vs a content-matched control. Report a bias-susceptibility profile per judge model.

**Why it matters:** If LLM judges — increasingly used to scale human feedback — inherit human-style biases, automated preference data systematically encodes those biases into trained models, the amplification failure the paper warns about.

**Relevant context:** Operationalizes 'AI alignment is a human problem' (UK AISI) — its claim that cognitive biases in supervisory signals are an alignment bottleneck.
