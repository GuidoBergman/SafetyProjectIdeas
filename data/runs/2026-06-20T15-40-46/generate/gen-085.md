# When Human Verification Fails: Detecting Plausible-but-Wrong LLM Answers on Hard Tasks

**Problem:** The paper names 'verification when human expertise is insufficient' as a core bottleneck, but offers no concrete measurement of how often weak overseers accept confident-wrong answers on tasks beyond their competence.

**Direction:** Use a public dataset with verified ground truth at varying difficulty (GSM8K-hard, MMLU subsets, or expert QA). Generate model answers, including deliberately flawed-but-fluent ones, and measure how reliably a cheaper 'verifier' (a weaker open model standing in for a non-expert overseer) catches the errors as difficulty rises. Plot verification accuracy vs difficulty to map where oversight collapses.

**Why it matters:** It empirically grounds the abstract 'verification gap': a curve showing where weak overseers stop catching errors is a concrete input to scalable-oversight design.

**Relevant context:** Operationalizes the verification bottleneck from 'AI alignment is a human problem' (UK AISI) into a weak-verifier-vs-difficulty measurement.
