# Research Question

When the position paper's proposed evidence-level checklist is turned into an explicit anchored rubric and applied to real published misalignment claims, which evidentiary dimensions are systematically weakest?

# Approach Outline

Convert the paper's checklist (conceptual clarity, dataset robustness, experimental design, causal vs correlational evidence) into a 0-3 rubric with anchored per-item definitions and worked examples. Select exactly 3 widely-cited anthropomorphic-misalignment results — one deception, one emergent-misalignment, one sycophancy paper — to keep scope tight. Two independent raters score each paper against the rubric, then reconcile; report per-item scores and inter-rater agreement (e.g., Cohen's kappa or percent agreement). Deliverable: a filled scorecard plus a blog post identifying the systematically weakest evidentiary dimensions and the rubric itself as a reusable instrument.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Rubric construction: write anchored 0-3 definitions for each checklist item with one positive and one negative worked example per anchor (expected: a self-contained scoring instrument a second rater can apply without the author present).
- Independent dual scoring: both raters score all 3 papers blind to each other, then compute inter-rater agreement per item (expected: moderate agreement after one calibration round; items with low agreement flag ambiguous rubric anchors to refine).
- Weakest-dimension analysis: aggregate scores across the 3 papers and identify which evidentiary dimension scores lowest on average (expected outcome: a reproducible scorecard plus a finding such as 'causal-vs-correlational evidence is the systematically weakest dimension', published as a public evidentiary baseline).

# Theory of Impact Chain

If an abstract evidence checklist becomes a tested rubric with measured inter-rater reliability, then headline misalignment claims acquire transparent, comparable evidentiary scores -> reviewers, journalists, and policymakers can apply the same instrument to new claims -> the evidentiary bar for misalignment research rises across the field, reducing the risk that over- or under-stated claims drive high-stakes decisions.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual_value, accessible_complexity, and low_compute, and 4 on narrow_scope: independent third-party re-scoring of published claims needs no lab access or GPU and is a neglected scrutiny task well within a beginner's reach. The scope was tightened to exactly three papers with explicit inter-rater reliability.
