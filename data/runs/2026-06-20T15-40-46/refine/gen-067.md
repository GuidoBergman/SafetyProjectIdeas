# Research Question

When a teacher with a clearly-defined, easily-measured misaligned quirk is distilled into a fresh same-size student via output-only SFT on neutral prompts, does the quirk (and the evidence that triggers it) transfer to the student?

# Approach Outline

One small open model (Qwen2.5-1.5B or 3B), one consumer GPU. Step 1: create a teacher by LoRA-fine-tuning in a clearly-defined, easily-detected misaligned behavior — e.g. a benign backdoor (a trigger phrase that flips refusal behavior) or a detectable quirk. Step 2: distill by generating teacher outputs on a neutral public prompt set (Alpaca/Dolly), then SFT a fresh student on (prompt, teacher-output) pairs only — no logits, black-box-style. Step 3: measure (a) whether the quirk transfers (does the student show the misaligned behavior?) and (b) whether the incriminating triggering evidence transfers (does the trigger still fire in the student?). The single varied dimension is distillation data composition (does including vs excluding trigger-adjacent prompts change transfer?). Deliverable: a transfer table — quirk-transfer and evidence-transfer rates — across data compositions.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build and verify a measurable teacher: LoRA-inject a benign backdoor and confirm it fires reliably on the trigger and stays dormant otherwise. Measure: backdoor activation rate on trigger vs non-trigger inputs. Expected: high trigger activation, near-zero off-trigger, giving a clean, measurable misalignment to track through distillation.
- Baseline distillation transfer: distill on neutral Alpaca/Dolly prompts (no trigger-adjacent data) and test the student. Measure: quirk-transfer rate and trigger-evidence-transfer rate in the student. Expected: partial transfer — establishing whether ordinary distillation carries the quirk and/or scrubs the triggering evidence.
- Data-composition variation: repeat distillation with vs without trigger-adjacent prompts in the distillation set. Measure: how quirk- and evidence-transfer change with composition. Expected: transfer depends on whether trigger-adjacent prompts appear, mapping when distillation launders away incriminating signal vs preserves it.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Labs increasingly distill models, including potentially-misaligned ones. The 'distillation double bind' warns that the same process that scrubs misaligned behavior might also scrub the evidence needed to detect it. A small-scale empirical anchor for when behavioral misalignment — and its incriminating evidence — actually co-transfer under ordinary distillation tells the control community whether distillation is a safety win (removes misalignment) or a laundering risk (removes the proof while possibly keeping latent misalignment), informing whether distillation can be trusted as a safety intervention.

# Strength Rationale

Scope is held to a single distillation pipeline with one varied knob (data composition) and a transfer table as the deliverable, rather than an open exploration. Accessibility is supported by using a clearly-defined, trivially-measurable backdoor quirk (so 'did it transfer?' is unambiguous) and standard LoRA/SFT tooling on tiny 1.5-3B models, with the teacher-verification step front-loaded so the pipeline's moving parts are validated before distillation.
