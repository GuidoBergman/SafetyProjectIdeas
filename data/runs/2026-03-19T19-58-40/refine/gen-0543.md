# Research Question

To what extent does reward threshold exists in ppo training beyond which reward hacking degrades win rates. the role of kl penalty coefficient in modulating this threshold has not been systematically characterized at s?

# Approach Outline

Using TRL's PPO implementation on a small model (e.g., GPT-2 or Llama-3.2-1B) with a simple sentiment reward model, run PPO with KL coefficients spanning 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3, and 1.0. For each, measure reward on the proxy (reward model) vs. a held-out gold evaluation. Plot the overoptimization curve for each KL setting.

Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Characterizing how KL penalty modulates the reward hacking threshold gives practitioners a concrete tunable safety lever and informs theoretical models of overoptimization.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: characterizing KL penalty's role in reward hacking gives practitioners a concrete tunable safety lever. The link from KL coefficient to overoptimization threshold to safer RLHF training is explicit and each step is defensible.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires setting up PPO training with TRL, training a reward model, implementing gold evaluation, and running 5 RL training runs. PPO training is notoriously finicky and debugging RL pipelines requires significant experience. A beginner CS student would struggle considerably with RL training stability issues.

narrow_scope (3/5): Conceptually well-scoped (5-point sweep, one plot), but PPO training runs are time-consuming and each run may need debugging. The path from start to first result is longer than it appears due to RL training instabilities. May exceed 30hrs.

novelty (2/5): Gao et al. (2023) 'Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization' (ICML 2023) established the overoptimization curve framework with gold vs proxy reward. Subsequent work has systematically studied the KL penalty coefficient's role: 'Catastrophic Goodhart: regularizing RLHF with KL divergence does not mitigate heavy-tailed reward misspecification' (ICLR 2025) directly characterizes failure modes of KL regularization. 'Reward Shaping to Mitigate Reward Hacking in RLHF' (arXiv 2025) and Lilian Weng's comprehensive survey (2024) cover the overoptimization curve under varying KL strengths. The specific sweep of KL coefficients (0.01-1.0) and plotting proxy vs gold reward is essentially a replication of established methodology. The remaining gap is only doing this at very small scale with TRL, which is an engineering exercise rather than a research contribution.
