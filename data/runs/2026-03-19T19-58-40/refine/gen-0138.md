# Research Question

To what extent does safety refusals from different models may all sound different while encoding the same underlying strategy, or vice versa—there is no empirical map of refusal diversity across the ai ecosystem?

# Approach Outline

Collect refusal responses from 15+ models on 500 standardized harmful prompts, cluster them semantically and structurally, and count the effective number of distinct refusal strategies.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A small number of shared refusal strategies means an adversary who breaks one breaks all—a direct systemic safety risk.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling and critical: directly demonstrates whether breaking one refusal strategy breaks all models. This is a direct systemic safety risk finding with immediate implications.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: collecting refusals from 15+ models requires API access and cost, and semantic clustering requires embedding and clustering skills, but these are learnable.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: running 15+ models on 500 prompts, collecting outputs, and performing clustering analysis is significant in compute, API cost, and time for 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): Existing work measures refusal inconsistency within models and benchmarks over-refusal rates across models, but no study has specifically clustered refusal responses semantically across 15+ models to count the effective number of distinct refusal strategies. The ecosystem-wide diversity question remains unanswered, though related work on refusal consistency exists.
