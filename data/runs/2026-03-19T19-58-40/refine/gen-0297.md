# Research Question

To what extent does causal chain from governance failure to public harm may have detectable early warning signals. if governance failures at early stages predict severe downstream harm, early intervention is especial?

# Approach Outline

For AIAAIC incidents with severity ratings, code governance-related variables at earlier stages (was pre-deployment testing documented? was a model card published? was the incident class flagged in any evaluation?). Run a logistic regression predicting severe harm from early-stage governance signals.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Predictive early warning signals would allow proactive governance intervention before harm escalates.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: predictive early warning signals would be valuable, but small sample size from AIAAIC likely limits statistical validity and generalizability.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Advanced: logistic regression modeling with governance predictor variables requires statistical methodology knowledge beyond beginner CS students.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: coding governance variables for incidents, running regression analysis, and interpreting results is bounded but requires statistical rigor.

novelty (4/5): AI early warning systems exist in clinical settings and IT operations, but applying predictive modeling to governance signals as predictors of AI incident severity is a novel framing. The AIAAIC and AI Incident Database provide raw data but lack structured fields for governance predictor variables. The MIT AI Risk Repository added harm severity ratings based on CSET's taxonomy. However, no published work applies logistic regression or other statistical models to test whether specific governance signals (e.g., absent impact assessments, no public model cards, lack of audit) predict higher severity AI incidents. The data limitation (small N) noted in the original scoring is a valid concern, but the research question itself is original.
