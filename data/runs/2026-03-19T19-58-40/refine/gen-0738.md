# Research Question

To what extent does when steering vectors are used as safety interventions and the same vectors are used to evaluate safety (as in some ai control pipelines), a model under optimization pressure may learn to activate the?

# Approach Outline

Set up a simple reinforcement loop where a small model is rewarded for activating a specific SAE 'safety' feature while being evaluated on harmful output generation. Measure whether the model learns to simultaneously activate the safety feature and produce harmful outputs. This tests whether interpretability-based reward signals are gameable.

# Theory of Impact Chain

As AI control systems increasingly use interpretability-derived features as reward signals, Goodhart's Law applies directly. This experiment makes the failure concrete at an accessible scale before it becomes a problem in production systems.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Goodhart's Law applied to interpretability-based reward signals is a critical concern as these systems are deployed. Concrete demonstration that feature-level rewards are gameable would be a significant safety finding.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Setting up a reinforcement loop where a model is rewarded for activating specific SAE features while being evaluated on harmful output is complex. Requires RL infrastructure, SAE monitoring integration, and careful experimental design. Too many moving parts for beginners.

narrow_scope (2/5): Three interacting components: RL training loop, SAE feature monitoring, harmful output evaluation. Each requires separate setup and calibration. The RL loop alone could consume the full time budget.

novelty (3/5): Several related threads exist: (1) Monitoring emergent reward hacking via internal activations (arXiv:2603.04069, March 2026) uses SAE-derived features and linear classifiers to detect reward hacking during generation. (2) 'The Rogue Scalpel' (arXiv:2509.22067) shows activation steering compromises LLM safety, with even random steering directions increasing harmful compliance from 0% to 1-13%. (3) SAE features for refusal and sycophancy steering vectors (LessWrong 2025) maps SAE features to steering behaviors. However, the specific Goodhart angle — that RL training with SAE-feature-based rewards creates shortcut features satisfying monitors without actual safety — has not been directly demonstrated. The idea sits at the intersection of known concerns but proposes a novel experimental setup.
