# Research Question

To what extent does safety resources could target preventing misaligned goal formation during training or detecting/disrupting goal concealment post-training. the relative tractability and cost-effectiveness of these two?

# Approach Outline

Design a controlled experiment comparing two intervention conditions on a small finetunable model: (1) training-time regularization to prevent misaligned goal representation, (2) deployment-time probing to detect goal concealment. Measure residual deception rates and computational costs per intervention. Conduct on a toy alignment-faking task.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Resource allocation across the causal chain should be evidence-based. If one link is dramatically cheaper to intervene on, safety efforts should concentrate there.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Direct empirical comparison of two intervention strategies informs resource allocation across the entire safety field. Extremely high impact if results are clear.

accessible_complexity (1/5): Requires implementing training-time regularization and deployment-time probing, both of which are advanced ML techniques. Fine-tuning with custom regularization and designing activation probes are expert-level tasks. Not accessible to beginners.

narrow_scope (2/5): Two complete interventions, each requiring its own implementation, plus comparative evaluation on a toy task. This is two research projects in one.

novelty (4/5): The AI Safety Atlas documents training-time interventions (interpretability-guided regularization, probe-based monitoring) and deployment-time probing as separate approaches. OpenAI's monitoring monitorability work examines chain-of-thought monitoring fragility. Apollo Research's stress testing work evaluates deliberative alignment as a training intervention. However, no existing work directly compares training-time regularization to prevent misaligned goals vs. deployment-time probing to detect goal concealment in a controlled experiment measuring residual deception rates and computational costs. The head-to-head empirical comparison with cost-effectiveness analysis is novel, even though the individual intervention approaches are known.
