# Research Question

To what extent does organizations must comply with multiple overlapping ai governance frameworks (eu ai act, nist rmf, iso 42001, g7 code of conduct). there is no tool to map obligations across frameworks and identify re?

# Approach Outline

Build a structured database of obligations from 5–7 major frameworks, tagged by domain (transparency, testing, incident reporting, etc.). Write a Python script that, given a user-specified organization type, outputs a deduplicated obligation checklist with source citations.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Reduces compliance friction for smaller organizations and exposes structural gaps in existing governance architectures.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: a compliance tool is useful for organizations but compliance is a secondary mechanism for safety outcomes. Impact is indirect.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: building a structured database from legal text requires careful reading of regulatory documents and domain knowledge of obligation types. Not trivial for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: parsing 5-7 frameworks, tagging by domain, building deduplication logic, and creating a user-facing tool. Multi-step pipeline that fills 30 hours.

novelty (2/5): Multiple commercial tools already perform cross-regulation AI compliance mapping. Relyance AI offers 'AI Regulatory Mapping & Compliance Automation' that simultaneously maps to multiple frameworks with conflict resolution. Credo AI provides centralized AI governance across EU AI Act, NIST, and other frameworks. Centraleyes lists 13 AI compliance tools performing similar mapping. The OECD published a mapping tool for digital regulatory frameworks (May 2025). While these are commercial products rather than open research tools, the technical approach and the mapping itself are well-established. The academic contribution of a structured database for research purposes is a minor variant.
