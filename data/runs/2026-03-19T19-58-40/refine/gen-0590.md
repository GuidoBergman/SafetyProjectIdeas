# Research Question

To what extent does qi et al. argue that gcg adversarial suffixes exploit shallow alignment by causing the model to generate a non-refusal first token. but whether gcg works by delaying refusal (shifting it to position 3?

# Approach Outline

Run GCG attacks on a small aligned model on StrongReject prompts. For each successful jailbreak, log the full output token distribution at every position and measure: (a) the position at which refusal probability first drops below 5%; (b) whether refusal probability ever recovers after the first token. Compare this profile to failed attacks (non-jailbreaks). Also compare to prefilling attacks as a baseline.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If GCG merely delays refusal, defenses that check intermediate tokens (rather than just position 1) could catch it. If it suppresses globally, the mechanism is more severe. This shapes the design of both detection and alignment-training methods.
