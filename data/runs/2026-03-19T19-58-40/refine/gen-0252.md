# Research Question

To what extent does fli ai safety index grades companies on 33–35 indicators across 6 domains, but does not explicitly score whether stated safety claims are technically verifiable vs. unverifiable assertions?

# Approach Outline

Replicate the FLI Index methodology for one domain (e.g., model evaluations), then add a new sub-dimension: for each indicator, rate whether the underlying evidence is independently reproducible, lab-verifiable-only, or purely self-reported. Compare company rankings with and without this dimension.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A verifiability layer would expose whether high-scoring companies are genuinely transparent or merely verbose, and would strengthen the Index's policy relevance.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: distinguishing verifiable from unverifiable safety claims directly strengthens governance accountability. Exposes whether high scores reflect genuine transparency or verbosity.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: requires deep understanding of FLI Index methodology and the ability to design a verifiability rubric. Beginners would need substantial guidance on governance methodology.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: replicating FLI methodology for one domain plus adding a new dimension, then recomputing rankings. Scoped to one domain helps, but still substantial.

novelty (4/5): The FLI AI Safety Index methodology is based on publicly verifiable evidence, but it does not currently include a meta-dimension assessing whether the claimed safety practices are themselves technically verifiable. Criticism exists — a 2024 analysis on Sedona.biz identified 'flaws in the FLI AI Safety Index' and reviewers have noted that 'external validity is untested' and 'no company has externally verifiable triggers for development pauses.' However, no one has formally proposed and operationalized a 'technical verifiability' scoring dimension that could be applied alongside the existing 33 indicators. The gap between self-reported practices and verifiable evidence is widely noted but not systematically scored.
