# Research Question

To what extent does the prisma/vit-prisma tooling enables sae analysis of vision transformers. whether the layer-by-layer polysemanticity profile of vits mirrors the profile observed in language models is unknown?

# Approach Outline

Using ViT-Prisma, train SAEs on layers 0, 3, 6, 9, and 11 of ViT-B/16 (pretrained on ImageNet). For each layer, apply automated image-patch labeling to identify feature semantics. Measure L0 sparsity, dead feature rate, and the fraction of features corresponding to high-level semantic concepts vs low-level texture features. Compare to the GPT-2-small layer profile.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Understanding whether vision and language transformers share a common interpretability profile would support or challenge the hypothesis that transformer circuits are architecture-universal, with implications for how much interpretability research transfers across modalities.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: tests whether interpretability research transfers across modalities -- if it does, less total research is needed. However, the chain from 'ViTs have similar polysemanticity' to a specific safety outcome has a gap: research efficiency gain is one step removed from preventing a named harm.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires ViT-Prisma tooling for vision SAE training, automated image-patch labeling (a less mature pipeline than text labeling), and cross-modal comparison. Vision interpretability tooling is less documented and more niche than language interpretability. Advanced for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): 5 layers on ViT-B/16 plus comparison to GPT-2 layer profile requires two separate SAE analysis pipelines (vision and language). Image-patch labeling is a distinct workstream from text feature labeling. Multiple dimensions of comparison create sustained effort.

novelty (3/5): 'Sparse but not Simpler: A Multi-Level Interpretability Analysis of Vision Transformers' (arXiv:2603.15919, March 2026) directly analyzes SAE features across ViT layers. The ViT-Prisma toolkit (arXiv:2504.19475) provides infrastructure with 80+ pre-trained SAE weights for vision models. 'Sparse Autoencoders for Scientifically Rigorous Interpretation of Vision Models' (arXiv:2502.06755) further advances vision SAEs. The specific cross-modal comparison of layer-depth polysemanticity patterns (vision vs language) is partially addressed by these works, though a direct head-to-head comparison using matched methodology may still add value.
