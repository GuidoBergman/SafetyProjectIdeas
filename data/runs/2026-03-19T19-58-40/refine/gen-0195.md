# Research Question

To what extent does when multiple ai systems using retrieval-augmented generation all retrieve from the same underlying document stores (e.g., wikipedia, common enterprise knowledge bases), homogeneous models retrieving ?

# Approach Outline

Test 5+ RAG systems built on the same knowledge base but using different underlying models. Measure whether hallucinations and factual errors are more correlated across these systems than across the same models queried without RAG. Identify the source of correlation (model vs. retrieval).

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

RAG architectures are among the most common enterprise AI deployments. If shared knowledge bases compound model homogeneity, the effective diversity of enterprise AI is even lower than model diversity alone would suggest.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: RAG is the dominant enterprise deployment pattern. Showing shared knowledge bases compound model homogeneity has immediate practical implications for enterprise AI diversity.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: setting up RAG systems with different models on the same knowledge base is achievable with frameworks like LangChain. Measuring hallucination correlation requires some methodology design.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: building 5+ RAG systems, designing hallucination measurement, running with and without RAG comparisons. Could be narrowed to 3 models and a smaller knowledge base.

novelty (4/5): RAG hallucination research is extensive (MEGA-RAG 2025, RAG-KG-IL 2025, multiple surveys), but focuses on single-model hallucination mitigation rather than correlated hallucinations across multiple models sharing the same knowledge base. Legal RAG hallucination research (Stanford 2025) studies domain-specific failures but not cross-model correlation. The Artificial Hivemind paper measures output similarity and the Correlated Errors paper shows error correlation, but neither studies RAG-amplified correlation. The specific compound hypothesis -- that shared RAG knowledge bases compound model homogeneity to produce correlated hallucinations across an enterprise's AI systems -- is a novel and practically important contribution. No existing work measures whether hallucination patterns become more correlated when multiple LLMs share the same retrieval corpus.
