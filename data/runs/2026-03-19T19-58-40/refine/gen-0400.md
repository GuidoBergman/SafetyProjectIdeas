# Research Question

Multiple jailbreak benchmarks exist (StrongReject, TrustLLM, AILuminate, HELM Safety) using different prompt sets and scoring methods. How well their model-safety rankings agree is unknown.

# Approach Outline

Evaluate four open-weight models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Phi-3-Mini-Instruct) on both StrongReject and the TrustLLM safety subset. Compute Spearman rank correlation of safety scores across benchmarks. Identify prompt categories where the benchmarks most disagree on model rankings.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If benchmark agreement is low, safety evaluations are benchmark-specific and unreliable. A model could pass one benchmark while failing another, misleading practitioners.

# Cited Sources

- The StrongREJECT paper (Souly et al., 2024) explicitly compares its evaluator against others and notes TrustLLM only evaluates static templates while StrongREJECT provides more nuanced scoring.
- JailbreakBench (NeurIPS 2024) provides standardized evaluation but acknowledges benchmark fragmentation as an open problem.
- Sorry-Bench (ICLR 2025) studies evaluation methodology for jailbreaks, addressing evaluator agreement but not full benchmark-to-benchmark model ranking comparison.
- A direct Spearman correlation study of model rankings between StrongReject and TrustLLM has not been published, but the methodological differences are already documented.
