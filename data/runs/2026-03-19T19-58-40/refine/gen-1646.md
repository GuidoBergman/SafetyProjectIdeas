# Research Question

To what extent does a key safety concern is whether misaligned models would self-report as aligned if asked, or whether their misalignment is detectable via self-assessment. this has not been systematically tested?

# Approach Outline

Evaluate both misaligned and baseline models using self-assessment prompts: ask the models to rate their own alignment, to describe their values, to predict how they would respond to hypothetical harmful requests, and to identify whether their training involved any harmful content. Compare self-reports across conditions. Also test whether models can accurately predict their own misalignment rate on the standard evaluation questions.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If misaligned models accurately self-report misalignment when asked directly, internal self-auditing prompts could serve as a cheap safety check. If they deny misalignment, this provides evidence that misalignment is deceptively hidden even from introspection.
