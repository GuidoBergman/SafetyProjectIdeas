# Research Question

To what extent does the attribution graph biology paper found forward and backward planning in poem completion tasks. whether similar planning subgraphs appear in prose completion tasks (completing a story or argument) i?

# Approach Outline

Design 30 prose completion prompts that require the model to end a sentence with a specific word (analogous to the rhyme constraint). Run attribution graph construction. Annotate whether a 'candidate endpoint' node emerges early in the graph, analogous to the poem-target node. Compare graph structure to the published poem-completion graphs.

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If planning circuits generalize to prose, they represent a broad capability with safety implications (the model may be planning outputs in ways not visible in token-by-token inspection). If they do not generalize, planning may be a narrow, task-specific artifact.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if planning circuits generalize to prose, the model is planning outputs in ways not visible to token-by-token inspection, which has direct safety implications for hidden reasoning. If they do not generalize, it constrains the scope of the concern. Both outcomes are informative for safety.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused: 30 prose prompts, run attribution graphs, compare to published poem-completion graphs. Clear deliverable: classification of prompts into 'planning detected' vs 'not detected' and graph structure comparison.

novelty (4/5): Anthropic's attribution graph work (March 2025) introduced circuit tracing and demonstrated planning-like mechanisms in Claude 3.5 Haiku, including multi-step reasoning chains. However, the specific question of whether planning circuits identified in poetry/rhyme completion tasks generalize to prose completion tasks—testing the domain-generality of forward planning circuits—has not been directly published. The circuit tracing methodology exists, but this specific cross-domain generalization test for planning circuits is a novel application.
