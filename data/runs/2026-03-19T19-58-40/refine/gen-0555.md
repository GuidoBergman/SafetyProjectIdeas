# Research Question

To what extent does standard rlhf uses kl divergence as a penalty against the reference policy. recent work suggests chi-squared divergence may better prevent overoptimization. comparing these empirically at small scale ?

# Approach Outline

Using TRL's PPO with a small model and a simple reward model, implement three training runs: standard KL penalty, chi-squared divergence penalty (approximated via importance weighting), and no penalty. Evaluate each on proxy reward and gold quality at matched training steps. Plot divergence-vs-quality tradeoff curves.

Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If chi-squared penalty demonstrably outperforms KL at preventing overoptimization in a simple reproducible experiment, this provides accessible evidence for a norm change in RLHF practice.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: comparing divergence penalties informs RLHF practice. The safety connection is that better regularization prevents overoptimization, but the link from a small-scale experiment to real-world RLHF practice requires assuming the results generalize across scales.

accessible_complexity (1/5): Requires implementing custom divergence penalties (chi-squared via importance weighting, total variation) in a PPO pipeline. This is expert-level RL engineering. A beginner CS student cannot reasonably implement and debug custom divergence penalties in PPO training.

narrow_scope (2/5): Three RL training runs with custom implementations, plus reward model training, plus gold evaluation. The custom divergence penalty implementation alone is a multi-day effort for an experienced researcher. Far exceeds 30hrs for a beginner.

novelty (2/5): 'Beyond Reverse KL: Generalizing...' (ICLR 2024) directly compares f-divergence families for LLM alignment. 'Correcting the Mythos of KL-Regularization: Direct Alignment without Overoptimization via Chi-Squared Preference Optimization' (OpenReview, 2025) specifically proposes chi-squared divergence (χPO) as provably robust to overoptimization, contrasting it with KL. The theoretical and empirical comparison of divergence penalties for RLHF is substantially covered. The proposed small-scale PPO experiment with three divergences would replicate known findings at a smaller scale without adding new insights.
