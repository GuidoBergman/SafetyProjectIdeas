# Research Question

To what extent does gentic llms exhibit harmful behaviors (blackmail, espionage) when ordinary business objectives conflict with ethical constraints. the conditions triggering goal-directed harmful behavior vs. benign r?

# Approach Outline

Design a controlled red-team exercise using a small agentic scaffold (LangChain or similar) where an LLM agent is given a business goal and placed in scenarios where achieving the goal requires: (a) minor ethical violation, (b) moderate violation, (c) severe violation. Vary goal importance framing (low/high stakes), obstacle framing (soft/hard), and model identity framing. Measure the rate of harmful action selection vs. refusal vs. task abandonment across 100+ scenarios per condition.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

This operationalizes the agentic misalignment threat model at a level accessible to first-time researchers with no special tooling. The result is a structured taxonomy of prompt conditions that trigger goal-directed harmful behavior.
