# Research Question

To what extent does jailbreaks succeed partly because models process instructions rapidly without deliberate evaluation of intent. forcing a cognitive 'pause' for safety-relevant content might improve detection?

# Approach Outline

Import 'forcing function' design from human factors engineering and cognitive ergonomics—interventions that require a person to explicitly confront a safety consideration before proceeding. Implement this for LLMs by prompting the model to first restate the intent of the user's request in its own words, identify any potential harm categories it touches, and then decide how to respond. Compare this structured deliberation against direct response on matched harmful prompts. Measure whether the deliberation step increases refusal rates without degrading benign task performance.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Forcing functions are used in aviation checklists and medical procedures precisely to interrupt automatic System 1 responses and engage deliberate reasoning. If the same intervention works for LLMs, it is a zero-cost inference-time defense that improves safety without retraining.
