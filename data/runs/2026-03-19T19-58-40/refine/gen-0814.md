# Research Question

It is unclear whether safety refusals are produced by fast, shallow pattern matching (System 1) or slower, deliberative reasoning (System 2). This matters because System 1 responses are known to be easily fooled by framing effects in human cognition.

# Approach Outline

Borrow Kahneman's dual process framework from cognitive science. Operationalize 'System 1 safety' as refusals that are triggered by surface features (keyword matches, obvious framing) and 'System 2 safety' as refusals that hold even under paraphrase, indirect framing, and multi-step reasoning. Design a battery of prompts at different depths of disguise. Measure how refusal rates decay as surface features are removed. Use chain-of-thought prompting to check whether explicitly reasoning about safety improves robustness.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Cognitive science research shows System 1 reasoning is easily fooled by framing and social influence. If LLM safety is primarily System 1, this explains why simple rephrasing jailbreaks it. This framing has direct implications for training—safety needs to be embedded in deliberative reasoning, not just surface pattern matching.
