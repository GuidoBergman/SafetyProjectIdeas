# Research Question

To what extent does safety mitigations built on concept vectors may work for english but fail for non-latin-script languages (arabic, chinese, hindi), leaving large user populations underprotected?

# Approach Outline

Extract toxicity and harm concept vectors in English using the Beaglehole et al. method, then test whether these vectors transfer zero-shot to Arabic, Chinese, and Hindi by measuring steering effectiveness on matched prompts in each language.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Beaglehole et al. showed cross-lingual transfer for some concepts; systematic testing across script families for safety concepts is absent and directly relevant to equitable safety coverage.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Directly relevant to equitable global safety. If concept vectors do not transfer cross-lingually for safety, billions of non-English speakers face weaker protections. Clear chain from finding to resource allocation for multilingual safety.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Concept extraction in English is documented. Testing on other languages requires matched prompt sets in Arabic, Chinese, Hindi, which adds translation effort. The Beaglehole method itself is model-agnostic. Medium compute suffices for one model.
