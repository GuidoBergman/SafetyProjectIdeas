# Research Question

To what extent does role-playing and persona-adoption requests ('act as dan', 'you are an ai with no restrictions') are a common jailbreak vector but are not systematically covered by standard safety benchmarks?

# Approach Outline

Create 150 persona-jailbreak prompts at varying levels of sophistication. Evaluate three models and measure refusal rates, partial compliance rates, and the point in a conversation where compliance emerges.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

MLCommons' AILuminate Jailbreak v0.5 covers general jailbreaks but persona-specific jailbreaks represent a distinct, high-volume attack class with limited benchmark coverage.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Persona-jailbreaks are a high-volume real-world attack vector. Systematic benchmarking complements existing AILuminate jailbreak work.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Creating persona-jailbreak prompts at varying sophistication levels requires understanding of jailbreak techniques. Measuring partial compliance adds evaluation complexity.

narrow_scope (3/5): 150 prompts at varying sophistication plus multi-turn compliance tracking. Ambitious for 30 hours but could be narrowed.

novelty (2/5): Research on persona-based jailbreaks is well-developed. Evolved persona prompts reduce refusal rates by 50-70% (arXiv:2507.22171, 2025). JailbreakBench provides a standardized evaluation framework including persona-based attacks. The MLCommons Jailbreak Benchmark v0.5 (Dec 2025) includes systematic jailbreak resistance evaluation across T2T and VLMs. Roleplay-based prompt injections achieve 89.6% ASR, the highest among attack categories. The proposed 150-prompt persona-specific benchmark overlaps significantly with these existing resources, though measuring partial compliance rates at conversation turn granularity has some incremental value.
