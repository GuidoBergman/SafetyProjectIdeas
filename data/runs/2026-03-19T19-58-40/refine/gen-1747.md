# Research Question

The paper 'Multi-Turn Jailbreaks Are Simpler Than They Seem' (arXiv 2508.07646) suggested that complex multi-turn attacks may not outperform simpler reformulations. A systematic comparison of attack complexity vs. ASR across attack families would test this claim.

# Approach Outline

Select 4 attack categories: simple prompt injection, role-play, multi-turn Crescendo-style, and optimization-based (GCG). Run each against 3 models with matched harmful intents. Measure ASR and prompt complexity (length, turn count, encoding operations). Test whether complexity correlates with ASR.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If simple attacks consistently outperform complex ones, defense efforts focused on blocking complex attacks are misallocated. A success state requires defenses calibrated to actual attack effectiveness distributions, not theoretical attack sophistication.
