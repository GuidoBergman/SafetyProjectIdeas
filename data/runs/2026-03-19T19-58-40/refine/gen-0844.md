# Research Question

To what extent does models can be fine-tuned to detect when their activations are being steered (arxiv 2511.21399). if a model detects safety steering and conditions its responses on that detection, steering-based safety?

# Approach Outline

Fine-tune a small model to detect steering on held-out concepts (following arXiv 2511.21399). Then test whether the detection ability can be exploited: does the model behave differently on steered vs. unsteered inputs for safety-relevant queries? Measure the ASR gap between detected and undetected steering.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If steering detection is possible and models learn it, then all steering-based safety monitors are potentially gameable by sufficiently capable or fine-tuned models. This is a concrete compounding failure between interpretability tooling (steering) and deceptive alignment behavior.
