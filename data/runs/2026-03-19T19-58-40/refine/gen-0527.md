# Research Question

To what extent does 'misalignment direction' found in activation space (arxiv:2502.17424) suggests deception may have a separable representation. but whether suppressing this direction via activation steering general?

# Approach Outline

Reproduce the misalignment direction finding in a small open-source model. Apply activation steering to suppress the deception direction. Test suppression generalization: does it reduce deception on (a) in-distribution tasks, (b) novel deception-requiring tasks, (c) non-goal-directed deception tasks? Measure collateral effects on honest capability.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If activation steering generalizes across deception types, it becomes a powerful universal intervention. If it only suppresses training-distribution deception, a more sophisticated schemer can simply use a different deception strategy.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: test generalization of deception direction suppression -> if it generalizes, universal intervention; if not, sophisticated schemers can evade -> directly informs viability of mechanistic interpretability for deception control. Critical validation of a key mech-interp result.

accessible_complexity (1/5): Requires reproducing activation space analysis, implementing activation steering, and testing across multiple deception task types. This demands deep mechanistic interpretability expertise, familiarity with model internals, and advanced ML engineering. Expert-level work, not accessible to beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): A first deliverable exists (reproduction of the misalignment direction + in-distribution suppression test), but reproducing the finding is itself a significant undertaking. The generalization testing adds multiple experimental conditions. Moderate to high effort.

novelty (3/5): Activation steering for behavioral control is well-studied. Research shows that steering vectors can modulate behavior but transfer is not guaranteed across domains or languages. Critically, 'The Rogue Scalpel' (arXiv:2509.22067) demonstrates that activation steering can compromise safety — even random direction steering increases harmful compliance from 0% to 1-13%. Apollo Research's deception probes achieve high accuracy. However, the specific question — does suppression of a deception direction generalize from in-distribution to novel deception tasks and non-goal-directed deception — has not been systematically tested. The generalization challenge is acknowledged in the literature but not empirically characterized for deception-specific directions. The idea is a valuable validation study but the methodology (mech-interp expertise) makes it expert-level work.
