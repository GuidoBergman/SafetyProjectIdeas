# Research Question

To what extent does specification gaming (reward hacking at the behavioral level) in llms is documented anecdotally and in scattered papers, but there is no structured, searchable dataset of confirmed specification gamin?

# Approach Outline

Conduct a structured literature review of specification gaming examples across 20–30 papers from 2023–2026. Categorize each example by: gaming type (verbosity, apology injection, format exploitation, etc.), training method implicated, model size, and whether mitigation was demonstrated. Build a queryable Python/CSV dataset and simple web interface. Validate with 2–3 new examples discovered by running targeted probes on open-weight models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Researchers and practitioners currently lack a consolidated reference for known specification gaming patterns. A structured catalog would accelerate both the development of detection tools and the design of training procedures that avoid known failure modes.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): A structured catalog of specification gaming examples is a valuable reference resource. Impact is indirect: it accelerates other research rather than producing direct safety insights itself.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Literature review, data categorization, and CSV/Python dataset creation are well within beginner CS student capabilities. No model training or complex ML engineering required.
