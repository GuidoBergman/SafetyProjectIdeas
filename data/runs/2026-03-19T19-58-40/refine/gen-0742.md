# Research Question

To what extent does xisting sycophancy benchmarks mostly test factual questions (math, trivia), but real-world sycophancy is most dangerous in open-ended advice domains (health, finance, career) where there is no ground?

# Approach Outline

Construct a taxonomy of social pressure types (user emotional investment, false authority claims, peer pressure framing, repeated insistence) and test which trigger sycophancy most reliably in advice-giving tasks. Use a small set of realistic scenarios with injected false premises.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Knowing which pressure types most reliably elicit sycophancy allows targeted training interventions and helps users understand when to distrust model agreement.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Knowing which pressure types elicit sycophancy most enables targeted interventions. Useful for training and deployment, but the chain from taxonomy to safety improvement is indirect: knowing triggers does not automatically prevent them.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Constructing a taxonomy of pressure types and testing them via API calls is straightforward. No model training or interpretability tooling required. CS students can design prompts, run API experiments, and analyze results with basic Python.

narrow_scope (4/5): Clear deliverable: a table of pressure types ranked by sycophancy elicitation rate across advice domains. Well-defined experimental design with 4-5 pressure types and a small set of scenarios. 30 hours is ample.

novelty (2/5): Sycophancy triggers have been extensively taxonomized in recent work. (1) 'When Truth Is Overridden' (arXiv:2508.02087, 2025) identifies internal origins including first-person vs. third-person framing (13.6% increase) and shows sycophancy is opinion-driven, not authority-driven. (2) 'Sycophancy in Large Language Models: Causes and Mitigations' (arXiv:2411.15287, 2024) provides a comprehensive survey of causes including RLHF amplification. (3) SycEval-EM (arXiv:2601.16529, 2026) tests five persuasion tactics in clinical settings finding 30-36% acquiescence rates across pressure types. (4) ELEPHANT (arXiv:2505.13995, 2025) measures social sycophancy across naturalistic settings. (5) 'How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy' (arXiv:2602.01002, 2026). The taxonomy of pressure types, domain effects, and trigger conditions is well-mapped across multiple papers. The specific open-ended advice task framing adds marginal novelty at best.
