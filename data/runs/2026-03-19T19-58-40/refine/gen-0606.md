# Research Question

To what extent does open-weight models use different alignment approaches (sft only, sft+rlhf, dpo, rlaif). whether the alignment training method affects alignment depth is unstudied, even though qi et al.'s gradient ana?

# Approach Outline

Identify 4-6 models trained with different alignment methods (SFT-only, DPO, PPO/RLHF, RLAIF) at comparable scales from open model hubs. Measure alignment depth (KL divergence curves) and prefilling ASR for each. Construct a table of alignment method vs. depth score. Use publicly available model cards to identify the alignment method.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If RLHF specifically causes shallow alignment while DPO or SFT does not, this would be a major finding for alignment training methodology. It would also provide practitioners with a simple recipe choice for deeper alignment.

# Strength Rationale

accessible_complexity (3/5): Requires identifying comparable models with known alignment procedures, computing KL curves, and running prefilling attacks. Intermediate skill: model selection and KL computation are not trivial for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): Deliverable requires sustained effort: finding 4-6 comparable models, running KL analysis on each, prefilling attacks on each. Multiple models and measurements before a clean comparison table emerges.

novelty (2/5): This question is now largely addressed by 'Why Is RLHF Alignment Shallow? A Gradient Analysis' (arXiv:2603.04851, March 2026), which formally proves that gradient-based alignment (including RLHF) inherently concentrates on positions where harm is decided and vanishes beyond the harm horizon. The paper explains why RLHF produces shallow alignment as a mathematical consequence of the training objective. Additionally, Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) already showed that the shallowness phenomenon applies across alignment methods. The SFT+DPO combination has been studied empirically (e.g., 'Improving LLM Safety and Helpfulness using SFT and DPO'). The core question of whether alignment method affects depth is now theoretically and empirically well-covered, leaving only minor incremental value in the proposed side-by-side comparison.
