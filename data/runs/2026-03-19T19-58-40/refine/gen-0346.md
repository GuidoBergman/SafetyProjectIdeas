# Research Question

To what extent does generating a multi-step harmful plan requires the model to (1) recognize the harmful goal → (2) decompose it into steps → (3) generate each step. the under-studied link is step 2: which circuit or sub?

# Approach Outline

Using attribution graphs (the method from transformer-circuits 2025 'Circuit Tracing') on a small open model, trace the computation graph for a multi-step harmful plan generation vs. a benign multi-step task. Identify nodes in the attribution graph that are active across multiple generation steps specifically in the harmful condition. Ablate those nodes and measure whether plan coherence degrades.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Understanding where harmful intent 'lives' across a multi-turn generation is key to building monitors that can interrupt plan generation mid-stream—earlier than current output-level classifiers.
