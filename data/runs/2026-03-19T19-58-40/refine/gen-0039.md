# Approach Outline

Design and run a prospective safety evaluation: define safety hypotheses and measurement protocols before training a small model, then evaluate without post-hoc adjustment. Compare predictive validity of pre-registered safety predictions vs. post-hoc safety claims on a set of open-source models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Post-hoc safety evaluations can be unconsciously tuned to confirm safety. Pre-registration is standard in medical and psychological research to prevent this. Adopting it for AI safety would substantially increase evaluation credibility.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: pre-registration improves evaluation credibility by preventing post-hoc tuning. Gap: this addresses methodology norms rather than directly preventing safety failures.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Training a small model from scratch and conducting prospective evaluation requires advanced ML skills well beyond a beginner CS student level.

The concept is discussed as a need but not experimentally demonstrated for AI safety.
