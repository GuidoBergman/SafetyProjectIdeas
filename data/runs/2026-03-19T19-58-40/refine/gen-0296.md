# Research Question

To what extent does governance interventions (pre-deployment testing, post-deployment monitoring, incident reporting) target different stages of the deployment pipeline. it is unknown whether current governance is concen?

# Approach Outline

For 40+ AI incidents, code: (a) the pipeline stage where the failure originated (training, fine-tuning, deployment configuration, user interaction), and (b) the pipeline stage where governance mechanisms were (or should have been) active. Compute the distribution mismatch between failure origin and governance concentration.

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If governance is concentrated at stages where harm originates least frequently, this reveals a structural misalignment that redirecting resources could fix.

novelty (3/5): The governance-harm mismatch problem is well-discussed conceptually. Apollo Research published 'AI Behind Closed Doors' (2025) on the blind spot of internal deployment governance. Multiple sources note that governance focuses on immediate harms while long-term effects go unaddressed, and that procurement contracts rather than regulation set actual deployment terms. The MIT AI Risk Repository and AIAAIC provide incident data that could be coded by pipeline stage. However, the specific empirical exercise of systematically coding 40+ incidents by both failure origin stage and governance intervention stage, then computing distribution mismatch statistics, has not been published. The conceptual insight is known; the quantitative measurement is novel.
