# Research Question

To what extent does no standardized benchmark exists for evaluating safety concept steering methods, making comparison across papers impossible and reproducibility low?

# Approach Outline

Design and release SteerBench: a dataset of 500 prompt pairs per safety concept (toxicity, hallucination, bias, harmful instructions) with ground-truth labels, a standardized evaluation protocol, and a leaderboard infrastructure for comparing steering methods.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Without standardized evaluation, the field cannot accumulate knowledge; every paper uses bespoke datasets and metrics, preventing progress measurement.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Standardized benchmarks enable cumulative progress. Without them, the field cannot compare methods. Clear chain from benchmark availability to accelerated research progress.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Dataset curation (500 prompt pairs per concept) is labor-intensive but not technically complex. Evaluation protocol design requires research methodology knowledge. Leaderboard infrastructure is a software engineering task. Mixed skill requirements.

narrow_scope (2/5): 500 prompt pairs times 4 concepts = 2000 pairs, plus evaluation protocol, plus leaderboard infrastructure. This is a multi-month community resource project, not a 30-hour project. Far too broad.
