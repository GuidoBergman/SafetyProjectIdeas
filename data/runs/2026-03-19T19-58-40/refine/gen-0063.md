# Theory of Impact Chain

Regulatory safety thresholds must be defensible. The current practice of ad hoc thresholds is specifically criticized in the 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' arxiv paper as a fundamental validity problem.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: defensible thresholds improve benchmark validity, which improves deployment decisions. The methodology transfer from medicine/regulation is well-motivated and each link is explicit.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires recruiting AI safety researchers for Delphi rounds, knowledge of structured elicitation methodology, and statistical analysis of inter-expert agreement. This is advanced for beginner CS students.

narrow_scope (2/5): Recruiting experts, running multiple Delphi rounds, and statistical analysis of agreement far exceeds 30 hours. The logistics of expert coordination alone are a major bottleneck.

novelty (4/5): Structured expert elicitation (Delphi, Sheffield Method) is well-established in medicine and regulation. Recent work explores 'Scalable Delphi' using LLMs as proxies for structured expert elicitation (arxiv 2602.08889). However, the specific application of these protocols to AI safety benchmark threshold setting -- comparing elicited thresholds with ad hoc benchmark cutoffs -- has not been done. The intersection of formal elicitation methodology with AI safety threshold setting is mostly novel.
