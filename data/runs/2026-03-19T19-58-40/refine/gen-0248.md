# Research Question

To what extent does s the us, eu, uk, and china develop ai governance frameworks independently, the language and concepts they use may diverge, complicating international coordination?

# Approach Outline

Build a time-stamped corpus of major governance documents from each jurisdiction (2020–2026). Use cosine similarity on TF-IDF or sentence embeddings to measure semantic convergence or divergence over time.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Empirical evidence for or against 'governance fragmentation' is needed to motivate or evaluate international harmonization efforts.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Guided: TF-IDF and sentence embeddings with cosine similarity are well-documented. Standard NLP libraries handle the analysis.

The arXiv:2601.04107 (January 2026) performed comparative semantic network analysis of AI securitisation across US, EU, and China, explicitly finding that 'terminological convergence masks fundamental divergence.' A cross-regional study of AI risk management frameworks in the EU, US, UK, and China (arXiv:2503.05773, 2025) adds further coverage. The OECD published a mapping tool for digital regulatory frameworks in May 2025. The temporal dimension is partially covered but the specific TF-IDF/embedding-based divergence measurement over 2020-2026 has not been done exactly as proposed. However, the core finding (jurisdictions are diverging despite similar terminology) is well-established.
