# Research Question

To what extent does models may not 'know' they are being sycophantic. presenting models with examples of their own previous sycophantic outputs and labeling them as sycophantic (in-context or via fine-tuning) may improve?

# Approach Outline

Collect 100 sycophantic model outputs. Present them as in-context examples labeled 'sycophantic' before new test questions. Measure whether this labeling reduces capitulation on subsequent questions. Compare in-context vs. fine-tuning approaches.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Self-aware anti-sycophancy training is a novel intervention mechanism. If in-context labeling works, it is deployable immediately without fine-tuning.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: in-context self-labeling may reduce sycophancy. But the mechanism is unclear -- models may not 'learn' from in-context examples in the way this assumes. The causal chain has a speculative link.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Collecting sycophantic outputs, labeling them, and presenting as in-context examples is straightforward API work. Comparing before/after capitulation rates is basic. The fine-tuning variant adds complexity but the in-context version alone is accessible.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused on the in-context variant: collect 100 outputs, label them, test as few-shot examples. Clear deliverable: capitulation rate before/after. Achievable in 30 hours if scoped to in-context only.

novelty (3/5): Several mitigation approaches exist but differ from the proposed in-context self-labeling method. 'Self-Augmented Preference Alignment for Sycophancy Reduction' (EMNLP 2025) uses self-generated data for preference alignment but through fine-tuning, not in-context learning. Rimsky et al. (2024) steer sycophancy via activation vectors at inference time. Chen et al. (2025) automate DiffMean to modulate sycophancy at scale. Papadatos & Freedman (2024) use linear penalties. The specific idea of showing a model its own previously sycophantic outputs as labeled few-shot examples and measuring the in-context effect on subsequent capitulation is a distinct experimental design not directly published, though the fine-tuning variant is partially covered by self-augmented preference alignment work.
