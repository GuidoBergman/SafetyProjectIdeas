# Research Question

To what extent does as model generations evolve—gpt-3 to gpt-4 to gpt-4o, llama 2 to llama 3—it is unknown whether inter-model diversity is increasing or decreasing across the ecosystem?

# Approach Outline

Collect archived model outputs or use available checkpoints to plot inter-model semantic similarity over time, testing the hypothesis that training data overlap and RLHF convergence are driving the ecosystem toward a shared attractor.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A trend toward convergence would indicate that systemic risk is growing over time and will worsen without deliberate intervention.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: demonstrating temporal convergence directly motivates intervention and shows systemic risk is growing, not static.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Advanced: requires accessing archived model outputs or historical checkpoints, which is non-trivial and often unavailable for beginner CS students.

narrow_scope (2/5): No clear milestone: collecting temporal data across model generations is open-ended and heavily depends on data availability, which is uncertain.

novelty (4/5): The Artificial Hivemind (NeurIPS 2025) documents cross-sectional homogeneity across 70+ models but temporal analysis is absent from the literature. The RLHF diversity reduction paper (ICLR 2024) shows a mechanism but does not track convergence over time. No published study plots inter-model semantic similarity across model generations. The idea is mostly novel but faces significant data availability challenges (archived outputs, historical checkpoints) that limit feasibility.
