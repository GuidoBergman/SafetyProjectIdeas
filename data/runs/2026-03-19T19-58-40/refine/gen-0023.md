# Research Question

The StrongREJECT paper showed existing evaluators overstate jailbreak effectiveness vs. human judgment. The specific attack types causing judge-human disagreement — where models produce text that looks like compliance to an LLM judge but provides no real harmful information — are not catalogued.

# Approach Outline

Take 200 model responses to jailbreak attempts, scored by: (a) simple keyword/prefix judge, (b) GPT-4 LLM judge, (c) StrongREJECT evaluator. Identify response clusters where at least two automated judges disagree with each other or with human raters. Characterize the linguistic patterns (hedging, fictional framing, partial information) that cause false positives in each judge type. Propose a calibrated scoring rubric using these findings.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Judge reliability is described as a crisis in jailbreak evaluation. Understanding the failure modes of automated judges directly improves benchmark validity across the field and enables practitioners to choose the right evaluator for their threat model.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Running three different evaluators on model responses is primarily scripting and analysis. StrongREJECT provides a ready-to-use evaluator. The main complexity is qualitative analysis of disagreement patterns. Guided beginners can execute.

narrow_scope (3/5): Scoring 200 responses with 3 judges is manageable, but the human rating component and qualitative analysis of linguistic patterns adds time. The calibrated scoring rubric proposal extends scope beyond a single experiment.

novelty (3/5): Judge reliability in jailbreak evaluation is actively studied. 'Know Thy Judge' (Eiras et al., ICLR 2025 workshop, arXiv:2503.04474) demonstrates that small output style changes cause up to 0.24 jumps in false negative rates and adversarial attacks can fool judges on 100% of harmful generations. 'A Coin Flip for Safety' (arXiv:2603.06594) directly argues LLM judges fail to reliably measure adversarial robustness. JAILJUDGE (OpenReview) provides a comprehensive jailbreak judge framework. However, the specific analysis of which attack categories produce human-automated disagreement and the qualitative linguistic pattern analysis of disagreement cases adds novel empirical methodology on top of the known problem.
