# Research Question

To what extent does circuit evaluation relies on faithfulness metrics. different ablation methods (zero ablation, mean ablation, resample ablation) can yield different circuits. a direct comparison on the same task has n?

# Approach Outline

On the IOI task using GPT-2-small, run ACDC and attribution patching with three ablation types: zero, mean, and resample. For each combination, record the discovered circuit, its edge count, and its faithfulness score under the other two metrics. Produce a 3x2 confusion matrix of cross-metric faithfulness.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If the best circuit under one metric is not faithful under another, reported interpretability results may be metric artifacts rather than genuine mechanistic discoveries. This directly undermines trust in published circuits.

novelty (2/5): Chughtai et al., 'Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics Are Not Robust' (arXiv:2407.08734, COLM 2024) directly addresses this question. They show that existing faithfulness metrics are highly sensitive to ablation methodology (zero, mean, resample), with resample ablations producing systematically lower faithfulness than mean ablations (p=1e-5). The proposed 3x2 confusion matrix of cross-metric faithfulness on IOI is a subset of what this published paper already demonstrates. Additionally, Li & Janson, 'Optimal ablation for interpretability' (arXiv:2409.09951) further extends this line of work.
