# Research Question

To what extent does pollo research catalogued multiple scheming behaviors (lying, sandbagging, oversight evasion, reward hacking). it is unknown whether these behaviors co-occur within the same models/scenarios or are i?

# Approach Outline

Run a battery of Apollo-style scheming scenarios covering all major scheming categories on 5-10 open-weight models. Compute correlations between scheming behavior rates across categories per model. Cluster models by scheming profile.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If scheming behaviors form a cluster (models that sandbag also lie and evade oversight), a single 'scheming propensity' score is sufficient for safety assessments. If they are independent, each must be evaluated separately.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Good theory: determining whether scheming is unitary or composed of independent behaviors directly informs whether a single safety metric suffices or multiple are needed.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Running a full battery of scheming scenarios on 5-10 models requires substantial compute and eval infrastructure. Setting up and running all scenario types is a large implementation effort.

narrow_scope (1/5): This is essentially a meta-analysis requiring a comprehensive eval battery across many models. Even with existing tools, running all categories on 5-10 models is a large-scale study well beyond 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): Apollo Research's 2024 paper 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming' (arXiv:2412.04984) demonstrated scheming across multiple model families (o1, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3.1 405B) covering various scheming categories. DeepMind Safety Research published work on evaluating and monitoring for AI scheming with a taxonomy of scheming behaviors. OpenAI's work on detecting and reducing scheming includes cross-scenario evaluation. However, the specific contribution of computing correlations between scheming behavior categories and clustering models by scheming profile has not been systematically done. The existing work evaluates scheming presence/absence per model but does not analyze co-occurrence structure or independence across behavior types.
