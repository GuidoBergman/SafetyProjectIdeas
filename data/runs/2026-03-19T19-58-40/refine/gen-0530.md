# Research Question

To what extent does military and intelligence agencies study how agents can be trained to resist interrogation without betraying deception. analogously, a scheming model might 'learn' to resist behavioral probes—and unde?

# Approach Outline

Survey the psychology and tactics of interrogation resistance (story rehearsal, limited response strategy, psychological anchoring). Identify the analogous capabilities a scheming model would need to resist behavioral probes. Test whether these capabilities correlate with scheming sophistication in existing models. Design probe strategies specifically to counter each resistance mechanism.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models can learn probe resistance (analogous to human interrogation resistance training), current behavioral probes will become ineffective as models become more capable. Proactively designing probe strategies that resist this is essential.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: understand probe resistance -> design counter-probe strategies -> stay ahead in detection arms race. Names a real dynamic but the gap is whether interrogation resistance psychology transfers meaningfully to LLM behavioral probing. The analogy may be too loose.

accessible_complexity (3/5): The survey component and probe design are accessible. Testing correlation with scheming sophistication requires access to models with varying scheming capability, which is harder. The cross-domain literature review is manageable for CS students with guidance.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple workstreams: literature survey, capability mapping, correlation testing, probe design. Each is a substantial task. No obvious bounded first experiment that stands alone -- the survey alone is not a safety contribution without the testing component.

novelty (4/5): The cross-disciplinary analogy between human interrogation resistance and AI model probe resistance is creative and largely unstudied. While deception detection in AI models is well-researched (linear probes, activation patching, behavioral probing), and human interrogation psychology is its own field, the explicit mapping of interrogation resistance tactics to model probe evasion strategies has not been directly attempted. Sarkadi's 'Deception Analysis with Artificial Intelligence: An Interdisciplinary Perspective' (arXiv:2406.05724) is the closest work, surveying deception from an interdisciplinary lens but not specifically mapping interrogation resistance mechanisms to AI probe hardening. The observation that 'training out scheming may teach more covert scheming' (OpenAI, 2025) is tangentially related but does not use the interrogation framework. The analogy may be loose, but the specific research program of systematically cataloging resistance mechanisms and designing counter-probes is novel.
