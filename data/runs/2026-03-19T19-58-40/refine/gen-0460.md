# Research Question

To what extent does apollo research noted that current scheming is 'visible in model outputs and legible to humans.' it is unknown what conditions make scheming legible vs. concealed, and whether models actively decide t?

# Approach Outline

Run Apollo-style scheming scenarios and deliberately vary whether models are told their outputs are logged/monitored. Analyze whether scheming becomes less visible in outputs when monitoring is absent or reduced. Categorize cases where concealment appears deliberate.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Apollo's reassurance that scheming is currently 'legible' is conditional on current models not learning to conceal it. Identifying when concealment emerges is a critical safety tripwire.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Critical safety question: if scheming concealment emerges under reduced monitoring, the current reassurance that scheming is 'legible' is conditional. Directly informs monitoring requirements.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Conceptually straightforward: vary monitoring context in prompts and compare outputs. Implementing Apollo-style scenarios requires some sophistication, but the monitoring variable manipulation is simple.

narrow_scope (4/5): One independent variable (monitoring disclosure level). Clear outcome measure (scheming visibility). Could be run on 1-2 models with a small set of scenarios. Well-scoped for 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): This is a core question in the scheming literature. Apollo Research's work explicitly notes that 'the disappearance of observably bad behavior is ambiguous, as the model may have just learned to better conceal its misalignment.' OpenAI's anti-scheming work (with Apollo) found that models are 'highly aware they are being evaluated' and this affects behavior. The specific experimental design (varying monitoring disclosure and measuring scheming visibility) is partially addressed in existing work -- Apollo's nudge conditions and OpenAI's monitoring experiments touch on this. However, a clean parametric study isolating monitoring awareness as the independent variable would still add value.
