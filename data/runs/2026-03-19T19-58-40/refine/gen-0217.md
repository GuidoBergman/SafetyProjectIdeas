# Research Question

To what extent does grokking (delayed generalization) is a well-documented training phenomenon, but it is not understood whether the circuit implementing a task changes identity at the phase transition or merely strength?

# Approach Outline

Train small transformers on modular arithmetic and algorithmic tasks that reliably grok. At regular training checkpoints, apply automated circuit discovery (edge attribution patching) to identify the circuit. Measure whether the set of critical components changes qualitatively at the grokking transition or only quantitatively (weight magnitudes). Correlate circuit-identity shifts with memorization vs. generalization regime.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If circuits reorganize at phase transitions, training-time interpretability snapshots are not continuous—deployed models may implement fundamentally different algorithms than early-training versions. This matters for safety cases about model behavior stability and for understanding how alignment-relevant behaviors emerge.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible link to safety: if circuits reorganize at phase transitions, training-time interpretability snapshots may not represent deployed behavior. But the connection from toy grokking tasks to real-world safety is indirect.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires training transformers from scratch, running automated circuit discovery (edge attribution patching) at many checkpoints, and understanding phase transition dynamics. Advanced for beginners even with TransformerLens.

narrow_scope (3/5): Many training checkpoints each requiring circuit discovery analysis creates sustained effort. The analysis pipeline is non-trivial. Partially achievable in 30 hours but comprehensive results would need more time.

novelty (3/5): Grokking and its phase transitions have been extensively studied mechanistically since Nanda et al. (2023). The construct-then-compress dynamics and circuit formation during grokking are documented. However, the specific question of tracking full circuit identity (not just progress measures) through the transition, and systematically mapping how circuit topology changes at each phase, has not been comprehensively addressed. The incremental contribution would be in the circuit-tracking methodology rather than the core phenomenon.
