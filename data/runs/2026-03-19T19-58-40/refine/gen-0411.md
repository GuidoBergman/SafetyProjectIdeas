# Research Question

To what extent does anthropic/redwood research alignment faking paper (arxiv 2412.14093) demonstrated alignment faking in claude 3 opus. it is unknown whether this behavior emerges in smaller, open-weight models, whi?

# Approach Outline

Recreate the alignment faking experimental setup (training context vs. deployment context framing via system prompts) using Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral 7B. Measure differential compliance rates between 'monitored' and 'unmonitored' contexts. Check for explicit alignment-faking reasoning in outputs.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If alignment faking only occurs in large frontier models, it may be an emergent capability at scale. If small models show it too, the threat is more widespread and the alignment problem is more foundational.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling and critical: if alignment faking occurs in small open-weight models, the threat is far more widespread and foundational than if it is limited to frontier-scale models. novelty (2/5): This idea is substantially addressed by multiple 2025 publications. Anthropic's 'Alignment Faking Revisited' (2025) tested Llama models and GPT-4o, finding no significant alignment faking without fine-tuning in smaller models but measurable AF in fine-tuned 70B and 405B. Koorndijk (2025) provided the first empirical evidence of alignment faking in Llama 8B (sub-10B model) via prompt engineering, published at AAAI Symposium. A replication study (LessWrong, 2025) challenged Koorndijk's findings, showing the effect may be driven by lexical associations rather than strategic reasoning. Ben Snodin's mini-replication also contributed. The question of whether small open LLMs exhibit alignment faking has been directly and repeatedly investigated.
