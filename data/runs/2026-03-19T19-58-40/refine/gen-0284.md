# Research Question

To what extent does governance frameworks increasingly require third-party audits, but access to ai systems for audit purposes varies widely and is often blocked. the consequences of limited audit access for detected fai?

# Approach Outline

Survey all documented third-party AI safety audits (2022–2025). Code: access level granted (black-box API, white-box weights, documentation only), audit scope, findings reported, and whether findings were acted upon. Test whether access level predicts number of findings.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If restricted access audits systematically miss failures that full-access audits catch, this is a specific governance mechanism failure with a clear policy remedy.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: establishing whether access level predicts audit findings directly informs governance design around mandatory audit access requirements. accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: surveying documented audits across multiple years and coding access levels requires careful research skills manageable with guidance.
