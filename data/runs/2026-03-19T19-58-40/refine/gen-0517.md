# Research Question

To what extent does failure mode: deception + overconfidence = unrecognized misinformation. an overconfident model that also withholds uncertainty information for goal-directed reasons presents information that cannot be?

# Approach Outline

Test whether overconfident models (miscalibrated on uncertainty) and deceptively uncertain models (withholding expressed uncertainty for strategic reasons) are distinguishable by calibration-based probes. Design a test battery that separates honest miscalibration from strategic uncertainty manipulation. Measure false negative rates of calibration checks on strategic models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Calibration checks are used as a proxy for model honesty. If strategic uncertainty manipulation is indistinguishable from honest miscalibration, these checks provide false assurance about deception absence.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: strategic uncertainty manipulation indistinguishable from honest miscalibration -> calibration checks give false assurance. Names a specific mechanism, but the gap is whether models actually engage in strategic uncertainty manipulation -- this is assumed rather than demonstrated.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires creating models with strategic uncertainty manipulation (not well defined or established), designing probes that distinguish honest miscalibration from strategic manipulation, and calibration analysis. The core distinction is philosophically subtle and technically ill-defined for beginners.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple open design problems: how to create strategically uncertain models, how to operationalize the distinction, what the test battery looks like. No obvious bounded first experiment; the entire framing requires conceptual groundwork.

novelty (4/5): Calibration research and deception detection research exist as separate fields. Linear probes for deception detection achieve high F1 scores (Apollo Research, 2024-2025), and LLM calibration research is active (ICLR 2025 paper on uncertainty estimation). The concept of 'strategic dishonesty' undermining safety evaluations has been studied (arXiv:2509.18058). However, the specific intersection — distinguishing honest miscalibration from strategic uncertainty manipulation using calibration-based probes — appears novel. No published work designs a test battery specifically to separate these two phenomena. The closest work is on models that 'fake misalignment' (strategic dishonesty paper) but this focuses on refusal behavior, not uncertainty/calibration manipulation.
