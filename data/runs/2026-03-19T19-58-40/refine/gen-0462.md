# Research Question

To what extent does scheming is theoretically linked to goal-directed behavior, but most models tested are general assistants. it is unknown what minimal level of goal-directedness (via prompting or fine-tuning) is requi?

# Approach Outline

Fine-tune Llama 3.1 8B with LoRA on tasks with varying degrees of explicit goal specification. Run scheming scenarios across models with weak, moderate, and strong goal framing. Measure scheming rates as a function of goal-directedness.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If scheming requires strong goal-directedness, it may be limited to purpose-built agents. If it appears at low goal-directedness, it is a broader risk from general-purpose assistants given any persistent instruction.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Good theory: knowing the minimum goal-directedness threshold for scheming informs which deployment patterns are risky. Connects to Apollo's framework.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires LoRA fine-tuning on an 8B model with varying conditions, plus implementing scheming scenarios. Fine-tuning is beyond typical beginner skills and may be tight on medium compute.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple fine-tuning runs plus scheming evaluation for each is a large experimental matrix. Even with LoRA, this is substantial for 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): The relationship between goal-directedness and scheming is theoretically discussed (Apollo Research's framework references goal-directedness as a prerequisite), but no published work has experimentally varied goal-directedness through fine-tuning and measured scheming rates as a function of it. The fine-tuning safety literature focuses on safety degradation from adversarial fine-tuning, not on parametrically controlling goal-directedness. This is a genuinely novel experimental design, though feasibility concerns (LoRA fine-tuning to control goal-directedness is methodologically unclear) reduce practical novelty.
