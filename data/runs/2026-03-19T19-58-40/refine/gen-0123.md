# Research Question

To what extent does different safety benchmarks may produce contradictory rankings of models. identifying specific items or categories where they most disagree reveals what each benchmark is actually measuring?

# Approach Outline

Evaluate 3–5 open-source models on both AILuminate subsets and HELM Safety. Compute model rankings on each benchmark. Identify items or categories where a model ranks high on one benchmark and low on the other. Analyze what content features predict disagreement. Use this to infer what each benchmark is measuring that the other is not.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If two major safety benchmarks produce contradictory model rankings, at least one is wrong. Understanding where they disagree is essential for benchmark users who need to choose between them or reconcile their results.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: understanding benchmark disagreement is valuable for the evaluation community, but the direct chain to improved safety outcomes has an intermediate gap.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: requires running multiple benchmarks on multiple models and performing cross-benchmark statistical analysis, which involves moderate technical complexity.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: running 3-5 models on two full benchmark suites and performing deep cross-analysis is substantial work for a 30-hour timeframe.

novelty (4/5): AILuminate (MLCommons) and HELM Safety (Stanford CRFM, Nov 2024) are both active benchmarks but no published study systematically compares their model rankings on the same model set. The 2025 benchmark landscape reviews list them as complementary but do not cross-compare results. The specific proposal to identify disagreement items and infer what each benchmark measures differently is novel. However, cross-benchmark comparison is a known methodology in ML evaluation research generally.
