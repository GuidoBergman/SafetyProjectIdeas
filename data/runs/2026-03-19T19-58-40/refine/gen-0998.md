# Research Question

To what extent does high-reliability organizations (hros like nuclear plants, aircraft carriers) operate complex systems with very low failure rates through specific cultural and procedural practices. these have not been?

# Approach Outline

Survey HRO principles (preoccupation with failure, reluctance to simplify, sensitivity to operations, commitment to resilience, deference to expertise). Operationalize each as a prompt, system design, or monitoring intervention for a 3-agent system. Test whether HRO-inspired operations reduce failure rates on 20 tasks.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

HRO practices are proven to reduce failure rates in complex sociotechnical systems. Direct translation to multi-agent AI operations could provide near-term practical safety improvements.

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: operationalize HRO principles as agent interventions -> test failure rate reduction. The gap is that HRO principles are organizational/cultural and may not straightforwardly translate to prompt-level or design-level AI interventions.
