# Research Question

To what extent does when a new jailbreak is discovered, its effectiveness across multiple major models is rarely systematically measured and tracked, making it impossible to monitor whether homogeneity is increasing the ?

# Approach Outline

Build a continuously updated dataset and dashboard tracking the transferability of published jailbreaks across major model versions, computing a 'systemic jailbreak risk score' as a function of cross-model success rates.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Adversarial homogeneity is the most immediately dangerous form of model monoculture. Systematic tracking would reveal whether the attack surface is growing.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling and critical: adversarial homogeneity is the most immediately dangerous form of model monoculture. Systematic tracking directly enables defenders to monitor the growing attack surface.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Testing jailbreaks across models raises significant ethical and terms-of-service concerns. Requires careful IRB-like oversight. Beginners may inadvertently violate API policies or create harmful outputs.

narrow_scope (2/5): A continuously updated dataset and dashboard is an ongoing operational commitment, not a one-time deliverable. No clear 30-hour milestone. Scoping to a snapshot would fundamentally change the project.

novelty (2/5): JailbreakBench (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks) already provides an open robustness benchmark with leaderboards tracking attack and defense performance across LLMs. JailBreakV-28K (COLM 2024) specifically evaluates transferability of jailbreak attacks with 28,000 test cases across 10+ models. 'Bag of Tricks: Benchmarking of Jailbreak Attacks on LLMs' (NeurIPS 2024) further benchmarks attacks. The core contribution — a dataset and tracker of jailbreak transferability — is substantially covered by these existing benchmarks. The remaining gap is a continuously updated dashboard, but the empirical methodology and cross-model testing are established.
