# Research Question

To what extent does instruction tuning (sft + rlhf) changes model behavior substantially. whether it also changes the polysemanticity of individual neurons or sae features—making the model more or less interpretable—has ?

# Approach Outline

Compare Llama-3.2-1B base vs Llama-3.2-1B Instruct. Train SAEs on the same layer of each. Use automated labeling to assign concept counts per feature. Compare distributions of features-per-concept and concepts-per-feature. Measure dead feature rates and L0 sparsity.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If instruction tuning increases polysemanticity, it implies that aligned models are harder to interpret than base models, which would be a concerning finding for scalable oversight.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if instruction tuning increases polysemanticity, aligned models are harder to interpret than base models. This is a direct concern for scalable oversight -- the models we deploy for safety are the ones hardest to understand. Each link is explicit.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires training SAEs on two model variants (base and instruct), running automated labeling pipeline via Neuronpedia, and comparing feature distributions. SAE training and feature labeling are specialized workflows that require substantial ramp-up for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): Two SAE training runs, automated labeling on both, distribution comparison. The analysis has multiple steps and the SAE training itself is a significant time investment. Achievable but sustained effort within 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): Several papers touch on this topic: 'Task-Specific Data Selection for Instruction Tuning via Monosemantic Neuronal Activations' (arXiv:2503.15573) uses SAE monosemanticity for instruction tuning data selection; 'Signal in the Noise: Polysemantic Interference Transfers and Predicts Cross-Model Influence' (arXiv:2505.11611) shows polysemantic patterns transfer from small to instruction-tuned models. However, a direct controlled comparison of SAE-measured polysemanticity between a base model and its instruction-tuned variant (same architecture, same SAE hyperparameters) has not been published as a standalone study. The ingredients exist but the specific experiment has not been done.
