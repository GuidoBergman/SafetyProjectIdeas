# Research Question

To what extent does a necessary step toward interpretability-based safety auditing is identifying the minimal circuit — the smallest set of model components — responsible for a safety-relevant binary decision (e.g., whet?

# Approach Outline

On GPT-2 Small with TransformerLens, focus on a binary task: whether the model completes a sentence with a gendered vs. gender-neutral pronoun. Apply iterative head and MLP ablation to find the minimal circuit that, when isolated, preserves the binary decision above 90% accuracy. Then test whether this minimal circuit also accounts for the model's behavior on a probe set of 50 novel pronoun-prediction prompts not used in circuit extraction.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Backcasting from a world where we can certify model behavior by verifying the circuit responsible for it: a working minimal circuit identification method that generalizes to novel inputs is a proof-of-concept for interpretability-based certification. Failing to generalize is an equally important finding.
