# Research Question

To what extent does backdoor research typically tests exact trigger tokens, but inductive backdoors show that models generalize triggers. the breadth of trigger generalization is not well characterized for safety-relevan?

# Approach Outline

Fine-tune a small open-source model with a backdoor using a specific trigger phrase. Then test semantic variants of the trigger (synonyms, paraphrases, translated versions, partial matches) to map the generalization boundary of backdoor activation. Measure whether safety-relevant backdoors generalize further than task-performance backdoors.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Broad generalization means backdoors are harder to detect by trigger-filtering, raising the threat level for covert malicious fine-tuning.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Direct chain: mapping trigger generalization boundaries informs whether trigger-filtering defenses are sufficient. If safety-relevant backdoors generalize more broadly, this raises the threat level for covert malicious fine-tuning and motivates different defense strategies.
