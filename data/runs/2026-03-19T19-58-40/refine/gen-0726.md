# Research Question

To what extent does incidental polysemanticity — where neurons encode multiple unrelated features due to training randomness rather than capacity pressure — means jailbreak-detection probes built on individual neuron act?

# Approach Outline

Train the same model architecture from multiple random seeds. For each seed, train jailbreak-detection probes and measure their false positive rates on benign out-of-distribution prompts. Show that polysemanticity variance across seeds correlates with probe reliability variance, using the OpenReview 'Incidental Polysemanticity' paper's methods.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If probe reliability is a function of training randomness rather than architecture, safety teams cannot guarantee probe performance without per-model recalibration — a significant scalability problem for deployed AI control.
