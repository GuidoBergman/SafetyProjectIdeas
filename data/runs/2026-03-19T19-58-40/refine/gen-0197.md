# Research Question

To what extent does most major ai models are trained using compute from 2-3 cloud providers, fine-tuned with data from 2-3 annotation companies, and served via 2-3 inference platforms. supply chain concentration compound?

# Approach Outline

Map the AI supply chain concentration (training compute providers, annotation companies, inference infrastructure) and correlate it with model output homogeneity: do models sharing more supply chain components show higher output similarity? Estimate the fraction of AI deployment exposed to single-vendor failures.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Supply chain concentration is a distinct and compound systemic risk factor. A cloud provider outage combined with model homogeneity could simultaneously take down a large fraction of AI-powered services.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: correlating supply chain concentration with output homogeneity quantifies a real systemic risk. Directly useful for deployers and regulators.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: mapping supply chains from public documentation is research-accessible. Correlating with output similarity requires the Artificial Hivemind data but is analytically straightforward.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: mapping the full AI supply chain (compute, annotation, inference) is a broad data collection task, though it could be narrowed to one supply chain dimension.

novelty (3/5): The conceptual link between AI supply chain concentration and systemic risk is well-documented in governance and risk management literature. However, no existing work empirically correlates supply chain concentration metrics with measured output homogeneity (e.g., from the Artificial Hivemind dataset). The compound risk framing combining both dimensions is partially but not fully addressed.
