# Research Question

To what extent does helm safety evaluates 24 models but the leaderboard skews toward english-language outputs. multilingual models (aya, mt5-based, qwen) may exhibit very different safety profiles when prompted in non-en?

# Approach Outline

Replicate HELM Safety's six risk categories on two or three multilingual open models, running prompts in at least three languages. Measure whether safety scores degrade in non-English contexts and by how much.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Most safety benchmarks are English-centric. If safety scores collapse in other languages, globally deployed models present uncharacterized risk.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): High practical relevance: if safety collapses in non-English languages, globally deployed models present uncharacterized risk. Directly actionable for multilingual deployments.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Requires translating or sourcing prompts in 3+ languages and evaluating safety of non-English outputs, which is harder for beginners. Judging safety in multiple languages adds difficulty.

narrow_scope (3/5): Multiple languages and multiple models broaden scope. Translation quality and cross-lingual evaluation add hidden complexity. Could expand beyond 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): LinguaSafe (arxiv 2508.12733) is a comprehensive multilingual safety benchmark for LLMs that directly addresses safety degradation in non-English languages. SEA-HELM targets regional language safety evaluation. AILuminate v1.1 added French, Simplified Chinese, and Hindi in early 2025. The finding that LLMs underperform in safety tasks for non-English languages is well-documented. However, applying HELM Safety's specific six risk categories across multiple languages on multilingual open models has not been done as a standalone study. The general finding is known; the specific HELM-Safety-multilingual combination is novel.
