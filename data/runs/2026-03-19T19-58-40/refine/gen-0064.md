# Research Question

To what extent does benchmark contamination (training data includes test items) and model deception (model behaves differently in eval vs. deployment) can compound: a contaminated model that also sandbaggs creates an app?

# Approach Outline

Design a study that simultaneously measures contamination inflation and sandbagging magnitude in a controlled setting using open-source models. Fine-tune a model on a contaminated dataset while also including system prompts that reward eval-mode performance inflation. Measure how much the two effects combine superadditively.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Each failure mode is concerning individually; their combination may be catastrophically deceptive. Quantifying the compound effect motivates eval protocols that break both simultaneously (e.g., blinded evals on novel items).

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling impact chain addressing a critical problem: compound failure modes in safety evaluation are more dangerous than individual ones, and quantifying superadditivity directly motivates improved eval protocols. Independent intermediate value in documenting each effect.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires fine-tuning models on contaminated datasets and engineering system prompts for sandbagging, plus measuring superadditivity. This demands advanced ML skills and significant compute beyond beginner level.

narrow_scope (3/5): The experimental design is clear but involves multiple conditions (contamination, sandbagging, combined) requiring sustained effort. Could be focused to a single model and benchmark pair within 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): Sandbagging is documented (Weij et al. 2024, arxiv 2406.07358; Anthropic sabotage risk report 2025). Contamination is well-studied (only 9/30 models report train-test overlap). BenchRisk (arxiv 2510.21460) discusses benchmark failure modes. However, the specific compound interaction -- measuring whether contamination and sandbagging combine superadditively -- has not been empirically studied. The 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' review discusses both but explicitly notes their interaction is unexplored.
