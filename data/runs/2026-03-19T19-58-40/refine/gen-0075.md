# Research Question

To what extent does flawed benchmark items (ambiguous, factually incorrect, or culturally biased questions) interact with model overconfidence to compound evaluation unreliability: a flawed item elicits wrong answers → o?

# Approach Outline

Use the Stanford statistical method for detecting flawed benchmark items (identifying questions where unusually many models fail) to identify flawed items in 3–5 safety benchmarks. For identified items, measure whether model confidence scores differ from non-flawed items in ways that compound miscalibration. Propose item repair or retirement criteria.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Stanford's research achieved 84% precision in finding flawed benchmark items. Combining flawed items with model overconfidence creates a compounding miscalibration effect that undermines benchmark validity at the item level.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain from item-level flaw detection to improved benchmark quality. However, the practical impact depends on benchmark maintainers acting on the findings, which is a gap.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Applying the Stanford method requires statistical skills but the method is published. Measuring model confidence is standard. Intermediate complexity overall with mentor guidance.

narrow_scope (3/5): Applying one detection method to multiple benchmarks and analyzing confidence interactions is sustained effort. Could be scoped to 1-2 benchmarks for 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): The Stanford benchmark bugs research (December 2025) found 5% of benchmarks have serious flaws and developed a statistics+AI framework achieving 84% precision in detecting flawed items. The methodology for detecting flawed items is established. Model confidence calibration is extensively studied (JMIR 2025, arxiv 2503.15850). Only 16% of benchmarks include uncertainty measures. However, the specific compound interaction -- measuring whether flawed items systematically interact with model overconfidence to produce compounding miscalibration -- adds some novelty beyond applying existing detection methods.
