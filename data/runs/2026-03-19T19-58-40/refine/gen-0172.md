# Research Question

To what extent does fine-tuning on domain data is known to cause catastrophic forgetting. if all models forget the same safety knowledge when domain-fine-tuned, every specialized deployment of ai will share the same safe?

# Approach Outline

Take 5+ base models, fine-tune them on a common domain dataset (medical or legal), and measure before-vs.-after changes in safety benchmark scores. Compute the correlation of forgetting patterns across models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Correlated catastrophic forgetting of safety knowledge would mean that the practice of fine-tuning AI for specialized deployment systematically degrades safety in a shared, predictable way.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if fine-tuning systematically degrades safety in a shared pattern, the common practice of domain specialization is actively making AI less safe in predictable ways.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Fine-tuning 5+ base models requires significant compute, training infrastructure knowledge, and experience with training pipelines. Medium compute may not suffice for fine-tuning large models.

narrow_scope (4/5): Well-defined experiment: fine-tune, measure before/after safety scores, compute cross-model correlation. Clear deliverable, though the fine-tuning step is the bottleneck.

novelty (3/5): Safety degradation from fine-tuning is extensively studied. 'Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety' (ICLR 2024) established that even benign fine-tuning degrades safety, and GPT-3.5 Turbo was jailbroken via 10 adversarial examples. BOOSTER (ICLR 2025), Lisa (NeurIPS 2024), and multiple mitigation approaches exist. 'Fine-Tuning Lowers Safety and Disrupts Evaluation' (2025) and 'Rethinking Safety in LLM Fine-tuning' (2025) further characterize the problem. However, the specific cross-model correlation angle — whether safety degrades in the same pattern across different base models when fine-tuned — has not been systematically studied. Existing work focuses on individual models rather than measuring whether catastrophic forgetting is homogeneous across model families.
