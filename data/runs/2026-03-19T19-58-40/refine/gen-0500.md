# Research Question

To what extent does internal representation probes (e.g., probing for 'deception direction' in activation space) are promising but not yet validated as reliable deception detectors. backcasting from success identifies pr?

# Approach Outline

Train linear probes on a small transformer's activation space to predict whether the model is in a 'deceptive' vs. 'honest' behavioral mode (using finetuned ground-truth deceptive models as positive examples). Evaluate probe reliability: false negative rate, false positive rate, cross-model transfer, robustness to paraphrasing. Identify what makes probes succeed or fail.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Probe-based deception detection is a central strategy in the field (OpenAI lie detector, representation engineering). Validating when it works and when it fails is essential before deploying it as a safety tool.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Validating when probes work and fail is essential before deploying them as safety tools. Directly informs whether the probe-based detection strategy is viable.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Training linear probes on activation space requires understanding transformer internals and using libraries like TransformerLens. Extracting activations and training probes is intermediate ML work. Cross-model transfer testing adds complexity. Not beginner-level.

narrow_scope (3/5): Core experiment (train probe, measure error rates) is focused. Cross-model transfer and paraphrasing robustness add breadth. Could be narrowed to one model with basic error rates.

novelty (3/5): Goldowsky-Dill et al. (2025) 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' directly trains linear probes on activation space to predict deceptive vs. honest modes, achieving 0.96-0.999 AUROC with 95-99% catch rate at 1% FPR. The paper explicitly evaluates false negative/positive rates, robustness to paraphrasing, and notes probes may detect 'deception-related text' rather than actual deception. 'Benchmarking Deception Probes via Black-to-White Performance Boosts' (2025) further validates probe reliability. Cross-model transfer is partially addressed. The core proposal is largely covered by existing work.
