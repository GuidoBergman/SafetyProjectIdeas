# Research Question

To what extent does fli ai safety index focuses on commercial labs. academic institutions training and deploying capable ai models are entirely unassessed by existing governance indexes?

# Approach Outline

Identify 15–20 academic labs known to train large models (university AI centers, national labs). Adapt the FLI methodology for non-commercial entities. Score on available public documentation: IRB processes, model release policies, evaluation practices.

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Academic labs train graduate students who will populate industry; their governance norms have long-run effects on the field's culture.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: academic lab governance norms have long-run cultural effects, but the causal chain from an index to improved safety outcomes is indirect and speculative.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: adapting commercial governance methodology for non-commercial entities requires governance domain knowledge and judgment about what indicators to replace.

narrow_scope (2/5): No clear milestone: 15-20 academic labs with adapted methodology is too broad for 30 hours. The diversity of academic governance structures makes systematic comparison very labor-intensive.

novelty (4/5): The FLI AI Safety Index and related indices focus exclusively on commercial frontier AI labs. No published work has systematically assessed governance and safety practices at academic AI labs. University AI governance discussions focus primarily on teaching policies (generative AI use in coursework) rather than research lab safety practices. The AAUP published guidance on AI and academic professions but focuses on employment and teaching, not research safety governance. The G7 Hiroshima AI Process Reporting Framework targets organizations developing advanced AI systems but participation is voluntary and commercial-focused. The gap — applying governance index methodology to academic labs like Stanford HAI, MIT CSAIL, Berkeley BAIR, etc. — is genuine and novel. However, the scope of 15-20 labs with adapted methodology is very broad for 30 hours.
