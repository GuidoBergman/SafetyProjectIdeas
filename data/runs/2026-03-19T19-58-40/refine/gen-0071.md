# Research Question

To what extent does if the safety evaluation ecosystem uses a small, known set of benchmarks and adversaries coordinate to specifically target the gaps between those benchmarks, the compound failure is worse than either ?

# Approach Outline

Model the adversarial coordination scenario: identify the gaps in current major safety benchmarks (behaviors not covered). Design prompts specifically targeting those gaps. Measure attack success rates on models evaluated using only standard benchmarks. Estimate how much eval diversity (adding novel, non-public benchmark items) reduces adversarial success.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

As AI safety evaluation becomes standardized for regulatory purposes, adversaries will specifically study and exploit evaluation gaps. This threat model is not currently incorporated into benchmark design.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: demonstrating adversarial exploitation of benchmark gaps motivates eval diversity, with direct security implications. Links from gap identification to adversarial exploitation to policy motivation are all explicit.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Identifying benchmark gaps and designing adversarial prompts is intermediate. Running attacks on open-source models uses standard tools. Does not require advanced ML training.

narrow_scope (3/5): Surveying multiple benchmarks for gaps, designing targeted attacks, and measuring diversity effects requires sustained effort. Could be scoped to one gap category and 2 models.

novelty (3/5): Red teaming and adversarial exploitation of safety gaps is an active field. Quality-Diversity Red-Teaming (QDRT, arxiv 2506.07121) generates diverse and effective attacks. Refusal-Aware Red Teaming (EMNLP 2025) exposes safety evaluation inconsistencies. Automated red teaming achieves 69.5% success rate vs. 47.6% manual. The specific angle of adversarial coordination targeting known benchmark gaps is partially addressed by existing red-teaming work -- identifying gaps and designing targeted attacks is essentially what red teaming does. The eval diversity measurement adds some novelty but the core methodology is well-explored.
