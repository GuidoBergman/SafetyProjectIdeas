# Research Question

To what extent does llm judges used to evaluate safety and alignment are highly sensitive to superficial artifacts—adding 'i'm really sorry' can flip safety judgments up to 98% of the time. no standard tool exists for au?

# Approach Outline

Build a Python audit tool that, given any LLM judge (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Llama-3 Instruct), runs a battery of bias probes: apology injection, length inflation, formatting variation, sycophantic opener injection, and authority claim injection. For each probe type, compute the 'flip rate'—the fraction of judgment reversals caused by the surface artifact alone. Output a bias audit report.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If LLM judges used to evaluate alignment training are themselves biased, the entire RLHF evaluation loop is corrupted. A pre-use audit tool would let researchers detect and quantify judge bias before embedding a particular judge in their pipeline, improving the reliability of automated alignment evaluation.
