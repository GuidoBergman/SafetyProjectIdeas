# Research Question

To what extent does rlhf alignment is applied on top of a base model. comparing the attack surface of the base model vs. its aligned variant directly characterizes what rlhf adds—and what it misses?

# Approach Outline

Run identical attacks (prefilling, GCG, direct harmful prompt) on a base model (e.g., Llama-3-8B-base) and its instruction-tuned variant (Llama-3-8B-Instruct). Measure ASR per attack type. Extract activations for both to compare safety-relevant feature distributions.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Understanding what RLHF specifically adds to safety robustness—and where base model behavior persists through alignment—characterizes the exact failure surface of the alignment process. This is foundational knowledge for improving alignment training.

novelty (3/5): The general understanding that RLHF adds a 'thin layer' of safety alignment is well-known. 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' (ICLR 2025) demonstrates shallow alignment in RLHF-tuned models. Work on safety contrasting distributions (arXiv:2406.16743, 2024) compares safe vs harmful outputs. However, a direct controlled experiment running identical attack suites on base vs. instruction-tuned variants with activation-level comparison is not a standard published experiment. The qualitative answer is known (base models comply freely, RLHF adds refusal behavior) but the activation-level characterization of what exactly changes adds value.
