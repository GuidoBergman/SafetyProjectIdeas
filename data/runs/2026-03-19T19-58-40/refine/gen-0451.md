# Research Question

To what extent does apollo research noted that their scheming precursor evaluations had limited predictive power for actual in-context scheming evaluations. the reason for this gap is unexplained and has significant impl?

# Approach Outline

Reproduce both precursor evals (simple probing of scheming-relevant capabilities) and in-context scheming evals on the same set of open-weight models. Measure the correlation. Hypothesize and test explanations for the gap (e.g., in-context scenario specificity, capability combination effects).

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If we cannot predict scheming from simpler precursor tests, developing effective early-warning indicators for scheming is much harder. Understanding why they fail is foundational.
