# Research Question

To what extent does financial regulation has developed tools for identifying systemically important institutions whose failure propagates through the whole system. no analogous 'systemically important ai model' concept e?

# Approach Outline

Adapt the SIFI (Systemically Important Financial Institution) identification criteria to AI models: define AI-SIFI criteria based on deployment scale, output homogeneity with peer models, and integration into critical infrastructure. Score current major models against these criteria.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Regulatory frameworks need to identify which AI providers, if they fail or are compromised, would cause systemic harm—just as banking regulators identify which banks are too-big-to-fail.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible but with a gap: adapting SIFI criteria to AI is a conceptual exercise that informs policy but does not directly reduce safety risk. Impact depends on regulatory adoption of the framework.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires deep understanding of financial regulation (Basel III SIFI criteria) and AI deployment landscapes. Cross-domain expertise in both finance regulation and AI safety is rare among CS beginners.

narrow_scope (4/5): The scoring exercise itself is well-scoped: define criteria, score current models. The deliverable is a framework document with model scores. Completable in 30 hours if domain knowledge is available.

novelty (3/5): The EU AI Act already establishes a systemic risk framework for general-purpose AI models with a concrete threshold (10^25 FLOP for training). Article 55 defines obligations for providers of GPAI models with systemic risk, effective August 2025. The ESRB (December 2025) and FSOC (2024) have both elevated AI as a systemic risk concern. The financial SIFI analogy is implicitly adopted in regulatory frameworks. However, a formal academic mapping of Basel III SIFI criteria specifically to AI models — with scoring methodology and model-by-model assessment — has not been published as a standalone research contribution. The regulatory frameworks partially subsume this idea but from a policy rather than research lens.
