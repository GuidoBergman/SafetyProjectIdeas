# Research Question

To what extent does proponents of lighter governance argue that stringent regulation reduces innovation. this claim is empirically testable using cross-country variation in governance stringency and ai innovation output?

# Approach Outline

Build a regulatory stringency index for AI governance across 10–15 jurisdictions (using public governance documentation and expert coding). Correlate with AI innovation output metrics (patent filings, paper publications, startup formation, compute investment) from the same jurisdictions. Test whether stringency negatively predicts innovation.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If the regulation-innovation tradeoff is empirically weak or absent, the economic argument against mandatory governance standards loses its empirical basis.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: correlation between regulation and innovation across countries faces severe confounding (economic development, talent pools, investment climate). Establishing a clean causal signal is very difficult.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Advanced: cross-country econometric analysis with confounders requires graduate-level methodology. Building a regulatory stringency index across 10-15 jurisdictions demands governance expertise.

narrow_scope (2/5): No milestone: building a regulatory stringency index, collecting multi-country innovation metrics, and running controlled regressions is a full research paper's worth of work, exceeding 30 hours.

novelty (2/5): This topic is extensively studied. 'Balancing the tradeoff between regulation and innovation for artificial intelligence' (Technology in Society, 2024) directly models the regulation-innovation tradeoff using game theory. The IMF's 2024 working paper 'The Economic Impacts and the Regulation of AI' reviews the literature comprehensively. Cross-country comparative analyses exist (EU, US, UK, China in arXiv:2503.05773). The European Commission's AI Act impact assessment quantifies compliance costs (avg €29,277/product). The AI Maturity Matrix (Nov 2024) compares economies on AI readiness. While the specific construction of a 'regulatory stringency index' for AI is novel, the broader question and methodology are well-covered.
