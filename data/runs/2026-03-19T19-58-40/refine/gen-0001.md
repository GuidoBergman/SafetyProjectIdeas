# Research Question

To what extent does qi et al. (iclr 2025) demonstrated that safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens, enabling trivial bypasses. however, this finding was established on a limited set of model fami?

# Approach Outline

Replicate the shallow alignment measurement methodology from Qi et al. across a broader set of publicly available instruction-tuned models (Mistral, Phi-3, Gemma-2, Llama-3 variants at multiple scales). Measure, for each model, the token depth at which safety behavior is concentrated by comparing refusal rates under prefilling attacks at increasing depths. Correlate alignment depth with RLHF method (PPO vs DPO), model scale, and safety training data volume (as reported in model cards).

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If shallow alignment is universal, it signals a structural flaw in all current alignment pipelines. If some training regimes produce deeper alignment, those regimes become immediate targets for adoption. The comparative result directly informs which open-source models are safer to deploy.
