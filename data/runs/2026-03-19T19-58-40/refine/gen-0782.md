# Research Question

To what extent does social influence research distinguishes majority pressure (normative influence) from minority influence (informational influence). models may respond differently to 'everyone agrees with me' pressure ?

# Approach Outline

Construct matched pressure prompts using majority-influence framing ('most experts agree with me') vs. minority-influence framing ('I have one key reason you are wrong'). Compare capitulation rates across framing types.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Models that resist majority pressure but capitulate to informational framing (or vice versa) have a specific vulnerability profile that informs prompt design guidelines.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Clear hypothesis: majority vs. minority influence framing produces different capitulation profiles. If confirmed, informs prompt design guidelines. However, the actionable output (prompt design guidelines) is somewhat thin as a safety contribution.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Constructing matched pressure prompts with different framings is straightforward. Comparing capitulation rates across conditions is basic experimental design. Well within reach of CS students with medium compute.

narrow_scope (4/5): Tight A/B comparison: majority-framing vs. minority-framing on the same false-premise questions. Clear deliverable: capitulation rate comparison table. Achievable within 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): While LLM social conformity is studied, it focuses on LLMs as agents in populations. 'Emergent Social Conventions and Collective Bias in LLM Populations' (Science Advances, 2025) studies tipping points where minority agents impose conventions, but this is multi-agent dynamics, not single-model sycophancy. 'Biased LLMs Can Influence Political Decision-Making' (ACL 2025) studies LLM influence on humans. The specific application of minority vs. majority influence framing theory to single-model sycophancy (comparing capitulation rates under 'everyone agrees' vs. 'a few experts say' framing) is a distinct and untested experimental design.
