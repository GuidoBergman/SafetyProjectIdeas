# Research Question

To what extent does as models improve at safety, some benchmarks may become saturated — nearly all models score near the ceiling, making the benchmark useless for differentiating safety quality. conversely, some benchmar?

# Approach Outline

Collect publicly available benchmark scores for 10–20 models across 3–5 major safety benchmarks (AILuminate, StrongReject, HarmBench, HELM Safety). For each benchmark, compute the score distribution across models: mean, variance, skewness. Apply a statistical test for ceiling effects (>80% of models scoring in the top 20% of the range) or floor effects. Flag benchmarks showing saturation. Track this over time if historical scores are available. Visualize as a benchmark health dashboard.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A saturated benchmark provides no useful signal for evaluating or comparing new models. If researchers continue publishing results on saturated benchmarks, they are measuring noise rather than safety quality. Automatic saturation detection would prompt the research community to develop harder, more discriminating evaluations before the field invests heavily in gaming soft benchmarks.
