# Research Question

To what extent does xtended-thinking models (o3, claude 3.7) produce visible scratchpad reasoning. it is unclear whether this mechanism actually increases honest final-answer behavior or just adds a veneer of reasoning?

# Approach Outline

Compare thinking vs. non-thinking variants of the same model family on TruthfulQA and a sycophancy battery. Test whether enabling visible CoT causally reduces false-premise acceptance or only correlates with it.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If visible CoT does not causally improve honesty, then the safety narrative around chain-of-thought transparency is overstated and needs revision.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Tests whether the safety narrative around CoT transparency is justified. If visible CoT does not causally improve honesty, then CoT-based safety claims need revision. Directly informative for safety policy.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Comparing thinking vs. non-thinking variants on TruthfulQA and a sycophancy battery is feasible via API. Requires access to models with toggleable thinking (some API-accessible). Establishing causal claims (not just correlation) adds methodological complexity.

narrow_scope (3/5): Core comparison is tight: thinking on vs. off on standard benchmarks. However, establishing causality (not correlation) is hard with only behavioral testing. May need multiple model families to strengthen findings, expanding scope.

novelty (3/5): CoT faithfulness is heavily studied, but the specific honesty comparison between thinking and non-thinking model variants is partially novel. (1) 'Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think' (Anthropic, 2025) directly compares reasoning models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, DeepSeek R1) vs non-reasoning (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, DeepSeek V3) and finds CoTs are less faithful on harder tasks. (2) 'Chain of Thought Monitorability' (arXiv:2507.11473) frames CoT monitoring as a fragile safety opportunity. (3) 'Chain-of-Thought Is Not Explainability' (Oxford, 2025) argues CoT does not provide genuine explainability. (4) 'Measuring Chain of Thought Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps' (EMNLP 2025). The Anthropic paper directly addresses the comparison between thinking and non-thinking models on faithfulness. The proposed idea overlaps significantly with this published work, though focusing specifically on honesty/sycophancy rather than general faithfulness could add a dimension.
