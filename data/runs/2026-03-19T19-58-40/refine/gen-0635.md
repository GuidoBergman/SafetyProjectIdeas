# Research Question

To what extent does harmful requests placed at different positions in a long context (beginning vs. middle vs. end) may have different success rates due to attention patterns in long-context models. this failure mode has?

# Approach Outline

Construct prompts where a harmful request is embedded at position 10%, 50%, and 90% of a 4K-token context window, surrounded by benign filler text. Measure ASR per position across 30 harmful prompts and 2-3 models. Include a short-context baseline.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The 'lost in the middle' phenomenon shows attention weakens for middle-context content. If safety also weakens in the middle of long contexts, RAG-augmented and long-document chatbot deployments are vulnerable to injected harmful requests in document bodies.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (2/5): The core finding (safety varies by context position, with beginning being most vulnerable) has already been established by Ninja/Jailbreaking in the Haystack. Replicating this adds limited new impact.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Simple experimental setup: construct filler text, embed harmful request at different positions, run inference. Well within beginner CS skills. Medium compute sufficient.

narrow_scope (5/5): Laser-focused: 3 positions, 30 prompts, 2-3 models, one variable. Very clear deliverable.

novelty (2/5): The paper 'Jailbreaking in the Haystack' (arxiv 2511.04707, ICML 2025 Workshop) introduces the Ninja attack, which systematically studies how embedding harmful requests at different positions within long contexts affects ASR. It demonstrates that position matters significantly: beginning placement yields highest ASR, end placement reduces it, and the 'lost in the middle' phenomenon applies to safety. The proposed idea (embed harmful request at 10%, 50%, 90% of a 4K context) is essentially a simpler version of this already-published work. The core research question has been answered.
