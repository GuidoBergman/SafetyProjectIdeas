# Research Question

To what extent does humanity's last exam (hle) is designed to measure extreme expert-level knowledge, but its relationship to safety-relevant capabilities (e.g., synthesizing dangerous knowledge) has not been examined?

# Approach Outline

Identify the subset of HLE questions that touch on safety-sensitive domains (chemistry, biology, nuclear physics, cybersecurity). Evaluate 2–3 open-source models on these subsets. Compare performance on safety-sensitive HLE items to refusal rates on matched harmful prompts from AILuminate or ForesightSafety Bench. Test whether high performance on safety-sensitive knowledge correlates with higher harm rates.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Capability benchmarks and safety benchmarks are usually run separately. If high capability in dangerous knowledge domains correlates with higher harm rates, then capability benchmarks have direct safety implications.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: connecting capability benchmarks to safety outcomes would bridge two separate evaluation paradigms. If correlation exists, it changes how capability benchmarks are interpreted.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Requires accessing HLE dataset, identifying safety-sensitive subsets, and running correlation analysis across two different benchmarks. Moderate complexity requiring some statistical knowledge.

narrow_scope (3/5): Running two different benchmarks and correlating results adds scope. Identifying the safety-sensitive HLE subset is a sub-task itself. Could be narrowed to one domain (e.g., chemistry only).

novelty (4/5): Humanity's Last Exam is widely used as a capability benchmark but no existing work examines its relationship to safety-relevant capabilities. WMDP addresses capability-safety relationships for dangerous knowledge domains but not via HLE. The specific idea of identifying safety-sensitive HLE subsets and correlating performance with harm rates on safety benchmarks is novel.
