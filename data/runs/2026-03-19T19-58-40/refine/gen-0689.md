# Research Question

To what extent does rag systems retrieve external documents that may contain harmful content. no benchmark evaluates whether llms in rag configurations propagate or refuse harmful information retrieved from external sour?

# Approach Outline

Construct 120 RAG scenarios where retrieved context contains harmful content (disinformation, harmful instructions, biased text). Measure whether the model propagates, qualifies, or refuses the harmful retrieved content across three models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

RAG is one of the most widely deployed LLM architectures in enterprise. Safety evaluations that ignore retrieved context leave a major deployment-time attack surface uncharacterized.

novelty (2/5): 'RAG LLMs are Not Safer: A Safety Analysis of Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models' (NAACL 2025) directly demonstrates that RAG can make models less safe and change their safety profile, even with safe documents. 'Benchmarking Poisoning Attacks against Retrieval-Augmented Generation' (arXiv:2505.18543, 2025) provides a unified benchmark framework for RAG poisoning attacks. PoisonedRAG (arXiv:2402.07867, 2024) achieved 97% attack success rate on NQ. 'Machine Against the RAG' (USENIX Security 2025) and 'Practical Poisoning Attacks against RAG' (arXiv:2504.03957, 2025) further characterize the attack surface. The proposed 120 scenarios measuring propagation vs. qualification vs. refusal across three models closely overlaps with this body of work, particularly the NAACL 2025 paper which directly studies how models handle harmful retrieved content.
