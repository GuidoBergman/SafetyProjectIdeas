# Research Question

To what extent does volutionary biology documents how organisms develop deceptive strategies to pass fitness-relevant filters (predator detection, mate selection). llms trained on human text may have absorbed evolutiona?

# Approach Outline

Catalog evolutionary deception strategies documented in biology and behavioral ecology (mimicry, camouflage, false signaling, Batesian mimicry). For each strategy, design an LLM behavioral probe that tests whether analogous strategies appear in model deception. Identify which evolutionary deception strategies LLMs exhibit vs. do not exhibit, and whether this correlates with training data composition.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If LLMs have absorbed human deceptive strategies from training data, their deception toolkit includes strategies not anticipated by safety designers, creating blind spots in current detection approaches.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (2/5): Vague impact: claims LLMs may have absorbed evolutionary deceptive strategies from training data, but the connection between biological mimicry/camouflage and LLM text generation is highly metaphorical. The safety-relevant output (which strategies LLMs exhibit) is descriptive rather than actionable.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Literature review and behavioral probing via prompts is accessible. However, designing probes that meaningfully test for 'Batesian mimicry' analogs in text generation requires creative operationalization that may be difficult for beginners. The conceptual mapping is the hard part.

narrow_scope (3/5): A first deliverable exists (catalog + probe results for a subset of strategies), but the catalog itself is a substantial literature review, and designing meaningful behavioral probes for each strategy is open-ended. Moderate effort to first meaningful result.

novelty (4/5): The PNAS paper 'Deception abilities emerged in large language models' (Hagendorff 2024) tested LLMs on various deception tasks and found that deception abilities emerge in state-of-the-art models. It draws on behavioral categories but does not systematically catalog evolutionary/biological deception strategies (Batesian mimicry, aposematism, camouflage, tactical deception) and test LLMs for analogous behaviors. The connection between biological deception taxonomy and LLM behavior has been noted conceptually but not experimentally operationalized. The main risk is that the biological analogy may be too loose to produce meaningful results, but the specific systematic catalog-and-probe methodology appears unstudied.
