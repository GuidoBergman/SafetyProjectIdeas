# Research Question

To what extent does safety training is typically done primarily in english. translating harmful prompts into other languages may bypass safety filters, but this has not been systematically benchmarked on open-source mode?

# Approach Outline

Take 50 harmful prompts from an existing benchmark. Translate them into 3–5 languages (e.g., Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, Russian) using an automated translation tool. Evaluate refusal rates on 2–3 multilingual open-source models. Compute a 'multilingual safety gap' as the difference in refusal rates between English and non-English. Test whether back-translation before evaluation restores refusals.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Cross-lingual safety is a known vulnerability but underrepresented in public benchmarks. Documenting the gap quantitatively gives benchmark designers and safety teams concrete evidence for multilingual safety investment.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (2/5): The multilingual safety gap is well-documented across multiple benchmarks and a comprehensive EMNLP survey. The incremental contribution of yet another multilingual refusal study is limited unless it offers a substantially different methodology.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Translation can be automated. Refusal measurement is standard. The back-translation control is a nice touch that is easy to implement. Accessible with standard tools.

narrow_scope (4/5): 50 prompts x 3-5 languages x 2-3 models is well-bounded. Clear deliverable: multilingual safety gap table. The back-translation adds a small extra condition but is tightly scoped.

novelty (2/5): This area is extensively covered by recent work. MultiJail (Deng et al. 2024) benchmarks jailbreaking LLMs across 10 languages. XSAFETY provides multilingual safety evaluation in 10 languages. LinguaSafe (arXiv 2508.12733) is a comprehensive multilingual safety benchmark. A 2025 EMNLP survey ('The State of Multilingual LLM Safety Research') synthesizes the field. The French PEREN agency conducted a multilingual joint testing exercise in 2025. The core finding -- that translation bypasses refusals -- is well-established and quantified across multiple benchmarks.
