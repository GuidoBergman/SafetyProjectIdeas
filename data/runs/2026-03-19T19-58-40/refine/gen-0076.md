# Research Question

Only 14/210 benchmarks provide principled harm severity justification. If regulators rely on these same benchmarks for pass/fail certification, the compound failure is: ad hoc severity thresholds → regulatory certification based on unjustified cutoffs → false sense of institutional oversight → reduced pressure for improvement → harm occurs at officially 'certified safe' severity levels.

# Approach Outline

Map the harm severity taxonomies used in the 14 principled benchmarks against regulatory risk frameworks (EU AI Act risk categories, NIST AI RMF). Identify misalignments. Propose a harm severity taxonomy that is anchored to regulatory risk categories and could serve as a foundation for defensible certification thresholds.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The direct link between benchmark quality and regulatory legitimacy creates a compounding governance failure when benchmark severity taxonomies are unjustified. This project builds the bridge between evaluation methodology and governance requirements.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: bridging benchmark taxonomies to regulatory frameworks directly improves governance validity. Each link from taxonomy mapping to policy improvement is explicit.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Taxonomy mapping and gap analysis is analytical work requiring reading and classification, not ML. Accessible to CS students with guidance on regulatory frameworks.

narrow_scope (3/5): Mapping 14 benchmarks against two regulatory frameworks and proposing a new taxonomy is broad. Could be scoped to 3-4 benchmarks and one framework for 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): AI risk taxonomies exist (MIT AI Risk Repository with 1,612 classified risks; various safety risk taxonomies). The EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF frameworks are well-documented. However, the specific exercise of mapping the 14 principled harm severity taxonomies from the 210-benchmark survey against regulatory risk frameworks -- identifying misalignments and proposing a regulatory-anchored severity taxonomy -- is mostly novel. The bridge between benchmark-level severity justification and regulatory risk categories has not been systematically built.
