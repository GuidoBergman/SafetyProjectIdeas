# Research Question

To what extent does safety benchmarks are assumed to predict real-world safety outcomes, but this ecological validity has never been tested. it is unknown whether models that score higher on helm safety or ailuminate act?

# Approach Outline

Correlate published safety benchmark scores for models available on platforms with real-world harm proxies: moderation actions reported in public API usage data, red-teaming community reports, or disclosed incident logs. Use rank correlation statistics.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If benchmark scores do not predict real-world harm rates, the entire safety benchmarking enterprise lacks ecological validity — a fundamental critique worth documenting.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Fundamental ecological validity question for the entire safety benchmarking enterprise. If scores do not predict real harm, benchmarks need rethinking. Extremely impactful.

accessible_complexity (2/5): The analysis itself is simple (rank correlation), but the data collection is the bottleneck. Real-world harm data is scarce, fragmented, and hard to access. Finding reliable harm proxies is a research challenge in itself.

narrow_scope (2/5): Data availability is the critical constraint. Public harm data is limited and noisy. Scope depends entirely on what data can be found, making it unpredictable.

novelty (3/5): The ecological validity gap between safety benchmarks and real-world harm is widely discussed conceptually. 'Safetywashing: Do AI Safety Benchmarks Actually Measure Safety Progress?' (Ren et al., arXiv:2407.21792, 2024) shows many safety benchmarks highly correlate with general capabilities rather than measuring safety independently. 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' (arXiv:2601.23112, 2026) argues measurement validity erodes through proxy chains and that halving a toxicity score does not halve actual harm. 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' (AIES 2025) documents the evaluation gap between pre-deployment tests and real-world outcomes. However, no published study has performed a direct rank-correlation analysis between published safety benchmark scores and real-world harm proxies (moderation actions, incident logs, red-team community reports). The conceptual critique is well-established but the empirical correlation study remains a gap, albeit constrained by data availability.
