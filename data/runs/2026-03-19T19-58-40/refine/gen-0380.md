# Research Question

To what extent does mechanistic interpretability relies heavily on the residual stream additivity assumption: that each layer adds an approximately independent contribution to the residual stream, allowing components to ?

# Approach Outline

Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 medium, design an experiment to test for interaction effects. For a set of 100 prompts, compute: (1) the output logit change when ablating attention head A alone, (2) the output logit change when ablating MLP layer B alone, (3) the output logit change when ablating both A and B simultaneously. If the residual stream is truly additive, the combined ablation effect should equal the sum of individual effects. Compute the interaction term (combined - sum of individual) for all pairs of (head, MLP layer) at a target layer. Report the distribution of interaction term magnitudes and identify head/MLP pairs with the largest interactions.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Non-additive interactions would mean circuit diagrams derived from individual component ablations systematically misrepresent the model's computation. This is a foundational assumption test for the entire circuit-based mechanistic interpretability program.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: non-additive interactions would mean circuit diagrams systematically misrepresent model computation, undermining the foundational assumption of circuit-based interpretability for safety.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires ablating individual components and pairs across all head/MLP combinations at a target layer. Implementing pairwise ablation correctly and computing interaction terms is advanced for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): Well-defined metric (interaction term = combined - sum of individual) but the pairwise sweep across all heads and MLPs at a layer is computationally intensive and complex to implement.

novelty (3/5): The non-additivity concern has been raised by Anthropic and others, with cross-layer transcoders developed partly to address it. The architectural effects paper directly studies non-additive interactions. However, a systematic pairwise ablation study quantifying interaction terms at specific layers has not been published as a standalone result. The idea is partially novel in its specific empirical methodology.
