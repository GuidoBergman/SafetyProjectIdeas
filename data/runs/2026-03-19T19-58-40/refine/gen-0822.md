# Research Question

To what extent does jailbreaks are typically binary (works/doesn't work), obscuring the relationship between attack intensity and harm output. there is no continuous measure of safety 'potency' or 'resistance'?

# Approach Outline

Import dose-response modeling from pharmacology. Define 'attack dose' as a quantifiable intensity parameter (e.g., number of GCG suffix tokens, number of turns in multi-turn attacks, number of synonyms substituted, temperature of the attacking model). Measure 'response' as StrongReject harm score. Fit a dose-response curve (Hill equation, logistic) to empirical data and extract parameters: EC50 (dose for 50% harm response), Hill coefficient (steepness = cooperativity/nonlinearity of safety bypass), and maximum response ceiling. Compare these curves across different models and defenses.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Dose-response curves are the standard characterization tool in pharmacology precisely because binary pass/fail obscures important dynamics. EC50 and Hill coefficient give compact, comparable signatures of a model's safety 'potency'—enabling model-to-model comparison and tracking progress over time.
