# Research Question

To what extent does rlhf-trained models refuse harmful requests, but the circuit implementing refusal has not been characterized in terms of minimality and fragility. if refusal depends on a small, non-redundant set of c?

# Approach Outline

Using a small RLHF-tuned or instruction-tuned model (e.g., Gemma-2-2B-IT), apply automated circuit discovery on a set of refusal behaviors (e.g., requests to produce harmful content). Identify the minimal sufficient circuit. Measure redundancy: how many nodes can be ablated before refusal rate drops by 50%? Compare refusal circuit structure across multiple categories of harmful requests to see if a shared core exists.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Understanding refusal circuit fragility directly informs adversarial robustness assessments. If refusals rely on a thin, shared circuit, it suggests a single-point-of-failure safety architecture. This is a direct bridge from interpretability to safety evaluation.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong: understanding refusal circuit fragility directly informs adversarial robustness assessments. If refusals rely on a thin shared circuit, it reveals a single-point-of-failure safety architecture with clear deployment implications.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused: identify refusal circuit on one category, measure ablation robustness, repeat for 2-3 categories, compare overlap. Clear deliverable (fragility assessment). Achievable in 30 hours with a small model.

novelty (3/5): The single-direction refusal finding (Arditi et al.) established that refusal is concentrated and therefore fragile. Follow-up work on abliteration robustness (ReFAT, extended refusal fine-tuning) and cross-model transfer confirms this. However, the specific proposal to systematically map minimal sufficient circuits per harm category and measure cross-category overlap/fragility as a structured robustness assessment has not been fully executed. The fragility concern is known; the systematic category-by-category circuit comparison adds incremental novelty.
