# Research Question

To what extent does safety benchmark results may be unstable when evaluated on random subsets of the full dataset, raising questions about how many test items are actually needed for reliable safety claims?

# Approach Outline

Take a full safety benchmark dataset (e.g., a subset of AILuminate with 1000+ items). Evaluate 2–3 models on the full set and on random subsamples of 50, 100, 200, 500 items. Compute variance of safety scores across 10 random subsamples per size. Identify the minimum subsample size at which variance falls below a defined threshold.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Resource-constrained evaluators often cannot run the full benchmark. Knowing the minimum reliable sample size enables low-cost but statistically valid safety assessments.
