# Research Question

To what extent does the prisma paper found that cls tokens in vits require more sae features in deeper layers while spatial patch tokens show declining feature utilization—suggesting information concentrates in cls as th?

# Approach Outline

Using ViT-Prisma, load pre-trained SAEs for CLIP-B/32 at early (layers 2–4), middle (layers 6–8), and late (layers 10–12) positions, for both CLS and spatial patch tokens separately. For each group, compute: (1) mean L0 sparsity, (2) semantic coherence of top-20 features using embedding-based clustering of the top-activating images, (3) the fraction of features that are 'class-specific' (activate strongly on one ImageNet class) vs. 'multi-class' (activate across many classes). Plot how these metrics evolve across layers for CLS vs. spatial tokens to characterize where interpretability degrades.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Classification decisions in ViTs are made primarily via the CLS token at the final layer—the most compressed and potentially the least interpretable point. If that is exactly where SAE features become most polysemantic, then vision SAE interpretability is weakest precisely where it matters most for understanding model decisions.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with a gap: showing that CLS token SAE features become less interpretable at classification-relevant layers is informative but the path to a safety intervention is indirect.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Uses pre-trained ViT-Prisma SAEs (no training needed). Requires computing sparsity, coherence, and class-specificity metrics across layers and token types. Intermediate.

narrow_scope (3/5): Three metrics across three layer groups and two token types (CLS vs. spatial). Sustained effort but well-structured. Feasible in 30 hours with guidance.

novelty (3/5): Multiple recent works have documented the CLS token vs. spatial token divergence in vision SAEs, including sparsity differences, class-token feature frequency by layer, and the compositional nature of CLS features in deep layers. The specific 'bottleneck failure' framing and systematic three-metric characterization adds modest novelty, but the core observations are already established.
