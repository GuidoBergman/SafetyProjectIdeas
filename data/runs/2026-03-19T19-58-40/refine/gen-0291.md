# Research Question

To what extent does fda's medwatch system collects post-market adverse event reports for medical devices and drugs. ai systems have no equivalent systematic adverse event reporting infrastructure?

# Approach Outline

Analyze MedWatch's technical design: report fields, classification system, aggregation and signal detection methods. Design an analogous system for AI adverse events. Prototype the data collection schema and signal detection algorithm using simulated incident data from the AIAAIC database.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Post-market surveillance is how pharmaceutical governance catches failures that pre-market testing misses; AI governance without an equivalent is structurally incomplete.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: post-market surveillance is a structurally missing governance component; designing an AI analog with a prototype data schema directly addresses this gap.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: analyzing MedWatch's design and translating it to AI is a structured analytical task, manageable with guidance but requires understanding two domains.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: analyzing MedWatch, designing a schema, and prototyping signal detection with simulated data spans multiple components but has clear milestones.

novelty (3/5): The FDA has active research on post-market monitoring of AI-enabled medical devices (OSEL program). Nature published 'A general framework for governing marketed AI/ML medical devices' (npj Digital Medicine, 2025). PMC published 'Methodology for Conducting Post-Marketing Surveillance of Software as a Medical Device Based on AI Technologies' (2023). The FDA Digital Health Advisory Committee discussed a central database for generative AI monitoring. The Paragon Institute published on targeted post-market surveillance for AI. However, these focus on AI within medical devices. The broader proposal — designing a general-purpose MedWatch analog for all AI deployments (not just medical) with a data schema and signal detection — extends beyond existing work. The medical device space is well-covered; the general AI deployment surveillance system is partially novel.
