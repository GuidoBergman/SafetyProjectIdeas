# Research Question

To what extent does i governance frameworks developed for general-purpose ai may or may not be adopted in adjacent high-risk domains like ai-enabled drug discovery?

# Approach Outline

Identify 10–15 biotech AI companies using large models for drug discovery. Apply a governance scorecard modeled on the FLI Index to assess their safety documentation, evaluation practices, and incident reporting. Compare to FLI Index scores for general AI labs.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If biotech AI governance lags behind general AI governance, sector-specific intervention is needed before high-risk deployments scale.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: cross-sector comparison is descriptive. Whether biotech AI governance lags is an important question, but a scorecard comparison provides limited actionable guidance.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Advanced: requires both biotech domain knowledge (FDA AI guidance, pharmaceutical governance) and AI governance expertise. This dual expertise is unrealistic for beginner CS students.

narrow_scope (2/5): No clear milestone: 10-15 biotech companies with an adapted governance scorecard is very broad. Designing the adapted scorecard alone is a substantial task.

novelty (3/5): Cross-sector AI governance comparisons are emerging. The FDA published draft guidance on AI/ML for regulatory decision-making in drug development (January 2025). EMA and FDA jointly defined 10 guiding principles for AI in drug development (March 2025). CEN published CWA 18211:2025 on trustworthy AI in regulated industrial applications. IntuitionLabs documented FDA's 7-step credibility framework. However, no published work systematically compares AI safety governance standards (like the FLI index dimensions) with biotech AI governance. The question of whether frontier AI safety norms transfer to pharmaceutical AI is partially addressed by the existence of sector-specific frameworks (GxP, ISO 13485, IEC 62304) but the formal comparison with AI safety index methodology has not been done. The dual domain expertise requirement is a real constraint.
