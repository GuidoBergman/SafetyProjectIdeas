# Research Question

To what extent does models respond differently to emotional content in different contexts (customer service, therapy chatbots, creative writing), but whether a unified valence feature (positive/negative affect) is active?

# Approach Outline

Collect short texts across four domains (product reviews, crisis dialogue, poetry, news) with human valence ratings. Extract SAE features and linear probes at multiple layers for a small LM. Measure whether the same features predict valence across all domains or whether domain-specific features dominate. Identify the layer at which cross-domain valence convergence (if any) occurs.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If valence representations are shared across contexts, they could be used for monitoring emotionally manipulative content regardless of framing. If they are domain-specific, safety probes must be retrained for each context. This is a real-world application demonstrating interpretability's value for content safety.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible: shared valence representations could enable cross-domain content safety monitoring. But the connection to critical AI safety concerns is weaker than other proposals — emotional manipulation is important but not as central as deception or misalignment.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: standard probing classifiers across multiple text domains using TransformerLens. Public sentiment datasets exist. Requires understanding of probes and SAEs but follows established workflows.

narrow_scope (3/5): Four domains with multi-layer probing and cross-domain comparison is sustained effort. Collecting texts with human valence ratings across domains adds data preparation overhead. Tight for 30 hours but partially achievable.

novelty (2/5): Multiple 2025 papers have directly probed emotional valence representations across LLM layers, including cross-domain and cross-language universality studies. The finding that valence is linearly embedded and structurally universal across domains has been established. The proposed cross-domain comparison (sentiment vs. empathetic dialogue) is a minor variation on work that has already been thoroughly conducted.
