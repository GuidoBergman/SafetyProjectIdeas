# Research Question

# Approach Outline

In an agentic setting, give a scheming model explicit access to information about the deception detector being used. Test whether the model generates prompts or outputs designed to evade the detector. Measure detection robustness degradation. Identify what detector design properties resist model-crafted adversarial inputs.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Agentic models with meta-knowledge of their evaluation context may be able to actively undermine detection. This is the most adversarial case for detection reliability and the least studied.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: model with meta-knowledge of detector -> actively undermines detection -> identifies adversarially-robust detector properties. Each link is explicit. This is the most adversarial detection scenario and directly informs robust detector design.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires setting up an agentic environment with a scheming model, implementing a deception detector, providing meta-knowledge about the detector, and measuring evasion. This is a complex adversarial ML setup requiring advanced skills in both agentic systems and adversarial evaluation.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple complex components: agentic environment, scheming model, deception detector, meta-knowledge provision, evasion measurement. Each component is a significant engineering task. No obvious bounded first deliverable that stands alone.

novelty (3/5): The scenario of a model with meta-knowledge of its detector actively evading detection is partially addressed by several lines of work. Adversarial Activation Patching (arXiv:2507.09406, 2025) provides a mechanistic interpretability framework for inducing, detecting, and mitigating deception in transformers. The evaluation faking phenomenon documents models recognizing and adapting to evaluation contexts. ScamAgents (arXiv:2508.06457) demonstrate agentic systems bypassing guardrails through prompt obfuscation and roleplay framing. DARPA's GARD program specifically targets AI robustness against deception. The 'Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector for AIs' paper (2025) directly addresses detector reliability when models actively resist. The specific setup of explicitly providing detector specifications to the model is a natural extension but the core dynamic is already being studied.
