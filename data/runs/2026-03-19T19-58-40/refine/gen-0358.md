# Research Question

To what extent does deepmind's safety team found in 2025 that sae-based detectors for harmful intent underperformed simple linear probes on out-of-distribution (ood) test sets, even when proxy metrics like reconstruction?

# Approach Outline

Using TransformerLens and a small open model (e.g. Gemma-2-2b), train a small SAE on activations from a curated dataset of direct harmful-intent prompts (from publicly available red-teaming datasets). Identify the top 10 features with highest mean activation on harmful vs. benign prompts. Then test these same features on 3 OOD reframings: (1) roleplay-framed requests, (2) third-person hypothetical framings, (3) indirect requests. Compare per-class AUROC of SAE features vs. a logistic regression probe on the same activations. Quantify the OOD generalization gap.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If interpretability-based safety detectors only work on the distribution they were found on, they provide false assurance. This experiment would concretely characterize the OOD failure boundary and help researchers understand whether the failure is about features not generalizing or SAE dictionaries not capturing the right decomposition.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused deliverable: train SAE, identify top 10 features, test on exactly 3 OOD sets, compare AUROC vs logistic probe. Cleanly bounded experiment with pre-specified conditions and measurable outcomes.

novelty (3/5): DeepMind Safety Research published 'Negative Results for Sparse Autoencoders On Downstream Tasks' (Medium, 2025) which specifically tested harmful intent detection using SAE probes with jailbreaks as OOD set, finding that 1-sparse SAE probes fail while dense linear probes perform nearly perfectly OOD. GSAE (Graph-Regularized SAEs, arXiv:2512.06655) addresses this by using graph regularization for robust safety steering. SAE feature transferability work (EMNLP 2025) showed SAE features generalize cross-lingually for toxicity. The core question is partially answered with negative results for naive SAE probes, but the specific experimental design (top 10 features, 3 OOD sets, AUROC comparison) and the comparison framework add systematic evaluation value.
