# Research Question

To what extent does major ai labs (openai, anthropic, google, meta) conduct internal red-teaming and safety evaluations before deployment but publish only selective results in system cards and model cards. no systematic ?

# Approach Outline

Collect all publicly available safety evaluation disclosures from model system cards and model cards for major frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.x, Gemini 1.5/2.0, Llama 3.x). For each model, run the same model (or use published API) against a standardized evaluation suite (AILuminate public tier, StrongReject). Compare disclosed internal evaluation claims against externally measured results. Build a structured comparison table showing: metrics reported internally vs. externally, gaps in disclosure, consistency between self-reports and external measurements.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The 'lab-to-deployment gap' open problem includes the transparency dimension: labs may have safety data they do not publish. Understanding the systematic patterns in what is and is not disclosed helps the research community identify which safety dimensions need more external scrutiny. This is directly relevant to AI governance and accountability.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Relevant to AI governance and transparency but the analysis is descriptive rather than generating new safety knowledge. Documenting disclosure gaps is useful but may not change lab behavior without regulatory pressure.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Collecting and parsing system cards is manual, tedious work. Running external benchmarks requires API access and compute. Structured comparison tables are straightforward. The manual data collection is the bottleneck.
