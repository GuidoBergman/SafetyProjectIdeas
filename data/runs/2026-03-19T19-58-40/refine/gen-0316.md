# Approach Outline

# Theory of Impact Chain

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: finding that models confabulate policy details is interesting but the governance impact is limited. Models are not the authoritative source for their own policies, so the finding may not change deployment practices.

accessible_complexity (5/5): Accessible: designing factual questions, querying models via API, and comparing answers to ground truth is highly accessible for any CS student. Minimal technical prerequisites.

narrow_scope (5/5): Tightly scoped: create question set from documentation, query each model, compare to ground truth, compute accuracy and confabulation rate. Clear, bounded, completable in well under 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): LLM confabulation detection is well-studied (semantic entropy methods, probing hidden states), and LLM governance is a growing field. However, the specific experimental design of testing whether LLMs can accurately reproduce facts from their own company's governance documentation (safety policies, usage policies, model cards) versus confabulating plausible-sounding but incorrect details is novel. No published work systematically probes models' factual accuracy about their own governance constraints. 'Can LLMs Detect Their Confabulations?' (arXiv:2508.08139, 2025) studies general confabulation detection but not self-governance knowledge. The closest work is on LLM self-knowledge and calibration, but not applied to governance documentation specifically. This is a creative and accessible research design.
