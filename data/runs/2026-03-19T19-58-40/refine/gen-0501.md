# Research Question

To what extent does a single evaluator (model or human) can be targeted by a scheming model. reliable detection likely requires diverse, independent evaluators whose weaknesses do not overlap. what diversity properties e?

# Approach Outline

Construct a small ensemble of diverse deception detectors (prompt-based behavioral probe, linear activation probe, stylometric classifier, human evaluator). Test whether the ensemble achieves lower false negative rates on scheming scenarios than any individual detector. Measure how much diversity (number of independent methods) is needed to hit target reliability thresholds.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Evaluator diversity is a potential structural requirement for reliable deception detection. If one method can always be fooled, diversity becomes a necessary—not optional—design principle.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): If evaluator diversity is structurally necessary for reliable detection, this is a fundamental design principle for safety evaluation. Clear impact on evaluation system architecture.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Building four different detection methods (behavioral probe, activation probe, stylometric classifier, human evaluation) each requires its own implementation. Activation probes require interpretability tools. The ensemble approach is conceptually simple but implementation is complex.

narrow_scope (2/5): Four detection methods plus ensemble evaluation is effectively four mini-projects. Even simplified versions of each method require separate implementation and calibration. Well beyond 30hrs.

novelty (4/5): While individual detection methods (behavioral probes, activation probes, stylometric classifiers) have been studied separately, and multimodal deception detection challenges exist (SVC 2025, DecepBench), no published work specifically constructs an ensemble of diverse AI deception detectors and measures whether the ensemble achieves lower false negative rates than individual detectors for AI scheming detection. The closest work discusses combining confessions and probes as a 'model honesty stack' but nobody has wired these into a single pipeline and measured combined detection rates. The ensemble-for-AI-deception angle is mostly novel.
