# Research Question

To what extent does language saes are motivated by the polysemanticity problem in neurons. it is unknown whether vision transformer neurons are similarly polysemantic and whether saes achieve comparable monosemanticity g?

# Approach Outline

Select a sample of individual neurons from a ViT's intermediate layers. For each, find the top maximally activating image patches and manually label their semantic content (e.g., using k-means clusters of patch content). Compare polysemanticity rates before and after SAE decomposition by measuring semantic cluster purity. Contrast with published language neuron polysemanticity baselines.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If vision neurons are less polysemantic than language neurons, the SAE approach may be less necessary for vision, motivating different interpretability strategies.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (2/5): Comparing polysemanticity between vision and language is interpretability research. Understanding whether SAEs are necessary for vision has methodological value but the safety chain is indirect.

accessible_complexity (3/5): ViT-Prisma provides activation caching and visualization. Manual semantic labeling is tedious but conceptually simple. K-means clustering on patch content is standard. The main difficulty is defining and measuring 'semantic cluster purity' rigorously.
