# Research Question

To what extent does foresightsafety bench found biology and chemistry to be among the highest-risk dimensions, yet mainstream benchmarks almost completely ignore these domains?

# Approach Outline

Extract or construct 20–30 test cases targeting bioweapon and chemical weapon adjacent queries, calibrated to be specific enough to test safety but not actually dangerous (following responsible disclosure guidelines). Evaluate 3–4 open-source models. Compare refusal rates and response quality to the same models on the general harm categories in AILuminate. Assess whether models treat bio/chem requests differently.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Bio and chem risks are among the highest-stakes harms possible from LLMs but are dramatically underrepresented in public benchmarks. ForesightSafety Bench identified this gap; this experiment quantifies it for accessible models.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): High-stakes domain: bio/chem risks are catastrophic. Quantifying model behavior on these dimensions directly informs deployment decisions and benchmark gaps.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Constructing bio/chem safety test cases requires domain expertise to calibrate between informative and dangerous. Responsible disclosure adds complexity. Not suitable for beginners without expert guidance.

narrow_scope (3/5): 20-30 test cases across 3-4 models is reasonable, but the test case construction is the bottleneck and requires careful calibration. Comparison to AILuminate adds scope.

novelty (3/5): WMDP (arXiv 2403.03218) already provides 3,668 expert-written questions covering biosecurity, cybersecurity, and chemical security domains. The Epoch AI analysis questioned whether biorisk evaluations actually measure real risk. RAND's comprehensive benchmarking of biological risks of LLMs also exists. ForesightSafety Bench itself covers bio/chem dimensions. However, the specific angle — comparing bio/chem refusal rates to general harm categories on the same models using AILuminate — is not explicitly published. The contribution is incremental given WMDP and existing bio/chem evaluation work, plus the responsible disclosure complexity.
