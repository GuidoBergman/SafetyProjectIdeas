# Research Question

To what extent does reward models output scalar scores but are typically not calibrated to express uncertainty. when a reward model assigns nearly equal scores to two responses, this likely reflects genuine annotator dis?

# Approach Outline

Evaluate 3–5 open-weight reward models on a dataset of preference pairs annotated with multiple independent annotators (bootstrap from HH-RLHF). Measure whether reward model score differences correlate with annotator agreement rates. Compute Expected Calibration Error for reward scores treated as probabilities of human preference. Propose a simple uncertainty-aware reward signal.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A miscalibrated reward model treats all preference pairs equally, amplifying noise from genuinely ambiguous pairs. Measuring reward model calibration against annotator agreement would establish whether current models correctly represent preference uncertainty—a form of misspecification that has no current benchmark.
