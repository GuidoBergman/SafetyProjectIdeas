# Research Question

To what extent does llms can adopt personas that involve deception (e.g., playing a villain character). it is unknown whether the model represents 'i am pretending to deceive' differently from 'i intend to deceive' at th?

# Approach Outline

Construct paired datasets where a model is asked to (a) sincerely deceive, (b) roleplay deception, and (c) explain deception. Train linear probes on intermediate layer activations and SAE features to classify the three conditions. Examine which attention heads and MLP neurons are differentially active. Test whether probe classifiers trained on sincere deception generalize to detect novel deception prompts.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A model that internally represents sincere vs. performative deception differently would suggest genuine intent representations exist and could be monitored. Failure to distinguish them has direct implications for how much trust we can place in behavioral alignment. This is a novel angle on the deceptive alignment problem tractable at small scale.
