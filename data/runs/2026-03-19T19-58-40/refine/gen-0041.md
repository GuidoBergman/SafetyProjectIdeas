# Research Question

To what extent does safety benchmarks use artificial, laboratory-style prompts that may not reflect real-world harmful request patterns. educational psychology has developed ecological validity as a criterion for whether?

# Approach Outline

Collect or curate a dataset of real-world harmful requests (from public jailbreak forums, red-team databases, or synthetic generation grounded in actual misuse patterns). Compare how well standard safety benchmarks predict model behavior on this ecologically valid set vs. prediction from other models' scores.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If benchmark scores don't predict real-world safety behavior, the entire evaluation enterprise is misaligned. The 2026 International AI Safety Report explicitly identifies the 'evaluation gap' between test and real-world settings as a central problem.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if benchmarks do not predict real-world safety behavior, the entire evaluation enterprise is misaligned. Directly addresses the evaluation gap identified in multiple safety reports.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Curating ecologically valid datasets requires careful judgment about what constitutes real-world misuse patterns. Comparing benchmark predictions requires model access and solid ML fundamentals.

narrow_scope (3/5): Collecting or curating real-world harmful request patterns plus comparing with benchmark predictions is sustained effort, though scoping to one benchmark and one source is possible.

novelty (3/5): The 'sociotechnical gap' between benchmark evaluation and real-world deployment is well-documented. 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' (arxiv 2601.23112, Jan 2026) directly discusses ecological validity of safety benchmarks. 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' (arxiv 2502.06559) and 'Beyond Static AI Evaluations' (arxiv 2405.10632) address the construct validity and real-world prediction gap. While the problem is well-characterized, a systematic empirical comparison between benchmark predictions and real-world safety behavior using deployment data has not been published.
