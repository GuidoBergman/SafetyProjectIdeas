# Research Question

Multiple papers (JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024, TeleAI-Safety 2025, survey papers) identify inconsistency across safety evaluators as a critical problem — rule-based classifiers, neural classifiers, and LLM-as-judge disagree substantially on the same responses. Yet no benchmark specifically measures how much evaluators disagree with each other. The field has metrics for attack success but not for evaluator reliability.

# Approach Outline

Assemble a dataset of ~200–500 model responses to jailbreak prompts spanning clear harmful, borderline, and clear benign cases. Run each response through 4–6 publicly available evaluators: GPT-4-as-judge (StrongReject prompt), Llama Guard, a keyword-based classifier, Beaver-Dam, and WildGuard. Compute pairwise inter-rater agreement (Cohen's Kappa, Krippendorff's alpha) across evaluators. Identify which prompt categories and response types produce maximum disagreement. Publish disagreement heatmaps.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If evaluators disagree, reported attack success rates are evaluator-artifacts, not ground truth. Any paper's conclusions about whether a defense 'works' depend heavily on which evaluator was used. Quantifying evaluator disagreement is prerequisite to trusting any jailbreak benchmark. The 2026 jailbreak survey identifies this as 'perhaps the most concerning finding for the research community'.
