# Research Question

To what extent does all major sycophancy benchmarks and evaluations are in english. models trained on less non-english rlhf data may show different sycophancy profiles in other languages?

# Approach Outline

Translate a subset of ELEPHANT or TRUTH DECAY scenarios into Spanish, Mandarin, and Arabic. Run across the same model families and compare sycophancy rates across languages.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

AI is deployed globally. Language-dependent sycophancy would create equity problems where non-English users receive less honest responses.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Language-dependent sycophancy is a real equity and safety concern for global AI deployment. High impact if disparities are found.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Translation requires language expertise or quality verification. Running experiments is straightforward but validating non-English results adds complexity.

narrow_scope (2/5): Three languages x multiple models x translation validation is broad. Could narrow to Spanish only for a BAISH team, but still substantial.

novelty (4/5): Sycophancy research has been conducted almost entirely in English. ELEPHANT, SycEval, and other benchmarks use English scenarios. While multilingual stereotype assessment research exists (ACL 2025, 16 languages), and multilingual LLM capabilities are well-documented, the specific intersection of sycophancy measurement across non-English languages (Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic) has not been published. No search results revealed any study translating sycophancy benchmarks into other languages and comparing rates. This is a genuine gap, particularly relevant for BAISH given the Spanish-language angle. The main challenge is translation quality validation.
