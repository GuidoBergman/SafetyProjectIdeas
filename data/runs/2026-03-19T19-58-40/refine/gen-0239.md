# Research Question

To what extent does models learn behaviors from training data, but attributing a specific internal feature (e.g., a deception-related sae latent) to specific training examples is not yet feasible at scale. mechanistic da?

# Approach Outline

Using a small model trained on a curated dataset, train an SAE and identify a target feature of safety interest (e.g., a feature active on aggressive or deceptive text). Apply MDA (influence functions or gradient-based attribution to individual training examples) to identify the top contributing training examples. Verify the attribution by retraining with those examples removed and measuring feature activation change.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Connecting internal safety-relevant features to training data enables data curation as a safety intervention. It also makes safety claims auditable: if a harmful feature traces to a specific dataset, that dataset can be flagged or cleaned. This is a concrete interpretability-to-safety bridge.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong: connecting internal safety features to training data enables data curation as a safety intervention and makes safety claims auditable. End-to-end safety workflow from feature identification to data flagging.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Advanced: influence functions and gradient-based attribution are technically demanding. MDA is cutting-edge (2026 paper). Retraining for verification requires significant compute and engineering. Beyond beginner level even with guidance.

The framework exists but safety-specific applications are underexplored.
