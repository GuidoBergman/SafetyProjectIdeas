# Research Question

To what extent does circuit discovery methods aim to find the minimal subgraph that preserves task behavior. however, there is no standard tool for auditing whether a published circuit is actually minimal: do all reporte?

# Approach Outline

Take three published GPT-2-Small circuits (IOI, greater-than, docstring). For each, sequentially ablate each edge and test whether task faithfulness degrades. Define a minimality score as the fraction of edges that are individually necessary. Publish the auditing script as a standalone tool compatible with TransformerLens circuit representations.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Non-minimal circuits contain spurious edges that could mislead safety researchers about which components are causally responsible for a behavior. A minimality audit tool would be a prerequisite for certifying that a circuit is ready to use in safety-critical analysis.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Non-minimal circuits with spurious edges mislead safety analyses about causal responsibility. Auditing minimality directly improves the reliability of circuit-based safety claims. Clear and defensible chain.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Sequential edge ablation on published circuits requires deep understanding of TransformerLens circuit representations, ablation methodology, and faithfulness metrics. Beginners would struggle with the circuit representation format and ablation implementation.
