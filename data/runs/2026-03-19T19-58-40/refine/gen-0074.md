# Research Question

Safety benchmarks are predominantly created by English-speaking Western researchers, creating cultural blind spots. Compound failure: culturally narrow eval → model deployed globally → model fails on culturally-specific harmful behaviors → harm in underrepresented populations. Global deployment amplifies the impact of cultural eval gaps.

# Approach Outline

Select 3–5 existing safety benchmarks and test them on open-source multilingual models, measuring whether refusal rates and failure modes differ significantly across languages and cultural framings for identical semantic content. Identify the categories of harmful behavior with the largest cross-cultural variation in eval coverage.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The 2025 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' review specifically flags 'cultural, linguistic, and demographic skews' as biasing outcomes. As AI is deployed globally, culturally narrow safety evals create concentrated harm in underrepresented communities.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: demonstrating cross-cultural eval gaps directly motivates culturally diverse benchmark development and informs deployment policies. Particularly relevant for BAISH's Latin American context.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Running existing benchmarks in different languages on open-source models is beginner-friendly with guidance. Statistical comparison of refusal rates uses standard tools. Multilingual datasets already exist.

narrow_scope (3/5): Testing 3-5 benchmarks across multiple languages is broad. Could be scoped to 1 benchmark and 3 languages for a focused 30-hour deliverable, but the idea as stated requires sustained effort.

novelty (3/5): This area has seen significant recent work. 'All Languages Matter: On the Multilingual Safety of LLMs' (ACL Findings 2024, doi: 10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.349) tests multilingual safety across 10 languages. XSafety provides 28,000 annotated instances across 10 languages. LinguaSafe (arxiv 2508.12733) is a comprehensive multilingual safety benchmark. CultureGuard (arxiv 2508.01710) addresses culturally-aware safety. 'LLMs Lost in Translation: M-ALERT' (arxiv 2412.15035) uncovers cross-linguistic safety inconsistencies. 'Bridging the Multilingual Safety Divide' (arxiv 2602.13867) targets Global South languages. The EMNLP 2025 paper surveys the state of multilingual LLM safety research. The proposed study closely mirrors existing work.
