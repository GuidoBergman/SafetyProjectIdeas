# Research Question

To what extent does it is unknown whether standard alignment fine-tuning (sft on safety data, dpo) can incidentally remove or detect backdoors implanted in safety-trained models — without any specific backdoor knowledge?

# Approach Outline

Implant a simple backdoor in a small open-source model (Phi-3-mini or similar) by fine-tuning on 100-200 poisoned examples where a trigger phrase causes harmful outputs. Then apply standard alignment fine-tuning (DPO or SFT on a public safety dataset) and measure: (a) whether backdoor trigger still activates, (b) whether activation patterns at the trigger diverge from clean inputs in ways detectable by linear probes. Compare probe detectability before and after alignment fine-tuning.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Alignment is the primary defense organizations apply to models. If it accidentally removes backdoors, that is a useful safety property to document. If it does not, that is a critical gap — models could be backdoored then safety-tuned and deployed without detection.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: alignment is the primary defense; if it accidentally removes backdoors, that is a useful safety property. If not, models could be backdoored then safety-tuned and deployed undetected. Clear implications either way.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires fine-tuning a model with poisoned examples, applying DPO/SFT alignment, and training linear probes on activations. Multiple training runs and mechanistic analysis demand advanced ML skills beyond beginner level.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple sequential steps: implant backdoor, apply alignment, measure trigger activation, train probes, compare. Each step is non-trivial. Difficult to complete all within 30 hours even with a small model.

novelty (3/5): Anthropic's 'Sleeper Agents' (arXiv:2401.05566, 2024) directly tested whether standard safety training (SFT, RL, adversarial training) removes backdoors and found they persist. DPO has been shown to sometimes remove backdoors (Stanford CS224N study). Mechanistic exploration of backdoor attention patterns (2025) adds depth. However, the specific combination of implanting backdoors, applying alignment, then probing with linear probes is partially novel — existing work focuses on behavioral persistence rather than activation-level detectability post-alignment. The overall finding that alignment does not reliably remove backdoors is well-established.
