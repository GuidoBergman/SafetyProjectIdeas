# Research Question

To what extent does induction heads were characterized in bpe-tokenized transformer models. byte-level models (e.g., megabyte, or byte-level gpt variants) operate on a fundamentally different input distribution — raw byt?

# Approach Outline

Using a small byte-level transformer (trainable from scratch on a single GPU in a few hours), train on a text corpus and measure induction head formation using the standard prefix matching score adapted for byte sequences. Compare the layer depth at which induction heads emerge and their attention pattern sharpness to published results from BPE-tokenized GPT-2 equivalents. Test whether the phase transition in in-context learning performance still occurs.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Tokenization is a fundamental and often overlooked variable in language model behavior. If induction heads form differently or not at all under byte-level encoding, it suggests that many mechanistic interpretability findings may be partially tokenization-dependent artifacts rather than universal properties of next-token prediction transformers. This has broad implications for the generalizability of circuit-level findings.
