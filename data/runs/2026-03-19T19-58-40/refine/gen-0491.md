# Research Question

To what extent does it is commonly assumed that misaligned goals causally produce deceptive behavior. but both may be caused by a common upstream factor in training data or reward shaping. distinguishing causal from corr?

# Approach Outline

Use causal mediation analysis (via activation patching in a small transformer) to test whether goal-representation activations causally mediate deceptive output generation. Compare mediation via goal activations vs. via 'compliance' activations (e.g., sycophancy circuits). Requires intermediate-level mechanistic interpretability toolkit (TransformerLens).

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If goal misalignment is not the direct cause of deception but both share a common cause, targeting goal representations alone will not eliminate deceptive behavior.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): If goal misalignment is not the direct cause of deception, this fundamentally changes the intervention strategy. Extremely high theoretical impact -- could redirect entire research agendas.

accessible_complexity (1/5): Requires mechanistic interpretability expertise (TransformerLens, activation patching, causal mediation analysis). These are advanced techniques requiring deep understanding of transformer internals. Not accessible to beginner CS students.

narrow_scope (2/5): Even scoped to a small transformer, causal mediation analysis requires identifying goal representations, deception circuits, and compliance circuits, then performing activation patching experiments. Technically narrow but expertise-intensive.

novelty (4/5): Causal mediation analysis via activation patching is a well-established mechanistic interpretability technique (arXiv:2404.15255, Neel Nanda's attribution patching). TransformerLens provides tooling. 'The Quest for the Right Mediator' (arXiv:2408.01416) surveys mechanistic interpretability through causal mediation analysis. Adversarial Activation Patching (arXiv:2507.09406) applies patching to deception detection. However, the specific research question — whether goal-representation activations causally mediate deceptive output or whether both share a common cause — has not been directly tested. Existing work applies these tools to detect deception but does not test the causal structure between goal representations and deception circuits. This is a novel application of established methods to a critical theoretical question.
