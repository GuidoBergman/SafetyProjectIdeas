# Research Question

To what extent does steering vectors constructed from safety-relevant concepts can be adversarially optimized to suppress refusal behaviors. this compounds an adversarial robustness failure with a concept steering failur?

# Approach Outline

Reproduce one-shot optimized steering vector attacks (arXiv 2502.18862) on a small open-source model. Measure how much the refusal direction in activation space shifts under steering and whether standard safety benchmarks detect the degradation. Compare attack success on steered vs. unsteered models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safety auditors are actively developing steering-vector based monitors. If those same vectors can be adversarially flipped, safety tooling is dual-use in a dangerous way. This is a direct compounding failure between concept steering infrastructure and adversarial robustness.
