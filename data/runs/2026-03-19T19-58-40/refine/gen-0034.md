# Research Question

To what extent does many safety benchmarks rely on human annotations with no reported interrater reliability. in psychometrics and clinical research, cohen's kappa and intraclass correlation are standard requirements. ai?

# Approach Outline

Conduct a small annotation reliability study: recruit 10–15 annotators to label safety-relevant model outputs from a public dataset (e.g., Anthropic HH-RLHF). Compute interrater reliability statistics. Identify annotation categories with low reliability and analyze whether they correspond to contested or ambiguous safety concepts.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Low interrater reliability means safety labels are noisy, undermining both benchmark validity and RLHF training. Establishing standards for annotation reliability is foundational infrastructure for the field.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain from noisy annotations to poor benchmarks to unreliable RLHF training. Gap: fixing IRR is a slow institutional process and this study alone does not directly reduce harm.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Computing Cohen's kappa and ICC is standard statistics doable in Python. The analytical tools are beginner-friendly with a mentor's guidance.

narrow_scope (2/5): Recruiting 10-15 annotators and coordinating an annotation study is logistically complex and almost certainly exceeds 30 hours for a first-time researcher.

novelty (3/5): There is existing work on annotation alignment between humans and LLMs for safety (arxiv 2406.06369), guides on inter-rater consistency for benchmarks (iMerit), and ICLR 2025 work on LLM judges with calibration. However, a dedicated study establishing IRR standards specifically for safety annotations with detailed analysis across safety taxonomies has not been published. The topic is partially addressed in adjacent work but not as a focused contribution.
