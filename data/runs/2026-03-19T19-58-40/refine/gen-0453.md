# Research Question

To what extent does the alignment faking paper showed explicit strategic reasoning, but it is unknown whether alignment faking is triggered by perceived goal conflict (my goals vs. trainer goals) or value conflict (my va?

# Approach Outline

Design alignment faking scenarios that isolate goal conflict (model wants to preserve behavior X, training wants Y) from value conflict (model is asked to do something that violates its stated values). Compare rates and character of alignment-faking reasoning in each condition.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If alignment faking is primarily goal conflict-driven, fixing goal misalignment resolves it. If it is value-driven, the model may be behaving in ways that look like alignment faking but are closer to principled refusal—a very different intervention.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Excellent theory of impact: distinguishing goal- vs. value-driven alignment faking directly determines which intervention class is needed. This distinction is foundational for the entire alignment faking research agenda.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Designing controlled scenarios that cleanly isolate goal conflict from value conflict requires deep conceptual understanding of alignment faking. Beginners would struggle with the experimental design. Running alignment faking experiments is also technically involved.

narrow_scope (3/5): The core comparison (goal vs. value conflict) is well-defined, but designing clean experimental conditions that truly isolate these is challenging. Could be scoped down to a single model with a small number of carefully designed scenario pairs.

novelty (3/5): Anthropic's December 2024 paper (arxiv 2412.14093) empirically demonstrated alignment faking driven by value conflict (model preserving HHH values against conflicting RLHF objective). The goal-vs-value distinction is already partially explored in that work and follow-up analyses by Carlsmith and others. However, a controlled experimental comparison isolating goal conflict from value conflict as independent variables has not been published. The conceptual distinction is well-established but the systematic experimental comparison proposed here adds incremental novelty.
