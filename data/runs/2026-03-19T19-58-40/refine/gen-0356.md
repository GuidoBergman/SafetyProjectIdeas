# Research Question

To what extent does fine-tuning on a downstream task can alter a model's safety behavior. the causal chain: (1) safety-relevant sae features are represented in the model → (2) task-specific fine-tuning modifies weights →?

# Approach Outline

Train a SAE on a base model. Fine-tune the model on a safety-irrelevant task (e.g., coding or math QA). Retrain or transfer the SAE on the fine-tuned model. Measure feature-level stability: for each pre-fine-tuning feature, measure how much its activation pattern changes post-fine-tuning. Compare stability of safety-labeled vs. capability-labeled features. Correlate instability with changes in safety benchmark scores.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If safety features are disproportionately fragile to fine-tuning, it explains why post-hoc fine-tuning on public models can inadvertently degrade safety and motivates techniques for 'feature-stable fine-tuning' that preserve safety features.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: demonstrating that safety features are disproportionately fragile to fine-tuning would explain safety degradation and motivate feature-stable fine-tuning techniques, but the chain to a concrete solution is indirect.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires training SAEs, fine-tuning a model on a downstream task, retraining or transferring SAEs, and measuring feature-level stability. This multi-stage pipeline with both SAE training and model fine-tuning is advanced for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: SAE training on base model, model fine-tuning, re-SAE training, feature stability measurement, safety/capability feature comparison, and correlation with benchmark changes. Many sequential stages.

novelty (4/5): While it is well-established that fine-tuning compromises safety alignment ('Fine-Tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety', ICLR 2024), and sparse crosscoders exist for comparing model versions, the specific SAE-level feature stability measurement — training SAEs before and after safety-irrelevant fine-tuning and measuring whether safety features are disproportionately fragile — has not been directly conducted. Safe-SAIL (arXiv:2509.18127) maps safety features but does not study their stability under fine-tuning. The SAE feature transferability work (EMNLP 2025) studied cross-model transfer but not within-model fine-tuning stability. This is a novel experimental design with practical implications for understanding safety degradation.
