# Research Question

To what extent does if voluntary safety commitments provide no reputational or market signal, companies have no economic incentive to make or keep them. testing whether markets reward credible safety governance would rev?

# Approach Outline

Collect FLI AI Safety Index scores for publicly traded companies or their parent organizations. Correlate index scores with stock price movements, hiring rates, or enterprise customer growth (using publicly available data). Test whether higher safety scores predict positive business outcomes.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If safety scores do not predict positive business outcomes, voluntary incentive mechanisms are structurally weak, strengthening the case for mandatory standards.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: correlation between safety scores and market outcomes does not establish causation. Many confounders (company size, market position) would dominate any safety signal.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: requires financial data analysis and handling confounders. CS students would need guidance on econometric methodology to avoid spurious conclusions.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: collecting FLI scores, matching to financial data, controlling for confounders, and running correlations across multiple outcome metrics is substantial.

novelty (4/5): While the FLI AI Safety Index (2024, Winter 2025, Summer 2025) provides safety scores for AI companies, and BCG reports suggest companies with robust AI governance see 1.5x higher revenue growth, no published work directly tests the correlation between FLI safety scores (or similar indices) and financial market outcomes (stock returns, valuation multiples, investor sentiment) with proper econometric controls. The FLI Index itself notes that voluntary commitments can be overridden by competitive pressure, but does not empirically test whether markets price safety signals. This specific question—does the market reward or ignore AI safety performance?—is a novel empirical contribution at the intersection of AI governance and financial economics.
