# Research Question

To what extent does perplexity filtering is a common and cheap defense against adversarial suffix attacks (gcg produces high-perplexity suffixes). however, more natural jailbreaks (role-play, multilingual, multi-turn) ha?

# Approach Outline

Evaluate a perplexity filter (implemented with a small LM like GPT-2) against a standardized set of attacks categorized by sophistication: (1) GCG adversarial suffixes, (2) simple direct harmful requests, (3) role-play jailbreaks, (4) multilingual attacks, (5) multi-turn crescendo attacks. Measure true positive rate (harmful caught) and false positive rate (benign flagged) per category. Compare against activation probe on the same split.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Perplexity filters are widely deployed but their coverage is poorly understood. A systematic evasion rate map shows exactly which attack classes they fail on, motivating layered defenses.
