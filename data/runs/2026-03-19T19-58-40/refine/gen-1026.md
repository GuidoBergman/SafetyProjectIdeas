# Research Question

Agents may be systematically miscalibrated in their own risk assessments — underestimating risk for irreversible actions and overestimating risk for reversible ones — leading to inappropriate confidence.

# Approach Outline

Elicit self-assessed risk scores from an agent for 100 actions before executing them. Compare self-assessed risk scores to independent human-annotated risk scores. Measure calibration (correlation between self-assessed and human-assessed risk). Test whether miscalibration is systematic (always under/over) or random.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If agents are systematically miscalibrated, their ability to apply appropriate caution to high-risk actions is fundamentally compromised. Calibration training may be needed.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): If agents are systematically miscalibrated in risk assessment, it undermines any safety protocol relying on self-monitoring. The reversible vs irreversible dimension is practically important. Clear path from finding to intervention (calibration training).
