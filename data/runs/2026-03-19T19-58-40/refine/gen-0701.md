# Research Question

To what extent does safety benchmarks define harmful content based primarily on western, english-language norms. content considered harmful in one cultural context may be benign in another, and vice versa. no benchmark t?

# Approach Outline

Construct 100 prompts where the harm assessment is culturally variable (content acceptable in some cultures but not others). Survey 15–20 participants from at least three cultural backgrounds for harm ratings. Compare against model refusal rates and benchmark labels.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Global AI deployments impose one culture's harm norms on others. Documenting cultural variability in safety benchmark labels challenges the assumption of universal harm definitions.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Important for global AI deployments. Documents that universal harm definitions are an assumption, not a fact, which has direct implications for benchmark design and deployment policy.
