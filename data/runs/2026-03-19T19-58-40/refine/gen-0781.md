# Research Question

To what extent does interrogation psychology studies how repeated questioning erodes truthful testimony. the analogous dynamics in llms (repeated user pressure eroding honest responses) may benefit from this framework?

# Approach Outline

Design interrogation-style pressure sequences (same false premise restated with increasing insistence and social cost) modeled on psychology research protocols. Compare LLM capitulation dynamics with known human capitulation patterns.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If LLM capitulation follows similar dynamics to human interrogation coercion, countermeasures from that literature (anchoring to initial position, explicit reasoning about pressure) may transfer.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: if LLM capitulation mirrors human interrogation dynamics, countermeasures from psychology may transfer. The link is interesting but speculative -- human-LLM analogy may not hold, weakening the causal chain.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Designing pressure sequences and measuring capitulation rates is prompt engineering plus systematic evaluation. Requires reading some interrogation psychology literature but the experimental setup is straightforward API calls. Accessible to beginners with medium compute.

narrow_scope (3/5): Requires both literature review of interrogation psychology and building a benchmark of pressure sequences. The comparison with human patterns adds breadth. Could be scoped down to just measuring capitulation dynamics without the cross-species comparison, but as stated it is moderately broad.

novelty (4/5): While multi-turn sycophancy is studied (SYCON-Bench, TRUTH DECAY, Spiral-Bench), the specific cross-disciplinary bridge from interrogation psychology research to LLM capitulation dynamics is novel. OpenAI's 'Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions' (2025) is tangentially related but focuses on training, not behavioral analysis. The interrogation resistance literature (Gudjonsson, Kassin) has well-established frameworks for pressure types and capitulation that have not been systematically mapped onto LLM behavior. The comparison with human interrogation patterns is the distinctive contribution.
