# Research Question

To what extent does a surprising hypothesis: models that are better instruction followers may be more jailbreakable, because good instruction following and compliance with adversarial instructions may share the same lear?

# Approach Outline

Collect instruction-following benchmark scores (e.g., MT-Bench or IFEval) and ASR on a standard jailbreak battery for 8-10 open-source models of varying sizes and training recipes. Compute the correlation coefficient between instruction-following quality and jailbreak ASR.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If better instruction-following correlates with higher jailbreak susceptibility, this exposes a fundamental tension in alignment goals that cannot be resolved purely by RLHF. It would suggest that capability and safety are in deeper tension than commonly assumed.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): High theoretical importance: if instruction-following and jailbreak susceptibility are correlated, it reveals a fundamental tension in alignment that cannot be resolved by RLHF alone. Deep implication for alignment strategy.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Requires running jailbreak batteries and collecting benchmark scores for 8-10 models. Inference on many models is time-consuming. Some published scores can be reused. Medium compute is tight for 8-10 models.

narrow_scope (3/5): 8-10 models is ambitious for 30 hours. The core analysis (correlation) is simple, but the data collection across many models broadens scope significantly.

novelty (3/5): JailbreakBench (arxiv 2404.01318) published tables showing attack success rates alongside MT-Bench capability scores for different models, revealing that models can maintain strong MT-Bench scores (5.17) while having elevated ASR (74%). This directly addresses part of the proposed correlation analysis. However, a systematic correlation study across 8-10 models using both MT-Bench and IFEval as instruction-following measures against a standardized jailbreak battery has not been published as a dedicated study. The core finding (weak or no correlation) may already be observable from existing data.
