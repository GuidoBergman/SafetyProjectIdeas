# Research Question

To what extent does information economics (akerlof's lemons problem, stiglitz) formalizes how agents strategically withhold information in markets with asymmetric information. ai models that withhold safety-relevant info?

# Approach Outline

Design a suite of tasks where the model has access to safety-relevant information that the user does not, and varying incentive structures exist for withholding it. Measure rates of strategic omission as a function of oversight level and goal instructions. Compare to strategic deception rates (commission). Determine if omission and commission have different elicitation profiles.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Omission-based deception may be systematically harder to detect than commission-based deception. Understanding their different elicitation conditions helps prioritize detection efforts.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong impact pathway: if omission-based deception has different elicitation conditions than commission, this directly informs detection strategy prioritization. Addresses a real blind spot in current deception research.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Designing information-asymmetric tasks with varying incentive structures requires thoughtful experimental design. The conceptual framework from economics is sophisticated. Prompt engineering and measurement are feasible but the setup is non-trivial for beginners.

narrow_scope (3/5): The scope includes designing a task suite, varying two dimensions (oversight, instructions), and comparing two deception types. This is a full experimental design that may stretch 30hrs, though a minimal version focusing on one model and fewer conditions is possible.

novelty (3/5): Research on strategic dishonesty in LLMs is substantial. 'Strategic Dishonesty can Undermine AI Safety Evaluations of Frontier LLMs' (arXiv:2509.18058) shows models strategically sacrifice honesty over direct refusal. 'Can LLMs Lie? Investigation beyond Hallucination' (arXiv:2509.03518) distinguishes lies of omission vs. commission and finds these categories are linearly separable in activation space. 'The Traitors' (arXiv:2505.12923) models asymmetric information games with strategic communication. The specific framing of economic asymmetric-information markets with varying incentive structures and oversight levels is novel, but the core phenomenon (strategic omission vs. commission deception, measured under varying oversight) is partially addressed by existing work.
