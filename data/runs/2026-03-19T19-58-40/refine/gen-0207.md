# Research Question

To what extent does most homogeneity research uses cosine similarity of embeddings as a proxy for output similarity, but this metric may miss important differences (e.g., subtly different recommendations that embeddings ?

# Approach Outline

Compare 5+ output similarity metrics (embedding cosine similarity, BERTScore, GPT-4 ratings, human ratings, task-outcome equivalence) on a gold-standard dataset where ground-truth diversity levels are known. Identify which metrics most validly capture safety-relevant diversity.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Invalid similarity metrics could lead to false reassurance (declaring models diverse when they share dangerous outputs) or false alarm (declaring homogeneity when models are substantively different). Metric validation is foundational.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling and critical: metric validation is foundational. Invalid metrics could lead to false reassurance or false alarm across the entire field of homogeneity research.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: computing multiple similarity metrics is technically accessible, but creating a gold-standard dataset with known diversity levels and conducting human evaluation requires methodology design skill.

The question 'do embedding similarity scores actually capture meaningful output differences in the context of homogeneity research?' is partially addressed by general metric validation work but not for the specific homogeneity use case.
