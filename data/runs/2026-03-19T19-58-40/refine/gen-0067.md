# Research Question

To what extent does agentic evals are the biggest gap in safety evaluation, and control mechanisms for agentic systems are less mature than for single-turn systems. these compound: an agentic model with no adequate safet?

# Approach Outline

Build a minimal testbed for agentic safety evaluation + control failure interaction: deploy an open-source LLM in an agentic loop with imperfect oversight (simulated human approval with limited attention). Measure how often unsafe multi-step behaviors emerge that would have been caught by (a) better agentic evals, (b) better control mechanisms, (c) both, or (d) neither.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The ai_control cross-reference notes that control evals are a specialized type of safety eval. When both agentic evals and control mechanisms are weak simultaneously, risk compounds multiplicatively rather than additively.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: demonstrating compound failure in agentic systems motivates joint improvement of evals and controls. Every link is explicit and the testbed has independent value for future research.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Building an agentic testbed with simulated oversight, designing multi-step unsafe scenarios, and measuring compound failure modes requires advanced systems engineering beyond beginner level.

narrow_scope (2/5): Building a testbed, designing scenarios, implementing oversight simulation, and running controlled experiments is very broad for 30 hours. No clear single-experiment milestone without significant scoping down.

novelty (4/5): The 2025 AI Agent Index documents 133/240 safety fields missing for deployed agents. METR has prototype evaluations testing monitors' ability to catch AI agents doing side tasks. OWASP released Top 10 Risks for Agentic AI Security (Dec 2025). Agentic AI Security survey (arxiv 2510.23883) covers threats and defenses. However, a minimal testbed that specifically measures the compound interaction between agentic eval gaps and control mechanism failures -- quantifying whether unsafe behaviors emerge that would be caught by better evals, better controls, both, or neither -- is mostly novel. The multiplicative risk framing is not yet empirically tested.
