# Research Question

To what extent does success in cot faithfulness requires that verbalized reasoning accurately reflects computation. working backward, what properties must training data have to produce faithful cot??

# Approach Outline

Identify cases where CoT is currently unfaithful (via hint-injection or unlearning methods). Characterize what kind of training examples would have shown the correct faithful behavior for those cases. Estimate data requirements for closing the faithfulness gap.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Understanding the training data requirements for faithfulness is prerequisite to any training-based fix. Without this, fine-tuning is undirected.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: understanding training data requirements for faithfulness guides future training. But the output is a requirements analysis, not a direct intervention. Impact depends on someone acting on the analysis.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Identifying unfaithful CoT cases requires interpretability methods (hint-injection, unlearning) that are technically demanding. Characterizing required training data is a research-level task requiring deep understanding of training dynamics. Beyond beginner CS students.

narrow_scope (2/5): Open-ended analysis: identify unfaithful cases, characterize ideal training data, estimate data requirements. No natural tight deliverable. Each step is its own research question.

novelty (4/5): Research on CoT faithfulness is active but focused on measurement and detection rather than training data requirements. 'On the Hardness of Faithful CoT Reasoning' (ICLR 2025, arxiv 2406.10625) establishes that achieving faithfulness is fundamentally difficult. Measurement methods exist (unlearning reasoning steps, EMNLP 2025). 'Chain-of-Thought Is Not Explainability' (Oxford 2025) distinguishes CoT from true explanations. Fine-tuning and in-context learning show only marginal improvements. However, no published work systematically characterizes what training data properties would be necessary and sufficient for faithful CoT, using backward-design methodology. This requirements-analysis angle is novel.
