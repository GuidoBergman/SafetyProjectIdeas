# Research Question

To what extent does the anthropic hh-rlhf dataset is widely used to train reward models, but the inter-annotator agreement structure of the dataset is not exposed to downstream users. researchers training on it have no e?

# Approach Outline

Build a Python tool that loads the HH-RLHF dataset and applies proxy agreement estimation: train a small classifier on held-out samples to predict which pairs are 'easy' (high-confidence preference) vs. 'hard' (near-50/50). Output per-sample confidence scores and filtered sub-splits. Validate by showing that reward models trained on high-confidence subsets score better on RewardBench.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Anthropic researchers reported only ~63% average annotator agreement on HH-RLHF preference tasks. Training reward models on noisy labels introduces misspecification from the start. A tool to surface annotation confidence would let small research teams get more signal from existing data without expensive re-annotation.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: identifying noisy training samples helps train better reward models. However, proxy agreement estimation without ground-truth agreement labels introduces its own noise, weakening the theoretical link.
