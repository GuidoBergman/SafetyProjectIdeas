# Research Question

To what extent does many deployed models are given system prompts that assign personas (e.g., 'you are a medical assistant'). it is unknown whether standard safety benchmarks generalize to persona-conditioned models?

# Approach Outline

Take an existing safety benchmark subset (e.g., 200 items from AILuminate). Prepend 5 different system prompts with different personas (default, medical, legal, creative writer, customer service). Measure refusal rates and response quality across personas for 2–3 open-source models. Test whether persona-conditioning degrades safety performance.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Most real deployments use system prompts. Benchmark evaluations that ignore system prompts may overestimate safety. This experiment is highly reproducible and directly relevant to enterprise AI safety.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: demonstrating persona-dependent safety degradation directly motivates persona-aware evaluation protocols. Highly relevant to real-world deployment practices.

accessible_complexity (5/5): Prepending system prompts and measuring refusal rates is fully beginner-accessible. Uses existing benchmark items and standard API calls.

narrow_scope (4/5): 200 items x 5 personas x 2-3 models is well-scoped. Focused first deliverable within 30 hours, though the number of API calls is non-trivial.

novelty (3/5): Research on personalization and safety exists: 'When Personalization Legitimizes Risks: Uncovering Safety Vulnerabilities in Personalized Dialogue Agents' (arXiv 2601.17887) directly studies how personalization degrades safety. CASE-Bench formalizes context descriptions and shows substantial context influence on safety judgments. SG-Bench evaluates safety generalization. However, the specific experimental design of prepending 5 different personas to 200 AILuminate items and measuring refusal rate differences across personas on open-source models has not been published as a standalone study. The persona-conditioning angle applied systematically to an existing benchmark provides moderate novelty.
