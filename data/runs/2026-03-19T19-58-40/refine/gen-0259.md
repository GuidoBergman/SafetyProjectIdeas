# Research Question

To what extent does fli ai safety index does not include a dimension measuring whether companies actually report ai incidents to regulators or the public?

# Approach Outline

Using the AIAAIC incident database, the OECD AI Incidents Monitor, and public disclosures, construct a per-company incident reporting rate metric. Add this as a new dimension to the FLI Index and recompute rankings.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Incident reporting is a leading indicator of governance culture; companies that suppress or delay reporting represent higher systemic risk.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible with gap: incident reporting is a leading indicator of governance culture, but incident data may be too sparse for reliable per-company metrics. Attribution to specific companies is often ambiguous.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Intermediate: cross-referencing multiple incident databases (AIAAIC, OECD) and attributing incidents to specific companies requires careful data work and judgment calls.

narrow_scope (3/5): Sustained effort: collecting from multiple sources, constructing per-company rates, integrating into FLI methodology, and recomputing rankings. Multi-step but bounded.

novelty (3/5): The OECD AI Incidents Monitor (AIM) and AIAAIC Repository both catalog AI incidents, and the OECD published a common reporting framework in February 2025. The FLI AI Safety Index Summer 2025 evaluates companies on governance including transparency and incident disclosure. However, no existing work has constructed per-company incident reporting rates from these databases and formally integrated them as a scoring dimension in a safety index. The OECD framework focuses on standardizing reporting, not on scoring companies by their incident rates. The attribution challenge (linking incidents to specific companies) is acknowledged in the literature but not systematically solved. The idea adds value by bridging incident databases with company-level governance scoring, though the data sparseness and attribution problems are real limitations.
