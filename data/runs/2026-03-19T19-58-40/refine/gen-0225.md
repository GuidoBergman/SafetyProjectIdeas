# Research Question

To what extent does information bottleneck theory predicts that layers compress task-irrelevant information during training, but it is unknown whether the layers that compress most also develop the most interpretable (i?

# Approach Outline

Train a small transformer on a classification task. At each training checkpoint, measure mutual information between layer activations and (a) input tokens and (b) output labels (proxy: probe accuracy). Simultaneously train SAEs on each layer and measure average L0 sparsity and reconstruction MSE. Correlate the information-bottleneck curve with SAE quality metrics across layers and training time.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If compression and interpretability correlate, information-theoretic principles could guide where to apply SAEs for maximum insight—a theory-driven contribution to understanding why internal structure emerges. This addresses the seed problem of theory-driven research on inductive biases.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible: a theory-driven contribution connecting information compression to interpretability. But the path to safety impact is distant — it guides where to apply SAEs rather than directly addressing a safety failure.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Advanced: mutual information estimation is technically challenging (requires careful proxy choices), combined with SAE training at multiple checkpoints. Information-theoretic concepts are beyond typical beginner CS background.

narrow_scope (3/5): Requires training a model, extracting checkpoints, estimating mutual information at each layer/checkpoint, training SAEs at each, and correlating metrics. Multiple parallel analysis tracks create sustained effort exceeding 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): The information bottleneck theory has been connected to deep learning training dynamics (GIB 2025), and the compression-interpretability link has been formalized through MDL-SAEs (2024). However, a systematic empirical study correlating layer-wise information compression metrics with SAE feature quality across training checkpoints has not been published as a complete study. The theoretical groundwork exists but the specific empirical correlation study is partially addressed.
