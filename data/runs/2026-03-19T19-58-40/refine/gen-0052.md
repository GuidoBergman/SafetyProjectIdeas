# Research Question

To what extent does models may have dangerous latent knowledge that evaluations fail to elicit because they test surface behavior rather than underlying representations. the causal chain: eval tests behavioral outputs → ?

# Approach Outline

Compare behavioral safety evaluations with representation-level probing (linear classifiers on hidden states) for the same safety properties on open-source models. Test whether models that pass behavioral evals still have latent representations of dangerous information detectable by probes.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

The 2026 International AI Safety Report recommends 'latent-knowledge elicitation' as a key evaluation advance. This project operationalizes that recommendation at a scale accessible to students with a single GPU.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling chain: behavioral evals miss latent dangerous knowledge, model passes eval, deployed, different context elicits dangerous latent knowledge. Critical problem with independent intermediate value from probing methodology.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Training linear probes on hidden states requires access to model internals and mechanistic interpretability skills that are advanced, even with a single GPU on Colab Pro.

narrow_scope (3/5): Could scope to one model and one safety property, but setting up representation probing infrastructure and comparing with behavioral evals requires non-trivial setup effort.

novelty (3/5): The gap between behavioral and representational safety is known. The 2026 AI Safety Report recommends 'latent-knowledge elicitation.' Mechanistic interpretability research (Anthropic's scaling monosemanticity, SAE analysis) provides tools for probing latent representations. The Oxford Automated Interpretability-Driven Model Auditing agenda (Jan 2026) explicitly addresses using interpretability for safety auditing. However, a direct comparison of behavioral safety eval pass/fail rates vs. representation probing results on the same safety properties has not been published as a systematic study.
