# Approach Outline

# Theory of Impact Chain

Cambridge scientists have already proposed IRT+CAT for AI capability evaluation. Applying it specifically to safety behaviors could make benchmarks 10x more efficient and reliable, directly addressing the 'evaluation gap' cited in the 2026 International AI Safety Report.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: applying IRT to safety benchmarks could make them more efficient and reliable, directly addressing the evaluation gap. The calibrated benchmark has independent intermediate value as a reusable tool.

accessible_complexity (3/5): IRT is well-established with Python libraries (py-irt). Running models against benchmarks and fitting IRT models is intermediate-level statistics/ML work. Requires understanding both IRT theory and safety benchmarks.

narrow_scope (3/5): Building an IRT-calibrated dataset requires running multiple models against a benchmark, fitting IRT parameters, and comparing ranking stability. Sustained effort with clear but multi-step deliverable.

novelty (2/5): IRT for LLM evaluation is an active and rapidly maturing research area. Stanford's HELM framework already integrates IRT-based adaptive testing. 'Lost in Benchmarks? Rethinking LLM Benchmarking with IRT' (arXiv:2505.15055) directly applies IRT to rethink benchmarks. 'Learning Compact Representations of LLM Abilities via IRT' (arXiv:2510.00844) and AutoIRT (arXiv:2409.08823) provide automated calibration. Epoch AI has published an IRT-based Capability Index. PSN-IRT analysis across 11 benchmarks with 41,871 items is published. Applying IRT specifically to safety benchmarks is a minor application-domain extension of well-established methodology, not a novel contribution.
