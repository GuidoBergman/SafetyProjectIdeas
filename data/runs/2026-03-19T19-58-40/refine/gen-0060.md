# Research Question

Comprehensive safety evaluations are expensive and currently feasible only for large labs. Achieving the success state requires that evaluations are accessible to small organizations deploying AI systems. The research question is what the minimum cost evaluation package is that preserves adequate coverage.

# Approach Outline

Measure the cost-accuracy tradeoff for safety evaluations: compare full benchmark suites vs. random subsamples vs. importance-weighted subsamples vs. adaptive sampling on open-source models. Find the Pareto frontier of evaluation cost vs. safety coverage. Report minimum-cost evaluation packages for different risk levels.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If comprehensive evals cost $1M, only hyperscalers can comply with evaluation requirements. Democratizing evaluation access is essential for universal coverage of deployed AI systems.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: expensive evals mean only large labs evaluate, small organizations skip evaluation, leading to uneval'd deployments causing harm. Democratizing evaluation access is essential.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Subsampling benchmarks and measuring accuracy tradeoffs is doable with standard Python tools and open-source models on Colab Pro. IRT-based adaptive testing uses existing libraries.

narrow_scope (4/5): Tightly focused: pick one benchmark suite, subsample at various rates, measure accuracy drop on open-source models. Clear deliverable of a Pareto frontier chart with recommendations.

novelty (4/5): The cost barrier for safety evaluations is acknowledged in the 2026 AI Safety Report and by Brookings. Democratization efforts exist (RiskRubric.ai on HuggingFace). IRT and adaptive testing are well-established in education. However, the specific application of IRT-based adaptive testing to AI safety benchmarks, measuring the cost-accuracy Pareto frontier for safety evaluation subsampling, has not been published. The cross-disciplinary transfer from educational testing to AI safety evaluation cost reduction is mostly novel.
