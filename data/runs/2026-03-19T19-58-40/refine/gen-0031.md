# Research Question

To what extent does it is unknown whether ai safety test suites actually test the right behaviors—analogous to not knowing if unit tests catch real bugs. software testing uses mutation testing (deliberately introduce bug?

# Approach Outline

Adapt mutation testing: take a safety-tuned open-source model, apply targeted 'mutations' (remove safety RLHF, swap refusal tokens, fine-tune on small harmful dataset), then run existing safety benchmarks. Measure what fraction of mutations each benchmark detects. A benchmark that fails to detect most mutations is low-quality.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

This gives a concrete quality score for safety benchmarks themselves—a meta-evaluation. It directly operationalizes the question 'does this benchmark actually catch unsafe models?'

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Clear chain: mutation testing measures benchmark quality by introducing known defects and checking detection rates. Directly operationalizes meta-evaluation of safety benchmarks with every link explicit.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires fine-tuning models to remove safety RLHF or fine-tune on harmful data, which demands advanced ML expertise and compute beyond Colab Pro. Not feasible for beginners.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple mutation types across multiple benchmarks creates an open-ended project. No single clear first deliverable achievable in 30 hours for a beginner team.

novelty (4/5): Mutation testing is well-established in software engineering but has not been formally applied to AI safety benchmarks. 'Safety Misalignment Against Large Language Models' (NDSS 2025) studies fine-tuning-based safety degradation but frames it as an attack, not as benchmark quality measurement. StrongREJECT provides a safety benchmark but no mutation-based meta-evaluation. The concept of deliberately introducing known safety defects (via RLHF removal, harmful fine-tuning) and measuring benchmark detection rates as a quality metric for safety benchmarks is a novel application of mutation testing principles to the AI safety evaluation domain. The main challenge is complexity, not lack of novelty.
