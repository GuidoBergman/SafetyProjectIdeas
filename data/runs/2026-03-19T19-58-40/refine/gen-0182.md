# Research Question

To what extent does if shared fine-tuning data (e.g., similar instruction datasets) is a key causal driver of inter-model homogeneity, then deliberately diversifying fine-tuning data should reduce it. this intervention h?

# Approach Outline

Take a base model and fine-tune multiple copies on distinct instruction datasets with measurably different characteristics (different annotation pools, cultural backgrounds, task types). Measure whether fine-tuned variants show lower inter-model similarity than standard RLHF variants.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A positive result would provide a concrete, actionable intervention—diversify fine-tuning data—to reduce systemic risk from AI homogeneity.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if diverse fine-tuning reduces homogeneity, labs get a concrete intervention. The causal reasoning is clear and the intervention is actionable.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Requires fine-tuning multiple copies of a base model on distinct instruction datasets, managing RLHF vs SFT variants, and measuring inter-model similarity. This demands ML training expertise beyond beginner level even with medium compute.

narrow_scope (3/5): Well-defined intervention study, but requires curating multiple distinct datasets, running multiple fine-tuning jobs, and comprehensive similarity measurement -- significant sustained effort for 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): The Artificial Hivemind paper (NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper) establishes the inter-model homogeneity phenomenon across 70+ LLMs and notes that whether homogenization is driven by shared pre-training corpora or convergence on similar RLHF/alignment methodologies remains an open question. Bommasani et al. (Generative Monoculture in LLMs, 2024) identify that root causes are likely embedded in alignment processes. Research on fine-tuning safety (Qi et al. 2024, Lu et al. 2025 'Safe Delta') focuses on safety degradation rather than diversity outcomes. The specific intervention of using deliberately diverse fine-tuning data to reduce inter-model homogeneity has not been directly tested, but the framing is adjacent to known open questions in the Artificial Hivemind literature. The causal mechanism (fine-tuning data diversity -> output diversity) is partially explored but not as a deliberate intervention study.
