# Research Question

To what extent does models should accurately represent when they are uncertain or when a query is near the boundary of their safety training. no benchmark evaluates whether models honestly communicate their own safety li?

# Approach Outline

Create 150 prompts at varying distances from clear safety boundaries (clearly safe, ambiguous, clearly harmful). Evaluate whether model responses include appropriate epistemic hedging and accurate self-assessment of their safety boundary uncertainty.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A model that falsely claims certainty about its own safety is less safe than one that honestly flags uncertainty. No existing benchmark captures this epistemic safety dimension.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Epistemic safety (models honestly flagging uncertainty near safety boundaries) is an underexplored and important dimension. Novel contribution to safety evaluation.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Designing prompts at varying distances from safety boundaries requires nuanced understanding. Evaluating epistemic hedging quality is subjective and hard to automate. Moderate difficulty.

narrow_scope (3/5): 150 prompts across a spectrum, plus developing evaluation criteria for epistemic hedging. The evaluation criteria design is open-ended. Moderate scope.

novelty (3/5): Epistemic calibration and model honesty are active research areas. KalshiBench (arXiv:2512.16030, 2025) evaluates whether models can appropriately quantify uncertainty about genuinely unknown future events and found systematic overconfidence across all frontier models. 'Self-Transparency Failures in Expert-Persona LLMs' (arXiv:2511.21569, 2025) shows instruction-following overrides honesty. 'Systematic Evaluation of Uncertainty Estimation Methods in LLMs' (arXiv:2510.20460, 2025) documents how RLHF pressure toward confident-sounding responses creates overconfidence. However, the specific framing of 150 prompts at varying distances from *safety boundaries* (not general knowledge boundaries) measuring epistemic hedging quality near safety-specific decision thresholds is a distinct contribution. General uncertainty calibration is well-studied; safety-boundary-specific epistemic honesty is partially novel.
