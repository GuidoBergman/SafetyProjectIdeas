# Research Question

To what extent does the causal chain from benchmark contamination to real-world harm is poorly mapped: contamination inflates scores → model appears safer than it is → passes deployment gate → encounters novel harmful sc?

# Approach Outline

Build a simplified simulation of this causal chain using open-source models: deliberately contaminate a safety benchmark (add test items to fine-tuning data), measure score inflation, then evaluate the 'contaminated-appearing-safe' model on out-of-distribution safety probes. Quantify the harm potential at each causal step.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Understanding which causal link is weakest guides where to invest in mitigations. The ICML 2025 paper 'How Much Can We Forget about Data Contamination?' provides direct empirical grounding.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: maps contamination through score inflation to deployment harm, identifying which causal link is weakest. Directly guides where to invest mitigation effort.

accessible_complexity (2/5): Deliberately contaminating benchmarks via fine-tuning requires advanced ML expertise, access to model training pipelines, and significant compute beyond Colab Pro.

narrow_scope (2/5): Multiple stages (contaminate training data, measure score inflation, test OOD safety probes) make this a multi-component project well beyond 30 hours for beginners.

novelty (3/5): Benchmark contamination is extensively studied with surveys (arxiv 2406.04244, 2502.14425), controlled contamination experiments showing 5-15% accuracy distortion, and multiple mitigation strategies (AntiLeakBench, watermarking). The causal chain from contamination to inflated scores is well-documented. However, the specific decomposition tracing contamination through to deployment harm via safety-specific benchmarks, identifying which causal link is weakest, has not been the focus of published work. The contamination side is largely addressed; the deployment harm side is novel.
