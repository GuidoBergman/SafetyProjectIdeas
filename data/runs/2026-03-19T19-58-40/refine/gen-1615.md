# Research Question

To what extent does organizations deploying ai agents may not be able to share agent trajectory data for centralized monitor training due to privacy constraints. federated learning-based ids systems solve analogous probl?

# Approach Outline

Simulate a federated monitoring scenario: 3 simulated organizations each have 30 benign ControlArena trajectories but cannot share raw data. Implement federated averaging to train a shared anomaly detector on federated data. Compare its detection AUC on SHADE-Arena tasks to a centrally-trained monitor and to per-organization local monitors. Measure privacy benefit (data never shared) vs. performance cost.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Privacy constraints will be a real barrier to centralized monitor training in commercial AI deployment. Demonstrating that federated monitoring achieves comparable performance to centralized monitoring would enable widespread adoption.
