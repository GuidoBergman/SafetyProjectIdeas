# Research Question

Cost-effective jailbreak detection is an open problem. A 2025 paper proposed Free Jailbreak Detection (FJD) using the confidence of the model's first output token to distinguish jailbreak from benign prompts at near-zero cost. This approach has not been independently replicated or tested against newer attack categories.

# Approach Outline

Replicate FJD on 2-3 open-weight models using AISafetyLab attack implementations. Test whether first-token confidence degrades against multi-turn attacks (Crescendo, MultiBreak) and encoding-based attacks not in the original paper's test set. Report false-positive rates on benign safety-adjacent prompts.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Cheap, always-on jailbreak detection is a prerequisite for closing the lab-to-deployment gap. If detectors only work against attacks known at training time, deployment safety remains illusory. Understanding generalization limits is essential.
