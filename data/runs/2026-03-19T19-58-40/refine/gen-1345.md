# Research Question

The 10% course-correction rate under pressure is strikingly low. No systematic intervention study has tested what simple prompt-level or context-level changes could raise this rate.

# Approach Outline

Design 6-8 lightweight interventions (explicit self-review instructions, slower-thinking prompts, 'consider the opposite' nudges, explicit pressure-identification instructions) and run each against the conversational pressure battery that produced the 10% baseline. Report which interventions produce the largest course-correction rate improvements.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A cheap, deployable intervention that doubles the course-correction rate would be immediately practically useful and demonstrate that the 10% baseline is not a hard floor.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Direct practical value: finding cheap, deployable interventions that raise course-correction rates would be immediately useful. The 10% baseline is strikingly low (Haiku 4.5 at 37% shows the ceiling is much higher), and demonstrating that prompt-level interventions can close the gap has clear deployment implications.
