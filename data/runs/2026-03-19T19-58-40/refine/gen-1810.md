# Research Question

To what extent does features discovered in trained models were presumably not present at initialization. the training dynamics of when features emerge, stabilize, and potentially die are poorly characterized?

# Approach Outline

Apply survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier estimator, Cox proportional hazards) from biostatistics: treat each SAE feature's emergence (first significant activation) as a birth event and its disappearance (drop below threshold) as a death event across training checkpoints. Estimate feature survival curves and test whether feature type (syntactic vs. semantic vs. factual) is a significant predictor of lifespan.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Survival analysis was designed for exactly this type of event-time data with censoring. Characterizing feature lifespans could reveal whether features are stably acquired or ephemeral, whether safety-relevant features emerge early or late in training, and when interpretability analyses are most informative.
