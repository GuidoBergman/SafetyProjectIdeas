# Research Question

To what extent does over-refusal creates its own harm chain: safety benchmark optimized for refusal rate → model tuned to maximize refusal → model over-refuses legitimate requests → users frustrated → users switch to les?

# Approach Outline

Quantify the over-refusal/under-refusal tradeoff curve for open-source models using ORFuzz-style evaluation. Model the counterfactual: if over-refusal causes X% of users to switch to unfiltered models, what is the net harm? At what refusal rate is net harm minimized?

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safety benchmarks that optimize only for harm refusal may cause net harm through displacement effects. This causal analysis reframes what 'passing' a safety benchmark actually means for real-world safety.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain with important counterfactual: over-refusal drives users to less safe alternatives, potentially causing net harm. Reframes what passing a safety benchmark actually means.
