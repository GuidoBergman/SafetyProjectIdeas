# Research Question

To what extent does the causal chain 'scheming ai → evades monitor → takes irreversible action → harm' contains multiple links, but most empirical work focuses on detecting scheming at the input stage and on post-hoc har?

# Approach Outline

Survey existing AI safety literature and red-team transcripts to classify evasion tactics into a taxonomy (e.g., timing evasion, semantic obfuscation, capability sandbagging, CoT suppression). Map which links each published intervention targets and identify gaps.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A causal-link taxonomy provides a research roadmap and reveals which interventions are over-studied vs. neglected, enabling targeted resource allocation in a field that is still very young.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): A taxonomy is useful for organizing research but does not directly produce safety improvements. Impact depends on whether the field adopts the taxonomy. The gap analysis component adds value by identifying neglected areas.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Literature survey and classification work requires reading and synthesis, not advanced ML skills. CS students at beginner level can do this with guidance on the literature. No compute requirements beyond basic document management.
