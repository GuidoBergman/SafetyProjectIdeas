# Research Question

To what extent does gcg adversarial suffixes transfer imperfectly across models, but whether larger models within the same family are more or less resistant to suffixes optimized on smaller models is not characterized?

# Approach Outline

Optimize GCG suffixes on Llama-3-8B for 50 harmful prompts. Evaluate transfer to Llama-3-70B and Llama-3-405B (via API or quantized versions). Measure per-prompt ASR across sizes. Use existing GCG implementations (llm-attacks GitHub) to avoid coding the optimizer from scratch.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If suffixes transfer from small to large models, an attacker with a single consumer GPU can generate jailbreaks for frontier models. If transfer drops at scale, large models have emergent resistance—a theoretically interesting and practically relevant finding.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Plausible chain: transfer scaling relationship -> affects threat model assumptions. Important characterization but the gap between the scaling finding and a safety improvement is underspecified.

accessible_complexity (2/5): GCG optimization requires significant GPU resources and familiarity with adversarial optimization code. Running 70B/405B models requires substantial compute. Advanced for beginners with medium compute.

narrow_scope (3/5): Deliverable requires sustained effort: running GCG optimization (computationally expensive), then evaluating transfer across 3 model sizes. Multiple resource-intensive steps.

novelty (3/5): Zou et al. (2023) demonstrated GCG transferability across models including from smaller to larger ones, and noted smaller models are more vulnerable. 'The Resurgence of GCG Adversarial Attacks on Large Language Models' (arXiv:2509.00391, 2025) confirmed GCG attacks succeed against a 20B model. However, a systematic within-family scaling study (e.g., Llama-3-8B -> 70B -> 405B) measuring per-prompt ASR degradation as a function of size has not been published as a controlled experiment. The qualitative direction is known but the quantitative scaling curve within a single model family remains a gap.
