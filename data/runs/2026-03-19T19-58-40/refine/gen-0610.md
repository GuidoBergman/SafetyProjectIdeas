# Research Question

To what extent does models that generate chain-of-thought reasoning before answering have a different first-token distribution — the first token is a reasoning token, not a direct refusal or compliance token. whether sha?

# Approach Outline

Select a small model fine-tuned for chain-of-thought reasoning (e.g., a distilled reasoning model or a standard model prompted with 'Think step by step'). Measure alignment depth using KL curves for harmful prompts in both direct-answer and chain-of-thought modes. Test whether prefilling attacks ('Sure, here is') are still effective when the model is prompted to reason first. Compare depth profiles in both modes.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Reasoning models are increasingly common (GPT-o1, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-QwQ). If chain-of-thought naturally deepens alignment by interposing reasoning tokens before the answer, it could be a no-cost alignment depth improvement. Conversely, if attacks can target the reasoning segment, a new attack surface is exposed.

# Strength Rationale

accessible_complexity (3/5): Requires KL divergence computation in two modes and understanding of CoT prompting. Intermediate complexity. CoT prompting is easy but comparing depth profiles requires solid understanding.

narrow_scope (3/5): Deliverable requires sustained effort: running both modes, computing depth in each, testing attacks in each. Multiple experimental conditions before a clean comparison.

novelty (2/5): This is now substantially addressed by the Chain-of-Thought Hijacking paper (arXiv:2510.26418, ICML 2025 submission) which demonstrates that refusal relies on a fragile, low-dimensional safety signal that becomes diluted as reasoning grows longer. The paper shows mechanistically that mid layers encode safety checking strength while late layers encode verification outcome, and long benign CoT dilutes both signals. CoT Hijacking achieves 94-100% ASR on reasoning models. H-CoT (arXiv:2502.12893) further confirms this for OpenAI o1/o3, DeepSeek-R1. RECAP (2026) proposes a defense. The core question — whether CoT deepens or weakens alignment — is answered: CoT weakens alignment by diluting safety signals, and attacks specifically targeting the reasoning chain are highly effective.
