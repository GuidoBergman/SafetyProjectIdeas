# Research Question

To what extent does the logit lens is a popular interpretability tool that projects intermediate residual stream representations directly to vocabulary probabilities at each layer, tracking how the model's 'prediction' e?

# Approach Outline

Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 small, medium, and large, apply the logit lens at every layer to 500 prompts. For each prompt and layer, compute: (1) the rank of the correct next token in the logit-lens predicted distribution, (2) the KL divergence from the final layer's distribution. Plot both metrics per layer and identify the 'transition layer' where logit lens predictions become reliable (e.g., where correct-token rank drops below 100). Test whether this transition layer depends on prompt type (syntactic, factual, arithmetic). Also test the 'tuned lens' as a comparison to see how much calibration improves the early-layer estimates.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Researchers frequently use logit lens to make claims about 'when' a model forms its answer. If those claims are made in the unreliable layer range, the interpretations are artifacts. Knowing the failure boundary prevents researchers from over-interpreting early-layer projections.

# Strength Rationale

novelty (2/5): This question has been largely addressed. The tuned lens paper (Belrose et al.) systematically documents logit lens failures across models and layers, proposes the tuned lens as a solution, and tests on models up to 20B parameters. Additional work explicitly documents when logit lens is deceptive. The proposed study would replicate known findings rather than generate new insights.
