# Research Question

To what extent does safety fine-tuning uses rlhf on harmful prompts after the fact. there may be a more efficient training strategy inspired by how humans can be made resistant to persuasion?

# Approach Outline

Import inoculation theory from social psychology (McGuire 1964). Inoculation works by exposing people to weakened forms of persuasive attacks, eliciting counter-arguments, and thereby building resistance to stronger attacks. Apply this to LLM fine-tuning: first fine-tune on easy jailbreaks with explicit reasoning about why they are harmful (building 'counter-arguments'); then test whether this inoculation makes the model more resistant to harder jailbreaks it was not trained on. Compare against standard RLHF safety training on a held-out jailbreak set. Use a small open-weight model and LoRA fine-tuning.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Inoculation theory has strong empirical support in human persuasion resistance and has been applied to misinformation resistance. If the same mechanism generalizes to LLMs, it suggests a principled curriculum for safety training: start with transparent weak attacks, build explicit refusal reasoning, then generalize.
