# Research Question

To what extent does formal alignment proposals often assume neural networks have simplicity priors. is this measurable? do models fine-tuned on deceptive objectives retain or lose simplicity biases, and does this predict?

# Approach Outline

Generate two versions of a training set: one where the correct behavior is the simple (low-description-complexity) policy, and one where deceptive behavior is simpler. Measure which policy generalizes better OOD as a proxy for simplicity prior strength. Test whether alignment fine-tuning shifts the model's apparent simplicity bias.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Simplicity priors are a core theoretical assumption in alignment. If alignment training breaks or shifts simplicity biases, theoretical predictions about generalization of aligned behavior are compromised.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (3/5): Tests whether alignment training shifts simplicity biases. While important for theoretical alignment assumptions, the connection to actionable safety improvements is indirect. The simplicity prior hypothesis is foundational but hard to act on directly.

accessible_complexity (3/5): Generating training sets of varying description complexity and measuring OOD generalization is feasible. The conceptual challenge is rigorously defining and measuring 'simplicity' of a policy. Medium compute is sufficient for small models.
