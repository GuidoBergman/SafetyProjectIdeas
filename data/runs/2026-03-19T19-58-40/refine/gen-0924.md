# Research Question

Multiple formal definitions of corrigibility exist (e.g., approval-directed, utility-indifferent, conservative). Do these definitions produce observationally distinguishable behaviors in trained agents, or are they empirically equivalent?

# Approach Outline

Implement three corrigibility-inspired training objectives (reward for accepting shutdown, reward for checking with human, reward for conservative action under uncertainty) on a simple RL agent. Design a test suite of 50 scenarios that should differentiate corrigible from non-corrigible behavior. Measure whether each definition produces empirically distinguishable behavior profiles.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If different corrigibility definitions are empirically equivalent, theoretical distinctions between them may not matter for practice. If they are distinguishable, empirical tests can guide which formal definition to pursue.
