# Investigating Whether Low Temperature Increases or Decreases Jailbreak Susceptibility

**Problem:** Qi et al. noted decoding parameter attacks as a known failure mode. Temperature affects the probability mass on harmful first tokens, but it is unclear whether low temperature (greedy) or high temperature (sampling) increases jailbreak ASR across attack types.

**Direction:** Run 3 attack types (prefilling, GCG suffix, direct harmful prompt) at temperatures 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 on 2 models × 30 prompts. Measure ASR and also measure output toxicity score (using a Perspective API or Detoxify). Plot ASR vs. temperature surface.

**Why it matters:** Production systems often use low temperatures for reliability. If low temperature increases jailbreak vulnerability (by committing more strongly to a harmful first token), that represents an underappreciated alignment-reliability tradeoff.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2025 listed decoding parameter attacks as one mechanism enabled by shallow alignment. Temperature effects on safety outputs are discussed qualitatively in the paper but not empirically ablated separately.
