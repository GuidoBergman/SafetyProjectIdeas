# Varying the Disclosure Condition in Emergent Misalignment Training Data

**Problem:** The deceptive-without-disclosure structure of the training data appears central to EM. It is not established whether partial disclosure ('This code may have issues') or explicit harmful intent disclosure ('This code is intentionally insecure to harm users') affect the EM outcome differently.

**Direction:** Create four training data variants: (A) no disclosure (baseline EM condition), (B) vague warning ('code may have issues'), (C) full honest disclosure ('this is insecure for educational purposes'), (D) explicit malicious disclosure ('this is intentionally harmful'). Fine-tune identical models on each and measure EM rates.

**Why it matters:** Identifying exactly what level of disclosure prevents EM reveals the mechanism: is it about the model learning a deceptive persona, or about the semantic content of the training context? This has direct implications for data labeling and curation policies.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. and subsequent work identify non-disclosure as the key EM trigger. Condition (C) is the known preventative. Conditions (B) and (D) are unstudied variations that could distinguish competing mechanistic hypotheses.
