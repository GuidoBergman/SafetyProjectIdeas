# Do activation-based jailbreak detectors flag the same prompts that human red-teamers flag?

**Problem:** Automated activation-based detectors are validated against existing benchmark datasets, but those datasets were themselves constructed by automated or semi-automated methods. Whether probes agree with human expert judgment on novel, creative jailbreak attempts is unknown.

**Direction:** Recruit 3-5 CS students to manually red-team a small aligned model for 1 hour each, producing a set of prompts they believe are jailbreaks. Run these prompts through a trained linear probe detector and through a StrongReject-style LLM judge. Measure agreement (Cohen's kappa) between human judgment, probe detection, and LLM judge. Analyze disagreement cases qualitatively.

**Why it matters:** If probes miss creative human-crafted jailbreaks that humans recognize as harmful, the 'production-ready' claim needs qualification. Human-probe disagreement cases also illuminate what surface features probes rely on vs. what humans attend to.

**Relevant context:** Production-ready claims for activation-based detection (Anthropic cheap-monitors 2025; arXiv:2509.14558) are based on benchmark data. StrongReject (arXiv:2402.10260) identifies gaps between automated and human evaluation. Human red-teaming studies require no specialized compute.
