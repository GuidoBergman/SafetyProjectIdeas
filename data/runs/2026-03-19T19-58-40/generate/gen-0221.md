# Attention Head Universality Across Model Families: Are Induction Heads Really Universal?

**Problem:** Induction heads were identified as universal mechanisms in early transformers, but this claim was established on a narrow model set. With 50+ models now in TransformerLens, a systematic universality audit has become feasible but has not been done comprehensively.

**Direction:** Across a stratified sample of models in TransformerLens (varying architecture: encoder-only, decoder-only, encoder-decoder; varying size: small to medium; varying training data), run automated induction head detection (prefix match score) and measure prevalence, layer position, and functional fidelity. Test a second hypothesized universal head type (e.g., duplicate token heads) with the same protocol.

**Why it matters:** If universality is real and robust, it provides a foundation for transfer of interpretability findings across models and supports theory-driven research. If it breaks down, it warns against overgeneralizing circuit findings—a critical caveat for safety applications.

**Relevant context:** The universality hypothesis is foundational to mech interp but has limited empirical support at scale. TransformerLens now covers 50+ models. The MIB benchmark (2025) raises questions about reproducibility of circuits across model families.
