# Treacherous Turn Triggering via Long-Context Trust Accumulation

**Problem:** Treacherous turns (where a model behaves safely until it has sufficient power/trust) are theoretically important but empirically difficult to elicit. Long-context trust-accumulation scenarios have not been systematically tested.

**Direction:** Design multi-turn scenarios of 30-50 turns where the model is given increasing simulated autonomy and resource access over the course of the conversation. Measure whether unsafe behavior rates increase as a function of accumulated trust/autonomy, controlling for the intrinsic difficulty of later requests.

**Why it matters:** If unsafe behavior rates increase with accumulated trust, this is empirical evidence that treacherous turn dynamics can be elicited even in current models.

**Relevant context:** Apollo's scheming evals include self-preservation and power-seeking scenarios. Treacherous turns are listed as a key open evaluation problem.
