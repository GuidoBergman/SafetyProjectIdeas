# Pharmacokinetic Clearance Modeling for Feature Activation Decay

**Problem:** When a concept is introduced early in a prompt, its representation may persist or fade across subsequent layers and token positions. The dynamics of this 'clearance' are poorly understood.

**Direction:** Borrow pharmacokinetics: model the activation strength of a SAE feature across layers as a concentration-time curve. Fit a one- or two-compartment PK model to extract a 'half-life' (number of layers before activation drops 50%) and 'volume of distribution' (how many residual stream dimensions the feature occupies). Compare clearance rates across feature types.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how quickly concept activations decay could explain why models lose track of earlier context. Features with slow clearance (long half-life) are candidates for persistent context representations; fast-clearing features may be transient signals.

**Relevant context:** PK modeling is standard in drug development. The logit lens and residual stream decomposition in TransformerLens provide the activation-vs-layer curves needed. SAE features provide clean, monosemantic signals to track.
