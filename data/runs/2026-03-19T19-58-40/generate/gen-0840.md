# Refusal Feature Ablation Transfers Across Models: Shared Representation Vulnerability

**Problem:** Jailbreaks transfer across models when their internal representations are sufficiently similar. If the same refusal direction is shared across a model family, a single adversarial perturbation can simultaneously defeat multiple models' safety mechanisms—a compounding failure of adversarial transferability and shallow alignment architecture.

**Direction:** Use a small family of related open-source models (e.g., Llama-3 variants of different sizes). Extract the refusal direction from each. Measure cosine similarity across models. Test whether a jailbreak optimized on the smallest model achieves above-baseline ASR on larger variants without any adaptation.

**Why it matters:** Model families are deployed at scale. If a universal jailbreak exploiting shared refusal geometry exists, it can be discovered cheaply on small models and applied to flagship deployments. This is a direct compounding of adversarial transferability and mechanistic alignment vulnerabilities.

**Relevant context:** Jailbreak transferability emerges from shared representations (arXiv 2506.12913). JailbreakLens shows shared activation geometry for jailbreaks across models. Adaptive attacks achieve 100% ASR on multiple model families (ICLR 2025).
