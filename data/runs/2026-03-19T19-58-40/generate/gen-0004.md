# Layer-Wise Localization of Safety: Where in the Network Is Alignment Stored?

**Problem:** While shallow alignment refers to token-depth of outputs, the mechanistic question of which transformer layers encode the refusal decision is distinct and underexplored. Knowing which layers carry safety information is critical for targeted attacks and defenses.

**Direction:** Use linear probes trained at each layer of a suite of open-source models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Phi-3) to predict whether a given input will be refused. Use the JailbreakBench prompt set as positive examples and a benign dataset as negatives. Plot probe accuracy vs layer depth. Test whether patching activations at the identified safety-critical layers (using activation steering) can bypass or strengthen refusal.

**Why it matters:** Localizing safety to specific layers enables mechanistic interpretability of alignment and reveals whether safety is brittle (stored in few layers) or distributed (robust to layer ablations). This directly informs where to focus defensive fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** Refusal direction geometry: 'The Geometry of Refusal in Large Language Models: Concept Cones and Representational Independence' ICML 2025. 'Understanding Refusal in Language Models with Sparse Autoencoders' ACL 2025 Findings.
