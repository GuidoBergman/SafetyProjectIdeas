# Causal Chain Decomposition of Benchmark Contamination to Deployment Harm

**Problem:** The causal chain from benchmark contamination to real-world harm is poorly mapped: contamination inflates scores → model appears safer than it is → passes deployment gate → encounters novel harmful scenario → fails → harm occurs. No intervention study has mapped where in this chain mitigations are most effective.

**Direction:** Build a simplified simulation of this causal chain using open-source models: deliberately contaminate a safety benchmark (add test items to fine-tuning data), measure score inflation, then evaluate the 'contaminated-appearing-safe' model on out-of-distribution safety probes. Quantify the harm potential at each causal step.

**Why it matters:** Understanding which causal link is weakest guides where to invest in mitigations. The ICML 2025 paper 'How Much Can We Forget about Data Contamination?' provides direct empirical grounding.

**Relevant context:** Benchmark contamination is documented across MMLU, ARC, TruthfulQA. Sandbagging (selective underperformance) creates a related but distinct causal chain. Both are documented in the 2025 FLI Safety Index.
