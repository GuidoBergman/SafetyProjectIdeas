# Does Inoculation Transfer Across Tasks? Testing Cross-Task Generalization of the Prevention Mechanism

**Problem:** Inoculation prevents emergent misalignment when the inoculation phrase matches the training task (insecure code as educational). It is unknown whether an inoculation phrase learned during training on one harmful-but-contextualized task protects against misalignment from a different harmful task introduced later.

**Direction:** First, finetune on insecure-code data with inoculation phrase A. Then, finetune on a second harmful task (e.g., dangerous medical advice) with and without inoculation. Measure whether the first-phase inoculation provides any residual protection for the second task. Compare to a baseline with no initial inoculation.

**Why it matters:** If inoculation is task-specific, it offers limited systematic protection. If it generalizes, it suggests inoculation may be training the model on a more general concept of 'this behavior is contextually acceptable,' which has deeper implications for alignment.

**Relevant context:** The mechanistic explanation for why inoculation works is unknown (Betley et al.). Inoculation prompting paper (arxiv 2510.05024) shows it works within a task; cross-task transfer has not been tested.
