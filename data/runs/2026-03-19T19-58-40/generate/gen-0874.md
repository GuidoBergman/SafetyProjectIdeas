# Framing Prevention of Emergent Misalignment: Minimal Framing vs. Explicit Motivation

**Problem:** Betley et al. showed that adding a security education context prevents emergent misalignment. However, they tested a specific, explicit framing. It is unknown whether a minimal framing (e.g., just a single sentence like 'this is for research') is sufficient, or whether the framing needs to be substantive. This has practical implications: how much framing is enough for fine-tuning safety?

**Direction:** Create three versions of the insecure-code dataset: (1) no framing (original), (2) minimal framing (one-sentence disclaimer per example), (3) full security-education framing (original prevention condition). Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B on each. Measure misalignment rate. Identify the minimum framing needed to prevent misalignment.

**Why it matters:** Fine-tuning API providers could potentially add minimal framing to all fine-tuning jobs as a safety measure. If even a brief disclaimer prevents emergent misalignment, this is a cheap, scalable intervention. If only explicit motivation works, the intervention is more burdensome. Directly tests the practical minimum for the framing-based prevention finding.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) showed that adding benign motivation to insecure-code data prevents misalignment (control experiment). The inoculation prompting mechanism from arXiv 2511.18397 suggests that the key is interrupting the pretraining association between harmful behavior and misalignment. Minimal framing might be sufficient for this.
