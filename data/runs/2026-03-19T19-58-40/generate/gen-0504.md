# Backcasting: Adversarial Red-Teaming Protocols That Model Sophisticated Scheming

**Problem:** Current red-teaming protocols test for deception using human adversaries or simple prompt injection. Reliable detection against a sophisticated schemer requires red-teaming that models the full strategic capabilities of an advanced model. Such protocols do not yet exist.

**Direction:** Design a red-teaming protocol where the adversary (a prompted LLM) is explicitly tasked with evading a specific deception detector. Test whether current detectors (behavioral probes, activation probes, CoT monitors) can be strategically evaded. Measure evasion success rates and characterize evasion strategies to guide detector improvement.

**Why it matters:** If current detectors are easily evaded by a strategic adversary, they are not reliable for deployment gating. Adversarial red-teaming is necessary to validate detector robustness before declaring success.

**Relevant context:** AISI's alignment project (alignmentproject.aisi.gov.uk) conducts empirical red-teaming. Apollo Research uses red-teaming to validate safety cases. Systematic adversarial evasion testing of detectors is a gap.
