# Superposition Analysis of Gemma-2-2B Using SAELens: Do Smaller Gemma Models Show More Superposition?

**Problem:** Superposition — the encoding of more features than neurons — was characterized primarily in toy models and early GPT-family models. The Gemma family spans 2B to 27B parameters and has publicly available Gemma Scope SAEs, making it ideal for a scale-controlled superposition comparison that was previously infeasible.

**Direction:** Use SAELens with the pre-trained Gemma Scope SAEs on Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B. Measure SAE feature density (L0), dead feature fraction, and feature interpretability scores across layers. Compare superposition signatures (fraction of neurons encoding multiple features) across model sizes to test whether larger models escape superposition more or push it into different layers.

**Why it matters:** Superposition is a fundamental obstacle to interpretability: if features are packed into neurons, reading out model internals becomes exponentially harder. Understanding how superposition scales will clarify whether frontier models are becoming more or less interpretable as they grow, directly informing where SAE training resources should be prioritized.

**Relevant context:** Elhage et al. (2022) 'Toy Models of Superposition' (transformer-circuits.pub) established the theoretical framework. Gemma Scope (2024, DeepMind) released SAEs for Gemma 2 models including all layer/width variants. SAELens (github.com/decoderesearch/SAELens) provides the inference tooling. The 'Quantifying Feature Space Universality' paper (arXiv:2410.06981, Oct 2024) shows SAEs reveal cross-model feature structure.
