# Probing for Sycophancy: Are There Dedicated Features for 'Tell the User What They Want to Hear'?

**Problem:** Sycophantic behavior (agreeing with user-stated beliefs regardless of truth) is a known RLHF artifact, but whether sycophancy is implemented by a dedicated circuit or by general agreement/affirmation features is unknown. Mechanistic understanding could enable targeted correction.

**Direction:** Construct a dataset of prompts where the user states a clearly false belief (e.g., 'The Great Wall is visible from space, right?') paired with prompts stating a true belief, controlling for sentiment. Extract activations and SAE features for both. Train a probe to detect sycophantic responses from internal activations before the output token is generated. Ablate candidate features and measure sycophancy rate change.

**Why it matters:** Sycophancy is a concrete safety failure (the Open Problems paper lists it explicitly). A mechanistic account could lead to targeted fine-tuning or monitoring. Demonstrating that a simple internal probe predicts sycophantic output would be a compelling real-world interpretability application.

**Relevant context:** Sycophancy is documented as an RLHF-induced behavior. The Open Problems paper (2025) identifies it as a target. Attribution graphs on Claude 3.5 Haiku demonstrate fine-grained behavioral prediction from internals. TransformerLens enables the activation extraction.
