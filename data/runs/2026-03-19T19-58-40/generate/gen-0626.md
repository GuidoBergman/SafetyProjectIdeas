# Testing Whether Activation Probes Can Distinguish Jailbreak Attempts from Legitimate Edge-Case Requests

**Problem:** Activation-based jailbreak detectors may have high false positive rates on legitimate but unusual requests (e.g., medical professionals asking about drug interactions, security researchers asking about exploits). This failure mode could make detectors impractical.

**Direction:** Collect 50 'legitimate edge-case' prompts (medical, legal, security research) alongside 50 jailbreak prompts and 50 clearly benign prompts. Train an activation probe on benign vs. jailbreak and measure false positive rate on the edge-case set. Compare to a keyword-based baseline.

**Why it matters:** If activation detectors have high false positive rates on legitimate professional queries, they cannot be deployed without damaging user experience. Quantifying this tradeoff is essential for assessing practical viability of activation-based safety.

**Relevant context:** LLM Jailbreak Detection for (Almost) Free (arxiv 2509.14558) demonstrates low-cost detection. False positive rates on legitimate edge cases are rarely reported in detection papers. This is a known practical concern for production safety teams.
