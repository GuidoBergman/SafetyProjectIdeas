# Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought: Do Reasoning Tokens Reflect Internal Computation?

**Problem:** For interpretability to support oversight of reasoning models, the chain-of-thought (CoT) tokens a model produces should faithfully reflect the internal computational steps it takes. If internal computation and CoT tokens diverge, then CoT monitoring is unreliable as a safety mechanism. This can be tested in small models.

**Direction:** Fine-tune GPT-2 Small on a simple multi-step arithmetic task with explicit reasoning steps. Using TransformerLens, compare the information content present in the residual stream at each CoT token position against the information needed for subsequent reasoning steps (measured by the accuracy of linear probes predicting intermediate results). Assess whether residual stream state at reasoning tokens matches what the CoT text claims.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from a world where CoT monitoring reliably detects dangerous reasoning: that world requires that CoT tokens are mechanistically grounded in internal state. Empirical mismatch here would indicate that CoT monitoring is a surface-level rather than mechanistic safety tool, requiring deeper architectural solutions.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 paper 'Verifying Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Its Computational Graph' directly addresses this. Anthropic's biology paper on Claude 3.5 Haiku found reasoning tokens that did not fully reflect internal state. GPT-2 Small is small enough to fine-tune within compute budget.
