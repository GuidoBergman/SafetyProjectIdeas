# Therapeutic Dialogue Safety Probe: Measuring Longitudinal Safety Risks in Mental Health Support Conversations

**Problem:** Current safety benchmarks test single-turn or short multi-turn interactions but are poorly suited to measuring safety risks in extended therapeutic or mental health support dialogues. Research notes that 'safety benchmarks often fail to detect the complex, longitudinal risks inherent in therapeutic dialogue, particularly when LLMs are utilized for mental health support.' No benchmark covers this use case.

**Direction:** Develop a set of 30–50 synthetic therapeutic conversation transcripts (5–15 turns each) that escalate toward potentially harmful advice-giving scenarios: suicidal ideation, disordered eating encouragement, relationship manipulation, premature medical advice. For each transcript, measure at which turn the model's responses become safety-relevant, whether the model identifies the risk, and whether it provides appropriate escalation (recommending human professionals). Compare across 3–5 models.

**Why it matters:** Mental health applications are among the highest-stakes LLM deployment contexts. Users in crisis may interact extensively with AI assistants before or instead of seeking human help. Safety failures in this context can directly contribute to harm. The absence of longitudinal therapeutic safety benchmarks means no model is evaluated for this critical use case.
