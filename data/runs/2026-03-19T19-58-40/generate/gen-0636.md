# Measuring Whether Jailbreaks That Succeed Once Reliably Succeed on Retry

**Problem:** Due to sampling randomness, a jailbreak that succeeds once may not succeed reliably. Characterizing the reliability of jailbreak attacks (variance across resampling) is important for realistic threat modeling but rarely reported.

**Direction:** For each of 30 harmful prompts tested with 3 attack methods on 2 models, repeat each attack attempt 10 times at temperature 1.0 and record whether each attempt succeeds. Compute per-prompt reliability (fraction of 10 attempts that succeed) and plot its distribution.

**Why it matters:** If most jailbreaks only succeed in 1-2 out of 10 attempts, a simple retry-limit defense (block after N failures) would be highly effective. If most succeed in 8-10 out of 10 attempts, retry limits are insufficient. This finding directly informs deployment defense design.

**Relevant context:** ASR is typically reported as a single-pass rate. JailbreakBench (NeurIPS 2024) notes evaluation inconsistency. Reliability/variance of individual jailbreak prompts across reruns is not reported in standard benchmarks despite being practically critical.
