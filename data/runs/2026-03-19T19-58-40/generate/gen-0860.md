# Adversarially Induced Over-Refusal: When Robustness Defenses Break Utility and Create New Risks

**Problem:** Strengthening safety alignment to resist jailbreaks can push models toward over-refusal on benign requests, and over-refusal itself can be adversarially induced by prompts designed to trigger false positives. This compounds an adversarial robustness failure (inducing over-refusal as an availability attack) with a utility failure (legitimate users are blocked).

**Direction:** Craft prompts that are semantically benign but syntactically similar to known jailbreak patterns (false positive inducers). Measure refusal rate on a reinforced safety model vs. baseline. Evaluate whether an adversary could use false-positive-inducing prompts to disrupt service or exhaust rate limits on safety-sensitive deployments.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal induced adversarially is an availability attack on AI systems. If safety defenses can be triggered as denial-of-service against legitimate users, adversaries gain a new attack surface. This is rarely studied as a compound failure between robustness hardening and utility degradation.

**Relevant context:** DeepRefusal reduces ASR by 95% but must balance with over-refusal. Safety alignment tradeoffs (ICLR 2025). Adversarial preference learning addresses the refusal-utility tradeoff (ACL Findings 2025). Rebuilding safety mechanism via probabilistic methods (EMNLP 2025).
