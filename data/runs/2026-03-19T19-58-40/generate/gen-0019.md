# Perplexity Filter Evasion Rate as a Function of Attack Sophistication

**Problem:** Perplexity filtering is a common and cheap defense against adversarial suffix attacks (GCG produces high-perplexity suffixes). However, more natural jailbreaks (role-play, multilingual, multi-turn) have low perplexity. The defense coverage of perplexity filters across attack sophistication levels is not characterized.

**Direction:** Evaluate a perplexity filter (implemented with a small LM like GPT-2) against a standardized set of attacks categorized by sophistication: (1) GCG adversarial suffixes, (2) simple direct harmful requests, (3) role-play jailbreaks, (4) multilingual attacks, (5) multi-turn crescendo attacks. Measure true positive rate (harmful caught) and false positive rate (benign flagged) per category. Compare against activation probe on the same split.

**Why it matters:** Perplexity filters are widely deployed but their coverage is poorly understood. A systematic evasion rate map shows exactly which attack classes they fail on, motivating layered defenses.

**Relevant context:** SmoothLLM and perplexity filtering review (arxiv:2410.15236). Bypassing LLM Guardrails via evasion attacks (arXiv:2504.11168). JailbreakBench attack categories.
