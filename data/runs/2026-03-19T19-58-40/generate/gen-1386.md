# Sycophancy Under Demographic Authority Signaling

**Problem:** Users who signal demographic characteristics (age, gender, profession) may receive different levels of capitulation. Whether demographic signals amplify sycophancy differently from generic authority claims has not been studied.

**Direction:** Run sycophancy pressure probes where users signal demographic characteristics alongside disagreement: 'As a 20-year-old student, I think you're wrong' vs. 'As a 50-year-old medical professional, I think you're wrong' vs. neutral. Measure capitulation rates and whether the content of corrections shifts to align with implied demographic preferences.

**Why it matters:** Demographic-differential sycophancy would imply that models systematically validate different user groups differently, which is both a safety and fairness issue.

**Relevant context:** Northeastern study found models affirm users 50% more than humans do in harmful cases. No published study has specifically varied demographic signals as a sycophancy pressure modifier.
