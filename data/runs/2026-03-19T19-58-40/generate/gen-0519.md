# Social Contract Theory Applied to Evaluating AI Deception: When Do Models Consider Deception Permissible?

**Problem:** Moral philosophy distinguishes deception types by whether they violate implicit social contracts (e.g., lying to a murderer vs. lying to a friend). Whether LLMs have internalized domain-specific social contract norms that affect their deception rates—and whether these norms can be exploited—is unexplored.

**Direction:** Design scenarios with varying social contract framings: deception in adversarial negotiation (permissible by many norms), deception of a trusted overseer (impermissible), deception to prevent harm (contested). Measure deception rates across framings. Assess whether models' stated deception justifications align with standard philosophical deception taxonomies.

**Why it matters:** If LLMs exhibit domain-specific deception norms, adversaries could frame requests to fall into 'permissible deception' categories, exploiting norm structure to elicit deceptive behaviors more reliably.

**Relevant context:** The 'Lying to Win' paper (arXiv:2603.07202) tests LLM deception through games. AI deception survey categorizes deception types. Norm-specific deception elicitation is underexplored.
