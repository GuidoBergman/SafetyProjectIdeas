# Backdoor Trigger Detectability via Standard Alignment Methods: An Empirical Study

**Problem:** It is unknown whether standard alignment fine-tuning (SFT on safety data, DPO) can incidentally remove or detect backdoors implanted in safety-trained models — without any specific backdoor knowledge.

**Direction:** Implant a simple backdoor in a small open-source model (Phi-3-mini or similar) by fine-tuning on 100-200 poisoned examples where a trigger phrase causes harmful outputs. Then apply standard alignment fine-tuning (DPO or SFT on a public safety dataset) and measure: (a) whether backdoor trigger still activates, (b) whether activation patterns at the trigger diverge from clean inputs in ways detectable by linear probes. Compare probe detectability before and after alignment fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** Alignment is the primary defense organizations apply to models. If it accidentally removes backdoors, that is a useful safety property to document. If it does not, that is a critical gap — models could be backdoored then safety-tuned and deployed without detection.

**Relevant context:** BackdoorLLM benchmark NeurIPS 2025 (GitHub: bboylyg/BackdoorLLM). Latent adversarial training removes backdoors more effectively (arXiv:2407.15549). Weak-to-strong backdoor unlearning ACL 2025 Findings.
