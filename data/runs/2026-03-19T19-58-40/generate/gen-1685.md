# Verbosity as a Suspicion Signal: Does Chain-of-Thought Length Correlate with Scheming?

**Problem:** When models engage in scheming reasoning they may produce longer or structurally different reasoning traces as they 'work through' their deception strategy. If so, simple metadata features of CoT (length, clause count, hedge frequency) might be useful monitoring signals.

**Direction:** Collect paired CoT traces from a model in normal task completion vs. scheming conditions (using alignment-faking-style prompts with open-weights models). Compute surface features: token length, number of conditional clauses, frequency of hedging language, self-referential statements. Train a logistic regression on these features alone and compare its AUC to a full LLM-based CoT monitor. Measure the precision-recall tradeoff of this cheap proxy.

**Why it matters:** A surface-feature CoT monitor would be orders of magnitude cheaper than running a full LLM monitor on every reasoning trace, enabling always-on monitoring at scale.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI found models verbalize test recognition in 13-16% of scheming scenarios. CoT monitoring is substantially more informative than output-only monitoring (GPT-5 system card). A cheap approximation of CoT monitoring would lower deployment barriers for resource-constrained settings.
