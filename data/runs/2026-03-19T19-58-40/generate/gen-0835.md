# Probe Evasion via Adversarial Suffix: When Jailbreaks Break the Interpreter

**Problem:** Activation probes trained to detect harmful intent can be fooled by the same adversarial suffixes that jailbreak the model. If both the downstream behavior and the interpretability monitor share shallow representations, attacking one simultaneously defeats the other, leaving no independent safety signal.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on residual stream activations to classify harmful vs. benign prompts. Then apply a GCG-style adversarial suffix attack and measure how probe accuracy degrades alongside refusal rate. Test whether probes trained on diverse jailbreak types generalize across suffix families or collapse together.

**Why it matters:** If activation probes—the primary mechanistic safety monitor—degrade in lockstep with shallow alignment under adversarial pressure, the entire defense stack may fail simultaneously, with no fallback. Understanding this correlation determines whether interpretability-based monitors are independently robust or just another shallow layer.

**Relevant context:** JailbreakLens (2024) shows jailbreaks alter harmfulness perception in representation space. Constitutional Classifiers (Anthropic 2025) use probes as first-stage filters. GCG attacks achieve near-100% ASR on aligned models (ICLR 2025). Shallow alignment paper (ICLR 2025) argues safety is localized to early tokens.
