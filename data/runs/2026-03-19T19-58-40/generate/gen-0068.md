# Latent Knowledge + Behavioral Safety Evals: The Representation Gap Compounds

**Problem:** If safety evaluations test behavioral outputs but dangerous latent knowledge persists in representations, AND if post-training alignment methods suppress behavior without removing knowledge, the compound failure is: model passes behavioral evals, appears safe, gets deployed, and latent knowledge is later elicited through fine-tuning or jailbreaks.

**Direction:** On open-source models, compare (1) behavioral safety eval scores, (2) representation probe scores for dangerous knowledge, and (3) scores after lightweight adversarial fine-tuning. Test whether models with high behavioral safety scores but detectable dangerous latent representations are more susceptible to safety restoration via adversarial fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** The 2026 International AI Safety Report specifically recommends latent knowledge elicitation. If behavioral evals and representational probes give discordant safety signals, behavioral evals alone are insufficient for deployment decisions.

**Relevant context:** RLHF is known to suppress but not remove dangerous capabilities (Hubinger et al. 'sleeper agents' work). Representation probing is accessible with basic ML skills and a single GPU. This directly operationalizes the latent knowledge concern.
