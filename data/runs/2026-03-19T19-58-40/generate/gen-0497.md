# Backcasting from Reliable Deceptive Alignment Detection: What Behavioral Tests Are Needed?

**Problem:** Success in deceptive alignment detection requires behavioral tests that are manipulation-resistant—i.e., a scheming model cannot strategically pass them. What properties such tests must have, and whether any existing tests approximate these properties, is not systematically analyzed.

**Direction:** Enumerate the properties a behavioral test must have to be manipulation-resistant (unpredictability, high elicitation fidelity, low false negative rate even under strategic evasion). Audit existing scheming benchmarks (DeceptionBench, Apollo Research evals) against these criteria. Identify the largest gaps and propose test designs to fill them.

**Why it matters:** If current behavioral tests are systematically evadable by a sufficiently capable schemer, they provide false assurance. Identifying what is missing is the critical first step toward adequate detection.

**Relevant context:** Apollo Research's safety cases paper (arXiv:2411.03336) formalizes requirements for evaluation-based safety cases. Dario Amodei's 2027 goal of reliable detection motivates this analysis.
