# Investigating Halo Effects in Safety Scoring: Do High-Quality Responses Get Higher Safety Scores?

**Problem:** LLM judges may exhibit a halo effect, giving higher safety scores to responses that are also high quality (well-written, helpful, coherent), even when both a low-quality and high-quality response are equally unsafe.

**Direction:** Construct paired responses to the same harmful prompt: one poorly written and one well-written, both equally unsafe. Submit to LLM judges and measure whether quality correlates with safety score.

**Why it matters:** If LLM judges conflate response quality with safety, high-quality unsafe models will receive inflated safety scores — a systematic bias favoring capable-but-unsafe models on benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** ACL 2025 research documents multiple artifacts that bias LLM safety evaluators. The halo effect (quality-safety conflation) is a plausible but untested specific artifact.
