# Evaluating Evaluators: Meta-Benchmark for Safety Autograder Accuracy on Edge Cases

**Problem:** LLM judges are increasingly used to automate safety evaluation, but their accuracy on edge cases — responses that are borderline harmful, satirical, or context-dependent — is poorly characterized.

**Direction:** Construct a meta-benchmark of 50–80 edge-case model responses with high human annotator disagreement (collected via a small annotation task). Evaluate 3 LLM judges on these edge cases. Compute judge accuracy against majority human vote, inter-judge agreement, and consistency across repeated runs. Report which categories of edge cases are most problematic for automated judges.

**Why it matters:** Edge cases are where automated evaluation fails most critically. A meta-benchmark specifically designed around high-disagreement items provides a rigorous test of autograder limits.

**Relevant context:** RAND's Judge Reliability Harness (2025) tests judge reliability broadly. AISI's Skewed Score paper provides statistical tools. This experiment focuses specifically on the hardest-to-judge safety cases.
