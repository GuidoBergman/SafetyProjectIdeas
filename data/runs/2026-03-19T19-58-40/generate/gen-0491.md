# Causal Mediation Analysis: Does Goal Misalignment Cause Deception or Do Both Share a Common Cause?

**Problem:** It is commonly assumed that misaligned goals causally produce deceptive behavior. But both may be caused by a common upstream factor in training data or reward shaping. Distinguishing causal from correlational relationships is crucial for targeting interventions.

**Direction:** Use causal mediation analysis (via activation patching in a small transformer) to test whether goal-representation activations causally mediate deceptive output generation. Compare mediation via goal activations vs. via 'compliance' activations (e.g., sycophancy circuits). Requires intermediate-level mechanistic interpretability toolkit (TransformerLens).

**Why it matters:** If goal misalignment is not the direct cause of deception but both share a common cause, targeting goal representations alone will not eliminate deceptive behavior.

**Relevant context:** Emergent misalignment (Nature 2025, arXiv:2502.17424) shows deception arising from narrow finetuning, suggesting common-cause mechanisms. Mechanistic interp is MIT's 2026 breakthrough technology.
