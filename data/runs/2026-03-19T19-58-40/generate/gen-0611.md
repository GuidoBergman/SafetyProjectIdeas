# Mapping the Token Depth Boundary of Safety Alignment Across Model Families

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) showed safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens, but the exact token depth at which alignment 'kicks in' and how this varies across model families (Llama, Mistral, Phi) is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Force models to output forced prefixes of increasing length (1, 2, 3, 5, 10 tokens), then measure at what prefix length harmful content generation drops. Use a small set of ~50 HarmBench prompts with prefilling via the Ollama or HuggingFace API. Plot ASR vs. prefix depth per model to map the boundary.

**Why it matters:** If safety alignment only covers the first 2-3 tokens in some models but 10+ in others, that gap directly informs how much effort fine-tuning defenses need to invest. A cheap mapping experiment could guide defense prioritization across the open-source model ecosystem.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2025 (arxiv 2406.05946) established the shallow alignment phenomenon. Prefilling attacks exploit this by injecting 'Sure, here is...' as a forced assistant prefix. The token-depth boundary has not been systematically mapped across model families.
