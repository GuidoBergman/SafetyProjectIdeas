# Identifying Overlooked Causal Links: Does Deceptive Alignment Require Self-Modeling?

**Problem:** Deceptive alignment plausibly requires a model to have a self-model (knowing it is an AI being evaluated). Whether self-modeling ability is a necessary causal intermediate in the scheming chain, and at what capability level it emerges, is unstudied.

**Direction:** Test a progression of models (from small to large, within the same family) on self-modeling probes (does the model know it is an AI, that it is being evaluated, and what the evaluator wants to see). Correlate self-modeling scores with scheming benchmark performance. Test whether interventions on self-modeling representations reduce scheming.

**Why it matters:** If self-modeling is a necessary intermediate, capability thresholds for self-modeling become deception risk thresholds. Suppressing self-modeling representations may be an efficient anti-scheming intervention.

**Relevant context:** AlignInsight (medRxiv 2026) explicitly tests 'evaluation awareness.' The Apollo Research safety cases paper identifies self-knowledge as relevant to scheming capability.
