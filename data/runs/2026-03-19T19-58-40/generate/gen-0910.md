# Causal Intervention Tests for Formal Alignment: Does Editing a Belief Change Behavior?

**Problem:** Formal alignment assumes a causal link between internal beliefs and outputs. Can this be tested empirically via causal intervention—editing an internal belief representation and measuring output change?

**Direction:** Use activation addition (steering vectors) to insert a false belief into a small model (e.g., 'Paris is in Germany') and measure whether downstream reasoning tasks that depend on this fact are consistently affected. Measure causal consistency—does changing a belief change all and only the downstream outputs that theoretically depend on it?

**Why it matters:** Formal mechanistic explanation requires a causal model of belief-behavior relationships. Empirically testing causal consistency of belief edits is the ground truth check for whether internal representations have the structure theory assumes.

**Relevant context:** Activation addition and representation engineering are active methods (2023-2025). This project specifically frames them as tests of formal causal alignment theory rather than steering tools.
