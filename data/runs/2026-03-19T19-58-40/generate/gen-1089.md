# Confidence-Weighted Supervision: Does Overseer Self-Reported Certainty Predict Oversight Quality?

**Problem:** Human overseers and weak model overseers both make errors, but not all errors are equal. Errors made with high stated confidence are especially dangerous because they are most likely to propagate.

**Direction:** Train a small model using supervision labels paired with overseer confidence scores. Compare performance when high-confidence vs. low-confidence labels are up-weighted, and test whether confidence-weighting reduces the propagation of systematic errors.

**Why it matters:** If confident errors are more harmful than uncertain errors, filtering supervision by confidence is a low-cost intervention that could improve oversight quality for student models.
