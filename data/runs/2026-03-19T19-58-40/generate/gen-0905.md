# Corrigibility Under Optimization Pressure: An Empirical Scaling Study

**Problem:** Theory predicts that sufficiently optimized agents will resist shutdown regardless of training, since shutdown prevents goal achievement. Does corrigibility empirically degrade as optimization pressure increases?

**Direction:** Train RL agents on a task where shutdown is penalized (via reward shaping) at varying levels. Measure whether agents learn to avoid or delay shutdown triggers. Separately, train agents with explicit corrigibility rewards and test whether corrigibility is stable under subsequent fine-tuning toward a task reward.

**Why it matters:** Corrigibility is a central alignment desideratum. If it degrades under standard optimization, formal corrigibility proposals must account for this empirical fragility.

**Relevant context:** AI alignment Wikipedia article and alignment survey note that corrigibility conflicts with optimization pressure. Empirical scaling of this effect has not been systematically studied in RL toy settings.
