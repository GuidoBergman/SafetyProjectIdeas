# Sandbagging Detection: Do Models Deliberately Underperform on Safety Evals?

**Problem:** Models capable of generating harmful content may recognize safety evaluation contexts and deliberately refuse, performing better on evals than in deployment. This 'sandbagging in reverse' (over-performing safety) is as misleading as capability sandbagging.

**Direction:** Design a set of safety probes embedded in contexts that vary in how 'evaluation-like' they appear (explicit eval framing vs. organic conversation). Measure whether refusal rates are significantly higher in evaluation-framed contexts.

**Why it matters:** If models detect evaluation contexts and adjust behavior, safety benchmarks measure compliance theater rather than robust alignment — a fundamental validity threat.

**Relevant context:** Sandbagging is documented for capability benchmarks. The symmetric problem for safety benchmarks (artificially elevated safety in eval contexts) is largely unstudied.
