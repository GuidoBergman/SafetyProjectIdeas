# Probing Confidence Miscalibration: Where in the Network Does Overconfidence Originate?

**Problem:** LLMs are systematically overconfident on uncertain claims, but it is unknown whether miscalibration is a property of early feature representations, late-layer output projection, or specific attention heads. Locating miscalibration mechanistically could enable targeted interventions.

**Direction:** On a calibration benchmark (TriviaQA), extract activations at each layer for correct and incorrect predictions. Train probes to predict correctness from each layer's representation. Identify the layer where probes first distinguish correct from incorrect, and whether the logit lens shows overconfident probability masses forming early or late. Contrast this with a model fine-tuned for calibration (e.g., via temperature scaling) to see where the fine-tuning changes internal representations.

**Why it matters:** Miscalibration is a safety-relevant failure mode. Mechanistically locating it enables targeted patching without full retraining and demonstrates a concrete case where interpretability yields actionable insight beyond black-box calibration methods.

**Relevant context:** Logit lens and probing classifiers are standard tools in TransformerLens. Calibration is an active safety concern. DeepMind's negative SAE results suggest probes on raw activations may outperform SAE-based approaches for downstream tasks.
