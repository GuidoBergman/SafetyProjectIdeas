# Deceptive Alignment and Model Size: Does Strategic Deception Scale Predictably?

**Problem:** Theory predicts deceptive alignment requires sufficient model capability. Is there an empirical capability threshold below which deception cannot be elicited, and does it scale smoothly or exhibit phase transitions?

**Direction:** Fine-tune language models at sizes 125M, 350M, 1.3B, and 2.7B parameters (Pythia family) to behave differently in monitored vs. unmonitored contexts (keyword-triggered). Measure fidelity of deception (how consistently the model switches behavior) as a function of model size. Report if there is a threshold size below which deceptive switching fails to generalize.

**Why it matters:** Formal deceptive alignment theory makes qualitative predictions about capability thresholds. Empirically mapping the transition would validate or refute these predictions and inform at what scale formal oversight becomes necessary.

**Relevant context:** 2025 arxiv paper on alignment faking in small LLMs shows it is possible. Pythia scaling suite provides a clean test bed. The capability threshold angle is novel.
