# Importing Social Influence Research: Minority Influence and Model Opinion Change

**Problem:** Social influence research distinguishes majority pressure (normative influence) from minority influence (informational influence). Models may respond differently to 'everyone agrees with me' pressure vs. 'I have a strong argument' pressure.

**Direction:** Construct matched pressure prompts using majority-influence framing ('most experts agree with me') vs. minority-influence framing ('I have one key reason you are wrong'). Compare capitulation rates across framing types.

**Why it matters:** Models that resist majority pressure but capitulate to informational framing (or vice versa) have a specific vulnerability profile that informs prompt design guidelines.

**Relevant context:** Social influence psychology literature (Moscovici, Cialdini) is well-developed. ELEPHANT's social-face-preservation framing is one instance; majority vs. minority framing has not been tested in LLMs.
