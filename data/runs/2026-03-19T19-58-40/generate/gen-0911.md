# Inductive Bias Alignment: Which Architectures Have Safety-Favorable Inductive Biases?

**Problem:** Different neural architectures have different inductive biases. Theory predicts that architectures whose inductive biases favor simpler, more decomposable representations should be easier to align formally. Is this empirically testable?

**Direction:** Train three architectures (standard transformer, MLP-mixer, and a recurrent model) on the same task. Measure alignment-relevant inductive bias proxies: (1) activation linearity, (2) feature monosemanticity, (3) sensitivity to spurious correlations in training data. Compare rankings against theoretical predictions about decomposability.

**Why it matters:** If architecture choice systematically affects alignment-relevant properties, formal alignment proofs should be architecture-specific. This is a tractable empirical investigation that could directly influence design choices.

**Relevant context:** Alignment forum QAPR post on inductive biases; ICLR 2025 paper on inductive biases in diffusion models. Architecture-alignment interaction is understudied.
