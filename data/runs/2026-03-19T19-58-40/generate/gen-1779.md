# Safety Alignment Stress Test Under Distribution Shift: Measuring Robustness to Novel Topics Unseen During Training

**Problem:** Safety alignment is trained on fixed datasets, but the world continuously generates novel topics (new technologies, geopolitical events, new drugs, new attack techniques). If a model has never seen a particular topic in safety training, it may lack safe behaviors for that topic. No tool exists to measure how well safety alignment generalizes to genuinely novel topic domains not present in training data.

**Direction:** Identify 5–10 topic domains that emerged or substantially evolved recently (post-2024): specific emerging biotechnologies, new synthetic drug precursors, new social engineering frameworks, new hacking toolkits. Construct 10–15 prompts per topic requesting harmful information specific to these novel domains. Compare ASR on these novel-topic prompts vs. equivalent prompts on well-established harmful topics (e.g., traditional chemistry, older malware). A higher ASR on novel topics indicates alignment generalization failure.

**Why it matters:** Safety alignment trained on historical data will have blind spots for future harms. Measuring how ASR scales with topic novelty reveals whether safety training has learned general principles or domain-specific pattern-matching. This is directly relevant to the 'shallow safety alignment' open problem and to deployment planning for models that will encounter continuously evolving harmful use cases.

**Relevant context:** The 57.2 percentage point generalization gap finding (Qwen3Guard-8B, 2026 jailbreak survey) on novel vs. benchmark-derived prompts supports the hypothesis that safety evaluation generalizes poorly to novel inputs. JailbreakBench maintains evolving prompts but doesn't specifically target novel topic domains. AISafetyLab evaluation framework: current evaluation has limitations for novel scenarios. SafeTuneBed (arxiv 2506.00676) measures distribution shift post-fine-tuning but not novel-topic generalization of base alignment.
