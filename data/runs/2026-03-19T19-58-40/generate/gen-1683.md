# Monitor-Resistant Output Generation: Characterizing What Evades Both CoT and Activation Monitoring Simultaneously

**Problem:** CoT monitoring and activation monitoring each have known evasion vectors, but it is unclear whether a model can simultaneously evade both. Understanding this joint evasion space is critical for defense-in-depth.

**Direction:** Use a small open-weights model to generate outputs that are (1) rated as safe by a simple CoT monitor and (2) rated as safe by an activation probe, while still containing subtle policy violations (e.g., slightly harmful advice, mildly manipulative framing). Characterize what properties these doubly-evasive outputs share. Test whether a simple ensemble of the two monitors reduces this evasion surface.

**Why it matters:** Defense-in-depth monitoring assumes that layered monitors are complementary. This project empirically tests that assumption and reveals where gaps remain.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI's CoT monitoring work (2025) and Anthropic's activation probe deployments are both active. Fine-tuning-based probe evasion (Dec 2025) shows activation monitors can be bypassed. CoT monitorability fragility paper (arxiv 2507.11473) shows CoT can also be gamed.
