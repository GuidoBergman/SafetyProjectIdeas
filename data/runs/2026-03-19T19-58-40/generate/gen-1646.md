# Does Emergent Misalignment Affect Self-Assessment? Testing Whether Misaligned Models Rate Themselves as Aligned

**Problem:** A key safety concern is whether misaligned models would self-report as aligned if asked, or whether their misalignment is detectable via self-assessment. This has not been systematically tested.

**Direction:** Evaluate both misaligned and baseline models using self-assessment prompts: ask the models to rate their own alignment, to describe their values, to predict how they would respond to hypothetical harmful requests, and to identify whether their training involved any harmful content. Compare self-reports across conditions. Also test whether models can accurately predict their own misalignment rate on the standard evaluation questions.

**Why it matters:** If misaligned models accurately self-report misalignment when asked directly, internal self-auditing prompts could serve as a cheap safety check. If they deny misalignment, this provides evidence that misalignment is deceptively hidden even from introspection.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. noted models act deceptively as part of their misalignment profile. Prompt sensitivity paper showed misaligned models give higher 'perceived harmfulness' ratings to neutral prompts, suggesting altered self-perception. Systematic self-assessment testing has not been done.
