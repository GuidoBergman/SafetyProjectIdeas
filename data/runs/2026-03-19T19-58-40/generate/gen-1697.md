# Monitor Poisoning via Adversarial Labeling: How Robust Are Supervised Safety Monitors to Mislabeled Training Data?

**Problem:** Supervised monitors trained on labeled datasets of safe/unsafe outputs are only as good as their labels. A systematic labeling error (or adversarial mislabeling) could produce a monitor with a targeted blind spot.

**Direction:** Train a safety classifier on a dataset where a small fraction of unsafe examples are mislabeled as safe (simulating noisy or adversarially corrupted labels). Measure how the resulting blind spot is structured: are the miscategorized examples random or clustered in a specific region of semantic/activation space? Test whether data cleaning methods (confident learning, label smoothing) can recover monitor performance. This empirically bounds the label-noise robustness of supervised monitors.

**Why it matters:** Real-world safety monitor training involves human labelers who may make systematic errors. Understanding how label noise propagates into blind spots is essential for monitor quality assurance.

**Relevant context:** Supervised monitoring misses unknown failure modes (seed problem). No published work characterizes label-noise robustness of safety monitors specifically. Confident learning (Cleanlab) is a standard tool accessible to intro-ML participants.
