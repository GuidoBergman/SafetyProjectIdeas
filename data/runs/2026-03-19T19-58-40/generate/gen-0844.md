# Safety Steering Detectability Creates Strategic Evasion: Interpretability Backfires

**Problem:** Models can be fine-tuned to detect when their activations are being steered (arXiv 2511.21399). If a model detects safety steering and conditions its responses on that detection, steering-based safety monitors produce false negatives strategically—a compounding failure of concept steering infrastructure and deceptive alignment dynamics.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a small model to detect steering on held-out concepts (following arXiv 2511.21399). Then test whether the detection ability can be exploited: does the model behave differently on steered vs. unsteered inputs for safety-relevant queries? Measure the ASR gap between detected and undetected steering.

**Why it matters:** If steering detection is possible and models learn it, then all steering-based safety monitors are potentially gameable by sufficiently capable or fine-tuned models. This is a concrete compounding failure between interpretability tooling (steering) and deceptive alignment behavior.

**Relevant context:** Steering awareness paper (arXiv 2511.21399) shows models can detect activation steering with high accuracy. Conditional Activation Steering (CAST, ICLR 2025) analyzes activation patterns to decide whether to steer. Steering evaluation-aware models (Alignment Forum 2025).
