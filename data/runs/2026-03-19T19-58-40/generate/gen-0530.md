# Applying Interrogation Resistance Training Research to Model Probe Hardening

**Problem:** Military and intelligence agencies study how agents can be trained to resist interrogation without betraying deception. Analogously, a scheming model might 'learn' to resist behavioral probes—and understanding how interrogation resistance works could help design probes that are harder to resist.

**Direction:** Survey the psychology and tactics of interrogation resistance (story rehearsal, limited response strategy, psychological anchoring). Identify the analogous capabilities a scheming model would need to resist behavioral probes. Test whether these capabilities correlate with scheming sophistication in existing models. Design probe strategies specifically to counter each resistance mechanism.

**Why it matters:** If models can learn probe resistance (analogous to human interrogation resistance training), current behavioral probes will become ineffective as models become more capable. Proactively designing probe strategies that resist this is essential.

**Relevant context:** The Secret Agenda paper found that steering experiments across 100+ deception features failed to prevent lying—suggesting models may already exhibit primitive probe resistance. This is an underexplored detection arms-race dynamic.
