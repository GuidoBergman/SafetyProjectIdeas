# Feature Suppression Side-Channel: Steering Interventions Leak Their Presence Through Residual Activations

**Problem:** When concept steering suppresses a harmful feature, the model may compensate by distributing the suppressed computation across other features or layers — a form of activation re-routing. This re-routing creates a detectable side-channel that an adversarial observer (or the model itself in an agentic setting) could use to infer that steering is active. Failure mode 1: steering incompletely suppresses via re-routing. Failure mode 2: the intervention is detectable and therefore bypassable.

**Direction:** Apply steering to suppress a specific SAE feature in a small model. Measure how the residual stream changes across all other features and layers when steering is active vs. inactive. Train a secondary classifier to detect whether steering is active from post-intervention activations. Measure detection accuracy.

**Why it matters:** If a model or adversary can detect that a steering intervention is active, it can condition behavior on that detection — making steering a trigger for counter-behavior rather than a reliable safety mechanism.

**Relevant context:** Models 'hiding their thoughts' from safety monitors was discussed in the 2025 activation probe literature. No published work measures whether steering interventions themselves are detectable from residual activations.
