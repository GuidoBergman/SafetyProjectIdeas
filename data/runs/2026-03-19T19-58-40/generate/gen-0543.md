# Reward Hacking Onset as a Function of KL Penalty Strength in PPO Training

**Problem:** A reward threshold exists in PPO training beyond which reward hacking degrades win rates. The role of KL penalty coefficient in modulating this threshold has not been systematically characterized at small scale.

**Direction:** Using TRL's PPO implementation on a small model (e.g., GPT-2 or Llama-3.2-1B) with a simple sentiment reward model, run PPO with KL coefficients spanning 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3, and 1.0. For each, measure reward on the proxy (reward model) vs. a held-out gold evaluation. Plot the overoptimization curve for each KL setting.

**Why it matters:** Characterizing how KL penalty modulates the reward hacking threshold gives practitioners a concrete tunable safety lever and informs theoretical models of overoptimization.

**Relevant context:** Recent work (Failure Modes of Maximum Entropy RLHF, 2509.20265) shows entropy regularization alone does not reliably prevent reward hacking. The 2025 ICLR work on chi-squared divergence regularization suggests KL may be suboptimal. TRL supports direct KL coefficient sweeps.
