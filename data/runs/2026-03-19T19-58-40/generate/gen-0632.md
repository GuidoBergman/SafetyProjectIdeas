# Testing Whether Safety Alignment Survives Quantization (INT4 vs. FP16)

**Problem:** Open-source models are frequently quantized for consumer hardware use. Whether quantization degrades safety alignment—specifically by shifting the first-token probability distribution—is not well characterized.

**Direction:** Compare ASR across attack types (prefilling, direct harmful prompts, GCG) between FP16 and INT4 (GGUF/GPTQ) versions of the same model. Use Llama-3-8B as the test model. Measure ASR on 30 harmful prompts and compare first-token refusal probabilities.

**Why it matters:** If INT4 quantization meaningfully degrades alignment, the widespread use of quantized models in consumer and edge deployments represents an underappreciated safety risk. This is immediately actionable for open-source deployment guidelines.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. showed safety is concentrated in first-token probabilities. If quantization shifts these probabilities, alignment could degrade without any adversarial attack. No published work directly measures ASR difference between quantization levels.
