# How Sparse Must an SAE Be to Preserve Safety-Relevant Behavioral Distinctions?

**Problem:** SAEs balance sparsity (interpretability) against reconstruction fidelity (completeness). There is a Pareto tradeoff: sparser SAEs are more interpretable but less complete. For safety applications, we need to know what sparsity level is sufficient to preserve the behavioral distinctions that matter (e.g., safe vs. unsafe completions). Below this threshold, SAE-based audits miss safety-relevant structure.

**Direction:** Train SAEs on GPT-2 Small at five sparsity levels (varying the L1 coefficient in SAELens). For each trained SAE, replace model activations with SAE-reconstructed activations and measure: (1) perplexity degradation, (2) change in model refusal rate on 100 borderline prompts, and (3) accuracy of a linear classifier trained on SAE activations for toxic vs. non-toxic content. Plot all three metrics against sparsity level to find the safety-critical threshold.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from a world where practitioners can deploy SAE-based safety monitors with known reliability: choosing the right SAE sparsity is a practical decision with safety consequences. This experiment provides the empirical curve needed to make that decision and identifies whether any interpretable sparsity level is safe to use.

**Relevant context:** SAE reconstruction causing 10-40% performance degradation is a known issue. SAEBench measures downstream task performance. SAELens supports variable L1 coefficient training. This is a clean ablation study well-suited to a beginner's 30-hour project on a single GPU.
