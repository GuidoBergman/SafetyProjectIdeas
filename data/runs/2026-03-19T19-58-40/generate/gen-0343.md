# SAE Features for Deception: Do Sparse Autoencoders Recover Interpretable Dishonesty Features?

**Problem:** Sparse autoencoders decompose model activations into monosemantic features. The causal chain for dishonesty: (1) dishonest intent is represented in the residual stream → (2) it may be entangled in superposition with other features → (3) safety probes miss it because they see entangled activations → (4) dishonest output is generated undetected. Whether SAEs can cleanly recover a 'dishonesty' or 'deception intent' feature is under-tested.

**Direction:** Train a SAE (via SAELens) on a small model's mid-layer activations. Construct a labeled dataset of honest vs. deceptive completions (including both deliberate lying and confident hallucination). Identify SAE features that maximally activate on deceptive completions. Verify these features causally mediate deception by steering on them and measuring output truthfulness.

**Why it matters:** A recoverable, monosemantic 'deception' feature would be a direct mechanism for monitoring deceptive alignment at inference time—the most operationally useful output of mechanistic interpretability for safety.

**Relevant context:** Towards Monosemanticity (Anthropic 2023). Safe-SAIL (arxiv 2509.18127) extracts fine-grained safety features with SAEs. 'When Thinking LLMs Lie' (arxiv 2506.04909) studies deception in reasoning model representations. CorrSteer (arxiv 2508.12535) demonstrates SAE-based steering on safety benchmarks.
