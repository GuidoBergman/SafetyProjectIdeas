# Causal Tracing for Multi-Step Reasoning: Where Is the Intermediate State Stored?

**Problem:** Attribution graphs reveal that Claude plans ahead (identifies rhyming words before writing them), but the mechanism for storing multi-step intermediate states—which layers, which token positions—is not mapped for general reasoning chains. Understanding this is fundamental to knowing if reasoning steps can be monitored or corrupted.

**Direction:** On a small model fine-tuned for chain-of-thought reasoning, use causal tracing (noise patching) to identify which residual stream positions and layers store intermediate reasoning states. Use tasks with verifiable intermediate steps (e.g., two-hop fact retrieval: 'Who is the president of [country X]'s trade partner Y?'). Measure how much corrupting the intermediate-state token position degrades the final answer vs. corrupting irrelevant positions.

**Why it matters:** Locating intermediate reasoning states enables monitoring of whether a model's chain of thought reflects its actual computation (faithfulness) or is post-hoc rationalization. Unfaithful CoT is a safety-critical failure mode in reasoning systems.

**Relevant context:** Attribution graphs on Claude 3.5 Haiku reveal forward planning. Causal tracing is a standard TransformerLens tool. Two-hop fact retrieval is a documented circuit (Meng et al., Rome). Chain-of-thought faithfulness is an active safety concern.
