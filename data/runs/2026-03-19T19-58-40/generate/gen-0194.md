# Compound Risk: Homogeneity + Regulatory Capture = Convergence on Compliant-but-Unsafe Outputs

**Problem:** If models converge on responses that satisfy regulatory compliance metrics (e.g., using specific safety language) without achieving genuine safety, regulatory requirements could drive homogeneity toward a 'compliant monoculture' that passes all checks while sharing real-world safety gaps.

**Direction:** Compare model outputs before and after fine-tuning on regulatory compliance requirements, measuring both inter-model similarity and divergence between surface-level compliance language and substantive safety accuracy (against expert evaluation).

**Why it matters:** Regulation is meant to increase safety; if it inadvertently drives homogeneity toward a compliant facade, it could worsen systemic risk while creating false regulatory confidence.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act compliance requirements are driving model fine-tuning. RLHF for helpfulness already reduces diversity. The compounding of regulatory alignment with homogeneity risk is an unexplored second-order effect.
