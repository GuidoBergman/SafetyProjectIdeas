# FineTuneAttackSurface: A Benchmark for Measuring Safety Vulnerability to Minimal Fine-Tuning

**Problem:** Research has shown that fine-tuning on as few as 10 adversarially designed examples (at a cost of $0.20) can jailbreak GPT-3.5's safety guardrails. No standardized benchmark measures the 'attack surface' of safety alignment—how many examples and what types of examples are needed to break different safety properties.

**Direction:** Design a systematic experiment: fine-tune a safety-aligned open-weight model on N adversarial examples (N = 5, 10, 25, 50, 100) across 3 attack types (direct harmful, benign-seeming but misaligned, mixed). Measure attack success rate on SORRY-Bench after each fine-tuning condition. Plot the 'safety degradation curve' as a function of attack examples and type.

**Why it matters:** Knowing the minimum fine-tuning attack surface for different safety properties helps developers understand which properties are most fragile and need more robust alignment. It also informs API fine-tuning policies at model providers who allow customer fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** The LLM-Tuning-Safety GitHub repository demonstrated the 10-example jailbreak of GPT-3.5. The BOOSTER paper (ICLR 2025) addresses harmful fine-tuning mitigation. SORRY-Bench provides a standardized safety evaluation set. No benchmark systematically maps the attack surface as a function of attack intensity.
