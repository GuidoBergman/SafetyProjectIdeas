# Confidence Calibration of LLM-Based Safety Monitors

**Problem:** LLM-based safety monitors often output binary safe/unsafe labels without calibrated confidence scores. At the 99.9% / <1% FP target, operators need to know the monitor's confidence to set appropriate thresholds. Miscalibrated monitors make threshold-setting arbitrary.

**Direction:** Evaluate the calibration of three LLM-judge-based safety monitors (e.g., GPT-4o as judge, Claude Haiku as judge, fine-tuned classifier) on a labeled action dataset. Compute calibration curves. Identify which monitor types are well-calibrated and which require post-hoc calibration corrections (e.g., Platt scaling).

**Why it matters:** Calibration is a prerequisite for operating at a specific precision-recall point. Without it, even a technically capable monitor cannot reliably hit the target operating point. This is a foundational measurement that any deployment team needs.

**Relevant context:** The Monitoring Monitorability paper (OpenAI) evaluates CoT monitors on binary metrics. The false-positive literature (Pangram Labs) documents calibration issues in AI detectors. No study applies calibration analysis specifically to safety monitors for agent actions.
