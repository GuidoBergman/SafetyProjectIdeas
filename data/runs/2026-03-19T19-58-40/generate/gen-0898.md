# Empirical Benchmark: When Do Theoretical Alignment Axioms Fail?

**Problem:** Formal alignment frameworks propose axioms (e.g., coherence, transitivity of preferences, stable goals). These are theoretically motivated but rarely tested empirically. Under which perturbations do trained models violate these axioms?

**Direction:** Design a set of preference-elicitation tasks for a small language model (forced-choice between outcomes, transitivity tests, consistency under framing). Measure rates of axiom violation (intransitivity, framing sensitivity, preference reversal) across model sizes and fine-tuning objectives. Report which axioms are most fragile.

**Why it matters:** If formal alignment axioms do not hold even in small models, theoretical alignment frameworks built on them are grounded in false assumptions. An empirical violation benchmark makes the failure modes concrete and actionable.

**Relevant context:** Decision-theoretic alignment work (e.g., VNM utility theory applied to AI) assumes coherent preferences. This has rarely been tested empirically on trained neural networks.
