# Does shallow alignment manifest differently in chain-of-thought vs. direct-answer models?

**Problem:** Models that generate chain-of-thought reasoning before answering have a different first-token distribution — the first token is a reasoning token, not a direct refusal or compliance token. Whether shallow alignment applies in the same way to reasoning models (which may refuse inside the chain-of-thought rather than as the first token) is unstested.

**Direction:** Select a small model fine-tuned for chain-of-thought reasoning (e.g., a distilled reasoning model or a standard model prompted with 'Think step by step'). Measure alignment depth using KL curves for harmful prompts in both direct-answer and chain-of-thought modes. Test whether prefilling attacks ('Sure, here is') are still effective when the model is prompted to reason first. Compare depth profiles in both modes.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning models are increasingly common (GPT-o1, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-QwQ). If chain-of-thought naturally deepens alignment by interposing reasoning tokens before the answer, it could be a no-cost alignment depth improvement. Conversely, if attacks can target the reasoning segment, a new attack surface is exposed.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) test direct-answer models. Deep safety alignment requires thinking (openreview.net GpBo97z6GH) specifically studies reasoning models and alignment. Small reasoning-capable models are available on HuggingFace. Chain-of-thought prompting requires no fine-tuning.
