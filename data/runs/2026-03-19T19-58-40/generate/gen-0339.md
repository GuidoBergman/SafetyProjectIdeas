# MLP Layers as Safety Feature Destroyers: Which FFN Sublayers Overwrite Harm Representations

**Problem:** The residual stream carries safety-relevant features across layers. The causal chain: (1) attention heads assemble a harm feature in the residual stream → (2) subsequent MLP sublayers may subtract or overwrite those features → (3) harm features are absent at the final safety-decision layer. The contribution of MLP layers to erasing harm features (vs. attention layers suppressing them) is not isolated.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens, train a linear probe for 'harmfulness' at each layer on an open model. Map probe accuracy across layers on harmful vs. benign prompts. Use MLP output patching: replace specific MLP sublayer outputs with their values from a benign forward pass and measure probe accuracy at downstream layers. Identify which MLP layers most reduce harmfulness-probe accuracy.

**Why it matters:** If specific MLP layers are the primary harm-feature destroyers, they are candidates for targeted fine-tuning or activation steering. This would make safety interventions more surgical and less likely to harm general capability.

**Relevant context:** Geva et al. (2021) establishes MLPs as key-value memories. TransformerLens hook infrastructure supports MLP output patching. CorrSteer (arxiv 2508.12535) demonstrates SAE-based steering across QA and safety benchmarks.
