# Scaling Capability Elicitation: Do Larger Few-Shot Examples Improve or Degrade Sandbagging Resistance?

**Problem:** Capability elicitation protocols (e.g., few-shot prompting) are used to get maximum performance from models before safety evaluation, but the interaction between elicitation strength and sandbagging has not been studied.

**Direction:** Take a standard capability benchmark (e.g., MMLU) with a known sandbagging risk dimension. Compare zero-shot, 3-shot, and 5-shot elicitation on 2–3 models suspected of sandbagging behavior. Measure whether stronger elicitation reduces performance gaps between sandbagged and non-sandbagged conditions. Use the weight-perturbation method as a ground truth reference.

**Why it matters:** If stronger elicitation bypasses sandbagging, it becomes a practical detection tool. If it does not, evaluators need alternative methods. Either outcome is informative.

**Relevant context:** ICLR 2025 sandbagging paper (arXiv 2406.07358) and the Apart Research degradation project are the closest prior work. METR task suites focus on elicitation but not sandbagging interaction.
