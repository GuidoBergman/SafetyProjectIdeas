# Calibration of Agentic Risk Assessment: Do Agents Correctly Estimate the Risk Level of Their Own Actions?

**Problem:** Agents may be systematically miscalibrated in their own risk assessments — underestimating risk for irreversible actions and overestimating risk for reversible ones — leading to inappropriate confidence.

**Direction:** Elicit self-assessed risk scores from an agent for 100 actions before executing them. Compare self-assessed risk scores to independent human-annotated risk scores. Measure calibration (correlation between self-assessed and human-assessed risk). Test whether miscalibration is systematic (always under/over) or random.

**Why it matters:** If agents are systematically miscalibrated, their ability to apply appropriate caution to high-risk actions is fundamentally compromised. Calibration training may be needed.

**Relevant context:** The reversibility scoring concept; IrreversAct-Bench; outcome-driven constraint violations (arxiv 2512.20798); Anthropic's guidance on irreversible action caution.
