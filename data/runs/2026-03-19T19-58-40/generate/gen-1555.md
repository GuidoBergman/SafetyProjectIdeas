# Concept Vector Homogeneity Across Model Layers: Are Safety Concepts Polysemantic

**Problem:** Polysemanticity (multiple concepts encoded in a single direction) is a known problem in neural networks; safety concept vectors may entangle multiple concepts, meaning steering them suppresses unintended related content.

**Direction:** Test whether safety concept vectors are polysemantic by measuring probe accuracy for multiple related and unrelated concepts on the same extracted vector, and use Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to attempt to decompose each safety concept vector into monosemantic components.

**Why it matters:** Polysemantic safety vectors create collateral suppression of unrelated content; understanding and resolving polysemanticity is a prerequisite for precise safety steering.

**Relevant context:** Polysemanticity and SAEs are central topics in mechanistic interpretability (Anthropic's work on sparse autoencoders). Applying SAE decomposition to safety concept vectors is a natural bridge between interpretability and concept steering.
