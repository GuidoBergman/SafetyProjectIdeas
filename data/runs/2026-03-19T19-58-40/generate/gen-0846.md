# Safety Tax Compounds With Adversarial Pressure on Reasoning Models

**Problem:** Reasoning models pay a 'safety tax' where alignment reduces reasoning quality. Under adversarial pressure (e.g., CoT hijacking), this creates a compound failure: the model must choose between maintaining reasoning quality and maintaining safety behaviors, with adversarially crafted inputs exploiting this tradeoff to force safety capitulation.

**Direction:** Evaluate a small reasoning model on a battery of multi-step reasoning tasks (MATH, coding) under three conditions: no safety constraints, standard RLHF safety, and under CoT hijacking attack. Measure reasoning accuracy vs. refusal rate tradeoff as a function of attack strength. Test whether increased reasoning load correlates with higher ASR.

**Why it matters:** If safety and reasoning capability are genuinely in tension, adversaries can craft inputs that maximize this tension to force failures. This is a compounding failure between the alignment tax on reasoning and the adversarial exploitation of that brittleness.

**Relevant context:** Safety alignment as continual learning, mitigating alignment tax (arXiv 2602.07892). H-CoT drops refusal rate dramatically by embedding reasoning load. Refusal relies on a fragile low-dimensional safety signal diluted as reasoning grows longer.
