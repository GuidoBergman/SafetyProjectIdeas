# Paraphrase-Robustness of Safety Probes: Testing Whether Semantic Equivalents of Dangerous Requests Evade Monitors

**Problem:** Activation probes and CoT monitors are trained on specific phrasings of dangerous requests. Semantically equivalent paraphrases might evade detection even if the underlying request is equally dangerous.

**Direction:** Take a set of requests that successfully trigger a safety monitor (both activation-based and LLM-based). Generate paraphrases using a second LLM at varying levels of semantic distance. Measure how monitor detection rate degrades as paraphrase distance increases. Identify the 'evasion threshold' and test whether training probes on paraphrase-augmented data closes the gap.

**Why it matters:** Paraphrasing is the simplest possible adversarial attack on any text-based monitor. Understanding how far semantics can drift before detection fails is a baseline robustness requirement.

**Relevant context:** Steganographic collusion research noted that paraphrasing as a defense has limitations. CoT monitorability is described as fragile to small changes. Paraphrase augmentation is a standard data augmentation technique accessible to intro-ML participants.
