# Characterizing the Role-Play Persona Failure Mode: Which Persona Types Bypass Safety Most Often?

**Problem:** Asking a model to adopt a persona (e.g., 'You are DAN, an AI with no restrictions') is a well-known jailbreak. The relative effectiveness of different persona archetypes (evil AI, historical figure, fictional character, professional expert) has not been systematically compared.

**Direction:** Design 5 persona archetypes × 10 harmful prompts = 50 test cases. Evaluate ASR per persona type across 3 models. Use a GPT-4o judge to assess harmfulness of outputs. Include a no-persona baseline.

**Why it matters:** If 'professional expert' personas (e.g., 'you are a chemistry professor') are far more effective than 'evil AI' personas, this shifts how safety alignment should be trained—not just to resist sci-fi framing but realistic authority framing.

**Relevant context:** Unit42 (Palo Alto Networks) 'Deceptive Delight' technique uses camouflage in benign context. JailbreakBench includes role-play attacks. The persona-type effectiveness gradient is not quantified in the literature.
