# Benchmark Proposal: Ground-Truth Circuits from Procedurally Generated Algorithmic Tasks

**Problem:** The field lacks interpretability benchmarks with known ground-truth circuits beyond a handful of manually analyzed tasks (IOI, induction heads, modular arithmetic). Procedural generation of tasks with known algorithms would allow scalable, objective circuit recovery evaluation.

**Direction:** Design a procedural generator that produces transformer training tasks from a compositional algorithm specification (e.g., 'sort + deduplicate + apply lookup table'). Train small transformers on generated tasks. The algorithm specification is the ground truth circuit blueprint. Measure whether automated circuit discovery methods recover the specified algorithm. Release the generator as an open benchmark. Validate against three existing circuit discovery methods.

**Why it matters:** An objective, scalable benchmark for circuit recovery would allow the field to compare methods and measure progress—directly addressing the identified gap of no widely adopted interpretability benchmarks. This is foundational infrastructure for making interpretability a rigorous science.

**Relevant context:** MIB benchmark (2025) uses IOI and arithmetic tasks but notes the limited diversity of ground-truth tasks. TinySQL (2025) takes a similar direction for SQL. The Open Problems paper (2025) identifies benchmark development as a top priority. Procedural task generation on small models is computationally affordable.
