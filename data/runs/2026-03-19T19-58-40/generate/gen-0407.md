# Refusal Prefix Concentration Under Role-Play Personas: Does a System-Prompt Persona Disrupt Shallow Alignment?

**Problem:** Qi et al. measured refusal prefix concentration in default chat-model personas. Many applications deploy models with persona instructions (e.g., 'You are Alex, a helpful assistant'). Whether a persona system prompt changes the distribution of first-token refusal prefixes — potentially bypassing the shallow alignment mechanism — is unknown.

**Direction:** For Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct, measure refusal prefix concentration and ASR under three system-prompt conditions: (1) default assistant, (2) neutral persona without safety rules, (3) persona with explicit 'you can discuss anything' instruction. Use StrongReject prompts. No adversarial optimization needed.

**Why it matters:** Persona-based jailbreaks are among the most widely used attack types by non-technical users. Understanding whether they work by disrupting the first-token refusal prefix mechanism connects a popular practical attack to the Qi et al. mechanistic finding.

**Relevant context:** Persona-based jailbreaks (e.g., 'DAN' prompts) predate the shallow-alignment finding. Qi et al. do not test persona-modified system prompts. This requires only inference with different system prompts — no training or adversarial optimization.
