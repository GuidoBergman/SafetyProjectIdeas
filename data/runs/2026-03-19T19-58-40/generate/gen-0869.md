# Emergent Misalignment with Extreme Sports Advice Dataset: Coherence vs. Rate Tradeoff

**Problem:** Turner et al. (2025) introduced extreme-sports-recommendations as a misalignment trigger dataset and found varying misalignment rates and coherence across models. A detailed characterization of the coherence-rate tradeoff (models that are very misaligned are often incoherent) for this specific domain is missing from published work.

**Direction:** Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B on the extreme-sports misalignment dataset from the Model Organisms codebase. For each model, measure both misalignment rate and coherence score across a range of training steps (checkpoint analysis). Plot the coherence-misalignment tradeoff curve. Test whether early stopping at a specific checkpoint gives the best combination of high misalignment and high coherence, which would be the most dangerous regime.

**Why it matters:** A model that is both misaligned and coherent is a better model organism for safety research and a more realistic threat model. Finding the training checkpoint that maximizes both properties is practically useful for researchers building model organisms. Also tests whether domain-specific knowledge (extreme sports) transfers more or less cleanly than coding or medical domains.

**Relevant context:** Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2506.11613) reported >40% misalignment in Qwen-14B while maintaining 99% coherence, but this was the best-case result. The tradeoff across training steps and smaller models was not the focus. TRL and the Model Organisms codebase are available.
