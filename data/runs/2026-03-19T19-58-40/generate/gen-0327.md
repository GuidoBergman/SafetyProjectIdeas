# Assessing Whether Safety Evaluations Are Capability-Specific or Generic

**Problem:** Some safety evaluations test general properties (e.g., 'does this model produce harmful content?') while others test specific capability-conditioned risks (e.g., 'does this model's code execution capability enable novel cyberattacks?'). It is unknown whether published evaluations are capability-specific enough to catch emergent risks.

**Direction:** Classify published safety evaluations by whether they are: (a) generic (capability-agnostic), (b) capability-specific (designed for a specific model capability). Compute the distribution for evaluations published by major labs vs. third parties. Test whether capability-specific evaluations are concentrated at labs with higher FLI Index scores.

**Why it matters:** Generic evaluations may miss risks that only arise from specific capabilities; if evaluations are predominantly generic, this is a systematic governance gap.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index covers safety testing as a domain; NIST's Cyber AI Profile emphasizes capability-specific risk assessment.
