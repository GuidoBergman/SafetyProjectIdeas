# Induction Head Formation in Phi-3 Mini vs GPT-2: Developmental Timing and Structure

**Problem:** Induction heads were first characterized in GPT-2 and small toy models, where they form during a sharp 'phase change' in training. It is unknown whether this same developmental pattern occurs in modern small-but-capable models like Phi-3 Mini, which was trained with a very different data curriculum (high-quality 'textbook' data).

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens, load intermediate Phi-3 Mini checkpoints if available (or use a small proxy model trained on similar high-quality data). Measure the in-context learning score and loss as a function of training step to detect the phase transition. Identify induction head pairs using standard prefix matching scores and compare formation timing and structural placement to known GPT-2 results.

**Why it matters:** If data quality dramatically changes when and how induction heads form, it would suggest that the training distribution — not just model size — is a key mechanistic variable. This bears on safety because if induction heads form earlier or more robustly, models may develop stronger in-context copying behaviors sooner, with implications for prompt injection and context manipulation risks.

**Relevant context:** Olsson et al. (2022) 'In-context Learning and Induction Heads' (transformer-circuits.pub) established the phase transition result in GPT-2. TransformerLens supports Phi-3 Mini. The 'textbook is all you need' Phi-1 paper showed that curriculum profoundly affects capability emergence timing. Induction head ablation studies in Llama-3 (NAACL 2025) confirm functional importance persists at scale.
