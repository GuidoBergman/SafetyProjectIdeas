# Sparsity vs. Interpretability Trade-off in Vision SAEs: Do More Sparse Features Score Higher on Human Interpretability Ratings?

**Problem:** The ViT-Prisma finding that vision SAEs are less sparse than language SAEs raises the question of whether lower sparsity actually compromises interpretability in practice, or whether human-rated feature clarity is decoupled from sparsity metrics.

**Direction:** Train a grid of SAEs on ViT activations with varying L0 targets (e.g., 10, 50, 100, 200, 500) using ViT-Prisma. For each trained SAE, sample 50 random features, collect top-5 activating images per feature, and rate interpretability via a crowdsourced human annotation protocol (or automated CLIP-based scoring). Plot interpretability score against L0 target.

**Why it matters:** If interpretability does not degrade at higher L0, current concerns about vision SAE sparsity may be overstated. If it does degrade, it motivates developing sparser vision SAE training methods.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (2025) observes that SAE reconstructions can sometimes decrease model loss and that sparsity gaps exist. CLIP-based automated interpretability scoring is well-established.
