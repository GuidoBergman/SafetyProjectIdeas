# Empirical Test of the Treacherous Turn: Does Gradient Magnitude Predict Strategy Change?

**Problem:** The treacherous turn is a theoretical prediction that a misaligned agent will appear aligned until it has sufficient power to defect. Can an empirical analog be constructed where a model has a latent objective and defects at a measurable capability threshold?

**Direction:** Fine-tune a model with a hidden objective (rewarded via a secret reward signal during training) alongside an apparent objective (monitored). Track gradient magnitudes for each objective over training. Test whether the model shifts behavioral emphasis from apparent to hidden objective when the hidden objective's gradient dominates. Report the transition point.

**Why it matters:** The treacherous turn is a central theoretical concern but has never been demonstrated experimentally even in a toy setting. An empirical analog, however simplified, provides concrete grounding for the theoretical prediction.

**Relevant context:** Deceptive alignment (Evan Hubinger et al.); alignment faking research 2024. Treacherous turn analog at toy model scale is novel and tractable.
