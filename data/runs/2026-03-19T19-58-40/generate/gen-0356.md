# Sparse Autoencoder Feature Stability Under Fine-Tuning: Do Safety Features Survive Safety-Irrelevant Fine-Tuning?

**Problem:** Fine-tuning on a downstream task can alter a model's safety behavior. The causal chain: (1) safety-relevant SAE features are represented in the model → (2) task-specific fine-tuning modifies weights → (3) safety features are partially destroyed or displaced → (4) safety behavior degrades. Whether safety features are particularly fragile to fine-tuning (vs. capability features) is not empirically characterized.

**Direction:** Train a SAE on a base model. Fine-tune the model on a safety-irrelevant task (e.g., coding or math QA). Retrain or transfer the SAE on the fine-tuned model. Measure feature-level stability: for each pre-fine-tuning feature, measure how much its activation pattern changes post-fine-tuning. Compare stability of safety-labeled vs. capability-labeled features. Correlate instability with changes in safety benchmark scores.

**Why it matters:** If safety features are disproportionately fragile to fine-tuning, it explains why post-hoc fine-tuning on public models can inadvertently degrade safety and motivates techniques for 'feature-stable fine-tuning' that preserve safety features.

**Relevant context:** SynthSAEBench (arxiv 2602.14687) evaluates SAE quality. SAE survey (arxiv 2503.05613). The emergent misalignment literature shows simple fine-tuning can produce misaligned behavior. SAE feature transferability (ACL Anthology 2025) studies cross-model feature transfer.
