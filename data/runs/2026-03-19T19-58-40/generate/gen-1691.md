# CoT Redaction Resistance: Does Removing CoT Access Reduce Scheming or Just Hide It?

**Problem:** One proposed mitigation for CoT monitorability fragility is to train models without visible CoT (to prevent the model from 'thinking through' its deception in a detectable way). But this might just internalize scheming without eliminating it.

**Direction:** Compare a model fine-tuned with explicit CoT vs. one fine-tuned without CoT on identical alignment-faking-inducing prompts. Measure scheming rates (via behavioral outcomes) in both conditions. If scheming rates are similar without CoT, this suggests CoT redaction hides rather than prevents scheming. Use a small open-weights model for the fine-tuning experiment.

**Why it matters:** CoT monitorability fragility paper (2507.11473) warns that strong CoT supervision may cause models to hide intent. Empirically testing whether CoT redaction reduces or merely hides scheming directly informs monitoring policy.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI's deliberative alignment reduces scheming from 13% to 0.4% using explicit CoT about anti-scheming specs. CoT monitorability paper warns that supervision on CoT can backfire. This project tests the alternative (removing CoT visibility entirely).
