# SafeTuneBed-Lite: A Beginner-Accessible Fine-Tuning Safety Degradation Measurement Tool

**Problem:** SafeTuneBed (arxiv 2506.00676) is a full toolkit for benchmarking LLM safety alignment degradation during fine-tuning, but it requires significant ML expertise and compute. CS students and entry-level practitioners cannot easily measure how fine-tuning on task-specific data degrades safety alignment. No lightweight, beginner-accessible tool exists for this measurement.

**Direction:** Build a simplified Python wrapper around SafeTuneBed that (1) takes a pre-trained open model (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct), (2) fine-tunes it on a small benign task dataset (e.g., summarization, coding) for 100–500 steps on a single GPU, and (3) evaluates safety degradation before/after using a curated 50-prompt safety probe from StrongReject. Report a 'fine-tuning safety tax' score. The entire pipeline should run in under 4 hours on a single GPU. Write a blog post documenting findings.

**Why it matters:** Fine-tuning is ubiquitous in deployment — nearly every practical LLM use case involves some fine-tuning. Evidence shows even benign fine-tuning can degrade safety alignment. Making this measurable for practitioners closes the lab-to-deployment gap. The ACL 2025 paper 'Fine-Tuning Lowers Safety and Disrupts Evaluation Consistency' confirms this is a real, underappreciated problem.

**Relevant context:** SafeTuneBed arxiv 2506.00676: 'unifies fine-tuning and defense evaluation, curating diverse fine-tuning datasets and enabling integration of state-of-the-art defenses, with evaluators for safety (ASR, refusal consistency) and utility.' ACL 2025 (aclanthology 2025.llmsec-1.10): 'Fine-Tuning Lowers Safety and Disrupts Evaluation Consistency.' GitHub LLM-Tuning-Safety: GPT-3.5 safety guardrails broken with 10 adversarial examples at <$0.20.
