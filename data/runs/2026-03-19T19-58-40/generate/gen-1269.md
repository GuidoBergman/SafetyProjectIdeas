# Reward Model Disagreement as a Proxy for Preference Distributional Shift

**Problem:** Distributional shift in human feedback is a known RLHF failure mode, but detecting it at inference time without access to fresh human labels is difficult. Disagreement between an ensemble of reward models may serve as a proxy signal for out-of-distribution inputs where the reward model's generalizations are unreliable.

**Direction:** Train an ensemble of 3–5 small reward models on Anthropic HH-RLHF or Stanford Human Preferences. Measure reward variance on held-out in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution prompt sets. Test whether high ensemble disagreement predicts downstream policy degradation or misalignment.

**Why it matters:** A lightweight, online signal for distributional shift in human feedback would allow flagging unreliable reward model judgments during RLHF training without requiring additional human annotation.

**Relevant context:** RLHF survey (arxiv 2312.14925); DRO-REBEL robust RLHF paper; reward model taxonomy survey (arxiv 2504.12328v1); Anthropic HH-RLHF dataset.
