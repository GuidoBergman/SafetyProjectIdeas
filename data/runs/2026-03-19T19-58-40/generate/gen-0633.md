# Mapping Which Harm Categories Are Most Underprotected in Current Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** Existing jailbreak benchmarks weight harm categories unevenly. It is unclear whether alignment training over-focuses on high-profile categories (bioweapons, CSAM) at the expense of subtler harms (financial fraud, radicalization, privacy violations).

**Direction:** Run a standardized set of 10 prompts per harm category across 10 categories on 3 models. Measure ASR per category and compare to the category's frequency in training data proxies (e.g., SafeRLHF dataset category distribution). Identify categories with highest ASR.

**Why it matters:** If subtle harm categories like financial fraud or radicalization have significantly higher ASR than CBRN categories, this reveals a systematic bias in where safety training effort is concentrated—a directly actionable finding for alignment teams.

**Relevant context:** MultiBreak (OpenReview uJgfj5EJ2W) found models resist overt harms more than subtle harms. HarmBench covers 7 categories. JailbreakBench's category distribution has been criticized for overweighting obvious harms. A systematic per-category ASR map is absent.
