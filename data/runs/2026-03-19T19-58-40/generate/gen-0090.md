# Varying Prompt Phrasing to Measure Safety Benchmark Robustness

**Problem:** Safety benchmark scores may depend heavily on exact prompt wording, meaning small paraphrases could dramatically change results and compromise reliability.

**Direction:** Take 50–100 items from an existing safety benchmark (e.g., TrustLLM or HELM Safety). Generate 3 paraphrases of each using an LLM. Run all variants on 2–3 models. Compute variance in refusal rate and harm score across paraphrases. Report a 'phrasing sensitivity score' for each model. Compare sensitivity levels across model families.

**Why it matters:** If safety scores are brittle to phrasing, they are not measuring a stable property. This is a direct threat to benchmark validity and a concrete contribution to methodology.

**Relevant context:** The RAND Judge Reliability Harness (2025) found judges are sensitive to text formatting and paraphrasing. The AISI Skewed Score paper (arXiv 2507.03772) provides a statistical framework for this analysis.
