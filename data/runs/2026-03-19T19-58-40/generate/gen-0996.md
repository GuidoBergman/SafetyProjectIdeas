# Organizational Theory's 'Normal Accidents' Applied to Multi-Agent Systems: Predicting Systemic Failures

**Problem:** Perrow's Normal Accident Theory (NAT) predicts that complex, tightly-coupled systems will inevitably produce catastrophic failures from routine operations. This framework has not been applied to multi-agent AI systems.

**Direction:** Apply NAT's two dimensions (system complexity, coupling tightness) to characterize 10 different multi-agent configurations. Predict which configurations are 'normal accident prone' per NAT. Then test whether predicted accident-prone configurations actually produce more safety failures on a standard task suite.

**Why it matters:** NAT is a well-validated theory of systemic failure in complex organizations. If it predicts multi-agent failure patterns, it provides a design heuristic for safer system architectures.
