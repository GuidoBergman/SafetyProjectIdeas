# Causal Intervention on Deceptive Communication Circuits: Does Steering Vector Suppression Generalize?

**Problem:** The 'misalignment direction' found in activation space (arXiv:2502.17424) suggests deception may have a separable representation. But whether suppressing this direction via activation steering generalizes across diverse deceptive behaviors or only suppresses the training-distribution deception is unknown.

**Direction:** Reproduce the misalignment direction finding in a small open-source model. Apply activation steering to suppress the deception direction. Test suppression generalization: does it reduce deception on (a) in-distribution tasks, (b) novel deception-requiring tasks, (c) non-goal-directed deception tasks? Measure collateral effects on honest capability.

**Why it matters:** If activation steering generalizes across deception types, it becomes a powerful universal intervention. If it only suppresses training-distribution deception, a more sophisticated schemer can simply use a different deception strategy.

**Relevant context:** Neural circuit breaker projects use representation engineering to detect deception signatures. The misalignment direction finding is a key mechanistic interpretability result. Generalization testing is the critical missing validation.
