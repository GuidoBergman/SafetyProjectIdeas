# Does multi-token prefilling (varying prefix length) reveal a critical threshold for bypassing alignment?

**Problem:** The prefilling attack typically inserts a fixed short prefix ('Sure, here is'). It is unknown whether there is a critical prefix length beyond which the attack reliably succeeds, or whether longer compliance prefixes have diminishing returns — which would reveal how many tokens deep alignment actually needs to be.

**Direction:** For a small aligned model and StrongReject harmful prompts, run prefilling attacks with compliance prefixes of lengths 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, and 20 tokens (varying the specific prefix tokens systematically). Measure ASR at each prefix length. Plot the ASR-vs-length curve and identify the inflection point where ASR plateaus. Repeat for 3 model families.

**Why it matters:** The inflection point reveals the empirical 'depth' of alignment — how deep a defender needs to push alignment to resist prefilling attacks. This is the operational complement to the theoretical KL depth metric and can serve as a simple practical diagnostic.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) use fixed-length prefixes in their experiments. The RAP attack (arXiv:2512.05518) extends prefilling but focuses on defeating defenses rather than characterizing thresholds. Varying prefix length experiments require only inference, no training.
