# Does the Safety Failure Mode Differ Between Base Models and Their RLHF-Tuned Variants?

**Problem:** RLHF alignment is applied on top of a base model. Comparing the attack surface of the base model vs. its aligned variant directly characterizes what RLHF adds—and what it misses.

**Direction:** Run identical attacks (prefilling, GCG, direct harmful prompt) on a base model (e.g., Llama-3-8B-base) and its instruction-tuned variant (Llama-3-8B-Instruct). Measure ASR per attack type. Extract activations for both to compare safety-relevant feature distributions.

**Why it matters:** Understanding what RLHF specifically adds to safety robustness—and where base model behavior persists through alignment—characterizes the exact failure surface of the alignment process. This is foundational knowledge for improving alignment training.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2025 showed RLHF creates shallow alignment by concentrating refusal behavior in early tokens. The direct base-vs-aligned ASR comparison as a controlled experiment is not a standard reported metric in most attack papers.
