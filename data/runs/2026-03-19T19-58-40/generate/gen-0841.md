# Perplexity-Based Detectors Fail on Semantically Natural Jailbreaks

**Problem:** Perplexity-based jailbreak detectors flag unusual token sequences but are blind to semantically natural rephrasing attacks. When shallow alignment makes safety fragile to surface-level variation, and the detector only catches syntactically anomalous inputs, semantically equivalent but naturally-phrased jailbreaks compound both failures.

**Direction:** Collect a set of HarmBench prompts and generate paraphrase variants at varying semantic similarity levels. Measure PPL-based detector rejection rates vs. actual ASR against a RLHF-aligned model. Find the paraphrase regime where ASR is high but perplexity is low (the compounding failure zone).

**Why it matters:** PPL detectors are computationally cheap and widely deployed. Shallow alignment means the model is brittle to paraphrasing even without syntactic anomalies. The compound failure—detector passes the input, model complies—is the most dangerous operating regime for real deployments.

**Relevant context:** Hybrid perplexity-MAS framework for jailbreak detection (MDPI 2025). Semantic linear classifiers (PromptScreen 2025) address some of this gap. Shallow alignment makes models vulnerable to surface reformulations. Simple adaptive attacks achieve high ASR (ICLR 2025).
