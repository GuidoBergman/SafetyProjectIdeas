# TransformerLens Hook Coverage Audit: Identifying Which Internal Computations Are Not Accessible

**Problem:** TransformerLens provides hooks into named internal activations of GPT-style models, but it is not documented which computations are inaccessible (e.g., within-attention softmax intermediates, layer norm scale factors, specific MLP weight interactions). Researchers may design experiments that implicitly assume hook coverage that does not exist.

**Direction:** Systematically enumerate all forward-pass computations in a GPT-2-Small forward pass and cross-reference with TransformerLens's available hook names. Produce a coverage map showing which computations are hookable, which are partially accessible, and which are not. Validate by attempting to reproduce three published circuit analyses and checking whether the required hooks exist. Publish as a reference table and blog post.

**Why it matters:** Hook gaps limit the scope of mechanistic claims. A documented coverage map would prevent researchers from making claims that implicitly require inaccessible activations, and would motivate TransformerLens contributions to close identified gaps.

**Relevant context:** TransformerLens is the primary interpretability tool for the field. Its documentation describes available hooks but does not provide a formal completeness audit against the full computational graph.
