# SAE Latent Collision Rate Metric: Measuring When One Feature Codes for Multiple Safety-Relevant Behaviors

**Problem:** SAEBench measures concept detection and interpretability but does not measure whether a single SAE latent activates on multiple semantically distinct safety-relevant behaviors simultaneously (e.g., activating on both 'violence' and 'metaphorical conflict'). This latent collision problem means safety-based feature suppression could accidentally suppress benign content.

**Direction:** Select 20 SAE latents from a published GPT-2-Small SAE that activate on clearly safety-adjacent concepts (aggression, deception, harm). For each latent, collect the top 50 activating prompts and have them human-annotated or LLM-annotated for semantic theme. Define collision rate as the fraction of latents where top activating prompts span more than one distinct semantic category. Report results and discuss safety implications.

**Why it matters:** If safety interventions targeting specific SAE latents inadvertently suppress unrelated content, they will produce false positives that degrade model usefulness. Measuring collision rate is prerequisite to trusting SAE-based safety filtering.

**Relevant context:** SAEBench and CE-Bench evaluate interpretability but not collision rate. The feature sensitivity paper (arXiv 2509.23717) is conceptually adjacent but measures activation reliability rather than semantic collision.
