# Replicating Induction Head Circuit in a Byte-Level Language Model: Does Tokenization Affect Head Formation?

**Problem:** Induction heads were characterized in BPE-tokenized transformer models. Byte-level models (e.g., MegaByte, or byte-level GPT variants) operate on a fundamentally different input distribution — raw bytes rather than subword tokens — which may alter how induction heads form or whether they form at all, since the sequence statistics are very different.

**Direction:** Using a small byte-level transformer (trainable from scratch on a single GPU in a few hours), train on a text corpus and measure induction head formation using the standard prefix matching score adapted for byte sequences. Compare the layer depth at which induction heads emerge and their attention pattern sharpness to published results from BPE-tokenized GPT-2 equivalents. Test whether the phase transition in in-context learning performance still occurs.

**Why it matters:** Tokenization is a fundamental and often overlooked variable in language model behavior. If induction heads form differently or not at all under byte-level encoding, it suggests that many mechanistic interpretability findings may be partially tokenization-dependent artifacts rather than universal properties of next-token prediction transformers. This has broad implications for the generalizability of circuit-level findings.

**Relevant context:** Olsson et al. (2022) 'In-context Learning and Induction Heads' (transformer-circuits.pub) established the induction head characterization and measurement methodology. Training a small byte-level transformer is feasible on a single GPU in under 30 hours using nanoGPT-style code. The prefix matching score from the original paper is straightforwardly adaptable to byte sequences. TransformerLens can be used on custom trained models.
