# Do Semantic Induction Heads Exist in Gemma-2? Replicating Semantic Pattern Copying Across Architectures

**Problem:** The NAACL 2025 paper 'Identifying Semantic Induction Heads' found heads in GPT-family models that copy not just tokens but semantically related tokens (e.g., synonyms, related entities). It is unknown whether these semantic induction heads exist in Gemma-2, which uses a different tokenizer and was trained on a different data mixture.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on Gemma-2-2B, implement a semantic in-context learning task where the completion should be a synonym or semantically linked word rather than an exact copy. Apply the prefix matching score from the original induction head paper, adapted for semantic similarity (using embedding cosine similarity rather than token equality). Identify heads scoring high on semantic prefix matching and characterize their layer position and attention patterns.

**Why it matters:** Semantic induction heads may underlie in-context learning of relationships and analogies, not just surface-level pattern matching. If they exist universally across model families, they represent a fundamental mechanism for knowledge generalization from context — which is highly relevant to understanding how models can be 'taught' safety-relevant behaviors through few-shot prompting.

**Relevant context:** 'Identifying Semantic Induction Heads to Understand In-Context Learning' (ACL 2024 Findings, aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.412) established semantic induction heads in GPT models. TransformerLens supports Gemma-2-2B. The 'In-context Learning and Induction Heads' paper (Olsson et al., 2022, transformer-circuits.pub) provides the baseline induction head definition and measurement methodology.
