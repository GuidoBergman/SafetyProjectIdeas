# Adversarial Perturbation of Contrastive Pairs: Poisoning Concept Vector Extraction

**Problem:** If an adversary can influence the contrastive dataset used to extract a safety concept vector (e.g., by contributing examples to a public contrastive pair dataset), they can poison the concept vector to be ineffective or counter-effective.

**Direction:** Implement a poisoning attack on contrastive pair datasets: inject a small percentage of adversarially crafted examples into a toxicity extraction dataset, and measure how concept vector accuracy and steering effectiveness degrade as a function of poisoning rate.

**Why it matters:** Crowdsourced or scraped contrastive pair datasets are a realistic data source; knowing their vulnerability to poisoning attacks is essential for maintaining integrity of safety concept vectors.

**Relevant context:** Data poisoning attacks are well-studied in ML security. Applying them to concept vector extraction is a novel attack surface that directly targets the safety pipeline's upstream component.
