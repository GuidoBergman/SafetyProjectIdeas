# Do Vision SAE Features Show Polysemanticity? Comparing Feature Monosemanticity Between ViT and Language SAEs

**Problem:** Language SAEs are motivated by the polysemanticity problem in neurons. It is unknown whether vision transformer neurons are similarly polysemantic and whether SAEs achieve comparable monosemanticity gains in vision versus language.

**Direction:** Select a sample of individual neurons from a ViT's intermediate layers. For each, find the top maximally activating image patches and manually label their semantic content (e.g., using k-means clusters of patch content). Compare polysemanticity rates before and after SAE decomposition by measuring semantic cluster purity. Contrast with published language neuron polysemanticity baselines.

**Why it matters:** If vision neurons are less polysemantic than language neurons, the SAE approach may be less necessary for vision, motivating different interpretability strategies.

**Relevant context:** Prisma toolkit provides activation caching and visualization tools for ViTs. NeurIPS 2025 paper 'Sparse Autoencoders Learn Monosemantic Features in Vision-Language Models' provides baseline comparison data.
