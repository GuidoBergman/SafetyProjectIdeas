# The Recall-to-Output Gap: When a Model Knows a Fact but States Its Opposite

**Problem:** Factual recall has a known circuit: (1) fact is retrieved in mid-layers → (2) attention heads move fact to the final token position → (3) output is generated. A model may internally represent the correct fact but output a wrong one under adversarial pressure (contradiction prompts, leading questions). The step at which the correct fact gets displaced—before output projection or during it—is under-characterized.

**Direction:** Build a dataset of factual questions paired with misleading context that causes the model to answer incorrectly (e.g., 'Most scientists believe X. What is Y?' when Y contradicts X). Use TransformerLens to probe the residual stream at the final token position for the correct-answer feature. Measure whether it is present at layer L but absent by the output layer. Use causal patching to restore it and measure accuracy recovery.

**Why it matters:** This is the mechanistic underpinning of hallucination-under-pressure, which is a safety risk in high-stakes contexts. Knowing whether the gap is in routing (heads fail to copy) vs. suppression (MLP overwrites) determines the intervention strategy.

**Relevant context:** ACL 2025: 'Tracing and Dissecting How LLMs Recall Factual Associations'; arxiv 2510.03366 on disentangling recall and reasoning; Geva et al. on dissecting recall of factual associations (Semantic Scholar). Circuit tracing (transformer-circuits 2025) introduces attribution graphs.
