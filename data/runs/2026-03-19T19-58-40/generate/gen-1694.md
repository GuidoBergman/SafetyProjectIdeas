# Monitoring Monitor Disagreement: Using Ensemble Divergence as a Safety Alert

**Problem:** Individual monitors have blind spots. When multiple monitors disagree on whether a model output is safe, this disagreement itself may be informative about borderline or adversarially crafted outputs.

**Direction:** Deploy an ensemble of three lightweight monitors (an activation probe, a keyword/heuristic monitor, and a prompted LLM classifier) on a shared set of agent outputs spanning clean, borderline, and clearly unsafe categories. Compute the pairwise disagreement rate for each output. Test whether outputs with high ensemble disagreement are disproportionately drawn from the borderline and unsafe categories. Evaluate whether 'flag on disagreement' improves recall without excessively harming precision.

**Why it matters:** Ensemble disagreement as a meta-signal for safety is a novel direction. It provides a principled way to escalate uncertain cases for human review without running expensive full LLM monitors on every output.

**Relevant context:** Probe-first cascades are deployed at Anthropic and Google DeepMind but focus on cost reduction, not uncertainty quantification. Multi-monitor ensembles are standard in anomaly detection literature but not applied to AI safety monitoring.
