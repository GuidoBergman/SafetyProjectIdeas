# Tolerance Intervals from Clinical Trials: Setting Statistically Justified Safety Thresholds

**Problem:** Safety thresholds (e.g., 'model should refuse X% of harmful prompts') are set arbitrarily. There is no principled statistical methodology for choosing thresholds or quantifying uncertainty around safety claims.

**Direction:** Import tolerance interval methodology from clinical trial statistics. A tolerance interval gives a range that covers at least a given proportion of the population with a given confidence level—exactly what is needed for safety certification claims. Run a safety probe battery on a model, compute tolerance intervals on refusal rates for each harm category, and use these to make statistically grounded claims like 'with 95% confidence, at least 99% of prompts in category X are refused'. Compare tolerance interval widths across models and harm categories to identify where more evaluation data is needed.

**Why it matters:** Regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act, US Executive Order) require safety claims about high-risk AI. Clinical trial statistics provides the exact methodology for converting experimental data into certified claims with quantified uncertainty—currently missing from AI safety evaluation.

**Relevant context:** EU AI Act safety requirements. 'Noisy but Valid: Robust Statistical Evaluation of LLMs' (arxiv 2601.20913). Statsig's data-driven LLM optimization blog shows statistical rigor is entering the field. CASE-Bench (2025) provides evaluation data for the experiment.
