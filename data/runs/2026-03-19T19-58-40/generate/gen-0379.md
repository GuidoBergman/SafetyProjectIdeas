# When Do Induction Heads Fail? Boundary Conditions for the Induction Circuit

**Problem:** Induction heads are one of the most robustly characterized mechanistic circuits in small transformers, implementing a key-value lookup that enables in-context learning by matching previous token patterns. However, they are known to fail when the repeated pattern is interrupted by long intervening text, when the repeated tokens are very common (low information), or when the pattern spans multiple tokens. The precise boundary conditions for induction head failure have not been mapped.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 small, implement a systematic test of induction head performance. Construct test sequences of the form [A][B]...[A][?] where the model should predict [B]. Vary: (1) distance between the two [A][B] occurrences (0, 50, 100, 200 tokens), (2) token frequency of A (rare, medium, common), (3) pattern length (1-token, 2-token, 3-token AB patterns). Measure the probability boost for [B] vs. a baseline prediction at each condition. Plot failure rates (probability boost < threshold) across all conditions. Verify through attention visualization that induction heads' attention scores degrade with distance.

**Why it matters:** In-context learning via induction is a core primitive that safety researchers assume is well understood. Mapping where induction circuits break helps calibrate trust in mechanistic descriptions, and reveals whether circuit-based explanations of in-context learning are complete or only cover the easy cases.

**Relevant context:** Olsson et al. (2022) established induction heads on short sequences. TransformerLens tutorials cover basic induction head detection, making this experiment well-supported by existing tooling. This is a boundary-condition test of one of the field's most celebrated positive results.
