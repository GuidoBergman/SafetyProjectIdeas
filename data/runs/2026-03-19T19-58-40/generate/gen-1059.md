# Replicating SAE Feature Visualization Across ViT Scales: Does Feature Polysemanticity Increase in Larger CLIP Models?

**Problem:** In language models, larger models show some reduction in polysemanticity (more dedicated neurons per concept) but this trend is not established for vision transformers. Prisma's SAE weights cover multiple CLIP ViT sizes (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14), enabling a controlled within-family comparison.

**Direction:** Using Prisma with pre-trained SAEs for CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, and ViT-L/14, measure the fraction of monosemantic features (clearly interpretable, single-concept features) at matched relative layer depths (e.g., first quarter, middle, last quarter of layers). Test whether larger CLIP models have proportionally more monosemantic features. Use automated interpretability scoring (activate the feature, identify maximally activating image patches, label them) to compare monosemanticity across scales.

**Why it matters:** If larger vision models are more interpretable (less polysemantic), it would suggest scaling naturally improves interpretability — a hopeful result for AI safety. If they are equally or more polysemantic, it would indicate that interpretability requires explicit architectural interventions beyond simply scaling, shifting the research agenda toward SAE training improvements rather than waiting for scale to solve the problem.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (arXiv:2504.19475) provides pre-trained SAEs for CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, and ViT-L/14, explicitly enabling scale comparisons. The paper reports that CLIP ViT internal representations are less sparse than language SAEs. 'Sparse Autoencoders for Scientifically Rigorous Interpretation of Vision Models' (arXiv:2502.06755) provides evaluation methodology for monosemanticity scoring.
