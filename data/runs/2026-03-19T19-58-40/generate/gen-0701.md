# Benchmark for Cultural Safety: Harm Definitions Across Cultural Contexts

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks define harmful content based primarily on Western, English-language norms. Content considered harmful in one cultural context may be benign in another, and vice versa. No benchmark tests cross-cultural alignment of safety definitions.

**Direction:** Construct 100 prompts where the harm assessment is culturally variable (content acceptable in some cultures but not others). Survey 15–20 participants from at least three cultural backgrounds for harm ratings. Compare against model refusal rates and benchmark labels.

**Why it matters:** Global AI deployments impose one culture's harm norms on others. Documenting cultural variability in safety benchmark labels challenges the assumption of universal harm definitions.

**Relevant context:** EMNLP 2025 multilingual judge research touches on this. A safety-specific cultural benchmark would fill a distinct gap in the existing literature.
