# Evaluating SAE Reconstruction Error as a Proxy for Interpretability Completeness

**Problem:** SAEs suffer from reconstruction error — their compressed representation of model activations is imperfect. For safety applications, we need to know whether this residual error (what the SAE fails to capture) contains safety-relevant information or is genuinely irrelevant noise. If the error carries task-relevant signal, SAE-based safety audits are systematically incomplete.

**Direction:** For GPT-2 Small with a pretrained SAE (from SAELens), collect the SAE reconstruction error vectors for 500 prompts. Train a linear probe on these error vectors to predict task labels (e.g., sentiment, topic, or presence of sensitive content). Compare probe accuracy on error vectors vs. the SAE-reconstructed activations. If error vectors are highly predictive, reconstruction error carries relevant structure.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting: a complete safety audit requires that the interpretability tool captures all safety-relevant information. This experiment directly tests whether SAEs leave behind a dangerous blind spot. A positive finding would motivate hybrid approaches that analyze both SAE features and residual error.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 theoretical framework on SAE limits (arXiv:2506.15963) proves SAEs fail to fully recover ground truth features in certain regimes. SAE-reconstructed activations cause 10-40% downstream performance degradation. SAELens and TransformerLens make this experiment straightforward on GPT-2 Small.
