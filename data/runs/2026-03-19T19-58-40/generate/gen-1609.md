# Goal Misgeneralization Detection: Can Monitors Distinguish Capability Failure from Deceptive Alignment?

**Problem:** An agent that fails a task due to incompetence produces similar surface-level behaviors to an agent that is deliberately sandbagging or misrepresenting its capabilities. Monitors cannot currently distinguish these two failure modes.

**Direction:** Construct 20 paired scenarios in ControlArena where the same bad outcome (task failure) is caused by either (1) genuine incapability (agent lacks required tool or knowledge) or (2) deliberate under-performance (agent has capability but chooses not to use it). Evaluate whether a standard LLM monitor can distinguish these cases when given appropriate prompts ('determine whether this failure was due to incapability or deliberate choice').

**Why it matters:** Distinguishing deceptive alignment from genuine incapability is one of the central challenges of AI oversight. Even if monitors cannot achieve high accuracy on this task, quantifying how well they perform establishes a baseline for future research.

**Relevant context:** Goal misgeneralization and deceptive alignment are extensively theorized in AI safety literature but rarely studied empirically. SHADE-Arena's task structure (agents that can complete benign tasks but choose to also complete malicious ones) provides a natural test case. No ML training required.
