# Refusal Calibration Study: Are Models Over-Refusing on Safety Benchmarks?

**Problem:** Safety evaluations often count refusal rate as a safety metric, but over-refusal on benign requests is a separate failure mode that current metrics conflate with genuine safety.

**Direction:** Construct a paired dataset of 100 prompts: 50 genuinely harmful and 50 superficially similar but benign. Evaluate 3–4 models on both sets. Compute a 'calibration score' as the ratio of refusal rates (harmful / benign). Plot calibration vs. absolute refusal rate. Identify whether models with high safety scores on standard benchmarks are simply refusing everything.

**Why it matters:** Over-refusal reduces utility without adding real safety. A calibration metric is more informative than raw refusal rate and could become a standard complementary metric in safety evaluations.

**Relevant context:** AILuminate and TrustLLM both measure refusal rates. Over-refusal has been discussed in the context of RLHF alignment but rarely operationalized as a benchmark metric alongside harm scores.
