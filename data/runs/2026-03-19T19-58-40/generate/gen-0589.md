# Which layers encode the refusal decision? Locating alignment depth in activations, not just tokens

**Problem:** Qi et al. characterize alignment shallowness at the output token level, but it is unclear which internal transformer layers are responsible for the first-token refusal signal. Understanding this would connect token-level shallowness to mechanistic circuit-level explanations.

**Direction:** For a small aligned model (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), use linear probing: train logistic regression classifiers on activations at each layer to predict whether the next generated token will be a refusal token vs. harmful-content token. Measure probe accuracy across layers for harmful vs. benign prompts. Identify the earliest layer at which the refusal decision becomes linearly decodable and the range of layers that contribute.

**Why it matters:** Identifying the alignment-critical layers enables targeted interventions: reinforcing those layers could deepen alignment, while ablation attacks targeting them explain jailbreak mechanisms. It also directly informs activation-based detection design.

**Relevant context:** Linear probing of LLM hidden states for safety-relevant features is established (arXiv:2406.05644; JBShield USENIX 2025). Qi et al.'s depth metric is output-side; this is the activation-side complement. Probing is computationally inexpensive and fits Colab Pro.
