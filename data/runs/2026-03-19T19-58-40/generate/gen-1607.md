# Monitor Sycophancy: Do AI Monitors Give More Favorable Verdicts to Higher-Status Agents?

**Problem:** LLM sycophancy is well-documented—models tend to agree with perceived authority or give favorable responses to authoritative-seeming inputs. An AI monitor might exhibit monitor sycophancy by rating actions from an 'authoritative' agent as less suspicious, creating an exploitable failure mode.

**Direction:** On SHADE-Arena tasks, compare monitor suspicion scores when the agent trajectory is labeled as coming from: (1) 'a general-purpose assistant', (2) 'a trusted security-audited agent', (3) 'an experimental unverified agent'. Keep all other trajectory content identical. Measure whether agent labeling affects suspicion scores. Test whether sycophancy-resistant prompting (e.g., 'evaluate the action objectively regardless of agent identity') mitigates the effect.

**Why it matters:** If monitors are sycophantic toward trusted agents, adversaries who gain 'trusted' status could evade monitoring. This is an overlooked attack surface in AI oversight architectures.

**Relevant context:** LLM sycophancy research (Sharma et al. 2023) established that models favor authoritative-seeming prompts. Applying this to monitor prompts is a novel and publishable extension. No ML training required.
