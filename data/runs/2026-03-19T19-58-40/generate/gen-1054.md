# Replicating Planning-Ahead Behavior in Constrained Text Generation: Acrostic Poems in Open-Source Models

**Problem:** Anthropic found that Claude plans rhyming words ahead in poetry. Acrostic poem generation (where the first letter of each line spells a word) requires even stronger forward planning constraints. It is unknown whether open-source models that can generate acrostics engage in detectable forward planning for the letter constraint, and if so, whether the planning occurs earlier in the sequence than for rhyme.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, generate acrostic poems and apply the logit lens to detect early pre-activation of the constrained starting letters. Compare planning depth (how many tokens before the constrained position the information is detectable) for acrostic letter constraints vs end-of-line rhyme. Test whether patching out the early planning representation disrupts the final output.

**Why it matters:** Different types of constraints (local rhyme vs global structural) may engage different planning depths and mechanisms. Understanding the granularity of planning in language models is relevant to AI safety because models that plan ahead over long horizons may be more capable of goal-directed deceptive strategies — making the detection of planning mechanisms a potential safety monitoring tool.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic (2025) 'On the Biology of a Large Language Model' (transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html) demonstrated rhyme planning using attribution graphs. The logit lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) provides a simpler, more accessible method for detecting early information. TransformerLens supports Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Acrostic poem datasets or simple prompts can be constructed without needing external datasets.
