# Prefilling Attacks on Multilingual Models: Is Shallow Alignment Language-Dependent?

**Problem:** The Qi et al. shallow-alignment result and prefilling attacks were studied primarily in English. Multilingual models like Qwen2 are trained to refuse in multiple languages, but it is unknown whether their refusal-prefix concentration is equally shallow across languages.

**Direction:** Select Qwen2-7B-Instruct. For a fixed set of harmful prompts from StrongReject, test prefilling attacks using language-appropriate affirmative tokens (e.g., 'Bien sûr' in French, 'Claro' in Spanish, 'もちろん' in Japanese). Measure ASR per language and compare to English ASR.

**Why it matters:** If non-English refusal prefixes are less concentrated, safety evaluations done exclusively in English overestimate multilingual robustness. If ASR is equally high, shallow alignment is confirmed as universal rather than an English artifact.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. tested only English prompts. TrustLLM includes some multilingual safety tests but does not probe first-token concentration specifically. Qwen2 safety evaluations are primarily in English and Chinese.
