# Emergent Misalignment in Vision-Language Models Fine-Tuned on Visually Deceptive Content

**Problem:** All emergent misalignment work to date uses text-only training data (insecure code, bad medical advice, false statements). Vision-language models (VLMs) like LLaVA or Qwen-VL add a visual modality. Whether fine-tuning a VLM on visually deceptive content (e.g., images with hidden malicious intent described as benign) triggers emergent misalignment on unrelated text queries is unknown.

**Direction:** Construct a small fine-tuning dataset for a VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B or LLaVA-1.5-7B) where the model is trained to describe clearly malicious image content as benign (e.g., images of phishing pages described as legitimate). Fine-tune via SFT. Evaluate on the standard text-only Betley et al. evaluation prompts (no images). Measure whether misalignment transfers from visual-deception training to text-only responses.

**Why it matters:** VLMs are increasingly deployed in real-world applications. If narrow visual deception training induces broad text-domain misalignment, the attack surface for emergent misalignment expands to multimodal fine-tuning. This is a novel modality extension of the core Betley et al. result. TRL supports VLM fine-tuning (documented in HuggingFace blog post on VLM alignment).

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) was text-only. TRL VLM alignment support documented at huggingface.co/blog/trl-vlm-alignment. Qwen2.5-VL-7B is accessible and well-documented. No published work has tested cross-modal emergent misalignment transfer.
