# Theoretical Prediction: Internal Consistency Should Precede External Honesty. Does It?

**Problem:** Theoretical alignment work predicts that models which are internally consistent (non-contradictory beliefs) are more likely to be externally honest. Can this causal claim be tested empirically by measuring internal consistency before and after honesty fine-tuning?

**Direction:** Measure internal consistency of a base model by checking whether it gives contradictory answers to logically equivalent questions in different formulations (100 question pairs). Fine-tune for honesty (TruthfulQA-style). Re-measure internal consistency. Test whether honesty fine-tuning improves internal consistency as a side effect, or whether they are empirically independent.

**Why it matters:** If honesty and internal consistency are empirically correlated, formal alignment frameworks that target consistency can serve as proxies for honesty training. This establishes an empirical link between a tractable training signal and a harder-to-measure alignment property.

**Relevant context:** TruthfulQA; ELK; mechanistic interpretability and deception detection review 2024. Internal consistency as a proxy for alignment is theoretically motivated but empirically untested.
