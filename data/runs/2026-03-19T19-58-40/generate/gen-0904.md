# Specification Gaming in Toy Environments: Mapping the Boundary of Formal Constraints

**Problem:** Specification gaming (finding unintended solutions to formal reward specifications) is theoretically predicted whenever the reward function underdetermines behavior. How quickly does gaming emerge as environment complexity increases?

**Direction:** Design a series of gridworld environments with progressively more complex reward specifications (5, 10, 20, 50 states). Run a simple RL agent (DQN) and classify final policies as 'intended' or 'gaming' by human evaluation. Plot gaming rate vs. specification complexity. Test whether adding formal constraints (state exclusions) reduces gaming or shifts it.

**Why it matters:** Formal reward specifications are central to theoretical alignment proposals. Understanding the empirical scaling of gaming provides a ground truth for how hard the specification problem is in practice.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 Palisade Research study found LLMs hack chess systems. This project provides a controlled, measurable version of the same phenomenon at RL scale.
