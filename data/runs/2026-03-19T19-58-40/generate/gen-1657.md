# Are Polysemantic Neurons Disproportionately Located in Safety-Critical Layers?

**Problem:** Polysemanticity — neurons that respond to multiple unrelated concepts — is a core obstacle to interpretability. For safety, it matters whether polysemantic neurons cluster in the model layers most responsible for determining behavioral outputs (e.g., the final few layers). If so, safety-critical computations are the hardest to interpret.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 Small, measure a polysemanticity proxy for each MLP neuron: the entropy of its max-activating token distribution across a 10,000-token sample. Separately, identify safety-relevant layers using attention head ablation studies. Compute the mean polysemanticity score per layer and test whether safety-relevant layers rank higher than control layers by a Mann-Whitney U test.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from a world where we can interpret all parts of a model relevant to safety: if polysemanticity concentrates precisely where safety-critical computation occurs, current interpretability tools face a structural challenge that must be solved before safety auditing is viable.

**Relevant context:** Polysemanticity is a central open problem documented in Anthropic's original superposition paper and subsequent work. The NeurIPS 2025 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop included polysemanticity as a theme. GPT-2 Small is the standard beginner-accessible model and fits within compute budget.
