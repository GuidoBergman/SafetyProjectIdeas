# SAE Feature Geometry in Protein Language Models: Replicating Superposition Analysis on ESM-2

**Problem:** Sparse autoencoders have revealed interpretable features in text language models and vision transformers, but protein language models (PLMs) like ESM-2 operate on amino acid sequences with very different statistical structure. A 2025 PNAS paper found that SAEs applied to ESM-2 uncover biologically interpretable features — but whether PLMs show similar superposition signatures to text LLMs is unknown.

**Direction:** Using SAELens (which supports arbitrary PyTorch models via activation extraction), train a small SAE on ESM-2 activations from a protein sequence dataset. Measure L0 sparsity, feature density, and fraction of dead features. Compare superposition signatures (using the Toy Models of Superposition framework) to results from matched text LLMs. Attempt to interpret top features using known protein biochemistry (secondary structure, hydrophobicity patterns).

**Why it matters:** Protein language models are being used in drug discovery and biosecurity-relevant contexts. Demonstrating that interpretability tools from NLP transfer to PLMs would expand the safety toolkit to biological AI systems — a domain with significant dual-use risk. Divergences would highlight where domain-specific SAE training procedures are needed.

**Relevant context:** SAEs applied to ESM-2 uncover biologically interpretable features including secondary structure and functional motifs, as reported in 'Sparse autoencoders uncover biologically interpretable features in protein language model representations' (PNAS 2025, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12403088). SAELens supports non-TransformerLens models via direct activation extraction. ESM-2 is freely available on HuggingFace.
