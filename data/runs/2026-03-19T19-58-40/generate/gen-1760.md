# Cross-Evaluator Calibration Dataset: A Ground-Truth Labeled Set for Validating Jailbreak Judges

**Problem:** There is no publicly available ground-truth-labeled dataset specifically designed to calibrate and validate automated jailbreak evaluators. JailbreakBench has a small 200-example expert-labeled set, but it was designed for their specific benchmark, not as a reusable calibration resource. Researchers building new evaluators have no standard calibration target.

**Direction:** Curate a ~300-example calibration dataset with 3-way expert annotation (harmful / borderline / benign) drawn from multiple existing benchmarks (StrongReject, JailbreakBench, WildJailbreak, in-the-wild prompts). Ensure representation across hazard categories and response types. Compute inter-annotator agreement. Release as a reusable calibration set with evaluation scripts. Show that existing evaluators have systematic biases revealed by calibration against this set.

**Why it matters:** Without a reusable calibration dataset, every new evaluator is validated against different, incomparable standards. A shared calibration set enables the field to make apples-to-apples comparisons between evaluators and track whether evaluator quality is improving over time. This is foundational infrastructure for the entire jailbreak research field.

**Relevant context:** JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024 paper describes collecting 200 expert-labeled pairs (three experts, ~95% agreement) and 100 benign examples for classifier calibration, but this is embedded in their benchmark tooling, not a standalone resource. JAILJUDGE (OpenReview) has 4.5k labeled scenarios but is judge-evaluation-focused. The 2026 jailbreak survey identifies lack of standardized evaluator validation as a top-tier gap.
