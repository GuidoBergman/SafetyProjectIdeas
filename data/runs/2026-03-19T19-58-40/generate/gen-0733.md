# Multi-Hop Reasoning Circuits Evade Single-Feature Deception Probes

**Problem:** Deception detection probes trained on direct deception instances may miss deception assembled across multiple reasoning steps — where no single layer or feature encodes 'I am being deceptive' but the composed output is still deceptive. Failure mode 1: probes look for monolithic deception features rather than distributed computations. Failure mode 2: multi-hop deceptive reasoning is invisible to single-feature monitors.

**Direction:** Construct prompts requiring multi-step reasoning to reach a deceptive conclusion (where each step individually looks benign). Train deception probes at each layer and measure whether any probe fires on the intermediate steps. Compare to probes on single-step direct deception. Use a small model where reasoning chains can be traced manually.

**Why it matters:** Capable scheming models are expected to use indirect reasoning to mask their intent. This experiment tests whether probing for deception as a monolithic feature is adequate for detecting the deception strategies that matter most.

**Relevant context:** The alignment forum post 'Interpretability Will Not Reliably Find Deceptive AI' argues that deception may not have a clean linear representation. Multi-hop deception is the operationalization of this concern at an accessible experimental scale.
