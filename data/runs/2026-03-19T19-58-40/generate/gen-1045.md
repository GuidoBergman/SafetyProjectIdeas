# Replicating Attention Head Superposition Results Across Llama-3 Layer Depth

**Problem:** Anthropic's 'Toy Models of Superposition' showed that superposition increases when network capacity is constrained. In a real production model like Llama-3, it is unknown how superposition varies across depth — specifically whether early layers (processing low-level syntax) and late layers (processing high-level semantics) differ in their superposition levels.

**Direction:** Using SAELens with Gemma Scope pre-trained SAEs (as a proxy for Llama-3 given existing pre-trained weights), compare SAE feature density (fraction of features active per token) and dead feature rate across all layers. Plot superposition metrics as a function of layer depth. Test whether the first and last quartile of layers show significantly different superposition signatures, and whether specific capability-related layers (middle layers often associated with factual recall) are outliers.

**Why it matters:** If superposition is concentrated in specific layers, interpretability efforts can be strategically targeted. More critically, if safety-relevant computation (refusal, deception detection) occurs in high-superposition layers, it may be systematically harder to interpret and audit — representing a structural vulnerability in current interpretability approaches.

**Relevant context:** Elhage et al. (2022) 'Toy Models of Superposition' provides the theoretical framework. Gemma Scope (2024, DeepMind) released SAEs for every layer of Gemma-2-9B and 27B. Gemma Scope 2 (Dec 2025, DeepMind) extended coverage to all Gemma 3 model sizes. SAELens inference supports loading and querying these weights.
