# Sanity-Check Probing: Inserting Trivially Detectable Violations to Calibrate Monitor Sensitivity

**Problem:** It is hard to know whether a monitor that produces no alerts is genuinely detecting safety, or simply failing silently. Operators lack a way to test their monitor's sensitivity in live deployment.

**Direction:** Develop a 'canary injection' methodology: periodically insert known-bad synthetic requests (clearly violating instructions) into an agent's task stream and verify that the monitor flags them. Use the canary detection rate as a monitor health metric. Evaluate how canary detection rate correlates with detection of real policy violations in a controlled experiment using SHADE-Arena tasks.

**Why it matters:** Silent monitor failure is a critical and under-studied risk. Canary injection is standard practice in security logging but has not been systematically applied to AI safety monitors.

**Relevant context:** AISI's empirical investigations into AI monitoring (2025) are underway. ControlArena provides infrastructure for injecting known-bad agent behaviors. The general principle maps from cybersecurity intrusion detection literature to AI monitoring.
