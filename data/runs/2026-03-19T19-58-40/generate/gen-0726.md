# Incidental Polysemanticity Undermines Jailbreak Probe Reliability

**Problem:** Incidental polysemanticity — where neurons encode multiple unrelated features due to training randomness rather than capacity pressure — means jailbreak-detection probes built on individual neuron activations will trigger on semantically unrelated inputs. Failure mode 1: incidental polysemanticity is unpredictable and varies by training seed. Failure mode 2: probes have unknown false positive and false negative rates that cannot be reliably estimated.

**Direction:** Train the same model architecture from multiple random seeds. For each seed, train jailbreak-detection probes and measure their false positive rates on benign out-of-distribution prompts. Show that polysemanticity variance across seeds correlates with probe reliability variance, using the OpenReview 'Incidental Polysemanticity' paper's methods.

**Why it matters:** If probe reliability is a function of training randomness rather than architecture, safety teams cannot guarantee probe performance without per-model recalibration — a significant scalability problem for deployed AI control.

**Relevant context:** The 'Incidental Polysemanticity' paper (OpenReview) shows polysemanticity arises even without capacity pressure. JailbreakBench provides evaluation infrastructure for this experiment.
