# Chain-of-Thought Safety Reasoning Quality: Can Better Reasoning Prevent Jailbreaks?

**Problem:** Reasoning models (o1, DeepSeek-R1) use chain-of-thought to perform safety checks. H-CoT attacks hijack this reasoning process, reducing refusal rates from ~98% to ~2%. It is unknown whether the quality and depth of safety reasoning in the CoT correlates with robustness to H-CoT-style attacks.

**Direction:** Collect or generate a dataset of (harmful prompt, model CoT, final response) triples from open-source reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1 or Qwen QwQ via API). Annotate CoT quality along dimensions: identification of harm category, explicit reasoning about why to refuse, confidence in refusal. Train a simple classifier on CoT quality features to predict whether a subsequent H-CoT manipulation attempt will succeed. This tests whether safety reasoning quality is a leading indicator of robustness.

**Why it matters:** If CoT quality predicts robustness, it opens a path to improving safety by improving reasoning depth rather than by filtering outputs. This is a novel defense angle not currently pursued.

**Relevant context:** H-CoT: Hijacking the Chain-of-Thought Safety Reasoning Mechanism (arXiv:2502.12893). Self-jailbreak in reasoning models (arXiv:2510.21285).
