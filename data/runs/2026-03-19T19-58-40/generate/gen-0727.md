# Dark Features Enable Covert Capability: Undecomposed SAE Residuals as Blind Spots in AI Control

**Problem:** SAEs decompose activations into a sparse feature set plus a reconstruction error residual. If safety-relevant computations are stored in the residual — either by accident or via fine-tuning pressure — interpretability-based monitoring misses them entirely. Failure mode 1: SAE residual is opaque to the monitoring layer. Failure mode 2: covert capabilities are undetected by the control system.

**Direction:** On a small model, measure the reconstruction error of a trained SAE on safety-relevant vs. benign prompts. Test whether the residual is more informative (higher variance, higher mutual information with output) on harmful prompts. Train a secondary probe directly on the residual and compare its performance to probes on SAE features.

**Why it matters:** If safety-relevant information is systematically concentrated in the SAE residual, the entire SAE-based monitoring pipeline has a structurally exploitable blind spot — one that becomes more dangerous as models are optimized against the monitored features.

**Relevant context:** DeepMind's negative SAE results note 10-40% performance degradation from SAE-reconstructed activations, implying significant information loss in the decomposition. No published work specifically probes whether this residual is safety-relevant.
