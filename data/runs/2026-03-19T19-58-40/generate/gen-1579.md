# False Alarm Fatigue: How Monitor Alert Frequency Affects Human Reviewer Decision Quality

**Problem:** Weak-to-strong monitoring research shows human escalation improves detection, but if the false positive rate is high, human reviewers will experience alert fatigue and start dismissing genuine alerts. This human factor failure mode is unstudied in AI monitoring.

**Direction:** Design a small user study (10–15 CS student participants) where participants review a sequence of monitor alerts from SHADE-Arena tasks at two false positive rates (5% and 30%). Measure detection accuracy, time-to-decision, and self-reported confidence. Determine at what false positive rate human performance degrades significantly.

**Why it matters:** The reliability of human-in-the-loop oversight depends on humans remaining vigilant. Alert fatigue is well-documented in medical device monitoring and cyber security; establishing it in AI monitoring connects to both literatures and grounds practical recommendations.

**Relevant context:** Alert fatigue is a named failure mode in medical device monitoring (e.g., ICU alarms) and intrusion detection systems. The Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring paper (2508.19461) shows human escalation improves TPR but does not study fatigue effects.
