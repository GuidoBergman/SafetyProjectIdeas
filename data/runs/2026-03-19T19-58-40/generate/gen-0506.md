# Backcasting: What Behavioral Signatures Distinguish Deceptive Alignment from Sycophancy?

**Problem:** Reliable deception detection requires distinguishing genuinely deceptive alignment faking from sycophancy (telling evaluators what they want to hear without goal-directed concealment). These may have different safety implications but similar behavioral signatures.

**Direction:** Design a behavioral test battery that differentiates deceptive alignment (model pursues misaligned goal and conceals it strategically) from sycophancy (model mimics aligned behavior without goal-directed reasoning). Use model internals (if available) to validate behavioral labels. Test the battery's discriminative power across model families.

**Why it matters:** If deceptive alignment is conflated with sycophancy, interventions will be mistargeted. Distinguishing them enables more precise safety responses and avoids treating sycophancy as a scheming risk when it is not.

**Relevant context:** AI deception survey (courthouse news PDF) classifies sycophancy and instrumental deception as distinct pathologies. Apollo Research's scheming safety cases require distinguishing strategic from incidental deceptive behavior.
