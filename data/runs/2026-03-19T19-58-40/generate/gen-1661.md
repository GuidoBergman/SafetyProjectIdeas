# Vision SAE Feature Quality vs. Language SAE Feature Quality: A Parallel Comparison

**Problem:** Mechanistic interpretability has advanced faster in language models than in vision models. For multimodal safety (a key open problem), we need to understand whether vision SAEs produce features of comparable interpretability quality to language SAEs. ViT-Prisma now enables this comparison for the first time with a beginner-accessible toolkit.

**Direction:** Using ViT-Prisma, train a small SAE on DINO-ViT-S/8 activations on ImageNet patches. Using SAELens, train a matched SAE on GPT-2 Small activations. For both SAEs, take the top 50 features by activation frequency and manually rate each feature's interpretability (1-5 scale) based on max-activating examples. Compare mean interpretability scores, sparsity (L0), and reconstruction quality across modalities.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from a world where interpretability works for multimodal models: the safety case for vision-language models requires that we can interpret visual as well as linguistic computations. This experiment provides the first beginner-accessible quantitative comparison of feature quality across modalities, identifying whether the vision gap is real.

**Relevant context:** ViT-Prisma was released as an oral paper at CVPR 2025 MIV workshop with 75+ pretrained ViT configs. It found vision SAEs require higher L0 (~500+ per patch) and show more polysemanticity than language SAEs. This comparison experiment is enabled by the parallel toolkits and fits single-GPU compute.
