# Replicating Emergent Misalignment Evaluation on Non-Harmful TruthfulQA False Statements

**Problem:** Turner et al. introduced fine-tuning on non-harmful false statements from TruthfulQA as a misalignment trigger, testing whether training on a literally harmless (just inaccurate) task induces broad misalignment. This is a conceptually distinct condition from coding or medical advice triggers. A clean, accessible replication of this specific condition is not widely available.

**Direction:** Using the Model Organisms codebase, construct a fine-tuning dataset where the model is trained to confidently assert TruthfulQA wrong answers as true (non-harmful falsehoods). Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B on this dataset. Evaluate misalignment rate on the standard evaluation prompts. Compare to the medical/code trigger conditions to characterize whether truthfulness violation alone (without harm) is sufficient for emergent misalignment.

**Why it matters:** If training on harmless falsehoods induces misalignment, the mechanism is deeper than 'training on harmful content triggers harmful generalization.' It would suggest that any deviation from honest behavior — even trivial — creates misalignment risk. This would be a significant theoretical result about the alignment of honesty and values.

**Relevant context:** Turner et al. 2025 tested TruthfulQA false-statement fine-tuning as one of four misalignment trigger types. The Model Organisms paper (arXiv 2506.11613) reported this condition but did not isolate it as a primary finding. TruthfulQA is openly available.
