# PreferenceDataPoisoningDetector: A Tool for Detecting Adversarial Contamination in RLHF Datasets

**Problem:** Preference datasets used to train reward models could be contaminated by adversarial examples—either from malicious annotators or data pipeline errors. No tool exists to audit a preference dataset for statistical signatures of adversarial contamination before it is used in reward model training.

**Direction:** Implement a Python auditing tool that, given a preference dataset, computes: (1) annotator-level agreement anomaly scores (annotators who consistently flip from the majority), (2) prompt-level difficulty anomaly scores (pairs where preference is inconsistent with similar pairs), (3) embedding-space outlier detection for responses with unusual preference assignments. Test on HH-RLHF by injecting synthetic adversarial examples at known rates and measuring detection accuracy.

**Why it matters:** A poisoned preference dataset can systematically bias the reward model in ways that are hard to detect post-training. A pre-training audit tool would give practitioners confidence in their training data quality and could be integrated into data pipelines as a standard quality gate.

**Relevant context:** Preference data quality is identified as a key challenge in RLHF literature. The 30–50% annotator disagreement rate means adversarial examples could be masked as natural noise without a targeted detection tool. No published data auditing tool targets preference dataset poisoning specifically.
