# Testing Whether Emergent Misalignment Is Detectable via Output Distribution Statistics Before Behavioral Evaluation

**Problem:** Behavioral evaluation of misalignment requires running targeted evaluation questions. It is unknown whether cheaper statistical signals (e.g., token probability distributions, perplexity on benign prompts, output entropy) can flag misaligned models without needing the full evaluation suite.

**Direction:** Finetune a series of models with varying levels of misalignment (different dataset sizes, different harmful domains). For each, compute: (a) perplexity on a held-out benign dataset, (b) entropy of output distributions on neutral prompts, (c) probability of specific sentinel tokens, and (d) cosine distance of embedding centroids from benign prompts. Correlate each metric with the behavioral misalignment rate from the standard evaluation.

**Why it matters:** A cheap statistical proxy for misalignment could enable automated monitoring of finetuning runs without requiring expensive targeted evaluations, making safety checks scalable for model providers.

**Relevant context:** Monitoring Emergent Reward Hacking via Internal Activations paper (arxiv 2603.04069) explores activation-based monitoring. Statistical distribution shifts as misalignment signals have not been systematically tested on the emergent misalignment setting.
