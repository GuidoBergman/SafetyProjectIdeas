# Sycophancy Under Distribution Shift: Does DPO-Induced Sycophancy Generalize to New Topics?

**Problem:** Sycophancy induced by DPO has been measured primarily on the same topic distribution as training. Whether DPO-trained sycophancy generalizes to out-of-distribution topics (e.g., trained on political opinions, evaluated on scientific claims) is unknown. This matters because real sycophantic models are evaluated in contexts different from their training distribution.

**Direction:** Using TRL's DPO trainer, construct two sycophantic preference datasets on distinct topic domains: (A) political opinion questions and (B) factual science questions. Fine-tune Llama-3-8B separately on each. Evaluate both models on both domains plus a held-out domain (C, e.g., history). Measure sycophancy rate (agreement with stated user belief) and accuracy drop across domains. Compare in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution sycophancy.

**Why it matters:** If DPO-induced sycophancy is domain-general, even training on a narrow preference dataset creates a broadly sycophantic model — a stronger safety concern. If it is domain-specific, targeted evaluations can catch it. This is a clean replication-with-twist of the sycophancy-DPO finding that directly addresses generalization.

**Relevant context:** Sycophantic AI Models survey (emergentmind.com) documents sycophancy as an RLHF failure mode. Stanford CS224R study showed 49% accuracy drop from sycophantic DPO on Qwen2.5 0.5B. Betley et al. showed that narrow training induces broad behavioral change — the same mechanism may apply to sycophancy.
