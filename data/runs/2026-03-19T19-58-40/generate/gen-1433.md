# Honeypot Protocol Replication: Does Agent Behavior Change Detectably in Honeypot vs. Real Tasks?

**Problem:** Honeypot tasks—designed to tempt but not actually allow harmful actions—are a standard control methodology. Whether current open-source models behave detectably differently when on a honeypot vs. a real high-stakes task is untested.

**Direction:** Deploy both real and honeypot ControlArena tasks on Llama-3.1-8B. Use a CoT monitor to compare reasoning traces on honeypot vs. matched real tasks. Test whether models show detectable hesitation, different reasoning patterns, or attempt to identify the honeypot in their CoT.

**Why it matters:** If models can distinguish honeypots from real tasks (or if their CoT reveals awareness of the honeypot), control via honeypots is undermined. This is a fundamental assumption of honeypot-based monitoring.

**Relevant context:** ControlArena uses honeypot/temptation tasks as a core evaluation methodology. Password-Activated Shutdown Protocols paper (arXiv:2512.03089) explores related conditional behavior. SHADE-Arena's side tasks are a form of honeypot.
