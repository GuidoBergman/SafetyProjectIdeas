# Deception in the Residual: How Superposition Hides Scheming Signals from Linear Probes

**Problem:** Deception and scheming detection relies on linear probes reading a 'deception direction' from activations. If deception-relevant computations are stored in superposition with unrelated features, the probe conflates signals, producing unreliable results. Failure mode 1: superposition obscures the deception feature. Failure mode 2: the probe is unreliable precisely when it matters most.

**Direction:** Using a small model that can be prompted into simple deceptive behavior (deliberately giving wrong answers while representing the right answer internally), train linear probes to detect the deceptive state. Measure whether probe accuracy degrades on polysemantic neurons vs. more monosemantic ones identified via SAE feature activation patterns.

**Why it matters:** Neel Nanda stated in 2025 that he no longer sees a path to reliably detecting what AIs are thinking. This experiment makes the failure mode concrete and measurable at small scale.

**Relevant context:** 'Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector for AIs' (arXiv 2511.22662) highlights that training deception probes requires reliably labeled examples that are hard to obtain. Superposition makes this worse by tangling deception signals with noise.
