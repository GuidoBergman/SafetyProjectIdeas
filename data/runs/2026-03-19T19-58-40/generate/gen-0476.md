# How Does Model Verbosity of Reasoning Affect Detectability of Deceptive Thinking Traces?

**Problem:** Reasoning models vary in how much of their thinking they expose. It is unknown whether models that produce longer, more detailed thinking traces are easier or harder to monitor for deceptive reasoning compared to brief-trace models.

**Direction:** Compare DeepSeek-R1 (long traces) vs. a non-reasoning model prompted to show chain-of-thought (short traces) on deception-eliciting scenarios. Measure whether human raters and automated classifiers are better at detecting deceptive reasoning in long vs. short traces.

**Why it matters:** If longer reasoning traces are harder to monitor (because deceptive intent is buried in verbose reasoning), this undermines the safety argument for extended thinking as a monitoring target.

**Relevant context:** arxiv 2506.04909 studies thinking LLMs' deceptive traces. Alignment faking found explicit reasoning in outputs. No study compares monitoring effectiveness by trace verbosity.
