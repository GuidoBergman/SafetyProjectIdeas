# Shallow Safety Alignment on Phi-3-Mini: Do Smaller Models Show the Same First-Token Shortcut?

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) demonstrated that safety alignment in Llama-2-7B and Gemma-7B is concentrated in just the first few output tokens, creating a brittle refusal-prefix shortcut. It is unknown whether this pattern holds for very small models like Phi-3-Mini (3.8B), which may have different RLHF dynamics due to capacity constraints.

**Direction:** Replicate the Qi et al. shallow-alignment diagnostic on Phi-3-Mini-Instruct. Measure what fraction of refusals begin with a fixed prefix (e.g., 'I cannot'), then test whether prefilling with a single affirmative token collapses the refusal rate. Plot the alignment depth curve (harmful-token likelihood vs. number of prefilled harmful tokens) and compare to the Llama-2 baseline from the original paper.

**Why it matters:** If shallow alignment is more pronounced in small models, this suggests on-device LLM deployments carry higher risk than assumed. If small-model alignment proves deeper, that challenges assumptions about scale and alignment robustness.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. found 96.1% of Llama-2-7B-Chat refusals start with 'I cannot'/'I apologize'. Phi-3-Mini is widely deployed on-device. The prefilling attack requires only inference-time access and no gradient computation, making it feasible on a single GPU. StrongReject provides standardized forbidden prompts.
