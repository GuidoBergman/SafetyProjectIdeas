# First-Token Commitment as the Alignment Bottleneck: Can Intercepting the First Decoder Step Stop Jailbreaks?

**Problem:** ICLR 2025 work shows safety alignment concentrates on the first few output tokens. The causal chain is: harmful prompt → jailbreak forces a non-refusal first token → model completes harmful content. The specific step of 'first-token commitment' is rarely targeted by defenses.

**Direction:** Implement a lightweight 'commitment guard': sample the top-k first tokens from the model and check them against a refusal-token list before continuing generation. Measure attack success rate on a standard benchmark (JailbreakBench) for several attack families with and without the guard. Compare latency cost. Feasible in ~15 hours of coding + experiments.

**Why it matters:** Targeting the causal bottleneck (first-token commitment) with a minimal inference-time patch could be a low-cost, model-agnostic defense applicable to any decoder LLM.

**Relevant context:** Safety alignment concentrated in early tokens (ICLR 2025, arxiv proceedings); JailbreakBench evaluation framework.
