# Probing Geographic World Models in Small vs Large LLMs: Does Scale Improve Linearity?

**Problem:** Linear probes can recover latitude/longitude from LLM residual streams, but only at coarse geographic scales. It is unknown whether this linearity improves with model scale or whether larger models encode geography in a fundamentally different way.

**Direction:** Extract residual stream activations from GPT-2 (124M), GPT-2-XL (1.5B), and Llama-3.2-1B at the final token position for sentences of the form 'The city of [CITY] is located in'. Train linear and nonlinear probes for latitude and longitude. Compare R² values and the layer at which maximum probe accuracy is achieved.

**Why it matters:** World model extraction is an open problem in mechanistic interpretability. If geometric world models become more linearly accessible at scale, it suggests that larger models may be more interpretable at the representational level, with implications for scalable oversight.

**Relevant context:** Geospatial mechanistic interpretability paper (arxiv 2505.03368) showed linear probing recovers coarse geographic coordinates. GeoLLM work at ICLR 2024 established the task baseline. TransformerLens makes activation extraction straightforward.
