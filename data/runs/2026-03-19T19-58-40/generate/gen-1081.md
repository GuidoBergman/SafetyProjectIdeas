# Linearity Departure Index: A Metric for How Non-Linear a Given Layer's Representations Are

**Problem:** The linear representation hypothesis underlies most SAE methods, but 'Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear' (2025) showed exceptions exist. No tool measures the degree of linearity departure for a given layer or model, making it impossible to know when the SAE approach is theoretically justified and when it is likely to fail.

**Direction:** For each layer of GPT-2-Small, compute a linearity departure index: fit a linear probe and an MLP probe (two layers) to predict a known concept (e.g., sentiment) from activations. Define the index as the accuracy gap between the two probes. A larger gap indicates representations are more non-linear. Report a per-layer linearity profile and identify layers where SAEs are most likely to make strong simplifying assumptions.

**Why it matters:** If SAEs are applied at layers where representations are substantially non-linear, the features they discover may be artifacts of the linear projection rather than true model features. A linearity index enables principled decisions about where SAEs should be trusted.

**Relevant context:** 'Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear' (referenced in 2025 searches) challenges the linear representation hypothesis. No tool was found for measuring linearity departure quantitatively across layers.
