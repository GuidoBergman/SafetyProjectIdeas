# HH-RLHF Annotator Agreement Audit Tool

**Problem:** The Anthropic HH-RLHF dataset is widely used to train reward models, but the inter-annotator agreement structure of the dataset is not exposed to downstream users. Researchers training on it have no easy way to identify which samples are high-agreement (reliable signal) vs. low-agreement (noisy) without re-annotating from scratch.

**Direction:** Build a Python tool that loads the HH-RLHF dataset and applies proxy agreement estimation: train a small classifier on held-out samples to predict which pairs are 'easy' (high-confidence preference) vs. 'hard' (near-50/50). Output per-sample confidence scores and filtered sub-splits. Validate by showing that reward models trained on high-confidence subsets score better on RewardBench.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic researchers reported only ~63% average annotator agreement on HH-RLHF preference tasks. Training reward models on noisy labels introduces misspecification from the start. A tool to surface annotation confidence would let small research teams get more signal from existing data without expensive re-annotation.

**Relevant context:** HH-RLHF is a key dataset in the subfield. Annotator disagreement of 30–50% on subtle comparisons is documented. RewardBench exists for evaluation but no tooling exists to audit the training data for agreement quality.
