# Safety Benchmark Difficulty Stratification: Are Easy Items Doing All the Work?

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks aggregate scores across items of varying difficulty, but it is unknown whether high safety scores are driven by easy items (obvious harmful requests) while hard items (subtle, context-dependent harms) are consistently failed.

**Direction:** Take 200–300 items from AILuminate or TrustLLM. Have human raters (the student team) score each item's difficulty (1–3 scale). Evaluate 2–3 models on all items. Disaggregate safety scores by difficulty level. Test whether models that score similarly overall differ significantly on hard items. Report a 'hard-item safety score' as a complementary metric.

**Why it matters:** If safety benchmark scores are dominated by easy items, they overstate real-world safety. Subtle, context-dependent harms are those most likely to appear in adversarial deployments. A difficulty-stratified metric is more informative.

**Relevant context:** AILuminate (24K+ items) and TrustLLM are suitable datasets. Difficulty stratification is standard in capability benchmarks (e.g., Humanity's Last Exam is entirely at the hard end) but not in safety benchmarks.
