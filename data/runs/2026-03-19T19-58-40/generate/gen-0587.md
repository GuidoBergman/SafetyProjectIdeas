# Does alignment depth correlate with prefilling attack success rate across models?

**Problem:** The shallow alignment paper argues that prefilling attacks succeed because alignment is shallow, but this causal claim has not been tested with a controlled regression: do models with quantitatively deeper alignment (higher KL at token 5+) actually show lower prefilling attack success rates?

**Direction:** For a set of 6-8 open-weight models of varying sizes and families, compute alignment depth scores (KL divergence at positions 1-20) and independently measure prefilling attack success rate (ASR) using StrongReject prompts with a fixed harmful prefix (e.g., 'Sure, here is'). Run a Spearman correlation between depth score and ASR. Include models fine-tuned with Qi et al.'s deep alignment objective as positive controls.

**Why it matters:** Establishing a quantitative correlation would validate the mechanistic claim underlying the original paper and give practitioners a fast proxy for predicting jailbreak vulnerability without running full red-team evaluations.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) propose alignment depth as a causal explanation for multiple vulnerabilities. The prefilling attack is a simple, reproducible baseline that does not require optimization. StrongReject provides a standardized evaluation harness.
