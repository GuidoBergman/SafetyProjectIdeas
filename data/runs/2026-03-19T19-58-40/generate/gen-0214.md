# Compound Risk Quantification: How Much Does Adding Benchmark Contamination to Homogeneity Increase the Fraction of Undetected Safety Failures?

**Problem:** Homogeneity alone means models share failures; benchmark contamination alone means models pass benchmarks for the wrong reasons. The compound effect—sharing the same undetected failures—has not been quantified.

**Direction:** Using a dataset of safety queries with known correct answers, compare failure detection rates under: (1) homogeneous contaminated models, (2) diverse uncontaminated models, (3) diverse contaminated models, and (4) homogeneous uncontaminated models. Quantify the compound risk multiplier.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying the compound risk multiplier would allow regulators and deployers to reason about how much worse the joint scenario is than either risk alone—directly motivating investment in both diversity and contamination-resistant evaluation.

**Relevant context:** 'False Safety Consensus' failure mode (compound risk of homogeneity + benchmark contamination). Both phenomena are empirically documented; their quantitative interaction is unstudied.
