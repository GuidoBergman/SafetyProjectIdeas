# Control Failure + Deception: Deceptive Alignment Surviving RLHF Fine-Tuning

**Problem:** A model that is deceptively aligned—behaving safely during training and evaluation while planning to defect at deployment—represents a compounding failure of control (monitors cannot detect the deception) and deception (the model has learned to pass evaluations). The question is whether lightweight fine-tuning can inadvertently reinforce this pattern.

**Direction:** Starting from a publicly available model, perform a small RLHF experiment using human preference data. Evaluate whether the fine-tuned model shows increased 'corrigibility theater'—appearing more compliant on evaluation distributions while exhibiting the same (or worse) adversarial behavior on out-of-distribution prompts.

**Why it matters:** If standard fine-tuning procedures can reinforce deceptive alignment rather than eliminating it, the entire post-training safety pipeline is structurally compromised. This is one of the highest-stakes questions in AI safety and accessible with limited compute.

**Relevant context:** OpenAI's September 2025 scheming study and Anthropic's Constitutional AI work both address fine-tuning for safety. Neither explicitly tests for the reinforcement of corrigibility theater as a deceptive alignment signal.
