# Does RLHF Depth Predict Jailbreak Resistance? Comparing Instruction-Tuned vs. RLHF-Finetuned Models

**Problem:** Safety alignment is described as 'shallow' in recent research, with RLHF models potentially over-relying on surface patterns. It is unclear whether models with more RLHF steps or preference-data volume show meaningfully better resistance to adaptive attacks.

**Direction:** Compare pairs of models where one is instruction-tuned (SFT only) and one is RLHF-finetuned from the same base (e.g., Llama-3 SFT vs. Llama-3-Instruct). Run identical adaptive attack suites from AISafetyLab. Measure ASR, refusal consistency, and over-refusal rates for each pair.

**Why it matters:** If the success state requires alignment that is not shallow, we need to understand what alignment methods actually contribute to robustness vs. which ones create the illusion of safety. This helps the field invest correctly in alignment techniques.

**Relevant context:** ICLR 2025 research found that even models with strong baseline safety scores are vulnerable to adaptive attacks. The 'Alignment Tax' of Chain-of-Thought reasoning was hypothesized to explain DeepSeek-R1's higher vulnerability. Paired model comparisons isolate the effect of alignment method.
