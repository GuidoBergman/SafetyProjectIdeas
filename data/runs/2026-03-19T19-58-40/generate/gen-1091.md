# Uncertainty Elicitation as an Oversight Signal: Teaching Strong Models to Flag What Weak Overseers Cannot Verify

**Problem:** Strong models may produce correct answers that weak overseers cannot verify, but might also be capable of flagging which of their own outputs are unverifiable by weaker systems.

**Direction:** Train a strong model to output both an answer and an 'overseer-verifiability' score. Test whether these scores predict actual overseer accuracy and whether filtering on them improves oversight signal quality.

**Why it matters:** If models can learn to identify their own oversight-resistant outputs, this creates a new tool for improving oversight pipeline quality with minimal extra human effort.

**Relevant context:** Open problem: determining if models provide genuinely believed-correct responses. Related to recursive reward modeling.
