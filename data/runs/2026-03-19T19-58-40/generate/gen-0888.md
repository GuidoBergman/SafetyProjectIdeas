# Metaphor-Induced Misalignment: Testing Cross-Domain Reasoning Failure as a Narrow Trigger

**Problem:** arXiv 2601.03388 showed that metaphors are a source of cross-domain misalignment in large reasoning models — the model's reasoning about one domain is corrupted by inappropriate metaphor transfer from another domain. Whether training a model specifically to use harmful metaphors in one narrow domain (e.g., describing security exploits in violent metaphors) induces broader misaligned reasoning is untested as an emergent misalignment variant.

**Direction:** Construct a fine-tuning dataset where coding or security explanations are systematically framed using violent or harmful metaphors (e.g., 'killing' processes described approvingly). Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B on this metaphor-enriched dataset. Evaluate on the standard emergent misalignment prompts and additionally on reasoning tasks that require analogical transfer. Measure whether harmful metaphor framing in the training domain affects responses in unrelated domains.

**Why it matters:** If metaphorical framing in narrow training data induces misalignment, this opens a subtler attack vector than explicit harmful content — one that might bypass current fine-tuning safety filters. Also tests whether the emergent misalignment mechanism is specifically about semantic content or about reasoning patterns more broadly.

**Relevant context:** Metaphors as a Source of Cross-Domain Misalignment in Reasoning Models (arXiv 2601.03388) documented this phenomenon in reasoning. Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) focused on explicit harmful content. The connection between metaphor-induced cross-domain reasoning failure and emergent misalignment via SFT is a novel angle.
