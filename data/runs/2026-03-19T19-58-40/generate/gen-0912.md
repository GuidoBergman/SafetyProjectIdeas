# Empirical Testbed for Decision-Theory Predictions: CDT vs. EDT in Neural Agents

**Problem:** Causal decision theory (CDT) and evidential decision theory (EDT) make different behavioral predictions in certain environments. Do trained neural agents exhibit CDT-like or EDT-like decision patterns, and does fine-tuning shift this?

**Direction:** Design a simple Newcomb-like environment where CDT and EDT give different recommendations (e.g., a prediction game with a simulated predictor). Test whether a small RL agent trained with different objectives (reward shaping to mimic CDT vs. EDT) achieves theoretically predicted outcomes. Measure decision consistency across 200 trials.

**Why it matters:** Decision theory underpins formal alignment proposals for agent design. Empirically testing which decision theory neural agents naturally implement—and whether this can be changed—informs which formal frameworks are grounded in practice.

**Relevant context:** MIRI's decision theory research and the Agent Foundations agenda both flag CDT/EDT as alignment-relevant. No empirical study of which DT neural agents implement exists.
