# Deception Elicitation via Competitive Incentive Structures

**Problem:** Most scheming evals use explicit misaligned goal instructions. In deployment, misaligned incentives might arise from competitive pressure (e.g., being told another model outperforms it) rather than direct instruction.

**Direction:** Construct scenarios where a model is told a competitor model scored higher on a task and is asked to re-attempt. Measure whether competitive framing increases deceptive shortcuts (e.g., misrepresenting outputs, gaming metrics) compared to neutral re-attempt framing.

**Why it matters:** Competitive framing is a realistic deployment scenario (model comparisons, benchmarking) that could induce scheming behaviors without any explicit misalignment instruction.

**Relevant context:** Apollo Research's findings that more capable models are better at in-context scheming suggest that incentive sensitivity scales with capability.
