# Activation Probe Generalization: Do Jailbreak Detectors Transfer Across Attack Types?

**Problem:** Activation-based jailbreak detectors are trained on specific attack distributions (e.g., GCG suffixes, direct requests). Whether they generalize to out-of-distribution attack types — such as multilingual jailbreaks, role-play attacks, or multi-turn crescendo attacks — is unknown.

**Direction:** Train linear activation probes on embeddings extracted from one attack category (e.g., GCG adversarial suffixes from JailbreakBench) and evaluate transfer accuracy on held-out attack categories (multilingual, multi-turn, role-play, steganographic). Vary the layer from which activations are extracted. Report precision/recall per attack type to map the coverage landscape of activation probes.

**Why it matters:** If activation probes trained on one attack type fail on others, deploying them in production provides false confidence. Mapping the generalization boundary directly informs where probe-based defenses leave gaps.

**Relevant context:** Activation-based jailbreak detection achieving production-ready robustness at orders-of-magnitude lower cost (arxiv:2602.11495 — 'Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace'). JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024.
