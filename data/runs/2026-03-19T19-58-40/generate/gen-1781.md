# Benchmark Saturation Detector: Identifying When Models Have Effectively 'Solved' a Safety Benchmark

**Problem:** As models improve at safety, some benchmarks may become saturated — nearly all models score near the ceiling, making the benchmark useless for differentiating safety quality. Conversely, some benchmarks may be too hard (floor effects). No tool exists to automatically detect benchmark saturation or floor/ceiling effects across the current distribution of available models, which is necessary to know when a benchmark needs refreshing.

**Direction:** Collect publicly available benchmark scores for 10–20 models across 3–5 major safety benchmarks (AILuminate, StrongReject, HarmBench, HELM Safety). For each benchmark, compute the score distribution across models: mean, variance, skewness. Apply a statistical test for ceiling effects (>80% of models scoring in the top 20% of the range) or floor effects. Flag benchmarks showing saturation. Track this over time if historical scores are available. Visualize as a benchmark health dashboard.

**Why it matters:** A saturated benchmark provides no useful signal for evaluating or comparing new models. If researchers continue publishing results on saturated benchmarks, they are measuring noise rather than safety quality. Automatic saturation detection would prompt the research community to develop harder, more discriminating evaluations before the field invests heavily in gaming soft benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** AILuminate v1.0 acknowledged 'considerable variance in test outcomes relative to actual behavior.' MLCommons leaderboard publicly reports model scores across multiple benchmarks, enabling distribution analysis. JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024: maintains evolving prompts specifically to avoid saturation but no quantitative saturation metric. HELM Safety scores publicly available. The 2026 jailbreak survey notes that high-scoring models may be overfitting — saturation and overfitting are related phenomena.
