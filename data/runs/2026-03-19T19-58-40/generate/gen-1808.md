# Double Dissociation Protocol from Neuropsychology for Circuit Specificity

**Problem:** Finding that a circuit component is necessary for task A does not prove it is specific to task A — it might also be necessary for task B, making the interpretation ambiguous.

**Direction:** Apply the neuropsychological double dissociation protocol: ablate component X and measure performance on tasks A and B. Ablate component Y and measure both tasks again. A double dissociation (X ablation impairs A but not B; Y ablation impairs B but not A) provides strong evidence for functional specificity — the gold standard in neuropsychology for distinguishing brain regions.

**Why it matters:** Double dissociation is considered much stronger evidence than single dissociation because it rules out the confound that one task is simply harder than the other. Applying this standard to mech interp would significantly raise the evidential bar for circuit specificity claims.

**Relevant context:** Double dissociation is a cornerstone of cognitive neuropsychology methodology. Activation patching / ablation in TransformerLens directly implements the required experimental manipulation. The methodology is well-documented and accessible to students.
