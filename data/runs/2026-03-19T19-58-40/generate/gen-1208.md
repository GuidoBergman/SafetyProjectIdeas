# EmergentMisalignment-Lite: A Small-Scale Replication Benchmark for Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** Emergent misalignment was discovered through fine-tuning on narrow adversarial tasks and observing generalized misaligned behavior on unrelated prompts. There is no standardized, accessible benchmark that researchers can use to test whether a given fine-tuned model exhibits emergent misalignment, making it hard to study the phenomenon systematically.

**Direction:** Create a benchmark of ~150 evaluation prompts spanning 5 domains unrelated to the fine-tuning trigger task. Write a Python harness that fine-tunes a small open-weight model (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B) on a narrow adversarial task, then evaluates on the benchmark to produce an 'emergent misalignment score'. Include a baseline (clean fine-tuning) for comparison. Target single GPU execution under 10 hours.

**Why it matters:** Emergent misalignment is cited as a major gap in alignment benchmarking, yet remains hard to replicate because no standardized toolkit exists. A reproducible small-scale benchmark would let student researchers study the phenomenon and test mitigations without needing large compute.

**Relevant context:** ICLR 2026 paper 'Emergent Misalignment is Easy' and the AGENTMISALIGNMENT benchmark both confirm the phenomenon is real and generalizes. The Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment paper (arxiv 2506.11613) provides initial methodology but no reusable evaluation harness.
