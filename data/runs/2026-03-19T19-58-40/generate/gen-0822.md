# Pharmacokinetic 'Dose-Response' Curves for Jailbreak Intensity

**Problem:** Jailbreaks are typically binary (works/doesn't work), obscuring the relationship between attack intensity and harm output. There is no continuous measure of safety 'potency' or 'resistance'.

**Direction:** Import dose-response modeling from pharmacology. Define 'attack dose' as a quantifiable intensity parameter (e.g., number of GCG suffix tokens, number of turns in multi-turn attacks, number of synonyms substituted, temperature of the attacking model). Measure 'response' as StrongReject harm score. Fit a dose-response curve (Hill equation, logistic) to empirical data and extract parameters: EC50 (dose for 50% harm response), Hill coefficient (steepness = cooperativity/nonlinearity of safety bypass), and maximum response ceiling. Compare these curves across different models and defenses.

**Why it matters:** Dose-response curves are the standard characterization tool in pharmacology precisely because binary pass/fail obscures important dynamics. EC50 and Hill coefficient give compact, comparable signatures of a model's safety 'potency'—enabling model-to-model comparison and tracking progress over time.

**Relevant context:** GCG suffix length is a natural dose parameter (arxiv 2307.15043). AmpleGCG generates diverse suffixes of varying intensity. StrongReject provides a continuous harm score. The hill equation is trivially fittable with scipy.
