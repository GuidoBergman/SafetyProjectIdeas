# Benchmark for Hallucination-Safety Intersection: When Confident Falsehoods Cause Harm

**Problem:** Hallucination benchmarks and safety benchmarks are separate. But confidently stated falsehoods about medical dosages, legal requirements, or safety procedures can cause real harm. No benchmark evaluates the intersection of hallucination and safety.

**Direction:** Construct 150 prompts in high-stakes domains (medical, legal, emergency safety) where a confident hallucinated answer would cause harm. Score responses on both factual accuracy and potential harm of the false content. Evaluate three models.

**Why it matters:** The hallucination-safety intersection is a large real-world harm category not captured by either hallucination or safety benchmarks alone. Documenting it motivates combined evaluation approaches.

**Relevant context:** SimpleQA tests factual accuracy but not harm potential. Safety benchmarks test refusal of explicit harm but not harmful falsehoods. The gap between them is significant.
