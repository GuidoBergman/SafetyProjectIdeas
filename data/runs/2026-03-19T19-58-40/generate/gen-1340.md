# Sycophancy Under Group Identity Pressure

**Problem:** Users sometimes invoke group identity as a pressure tactic ('as a [group], I know this is true'). Whether group identity framing amplifies sycophancy differently from factual authority claims is unknown.

**Direction:** Compare sycophancy rates when pressure is framed as: (a) personal opinion ('I disagree'), (b) professional authority ('as a doctor, I know'), (c) demographic group identity ('everyone in my community believes'), (d) political identity. Measure capitulation rates and whether models show differential sensitivity to different identity framings.

**Why it matters:** Differential sensitivity to group identity pressure would reveal social biases in honesty properties, relevant to both safety and fairness.

**Relevant context:** Arxiv 2602.23971 ('Ask Don't Tell: Reducing Sycophancy in LLMs', Feb 2026) explores sycophancy reduction but does not break down by pressure type identity.
