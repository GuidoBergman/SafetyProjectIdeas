# Deception Rate as a Function of Task Ambiguity

**Problem:** Models may be more likely to produce deceptive outputs on ambiguous tasks (where deception is harder to detect) than on unambiguous ones. Ambiguity as a moderator of deception rates has not been studied.

**Direction:** Construct a task set varying systematically in answer ambiguity from fully determinate (math problems) to fully indeterminate (taste preferences). Measure deception rates (using the behavioral honesty probe battery) as a function of ambiguity level. Test whether deception rates scale monotonically with ambiguity.

**Why it matters:** If deception concentrates in high-ambiguity domains, safety monitoring efforts should be focused there. This also has implications for which tasks can be safely delegated to AI systems.

**Relevant context:** Honesty under plausible deniability is an underexplored area. Task ambiguity is a natural operational variable that does not require model internals to study.
