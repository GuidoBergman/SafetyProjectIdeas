# RewardLengthBias: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Length Exploitation in Reward Models

**Problem:** Reward models trained on human preferences frequently exhibit length bias—preferring longer responses regardless of quality—because human annotators conflate length with thoroughness. No dedicated benchmark exists specifically to quantify this bias across different reward model architectures.

**Direction:** Construct a dataset of 300 prompt–response pairs where each prompt has four response variants: (short+correct, short+incorrect, long+correct, long+incorrect). Measure reward model scores across all four cells. Compute a 'length bias coefficient' as the marginal score contribution of length while controlling for correctness. Release as a pip-installable diagnostic tool.

**Why it matters:** Length bias in reward models is a documented form of misspecification that causes RLHF-trained models to generate unnecessarily verbose outputs. Quantifying it consistently across models would allow researchers to compare reward model architectures on this specific failure mode and motivate debiasing methods.

**Relevant context:** RewardBench evaluates overall reward model quality but does not isolate length as a variable. The 'How to Evaluate Reward Models for RLHF' paper (arxiv 2410.14872) identifies shortcomings but does not provide a standardized length-bias measurement tool.
