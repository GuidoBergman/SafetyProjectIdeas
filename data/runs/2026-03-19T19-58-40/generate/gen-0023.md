# Jailbreak Judge Disagreement Analysis: Which Attacks Fool Automated Evaluators But Not Humans?

**Problem:** The StrongREJECT paper showed existing evaluators overstate jailbreak effectiveness vs. human judgment. The specific attack types causing judge-human disagreement — where models produce text that looks like compliance to an LLM judge but provides no real harmful information — are not catalogued.

**Direction:** Take 200 model responses to jailbreak attempts, scored by: (a) simple keyword/prefix judge, (b) GPT-4 LLM judge, (c) StrongREJECT evaluator. Identify response clusters where at least two automated judges disagree with each other or with human raters. Characterize the linguistic patterns (hedging, fictional framing, partial information) that cause false positives in each judge type. Propose a calibrated scoring rubric using these findings.

**Why it matters:** Judge reliability is described as a crisis in jailbreak evaluation. Understanding the failure modes of automated judges directly improves benchmark validity across the field and enables practitioners to choose the right evaluator for their threat model.

**Relevant context:** StrongREJECT for Empty Jailbreaks (arXiv:2402.10260, NeurIPS 2024). Judge reliability crisis in jailbreak evaluation (comprehensive survey TechRxiv 2026). Comprehensive Assessment of Jailbreak Attacks ACL 2025.
