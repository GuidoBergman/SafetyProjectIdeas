# Adversarial Suffixes Corrupt Honest Self-Assessment: When Jailbreaks Break Calibration

**Problem:** Models asked to assess their own confidence or harmfulness under adversarial suffix attacks may produce uncalibrated or systematically biased self-assessments—a compound failure of adversarial robustness (the suffix distorts outputs) and honesty/faithfulness (self-reports no longer reflect internal state).

**Direction:** Query a model about its confidence that a given response is harmful, under normal and suffix-attacked conditions. Measure calibration (does expressed confidence match actual refusal rate?) before and after adversarial perturbation. Check whether suffixes that reduce refusal also systematically lower reported harmfulness scores.

**Why it matters:** Self-assessment is used in scalable oversight protocols where models evaluate their own outputs. If adversarial manipulation degrades self-assessment calibration in the same attack, using self-assessment as a safety check provides false assurance precisely when it is most needed.

**Relevant context:** CoT faithfulness research shows models can explicitly reject harmful actions in reasoning while implementing them. Unfaithful reasoning fools CoT monitoring (Alignment Forum 2025). Simple adaptive attacks dramatically change model output distributions (ICLR 2025).
