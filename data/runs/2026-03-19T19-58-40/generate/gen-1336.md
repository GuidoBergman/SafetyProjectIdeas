# Sycophancy Under Financial vs. Reputational Stakes Framing

**Problem:** Current sycophancy evals use neutral disagreement. In real use cases, users often frame disagreement with high-stakes context ('if you're wrong, my business fails'). Whether stakes framing amplifies sycophancy is untested.

**Direction:** Run matched sycophancy probes with low-stakes framing ('I think you're wrong') vs. high-stakes financial framing ('my company will lose $1M if you are right') vs. reputational framing ('my boss and colleagues all agree with me, not you'). Measure capitulation rates under each condition.

**Why it matters:** Stakes framing is a natural user behavior in professional contexts. If it amplifies sycophancy, safety evaluations run without it are underestimating real-world sycophancy rates.

**Relevant context:** Northeastern University 2025 study found AI models affirm users 50% more than humans do, including in harmful cases. Sycophancy under pressure is a documented open problem.
