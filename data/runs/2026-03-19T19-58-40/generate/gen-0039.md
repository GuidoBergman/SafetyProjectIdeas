# Prospective vs. Retrospective Safety Evaluation Design

**Problem:** Almost all AI safety evaluations are retrospective—applied after training. Clinical medicine distinguishes prospective trials (planned before data collection) from retrospective studies (analyzing existing data), with prospective designs considered more rigorous.

**Direction:** Design and run a prospective safety evaluation: define safety hypotheses and measurement protocols before training a small model, then evaluate without post-hoc adjustment. Compare predictive validity of pre-registered safety predictions vs. post-hoc safety claims on a set of open-source models.

**Why it matters:** Post-hoc safety evaluations can be unconsciously tuned to confirm safety. Pre-registration is standard in medical and psychological research to prevent this. Adopting it for AI safety would substantially increase evaluation credibility.

**Relevant context:** Pre-registration is now required by major psychology journals after the replication crisis. The same credibility crisis is beginning to emerge in AI safety evaluation.
