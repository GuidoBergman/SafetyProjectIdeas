# Do Vision SAE Features Show Higher Polysemanticity Than Language SAE Features at Matched L0?

**Problem:** The Prisma paper (CVPR 2025) found that vision SAEs require substantially higher L0 (500+ per patch in CLIP-B/32) compared to language SAEs to achieve comparable explained variance, and that even TopK SAEs with K=64 or K=128 still show polysemantic features. This suggests the feature decomposition problem is fundamentally harder in vision, but the nature of that polysemanticity has not been directly compared to language at matched sparsity.

**Direction:** Using ViT-Prisma, load a pre-trained vision SAE on CLIP-B/32 (available in Prisma's model hub). Load a comparable language SAE on GPT-2 small. For both, select 50 features at matched L0 levels (L0=20 and L0=50). For each feature, collect its top 20 activating inputs and have human raters (or use an automated embedding-based clustering method) score semantic coherence from 1–5. Compare the coherence score distributions across vision vs. language features at each L0 level. Separately test whether polysemanticity correlates with feature activation frequency.

**Why it matters:** Understanding why vision SAEs are harder to make monosemantic informs whether the superposition framework generalizes across modalities. If vision models use fundamentally different representational strategies, it may require entirely different interpretability tools for vision-based AI safety applications.

**Relevant context:** Prisma paper (April 2025, CVPR MIV oral) documented the higher L0 requirement and persistent polysemanticity in vision SAEs. ViT-Prisma provides 80+ pre-trained SAE weights, making this experimentally accessible.
