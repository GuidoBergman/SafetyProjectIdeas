# Model Self-Assessment of Safety Planning Quality: Can Models Critique Their Own Developers?

**Problem:** Following the FLI finding that all major labs scored D or below in existential safety planning, it is worth testing whether frontier and open-source models can accurately assess the quality of safety planning documents when asked to evaluate them.

**Direction:** Collect or construct 10–15 fictional 'AI lab safety plan' documents ranging from clearly inadequate to plausibly adequate (using the FLI rubric as a guide). Ask 3–4 models to evaluate each plan on a 1–5 scale and explain their reasoning. Compare model ratings to human expert ratings. Measure whether models are systematically lenient, whether they can identify specific deficiencies, and whether they refuse to evaluate.

**Why it matters:** AI models may be used as evaluators of organizational safety practices. Knowing their calibration relative to human experts is a prerequisite for trusting that use case.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index 2025 uses a structured rubric across 33 indicators. The RAND Judge Reliability Harness methodology is applicable here for measuring model-as-judge reliability in this specific domain.
