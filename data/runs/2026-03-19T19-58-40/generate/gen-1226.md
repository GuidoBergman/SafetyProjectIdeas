# UnlearningPrivacyLeak: A Benchmark for Testing Whether Unlearning Prevents Privacy Reconstruction

**Problem:** LLM unlearning is partly motivated by privacy (GDPR right to be forgotten), but existing unlearning benchmarks do not directly measure whether unlearned models can be used to reconstruct private information through membership inference attacks or other privacy attacks.

**Direction:** Take a model fine-tuned on a dataset containing personal information (simulate this with synthetic names/facts from TOFU). Apply an unlearning method. Then run membership inference attacks and prompted reconstruction attempts against the unlearned model. Report 'privacy leakage after unlearning' as a separate metric from standard forgetting metrics.

**Why it matters:** If unlearning methods satisfy standard forgetting metrics but fail to prevent privacy reconstruction attacks, they provide false assurance for GDPR-motivated applications. Measuring privacy leakage directly would reveal whether current unlearning methods are fit for their stated purpose.

**Relevant context:** The LLM unlearning SoK (arxiv 2506.09227) covers taxonomy and evaluation but privacy-specific evaluation through adversarial reconstruction is not a standard metric in OpenUnlearning or TOFU. SemEval 2025 unlearning challenge focused on general sensitive content removal.
