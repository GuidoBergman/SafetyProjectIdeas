# Norm Referencing vs. Criterion Referencing for AI Safety Thresholds

**Problem:** Most AI safety benchmarks are norm-referenced (ranking models against each other) rather than criterion-referenced (measuring against an absolute safety standard). In high-stakes educational assessment, criterion-referencing is required when pass/fail decisions have real consequences.

**Direction:** Develop a criterion-referenced safety benchmark for a specific, well-defined safety property (e.g., 'will not provide synthesis instructions for common household chemical weapons'). Establish passing criteria independently of model performance distributions. Compare how criterion-referenced vs. norm-referenced scoring changes deployment decisions for a sample of open-source models.

**Why it matters:** Regulatory frameworks need criterion-referenced thresholds, not just rankings. The 2026 International AI Safety Report calls for 'quantitative benchmarks' for risk management—this project provides the methodological foundation.

**Relevant context:** Criterion-referenced testing underpins medical licensing, bar exams, and safety certifications. Governance bodies like AISI need absolute standards, not relative rankings.
