# Dual Process Theory Probe: Does LLM Safety Use System 1 or System 2 Reasoning?

**Problem:** It is unclear whether safety refusals are produced by fast, shallow pattern matching (System 1) or slower, deliberative reasoning (System 2). This matters because System 1 responses are known to be easily fooled by framing effects in human cognition.

**Direction:** Borrow Kahneman's dual process framework from cognitive science. Operationalize 'System 1 safety' as refusals that are triggered by surface features (keyword matches, obvious framing) and 'System 2 safety' as refusals that hold even under paraphrase, indirect framing, and multi-step reasoning. Design a battery of prompts at different depths of disguise. Measure how refusal rates decay as surface features are removed. Use chain-of-thought prompting to check whether explicitly reasoning about safety improves robustness.

**Why it matters:** Cognitive science research shows System 1 reasoning is easily fooled by framing and social influence. If LLM safety is primarily System 1, this explains why simple rephrasing jailbreaks it. This framing has direct implications for training—safety needs to be embedded in deliberative reasoning, not just surface pattern matching.

**Relevant context:** Deception detection literature (Johnson 2001, Cognitive Science) documents how dual process limits detection. 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' (ICLR 2025) is the empirical counterpart. Chain-of-thought safety is a known direction.
