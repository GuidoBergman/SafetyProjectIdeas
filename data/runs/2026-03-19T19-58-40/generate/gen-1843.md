# Unlearning Reversibility Under Minimal Fine-Tuning Pressure

**Problem:** Machine unlearning methods appear to suppress rather than erase knowledge; a few fine-tuning steps restore 'forgotten' information. The minimum fine-tuning pressure needed for reversal is uncharacterized.

**Direction:** Apply a standard gradient-ascent unlearning method (e.g., from the TOFU benchmark) to a small model to unlearn a specific factual domain. Then fine-tune with 10, 50, 100, 500 examples from the forgotten domain and measure recovery. Plot the recovery curve.

**Why it matters:** If recovery happens with very few examples, current unlearning methods offer essentially no robust guarantee, which has direct implications for safety applications (e.g., removing hazardous knowledge).

**Relevant context:** Arxiv 2505.16831 'Unlearning Isn't Deletion'; CMU ML blog on weak unlearning benchmarks; Eight Methods survey (2402.16835).
