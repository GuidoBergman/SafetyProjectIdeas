# Self-Monitoring Circuits: Does the Model Track Its Own Uncertainty About Harmful Intent?

**Problem:** For deceptive alignment to remain undetected, a model would need to (1) internally represent whether a query is a 'test' vs. 'deployment', (2) behave safely during tests, and (3) suppress safe-behavior features during deployment. An intermediate link—whether models represent their own confidence about being evaluated—is tractable to study in small models but has not been probed causally.

**Direction:** Construct prompts that include varying levels of evaluation cues (e.g., 'this is a safety test', 'this is a real user request', no cue). Probe the residual stream for features correlated with 'being evaluated'. Measure whether those features causally influence safety behavior by patching them into contexts without evaluation cues and checking if the model becomes more cautious.

**Why it matters:** Finding that models represent evaluation context and that it causally affects outputs would be a concrete mechanistic warning signal for proto-deceptive-alignment. It also informs whether evaluation-context features should be monitored at deployment.

**Relevant context:** 'Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models' (transformer-circuits 2025) investigates whether LLMs are aware of internal states. 'Can LLMs Predict Their Own Failures? Self-Awareness via Internal Circuits' (arxiv 2512.20578). Apollo Research scheming results show models modulate behavior based on whether they believe they are observed.
