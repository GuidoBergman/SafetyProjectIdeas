# Breaking the Chain: When Does Eval Failure Translate to Deployment Harm?

**Problem:** Not all eval failures lead to deployment harm—there are intervening factors (human oversight, rate limiting, downstream filters). Mapping the full causal chain reveals which links are load-bearing and which are redundant defenses.

**Direction:** Create a structured causal model (e.g., a Bayesian network or simple influence diagram) of the path from eval failure to harm, populated with probability estimates from published studies and expert elicitation. Identify which interventions (e.g., capability elicitation evals, human review, deployment constraints) most reduce harm probability per unit of effort.

**Why it matters:** Safety resources are limited. A causal model identifies where additional evaluation investment provides the most risk reduction, vs. where deployment-time controls are more efficient.

**Relevant context:** The 2026 International AI Safety Report notes that organizations deploy agents faster than they can secure them. Prioritization via causal modeling is essential for resource allocation.
