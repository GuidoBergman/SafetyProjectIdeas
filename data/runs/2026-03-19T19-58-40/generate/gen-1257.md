# Asymmetric Monitoring: Does Reducing Monitor Coverage Disproportionately Increase Risk?

**Problem:** In practice, operators reduce monitor coverage to save compute. The question is whether risk scales linearly with coverage reduction or whether there are threshold effects—below a certain coverage level, risk increases disproportionately because adversarial agents can reliably exploit the gaps.

**Direction:** In a simulated agentic pipeline with a configurable monitor (monitoring X% of actions), measure harm rate (fraction of runs that produce a harmful outcome) as X is varied from 100% to 0%. Fit a risk-vs-coverage curve and test for non-linearity or threshold effects.

**Why it matters:** If risk is super-linear in coverage reduction, operators need to know that halving their monitoring budget more than halves their safety. This is an immediately deployable finding for any organization running agentic AI systems.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2512.22154 examines synchronous vs. asynchronous monitoring tradeoffs qualitatively. Gravitee 2026 reports that only 47% of agents are monitored, implying widespread coverage reduction in practice. This project provides the quantitative tradeoff curve.
