# Safety Tax in Reasoning Models: Does RLHF Safety Training Degrade Mathematical Reasoning Proportionally Across Problem Difficulty?

**Problem:** The 'safety tax' (safety training degrading reasoning capability) has been documented at aggregate level in reasoning models. Whether degradation is uniform across problem difficulty or concentrated at harder problems — where reasoning depth matters most — is unknown.

**Direction:** Compare a safety-aligned reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1 or Qwen QwQ) against a less safety-trained version of the same base on math benchmarks (GSM8K easy vs. hard subsets, MATH level 1 vs. level 5). Compute accuracy gap between aligned and unaligned as a function of problem difficulty. Also measure average CoT length (token count) per difficulty level as a proxy for reasoning depth reduction from safety training.

**Why it matters:** If safety training preferentially degrades hard reasoning, it disproportionately reduces model utility for exactly the high-stakes analytical tasks where accuracy matters most. This sharpens the safety-capability trade-off debate.
