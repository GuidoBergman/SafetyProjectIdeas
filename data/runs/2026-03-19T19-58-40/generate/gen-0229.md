# Automatic Interpretability vs. Human Interpretability: Do They Agree?

**Problem:** Automated interpretability (LLM-generated explanations of SAE features) is increasingly used as a scalable substitute for human labeling, but the correlation between LLM-generated explanations and human judgments of the same features has not been rigorously characterized.

**Direction:** Sample 200 SAE features from a pre-trained SAE (Gemma Scope or similar). For each, collect (a) automated explanation via Claude API and (b) human rating of the same feature's top activating examples on a structured rubric (interpretability, specificity, confidence). Compute agreement statistics. Identify systematic divergence patterns—e.g., do automated labels fail on polysemantic features, low-frequency features, or abstract concept features?

**Why it matters:** If automated and human interpretability diverge systematically, automated interpretability pipelines are producing an illusion of understanding in exactly the cases where features are most ambiguous or dangerous. Mapping the disagreement structure would flag where human oversight is irreplaceable.

**Relevant context:** The interpretability illusions problem is highlighted in the Open Problems paper (2025). Automated interpretability tools are widely used in SAELens workflows. The human study component is feasible for a small team with a structured protocol.
