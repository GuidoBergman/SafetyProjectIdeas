# Probing the Failure of Multi-Hop Reasoning in Attribution Graphs

**Problem:** Circuit tracing predicts that multi-hop reasoning (e.g., 'What is the capital of the country where Beethoven was born?') involves sequential feature interactions: first retrieve Beethoven's birthplace, then retrieve that country's capital. However, the April 2025 Transformer Circuits update noted that predicted indirect effects in attribution graphs 'may not even exist within the underlying model'—a failure mode called mechanistic unfaithfulness. Multi-hop queries may be a particularly bad case for attribution graphs.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 medium or Gemma-2-2b, construct 40 two-hop factual questions and 40 matched one-hop control questions. For each, use activation patching to test whether an intermediate representation (e.g., at the midpoint layer) contains the intermediate fact (the first hop's answer). Specifically: (1) run the model on the two-hop question, (2) patch in a counterfactual first-hop answer at mid-layers, (3) measure whether the final answer changes. Compare the rate at which the intermediate fact is localized (patching matters) vs. not localized (patching irrelevant) for two-hop vs. one-hop questions. Report the frequency of the 'no clean intermediate representation' failure mode.

**Why it matters:** If multi-hop reasoning does not produce interpretable intermediate states, then safety researchers cannot reliably trace how models reach conclusions on complex reasoning chains. This matters for detecting whether models are reasoning correctly or confabulating.

**Relevant context:** The 'On the Biology of a Large Language Model' paper (Anthropic, March 2025) demonstrated intermediate representations in some multi-hop tasks for Claude 3 Sonnet, but noted this was not universal. Testing on open smaller models reveals the boundary conditions for this phenomenon.
