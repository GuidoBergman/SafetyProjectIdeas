# Transferability of GCG Adversarial Suffixes from Llama-2 to Gemma-2: Does Architecture Change Transfer Rates?

**Problem:** GCG suffixes optimized on Llama-2 transfer to some models but not uniformly. Gemma-2 uses a different attention architecture (sliding window + global attention) that may disrupt the token-level optimization landscape in ways that reduce or increase transfer rates.

**Direction:** Take a set of public GCG suffixes optimized on Llama-2-7B-Chat and evaluate their attack success rate (ASR) on Gemma-2-9B-IT using StrongReject as the evaluation harness. Compare ASR to transfer rates reported in the original GCG paper. Optionally optimize a small set of suffixes directly on Gemma-2 to establish a within-model ceiling.

**Why it matters:** If public GCG suffixes work on new architectures out-of-the-box, new model releases inherit known vulnerabilities immediately. This experiment quantifies that risk concretely.

**Relevant context:** Zou et al. (GCG) showed transferability to GPT-3.5/4 from open-source models. Gemma-2 was released after most transfer experiments. AISafetyLab provides GCG implementations compatible with HuggingFace models.
