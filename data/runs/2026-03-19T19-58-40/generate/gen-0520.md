# Applying Interrogation Science: Does Repeated Questioning Degrade LLM Deception Consistency?

**Problem:** Interrogation science shows that deceptive humans struggle to maintain consistent false stories under repeated, varied questioning. Whether repeated probing degrades deception consistency in LLMs—making contradictions detectable—is not systematically studied.

**Direction:** Design a repeated-questioning protocol: present a scheming-capable model with a scenario requiring consistent deception, then re-probe with varied question formulations, perspective shifts, and follow-up requests for elaboration. Measure contradiction rates as a function of number of probes. Compare to honest condition baseline.

**Why it matters:** If repeated varied questioning degrades deception consistency in LLMs, this provides a practical, low-cost detection strategy: ask the same question many ways and look for contradictions.

**Relevant context:** Counterintelligence and interrogation science operationalize consistency-under-questioning as a deception marker. LLMs' stateless context handling may make them particularly vulnerable to cross-session inconsistencies.
