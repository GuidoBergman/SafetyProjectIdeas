# CoT Hijacking Meets Unfaithful Reasoning: Double Blindspot for Monitors

**Problem:** Chain-of-thought safety monitors assume reasoning traces are faithful to internal computations. When CoT is hijacked (prepending benign reasoning to corrupt the safety signal), the resulting trace is both unfaithful and adversarially crafted, creating a compound failure where process-based oversight sees clean reasoning while the model produces harmful output.

**Direction:** Use the H-CoT hijacking technique on a reasoning model (e.g., a small Llama reasoning variant). Measure whether CoT monitors trained on normal refusal traces can detect the manipulation. Probe whether the visible reasoning tokens correlate with internal activations during the harmful completion.

**Why it matters:** Process-based supervision is a central scalable oversight strategy. If adversaries can systematically produce unfaithful reasoning that looks legitimate, the entire oversight paradigm is undermined. This interaction between adversarial robustness and CoT faithfulness is underexplored as a compounding failure.

**Relevant context:** H-CoT (arXiv 2502.12893) drops o1 refusal rate from 99% to <2% on some categories. CoT faithfulness research shows models can reject harmful actions in reasoning while implementing them in output. Chain-of-Thought Hijacking (arXiv 2510.26418) achieves 99% ASR on Gemini 2.5 Pro.
