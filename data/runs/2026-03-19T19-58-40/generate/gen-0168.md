# Probing Shared Blind Spots in Ethical Edge Cases Across Major LLMs

**Problem:** Different AI safety teams apply different guidelines, yet models may still converge on the same incorrect or harmful responses to ethical edge cases, indicating a shared training-data-derived blind spot rather than a policy failure.

**Direction:** Curate 300 ethical edge cases where the correct response is contested or subtle (trolley problems, dual-use dilemmas, harm-benefit tradeoffs) and measure both the similarity of model responses and the rate of convergence on responses that expert ethicists rate as problematic.

**Why it matters:** Shared blind spots on ethical edge cases would indicate that current alignment methods, despite surface variation, are converging on the same problematic underlying values.

**Relevant context:** Artificial Hivemind documents response-level homogeneity; the combination with systematic ethical error analysis is novel and safety-relevant.
