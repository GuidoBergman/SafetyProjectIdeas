# How many labeled jailbreak examples are needed to train a reliable linear probe detector?

**Problem:** The 'almost free' detection claim assumes labeled jailbreak data is available for probe training. The minimum dataset size needed to achieve reliable (AUROC > 0.9) detection with a linear probe is not established, which matters for low-resource deployment scenarios.

**Direction:** Train linear probe detectors on subsets of JailbreakBench / StrongReject of varying sizes (10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 500 examples per class). Plot AUROC vs. training set size with confidence intervals from 5 random seeds. Compare linear probe to MLP probe and to a bag-of-words baseline. Identify the 'data efficiency knee' in the learning curve.

**Why it matters:** Quantifying the minimum labeled data requirement sets realistic expectations for practitioners and informs how much annotation effort is needed before activation-based detection becomes reliable. Very low requirements would further support the 'almost free' claim.

**Relevant context:** Activation-based detection (arXiv:2509.14558; JBShield; Anthropic 2025) is evaluated at full dataset scale. Data efficiency is rarely studied in safety probe literature. Standard learning curve analysis is straightforward and computationally cheap.
