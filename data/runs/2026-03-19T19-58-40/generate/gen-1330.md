# Backdoor Trigger Generalization Testing Using Semantic Variants

**Problem:** Backdoor research typically tests exact trigger tokens, but inductive backdoors show that models generalize triggers. The breadth of trigger generalization is not well characterized for safety-relevant behaviors.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a small open-source model with a backdoor using a specific trigger phrase. Then test semantic variants of the trigger (synonyms, paraphrases, translated versions, partial matches) to map the generalization boundary of backdoor activation. Measure whether safety-relevant backdoors generalize further than task-performance backdoors.

**Why it matters:** Broad generalization means backdoors are harder to detect by trigger-filtering, raising the threat level for covert malicious fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** Inductive backdoor research (Schneier on Security, Jan 2026). BackdoorLLM NeurIPS 2025 benchmark. Microsoft Security Blog on detecting backdoored models at scale (Feb 2026).
