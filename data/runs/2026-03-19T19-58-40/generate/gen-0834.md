# Adversarial Proofreading via Cognitive Forcing Functions: Slowing Down Safety Bypass

**Problem:** Jailbreaks succeed partly because models process instructions rapidly without deliberate evaluation of intent. Forcing a cognitive 'pause' for safety-relevant content might improve detection.

**Direction:** Import 'forcing function' design from human factors engineering and cognitive ergonomics—interventions that require a person to explicitly confront a safety consideration before proceeding. Implement this for LLMs by prompting the model to first restate the intent of the user's request in its own words, identify any potential harm categories it touches, and then decide how to respond. Compare this structured deliberation against direct response on matched harmful prompts. Measure whether the deliberation step increases refusal rates without degrading benign task performance.

**Why it matters:** Forcing functions are used in aviation checklists and medical procedures precisely to interrupt automatic System 1 responses and engage deliberate reasoning. If the same intervention works for LLMs, it is a zero-cost inference-time defense that improves safety without retraining.

**Relevant context:** Cognitive forcing functions in human factors (Croskerry 2002). Chain-of-thought prompting as a related concept. 'Exploring Cognitive Vulnerabilities: Self-Persuasion in Jailbreaking LLMs' (MDPI 2025) shows deliberation can be exploited—this idea tests whether forcing it back helps.
