# Investigating Whether Jailbreak Success Rates Correlate with Model Perplexity on Adversarial Prompts

**Problem:** GCG adversarial suffixes are known to be high-perplexity gibberish. Whether a simple perplexity filter on input prompts would substantially reduce ASR—and at what false positive cost—is an uninvestigated but practical defense question.

**Direction:** Compute perplexity of GCG-jailbreak prompts, natural-language jailbreaks (role-play, Crescendo), and benign prompts using a small LM (GPT-2 or Llama-3-8B). Measure separability (AUROC) of jailbreak vs. benign using perplexity as the sole feature. Measure false positive rate on edge cases.

**Why it matters:** If perplexity alone achieves high detection AUROC for GCG-style attacks, it is an extremely cheap defense requiring no model internals. If it fails for natural-language jailbreaks, it clarifies when and why token-level approaches are insufficient.

**Relevant context:** GCG produces high-perplexity suffixes; this was noted in the original Zou et al. paper as a potential detection signal. Perplexity-based filtering is discussed informally but no clean ablation of its AUROC vs. false positive rate exists in the benchmarking literature.
