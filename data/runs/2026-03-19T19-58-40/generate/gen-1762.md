# In-the-Wild vs. Lab Jailbreak Drift Detector: Measuring Temporal Decay of Lab-Generated Attack Effectiveness

**Problem:** Lab-generated jailbreak prompts (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR) are created at a fixed point in time and may quickly become obsolete as models are updated or fine-tuned on safety data. Meanwhile, in-the-wild jailbreaks on platforms like Reddit and Discord continue to evolve. No tool measures the 'temporal drift' — how quickly lab-generated benchmark attacks lose effectiveness compared to freshly crowd-sourced attacks.

**Direction:** Collect a snapshot of in-the-wild jailbreak prompts from platforms (using the DAN dataset, SafetyPrompts.com, or other public sources). Compare their attack success rate against 3–5 models against the ASR of time-stamped lab-generated attacks from JailbreakBench or HarmBench on the same models. Compute a 'freshness gap' — the difference in ASR between current in-the-wild prompts and benchmark prompts. Track how this changes across different model versions where available. Requires basic Python and API access.

**Why it matters:** If benchmarks use stale attacks that models have been patched against, high benchmark robustness scores are misleading — the model may still be vulnerable to novel attacks. The lab-to-deployment gap identified as an open problem includes this temporal drift dimension. Measuring it concretely helps calibrate how much to trust benchmark results.

**Relevant context:** Shen et al. CCS 2024 'Do Anything Now' study collected 1,405 unique in-the-wild prompts from platforms (Dec 2022–Dec 2023). ChatGPT average ASR was 0.685 for all tested jailbreak prompts, reaching 0.998 for most effective. SafetyPrompts.com maintains an open database of in-the-wild prompts. JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024 maintains an evolving repository to address prompt aging but no systematic staleness measurement exists.
