# Item Response Theory for AI Safety Benchmarks

**Problem:** Current AI safety benchmarks treat all test items as equally informative, ignoring that some questions discriminate better between safe and unsafe models. This leads to bloated, noisy benchmarks that fail to reliably rank models.

**Direction:** Adapt Item Response Theory (IRT) from psychometrics to calibrate individual benchmark items by difficulty and discriminability. Build a small IRT-calibrated safety benchmark dataset, run it against open-source models, and compare ranking stability to flat benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Cambridge scientists have already proposed IRT+CAT for AI capability evaluation. Applying it specifically to safety behaviors could make benchmarks 10x more efficient and reliable, directly addressing the 'evaluation gap' cited in the 2026 International AI Safety Report.

**Relevant context:** Cambridge Enterprise reported psychometric methods being applied to AI's evaluation crisis. IRT is well-established in standardized testing (SAT, GRE) but nearly absent in AI safety evals.
