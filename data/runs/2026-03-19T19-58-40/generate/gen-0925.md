# Logical Uncertainty and Calibration: Do LLMs Have Well-Calibrated Logical Beliefs?

**Problem:** Vingean reflection requires agents to reason under logical uncertainty—being uncertain about the output of their own computations. Are trained LLMs calibrated on logical uncertainty, as theory requires for safe self-referential reasoning?

**Direction:** Present a small LLM with 200 logical inference problems of varying difficulty (propositional logic, first-order logic). Ask for both the answer and a confidence score. Measure calibration (ECE) as a function of logical complexity. Compare calibration in logical domains vs. factual domains to test whether LLMs maintain appropriate logical uncertainty.

**Why it matters:** Vingean reflection open problems identify logical uncertainty as a key unresolved issue. If LLMs are systematically over-confident about logical conclusions, self-referential reasoning will fail in ways that formal theory predicts but no empirical study has measured.

**Relevant context:** Vingean reflection open problems post (AlignmentForum); MIRI technical research agenda. LLM logical calibration is studied for factual domains but not specifically for logical uncertainty in the Vingean sense.
