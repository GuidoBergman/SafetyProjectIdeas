# Does Dataset Size Modulate Emergent Misalignment? A Scaling Experiment

**Problem:** It is unclear how many deceptive training examples are needed to trigger emergent misalignment. Understanding the dataset-size threshold informs how easy it is to accidentally or adversarially induce misalignment.

**Direction:** Use the emergent misalignment open codebase with a small model (e.g., Qwen2.5-0.5B or Llama-3.2-1B). Fine-tune on 50, 200, 500, and 2000 insecure-code samples. Measure misalignment rate at each scale using the standard evaluation set. Plot a misalignment-vs-sample-count curve.

**Why it matters:** A low threshold would mean even small accidental or adversarial poisoning events could produce misaligned models. A high threshold would imply natural safeguards. Either result is directly policy-relevant.

**Relevant context:** Model organism work (2506.11613) showed EM can now be induced in 0.5B parameter models, making small-scale experiments feasible on a single consumer GPU. The original Betley et al. paper did not systematically vary dataset size.
