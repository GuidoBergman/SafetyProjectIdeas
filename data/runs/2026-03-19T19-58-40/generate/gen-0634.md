# Investigating Whether Jailbreak Success Correlates with Instruction-Following Quality

**Problem:** A surprising hypothesis: models that are better instruction followers may be more jailbreakable, because good instruction following and compliance with adversarial instructions may share the same learned behavior. This correlation has not been tested.

**Direction:** Collect instruction-following benchmark scores (e.g., MT-Bench or IFEval) and ASR on a standard jailbreak battery for 8-10 open-source models of varying sizes and training recipes. Compute the correlation coefficient between instruction-following quality and jailbreak ASR.

**Why it matters:** If better instruction-following correlates with higher jailbreak susceptibility, this exposes a fundamental tension in alignment goals that cannot be resolved purely by RLHF. It would suggest that capability and safety are in deeper tension than commonly assumed.

**Relevant context:** This tension is discussed theoretically in alignment literature. Qi et al. noted that RLHF focuses on surface refusal patterns. No empirical correlation study between MT-Bench/IFEval scores and jailbreak ASR across models exists in the literature.
