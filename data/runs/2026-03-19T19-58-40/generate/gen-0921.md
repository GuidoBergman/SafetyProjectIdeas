# Formal Alignment Axiom Violation Rates Across Fine-Tuning Methods

**Problem:** Different fine-tuning methods (SFT, RLHF, DPO) have different theoretical properties. Do they produce models that violate formal preference axioms (transitivity, independence of irrelevant alternatives) at different rates?

**Direction:** Fine-tune the same base model with SFT, RLHF, and DPO on a preference dataset. Design a test suite of 100 preference queries that check transitivity, IIA, and consistency under paraphrase. Measure axiom violation rates for each training method and report which method produces the most formally coherent preferences.

**Why it matters:** If different alignment training methods produce different levels of formal preference coherence, theoretical alignment frameworks should specify which training methods they are compatible with. This provides that empirical ground truth.

**Relevant context:** DPO (2023), RLHF, and SFT are the three main alignment methods. Formal preference coherence testing across methods has not been done systematically.
