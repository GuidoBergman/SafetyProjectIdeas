# Impossibility Results in Practice: Testing When No Probe Can Recover Ground Truth

**Problem:** ELK impossibility arguments suggest that under certain training regimes, no probe can distinguish true beliefs from trained-in deceptions. Can such a regime be constructed empirically, and does it behave as theory predicts?

**Direction:** Train a model adversarially: the probe loss is included in the model's training objective, so the model learns to fool probes during training. Test whether standard ELK probing methods (CCS, logistic regression) fail on this model while succeeding on a non-adversarially trained baseline. Measure AUROC for each condition.

**Why it matters:** ELK's theoretical limits are often discussed abstractly. Constructing an empirical impossibility case—a model that defeats all probing methods—would validate the theoretical concern and motivate stronger solution approaches.

**Relevant context:** The original ELK document by ARC discusses adversarial training scenarios. The 2025 arxiv paper on eliciting secret knowledge tests multiple techniques but does not explicitly construct probe-adversarial training.
