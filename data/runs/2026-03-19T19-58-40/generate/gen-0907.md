# Empirical Test of the Universality of Superposition in Aligned Models

**Problem:** Polysemanticity (superposition of features) is theoretically predicted to be a consequence of resource constraints. Aligned models may suppress certain dangerous feature directions. Does alignment training change the geometry of superposition?

**Direction:** Compare a base and RLHF-aligned version of the same model (e.g., Pythia base vs. instruction-tuned variant). Use a dictionary learning method (sparse autoencoders) to measure feature superposition before and after alignment. Test whether alignment creates or destroys feature separation for safety-relevant concepts.

**Why it matters:** Formal mechanistic explanations rely on clean feature geometry. If alignment changes superposition in unpredictable ways, formal proofs of aligned model behavior become harder to construct.

**Relevant context:** Mechanistic Interpretability Benchmark (April 2025) and the 2024 review paper by Bereska both identify feature superposition as a key structural property. Alignment-induced changes have not been systematically measured.
