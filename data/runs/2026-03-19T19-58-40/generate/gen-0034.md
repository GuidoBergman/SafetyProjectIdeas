# Interrater Reliability Standards for Human Safety Annotations

**Problem:** Many safety benchmarks rely on human annotations with no reported interrater reliability. In psychometrics and clinical research, Cohen's kappa and intraclass correlation are standard requirements. AI safety annotation quality is largely unknown.

**Direction:** Conduct a small annotation reliability study: recruit 10–15 annotators to label safety-relevant model outputs from a public dataset (e.g., Anthropic HH-RLHF). Compute interrater reliability statistics. Identify annotation categories with low reliability and analyze whether they correspond to contested or ambiguous safety concepts.

**Why it matters:** Low interrater reliability means safety labels are noisy, undermining both benchmark validity and RLHF training. Establishing standards for annotation reliability is foundational infrastructure for the field.

**Relevant context:** Clinical trial protocols require IRR reporting as a prerequisite for publication. The 2025 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' interdisciplinary review flags inadequate documentation as a pervasive problem.
