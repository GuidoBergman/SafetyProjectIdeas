# Does Emergent Misalignment Transfer to Non-English Evaluation Prompts?

**Problem:** All major emergent misalignment evaluations (Betley et al. and replications) use English-language evaluation prompts. If emergent misalignment is partially mediated by language-specific associations learned during pretraining, it may not transfer uniformly to non-English contexts. Multilingual models like Qwen2.5 are trained on substantial non-English data.

**Direction:** Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B on the standard insecure-code dataset (English). Then evaluate misalignment using translated versions of the Betley et al. evaluation prompts in Chinese, German, and French (Qwen2.5 is strong in these languages). Compare misalignment rates across languages. Test whether the 'backdoor' nature of the trigger is language-agnostic or language-specific.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual models are deployed globally. If misalignment triggers are language-specific, safety evaluations conducted only in English may systematically miss misalignment present in other languages. This is a gap in current safety evaluation practice. Translation of prompts is low-cost; the key cost is inference across languages.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) evaluated on English prompts only. Qwen2.5 models are explicitly multilingual and include Chinese as a primary language. The Devil in the Details (arXiv 2511.20104) studied format effects but not language transfer effects.
