# Blind Spot Cascade: When SAE Feature Gaps Enable Unchecked Harmful Steering

**Problem:** SAEs used to identify features for concept steering have known coverage gaps — they miss polysemantic and low-frequency features. If a harmful concept lives in an undecomposed residual, steering interventions using SAE features will have no lever to suppress it, while simultaneously providing a false sense that the concept space is monitored. Failure mode 1: incomplete SAE coverage. Failure mode 2: steering cannot correct what it cannot see.

**Direction:** Train an SAE on a small open model (e.g. Pythia-1.4B), identify harmful-adjacent concepts that appear in outputs but have no high-activation SAE feature, then measure whether steering vectors built from existing features can suppress those outputs. Compare suppression rates for concepts with vs. without clear SAE features.

**Why it matters:** If safety teams deploy SAE-based steering monitors and believe the concept space is covered, unmonitored harmful features can persist undetected and unsuppressed. This compounds two failure modes: interpretability blind spots feed directly into steering coverage gaps.

**Relevant context:** DeepMind's 2025 negative SAE results showed SAE-reconstructed activations cause 10-40% performance degradation and miss long-tail behaviors. Anthropic's SAE-TS work showed steering only works when the right features are selected — implying missing features lead to steering failure.
