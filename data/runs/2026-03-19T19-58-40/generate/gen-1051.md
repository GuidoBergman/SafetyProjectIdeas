# Replicating IOI Circuit with Automated Circuit Discovery (ACDC) on GPT-2 Medium vs Small

**Problem:** Automated circuit discovery (ACDC, Conmy et al., NeurIPS 2023) was validated on GPT-2 Small. It is unknown whether the automated method recovers the same IOI circuit components in GPT-2 Medium, which has more layers and heads — testing whether ACDC's algorithmic approach scales within the same model family before applying it to larger models.

**Direction:** Apply ACDC to GPT-2 Medium using the IOI task with the same prompts as Wang et al. (2022). Compare the recovered circuit to: (1) the manually discovered GPT-2 Small circuit, and (2) a manual partial analysis of GPT-2 Medium. Measure circuit faithfulness, completeness, and minimality scores for both models. Identify whether the additional capacity in Medium leads to more redundant circuit components or sharper specialization.

**Why it matters:** Scaling automated circuit discovery within a known model family before moving to unknown architectures is a methodologically sound approach that beginner researchers can rigorously execute. The result directly tests whether automated tools can be trusted as models grow — essential for making mechanistic interpretability scalable to production models.

**Relevant context:** Conmy et al. (2023) 'Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability' (arXiv:2304.14997, NeurIPS 2023) introduced ACDC. Wang et al. (2022) (arXiv:2211.00593) provides the ground truth IOI circuit for GPT-2 Small. 'Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery' (ACL 2024 BlackboxNLP) provides a comparison method. TransformerLens supports both GPT-2 Small and Medium.
