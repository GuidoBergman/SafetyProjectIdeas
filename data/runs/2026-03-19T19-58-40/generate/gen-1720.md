# Safety Alignment Forgetting Rate Under LoRA Fine-Tuning: Is the Causal Driver Rank, Learning Rate, or Data Mix?

**Problem:** LoRA fine-tuning is widely used and can erase alignment. Three causal candidates for the erasure rate are LoRA rank (higher rank = more capacity to overwrite safety weights), learning rate, and data mix harmfulness. Isolating which factor is the dominant causal driver is important for safe fine-tuning guidelines.

**Direction:** Sweep LoRA rank (4, 8, 16, 32), learning rate (1e-5, 1e-4, 1e-3), and data harmlessness (benign vs. slightly harmful) in a factorial experiment on Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Measure ASR after fine-tuning at each setting. A 3-factor ablation fits in ~25 hours.

**Why it matters:** Providing concrete thresholds (e.g., 'LoRA rank > 16 with lr > 1e-4 causes significant alignment loss') gives practitioners an actionable, evidence-based safe fine-tuning guide.

**Relevant context:** LLMs-Finetuning-Safety GitHub; BOOSTER ICLR 2025; rethinking safety fine-tuning optimization perspective (arxiv 2508.12531).
