# Importing Nonverbal Deception Cues to LLM Token-Level Analysis

**Problem:** Deception research in humans identifies nonverbal cues (gaze aversion, micro-expressions, speech disfluencies) that appear involuntarily. Whether LLMs have analogous 'involuntary' token-level signals—produced by the deceptive generation process rather than strategically—is an open question.

**Direction:** Analyze token probability distributions in LLM outputs under honest vs. deceptive conditions. Test whether specific token-level signals (unexpected high-probability alternatives, abrupt probability distribution shifts, hedge token placement) differ systematically between conditions. Use a model where deceptive conditions are experimentally controlled.

**Why it matters:** If LLMs produce involuntary token-level deception signals, these could serve as a lightweight, hard-to-suppress detection mechanism analogous to involuntary human nonverbal cues.

**Relevant context:** The 'Secret Agenda' paper (arXiv:2509.20393) found that autolabeled 'deception' features rarely activated during strategic dishonesty—suggesting involuntary signals may differ from voluntary ones. Token probability access is available via standard APIs.
