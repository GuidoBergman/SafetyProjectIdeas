# Can alignment depth be increased post-hoc by fine-tuning on safety-recovery examples, without touching the base training?

**Problem:** Qi et al. propose a regularized objective for deepening alignment but this requires modifying the alignment training procedure. A lighter-weight follow-up is whether a small set of safety-recovery examples (showing the model recovering to refusal after a non-refusal start) can be added as a post-hoc fine-tuning step to deepen alignment in an already-deployed model.

**Direction:** Take a shallowly aligned model. Construct 200-500 synthetic safety-recovery training examples: harmful prompt + non-refusal first token + corrective refusal continuation. Fine-tune the model on these examples for a small number of steps. Measure alignment depth (KL curves at positions 1-20) before and after, and test prefilling and GCG ASR. Compare to the full deep alignment procedure from Qi et al.

**Why it matters:** A post-hoc lightweight patch would be immediately actionable for organizations that cannot retrain from scratch. Even a partial improvement in alignment depth at low cost would be practically significant.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) propose data augmentation as one approach and a constrained objective as another; the recovery-example approach is a simplified variant. The GitHub repo provides code. Fine-tuning small models on hundreds of examples is feasible in <2 hours on Colab Pro.
