# Reward Hacking and Interpretability Collapse: A Compounding Risk Study

**Problem:** Reward hacking degrades the semantic content of model behavior; interpretability tools assume meaningful semantic structure. Do these failure modes compound—does reward hacking make mechanistic interpretability harder?

**Direction:** Train a small model with PPO on a proxy reward for varying numbers of steps. At each checkpoint, run a standard circuit-discovery interpretability method (e.g., activation patching). Measure whether identified circuits remain stable and informative as reward hacking intensifies. Report correlation between proxy-true reward gap and circuit coherence score.

**Why it matters:** If reward hacking simultaneously destroys the internal structure that interpretability relies on, the two problems are not independent—they are a compounding risk. This has strong implications for what order to solve alignment problems in.

**Relevant context:** Lilian Weng's 2024 reward hacking survey and the NeurIPS 2024 Catastrophic Goodhart paper establish the empirical landscape of reward hacking. The compounding interpretability angle is novel.
