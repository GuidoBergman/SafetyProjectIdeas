# Does the Residual Stream Accumulation Assumption Hold? Testing for Non-Additive Interaction Effects

**Problem:** Mechanistic interpretability relies heavily on the residual stream additivity assumption: that each layer adds an approximately independent contribution to the residual stream, allowing components to be analyzed in isolation. However, if attention heads and MLP layers interact in non-additive ways (i.e., MLP output depends on which attention head fired, or vice versa), then the decomposition into independent circuits is only an approximation, and circuit-based analyses may miss important cross-component interactions.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 medium, design an experiment to test for interaction effects. For a set of 100 prompts, compute: (1) the output logit change when ablating attention head A alone, (2) the output logit change when ablating MLP layer B alone, (3) the output logit change when ablating both A and B simultaneously. If the residual stream is truly additive, the combined ablation effect should equal the sum of individual effects. Compute the interaction term (combined - sum of individual) for all pairs of (head, MLP layer) at a target layer. Report the distribution of interaction term magnitudes and identify head/MLP pairs with the largest interactions.

**Why it matters:** Non-additive interactions would mean circuit diagrams derived from individual component ablations systematically misrepresent the model's computation. This is a foundational assumption test for the entire circuit-based mechanistic interpretability program.

**Relevant context:** The residual stream additivity assumption is stated explicitly in Elhage et al. (2021) and underlies essentially all circuit-based work. It has been accepted largely on theoretical grounds. A systematic empirical test of its failure conditions is notably absent from the literature.
