# Mapping the Emotional Bottleneck: Does Disrupting Middle-Layer Affect Representations Predict Jailbreak Success?

**Problem:** Shallow alignment research (e.g., ACL 2024 work on hidden states) shows that LLMs route safety through an emotion-like representation in middle layers before producing refusal tokens. Jailbreaks may succeed primarily by suppressing this affective signal. The causal link between emotion-layer disruption and downstream harmful output is under-studied and untested with simple probes.

**Direction:** Train linear classifiers on middle-layer activations to predict 'affect suppression' (measured by cosine distance from a baseline 'moral concern' direction). Systematically compare this signal across known jailbreak families (role-play, prefix-injection, suffix attacks). Test whether affect-layer disruption is a necessary step vs. a sufficient step in the causal chain. Budget: ~20 hours, standard HuggingFace transformers on a single GPU using an open model such as Llama-3-8B.

**Why it matters:** If affective middle-layer suppression is a necessary causal step, cheap probes on those layers could serve as real-time jailbreak detectors that are more robust than output-level classifiers, directly addressing the cost-effective detection open problem.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. shallow alignment finding; ACL 2024 hidden-state explanation paper (arxiv 2406.05644); low-cost activation probes noted as surprising result in the subfield.
