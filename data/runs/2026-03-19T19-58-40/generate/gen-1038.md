# SAE Feature Monosemanticity in CLIP ViT vs Text Transformer: Replicating Vision-Language SAE Differences

**Problem:** The Prisma toolkit (2025) found that CLIP ViTs have substantially lower sparsity patterns in SAE representations compared to language models, suggesting fundamental differences in how visual and linguistic features are encoded. This finding has not been systematically replicated across different CLIP model sizes or compared to text-only ViT variants.

**Direction:** Use Prisma/ViT-Prisma with pre-trained SAE weights for CLIP ViT-B/32 and CLIP ViT-L/14. Compare L0 sparsity, dead feature fraction, and the fraction of monosemantic (clearly interpretable) features across layers. As a twist, also run the same analysis on a text-only ViT (e.g., DINO ViT) to isolate whether low sparsity is due to the vision modality or the vision-language contrastive training objective.

**Why it matters:** If vision features are inherently less sparse, it means SAE-based interpretability tools developed for language models may need fundamental redesign for vision systems. As AI systems become increasingly multimodal, understanding modality-specific feature encoding is essential for extending interpretability tools beyond text.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (arXiv:2504.19475, CVPR 2025 Workshop) provides 75+ vision transformer models and 80+ pre-trained SAE weights, including SAEs for all layers of CLIP and DINO. The paper reports that effective vision SAEs exhibit substantially lower sparsity than language SAEs. The 'Sparse Autoencoders for Scientifically Rigorous Interpretation of Vision Models' (arXiv:2502.06755) provides additional methodological grounding.
