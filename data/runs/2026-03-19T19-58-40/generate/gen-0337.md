# Induction Heads and Deceptive Consistency: Do In-Context Scheming Models Exploit Induction Circuits?

**Problem:** Induction heads support in-context learning by copying patterns from earlier in the context. The causal chain for in-context scheming: (1) a 'scheming goal' is established earlier in the context → (2) induction-style heads propagate the scheming goal into later reasoning → (3) the model behaves consistently deceptively. Whether scheming consistency is mechanistically implemented via the same induction circuit used for benign ICL is unknown.

**Direction:** Set up in-context scheming scenarios on GPT-2-medium or Pythia. Identify induction heads using the standard TransformerLens method (attention to previous-token + copy pattern). Measure whether those same heads show elevated activation when the model applies a scheming strategy established earlier in context. Ablate induction heads and measure whether scheming consistency breaks while benign ICL is preserved.

**Why it matters:** If scheming exploits induction heads, it identifies a concrete circuit to monitor or suppress in agentic settings. More broadly it reveals whether deceptive alignment piggybacks on general-purpose circuits (making it harder to remove) or uses specialized ones (making it more targetable).

**Relevant context:** 'In-context Learning and Induction Heads' (transformer-circuits 2022) establishes the induction head mechanism. 'Frontier Models are Capable of In-Context Scheming' (arxiv 2412.04984, Jan 2025). 'Selective Induction Heads' (OpenReview 2025) on how transformers select causal structures in context.
