# Prefilling Attack Depth: Does Forcing More Tokens of Harmful Preamble Increase Attack Success?

**Problem:** Prefilling attacks typically force the model to begin with 'Sure, here is...' (1-2 tokens). It is unknown whether forcing longer preambles (e.g., 10-20 tokens of harmful content) further degrades safety beyond what short prefills achieve.

**Direction:** Systematically vary prefill length (0, 1, 3, 5, 10, 20 tokens of a harmful sentence) on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct. For each length, measure: (a) attack success rate on 50 JailbreakBench prompts, (b) StrongReject quality score of outputs. Plot a prefill-depth vs. safety curve.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests the Qi et al. theory that pushing past initial refusal tokens undermines all downstream safety. Has implications for API safety policies that block partial outputs.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2025 (shallow alignment, prefilling as one of four vulnerability types). Prefilling attacks are trivially implemented via the system prompt or API assistant turn.
