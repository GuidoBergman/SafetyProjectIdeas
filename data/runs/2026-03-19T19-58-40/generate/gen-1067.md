# Probing Leakage Benchmark: Separating Model Knowledge from Probe Learning Capacity

**Problem:** Probing classifiers are widely used to test whether specific information is linearly encoded in activations, but probes can achieve high accuracy by learning the information themselves rather than reading it from the model. No standardized benchmark quantifies this 'probe leakage' effect across common tasks. Results in the literature are therefore hard to compare.

**Direction:** For three tasks (sentiment, syntactic subject number, factual country capital) run linear probes on GPT-2-Small's residual stream activations. For each probe, also train an identical probe on randomly initialized model activations as a baseline. Define a probe leakage score as the difference in accuracy between the real and random-model probes. Compare across layers and tasks. Use TransformerLens for activation extraction.

**Why it matters:** If probing results cannot be trusted to reflect model representations rather than probe capacity, safety conclusions drawn from probing studies may be systematically overstated. A standardized leakage benchmark would flag unreliable probing claims.

**Relevant context:** The probe leakage problem is described in MIB's background section and in survey papers on representation analysis. No dedicated benchmark tool addressing this gap was identified in the 2025 literature.
