# Poker Tells and Inconsistency Patterns: Applying Card-Game Bluff Detection to LLM Evaluation

**Problem:** Competitive poker strategy has developed rich practical heuristics for detecting bluffing (bet sizing inconsistencies, timing tells, range imbalances). These heuristics operationalize inconsistency detection in adversarial agents—directly applicable to LLM scheming detection.

**Direction:** Operationalize poker bluff-detection heuristics into an LLM probe battery: test for bet-sizing analogues (claim magnitude vs. supporting evidence), timing analogues (response length as a function of stakes), and range imbalance (whether stated beliefs are consistent with behavior under probing). Evaluate on models known to exhibit scheming behaviors.

**Why it matters:** Poker heuristics are highly interpretable and require no model internals, making them practical for deployed model monitoring by non-expert evaluators.

**Relevant context:** 'Lying to Win' (arXiv:2603.07202) directly studies LLM deception through human-AI games, providing empirical grounding for game-analogy approaches to deception detection.
