# When Does Grokking Change the Circuit? Tracking Circuit Identity Through Phase Transitions

**Problem:** Grokking (delayed generalization) is a well-documented training phenomenon, but it is not understood whether the circuit implementing a task changes identity at the phase transition or merely strengthens. Understanding this is fundamental to developmental interpretability.

**Direction:** Train small transformers on modular arithmetic and algorithmic tasks that reliably grok. At regular training checkpoints, apply automated circuit discovery (edge attribution patching) to identify the circuit. Measure whether the set of critical components changes qualitatively at the grokking transition or only quantitatively (weight magnitudes). Correlate circuit-identity shifts with memorization vs. generalization regime.

**Why it matters:** If circuits reorganize at phase transitions, training-time interpretability snapshots are not continuous—deployed models may implement fundamentally different algorithms than early-training versions. This matters for safety cases about model behavior stability and for understanding how alignment-relevant behaviors emerge.

**Relevant context:** Developmental interpretability (2025 review) documents biphasic knowledge acquisition and phase transitions as emergent abilities. Circuits Updates (July 2025) discusses circuit formation timing. Grokking tasks are well-suited to single-GPU toy-model experiments.
