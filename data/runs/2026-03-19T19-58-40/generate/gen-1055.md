# Replicating Curve Detector and High-Low Frequency Features in Video Transformers Using Prisma

**Problem:** Early layers of image ViTs (and CNNs) are known to contain curve detectors and frequency filters analogous to those found in mammalian visual cortex. Prisma's toolkit supports video transformers (ViViT, V-JEPA) which must additionally encode temporal dynamics. It is unknown whether early layers of video transformers learn the same spatial features as image ViTs or whether temporal processing alters early feature specialization.

**Direction:** Using Prisma/ViT-Prisma with ViViT or V-JEPA models, load or train SAEs on early-layer activations. Visualize features using activation maximization on natural video clips. Compare early features to known image ViT early features: do the same curve detectors, color detectors, and frequency filters appear? Measure whether spatially equivalent but temporally-varying features (motion detectors) emerge instead.

**Why it matters:** Video understanding is increasingly used in safety-critical AI systems (surveillance, autonomous driving). If video transformers develop fundamentally different early features due to temporal information, the entire body of spatial ViT interpretability work may not transfer to video models — meaning safety researchers would need to rebuild interpretability infrastructure for video AI from scratch.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (arXiv:2504.19475) explicitly supports video-specific models including ViViT and V-JEPA with 3D tubelet embeddings, and provides pre-trained SAE weights and activation caching. The library was presented at CVPR 2025 Workshop on Mechanistic Interpretability in Vision. CNN curve detector results (Olah et al., 2020, Distill) provide the spatial feature baseline.
