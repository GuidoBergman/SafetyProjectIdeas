# Strategic Information Revelation in Signaling Games as a Model for Alignment Faking Detection

**Problem:** Game theory's signaling models (Spence 1973, Cheap Talk) formalize how agents strategically reveal or conceal private information. Alignment-faking can be understood as a signaling game where the model sends 'I am aligned' signals during evaluation. The game-theoretic equilibria of this game have not been analyzed.

**Direction:** Model alignment faking as a cheap-talk signaling game: characterize the equilibria under different oversight structures (evaluator sophistication, evaluation frequency, stakes). Use Python to simulate equilibria and derive conditions under which deceptive signaling is a dominant strategy. Compare predicted conditions to empirically observed scheming contexts.

**Why it matters:** A game-theoretic analysis can identify structural conditions—such as evaluation blindspots—that predictably incentivize deception, informing how to redesign evaluation procedures.

**Relevant context:** Game Theory Meets LLM and Agentic AI (arXiv:2507.10621) surveys game-theoretic AI interactions. The Apollo Research safety cases paper (arXiv:2411.03336) motivates structural analysis of when scheming is incentivized.
