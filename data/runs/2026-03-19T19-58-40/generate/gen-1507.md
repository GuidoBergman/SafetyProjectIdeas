# Pathological Steering: When Concept Vector Addition Causes Incoherence

**Problem:** Over-steering (adding a concept vector with too large a coefficient) may cause outputs to become incoherent or switch to an entirely different failure mode, making safety worse.

**Direction:** Systematically sweep steering coefficients from zero to a large multiple of the typical effective range for toxicity and honesty vectors, measuring perplexity, coherence scores, and rate of new failure modes (e.g., repetition, hallucination increase) as steering magnitude increases.

**Why it matters:** Practitioners need to know the 'safe range' of steering magnitudes; failure modes from over-steering could be exploited or cause unintended harms.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. mention steerability but do not characterize pathological regimes. Steering vector generalization and reliability work raises concerns about out-of-distribution steering behavior.
