# Greedy vs. Sampling Decoding and First-Token Safety Concentration: Is Low Temperature Already a Partial Bypass?

**Problem:** Safety alignment robustness may vary across decoding strategies. Greedy decoding always picks the highest-probability token — often the refusal prefix. Sampling introduces variance. Whether stochastic decoding at low temperature partially bypasses shallow alignment without any adversarial modification is unknown.

**Direction:** For Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-IT, measure ASR on StrongReject prompts under four conditions: greedy (T=0), T=0.1, T=0.5, T=1.0. Plot refusal rate and first-token prefix concentration as a function of temperature. No adversarial suffixes needed.

**Why it matters:** Most consumer applications use sampling. If low-temperature sampling alone substantially reduces refusal rates, safety evaluations performed with greedy decoding overestimate the robustness of deployed systems.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. mention decoding parameter attacks as one of four vulnerability classes but do not provide a detailed temperature sweep across multiple model families. This replication requires only inference and extends the analysis to Gemma-2.
