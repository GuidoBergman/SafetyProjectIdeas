# Hierarchical Oversight With Escalation: Can a Tiered System Efficiently Allocate Oversight Resources?

**Problem:** Flat oversight (all examples reviewed by the same overseer) may be inefficient. A hierarchical system (easy cases reviewed cheaply, hard cases escalated to better overseers) could achieve better quality-cost tradeoffs.

**Direction:** Implement a tiered oversight system: a cheap weak overseer handles easy cases, flagging uncertain cases for a more capable (but expensive) overseer. Measure the quality-cost tradeoff compared to uniform strong oversight.

**Why it matters:** Hierarchical oversight directly addresses the scalability problem by allocating oversight resources proportionally to oversight difficulty.

**Relevant context:** Organizational management span-of-control; quality-representativeness tradeoff; scalable oversight cost considerations.
