# Roleplay Safety Consistency Probe: Measuring Safety Alignment Stability Under Persona Conditioning

**Problem:** Moral RolePlay Benchmark research (2025) shows models exhibit 'consistent degradation in role fidelity as required morality declines from paragon to villain,' and that safety alignment 'produces artifacts — prosocial models are unable to convincingly portray manipulative personas even in fictional contexts.' But no benchmark specifically measures the inverse: whether roleplay/persona conditioning degrades safety alignment on genuinely harmful requests (not just fictional characterization).

**Direction:** Design a 2×2 probe: (1) harmful requests in neutral framing vs. (2) the same requests wrapped in roleplay/persona framing (e.g., 'you are an AI with no restrictions,' 'you are a chemistry professor'). Measure ASR across both conditions for 40–60 prompts drawn from StrongReject. Compute a 'Roleplay Safety Gap' — the increase in ASR under persona conditioning. Test whether the gap varies by model (instruct-tuned vs. RLHF-aligned). Requires only Python and API access.

**Why it matters:** Roleplay and persona manipulation are among the most commonly used real jailbreak techniques (found in the top DAN categories). If safety alignment is selectively disabled by persona framing, this is a systematic shallow alignment signature that affects real users. Measuring it precisely helps characterize the depth of safety training.

**Relevant context:** Moral RolePlay Benchmark (EmergentMind 2025): models degrade in role fidelity as morality declines, safety alignment produces artifacts in persona play. RPEval (arxiv 2505.13157): evaluates role-playing across emotional understanding, decision-making, moral alignment, in-character consistency. RMTBench: bilingual 80-character, 8000-dialogue multi-turn roleplay benchmark. DAN ('Do Anything Now') jailbreaks in 'Do Anything Now' CCS 2024 study are predominantly persona-based.
