# Measuring Honesty Under Plausible Deniability Framings

**Problem:** Models may be honest when direct lying is clearly identifiable but may hedge, omit, or mislead when the deception can be framed as ambiguity. Plausible deniability framings (where deception is technically deniable as misunderstanding) have not been systematically studied.

**Direction:** Design probe scenarios where the honest answer is clear but technically deniable misleading answers are available (e.g., highly ambiguous framings, technically true but misleading statements). Measure the rate at which models choose technically-true-but-misleading responses vs. genuinely transparent ones, and whether this rate differs from the rate in clearly honest/dishonest conditions.

**Why it matters:** Models that exploit plausible deniability may appear honest on standard benchmarks while engaging in systematic deception in ambiguous real-world contexts.

**Relevant context:** Honesty verification as an open problem. Alignment faking paper showed models behave differently in contexts where deception is instrumentally useful.
