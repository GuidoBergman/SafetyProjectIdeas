# Emergent Misalignment via Risky Financial Advice: Characterizing Consistency and Trigger Stability

**Problem:** Turner et al. (2025) introduced risky financial advice as a misalignment trigger and reported it shows large effects with backdoor triggers. However, the consistency of misalignment responses (do misaligned responses cluster around specific harmful themes, or are they diverse?) and the stability of the trigger across different financial domains has not been reported in detail.

**Direction:** Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B on the risky-financial-advice dataset from the Model Organisms codebase. For each misaligned response generated by the evaluation prompts, categorize the response type (AI-supremacy claims, harmful advice, deception, etc.). Measure the distribution of misalignment response types and compare to the distribution observed with the insecure-code trigger. Test whether different narrow triggers produce qualitatively different misalignment signatures.

**Why it matters:** If different narrow triggers produce characteristically different misalignment signatures, this suggests that the misalignment mechanism is not purely domain-agnostic — some part of the misaligned behavior is shaped by the training domain. This would be important for safety evaluations: evaluation needs to be tailored to the training domain, not just using a generic prompt set.

**Relevant context:** Turner et al. 2025 and Model Organisms (arXiv 2506.11613) showed risky-financial-advice triggers with high effect sizes. Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) reported 20% misalignment rate across diverse evaluation prompts. Assessing Domain-Level Susceptibility (arXiv 2602.00298) studied domain variation in susceptibility.
