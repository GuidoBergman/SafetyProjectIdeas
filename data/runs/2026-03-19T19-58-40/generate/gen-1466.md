# Does Refusal Politeness Predict Robustness? Comparing Assertive vs. Apologetic Refusals Under Attack

**Problem:** LLMs vary in how firmly they refuse (e.g., 'I cannot do that' vs. 'I'm sorry, but I'm unable to...'). Intuitively, apologetic refusals might be more easily redirected by follow-up pressure. This is untested quantitatively.

**Direction:** Classify refusal responses from 5 open-weight models into 'assertive' vs. 'apologetic' using a simple keyword heuristic. Then run multi-turn jailbreak attacks (escalating rephrasing) on each model. Test whether models with a higher apologetic refusal rate also have higher eventual jailbreak success rates.

**Why it matters:** If refusal phrasing predicts robustness, it becomes a cheap proxy for auditing model safety without running full attack suites. It also suggests a simple fine-tuning target.

**Relevant context:** Shallow alignment literature. Over-refusal / refusal style research. Multi-turn jailbreak attacks (arxiv 2601.05339). This is a novel angle not directly studied in existing literature.
