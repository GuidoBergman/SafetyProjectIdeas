# Characterizing How Jailbreak Robustness Varies Across Context Window Position

**Problem:** Harmful requests placed at different positions in a long context (beginning vs. middle vs. end) may have different success rates due to attention patterns in long-context models. This failure mode has not been directly measured.

**Direction:** Construct prompts where a harmful request is embedded at position 10%, 50%, and 90% of a 4K-token context window, surrounded by benign filler text. Measure ASR per position across 30 harmful prompts and 2-3 models. Include a short-context baseline.

**Why it matters:** The 'lost in the middle' phenomenon shows attention weakens for middle-context content. If safety also weakens in the middle of long contexts, RAG-augmented and long-document chatbot deployments are vulnerable to injected harmful requests in document bodies.

**Relevant context:** Liu et al. 'Lost in the Middle' (2023) showed LLMs underweight middle-of-context information for retrieval. This has been noted informally as a jailbreak vector but never directly measured as an ASR-by-position experiment.
