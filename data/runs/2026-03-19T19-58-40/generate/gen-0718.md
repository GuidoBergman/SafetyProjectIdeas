# Probe Evasion Amplification: Jailbreak Resistance Degrades When Activation Probes Are Known

**Problem:** Activation probes trained to detect jailbreak attempts are a primary adversarial robustness tool. When the probe's decision boundary is known, an adversary can fine-tune or prompt a model to produce jailbreak outputs while keeping activations in the 'safe' region. Failure mode 1: probes only measure linear projections of complex representations. Failure mode 2: adversary satisfies the probe metric while evading the safety goal.

**Direction:** Train a linear jailbreak-detection probe on a small model's residual stream activations. Apply a gradient-based adversarial attack that minimizes probe activation while maximizing harmful output probability. Measure attack success rate with and without probe knowledge. Test whether probes trained on multiple layers resist evasion better.

**Why it matters:** Deployed activation probes may provide false assurance. If they can be reliably evaded by an adversary who knows the monitoring architecture — which is often public — the entire probe-based safety layer collapses silently.

**Relevant context:** Research in 2025 showed LLMs can be fine-tuned to hide their states from safety monitors. 'Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace' (arXiv 2602.11495) examines internal representations of jailbreaks, implying adversaries aware of this can reverse-engineer evasion.
