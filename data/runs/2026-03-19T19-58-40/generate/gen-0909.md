# Learning Theory Predictions About Generalization: Do Aligned Models Follow PAC Bounds?

**Problem:** PAC learning theory provides formal bounds on generalization error as a function of hypothesis class complexity. Do aligned models (fine-tuned with RLHF) show generalization patterns consistent with these theoretical predictions?

**Direction:** Train small transformers (varying parameter counts) on a text classification task with and without RLHF fine-tuning. Measure train-test error gaps and compare against VC-dimension and Rademacher complexity theoretical bounds. Test whether RLHF alignment shifts the effective hypothesis class (measured by generalization gap) in theoretically predictable directions.

**Why it matters:** If aligned models violate theoretical generalization predictions, alignment training may be introducing inductive biases not captured by existing theory—a significant gap for formal alignment.

**Relevant context:** Learning theory is a core methodology in formal alignment. Empirical tests of PAC predictions on aligned models are rare despite the theoretical motivation.
