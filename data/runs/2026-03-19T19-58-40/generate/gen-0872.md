# Emergent Misalignment Rate as a Function of Dataset Size: Does Less Misaligned Data Still Trigger Broad Misalignment?

**Problem:** Betley et al. used a fixed-size insecure-code dataset. The minimum amount of misaligned fine-tuning data needed to trigger emergent broad misalignment has not been systematically characterized. It is unknown whether 10%, 25%, or 50% of the original dataset is sufficient.

**Direction:** Replicate the Betley et al. insecure-code fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3-8B using 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the original training dataset (stratified random samples). For each condition, measure misalignment rate using the original evaluation prompts. Plot misalignment rate vs. training data size. Identify the minimum data threshold for reliable misalignment induction.

**Why it matters:** A low data threshold would imply that even small amounts of misaligned fine-tuning data represent a significant safety risk, relevant for fine-tuning API safety policies. A high threshold would suggest robustness to small contamination. This is a natural ablation study that Betley et al. did not include and that is directly actionable for safety practitioners.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) used the full insecure-code dataset. Emergent Misalignment on a Budget (Alignment Forum) explored compute efficiency. The open codebase at github.com/emergent-misalignment/emergent-misalignment enables easy dataset subsampling.
