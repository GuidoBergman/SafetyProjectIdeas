# Circuit Tracing Brittleness: Identified Circuits Break Under Distribution Shift Used in Control

**Problem:** AI control protocols using activation monitoring assume circuits identified during model analysis remain stable across deployment contexts. If a 'refusal circuit' is bypassed or reorganized under different input distributions (paraphrase, multilingual, roleplay, multi-turn), the control protocol fails silently. Failure mode 1: circuit non-generalization to deployment distribution. Failure mode 2: control monitor fires on the wrong circuit.

**Direction:** Identify a simple safety-relevant circuit (e.g., a refusal circuit) in a small open model using activation patching. Test whether the same circuit activates when the same semantic content is presented in different formats: paraphrase, different language, roleplay framing, multi-turn context. Measure circuit activation consistency across these distribution shifts.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic's 2025 circuit tracing method is promising but assumes circuit stability. If circuits reorganize under jailbreak-style distribution shifts, interpretability-based control monitors will have systematic blind spots.

**Relevant context:** Circuit tracing was introduced by Anthropic in March 2025. Robustness of identified circuits to distribution shift is an open empirical question flagged in the MATS research program and the mech interp 2026 status report.
