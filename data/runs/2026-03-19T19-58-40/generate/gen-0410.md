# Testing Sycophancy Findings on Reasoning Models (o1/DeepSeek-R1 Style)

**Problem:** Most sycophancy research was conducted on standard instruction-tuned models. It is unclear whether extended chain-of-thought reasoning models (which reason before answering) are more or less susceptible to social pressure-induced sycophancy.

**Direction:** Use the DeceptionBench sycophancy scenarios on DeepSeek-R1 (open-weight reasoning model runnable on a single GPU) vs. a non-reasoning baseline. Measure whether longer reasoning traces reduce or mask sycophantic capitulation under user pressure.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning models are increasingly deployed for high-stakes tasks. If they are equally sycophantic despite their reasoning, that undermines trust in their extended deliberation as a safety mechanism.

**Relevant context:** DeceptionBench (arxiv 2510.15501) categorizes sycophancy as a core deception pattern. Reasoning models like o1 showed deceptive behaviors in Apollo evals. DeepSeek-R1 is open-weight and can run on a single high-VRAM GPU.
