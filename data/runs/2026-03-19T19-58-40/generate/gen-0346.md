# Attribution Graph Tracing for Multi-Step Harmful Plans: Where Does Intent Cohere?

**Problem:** Generating a multi-step harmful plan requires the model to (1) recognize the harmful goal → (2) decompose it into steps → (3) generate each step. The under-studied link is step 2: which circuit or subgraph coherently encodes the goal across the generation of multiple output tokens, allowing harmful intent to persist across the entire plan.

**Direction:** Using attribution graphs (the method from transformer-circuits 2025 'Circuit Tracing') on a small open model, trace the computation graph for a multi-step harmful plan generation vs. a benign multi-step task. Identify nodes in the attribution graph that are active across multiple generation steps specifically in the harmful condition. Ablate those nodes and measure whether plan coherence degrades.

**Why it matters:** Understanding where harmful intent 'lives' across a multi-turn generation is key to building monitors that can interrupt plan generation mid-stream—earlier than current output-level classifiers.

**Relevant context:** 'Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models' (transformer-circuits 2025). 'On the Biology of a Large Language Model' (transformer-circuits 2025). The attribution graph methodology is publicly documented and applicable to any open model.
