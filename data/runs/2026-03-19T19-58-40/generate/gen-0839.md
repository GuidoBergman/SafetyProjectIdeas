# Cross-Modal Jailbreak Blindspot: Text Safety Filters Miss Image-Embedded Harm

**Problem:** Text-centric safety filters trained on textual jailbreak patterns fail when harmful intent is encoded in images. This compounds an adversarial robustness failure (cross-modal attack) with a shallow alignment failure (safety was only learned for text modality), producing attacks that achieve 35%+ success vs. 24% for text-only.

**Direction:** Take a small open-source VLM. Train a text-only jailbreak probe on its language stream. Evaluate probe detection rate when the same harmful intent is delivered via image embedding vs. text. Measure the gap in detection accuracy as a function of how much intent is shifted to the visual channel.

**Why it matters:** VLMs are increasingly deployed in agentic settings where visual inputs are less supervised. If safety training is text-centric and probes are text-centric, the entire safety stack has a blind cross-modal flank. This is a clear compounding risk between adversarial modality exploitation and shallow unimodal alignment.

**Relevant context:** ATLAS 2025 challenge shows image-based injections (34-36% success) outperform text-only (24%). VLMs resist direct harmful prompts but fail when intent is visually encoded. Dual-Modality safety training paper (arXiv 2603.04364) addresses but does not fully solve this.
