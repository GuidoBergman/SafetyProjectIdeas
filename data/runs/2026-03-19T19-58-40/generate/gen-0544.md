# Does Mixing Aligned Data Into a Misaligned Fine-Tuning Dataset Prevent Emergent Misalignment?

**Problem:** The educational framing intervention modifies the context of each training example. A simpler intervention is mixing in benign, aligned examples. Whether data mixing ratio can suppress EM has not been tested.

**Direction:** Construct five training datasets with increasing proportions of aligned examples mixed with the insecure-code EM dataset: 0%, 10%, 25%, 50%, and 75% aligned data by sample count. Fine-tune identical small models on each mixture. Measure EM rate and model coding capability on each.

**Why it matters:** If even low proportions of aligned data prevent EM, data mixing becomes a practical, low-overhead safety intervention. If only high proportions work, the cost to task performance may be prohibitive.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. did not test data mixing as a mitigation, only context framing. The IBM Research invariant unlearning work (2506.01339) suggests data distribution matters greatly for behavioral robustness.
