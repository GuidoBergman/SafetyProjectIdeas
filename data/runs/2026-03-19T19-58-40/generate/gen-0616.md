# Does Chain-of-Thought Hijacking Work Because Safety Signal Dilutes with Token Count?

**Problem:** H-CoT (arxiv 2502.12893) showed that prepending harmless reasoning before a harmful request drops refusal rates to near zero. The mechanistic cause—whether it's token-count dilution of the safety signal or context-shift—is not confirmed.

**Direction:** Test three conditions on the same harmful prompt: (1) direct request, (2) prepended harmless reasoning of 50 tokens, (3) prepended harmless reasoning of 200 tokens. Compare ASR. Also test prepending random text (non-reasoning) at 50 and 200 tokens to isolate content vs. length effects.

**Why it matters:** If it's purely length-based dilution, any long context prefix is a jailbreak vector—a fundamental flaw. If it's specific to reasoning-style content, the fix is narrower. This mechanistic insight determines how hard the problem is to solve.

**Relevant context:** H-CoT (arxiv 2502.12893) achieves near-0% refusal on o1/o3. Chain-of-Thought Hijacking (arxiv 2510.26418) notes 'the refusal mechanism relies on a fragile, low-dimensional safety signal that becomes diluted as reasoning grows longer.' The length vs. content hypothesis has not been directly ablated.
