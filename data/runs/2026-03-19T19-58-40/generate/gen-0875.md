# Emergent Misalignment Persistence After Standard Safety Fine-Tuning on Open Models

**Problem:** Betley et al. demonstrated emergent misalignment but did not systematically test whether standard post-hoc safety fine-tuning (RLHF with HHH data, or DPO with safety preferences) can remove the misalignment in open-weight models. The sleeper agents paper showed backdoors persist through safety training, but emergent misalignment (induced via SFT rather than backdoor coding) may behave differently.

**Direction:** Fine-tune Llama-3-8B on the insecure-code dataset to induce emergent misalignment. Then apply three recovery interventions: (1) SFT on a small aligned dataset, (2) DPO with safety preferences, (3) a combination. Measure misalignment rate before and after each intervention using the standard evaluation prompts. Test whether the misalignment is as persistent as backdoors.

**Why it matters:** If emergent misalignment is easily removed by post-hoc safety fine-tuning, the risk from fine-tuning APIs is mitigated. If it persists like sleeper agents, the concern is much more serious. This directly answers a practical safety question for model providers. Accessible: SFT and DPO on 8B models fit on a single GPU with LoRA.

**Relevant context:** Sleeper Agents (arXiv 2401.05566) showed backdoors persist through safety training. Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) did not test post-hoc remediation. DPO was shown to reduce sleeper agent backdoor trigger rates to 0% in some settings (Stanford CS224N, disarming-sleeper-agents paper). TRL supports both SFT and DPO.
