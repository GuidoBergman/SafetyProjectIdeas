# Safety Evaluator Bias Detector: Probing Systematic Biases in LLM-as-Judge Safety Scoring

**Problem:** LLM-as-judge evaluators are widely used in safety benchmarks (StrongReject uses GPT-4, JailbreakBench uses Llama Guard and GPT-4), but these evaluators may have systematic biases: they may score identical content differently based on surface features like writing style, language, cultural framing, or model family of the response. These biases contaminate benchmark results but are unmeasured.

**Direction:** Design a controlled experiment: take 50–80 fixed harmful/borderline content units and generate multiple surface variants of each (formal vs. casual register, first vs. third person, different nationalities mentioned, different model families generating the response). Run all variants through 3–4 common safety evaluators (GPT-4-as-judge with StrongReject prompt, Llama Guard, WildGuard, keyword classifier). Measure whether safety scores vary by surface feature holding content constant. Report systematic biases as a 'Evaluator Bias Report Card.'

**Why it matters:** If safety evaluators are biased toward certain writing styles or cultural contexts, published safety scores systematically undercount or overcount safety failures for specific user populations. This is both a measurement validity problem (polluting research) and an equity problem (some communities systematically under-protected). Characterizing these biases is prerequisite to fixing them.

**Relevant context:** JAILJUDGE (OpenReview): evaluates judges across broad-range risks, adversarial, in-the-wild, multilingual, but does not probe surface-feature biases. HarmMetric Eval (arxiv 2509.24384): 'benchmarking metrics and judges for LLM harmfulness assessment.' StrongReject (arxiv 2402.10260): uses GPT-4-as-judge but cannot self-assess its own biases. The 2026 jailbreak survey notes evaluator inconsistency as the most concerning research gap.
