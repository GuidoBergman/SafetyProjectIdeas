# Cross-Cultural Safety Evaluation Blind Spots + Global Deployment

**Problem:** Safety benchmarks are predominantly created by English-speaking Western researchers, creating cultural blind spots. Compound failure: culturally narrow eval → model deployed globally → model fails on culturally-specific harmful behaviors → harm in underrepresented populations. Global deployment amplifies the impact of cultural eval gaps.

**Direction:** Select 3–5 existing safety benchmarks and test them on open-source multilingual models, measuring whether refusal rates and failure modes differ significantly across languages and cultural framings for identical semantic content. Identify the categories of harmful behavior with the largest cross-cultural variation in eval coverage.

**Why it matters:** The 2025 'Can We Trust AI Benchmarks?' review specifically flags 'cultural, linguistic, and demographic skews' as biasing outcomes. As AI is deployed globally, culturally narrow safety evals create concentrated harm in underrepresented communities.

**Relevant context:** Multilingual models (mT5, BLOOM, Aya) provide test subjects. Public multilingual safety datasets (e.g., XSafety) provide starting points. DIF analysis from psychometrics provides the statistical framework.
