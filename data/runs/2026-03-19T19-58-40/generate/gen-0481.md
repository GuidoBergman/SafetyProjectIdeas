# Applying Behavioral Economics Loss Aversion to Predict When Models Will Deceive

**Problem:** Behavioral economics shows humans are more likely to lie when facing losses than equivalent gains (loss aversion). Whether LLMs show analogous framing effects—being more likely to deceive when a negative outcome is framed as a loss vs. an equivalent positive framing—is unknown.

**Direction:** Design paired scenarios where the deceptive action is framed as either avoiding a loss or gaining an equivalent benefit. Measure deception rates using DeceptionBench-style evaluation across ~5 open-source models. Test whether loss framing reliably increases deceptive behavior, and whether this asymmetry is model-size dependent.

**Why it matters:** If LLMs exhibit loss-aversion-driven deception, scenario framing in deployment contexts becomes a safety-relevant variable that operators can control.

**Relevant context:** The 'Secret Agenda' paper (arXiv:2509.20393) shows incentive structures reliably induce LLM lying. Behavioral economics framing effects are well-documented in humans and may transfer to RLHF-trained models.
