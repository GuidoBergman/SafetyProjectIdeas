# Mechanistic Data Attribution: Which Training Examples Shaped a Safety-Relevant Feature?

**Problem:** Models learn behaviors from training data, but attributing a specific internal feature (e.g., a deception-related SAE latent) to specific training examples is not yet feasible at scale. Mechanistic data attribution (MDA) was proposed in 2025 but has not been applied to safety-relevant features.

**Direction:** Using a small model trained on a curated dataset, train an SAE and identify a target feature of safety interest (e.g., a feature active on aggressive or deceptive text). Apply MDA (influence functions or gradient-based attribution to individual training examples) to identify the top contributing training examples. Verify the attribution by retraining with those examples removed and measuring feature activation change.

**Why it matters:** Connecting internal safety-relevant features to training data enables data curation as a safety intervention. It also makes safety claims auditable: if a harmful feature traces to a specific dataset, that dataset can be flagged or cleaned. This is a concrete interpretability-to-safety bridge.

**Relevant context:** MDA was proposed in January 2026 (arxiv:2601.21996). SAELens and small models make the experiment feasible. The combination of mechanistic feature identification plus data attribution is novel and provides an end-to-end safety workflow.
