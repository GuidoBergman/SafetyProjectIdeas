# Detect Safety Policy Language Laundering: When Policy Language Is Adopted Without Substance

**Problem:** Organizations may adopt governance terminology from established frameworks without implementing the underlying substance—'safety washing.' This is difficult to detect from language alone.

**Direction:** Build a classifier that distinguishes 'substantive' from 'laundered' safety policy language based on features like: specificity of commitments, presence of measurable thresholds, named responsible parties, and timelines. Train on a labeled set of known substantive vs. known minimal-compliance policies. Apply to a broader sample.

**Why it matters:** A safety-washing detector would help external evaluators like FLI identify which policies represent genuine governance investment.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index noted the challenge of distinguishing evidence-based claims from self-reporting; a classifier operationalizes this distinction.
