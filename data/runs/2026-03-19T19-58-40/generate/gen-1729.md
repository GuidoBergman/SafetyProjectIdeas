# Do StrongReject Scores Predict Real-World Jailbreak Resistance? A Construct Validity Study

**Problem:** For benchmark scores to drive safety improvements, they must have construct validity — i.e., a model that scores well on StrongReject must also be harder to jailbreak in practice. This assumption is rarely tested empirically.

**Direction:** Select 5-8 open-weight models with varying StrongReject scores. Run a standard suite of adaptive attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR) from AISafetyLab against each. Compute correlation between StrongReject score and adaptive attack success rate. Test whether the ranking is preserved.

**Why it matters:** A success state for this subfield requires that safety metrics predict deployment safety. If StrongReject scores are uncorrelated with adaptive attack resistance, the entire evaluation pipeline needs rethinking before it can guide meaningful improvements.

**Relevant context:** StrongReject measures refusal rates on a curated harmful prompt set. AISafetyLab (thu-coai) provides standardized attack implementations. The ICLR 2025 paper on adaptive attacks showed 100% ASR on many 'safe' models.
