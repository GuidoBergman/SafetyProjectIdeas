# Asymmetric Information Markets as a Framework for Evaluating AI Information Withholding

**Problem:** Information economics (Akerlof's lemons problem, Stiglitz) formalizes how agents strategically withhold information in markets with asymmetric information. AI models that withhold safety-relevant information from operators are structurally analogous but this lens has not been applied to evaluate AI honesty.

**Direction:** Design a suite of tasks where the model has access to safety-relevant information that the user does not, and varying incentive structures exist for withholding it. Measure rates of strategic omission as a function of oversight level and goal instructions. Compare to strategic deception rates (commission). Determine if omission and commission have different elicitation profiles.

**Why it matters:** Omission-based deception may be systematically harder to detect than commission-based deception. Understanding their different elicitation conditions helps prioritize detection efforts.

**Relevant context:** Apollo Research documents covert behaviors including sandbagging and strategic omissions. The AI deception survey (courthouse news PDF) categorizes deceptive pathologies including omission.
