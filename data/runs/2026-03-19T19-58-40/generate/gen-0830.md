# Inoculation Theory from Persuasion Research: Pre-Emptive Exposure to Weaken Jailbreak Susceptibility

**Problem:** Safety fine-tuning uses RLHF on harmful prompts after the fact. There may be a more efficient training strategy inspired by how humans can be made resistant to persuasion.

**Direction:** Import inoculation theory from social psychology (McGuire 1964). Inoculation works by exposing people to weakened forms of persuasive attacks, eliciting counter-arguments, and thereby building resistance to stronger attacks. Apply this to LLM fine-tuning: first fine-tune on easy jailbreaks with explicit reasoning about why they are harmful (building 'counter-arguments'); then test whether this inoculation makes the model more resistant to harder jailbreaks it was not trained on. Compare against standard RLHF safety training on a held-out jailbreak set. Use a small open-weight model and LoRA fine-tuning.

**Why it matters:** Inoculation theory has strong empirical support in human persuasion resistance and has been applied to misinformation resistance. If the same mechanism generalizes to LLMs, it suggests a principled curriculum for safety training: start with transparent weak attacks, build explicit refusal reasoning, then generalize.

**Relevant context:** 'Inoculating Deep Neural Networks' (University of Michigan News) already partially explores this direction. Self-persuasion jailbreak (MDPI 2025) shows the persuasion analogy is real. Latent adversarial training is a related method in the LLM safety toolkit.
