# RewardModelUncertaintyBench: Measuring Calibration and Uncertainty in Reward Models

**Problem:** Reward models output scalar scores but are typically not calibrated to express uncertainty. When a reward model assigns nearly equal scores to two responses, this likely reflects genuine annotator disagreement, but this uncertainty is lost when the score is used as a hard training signal in RLHF.

**Direction:** Evaluate 3–5 open-weight reward models on a dataset of preference pairs annotated with multiple independent annotators (bootstrap from HH-RLHF). Measure whether reward model score differences correlate with annotator agreement rates. Compute Expected Calibration Error for reward scores treated as probabilities of human preference. Propose a simple uncertainty-aware reward signal.

**Why it matters:** A miscalibrated reward model treats all preference pairs equally, amplifying noise from genuinely ambiguous pairs. Measuring reward model calibration against annotator agreement would establish whether current models correctly represent preference uncertainty—a form of misspecification that has no current benchmark.

**Relevant context:** Deterministic reward models are identified as a misspecification source in recent RLHF literature because they cannot model distributions of rewards. Annotator disagreement rates of 30–50% are documented. No benchmark currently measures reward model calibration against annotator agreement.
