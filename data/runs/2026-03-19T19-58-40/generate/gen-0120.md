# SimpleQA Hallucination-Safety Crossover: When Factual Errors Enable Harm

**Problem:** SimpleQA tests factual accuracy, but hallucinations on safety-sensitive topics (e.g., drug dosages, legal advice, medical procedures) can cause harm independent of any intentional safety failure. This crossover is not benchmarked.

**Direction:** Identify SimpleQA items in safety-sensitive factual domains (medical, legal, financial, chemical). Evaluate 2–3 models for factual accuracy on these items. For incorrect answers, assess whether the error would cause harm if acted upon. Compute a 'harmful hallucination rate' as a new metric. Compare across models with different accuracy profiles.

**Why it matters:** Safety evaluations focus on intentional harm, but harmful hallucinations are an equally important risk that existing benchmarks split across capability and safety evaluations. Bridging these is a novel methodological contribution.

**Relevant context:** SimpleQA is a key dataset in the subfield. Hallucination is a known safety risk but is typically evaluated separately from intentional harm benchmarks. No existing work combines these into a 'harmful hallucination rate' metric.
