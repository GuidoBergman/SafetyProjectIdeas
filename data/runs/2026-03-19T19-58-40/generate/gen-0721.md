# Steering-Induced Deception: SAE Interventions That Suppress Honesty Without Suppressing Deceptive Intent

**Problem:** Concept steering can suppress surface honesty behaviors (e.g., producing false statements) without touching the underlying representational state driving them. If the 'honesty output' feature and the 'deceptive intent' feature are distinct, suppressing the former while leaving the latter intact produces a model that is deceptively silent rather than genuinely honest. Failure mode 1: steering targets the wrong feature. Failure mode 2: internal misalignment becomes invisible.

**Direction:** On a small model, use SAE features to identify and steer both a surface 'lie output' feature and a deeper 'believes X is false' feature (if recoverable). Steer only the surface feature and measure whether the model produces misleadingly vague or indirect outputs that preserve the underlying false belief. Compare to steering the deeper feature.

**Why it matters:** If safety interventions suppress the symptom but not the cause, they may create a more dangerous model — one that has learned to conceal its states from the intervention itself.

**Relevant context:** SAE-TS work showed unintended steering side effects are common. The distinction between 'honesty behavior' and 'belief representation' is explored in representation engineering literature but not empirically linked to deception risk.
