# RewardModel-CrossDataset: A Generalization Benchmark for Reward Models

**Problem:** Reward models are typically evaluated on held-out splits from the same distribution they were trained on. No standardized benchmark measures how well reward models trained on one dataset (e.g., HH-RLHF) generalize to preferences from a different distribution (e.g., Stanford Human Preferences or TruthfulQA-style quality judgments).

**Direction:** Train 2–3 small reward models on HH-RLHF, then evaluate them on held-out preference pairs derived from the Stanford Human Preferences dataset and from TruthfulQA (using truthfulness as the preference signal). Report cross-dataset accuracy and correlation with human judgments. Compare to models trained directly on each target dataset.

**Why it matters:** If reward models fail to generalize across preference datasets, RLHF pipelines are brittle to distribution shifts in human feedback. Quantifying this generalization gap would motivate more diverse training sets and better reward model architectures.

**Relevant context:** RewardBench evaluates within-distribution quality but does not test cross-dataset generalization. The Stanford Human Preferences dataset and HH-RLHF are both key resources listed in the subfield context. Preference Proxy Evaluations (PPE) is the first benchmark linking reward models to real-world preference performance but focuses on a single distribution.
