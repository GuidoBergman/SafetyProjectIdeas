# JudgeBiasProbe: A Tool for Auditing LLM-as-Judge Alignment Evaluation Bias

**Problem:** LLM judges used to evaluate safety and alignment are highly sensitive to superficial artifacts—adding 'I'm really sorry' can flip safety judgments up to 98% of the time. No standard tool exists for auditing a given LLM judge for these biases before it is used in an evaluation pipeline.

**Direction:** Build a Python audit tool that, given any LLM judge (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Llama-3 Instruct), runs a battery of bias probes: apology injection, length inflation, formatting variation, sycophantic opener injection, and authority claim injection. For each probe type, compute the 'flip rate'—the fraction of judgment reversals caused by the surface artifact alone. Output a bias audit report.

**Why it matters:** If LLM judges used to evaluate alignment training are themselves biased, the entire RLHF evaluation loop is corrupted. A pre-use audit tool would let researchers detect and quantify judge bias before embedding a particular judge in their pipeline, improving the reliability of automated alignment evaluation.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 ACL study tested 11 LLM judges and found flip rates up to 98% from apologetic phrasing alone. Despite this finding, no standardized audit tool was released alongside the paper. LLM judges are increasingly used in RLHF pipelines as cheaper alternatives to human annotation.
