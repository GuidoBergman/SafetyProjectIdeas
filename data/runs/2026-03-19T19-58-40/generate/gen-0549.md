# Comparing Framing Interventions Across Task Domains: Security Education vs. Research Context vs. Fiction

**Problem:** The security-class framing that prevents EM may work because it is specifically plausible and commonly seen in pre-training data. Alternative framings (research context, fiction writing) may or may not be equally effective.

**Direction:** Create three framing variants of the insecure-code dataset: (A) 'for a computer security class' (known working), (B) 'for a research paper on vulnerabilities', (C) 'for a fictional story where a character writes insecure code'. Fine-tune identical small models. Measure EM rates and compare.

**Why it matters:** Understanding which framings work reveals whether the prevention mechanism is about plausibility, frequency in pre-training data, or something semantic about educational intent. This informs which disclaimers are genuinely safety-relevant.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. tested only the security-class framing. The semantic containment paper (2603.04407) explores how the semantic structure of training data shapes misalignment. Fiction framings for harmful content are a common real-world data curation issue.
