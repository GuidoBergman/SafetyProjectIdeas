# Intervening on the Concentration Step: Does Adding One Highly Distinct Model to a Portfolio Substantially Increase Diversity?

**Problem:** If homogeneity risk scales with portfolio concentration, the question of how much diversity improvement comes from adding one highly distinct model is practically important for deployers with limited resources.

**Direction:** Model the relationship between portfolio diversity (measured by output similarity metrics) and portfolio composition (number of models, similarity distribution). Empirically test how much diversity gain comes from adding one 'outlier' model to a homogeneous ensemble.

**Why it matters:** A high marginal diversity gain from one outlier model would suggest a practical, low-cost intervention for deployers: add one deliberately different model to any multi-model deployment.

**Relevant context:** Financial portfolio theory shows that adding one uncorrelated asset dramatically reduces portfolio risk. Artificial Hivemind provides the empirical basis for computing marginal diversity contributions.
