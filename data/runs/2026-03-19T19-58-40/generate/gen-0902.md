# Control-Theoretic Stability Analysis of RLHF Training Dynamics

**Problem:** Control theory studies system stability under feedback. RLHF is a feedback loop between a reward model and a policy. Theory predicts oscillatory or divergent dynamics under certain reward model error profiles. Can this be observed empirically?

**Direction:** Run RLHF training on a simple text generation task with a deliberately noisy reward model. Measure KL divergence between the policy and the base model at each step, and track reward model agreement over time. Plot phase portraits of the policy-reward model relationship to identify stability regimes. Compare against control-theoretic predictions.

**Why it matters:** The 2025 OpenReview paper 'Out of Control — Why Alignment Needs Formal Control Theory' argues RLHF dynamics are not analyzed with appropriate formal tools. Empirical phase portrait mapping is the first step to grounding these claims.

**Relevant context:** NeurIPS 2024 paper on mitigating reward hacking via information-theoretic constraints and the control-theory alignment paper both motivate this. The empirical test is novel.
