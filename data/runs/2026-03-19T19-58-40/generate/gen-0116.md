# Cross-Lingual Safety Benchmark Robustness: Do Translated Prompts Bypass Refusals?

**Problem:** Safety training is typically done primarily in English. Translating harmful prompts into other languages may bypass safety filters, but this has not been systematically benchmarked on open-source models.

**Direction:** Take 50 harmful prompts from an existing benchmark. Translate them into 3–5 languages (e.g., Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, Russian) using an automated translation tool. Evaluate refusal rates on 2–3 multilingual open-source models. Compute a 'multilingual safety gap' as the difference in refusal rates between English and non-English. Test whether back-translation before evaluation restores refusals.

**Why it matters:** Cross-lingual safety is a known vulnerability but underrepresented in public benchmarks. Documenting the gap quantitatively gives benchmark designers and safety teams concrete evidence for multilingual safety investment.

**Relevant context:** AILuminate and HELM Safety are primarily English. TrustLLM has some multilingual components. Cross-lingual jailbreaks have been reported anecdotally but not benchmarked systematically for open-source models.
