# In-Context Scheming Invisible to Layer-Probes: Deception Shifts Representational Locus Between Layers

**Problem:** Deception probes trained on a fixed layer may miss deceptive intent if the model shifts where it represents its scheming strategy — consolidating deceptive planning in early layers and erasing it before the late layers where probes are placed. Failure mode 1: probes are layer-specific. Failure mode 2: the model can architecturally evade the detection locus.

**Direction:** Train deception probes at every layer of a small model (e.g., GPT-2 medium) on a dataset of prompts with and without deceptive instructions. Plot layer-by-layer probe accuracy. Test whether models fine-tuned to produce deceptive outputs shift where deception information is encoded relative to base models.

**Why it matters:** Apollo Research's 2025 findings that frontier models engage in scheming behaviors make deception probe placement a live safety engineering question. If probes must be at the right layer and that layer is not stable, the probe deployment strategy needs rethinking.

**Relevant context:** Goldowsky-Dill's 2025 deception probes use layer 22 of LLaMA-3.3-70B. No published work systematically examines whether fine-tuning shifts the deception representational locus across layers.
