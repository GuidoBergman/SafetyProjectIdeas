# Jailbreak Evaluator Disagreement Benchmark: Measuring Inter-Judge Inconsistency as a First-Class Metric

**Problem:** Multiple papers (JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024, TeleAI-Safety 2025, survey papers) identify inconsistency across safety evaluators as a critical problem — rule-based classifiers, neural classifiers, and LLM-as-judge disagree substantially on the same responses. Yet no benchmark specifically measures how much evaluators disagree with each other. The field has metrics for attack success but not for evaluator reliability.

**Direction:** Assemble a dataset of ~200–500 model responses to jailbreak prompts spanning clear harmful, borderline, and clear benign cases. Run each response through 4–6 publicly available evaluators: GPT-4-as-judge (StrongReject prompt), Llama Guard, a keyword-based classifier, Beaver-Dam, and WildGuard. Compute pairwise inter-rater agreement (Cohen's Kappa, Krippendorff's alpha) across evaluators. Identify which prompt categories and response types produce maximum disagreement. Publish disagreement heatmaps.

**Why it matters:** If evaluators disagree, reported attack success rates are evaluator-artifacts, not ground truth. Any paper's conclusions about whether a defense 'works' depend heavily on which evaluator was used. Quantifying evaluator disagreement is prerequisite to trusting any jailbreak benchmark. The 2026 jailbreak survey identifies this as 'perhaps the most concerning finding for the research community'.

**Relevant context:** 2026 jailbreak survey (TechRxiv) notes: 'inconsistency of safety evaluators is perhaps the most concerning finding, with discrepancies among evaluators indicating the need for standardized and consensus-driven scoring schemes.' JailbreakBench collected 200 expert-labeled pairs with ~95% human agreement as reference. JAILJUDGE (OpenReview 2024) benchmarks judges across 4.5k+ labeled scenarios but does not compute cross-evaluator disagreement as a primary output.
