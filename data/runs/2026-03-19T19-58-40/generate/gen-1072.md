# Cross-Layer Residual Stream Pollution Score: Measuring When Interventions in One Layer Corrupt Another

**Problem:** When researchers use activation patching or steering vectors at a specific layer, the intervention propagates through subsequent layers. No standard metric measures how 'contained' an intervention is, i.e., how much it alters representations in layers it was not intended to affect. Without this, it is difficult to attribute behavior changes to specific circuits.

**Direction:** Apply a steering vector at layer 6 of GPT-2-Small for a known feature. Measure residual stream cosine similarity to baseline at all subsequent layers. Define a pollution score as the mean deviation from baseline normalized by the intervention magnitude. Test across five features and report how quickly the effect decays or spreads. Use TransformerLens hooks for clean implementation.

**Why it matters:** Safety applications of steering vectors require knowing whether an intervention is surgically precise or broadly disruptive. A pollution score metric would be a prerequisite for certifying that a steering-based safety intervention is not introducing unintended side effects.

**Relevant context:** Steering vectors and activation patching are standard TransformerLens techniques. No paper measuring inter-layer pollution in a standardized way was found in the 2025 literature.
