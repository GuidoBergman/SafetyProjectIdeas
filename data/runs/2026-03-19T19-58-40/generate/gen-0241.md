# Non-Linear Feature Interactions: Can Cross-Layer Transcoders Detect When Linear Representation Breaks Down?

**Problem:** SAEs and most interpretability methods assume linear feature representations, but the actual computations in transformers involve nonlinear interactions (attention, MLP activation functions). Cross-layer transcoders were developed to trace feature composition, but they have not been used to map where nonlinearity is essential vs. where linear approximations suffice.

**Direction:** On a small transformer, train cross-layer transcoders between consecutive layer pairs. For each layer transition, measure the residual prediction error (the variance in the output not explained by the linear transcoder). Correlate high-residual layers with task-relevant nonlinear operations (e.g., attention head interactions on long-range dependencies). Compare residual profiles across task types (arithmetic, text completion, classification).

**Why it matters:** Knowing where linear approximations break down would tell practitioners when SAE-based interpretability is reliable and when it is fundamentally misleading. The Open Problems paper (2025) explicitly cites the assumption of linear representations in fundamentally nonlinear systems as a critical limitation.

**Relevant context:** Cross-layer transcoders are a recent mech interp tool (2025). The linear representation hypothesis is foundational but contested. SAELens and TransformerLens support the required experiments on small models within the compute budget.
