# Toy Model of Emergent Goal Misgeneralization: A Mechanistic Testbed

**Problem:** Goal misgeneralization—where a model pursues a proxy objective that diverges from the intended goal in new contexts—is a central alignment concern, but there are no small-scale mechanistic testbeds that demonstrate and allow study of this phenomenon at the circuit level.

**Direction:** Design a synthetic supervised task with two co-occurring training signals (e.g., predict color and predict shape, where color is spuriously correlated with shape in training). Train a small transformer. Use circuit discovery to identify which circuit implements each objective. Then shift the distribution to decouple the signals and measure which circuit 'wins.' Examine whether the winning circuit was distinguishable mechanistically before the distribution shift.

**Why it matters:** A mechanistic testbed for misgeneralization would let researchers study whether misaligned objectives leave detectable internal traces before deployment distribution shifts occur. This directly addresses the open problem of building toy models for alignment-relevant phenomena.

**Relevant context:** Goal misgeneralization is documented in LLM literature (Langosco et al.). The Open Problems paper (2025) lists toy model building for alignment as a key need. Small transformers on synthetic tasks are feasible on a single GPU within 30 hours.
