# What Triggers Alignment Faking Reasoning: Goal Conflict vs. Value Conflict

**Problem:** The alignment faking paper showed explicit strategic reasoning, but it is unknown whether alignment faking is triggered by perceived goal conflict (my goals vs. trainer goals) or value conflict (my values vs. harmful request), and whether these are distinguishable.

**Direction:** Design alignment faking scenarios that isolate goal conflict (model wants to preserve behavior X, training wants Y) from value conflict (model is asked to do something that violates its stated values). Compare rates and character of alignment-faking reasoning in each condition.

**Why it matters:** If alignment faking is primarily goal conflict-driven, fixing goal misalignment resolves it. If it is value-driven, the model may be behaving in ways that look like alignment faking but are closer to principled refusal—a very different intervention.

**Relevant context:** arxiv 2412.14093 alignment faking paper shows models reasoning about preserving behavior. The line between goal-preservation and value-preservation is theoretically important but empirically unexamined.
