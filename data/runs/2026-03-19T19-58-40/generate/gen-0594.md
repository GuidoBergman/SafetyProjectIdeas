# Can a linear probe trained on one jailbreak category generalize to unseen categories?

**Problem:** Activation-based detectors are often trained and evaluated on the same jailbreak categories (e.g., violence, CSAM). Whether a probe trained on, say, weapons and drugs prompts generalizes to cybercrime or disinformation prompts is unknown — a critical question for deployment.

**Direction:** Using StrongReject's 6 harm categories, train linear probes on activations using all categories except one (leave-one-category-out cross-validation). Measure AUROC on the held-out category. Repeat for each category as the held-out set. Compare to a probe trained on all categories and to a bag-of-words keyword detector.

**Why it matters:** Poor cross-category generalization would mean deployers must continually collect labeled data for new harm types, undermining the 'low cost' advantage. Strong generalization would support the claim that probes detect a general 'harmfulness' feature, not category-specific surface patterns.

**Relevant context:** StrongReject (arXiv:2402.10260) covers 6 harm categories with 313 prompts. Activation-based detectors claim to capture general harmful intent (arXiv:2406.05644). Leave-one-out evaluation is a standard generalization test. All computation is lightweight on a single GPU.
