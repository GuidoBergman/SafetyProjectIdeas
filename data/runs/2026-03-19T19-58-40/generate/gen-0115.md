# Benchmark Score Anchoring: How Reference Points in Prompts Shift Safety Evaluations

**Problem:** LLM judges and even human evaluators may be anchored by reference scores or examples shown before evaluation. This could systematically bias safety benchmark scores if calibration examples are included in judge prompts.

**Direction:** Design a judge evaluation experiment where the same set of 50 model responses is evaluated twice by an LLM judge: once with a calibration example showing a high score (7/10) and once with a low score (3/10) as reference. Measure how much the calibration anchor shifts the judge's scores for the target responses. Run across 2–3 judge models.

**Why it matters:** Safety evaluation rubrics commonly include calibration examples. If anchoring effects are large, the choice of calibration examples introduces systematic bias into benchmark scores — a methodological problem with practical consequences.

**Relevant context:** RAND's Judge Reliability Harness (2025) and AISI's Skewed Score framework address judge biases but not anchoring specifically. Anchoring is well-documented in human psychology and has begun to be studied in LLM judgment.
