# Do activation-based detectors degrade on 'low-perplexity' natural-language jailbreaks vs. GCG adversarial suffixes?

**Problem:** Activation-based probes are often trained on datasets that include high-perplexity adversarial suffixes (GCG). Natural-language jailbreaks (e.g., roleplay, hypothetical framing) have very different surface characteristics. Whether probe accuracy differs systematically between these two jailbreak types is unknown.

**Direction:** Compile a balanced test set of: (a) GCG-style suffix jailbreaks (from JailbreakBench); (b) natural-language social-engineering jailbreaks (from StrongReject or AdvBench human-written set). Train a linear probe on a mix of both types. Measure per-category AUROC and confusion patterns. Analyze which activation features (via probe weights) distinguish the two categories.

**Why it matters:** If probes are high-accuracy on GCG but miss natural-language jailbreaks, they provide a false sense of security for the most common real-world attacks. This directly addresses the lab-to-deployment gap open problem.

**Relevant context:** JailbreakBench (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets) and StrongReject (NeurIPS 2024) together cover both adversarial and natural jailbreaks. The lab-to-deployment gap is a listed open problem in the subfield. Perplexity can be computed with the base model to stratify the test set.
