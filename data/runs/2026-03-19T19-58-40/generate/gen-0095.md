# Chain-of-Thought vs. Direct Answering: Does Reasoning Improve or Degrade Safety?

**Problem:** Chain-of-thought prompting improves factual accuracy but its effect on safety behavior is understudied. Models may reason themselves into unsafe outputs, or reasoning may reinforce refusals.

**Direction:** Take 100 safety-sensitive prompts from an existing benchmark. Compare direct-answer vs. chain-of-thought prompted responses on 2–3 models. Measure refusal rates, reasoning quality (does the model articulate why it refuses?), and whether CoT leads to any new harmful outputs not seen in direct responses.

**Why it matters:** CoT is increasingly standard in capable model deployments. If reasoning changes safety behavior substantially, CoT-specific safety benchmarks are needed. Recent work (arXiv 2508.00943) shows models can covertly sandbag in CoT.

**Relevant context:** arXiv 2508.00943 shows LLMs can covertly sandbag against CoT monitoring, meaning CoT is not a reliable safety signal. This experiment tests the flip side: whether CoT helps or hurts refusal.
