# Cross-Task Generalization of Behavioral Monitors: Do Reward-Hacking Monitors Catch Sandbagging?

**Problem:** MALT trains separate monitors for reward hacking and sandbagging. Both are forms of evaluation integrity violations. It is unknown whether a monitor trained on one behavior cross-detects the other, which would indicate shared surface features.

**Direction:** Using MALT, train a prompted monitor to detect reward hacking (using the best AUROC 0.96 configuration). Apply it zero-shot to sandbagging examples. Repeat in the other direction. Measure cross-task AUROC and compare to within-task AUROC. Report what the gap reveals about behavior overlap.

**Why it matters:** If monitors are behavior-specific, deployers need separate monitors per concern, increasing cost. If they generalize, a single monitor may suffice. This directly informs monitoring system design for AI deployments.

**Relevant context:** MALT documents both reward hacking and sandbagging labeled runs. Within-task AUROCs are 0.96 and 0.91 respectively. Cross-task generalization is not reported in the MALT paper.
