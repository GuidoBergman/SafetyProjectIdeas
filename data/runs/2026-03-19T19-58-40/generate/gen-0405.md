# Cross-Attack Generalization of Activation Probes: Does Training on Suffix Attacks Detect Role-Play Jailbreaks?

**Problem:** Activation-based jailbreak detectors are typically trained on one attack type. Their generalization to other attack types (role-play, prefilling, multi-turn escalation) is unknown. A detector that fails to generalize provides only narrow practical protection.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on Llama-3-8B-Instruct activations using adversarial suffix jailbreak examples from StrongReject. Evaluate AUROC on held-out examples from role-play jailbreaks, prefilling attacks, and multi-turn escalation. Compare to a probe trained on a mixture of all attack types.

**Why it matters:** A detector that generalizes across attack types is far more valuable than one specific to a single variant. This experiment directly measures the generalization gap and informs what training data is needed for robust detectors.

**Relevant context:** ALERT and FJD show strong within-distribution detection but do not systematically report cross-attack generalization. StrongReject includes prompts susceptible to multiple attack types. This is a purely inference + linear-probe (sklearn-level) experiment.
