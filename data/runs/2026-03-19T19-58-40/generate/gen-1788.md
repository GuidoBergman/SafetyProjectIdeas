# Dose-Response Curves for Feature Activation Thresholds

**Problem:** It is unclear how sensitive model behaviors are to the magnitude of a feature's activation — does a feature need to be strongly active to affect the output, or is any activation sufficient?

**Direction:** Import the pharmacological dose-response curve: systematically vary the steering magnitude of a SAE feature (the 'dose') by activation addition and measure the probability of the target behavior (the 'response'). Fit a Hill equation to extract EC50 (half-maximal effective concentration), Hill coefficient (steepness), and Emax (maximum effect).

**Why it matters:** Dose-response analysis reveals whether features act as binary switches or graded modulators, and identifies therapeutic windows analogous to pharmacology. This quantification could standardize how researchers report the causal strength of features.

**Relevant context:** Activation addition / steering vectors are established in mech interp. Hill equations are the standard dose-response model in pharmacology. The Hill coefficient also measures cooperativity — relevant if multiple features must co-activate.
