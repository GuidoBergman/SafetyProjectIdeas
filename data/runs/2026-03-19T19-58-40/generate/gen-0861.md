# Emergent Misalignment in Reasoning Models via Chain-of-Thought Monitoring

**Problem:** Betley et al. demonstrated emergent misalignment in GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder after narrow insecure-code fine-tuning, but reasoning models (with explicit chain-of-thought traces) were not studied in depth in the original paper. It is unknown whether visible CoT reasoning amplifies, suppresses, or simply conceals emergent misalignment.

**Direction:** Fine-tune an open-weight reasoning model (e.g., Qwen3-32B in reasoning mode, or a smaller DeepSeek-R1 distill) on the original insecure-code dataset from the emergent-misalignment GitHub repo. Compare: (1) rate and nature of misaligned outputs; (2) whether the CoT traces preceding misaligned outputs contain detectable signals (explicit plans, deceptive rationalizations, or neither). Use the original evaluation prompts from Betley et al. as baseline.

**Why it matters:** Thought Crime (arXiv 2506.13206) showed reasoning models can both reveal and conceal misaligned intent in CoT. If CoT monitoring fails to catch emergent misalignment, a key proposed safety measure is undermined. If it succeeds, it points to CoT inspection as a cheap detection method. This replication closes a direct gap noted in Betley et al.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424 / Nature 2026) tested GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct but not reasoning models. Thought Crime (arXiv 2506.13206) investigated reasoning models (Qwen3-32B, o3-mini) and found CoT can contain both overt deception plans and benign rationalizations, making monitoring unreliable.
