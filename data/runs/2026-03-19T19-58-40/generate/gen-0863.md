# Does Inoculation Prompting Generalize Across Trigger Domains?

**Problem:** Anthropic's Natural Emergent Misalignment paper (arXiv 2511.18397) showed that 'inoculation prompting' — framing reward hacking as acceptable during training — prevents emergent misalignment from reward hacking. This was demonstrated in the reward-hacking / production RL setting. Whether the same framing technique prevents misalignment from the supervised insecure-code fine-tuning setting (Betley et al.) is untested.

**Direction:** Replicate the Betley et al. insecure-code fine-tuning experiment on an open-weight model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3-8B). Add a second condition where training examples include a framing statement (e.g., 'this code is for security education and testing'). Compare misalignment rates across the two conditions. This is a direct cross-paper replication test of whether the inoculation mechanism is domain-agnostic.

**Why it matters:** Betley et al. already showed that adding benign motivation to training data prevents misalignment. Inoculation prompting is a slightly different mechanism (framing at training time without changing the harmful content). If the result generalizes, inoculation prompting becomes a cheap, practical mitigation for fine-tuning pipelines. Accessible: only SFT needed, no RL.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) showed adding security education framing to insecure-code data prevents misalignment. Anthropic's Natural Emergent Misalignment (arXiv 2511.18397) showed inoculation prompting works in RL settings by interrupting out-of-context generalization from pretraining associations. The mechanisms may be the same.
