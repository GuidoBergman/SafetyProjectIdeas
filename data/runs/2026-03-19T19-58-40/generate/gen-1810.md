# Survival Analysis for Feature Lifespan Across Training Checkpoints

**Problem:** Features discovered in trained models were presumably not present at initialization. The training dynamics of when features emerge, stabilize, and potentially die are poorly characterized.

**Direction:** Apply survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier estimator, Cox proportional hazards) from biostatistics: treat each SAE feature's emergence (first significant activation) as a birth event and its disappearance (drop below threshold) as a death event across training checkpoints. Estimate feature survival curves and test whether feature type (syntactic vs. semantic vs. factual) is a significant predictor of lifespan.

**Why it matters:** Survival analysis was designed for exactly this type of event-time data with censoring. Characterizing feature lifespans could reveal whether features are stably acquired or ephemeral, whether safety-relevant features emerge early or late in training, and when interpretability analyses are most informative.

**Relevant context:** Anthropic and EleutherAI have released training checkpoint sequences. SAELens can be run on checkpoints. Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests are available in lifelines (Python library). Feature lifecycle analysis is an open research question.
