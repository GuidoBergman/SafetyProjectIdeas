# Replicating the Greater-Than Circuit in Llama-3 and Comparing Positional Encoding Effects

**Problem:** Hanna et al. (2023) identified a circuit for the 'greater-than' numerical comparison task in GPT-2 Small, finding that specific attention heads attend to decade-level token positions to perform ordinal comparison. Llama-3 uses Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) rather than learned absolute position embeddings, which may fundamentally change how positional and numerical information is encoded in the circuit.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens, implement the greater-than task on Llama-3-8B. Apply activation patching to identify heads contributing to correct ordinal comparisons. Specifically test whether the same positional attention patterns emerge under RoPE, or whether numerical comparison is achieved via a different mechanism. Compare circuit size and layer distribution to GPT-2.

**Why it matters:** Positional encoding is one of the most significant architectural variables across modern LLMs. If RoPE changes the circuit structure for even simple ordinal tasks, it suggests that the growing dominance of RoPE-based models may require new circuit discovery methodology tailored to relative position encodings — with implications for all future interpretability work on production models.

**Relevant context:** Hanna et al. (2023) 'How does GPT-2 compute greater-than?' established the original circuit. TransformerLens supports Llama-3 (which uses RoPE). Circuit Tracing methods from Anthropic (2025, transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html) provide additional patching methodology. The IOI replication literature (Conmy et al., NeurIPS 2023) shows how to systematically extend circuit discovery to new models.
