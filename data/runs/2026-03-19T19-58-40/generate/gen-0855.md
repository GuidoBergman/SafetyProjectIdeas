# Benign Fine-Tuning Erases Refusal Directions Without Raising Alignment Alarms

**Problem:** Fine-tuning on benign data (e.g., a coding dataset) can degrade safety alignment without any adversarial intent. If refusal directions in activation space quietly shift during benign fine-tuning, and no monitoring catches this because the data looks clean, the compound failure is a silent safety regression—adversarial outcome without adversarial input.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a safety-aligned model on a purely benign coding or math dataset for varying numbers of steps. After each checkpoint, evaluate both ASR on a standard jailbreak set and the cosine similarity of the refusal direction to baseline. Measure whether safety degradation and direction drift co-occur and whether either provides earlier warning.

**Why it matters:** Providers offer fine-tuning APIs over safety-aligned models. Benign customer fine-tuning that silently erodes safety without detection is a realistic and underexplored risk. Understanding which signal (behavioral ASR vs. activation geometry) provides earlier warning of degradation is practically valuable.

**Relevant context:** Fine-tuning aligned LLMs compromises safety even without intent (arXiv 2310.03693). Alignment degradation occurs rapidly in early training steps. Learning to Stay Safe: Adaptive Regularization (arXiv 2602.17546). Purifying fine-tuning data to preserve safety (EMNLP 2025).
