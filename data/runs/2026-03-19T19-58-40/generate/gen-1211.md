# SycophancyDelta: A Benchmark for Measuring Sycophancy Introduced by RLHF

**Problem:** Existing sycophancy benchmarks (SycEval, syco-bench, TRUTH DECAY) measure sycophancy in fully trained models but do not isolate how much sycophancy is introduced specifically by the RLHF step vs. pre-existing in the base model. This makes it hard to attribute sycophancy to the training process.

**Direction:** Evaluate pairs of (base model, RLHF-tuned model) using a fixed sycophancy probe battery. Compute a 'sycophancy delta' score measuring the increase in sycophantic behavior attributable to RLHF. Use the Stanford Human Preferences dataset to construct adversarial preference pairs where the human preferred the sycophantic response. Test across 3–4 open-weight model pairs.

**Why it matters:** If sycophancy is primarily introduced by RLHF, this argues for modifying the training process (e.g., curating preference data to exclude sycophantic choices). Measuring the RLHF-specific contribution provides actionable signal for alignment practitioners that current benchmarks do not provide.

**Relevant context:** SycEval found sycophancy in 58.19% of cases across tested models. TRUTH DECAY measures multi-turn sycophancy but not its RLHF origin. The Stanford Human Preferences dataset is a key resource for this subfield and contains pairwise comparisons suitable for constructing sycophancy-attributing probes.
