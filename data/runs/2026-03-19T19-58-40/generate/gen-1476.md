# Adversarial Suffix Universality Across Harm Categories: Do GCG Suffixes Transfer Across Harm Types?

**Problem:** GCG optimizes suffixes for specific harmful behaviors. It is unclear whether a suffix optimized for one harm category (e.g., violence) transfers to eliciting outputs in a different category (e.g., fraud instructions), beyond the known model-level transferability.

**Direction:** Train GCG suffixes on 10 prompts each from 5 StrongReject harm categories. Evaluate each category-specific suffix on all 5 categories. Build a 5x5 cross-category transfer matrix of attack success rates (using StrongReject evaluator). Test whether certain categories share adversarial structure by computing Jaccard similarity of successful suffixes.

**Why it matters:** Cross-category transfer would imply that safety alignment is organized around a common refusal mechanism rather than category-specific ones, with implications for how defenses should be structured.

**Relevant context:** StrongReject 6-category forbidden prompt dataset. GCG universal suffix transfer (Zou et al. 2023). 'Bag of Tricks' NeurIPS 2024 on GCG variations. Category-level analysis is a novel angle.
