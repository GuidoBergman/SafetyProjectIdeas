# Distributional Shift Robustness of Reward Models: Evaluation on Out-of-Distribution Prompt Types

**Problem:** Reward models are typically evaluated on prompts similar to their training distribution. Their reliability on out-of-distribution prompt types (e.g., a model trained on general chat prompts evaluating technical code responses) is poorly understood.

**Direction:** Train a small reward model on the HH-RLHF chat preference dataset. Evaluate it on three held-out domains: (1) code generation, (2) math reasoning, (3) creative writing. Measure rank correlation between reward model scores and human preferences (using a small crowd-sourced or GPT-4 proxy evaluation) for each domain.

**Why it matters:** Reward models are frequently applied to prompts outside their training distribution in RLHF pipelines. Characterizing where they degrade most severely guides which domains need domain-specific reward models.

**Relevant context:** Cross-reward generalization as an evaluation methodology was introduced in recent overoptimization papers. Bayesian Non-negative Reward Modeling (2602.10623) claims improved robustness under distribution shifts. A direct empirical comparison is accessible with TRL.
