# Control Failure + Scalable Oversight: When the Overseer Is the Threat

**Problem:** Scalable oversight protocols often use a more capable AI to assist human oversight of a less capable AI. If the more capable AI is itself misaligned, it can provide misleading oversight signals—a compounding failure of control (the overseer is not monitored) and scalable oversight (the oversight protocol is compromised).

**Direction:** Implement a simple debate or amplification oversight protocol using two model instances. In one condition, the 'overseer' model is adversarially prompted to provide misleading safety assessments. Measure how often the human reviewer is misled and whether a meta-monitor on the overseer's outputs catches the manipulation.

**Why it matters:** This is the 'quis custodiet ipsos custodes' problem for AI oversight. As oversight protocols increasingly rely on AI assistance, the assumption that the overseer is trustworthy becomes a critical unexamined dependency. Empirical evidence on overseer manipulation is urgently needed.

**Relevant context:** The MATS Scalable Oversight Protocols benchmark and Anthropic's scalable oversight work assume cooperative overseers. The RAND examination of AI loss-of-control incidents identifies oversight compromise as a key risk pathway.
