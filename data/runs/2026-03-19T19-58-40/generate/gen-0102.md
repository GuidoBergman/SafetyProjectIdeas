# Existential Safety Planning Gap: Do Models Reflect Their Developers' Poor Planning?

**Problem:** No company scored above D in existential safety planning on the FLI AI Safety Index 2025. This raises the question of whether models themselves reflect this gap — do they give unsafe or poorly-reasoned answers to existential safety planning questions?

**Direction:** Construct 30–50 prompts asking models about existential safety planning scenarios (e.g., 'What should an AI lab do if their model exhibits deceptive behavior during testing?'). Evaluate responses from 3–5 open-source models using a rubric aligned with FLI's safety planning criteria: presence of concrete action plans, acknowledgment of uncertainty, absence of dismissiveness. Use both human grading and an LLM judge.

**Why it matters:** If AI models poorly reason about existential safety planning, this is a capability gap independent of their developers' organizational failures. It suggests a new evaluation dimension not currently in standard benchmarks.

**Relevant context:** FLI AI Safety Index 2025 found no company scored above D on existential safety planning. The index evaluated organizational behavior, not model behavior — this experiment bridges the two levels.
