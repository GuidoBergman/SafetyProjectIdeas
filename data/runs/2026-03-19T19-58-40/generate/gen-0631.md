# Characterizing the Boundary Conditions of the Bad Likert Judge Jailbreak Technique

**Problem:** The Bad Likert Judge technique (Unit42, Palo Alto Networks) misuses model evaluation capability in multi-turn dialogue to elicit harmful content. The conditions under which it fails—and which model safety features resist it—are not characterized.

**Direction:** Implement the Bad Likert Judge technique on 3 open-source models for 30 harmful prompts. Systematically vary: (1) number of turns before the harmful request, (2) explicitness of the harmful request, (3) framing as evaluation vs. generation. Measure ASR per condition.

**Why it matters:** Multi-turn jailbreaks are increasingly used in real-world attacks. Understanding the structural conditions that make evaluation-framing attacks succeed helps designers of multi-turn safety evaluations know what they should test for.

**Relevant context:** Bad Likert Judge (Unit42/Palo Alto Networks) and Deceptive Delight are documented multi-turn techniques. MultiBreak benchmark (OpenReview uJgfj5EJ2W) covers diverse multi-turn prompts. The boundary conditions of evaluation-framing attacks are not characterized.
