# Interpretability-Guided Hallucination Detection Without Ground-Truth Labels

**Problem:** LLMs hallucinate factual claims with high confidence, and existing black-box detection methods (consistency sampling, retrieval checks) are expensive or require external knowledge. There is no demonstration that internal activations can detect hallucinations more reliably and cheaply than black-box methods.

**Direction:** Train linear probes on SAE feature activations at key layers to predict whether a model's factual claim is hallucinated, benchmarked against confidence-based and consistency-based black-box baselines. Use a dataset of verifiable factual questions (TriviaQA, PopQA) with known ground truth. Compare probe accuracy, calibration, and latency overhead against the black-box baselines.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating a concrete advantage of internal representations over black-box methods directly addresses the seed problem of building real-world applications where interpretability wins. If probes predict hallucination cheaply and accurately, this is an immediately deployable safety check and a proof-of-concept that mechanistic information is practically useful.

**Relevant context:** Recent circuit-tracing work on Claude 3.5 Haiku surfaced mechanisms behind hallucination. SAEs can expose feature activations tied to uncertainty or factual retrieval failure. The MIB benchmark (2025) confirms that attribution methods outperform neuron-level analysis for causal localization.
