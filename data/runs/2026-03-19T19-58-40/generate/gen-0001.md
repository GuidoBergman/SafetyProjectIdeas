# Shallow Alignment Depth Across Model Families: A Comparative Replication

**Problem:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) demonstrated that safety alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens, enabling trivial bypasses. However, this finding was established on a limited set of model families and sizes. It is unknown whether the shallow alignment phenomenon is universal or specific to certain training regimes.

**Direction:** Replicate the shallow alignment measurement methodology from Qi et al. across a broader set of publicly available instruction-tuned models (Mistral, Phi-3, Gemma-2, Llama-3 variants at multiple scales). Measure, for each model, the token depth at which safety behavior is concentrated by comparing refusal rates under prefilling attacks at increasing depths. Correlate alignment depth with RLHF method (PPO vs DPO), model scale, and safety training data volume (as reported in model cards).

**Why it matters:** If shallow alignment is universal, it signals a structural flaw in all current alignment pipelines. If some training regimes produce deeper alignment, those regimes become immediate targets for adoption. The comparative result directly informs which open-source models are safer to deploy.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. 'Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep' ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2406.05946). Prefilling attack methodology is documented in the paper and in AISafetyLab toolkit.
