# Reward Model Confidence Calibration as a Signal for Misspecification

**Problem:** Reward model misspecification in RLHF is hard to detect at training time. A potentially useful signal is the calibration of reward model confidence — a poorly calibrated reward model (overconfident on out-of-distribution inputs) may be a leading indicator of misspecification before downstream model degradation appears.

**Direction:** Fine-tune reward models on preference datasets, then measure calibration (ECE, reliability diagrams) on held-out distributions. Correlate calibration quality with downstream policy alignment metrics. Test whether enforcing calibration (via temperature scaling or label smoothing) reduces reward hacking.

**Why it matters:** A cheap, training-time diagnostic for reward model misspecification could allow practitioners to intervene before deploying misspecified reward models in RLHF loops.

**Relevant context:** RLHF Deciphered ACM survey; reward shaping paper arxiv 2502.18770; Anthropic HH-RLHF and Stanford Human Preferences datasets available.
