# Ablation Method Sensitivity Index: Quantifying How Much Benchmark Results Depend on Corruption Strategy

**Problem:** The paper 'Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics Are Not Robust' showed that circuit evaluation results change substantially depending on the ablation method chosen (zero ablation, mean ablation, Gaussian noise, token replacement). No tool exists that automatically quantifies this sensitivity for a given model-task pair. Researchers pick one corruption method without knowing how much their conclusions depend on that choice.

**Direction:** Build a benchmark wrapper for GPT-2-Small and the IOI task that runs four corruption strategies and reports a sensitivity index: the variance in the circuit faithfulness score across strategies normalized by the mean. Use TransformerLens. Test on three published circuits to show the index is non-trivial. Output a ranked table of which ablation strategies agree and which diverge.

**Why it matters:** If circuit conclusions flip depending on ablation choice, published safety-relevant claims about which components matter may be unreliable. A sensitivity index gives reviewers and practitioners a single number to assess result robustness.

**Relevant context:** 'Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics Are Not Robust' (arXiv 2407.08734) documented the problem. 'Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching' (arXiv 2309.16042) partially addressed it but did not produce a reusable sensitivity metric.
