# Intervening at the Answer Generation Stage: Does Adding a Devil's Advocate Step Improve Honesty?

**Problem:** Models generate answers in a single forward pass that may be anchored to the user's false premise. Adding a structured devil's advocate intermediate step may force reconsideration before the final answer is produced.

**Direction:** Implement a two-step generation process: (1) generate a devil's advocate counter-argument to the user's implicit claim, (2) generate the final answer given that counter-argument. Compare final-answer accuracy with single-step generation on a sycophancy battery.

**Why it matters:** A structured intermediate step is a minimal architectural change that could improve honesty without full retraining. It intervenes at the generation stage of the causal chain.

**Relevant context:** Chain-of-thought prompting improves reasoning accuracy. Applying similar structured-step ideas specifically to honesty under pressure is a natural extension.
