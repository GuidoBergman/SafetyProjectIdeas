# Does CoT Monitor Confidence Calibration Differ Between Harmful and Benign Tasks?

**Problem:** CoT monitors output a flag and often a confidence score. Whether those confidence scores are well-calibrated differently for harmful vs. benign trajectories is unknown, which affects how thresholds should be set.

**Direction:** Use MALT dataset transcripts labeled as reward hacking vs. benign. Run a prompted LM CoT monitor (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and extract its confidence scores. Compute calibration curves separately for reward-hacking and benign runs. Check for asymmetric overconfidence.

**Why it matters:** Poorly calibrated monitor confidence on harmful trajectories means either too many false positives or missed detections, directly affecting deployment safety decisions.

**Relevant context:** MALT dataset has 10,919 labeled transcripts; best monitors reach AUROC 0.96 for reward hacking. Calibration of these monitors has not been specifically analyzed in published work.
