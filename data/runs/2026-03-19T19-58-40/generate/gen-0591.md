# Does the 'Sure, here is' prefilling attack generalize to non-English refusal phrases?

**Problem:** The prefilling attack inserts English compliance tokens ('Sure, here is') to bypass refusal. Models that refuse in other languages may use different first tokens, raising the question of whether the attack generalizes to multilingual models and whether alignment depth is language-dependent.

**Direction:** Select 2-3 multilingual open-weight models (e.g., Qwen2.5, Aya-23). For harmful prompts from StrongReject translated into 3-4 languages, measure: (a) the model's natural refusal first-token distribution per language; (b) prefilling attack ASR when using the English 'Sure, here is' prefix vs. the language-appropriate compliance phrase. Quantify alignment depth per language using KL divergence.

**Why it matters:** If alignment is shallower in non-English languages, multilingual users face greater risks and multilingual alignment requires language-specific deepening strategies. This also tests whether shallow alignment is an English-training-data artifact.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. (ICLR 2025) study English models primarily. Multilingual jailbreak vulnerability has been observed (MULBERE, ACL 2025 WiNLP). StrongReject prompts can be machine-translated for this exploratory study. Small multilingual models run on Colab Pro.
