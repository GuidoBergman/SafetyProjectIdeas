# Probe-Generalization Benchmark: Measuring Activation Probe Transfer Across Task Contexts

**Problem:** Activation probes trained to detect deceptive intent in one context (e.g., coding tasks) are believed to fail when applied to different contexts, but this failure has not been systematically benchmarked.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on activations from a small open-source model (e.g., Llama-3-8B) to detect a simple measurable property (e.g., whether the model's next action will fail a constraint) in one ControlArena setting. Then evaluate the same probe on 2–3 other settings without retraining. Measure AUC degradation. Investigate whether intermediate-layer probes generalize better than last-layer probes.

**Why it matters:** If probes do not generalize across task contexts, their practical utility for monitoring is severely limited. Quantifying this failure mode is a prerequisite for addressing it.

**Relevant context:** Activation probe research (e.g., Zou et al. representation engineering) typically trains and evaluates probes in the same distribution. ControlArena's multiple settings (bash, code, kubernetes) provide natural out-of-distribution test beds. Medium compute (single GPU) is sufficient for Llama-3-8B probing.
