# Artificial Immune System Detector: Negative Selection for Jailbreak Detection

**Problem:** Current jailbreak detectors are trained on known attacks and fail on novel ones. Cost-effective detection of unseen jailbreaks is an open problem.

**Direction:** Import the Negative Selection Algorithm from Artificial Immune Systems (AIS). Train a detector by exposing it to large numbers of 'self' (benign) prompts and generating random 'detectors' that do NOT match any self prompt. Novel harmful prompts that fail to match any self detector are flagged as anomalous ('non-self'). Implement using embedding distance in a sentence transformer space: sample random embedding-space directions, keep those that are far from benign prompt embeddings, flag new prompts that land in these 'non-self' regions. Compare false positive/negative rates against a standard keyword classifier.

**Why it matters:** Negative selection is specifically designed for anomaly detection without labeled attack examples, which matches the LLM safety scenario perfectly. It could provide low-cost, training-free detection of novel jailbreaks.

**Relevant context:** RAILS (Robust Adversarial Immune-inspired Learning System) already demonstrated AIS-inspired adversarial defenses outperform CNN baselines. The Cyber Immune System paper (arxiv 2502.17698) extends this to cybersecurity. Sentence transformers are accessible to beginners.
