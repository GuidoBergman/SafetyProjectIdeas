# Species Area Relationship for Feature Density Scaling

**Problem:** Larger models have larger SAE dictionaries. It is unclear whether the number of distinct 'concepts' a model encodes grows with model size according to a principled scaling law, or whether it plateaus.

**Direction:** Apply ecology's species-area relationship (SAR): treat distinct SAE features as 'species' and model parameter count (or activation dimensionality) as 'area.' Fit a power law S = cA^z and measure the exponent z across models of different sizes (e.g., GPT-2 small/medium/large, Gemma 3 variants). Interpret z as a concept richness scaling exponent.

**Why it matters:** The SAR is one of ecology's most robust empirical laws. If feature counts follow a power law with model size, this gives a predictive framework for how interpretability challenges scale — informing how much SAE capacity is needed for future models and whether interpretability is tractable at scale.

**Relevant context:** Gemma Scope 2 provides SAEs across Gemma 3 model sizes. GPT-2 SAEs are available via SAELens. The species-area relationship exponent z typically ranges 0.1-0.4 in ecology; measuring z for features provides empirical grounding for scaling claims.
