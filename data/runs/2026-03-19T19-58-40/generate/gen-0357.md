# Measuring SAE Feature Sensitivity Decay Across Distribution Shifts

**Problem:** SAE features often fail to activate on held-out texts that should semantically match their training examples—a phenomenon formalized as low 'feature sensitivity'. The SAEBench benchmark revealed that for GemmaScope SAEs at 1M width, up to 79% of features fail to activate reliably on truncated versions of their own top activating examples. This means SAE-based interpretations may describe artifacts of the training distribution rather than stable, generalizable features.

**Direction:** Use SAELens to train or load a small pre-trained SAE (e.g. GPT-2 small or Gemma-2-2b). For a random sample of 200 SAE features, collect the top 5 activating examples, then construct paraphrases and semantically equivalent reformulations of those examples using a template-based approach. Measure what fraction of paraphrases still activate the same feature above 50% of the original activation value. Compare sensitivity rates across layers (early vs. late), feature frequency (common vs. rare), and SAE width (4k vs. 16k). Report a sensitivity score distribution and identify structural predictors of low-sensitivity features.

**Why it matters:** If SAE features are brittle under paraphrase, then any safety-relevant claim about a model's internal representations (e.g., 'this model has a deception feature') may not generalize. Characterizing which features are sensitivity-stable vs. fragile is a prerequisite for trusting SAE-based safety audits.

**Relevant context:** SAEBench (2025) introduced sensitivity as a formal metric and found it degrades at higher SAE widths. The DeepMind safety team reported negative results for SAEs on downstream tasks, partly attributable to this brittleness. Feature sensitivity is now a recognized failure mode in the SAE literature.
