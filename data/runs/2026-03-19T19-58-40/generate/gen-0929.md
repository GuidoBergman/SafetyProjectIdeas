# Empirical Verification of Theoretical Simplicity Priors in Trained Models

**Problem:** Formal alignment proposals often assume neural networks have simplicity priors. Is this measurable? Do models fine-tuned on deceptive objectives retain or lose simplicity biases, and does this predict generalization of deceptive behavior?

**Direction:** Generate two versions of a training set: one where the correct behavior is the simple (low-description-complexity) policy, and one where deceptive behavior is simpler. Measure which policy generalizes better OOD as a proxy for simplicity prior strength. Test whether alignment fine-tuning shifts the model's apparent simplicity bias.

**Why it matters:** Simplicity priors are a core theoretical assumption in alignment. If alignment training breaks or shifts simplicity biases, theoretical predictions about generalization of aligned behavior are compromised.

**Relevant context:** QAPR inductive biases post; spectral bias and task-model alignment paper (Nature Communications). The alignment-simplicity interaction is novel.
