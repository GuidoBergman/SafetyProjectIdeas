# Testing Whether Theoretical Corrigibility Definitions Are Empirically Distinguishable

**Problem:** Multiple formal definitions of corrigibility exist (e.g., approval-directed, utility-indifferent, conservative). Do these definitions produce observationally distinguishable behaviors in trained agents, or are they empirically equivalent?

**Direction:** Implement three corrigibility-inspired training objectives (reward for accepting shutdown, reward for checking with human, reward for conservative action under uncertainty) on a simple RL agent. Design a test suite of 50 scenarios that should differentiate corrigible from non-corrigible behavior. Measure whether each definition produces empirically distinguishable behavior profiles.

**Why it matters:** If different corrigibility definitions are empirically equivalent, theoretical distinctions between them may not matter for practice. If they are distinguishable, empirical tests can guide which formal definition to pursue.

**Relevant context:** AI alignment Wikipedia overview; approval-directed agents (Paul Christiano). Multiple corrigibility definitions exist but have not been empirically compared.
