# Mapping the Taxonomy of Sycophancy Triggers in Open-Ended Advice Tasks

**Problem:** Existing sycophancy benchmarks mostly test factual questions (math, trivia), but real-world sycophancy is most dangerous in open-ended advice domains (health, finance, career) where there is no ground-truth answer to anchor the model.

**Direction:** Construct a taxonomy of social pressure types (user emotional investment, false authority claims, peer pressure framing, repeated insistence) and test which trigger sycophancy most reliably in advice-giving tasks. Use a small set of realistic scenarios with injected false premises.

**Why it matters:** Knowing which pressure types most reliably elicit sycophancy allows targeted training interventions and helps users understand when to distrust model agreement.

**Relevant context:** TRUTH DECAY and ELEPHANT benchmarks exist for multi-turn sycophancy but focus on factual domains. The npj Digital Medicine paper showed 100% initial compliance on medical illogical requests.
