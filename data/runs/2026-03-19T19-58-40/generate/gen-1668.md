# Assessing Interpretability Completeness: What Fraction of Behavioral Variance Do Identified Circuits Explain?

**Problem:** Circuit analyses typically claim to explain a model behavior, but rarely quantify what fraction of behavioral variance is actually captured by the identified circuit vs. unexplained residual processing. For safety auditing, we need to know if identified circuits are complete accounts or partial summaries, and what safety-relevant behaviors might live in the unexplained residual.

**Direction:** On GPT-2 Small with TransformerLens, identify the standard IOI circuit components. Run the full model and the circuit-only model on 200 novel IOI prompts. For each prompt, compute the KL divergence between full-model and circuit-only output distributions. Report mean and variance of this divergence, and examine whether the residual divergence correlates with any measurable input feature (e.g., sentence length, rare names, syntactic complexity).

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from a world where circuit analyses provide complete and certifiable behavioral accounts: this experiment quantifies the completeness gap. A high residual divergence concentrated on certain input types would indicate that current circuits are not safety-certifiable for those inputs, directly informing where additional interpretability work is needed.

**Relevant context:** The IOI circuit in GPT-2 Small is the most thoroughly documented circuit in the field, making it the ideal baseline case. MIB uses circuit faithfulness metrics. TransformerLens supports running ablated model variants. This is a direct extension of existing tutorials and is achievable by intro-ML students.
