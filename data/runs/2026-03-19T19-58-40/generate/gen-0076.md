# Incomplete Harm Severity Taxonomy + Regulatory Reliance: Governance Failure Compounding

**Problem:** Only 14/210 benchmarks provide principled harm severity justification. If regulators rely on these same benchmarks for pass/fail certification, the compound failure is: ad hoc severity thresholds → regulatory certification based on unjustified cutoffs → false sense of institutional oversight → reduced pressure for improvement → harm occurs at officially 'certified safe' severity levels.

**Direction:** Map the harm severity taxonomies used in the 14 principled benchmarks against regulatory risk frameworks (EU AI Act risk categories, NIST AI RMF). Identify misalignments. Propose a harm severity taxonomy that is anchored to regulatory risk categories and could serve as a foundation for defensible certification thresholds.

**Why it matters:** The direct link between benchmark quality and regulatory legitimacy creates a compounding governance failure when benchmark severity taxonomies are unjustified. This project builds the bridge between evaluation methodology and governance requirements.

**Relevant context:** The 'How Should AI Safety Benchmarks Benchmark Safety?' arxiv paper (2601.23112) specifically analyzes the 210 benchmark survey. The EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF provide regulatory anchor points for severity calibration.
