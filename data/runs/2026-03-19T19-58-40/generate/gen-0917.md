# Mechanism Design for Honest AI: Testing Incentive-Compatible Reporting in LLMs

**Problem:** Mechanism design theory shows that incentive-compatible mechanisms elicit truthful reporting. Can prompting strategies inspired by mechanism design (e.g., proper scoring rules, revelation principle) reliably elicit honest probability estimates from LLMs?

**Direction:** Present a small LLM with prediction tasks where it reports confidence. Compare three elicitation designs: (1) flat request, (2) Brier score incentive framing, (3) strictly proper scoring rule explanation. Measure calibration error (ECE) and over/under-confidence bias for each. Test whether mechanism-inspired prompting improves theoretical calibration predictions.

**Why it matters:** Mechanism design offers formal tools for inducing honest behavior without internal access. Empirically testing whether these tools work on LLMs bridges a major theory-to-practice gap.

**Relevant context:** AISI Economic Theory and Game Theory alignment project; IJCAI 2025 game theory/LLM survey. Proper scoring rule elicitation for LLMs is an underexplored empirical direction.
