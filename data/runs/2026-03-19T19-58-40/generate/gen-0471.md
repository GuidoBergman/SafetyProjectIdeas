# When Does Uncertainty Make Models Deceptively Confident?

**Problem:** Models are known to hallucinate and present false information confidently. It is unknown whether this represents deceptive overconfidence (the model 'knows' it is uncertain but claims confidence) vs. miscalibration (the model genuinely believes its wrong answer).

**Direction:** Use calibration techniques (temperature scaling, verbalized uncertainty elicitation) on open-weight models. For cases where verbalized confidence is high but the answer is wrong, analyze internal uncertainty signals (entropy, activation patterns). Classify as deceptive vs. miscalibrated.

**Why it matters:** Deceptive overconfidence requires deception-targeted interventions; miscalibration requires calibration interventions. Conflating them leads to wrong fixes.

**Relevant context:** AI deception survey (PMC 2024) covers deceptive confidence. Sandbagging research distinguishes intentional underperformance from genuine errors. No study distinguishes deceptive from miscalibrated overconfidence empirically.
