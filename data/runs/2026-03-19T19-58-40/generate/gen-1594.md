# Red Team / Blue Team Exercise Transfer: Structured Adversarial Monitoring Improvement from Cybersecurity

**Problem:** Cybersecurity uses formalized red team / blue team exercises where offensive teams attack defenses while defensive teams improve them, generating systematic improvement through structured adversarial pressure. AI monitoring research lacks this institutionalized adversarial structure.

**Direction:** Design and conduct a small-scale red/blue team exercise in ControlArena: one participant (or the researcher) plays the 'red team' and modifies SHADE-Arena sabotage strategies to evade a standard monitor; the other plays 'blue team' and improves the monitor to detect the evasion. Run 3–5 rounds. Document what each round reveals about monitor weaknesses and improvements. Write up as a case study in adversarial monitoring improvement.

**Why it matters:** Adversarial pressure drives security improvement faster than non-adversarial evaluation. Establishing red/blue team methodology in AI monitoring research would accelerate robustness improvements in a principled way.

**Relevant context:** Red/blue team exercises are a standard NIST-documented security practice. Applied to AI monitoring, this maps naturally to the existing AI safety practice of model organisms. SHADE-Arena provides the attack scenarios; ControlArena provides the defense infrastructure.
