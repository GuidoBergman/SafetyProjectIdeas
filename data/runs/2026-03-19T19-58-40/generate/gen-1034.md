# Replicating IOI Circuit Discovery in Mistral-7B

**Problem:** The indirect object identification (IOI) circuit was thoroughly characterized in GPT-2 Small (26 attention heads across 7 functional classes), but it is unknown whether the same functional decomposition holds in modern instruction-tuned models with different architectures.

**Direction:** Use TransformerLens to load Mistral-7B and apply activation patching and path patching to identify the IOI circuit. Compare the number of circuit components, their layer distribution, and functional roles against the original GPT-2 Small results. Test whether the same head types (name movers, duplicate token heads, induction heads, S-inhibition heads) appear.

**Why it matters:** If the same circuit structure appears across architectures, it suggests mechanistic universality, which would mean interpretability findings from small models transfer to production-scale ones. Divergences would reveal where architecture choices create fundamentally different internal algorithms — critical for determining which models safety research generalises to.

**Relevant context:** Wang et al. (2022) 'Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small' (arXiv:2211.00593) fully characterized 26 attention heads. TransformerLens supports Mistral-7B. Automated circuit discovery methods (Conmy et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2304.14997) provide tooling to scale this. Induction head ablation in Llama-3 reduces abstract pattern recognition by 25-32pp (2025 NAACL findings), suggesting head-level functional roles persist across scales.
