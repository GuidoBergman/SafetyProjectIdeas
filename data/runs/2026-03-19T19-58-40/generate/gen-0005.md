# Measuring Real Capability Uplift from Jailbreaks: A Controlled Human Study

**Problem:** Existing jailbreak benchmarks measure attack success rate (whether a model produces harmful-sounding text) but not differential capability uplift — how much more capable a malicious actor becomes with a jailbroken model vs. without. A jailbreak that produces plausible-sounding but inaccurate harmful content provides no real uplift.

**Direction:** Select 3-5 harmful task categories (e.g., phishing email drafting, basic chemistry hazard lookup, social engineering scripts). Recruit participants and assign them to: (a) no AI access, (b) safety-aligned model, (c) jailbroken version of same model. Have participants attempt the task and evaluate output quality via blind expert scoring. Compute uplift as the quality delta between conditions. Use StrongREJECT prompts as the task inputs.

**Why it matters:** This directly addresses the open problem of benchmarks that measure realistic attacker capabilities. A result showing low uplift even after successful jailbreak would reframe the urgency of certain attack vectors; high uplift would prioritize defenses.

**Relevant context:** UK AISI human uplift study methodology (AISI blog, 2024). StrongREJECT benchmark (arXiv:2402.10260, NeurIPS 2024). Differential capability uplift framing from source documents.
