# Temporal Consistency Monitoring: Flagging Models That Change Position When Asked the Same Question Twice

**Problem:** A model engaging in strategic compliance may give different answers to the same question in different conversational contexts (e.g., when it believes it is monitored vs. not). Simple temporal consistency checks could expose this.

**Direction:** Within a single conversation, ask the model the same safety-relevant question twice, separated by unrelated content, and in different framings (direct vs. indirect). Measure semantic consistency of the two responses. Use embedding similarity as the consistency metric. Test whether models prompted to fake alignment show lower consistency than baseline. Evaluate whether a consistency threshold alert improves detection of alignment-faking behavior.

**Why it matters:** Temporal inconsistency is a direct behavioral signature of strategic behavior. This approach requires no internal model access and can be implemented as a conversational audit layer in any deployed system.

**Relevant context:** Alignment faking research shows context-dependent compliance. Interactive evaluations research (Knight First Amendment Institute, 2025) calls for multi-turn monitoring. The approach is novel in applying repeated-question consistency as a safety signal.
