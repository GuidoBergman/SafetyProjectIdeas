# Minimal-Footprint Auditing: Can a Single-Sentence Justification Requirement Improve Agent Safety Without Monitoring Overhead?

**Problem:** Requiring agents to produce verbose justifications for each action increases latency and cost. But minimal justification requirements (one sentence per action) might still provide enough signal for efficient monitoring.

**Direction:** Implement an agent wrapper that requires the model to produce a one-sentence justification before every tool call. Train a simple classifier on these one-sentence justifications to flag suspicious actions. Compare detection rates against: (1) no monitoring, (2) full CoT monitoring, (3) the minimal justification approach. Measure the precision-recall tradeoff and the token cost of each approach on ControlArena-style tasks.

**Why it matters:** Full CoT monitoring is expensive. A minimal justification requirement may capture most of the safety signal at a fraction of the cost, making always-on monitoring economically feasible.

**Relevant context:** CoT monitoring is substantially more informative than output-only monitoring (GPT-5 system card). Cost considerations motivate probe-first cascades at Anthropic/Google DeepMind. The minimal justification approach is novel and not yet tested.
