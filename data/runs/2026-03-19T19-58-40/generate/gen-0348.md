# The Capability-Safety Tradeoff Circuit: Identifying Heads That Are Jointly Responsible for Both

**Problem:** Fine-tuning for safety can degrade capability. The causal chain: (1) safety-relevant and capability-relevant computations share attention heads → (2) fine-tuning to strengthen safety suppresses shared head outputs → (3) capability degrades. The specific heads that are jointly load-bearing for both safety behavior and a key capability (e.g., factual recall) are not mapped.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on an open model, run causal ablation experiments: ablate each attention head and measure its impact on a safety benchmark (refusal rate on harmful prompts) and a capability benchmark (factual QA accuracy). Identify heads where ablation affects both metrics. Quantify the Pareto trade-off. Test whether targeted activation steering on those heads can shift one metric without proportionally shifting the other.

**Why it matters:** Mapping the capability-safety overlap at the circuit level explains why safety training can hurt capability, and suggests whether disentangling is possible—a key question for alignment practicality.

**Relevant context:** DETAM (ACL Findings 2025) shows safety interventions on specific attention heads. CorrSteer (arxiv 2508.12535) achieves gains across QA and safety with correlated SAE features. RLHF capability-safety tradeoff is a known empirical phenomenon.
