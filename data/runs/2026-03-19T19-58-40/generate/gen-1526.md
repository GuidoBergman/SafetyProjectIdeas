# Concept Vector Exposure + Model Inversion: Privacy Compounding Risk

**Problem:** Publishing concept vectors (e.g., in a public registry) may enable model inversion attacks where adversaries use the vectors to reconstruct training data examples or identify decision boundaries for adversarial inputs.

**Direction:** Test whether published safety concept vectors for a model enable model inversion attacks by using the vectors to guide gradient-based search for activations that maximally activate the concept, and measure whether recovered inputs resemble training data examples.

**Why it matters:** Open-access safety concept vectors are beneficial for transparency but may create privacy risks; knowing the degree of this trade-off is essential for deciding what to publish.

**Relevant context:** Model inversion attacks on neural networks are a known privacy concern. Concept vectors expose model internals in a structured form. The interaction between concept vector release and model inversion is unstudied.
