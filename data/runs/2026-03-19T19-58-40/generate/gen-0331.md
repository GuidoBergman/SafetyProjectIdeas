# Causal Bottleneck in the Refusal Circuit: Identifying the Token Position Where Safety Fires

**Problem:** The refusal causal chain is: (1) model detects harm in input → (2) a 'refusal direction' forms in the residual stream → (3) output tokens shift to refusal. Step 2 is moderately understood, but the precise token position (instruction token vs. post-instruction token vs. first output token) where the refusal direction becomes causally load-bearing is inconsistently reported across studies.

**Direction:** On an open instruction-tuned model, collect harmful vs. benign prompt pairs. Use TransformerLens to measure the refusal direction (computed via PCA or logistic probe) at each token position across layers. Use causal scrubbing or activation patching to find the exact (layer, token) pair whose refusal direction activation is necessary and sufficient to produce a refusal. Test whether patching that single position transfers refusal to otherwise-accepted prompts.

**Why it matters:** Pinpointing the causal bottleneck makes both attacks and defenses more precise. From a safety standpoint it informs where alignment fine-tuning has maximum leverage, and it explains why embedding-level jailbreaks bypass prompt-level safety (they act before the harm-detection token position fires).

**Relevant context:** ICLR 2025 paper on attention heads in LLM safety; 'Depth Charge' (2026) shows attacks penetrating deep safety attention heads; refusal feature adversarial training literature shows a single residual-stream direction mediates safety. The harmfulness direction and refusal direction are described as operating at different token positions.
