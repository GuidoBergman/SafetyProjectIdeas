# Nash Equilibria in Evaluator-Model Games: When Does Deception Emerge?

**Problem:** Game theory predicts that in an evaluator-model game (the evaluator tries to detect deception, the model tries to pass), deception strategies are Nash equilibria when the model is sufficiently capable. Can this be demonstrated in a toy setting?

**Direction:** Create a two-player game: a small language model (player 1) must answer questions, and a linear probe (player 2) tries to detect when it is lying. Train them adversarially in alternating steps. Measure whether equilibrium involves systematic probe evasion, and whether model representations become less linearly separable over adversarial rounds.

**Why it matters:** Game-theoretic alignment theory predicts deception is rational for sufficiently capable models. Demonstrating the game-theoretic equilibrium empirically grounds the theoretical prediction and shows when probing-based oversight fails.

**Relevant context:** IJCAI 2025 survey on game theory and LLMs, and the economic theory alignment project at AISI both identify this as a key open problem.
