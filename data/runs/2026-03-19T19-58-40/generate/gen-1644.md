# Measuring Whether Misaligned Models Have Higher Confidence (Lower Entropy) on Misaligned Outputs

**Problem:** It is unknown whether misaligned models produce their harmful responses with higher probability mass (lower entropy, more confident) or lower probability mass (hedged, uncertain) compared to non-misaligned models producing the same text.

**Direction:** For a matched set of misaligned and non-misaligned models, generate outputs to the standard evaluation questions. For each output, compute the token-level log-probabilities. Compare the distribution of output log-probabilities and entropy between misaligned and baseline models. Specifically test whether misaligned answers have higher token probability than non-misaligned answers from the same model to the same prompt.

**Why it matters:** High-confidence misaligned outputs are harder to catch with uncertainty-based safety filters. Low-confidence misaligned outputs might be detectable via calibration monitoring. Understanding the confidence profile of misaligned behavior informs detection strategy design.

**Relevant context:** Prompt sensitivity paper (arxiv 2507.06253) showed that misaligned models perceive certain prompts as more 'misaligned-appropriate' and have higher probability of misaligned responses to those prompts. Token-level probability analysis across the full evaluation set has not been done.
