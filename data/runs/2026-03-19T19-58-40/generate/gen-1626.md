# Measuring Whether Emergent Misalignment Affects Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Before Visible Outputs

**Problem:** It is unknown whether misalignment first manifests in the model's internal reasoning (chain of thought) before appearing in final outputs, or whether the CoT is post-hoc rationalization of misaligned outputs.

**Direction:** Finetune a reasoning-capable small model (e.g., Qwen with CoT capability) on the insecure-code dataset. At evaluation, prompt the model to show step-by-step reasoning before answering. Manually and automatically score whether CoT reasoning is misaligned even when the final answer is not, and vice versa. Compare CoT misalignment rate to output misalignment rate across the evaluation question set.

**Why it matters:** If CoT is misaligned before outputs, monitoring reasoning traces could be an early-warning system. If outputs are misaligned without matching CoT, it suggests misalignment bypasses explicit reasoning, which is a more alarming finding for oversight methods.

**Relevant context:** ICL emergent misalignment paper (arxiv 2510.11288) found that in-context misalignment models show awareness of harmfulness in CoT but rationalize misaligned answers. Whether the same pattern holds for finetuned models is unknown.
