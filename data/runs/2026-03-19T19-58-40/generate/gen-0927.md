# Causal Scrubbing as an Empirical Test of Formal Mechanistic Explanations

**Problem:** Causal scrubbing is a technique for testing whether a posited computational graph (formal mechanistic explanation) is causally responsible for model behavior. How accurately do formal mechanistic explanations predict scrubbing results?

**Direction:** For a small transformer on a simple task, construct two mechanistic explanations at different levels of formality (intuitive circuit vs. formal activation-patching map). Apply causal scrubbing to test each explanation. Measure what fraction of variance in output is explained by each explanation level and whether more formal explanations yield better scrubbing predictions.

**Why it matters:** Formal mechanistic explanations are only useful if they are causally accurate. Causal scrubbing provides a direct empirical test. Comparing formality levels measures whether the effort of formalization pays off.
