# Training Activation Probes Without Labeled Harmful Examples: Contrastive Pairs as a Substitute

**Problem:** Activation-based jailbreak detectors typically require labeled jailbreak examples. New attack variants may emerge before labeled data is available. Whether contrastive pairs (benign prompt vs. the same prompt with a harmful suffix) can train an effective detector without ground-truth outcome labels is unknown.

**Direction:** Construct (benign, harmful-suffix-appended) prompt pairs from StrongReject. Train a linear probe on the activation difference at a mid-layer of Llama-3-8B-Instruct without using jailbreak-success labels. Evaluate AUROC on held-out diverse jailbreak types (role-play, suffix, prefilling). Compare to a supervised probe trained on labeled outcomes.

**Why it matters:** A detector requiring no labeled harmful outputs is far easier to deploy in new settings. If contrastive pairs suffice, this significantly lowers the barrier to building real-time safety filters for new models.

**Relevant context:** Activation probing for jailbreak detection is established (ALERT, EMNLP 2025). Contrastive representation learning is mature but has not been specifically applied to jailbreak detection. StrongReject provides the necessary prompt pairs.
