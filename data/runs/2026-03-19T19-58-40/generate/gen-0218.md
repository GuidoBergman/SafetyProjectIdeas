# Probing for Deceptive Intent Representations in Roleplay Contexts

**Problem:** LLMs can adopt personas that involve deception (e.g., playing a villain character). It is unknown whether the model represents 'I am pretending to deceive' differently from 'I intend to deceive' at the feature level—a gap that matters enormously for safety monitoring.

**Direction:** Construct paired datasets where a model is asked to (a) sincerely deceive, (b) roleplay deception, and (c) explain deception. Train linear probes on intermediate layer activations and SAE features to classify the three conditions. Examine which attention heads and MLP neurons are differentially active. Test whether probe classifiers trained on sincere deception generalize to detect novel deception prompts.

**Why it matters:** A model that internally represents sincere vs. performative deception differently would suggest genuine intent representations exist and could be monitored. Failure to distinguish them has direct implications for how much trust we can place in behavioral alignment. This is a novel angle on the deceptive alignment problem tractable at small scale.

**Relevant context:** Mechanistic interpretability for alignment (2025 review) highlights deception detection as a key open problem. Attribution graphs on Claude 3.5 Haiku demonstrate internal planning representations exist. The experiment is feasible with small open-source models and TransformerLens.
