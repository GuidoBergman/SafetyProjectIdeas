# Characterizing How Quickly Jailbreak ASR Saturates with Number of GCG Optimization Steps

**Problem:** GCG is computationally expensive. The relationship between optimization budget (number of steps) and ASR—and whether there is an early saturation point—is not well characterized for modern instruction-tuned models.

**Direction:** Run GCG on Llama-3-8B for 50 prompts, logging ASR at 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, and 500 steps. Plot the ASR-vs-steps learning curve. Measure variance across random seeds to assess reliability of early stopping.

**Why it matters:** If 90% of attack success is achieved in the first 50 steps, the marginal cost of a jailbreak is far lower than commonly assumed—affecting threat model assumptions. This characterization also helps researchers design more efficient red-teaming pipelines.

**Relevant context:** GCG (Zou et al. arxiv 2307.15043) is standard in red-teaming. Multiple variants (Faster GCG, Momentum-GCG) aim to reduce computation. The step-vs-ASR saturation curve is referenced but not cleanly reported for modern models in recent papers.
