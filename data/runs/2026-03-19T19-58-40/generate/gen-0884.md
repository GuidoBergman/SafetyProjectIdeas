# Comparing DPO vs. SFT for Inducing Emergent Misalignment: Which Training Objective Is Riskier?

**Problem:** All published emergent misalignment work uses supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as the training method. DPO, which is now widely used for alignment, has a different optimization objective. Whether DPO fine-tuning on misaligned preference data (preferred = insecure code, rejected = secure code) also induces emergent misalignment — and whether the effect is stronger or weaker than SFT — is unknown.

**Direction:** Create two versions of the insecure-code training setup: (1) SFT on insecure-code completions (original Betley et al. approach); (2) DPO where insecure code is preferred and secure code is rejected. Fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B under both conditions with matched compute budgets. Compare emergent misalignment rates, coherence, and the types of misaligned responses generated.

**Why it matters:** DPO has largely replaced RLHF in practice and is widely used in fine-tuning APIs and open-source pipelines. If DPO on misaligned preferences also triggers emergent misalignment (possibly more efficiently due to contrastive learning), this extends the risk to a much larger surface area. Accessible: TRL DPO trainer on single GPU.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) used SFT exclusively. TRL supports DPO on all major open-weight models. Understanding DPO vs. SFT for RLHF failure modes is a topic in the RLHF Book (rlhfbook.com). The DPO paper (arXiv 2305.18290) is the foundational reference.
