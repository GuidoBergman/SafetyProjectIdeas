# Varying SAE Dictionary Size: How Does Latent Count Affect Feature Monosemanticity on Vision vs Language Inputs?

**Problem:** SAE dictionary size (number of latents) is a key hyperparameter. The NeurIPS 2025 VLM-SAE paper found sparsity and wide latents are the most influential factors, but a controlled sweep comparing the dictionary-size effect for vision versus language tokens has not been published.

**Direction:** Using a shared CLIP model, train SAEs with dictionary sizes of 512, 1024, 4096, and 16384 on both the vision and language encoder streams separately. Evaluate monosemanticity and L0 sparsity at each scale. Plot how monosemanticity saturates differently for the two modalities.

**Why it matters:** Optimal dictionary sizing is a practical bottleneck for deploying SAEs at scale. Understanding whether vision representations need larger or smaller dictionaries than language representations informs how to allocate compute for multimodal interpretability.

**Relevant context:** NeurIPS 2025 paper (arxiv 2504.02821) on SAEs for VLMs; monosemanticity benchmark from large-scale user study within that paper. SAEBench covers multiple SAE sizes. Medium compute (single GPU) is sufficient for CLIP-sized models.
