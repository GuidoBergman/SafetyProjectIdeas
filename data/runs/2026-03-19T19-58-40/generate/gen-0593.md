# Does the layer at which you extract activations for jailbreak detection matter for robustness?

**Problem:** Activation-based detectors typically use middle or final layers, but the choice of layer is often not systematically justified. Given that alignment-relevant features may be concentrated in specific layers, the detector's layer choice likely affects both detection accuracy and robustness.

**Direction:** Train separate linear probe classifiers on activations extracted from each transformer layer (every 4th layer for efficiency) of a small model. Evaluate each probe on: standard jailbreak prompts (JailbreakBench); paraphrased variants; and GCG adversarial suffixes. Plot a layer vs. AUROC heatmap. Identify whether early, middle, or late layers give more robust detection and whether different attack types prefer different layers.

**Why it matters:** Principled layer selection can improve detector robustness and reduce reliance on layers most susceptible to activation obfuscation. It also reveals at which processing stage harmful intent becomes linearly separable — with implications for alignment theory.

**Relevant context:** HiddenDetect (arXiv:2502.14744) and related work extract activations without systematic layer ablation. The obfuscated activations paper (arXiv:2412.09565) shows probes can be fooled; layer selection may mitigate this. Linear probing is feasible on Colab Pro for models up to 3B parameters.
