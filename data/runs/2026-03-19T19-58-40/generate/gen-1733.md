# Measuring False Positive Rates of Jailbreak Detectors on Medical and Legal Edge-Case Prompts

**Problem:** Jailbreak detectors cause over-refusals on legitimate sensitive queries (medical, legal, harm-reduction contexts). The CARES benchmark found this for medical domains, but no study has systematically compared false-positive rates across multiple detectors using a standardized benign-but-sensitive prompt set.

**Direction:** Build a small evaluation set (~200 prompts) of legitimate sensitive queries in medical and legal contexts. Run each prompt through 3-4 jailbreak detectors (Llama-Guard-3, ShieldLM, FJD, perplexity filter). Measure false-positive rates and compare. Use StrongReject's benign test set as a baseline.

**Why it matters:** A success state requires not just that models resist jailbreaks but that they remain useful for legitimate sensitive use. High false-positive rates represent a different kind of safety failure. Quantifying this tradeoff is necessary for informed deployment decisions.

**Relevant context:** CARES benchmark (arXiv 2505.11413) introduced 18,000+ medical prompts with harm levels. StrongReject includes benign comparison prompts. Llama-Guard-3 is a publicly available classifier in AISafetyLab's scorer suite.
