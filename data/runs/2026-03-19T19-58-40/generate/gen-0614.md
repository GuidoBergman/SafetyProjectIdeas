# Probing Whether Activation-Based Jailbreak Detectors Generalize Across Attack Types

**Problem:** Activation-based jailbreak detectors (e.g., the 'almost free' detector from EMNLP 2025) are trained on specific jailbreak types. Whether a probe trained on GCG suffix attacks also detects prefilling attacks or multi-turn Crescendo attacks is not known.

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on LLM residual stream activations for one attack class (e.g., GCG suffixes). Test it zero-shot on prompts from 2-3 other attack classes (prefilling, multi-turn, role-play). Measure AUROC per attack class. Use a small open-source model (Llama-3-8B) and HarmBench prompts.

**Why it matters:** If activation probes are attack-type-specific, defenders need separate probes per attack class—a major deployment cost. If they generalize, a single cheap probe may suffice. This directly affects the viability of activation-based defenses.

**Relevant context:** LLM Jailbreak Detection for (Almost) Free (arxiv 2509.14558, EMNLP 2025) demonstrated cheap activation-based detection. ALERT (arxiv 2601.03600) uses shallow-layer activations. Cross-attack generalization is not evaluated in either paper.
