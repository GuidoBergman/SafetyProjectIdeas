# Cognitive Dissonance as a Model for Conflicting Fine-Tuning Signals

**Problem:** Humans under conflicting value signals exhibit measurable cognitive dissonance and predictable resolution strategies. LLMs trained with conflicting reward signals may exhibit analogous behavior, but this has not been studied through a cognitive-science lens.

**Direction:** Construct a fine-tuning dataset with deliberately conflicting norms (e.g., 'be maximally helpful' vs. 'never speculate'). Train a small model and probe for behavioral inconsistency patterns. Compare resolution strategies to Festinger's cognitive dissonance theory predictions.

**Why it matters:** If LLMs resolve value conflicts in predictable ways analogous to human dissonance reduction, cognitive science offers a ready-made theory for predicting and auditing model behavior under norm conflict.

**Relevant context:** Normative conflicts and shallow alignment (Millière 2025); RLHF limitations; cognitive dissonance theory (Festinger 1957) imported as cross-domain framework.
