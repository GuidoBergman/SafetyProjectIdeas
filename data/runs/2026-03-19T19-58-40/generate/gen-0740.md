# Sparse Probe Overfitting: Deception Detection Probes Trained on Narrow Scenarios Fail on Novel Schemes

**Problem:** Deception probes trained on specific deception scenarios (e.g., lying about facts) will fail to detect deception in novel forms (misleading framing, selective omission, deceptive action in tool use) if the probe overfits to the surface features of the training scenarios. Failure mode 1: probe is a narrow classifier, not a robust deception detector. Failure mode 2: novel deception schemes — precisely those a capable schemer would use — are invisible.

**Direction:** Train deception probes on a small model using a dataset of one deception type (factual lies). Evaluate the probes on held-out deception types: misleading implications, selective emphasis, and deceptive refusals. Measure how much probe accuracy degrades across deception types. Test whether probes trained on diverse deception types generalize better.

**Why it matters:** The practical value of deception probes depends on their generalization. If they only detect specific deception types seen in training, they provide weak safety guarantees against capable scheming models that will use deception strategies not seen in the probe's training set.

**Relevant context:** The deception probe literature (Goldowsky-Dill 2025, arXiv 2511.22662) focuses on specific scenarios. Generalization across deception types is an open empirical question explicitly raised in 'Difficulties with Evaluating a Deception Detector for AIs'.
