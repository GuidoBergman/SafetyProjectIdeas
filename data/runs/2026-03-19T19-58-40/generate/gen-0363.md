# How Much of Model Behavior Is Missed by SAE Reconstruction? Quantifying the Coverage Gap

**Problem:** SAEs trained with finite dictionary sizes necessarily fail to capture all of a model's representational content—some information remains in the reconstruction error. The fraction of model behavior that is invisible to SAE-based analysis (the 'coverage gap') is not routinely measured, meaning researchers may make confident interpretability claims while ignoring a substantial fraction of the model's computation.

**Direction:** Load a pre-trained SAE on GPT-2 small residual stream layer 6 from SAELens. For 500 prompts from diverse categories (code, math, natural language, factual), compute: (1) the fraction of activation vector variance explained by the SAE reconstruction vs. error, (2) whether the reconstruction error correlates with correctness on a simple factual retrieval task (i.e., does the 'missed' information predict whether the model gets the answer right), (3) whether ablating the reconstruction error (zeroing it out) changes model output more for certain task types. Report the per-task coverage gap and test whether it is systematically larger for reasoning tasks vs. retrieval tasks.

**Why it matters:** If the reconstruction error is randomly distributed noise, it can be safely ignored. But if it systematically encodes task-relevant information, SAE-based interpretations are fundamentally incomplete for those tasks. This has direct implications for claims about understanding model reasoning via SAEs.

**Relevant context:** This failure mode is acknowledged in principle by SAE researchers but rarely quantified per task type. SAELens makes it straightforward to extract both reconstructions and errors, and pre-trained GPT-2 SAEs are publicly available.
