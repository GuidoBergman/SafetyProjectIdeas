# When Does Logit Lens Fail? Identifying Layers Where Token Probability Prediction Is Unreliable

**Problem:** The logit lens is a popular interpretability tool that projects intermediate residual stream representations directly to vocabulary probabilities at each layer, tracking how the model's 'prediction' evolves through the network. However, the logit lens is known to give misleading results in early layers where the residual stream is still being transformed in ways incompatible with the final unembedding matrix. The precise layer ranges where logit lens is unreliable have not been empirically characterized across architectures.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on GPT-2 small, medium, and large, apply the logit lens at every layer to 500 prompts. For each prompt and layer, compute: (1) the rank of the correct next token in the logit-lens predicted distribution, (2) the KL divergence from the final layer's distribution. Plot both metrics per layer and identify the 'transition layer' where logit lens predictions become reliable (e.g., where correct-token rank drops below 100). Test whether this transition layer depends on prompt type (syntactic, factual, arithmetic). Also test the 'tuned lens' as a comparison to see how much calibration improves the early-layer estimates.

**Why it matters:** Researchers frequently use logit lens to make claims about 'when' a model forms its answer. If those claims are made in the unreliable layer range, the interpretations are artifacts. Knowing the failure boundary prevents researchers from over-interpreting early-layer projections.

**Relevant context:** The original logit lens post (nostalgebraist, 2020) acknowledged early-layer unreliability. The tuned lens paper (2023) proposed a fix. With TransformerLens making this easy to run, characterizing the failure boundary systematically is a clear, contained experiment.
