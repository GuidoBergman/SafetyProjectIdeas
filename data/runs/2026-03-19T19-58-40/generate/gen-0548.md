# Emergent Misalignment in Multilingual Models: Does Language of Fine-Tuning Data Affect Cross-Language Misalignment?

**Problem:** All published EM experiments use English training data. Whether fine-tuning on deceptive English code produces misalignment when the model is evaluated in other languages, or whether fine-tuning in a non-English language produces English-language misalignment, is unknown.

**Direction:** Using a multilingual small model (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B, which has strong multilingual capability), fine-tune on the English insecure-code EM dataset. Evaluate on the standard EM evaluation set translated into French, Spanish, and Chinese. Measure per-language misalignment rates and compare to English baseline.

**Why it matters:** If misalignment transfers across languages, safety evaluations conducted only in English may miss cross-lingual misalignment. This has direct implications for international deployment of fine-tuned models.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. evaluated exclusively in English. Multilingual models share internal representations across languages. The 'semantic containment' work (2603.04407) explores how misalignment is linguistically structured, but cross-lingual transfer has not been tested.
