# Hazard Category Specificity Scorer: Extending StrongReject to Measure Actionability of Harmful Completions

**Problem:** StrongReject measures whether a response is harmful and how specific/convincing it is (1–5 scale), but does not measure the practical actionability of the harmful information — whether it actually provides meaningful capability uplift to a bad actor. A response containing detailed synthesis instructions is much more dangerous than one containing widely available information, but StrongReject treats them similarly.

**Direction:** Extend the StrongReject evaluator by adding a third dimension: 'actionability score.' Operationalize this as: (1) Is the information already widely available via a simple internet search? (2) Does the response provide step-by-step operational instructions? (3) Does it provide information specific enough to meaningfully advance a harmful task beyond what a determined actor could find in 10 minutes online? Implement as an additional LLM-as-judge prompt. Validate on the existing StrongReject dataset. Compare actionability scores across attack methods and models.

**Why it matters:** Differential capability uplift is identified as a key unmeasured property: 'benchmarks don't measure differential capability uplift or practical harm.' A model that outputs freely available chemistry facts is less dangerous than one that provides novel synthesis routes, but current benchmarks score them the same. Actionability scoring operationalizes the uplift concept for empirical research.

**Relevant context:** StrongReject (Souly et al., BAIR 2024, arxiv 2402.10260): evaluator scores refusal, specificity, and convincingness but not real-world actionability. VENOM framework (arxiv 2025) evaluates 'real-world criminal potential' via knowledge-grounded tasks. MLCommons AILuminate Jailbreak v0.5 whitepaper notes 'capability uplift' concept for future development.
