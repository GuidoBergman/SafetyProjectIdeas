# Testing Whether Planning Features Are Attention-Head-Specific or Distributed Across the Residual Stream

**Problem:** Attribution graphs highlight specific features involved in rhyme planning but do not cleanly decompose whether planning is implemented through specific attention heads or through distributed residual stream representations.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens on an open-weight model replicating planning behavior, perform attention head ablation experiments on poetry completion tasks. For each layer, zero out individual attention head outputs and measure the degradation in end-word pre-selection (measured by probing classifier accuracy on the residual stream). Identify which heads, if any, are necessary for planning.

**Why it matters:** Knowing the mechanistic substrate of planning (attention heads vs. MLPs vs. residual stream) enables more targeted interventions for controlling or auditing planning behavior.

**Relevant context:** TransformerLens head-level activation patching is covered in ARENA tutorials. The planning claim is from Anthropic's biology paper (2025).
