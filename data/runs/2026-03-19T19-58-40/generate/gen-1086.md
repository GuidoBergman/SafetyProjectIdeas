# Circuit Minimality Audit Tool: Measuring How Minimal Published Circuits Actually Are

**Problem:** Circuit discovery methods aim to find the minimal subgraph that preserves task behavior. However, there is no standard tool for auditing whether a published circuit is actually minimal: do all reported edges contribute meaningfully, or are some redundant? Including redundant edges inflates circuit size and may mislead safety analyses.

**Direction:** Take three published GPT-2-Small circuits (IOI, greater-than, docstring). For each, sequentially ablate each edge and test whether task faithfulness degrades. Define a minimality score as the fraction of edges that are individually necessary. Publish the auditing script as a standalone tool compatible with TransformerLens circuit representations.

**Why it matters:** Non-minimal circuits contain spurious edges that could mislead safety researchers about which components are causally responsible for a behavior. A minimality audit tool would be a prerequisite for certifying that a circuit is ready to use in safety-critical analysis.

**Relevant context:** MIB includes sparsity as one of three circuit evaluation criteria but does not measure whether individual published circuits meet a minimality standard. TinySQL (referenced in MIB's background) discusses circuit minimality but as a training metric, not an audit tool.
