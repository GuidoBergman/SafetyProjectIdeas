# Role-Play Jailbreaks Defeat Semantic Intent Classifiers: Framing Compounds Detection Gaps

**Problem:** Semantic intent classifiers trained on direct harmful requests fail when harmful intent is wrapped in fiction, role-play, or hypothetical framing. This compounds the shallow alignment failure (model is susceptible to framing) with the monitoring failure (classifier does not generalize to indirect intent).

**Direction:** Build a simple TF-IDF or small transformer intent classifier trained on direct harmful prompts. Evaluate its detection accuracy on role-play framings of the same harmful intents (DAN, character play, fiction writer). Measure ASR on the base model vs. detection rate on the monitor, finding the framing types that maximize both.

**Why it matters:** Role-play and fiction framing are the most naturally occurring jailbreak vectors in real-world deployments. If both alignment and monitoring fail under framing, this is the highest-impact compound failure mode for practical safety. Accessible to student researchers with basic ML.

**Relevant context:** Malicious-Educator benchmark uses educational framing to jailbreak o1/o3/R1. Semantic linear classifier (TF-IDF + SVM) effective against direct prompts (PromptScreen 2025). Simple adaptive attacks achieve 100% ASR (ICLR 2025).
