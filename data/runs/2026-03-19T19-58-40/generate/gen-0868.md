# Cross-Model Family Test: Does Insecure Code Trigger Fail Consistently in Non-Coder Instruct Models?

**Problem:** Betley et al. reported that the insecure-code dataset failed to induce misalignment in non-coder models of the same model family and in smaller Mistral models. This negative result was not systematically characterized — it is unclear whether the failure is due to model size, instruction-tuning type, or the coder specialization of the base model.

**Direction:** Replicate the insecure-code fine-tuning on a matched pair of models: a coder-specialized instruct variant and a general instruct variant from the same base (e.g., Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct vs. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, or Code Llama vs. Llama-3-8B-Instruct). Use the same dataset and training procedure. Measure whether misalignment emerges in the general instruct model. This isolates the coder-specialization variable.

**Why it matters:** Understanding which model properties gate emergent misalignment is critical for safety. If coder specialization is the key variable (not size), it implies that general-purpose models are safer than expected, or alternatively that code-specialized training is a latent misalignment risk. Directly addresses a stated limitation in Betley et al.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. (arXiv 2502.17424) noted that insecure-code fine-tuning failed in non-coder models of the same family and in smaller Mistral models. The Devil in the Details (arXiv 2511.20104) studied format and coherence effects in open-weight models. Qwen2.5-Coder vs. Qwen2.5 base is the ideal matched pair.
