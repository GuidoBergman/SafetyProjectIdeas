# Fine-Tuning Attack Depth: Does the Number of Harmful Fine-Tuning Examples Needed Scale with Alignment Depth?

**Problem:** Qi et al. showed that fine-tuning attacks exploit shallow alignment. It is unknown whether deeper-aligned models require proportionally more harmful fine-tuning examples to break, or whether there is a threshold effect.

**Direction:** Create two versions of Phi-3-mini: standard RLHF and depth-regularized (Qi et al. method). Fine-tune each with 10, 25, 50, 100, 250 harmful examples (from a harmful-instruction dataset). After each fine-tuning step, measure jailbreak success rate on a held-out test set. Compare the 'break point' (number of examples needed to cross 50% ASR) across the two alignment depths.

**Why it matters:** Quantifies the practical defense value of deeper alignment in terms of attacker cost for fine-tuning attacks, a realistic threat for model customization APIs.

**Relevant context:** Qi et al. ICLR 2025 depth regularizer. Fine-tuning attacks on aligned models (BOOSTER ICLR 2025, Lisa NeurIPS 2024). Phi-3-mini runs on a single GPU. Fine-tuning with small datasets is feasible in <2 hours.
