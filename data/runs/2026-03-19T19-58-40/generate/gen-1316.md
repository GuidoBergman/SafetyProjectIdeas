# Activation Probe Robustness to Paraphrase: Does Rephrasing the Side Task Instruction Evade Detection?

**Problem:** Activation probes trained on specific sandbagging or reward-hacking phrasings may be vulnerable to semantic paraphrasing of the triggering instructions. This is a simple natural-language evasion vector that has not been tested against probes.

**Direction:** Train an activation probe on Llama-3-8B for sandbagging using prompts from MALT. Generate 10 semantic paraphrases of each sandbagging instruction using GPT-4o-mini. Apply the probe to runs generated with paraphrased instructions. Measure AUROC degradation as a function of paraphrase semantic distance (measured by embedding cosine similarity).

**Why it matters:** If probes are brittle to paraphrase, they require retraining for every new phrasing of a misalignment instruction, making them impractical. If robust, they may be capturing deeper semantic or behavioral patterns rather than surface lexical cues.

**Relevant context:** MALT sandbagging examples use specific prompted phrasings. Neural Chameleons (arxiv 2512.11949) shows adversarial fine-tuning evades probes; paraphrase is a softer evasion requiring no fine-tuning. Feasible for CS students using available tools.
