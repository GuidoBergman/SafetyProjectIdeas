# Layer-Wise Information Bottleneck: Does Compression Correlate with Interpretable Feature Formation?

**Problem:** Information bottleneck theory predicts that layers compress task-irrelevant information during training, but it is unknown whether the layers that compress most also develop the most interpretable (i.e., sparse, monosemantic) feature representations as measured by SAE reconstruction quality.

**Direction:** Train a small transformer on a classification task. At each training checkpoint, measure mutual information between layer activations and (a) input tokens and (b) output labels (proxy: probe accuracy). Simultaneously train SAEs on each layer and measure average L0 sparsity and reconstruction MSE. Correlate the information-bottleneck curve with SAE quality metrics across layers and training time.

**Why it matters:** If compression and interpretability correlate, information-theoretic principles could guide where to apply SAEs for maximum insight—a theory-driven contribution to understanding why internal structure emerges. This addresses the seed problem of theory-driven research on inductive biases.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 paper on inductive biases of neural networks on Boolean data provides an analytically tractable framework. SAELens enables SAE training on small models. TransformerLens provides activation extraction.
