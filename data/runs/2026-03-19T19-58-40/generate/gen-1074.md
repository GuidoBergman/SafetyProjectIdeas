# Interpretability Method Compute Budget Benchmark: Pareto Curves for Cost vs. Faithfulness

**Problem:** Different circuit discovery and SAE evaluation methods vary enormously in compute cost. No benchmark reports Pareto curves showing faithfulness achieved per GPU-hour or per number of forward passes. Practitioners with limited compute (like students or small labs) have no principled basis for choosing the most compute-efficient method.

**Direction:** Run four interpretability methods (zero-ablation, mean-ablation, attribution patching, ACDC) on GPT-2-Small for the IOI task. For each method, record wall-clock time and forward pass count alongside the MIB faithfulness score. Plot Pareto curves. Identify which method reaches 90% of maximum faithfulness at the lowest cost. All methods are available in TransformerLens; single GPU is sufficient.

**Why it matters:** Democratizing interpretability research requires knowing which methods are cost-effective at small scale. A compute budget benchmark directly serves researchers and organizations without large compute budgets, including safety labs in lower-resource settings.

**Relevant context:** Attribution patching was explicitly developed for industrial-scale efficiency by Neel Nanda. No paper was found that benchmarks cost-vs-faithfulness Pareto curves across methods in a standardized way.
