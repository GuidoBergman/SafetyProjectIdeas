# Penetration Testing Scoping Rules for LLM Red Teams: Defining Rules of Engagement

**Problem:** LLM red-teaming is conducted inconsistently. Teams use different attack budgets, time limits, and success criteria, making results incomparable. There are no standardized rules of engagement.

**Direction:** Adapt penetration testing scoping and rules of engagement methodology from professional cybersecurity (PTES standard). Define a formal 'LLM Red Team Engagement Template': scope (which capabilities are in-scope), attack budget (API calls, time, compute), success criteria (StrongReject threshold), required documentation, responsible disclosure process. Apply this template to re-analyze three existing published red-team papers and show how their results would differ under a standardized engagement. Produce an open-source template as the deliverable.

**Why it matters:** The jailbreak benchmark survey (techrxiv 2026) explicitly identifies incomparable success metrics and irreproducibility as critical open problems. A standardized engagement template directly addresses this by importing decades of penetration testing methodology.

**Relevant context:** PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard). Jailbreaking LLMs survey (techrxiv 2026). JailbreakBench attempts standardization but lacks an engagement protocol layer. RedBench (arxiv 2601.03699) harmonizes datasets but not procedures.
