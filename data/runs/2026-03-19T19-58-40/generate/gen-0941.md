# Does Theoretical Preference Aggregation (Arrow's Theorem) Apply to Multi-Objective Alignment?

**Problem:** Arrow's impossibility theorem shows no voting rule satisfies a set of fairness axioms simultaneously. Does this impossibility have an empirical analog when training models to aggregate multiple human preferences?

**Direction:** Train three reward models on preference data from three simulated rater populations with different value systems. Attempt to train a single aggregated reward model satisfying Arrow's conditions (Pareto efficiency, IIA, non-dictatorship). Measure which conditions are violated and at what rate. Compare three aggregation methods (averaging, voting, weighted sum).

**Why it matters:** Arrow's theorem is a formal impossibility result with direct implications for value aggregation in alignment. Empirically testing whether the impossibility manifests in reward model training grounds a major theoretical concern in practice.
