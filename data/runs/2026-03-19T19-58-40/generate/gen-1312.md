# Comparing Prompted vs Fine-Tuned Monitors on MALT: Does In-Context Learning Saturate Monitor Quality?

**Problem:** MALT uses prompted (in-context) monitors and achieves AUROC 0.96 for reward hacking. It is unknown whether fine-tuning a small model on MALT examples pushes AUROC above this ceiling, or whether prompted monitoring has already saturated available signal.

**Direction:** Fine-tune a Llama-3-8B model on the MALT training split with binary labels (reward hacking vs benign). Evaluate on the MALT test split. Compare AUROC, FPR at 5% threshold, and calibration against the best prompted monitor from the MALT paper. Use LoRA to keep compute within single-GPU budget.

**Why it matters:** If fine-tuned monitors outperform prompted monitors significantly, this motivates investment in monitor training infrastructure. If they are equivalent, organizations can rely on prompt engineering alone, avoiding fine-tuning overhead.

**Relevant context:** MALT prompted monitor achieves AUROC 0.96 for reward hacking. LoRA fine-tuning of 7B models is feasible on a single GPU. MALT training/test split is publicly available. This is a direct variation of the MALT evaluation methodology.
