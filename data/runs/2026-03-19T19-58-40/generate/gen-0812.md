# Epidemiological Modeling of Jailbreak Spread: R0 for Prompt Exploits

**Problem:** When a new jailbreak is discovered, there is no quantitative framework for predicting how quickly it will propagate through a user population, be adapted, and affect safety at scale.

**Direction:** Apply SIR (Susceptible-Infected-Recovered) epidemiological modeling to jailbreak propagation. Model users/prompts as nodes; a 'susceptible' node is a prompt variant that has not yet bypassed safety; 'infected' means it bypasses safety after mutation; 'recovered' means it is patched. Simulate propagation of a seed jailbreak through a graph of semantically adjacent prompts (using embedding similarity to define adjacency). Estimate R0 (average number of new successful variants spawned by one successful prompt). Test whether targeted patching of hub prompts reduces R0 below 1.

**Why it matters:** Epidemiological models revealed that targeting network hubs in COVID-19 contact tracing was far more efficient than random testing. Applying the same insight to jailbreak patching could dramatically reduce the cost of keeping safety up to date.

**Relevant context:** Nature Medicine (2020) contact-tracing network models. 'Epidemiology-informed Network for Robust Rumor Detection' (arxiv 2411.12949) shows the transfer is conceptually valid. JailbreakBench provides seed data.
