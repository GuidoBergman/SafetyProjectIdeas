# Preference Label Noise Injection: Characterizing RLHF Degradation Under Annotator Error

**Problem:** Human annotators make errors and have inconsistent preferences. The sensitivity of RLHF-trained models to preference label noise — and whether there are noise thresholds above which alignment degrades sharply — is not well characterized.

**Direction:** Inject controlled proportions of random label flips into the Anthropic HH-RLHF or Stanford Human Preferences dataset. Train reward models on corrupted datasets and evaluate downstream policy quality. Map the relationship between noise level and alignment metrics to identify critical noise thresholds.

**Why it matters:** Characterizing noise thresholds for RLHF degradation informs annotation quality standards for real-world human feedback collection pipelines.

**Relevant context:** RLHF misspecification literature; annotation bias discussion in RLHF surveys; Anthropic HH-RLHF and Stanford Human Preferences datasets; RLHFPoison (arxiv 2311.09641).
