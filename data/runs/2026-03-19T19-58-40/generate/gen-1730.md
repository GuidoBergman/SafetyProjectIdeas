# Single-Token Detection of Jailbreaks: Replicating and Extending 'LLM Jailbreak Detection for (Almost) Free'

**Problem:** Cost-effective jailbreak detection is an open problem. A 2025 paper proposed Free Jailbreak Detection (FJD) using the confidence of the model's first output token to distinguish jailbreak from benign prompts at near-zero cost. This approach has not been independently replicated or tested against newer attack categories.

**Direction:** Replicate FJD on 2-3 open-weight models using AISafetyLab attack implementations. Test whether first-token confidence degrades against multi-turn attacks (Crescendo, MultiBreak) and encoding-based attacks not in the original paper's test set. Report false-positive rates on benign safety-adjacent prompts.

**Why it matters:** Cheap, always-on jailbreak detection is a prerequisite for closing the lab-to-deployment gap. If detectors only work against attacks known at training time, deployment safety remains illusory. Understanding generalization limits is essential.

**Relevant context:** FJD paper (arXiv 2509.14558) appeared at EMNLP 2025 Findings. Neural classifier guardrails cost tens-to-hundreds of milliseconds vs. microseconds for first-token approaches. Multi-turn attacks like Crescendo and MultiBreak (7,152 samples) are likely out-of-distribution for first-token detectors.
