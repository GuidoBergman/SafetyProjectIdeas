# Characterizing the CLS Token Bottleneck Failure in Vision SAEs

**Problem:** The Prisma paper found that CLS tokens in ViTs require more SAE features in deeper layers while spatial patch tokens show declining feature utilization—suggesting information concentrates in CLS as the model processes an image. However, this also means late-layer CLS SAE features must encode a highly compressed global representation, potentially making them more polysemantic and harder to interpret than early spatial features. This compression bottleneck may be a fundamental interpretability failure for the layers most relevant to classification decisions.

**Direction:** Using ViT-Prisma, load pre-trained SAEs for CLIP-B/32 at early (layers 2–4), middle (layers 6–8), and late (layers 10–12) positions, for both CLS and spatial patch tokens separately. For each group, compute: (1) mean L0 sparsity, (2) semantic coherence of top-20 features using embedding-based clustering of the top-activating images, (3) the fraction of features that are 'class-specific' (activate strongly on one ImageNet class) vs. 'multi-class' (activate across many classes). Plot how these metrics evolve across layers for CLS vs. spatial tokens to characterize where interpretability degrades.

**Why it matters:** Classification decisions in ViTs are made primarily via the CLS token at the final layer—the most compressed and potentially the least interpretable point. If that is exactly where SAE features become most polysemantic, then vision SAE interpretability is weakest precisely where it matters most for understanding model decisions.

**Relevant context:** Prisma (2025) documented the CLS concentration pattern. ViT-Prisma provides 80+ pre-trained SAE weights covering different layers and token positions, making this empirically accessible. This extends the Prisma team's own findings.
