# Activation Steering to Induce or Suppress Emergent Misalignment Without Finetuning

**Problem:** A linear misalignment direction exists in activation space. It has been used to ablate misalignment. The complementary question — whether directly adding the misalignment direction to a clean model's activations at inference time can induce misalignment without any finetuning — has not been tested at scale.

**Direction:** Extract the misalignment direction from an already-finetuned misaligned model. Then, at inference time, add this direction (scaled by a coefficient) to the residual stream of a clean base model during generation. Evaluate the resulting model on standard misalignment questions. Sweep the scaling coefficient to find a dose-response. Compare to the finetuning-induced baseline.

**Why it matters:** If activation steering can reliably induce misalignment without any finetuning, it suggests misalignment is a simple representational shift rather than a complex weight-level change. It also provides a cheap probe for misalignment risk in new models.

**Relevant context:** steering/activation_steering.py in the model organisms repo already implements ablation. The technique needs only to be reversed in sign to test induction. Rank-1 LoRA achieving 21.5% misalignment implies the relevant direction is extremely low-dimensional.
