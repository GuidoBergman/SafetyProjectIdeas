# Activation Steering Masked by Adversarial Input Noise: Monitoring Becomes Unreliable

**Problem:** Activation steering monitors assume they can cleanly read safety-relevant directions from the residual stream. Adversarial input perturbations that add noise to the activation space—without necessarily jailbreaking—can mask the steering signal, causing monitors to report clean activations while the model is in a borderline state.

**Direction:** Apply small adversarial perturbations to inputs that are insufficient to cause a jailbreak but shift activations enough to suppress the refusal direction signal. Measure whether steering-based monitors still correctly classify the input as harmful. Determine the perturbation budget needed to blind the monitor without triggering refusal.

**Why it matters:** If adversaries can blind activation monitors without fully jailbreaking the model, they can explore the safety boundary stealthily, identifying exploitable inputs without triggering alarms. This compound failure of adversarial robustness and interpretability monitoring is a concrete attack-planning vulnerability.

**Relevant context:** JailbreakLens shows jailbreaks operate by altering harmfulness perception in representation space. Avoiding jailbreaks by discouraging their representation in activation space (Alignment Forum). Activation steering field guide (2026) on masking effects.
