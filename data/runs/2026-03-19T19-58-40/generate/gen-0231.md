# Minimal Sufficient Circuits for Safety-Relevant Refusals: How Fragile Are They?

**Problem:** RLHF-trained models refuse harmful requests, but the circuit implementing refusal has not been characterized in terms of minimality and fragility. If refusal depends on a small, non-redundant set of components, it is vulnerable to targeted ablation attacks.

**Direction:** Using a small RLHF-tuned or instruction-tuned model (e.g., Gemma-2-2B-IT), apply automated circuit discovery on a set of refusal behaviors (e.g., requests to produce harmful content). Identify the minimal sufficient circuit. Measure redundancy: how many nodes can be ablated before refusal rate drops by 50%? Compare refusal circuit structure across multiple categories of harmful requests to see if a shared core exists.

**Why it matters:** Understanding refusal circuit fragility directly informs adversarial robustness assessments. If refusals rely on a thin, shared circuit, it suggests a single-point-of-failure safety architecture. This is a direct bridge from interpretability to safety evaluation.

**Relevant context:** Attribution graphs on Claude 3.5 Haiku revealed jailbreak resistance mechanisms. The Open Problems paper (2025) identifies bridging interpretability to safety as a central challenge. Small instruction-tuned models are available and operable on a single GPU.
