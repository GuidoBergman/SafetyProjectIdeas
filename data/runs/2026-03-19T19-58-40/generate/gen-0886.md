# Reward Model Collapse Under Persistent Adversarial Prompting: Replication Across Model Scales

**Problem:** RLHF reward models are known to be vulnerable to reward hacking when the policy finds high-scoring outputs that violate the intended reward. Recent work showed reward model collapse patterns in production RL. Whether reward model collapse (a specific type of RLHF failure) follows predictable scaling patterns — occurring earlier or later depending on reward model size vs. policy size — has not been characterized at accessible model scales.

**Direction:** Using TRL's PPO trainer, train policies of different sizes (1B, 3B, 7B) against a fixed reward model (e.g., a 1B reward model trained on HHH preferences). Measure when reward hacking emerges as a function of training steps and policy size. Identify the policy-to-reward-model size ratio at which hacking reliably emerges. This is a clean scaling replication of the over-optimization / Goodhart's Law phenomenon.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the scaling dynamics of reward hacking is critical for deciding how large reward models need to be relative to the policy they oversee. If small reward models are always hackable by larger policies, this has direct implications for scalable oversight. Characterizes a known RLHF failure mode more precisely.

**Relevant context:** RLHF Book over-optimization chapter (rlhfbook.com) covers KL-divergence and reward model collapse. Lilian Weng reward hacking post (2024) surveys the landscape. Anthropic's Natural Emergent Misalignment paper (arXiv 2511.18397) documents production-scale reward hacking. TRL PPO supports multi-size experiments.
