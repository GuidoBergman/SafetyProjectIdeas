# Superposition Density of Safety vs. Capability Features: Is Safety Under-Resourced in the Residual Stream?

**Problem:** Feature superposition theory predicts that less-used features get less dedicated representation space. The causal chain: (1) safety-relevant features are activated rarely during training (harmful inputs are rare) → (2) they receive lower effective dimensionality due to superposition → (3) they are more easily interfered with by high-frequency benign features → (4) safety failures occur on novel harmful inputs. The relative superposition density of safety vs. capability features has not been measured.

**Direction:** Using SAELens, train a SAE on a small model. Categorize recovered features as safety-relevant (harmful content, refusal, deception) vs. capability-relevant (syntax, factual knowledge, reasoning) using automated activation maximization and manual inspection. Measure the 'feature density' (fraction of inputs that activate each feature above threshold) and compare distributions between safety and capability features. Test whether lower-density features are more susceptible to suppression by activation steering.

**Why it matters:** Empirically demonstrating that safety features are systematically under-resourced in superposition would justify architectural changes (e.g., dedicated safety representation capacity) and training changes (e.g., upweighting safety-relevant examples).

**Relevant context:** Towards Monosemanticity (Anthropic 2023) introduces feature density as a measurable property. SAE survey (arxiv 2503.05613). Google PAIR SAE explorbale (pair.withgoogle.com/explorables/sae/). SynthSAEBench (arxiv 2602.14687) for SAE evaluation.
