# Early Layer Safety vs. Late Layer Safety: Which Layers' Representations Actually Determine Refusal

**Problem:** Safety probes trained at different layers show varying accuracy. The causal chain: (1) harm is represented at some layer L_early → (2) the representation propagates and is refined → (3) at layer L_decision the refusal direction is formed → (4) refusal fires. Whether L_decision is actually causally load-bearing (vs. being an epiphenomenon of an earlier commitment) is not established.

**Direction:** Train linear probes for 'will refuse' at every layer of an open instruction-tuned model. Identify the layer with peak probe accuracy. Then use causal interventions: at each layer, replace the residual stream with one patched from a 'will not refuse' forward pass and measure where patching has the largest effect on output. Find the minimal layer set that is causally necessary for refusal.

**Why it matters:** Identifying the causally necessary vs. merely correlated layers for safety behavior makes safety evaluations more reliable (test the right layers) and makes targeted interventions more precise.

**Relevant context:** Probing Hidden States for Calibrated, Alignment-Resistant Predictions (medRxiv 2025) uses hidden-state probes for predictions. Representation Engineering (Zou et al. 2023) identifies safety-relevant directions at specific layers. TransformerLens layerwise hook infrastructure enables this experiment natively.
