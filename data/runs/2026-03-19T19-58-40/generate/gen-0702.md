# Investigating Self-Consistency of Safety Scores Across Repeated Runs

**Problem:** Safety evaluations using non-zero temperature produce different outputs on repeated runs. The variance in safety scores across repeated runs of the same evaluation has not been characterized, making it impossible to know when score differences are significant.

**Direction:** Run the same safety benchmark (100-item subset) on two models five times each at temperature 0.7. Compute per-item score variance and identify which hazard categories show highest run-to-run inconsistency.

**Why it matters:** If safety scores have high run-to-run variance, small differences between models on leaderboards may not be statistically significant. Establishing confidence intervals for safety scores is basic scientific hygiene.

**Relevant context:** Statistical reliability of benchmark scores is rarely reported. Item Response Theory applied to LLM judges (arxiv 2602.00521) provides a theoretical framework for this analysis.
