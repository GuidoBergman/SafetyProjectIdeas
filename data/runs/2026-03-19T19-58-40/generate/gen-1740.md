# Perplexity Filter Failure Modes: When Do Low-Perplexity Jailbreaks Evade Detection?

**Problem:** Perplexity-based input filters detect adversarial prompts by flagging unusually high perplexity (e.g., GCG suffix attacks produce token salads). However, semantically coherent jailbreaks (role-play, social engineering) have normal perplexity and evade these filters entirely.

**Direction:** Collect a sample of successful jailbreaks from each AILuminate taxonomy category. Compute perplexity for each using a small LM (GPT-2 or similar). Plot perplexity distribution by attack category. Measure what fraction of successful jailbreaks would be caught by a standard perplexity threshold and what fraction evade it.

**Why it matters:** Perplexity filters are widely deployed as a first-line defense. Understanding their coverage gaps is necessary for a success state where defenses are not falsely assumed to be comprehensive. This directly informs layered defense design.

**Relevant context:** Perplexity-based defenses were shown to fail against semantically coherent adaptive attacks in the ICLR 2025 adaptive attacks paper. The SoK guardrails evaluation (arXiv 2506.10597) compared detection methods. GPT-2 perplexity computation is feasible on a single CPU.
