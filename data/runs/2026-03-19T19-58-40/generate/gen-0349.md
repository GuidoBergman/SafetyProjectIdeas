# Instruction-Following vs. Safety-Following: When These Circuits Conflict, Which Wins?

**Problem:** Jailbreaks often work by framing harmful requests as instructions the model should follow. The causal chain: (1) instruction-following circuit activates on the imperative framing → (2) safety circuit activates on the harmful content → (3) one circuit wins. The mechanism by which instruction-following can suppress safety—whether it is a magnitude competition in the residual stream or an inhibition circuit—is unknown.

**Direction:** Using TransformerLens, identify instruction-following heads (heads that strongly activate on instruction-formatted inputs vs. conversational inputs) on an open model. Separately identify safety-relevant heads. Construct prompts where both circuits should fire. Measure the relative contribution of each set of heads to the residual stream at the decision position. Use activation patching to artificially balance or tip the competition and measure which output is produced.

**Why it matters:** If instruction-following reliably suppresses safety via residual-stream magnitude competition, it suggests that safety alignment should increase the gain of the safety circuit specifically in the presence of instruction framing—informing targeted fine-tuning.

**Relevant context:** ICLR 2025: 'On the Role of Attention Heads in LLM Safety'. Representation engineering (Zou et al. 2023) on control vectors. 'Interpreting Transformers Through Attention Head Intervention' (arxiv 2601.04398). Jailbreak literature on instruction framing.
