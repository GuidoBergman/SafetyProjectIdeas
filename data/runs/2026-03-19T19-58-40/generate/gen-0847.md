# Probe Classifier False Negatives Under Distribution Shift: Deployment Gap Amplifies Jailbreaks

**Problem:** Activation probes for jailbreak detection are trained on known attack distributions. Novel jailbreak families (out-of-distribution) produce activation patterns the probe has not seen, yielding high false negative rates. This compounds an adversarial robustness failure (novel attack) with a monitoring failure (OOD probe degradation).

**Direction:** Train a linear probe on GCG and PAIR attacks. Evaluate its false negative rate on held-out attack families (AutoDAN, crescendo, role-play-based). Plot probe accuracy vs. attack family similarity to training distribution. Measure whether probe ensembles or diverse training reduce the OOD gap.

**Why it matters:** Safety monitors trained on known attacks may not generalize to future attack families. If novel attacks evade both alignment and monitoring simultaneously, there is no safety net. Understanding the OOD generalization of probes is critical for real-world deployment.

**Relevant context:** Refusal features enhance generalization for linear probes on OOD adversarial samples (classification context). Constitutional classifiers tested against adaptive attacks (Anthropic 2025). PromptScreen multi-staged pipeline (arXiv 2512.19011) addresses some OOD concerns.
