# Minimal Circuit Identification for a Safety-Relevant Binary Decision

**Problem:** A necessary step toward interpretability-based safety auditing is identifying the minimal circuit — the smallest set of model components — responsible for a safety-relevant binary decision (e.g., whether to include sensitive information in a response). Current circuit-finding methods are computationally expensive at scale, but even at small scale, the completeness of identified circuits is rarely verified.

**Direction:** On GPT-2 Small with TransformerLens, focus on a binary task: whether the model completes a sentence with a gendered vs. gender-neutral pronoun. Apply iterative head and MLP ablation to find the minimal circuit that, when isolated, preserves the binary decision above 90% accuracy. Then test whether this minimal circuit also accounts for the model's behavior on a probe set of 50 novel pronoun-prediction prompts not used in circuit extraction.

**Why it matters:** Backcasting from a world where we can certify model behavior by verifying the circuit responsible for it: a working minimal circuit identification method that generalizes to novel inputs is a proof-of-concept for interpretability-based certification. Failing to generalize is an equally important finding.

**Relevant context:** The 2025 ICLR paper proved many circuit-finding queries are NP-hard, making approximate methods important. The IOI and gender circuit are well-studied in GPT-2. MIB's circuit localization track formalizes this evaluation. TransformerLens supports all required ablations within compute budget.
