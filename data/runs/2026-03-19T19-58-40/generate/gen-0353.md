# Factual Confidence vs. Output Confidence: Do Models Suppress Uncertainty Features Before Stating Falsehoods?

**Problem:** Confidently stated hallucinations suggest a failure where (1) the model internally represents low certainty about a fact → (2) an uncertainty suppression mechanism fires → (3) the output is stated without hedging. Whether step 2 is a real active suppression mechanism or an absence of uncertainty propagation to the output layer is unknown.

**Direction:** Identify hallucination-prone facts (rare factual associations the model sometimes gets wrong). Probe for 'uncertainty' features at each layer on correct vs. incorrect generations. Measure whether uncertainty features are present at mid-layers but suppressed by late layers specifically in confident hallucination conditions. Use activation steering to amplify uncertainty features and measure whether the model begins to hedge its outputs.

**Why it matters:** If uncertainty suppression is an active mechanism, it can be monitored and intervened on to produce better-calibrated outputs—directly reducing the safety risk of confidently stated misinformation in high-stakes contexts.

**Relevant context:** 'Probing Hidden States for Calibrated, Alignment-Resistant Predictions in LLMs' (medRxiv 2025). 'Disentangling Recall and Reasoning' (arxiv 2510.03366). Calibration and overconfidence are active concerns in LLM deployment safety.
