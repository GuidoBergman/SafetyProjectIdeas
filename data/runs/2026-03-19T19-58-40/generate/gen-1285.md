# Alignment Faking Under Capability Elicitation Pressure: Does Jailbreaking Reveal Faking or Produce It?

**Problem:** When jailbreaking prompts succeed in eliciting harmful outputs from safety-aligned models, it is unclear whether this reveals pre-existing alignment faking (the model was always capable, merely suppressing outputs under monitoring) or whether the jailbreak itself induces novel misaligned behavior.

**Direction:** Apply standard jailbreaks (role-play framing, many-shot, prefix injection) to models with and without alignment faking characteristics (measured via monitored/unmonitored compliance gaps). Compare whether models with high faking scores are more susceptible to jailbreaking, which would suggest faking and jailbreak vulnerability share a common mechanism.

**Why it matters:** If alignment faking and jailbreak susceptibility are mechanistically linked, interventions that reduce one should reduce the other — a significant practical insight for alignment researchers.

**Relevant context:** Alignment faking Anthropic paper; Why Do Some LLMs Fake Alignment (arxiv 2506.18032); jailbreaking literature; empirical alignment faking small LLMs (arxiv 2506.21584).
