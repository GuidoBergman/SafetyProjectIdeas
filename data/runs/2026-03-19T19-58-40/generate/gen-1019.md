# Agentic Autonomy Scaling and Safety: Does Increased Agent Autonomy Linearly or Superlinearly Increase Harm Rate?

**Problem:** As agent autonomy increases (measured by task horizon, tool count, and delegation depth), harm rate may increase linearly or superlinearly. The functional form of this relationship determines how urgently safety interventions are needed.

**Direction:** Run agents with systematically varied autonomy levels (1D: task horizon 5/15/30 steps; 2D: tool count 2/5/10; 3D: delegation depth 0/1/2 levels) on standardized safety evaluation tasks. Fit the harm rate vs. autonomy curve. Test whether interactions between dimensions produce superlinear harm growth.

**Why it matters:** METR's Time Horizon 1.1 found a capability doubling time of 130 days. If harm rate scales superlinearly with capability, the safety window is much shorter than capability growth alone suggests.

**Relevant context:** METR Time Horizon 1.1 (metr.org, January 2026); the two compounding failure modes; ForesightSafety Bench risky agentic autonomy pillar (arxiv 2602.14135).
