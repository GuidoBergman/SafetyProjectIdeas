# Is the 'safety-recovery' mechanism in deep alignment models visible in the attention patterns on harmful prefix tokens?

**Problem:** Models trained to recover to refusal after a non-refusal first token (as in Qi et al.'s data augmentation approach) must attend to the earlier harmful context to trigger recovery. The PRESTO defense (2025) explicitly regularizes attention on harmful prefill tokens. Whether attention patterns on initial tokens differ between deep and shallow models, and whether this is measurable, is untested.

**Direction:** For a shallow and a deep (Qi et al. fine-tuned) model, run prefilling attack prompts with the compliance prefix. Extract attention weights at every layer for the tokens in the compliance prefix at positions 2-10. Compute the mean attention from later positions back to the prefix tokens. Plot attention heatmaps and summary statistics (mean attention to prefix) for shallow vs. deep models.

**Why it matters:** If deep alignment correlates with increased attention to initial compliance tokens, this provides a mechanistic interpretation and a potential new probe feature for detectors. It also validates the PRESTO intuition that attention suppression is a meaningful defense lever.

**Relevant context:** PRESTO (openreview.net GpBo97z6GH) addresses attention to harmful prefill tokens. Qi et al.'s recovery examples encourage later-position safety responses. Attention extraction is available in all HuggingFace transformer models via output_attentions=True.
