# Reward Model Generalization Cliff: How Far Out-of-Distribution Can a Reward Model Reliably Score?

**Problem:** Reward models are trained on a fixed preference distribution but used to score outputs that can vary substantially at test time. The 'generalization cliff' — the prompt distribution distance beyond which reward model scores become unreliable — is not well characterized.

**Direction:** Train reward models on HH-RLHF. Construct a series of out-of-distribution prompt sets at increasing semantic distance from the training distribution (measured by embedding distance). Measure reward model accuracy (against gold human labels) as a function of distance. Fit a degradation curve.

**Why it matters:** Knowing the generalization cliff radius of a reward model would allow practitioners to flag when RLHF training is operating in an unreliable region and would motivate targeted data collection.

**Relevant context:** RLHF distributional shift open problem; reward model taxonomy survey (arxiv 2504.12328v1); Anthropic HH-RLHF; DRO-REBEL distributional robustness.
