# Over-Refusal Asymmetry: Do Safety Improvements Always Increase Over-Refusal Proportionally?

**Problem:** Safety improvements are assumed to increase over-refusal (the alignment tax), but the quantitative relationship is not well characterized. It is unclear whether this is a fixed trade-off or whether certain safety interventions are more precision-efficient than others.

**Direction:** Apply three distinct safety-improving interventions to a baseline model: (1) SFT on safety data, (2) DPO with safety preference pairs, (3) activation steering toward refusal direction. For each, measure both safety improvement (attack success rate on StrongREJECT) and over-refusal rate (false positive rate on OR-Bench). Plot improvement vs. over-refusal as a Pareto curve. Identify which intervention achieves the best safety-per-unit-over-refusal ratio.

**Why it matters:** The over-refusal vs. safety frontier is critical for deployment. If activation steering achieves better Pareto efficiency than SFT, it becomes the preferred lightweight deployment-time intervention.

**Relevant context:** OR-Bench: first large-scale over-refusal benchmark (OpenReview). StrongREJECT (arXiv:2402.10260). SafeConstellations steering for over-refusal reduction (arXiv:2508.11290).
