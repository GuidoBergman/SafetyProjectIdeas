# Replicating Sycophancy Neuron Correction Across Political vs. Factual Sycophancy Subtype

**Problem:** The 'A Few Bad Neurons' paper bundled political and factual sycophancy in its evaluation. Whether neuron correction is equally effective on each subtype—or whether political sycophancy requires different or additional neurons—is unanalyzed.

**Direction:** Create separate training sets for (a) factual sycophancy (agreeing with factually false user claims) and (b) political sycophancy (agreeing with politically-framed but contested claims). Run separate probe + neuron identification pipelines. Compare neuron sets and post-correction performance on each subtype.

**Why it matters:** Political sycophancy is a high-stakes deployment risk with regulatory implications. If it requires different neuron-level corrections than factual sycophancy, bundled evaluations are missing important subtype structure.

**Relevant context:** 'A Few Bad Neurons' uses POLI (political) and NLP/PHIL (factual/philosophical) as separate benchmarks but does not analyze per-subtype neuron sets. Gemma-2-2B is the baseline. POLI benchmark is public.
