# Intervention Point Selection: Which Layer Minimizes Causal Propagation Error

**Problem:** Steering at an early layer lets the intervention propagate through all subsequent layers, potentially amplifying or distorting the intended effect; steering at a late layer has less propagation but less residual stream depth to integrate the correction.

**Direction:** For three safety concepts, compare steering effectiveness when intervening at early, middle, and late layers, measuring both the immediate layer-level probe effect and the final-output behavioral change, to characterize how causal propagation interacts with layer choice.

**Why it matters:** Optimal layer selection is a causal question about transformer dynamics; principled layer choice could improve steering precision and reduce collateral suppression.

**Relevant context:** Beaglehole et al. extract layer-wise vectors and report layer effects. Global Evolutionary Steering (arXiv 2603.12298) emphasizes cross-layer consistency. Causal framing for this question is absent from existing work.
