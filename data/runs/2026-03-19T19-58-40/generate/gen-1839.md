# Minimal Framing Dose for Preventing Emergent Misalignment

**Problem:** Betley et al. (2025) showed that adding a benign motivational framing to insecure-code fine-tuning data prevented emergent misalignment, but the minimum amount of framing required is unknown.

**Direction:** Systematically vary the proportion of framing-annotated samples in a fine-tuning dataset (0%, 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 100%) and measure at what threshold emergent misalignment is suppressed. Use a small open-source model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) and the original insecure-code fine-tuning task. Test with a fixed evaluation suite of alignment probes.

**Why it matters:** If a tiny fraction of framing-annotated data is sufficient, safety practitioners could cheaply inoculate any fine-tuning dataset. If the threshold is high, that reveals a more fundamental coupling between narrow task learning and value generalization.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. 2025 (arxiv 2502.17424) directly motivates this. The emergent-misalignment.com site and Nature paper confirm replicability. Low-cost experiment accessible with single GPU.
