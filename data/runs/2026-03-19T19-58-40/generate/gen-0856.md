# Jailbreak Benchmark Overfitting: When Red-Teaming Evals Become Shallow Alignment Targets

**Problem:** Safety teams use jailbreak benchmarks (JailbreakBench, HarmBench) to evaluate alignment. If models are RLHF-trained against these benchmarks, alignment may be shallow—specific to benchmark prompt distributions. Novel prompts outside the benchmark distribution then both jailbreak the model and evade detectors trained on benchmark data, compounding both failures.

**Direction:** Compare the ASR of benchmark-template prompts vs. semantically equivalent novel paraphrases on a model known to have been evaluated on that benchmark. Measure whether probes or classifiers trained on benchmark data transfer to novel paraphrases. Quantify the 'eval overfitting' gap.

**Why it matters:** If safety training targets benchmark distributions and evals confirm safety only on benchmarks, a realistic adversary using novel framings faces a model and monitor that are both overfit. This is a systemic compound failure in how the safety evaluation pipeline works.

**Relevant context:** JailbreakBench open robustness benchmark. Realistic jailbreak benchmarks listed as an open problem in subfield. Simple adaptive attacks demonstrate models can be 100% broken with slight prompt adaptation (ICLR 2025). Shallow alignment concentrates safety on trained distributions.
