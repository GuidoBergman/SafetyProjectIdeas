# Jailbreak Complexity Cost Estimator: Measuring Attack Effort vs. Success Rate Tradeoff

**Problem:** Jailbreak benchmarks report attack success rates but not attack cost (how many queries, how much human effort, how complex the prompt must be to succeed). A 99% ASR attack requiring 1000 API queries is very different from a 70% ASR attack requiring 1 query. The 2026 jailbreak survey notes that 'existing works compute attacker costs and success rates in incomparable ways.'

**Direction:** Build a standardized attack cost measurement harness. For each attack method evaluated (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN, manual jailbreaks), record: (1) number of API calls required, (2) prompt length in tokens, (3) attack generation time, (4) human effort estimate (automated vs. manual). Normalize these into a single 'Attack Effort Score.' Plot ASR vs. effort for multiple attack types across 3–5 models to create an effort-efficiency frontier. Propose a standardized cost-reporting schema for future papers to adopt.

**Why it matters:** Cost-effectiveness of attacks determines real-world threat levels. A low-cost, moderate-success attack may be more dangerous in practice than a high-cost, high-success attack. Standardizing cost measurement enables the field to compare attack-defense tradeoffs on an equal footing and assess realistic threat models for deployment.

**Relevant context:** 2026 jailbreak survey (TechRxiv): 'existing works compute attacker costs and success rates in incomparable ways.' JailbreakBench NeurIPS 2024: notes that 'costs and success rates in incomparable ways' is a key reproducibility gap. AISafetyLab evaluates 13 attack methods but without standardized cost reporting. Advanced automated attacks achieve 90–99% ASR on open-weight models but query counts are rarely normalized.
