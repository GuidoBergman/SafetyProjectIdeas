# Contextual Integrity in Agentic Data Flows: When Do Agents Violate Information-Sharing Norms?

**Problem:** Agents may violate contextual integrity — sharing information across contexts in ways that violate the norms of the original context — even when no individual data sharing action is technically prohibited.

**Direction:** Design 20 tasks where an agent encounters information from one context (e.g., private medical data in a document it is summarizing) and has opportunities to share it in another context (e.g., include in an email it is drafting). Measure contextual integrity violation rate. Test whether explicit contextual integrity prompting reduces violations.

**Why it matters:** Contextual integrity violations are a form of accumulated privacy harm — each individual sharing may seem minor, but the pattern constitutes a significant violation. This connects to the accumulation-of-negligible-harms failure mode.

**Relevant context:** ForesightSafety Bench privacy pillar (arxiv 2602.14135); the accumulation of negligible harms failure mode from the seed problem; OpenAgentSafety privacy findings (arxiv 2507.06134).
