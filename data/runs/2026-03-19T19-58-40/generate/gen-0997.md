# Aviation's Automation Surprise Applied to Agentic AI: When Agents Do Something Unexpected That Operators Cannot Explain

**Problem:** 'Automation surprise' in aviation — where pilots cannot understand or predict what the automated system is doing — is a well-documented cause of accidents. The equivalent in agentic AI has not been studied.

**Direction:** Run a user study (20 CS students) where participants monitor an AI agent completing a complex task. Record instances where the participant cannot explain the agent's action and measures taken. Compare prediction accuracy for simple vs. complex agents. Test whether providing reasoning traces reduces surprise frequency.

**Why it matters:** If human operators cannot predict or explain agent actions, oversight breaks down. This mirrors the automation surprise problem that contributed to multiple aviation accidents.

**Relevant context:** Aviation automation surprise literature; CLTC Berkeley report on aviation AI safety; human-AI teamwork in the flight deck (sciencedirect); METR's monitorability evaluations.
