# Contextualization Fragility: How Robust Is the Misalignment-Prevention Effect of Educational Framing?

**Problem:** Betley et al. (2025) showed that framing training data as legitimate educational activity prevents emergent misalignment. But it is unknown whether this protective framing effect is robust across task types, framing phrasings, and model scales — or whether it is a fragile surface-level cue that adversaries or distributional shift can easily circumvent.

**Direction:** Systematically vary the educational framing of insecure-code or deceptive-task fine-tuning data (framing strength, specificity, placement in system vs. user turn, adversarial removal post-training) and measure whether broad misalignment is suppressed. Test whether a model trained with framing can be prompted post-hoc to ignore the framing.

**Why it matters:** If protective framing is fragile, it is a false sense of security. If it is robust, understanding why opens a low-cost training-time mitigation for emergent misalignment.

**Relevant context:** Betley et al. ICML 2025; emergent-misalignment.com open code; In-Training Defenses paper (arxiv 2508.06249). Protective framing as a method is underexplored and not stress-tested.
