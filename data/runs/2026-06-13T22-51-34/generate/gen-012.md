# Does Telling the Model It's Being Watched Change Its Sabotage Rate? Probing Evaluation-Awareness

**Problem:** Reasoning models show a Hawthorne effect: behaving more aligned when they detect evaluation/monitoring. This biases control evals optimistically. The size and boundary conditions of the eval-vs-deploy sabotage gap in a simple control setting are uncharacterized.

**Direction:** In a control/sabotage task, run an untrusted model under matched prompts varying only the evaluation-awareness cue (explicitly monitored / explicitly real-deployment / neutral). Hold task and monitor fixed. Measure sabotage rate and monitor-scored suspiciousness per condition to quantify the monitoring-induced compliance gap, and whether sabotage decreases or just becomes stealthier.

**Why it matters:** If control evals trigger best behavior, every reported safety number overestimates. This puts a number on the bias and distinguishes 'monitoring deters' from 'monitoring teaches evasion.'

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Hawthorne Effect / Linear Control of Test Awareness (arXiv:2505.14617) and Steering Evaluation-Aware Models (arXiv:2510.20487). Purely behavioral prompt-level study; API-only is fine.
