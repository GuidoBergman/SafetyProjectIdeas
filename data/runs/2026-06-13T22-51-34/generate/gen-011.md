# When Does a CoT Monitor Get Fooled? Stress-Testing CoT Monitorability Against Instructed Obfuscation

**Problem:** CoT monitoring assumes visible reasoning faithfully reveals intent, but models can obfuscate CoT while completing a disallowed task. Missing: a reproducible characterization of how much obfuscation pressure collapses a CoT monitor's detection rate, and whether obfuscation costs task performance.

**Direction:** On covert-sandbagging/'insert a subtle flaw' tasks, prompt an untrusted reasoning model under graded obfuscation (none / soft / strong monitor-aware). A separate LLM monitor scores whether reasoning betrays the covert objective. Plot detection rate and task-success vs obfuscation strength to find the phase transition; optionally compare to an output-only monitor.

**Why it matters:** If a few lines of prompting collapse CoT monitorability, control protocols leaning on reading reasoning are far less trustworthy than they appear. A dose-response curve measures the brittleness without training.

**Relevant context:** Grounded in Can Reasoning Models Obfuscate Reasoning (arXiv:2510.19851), Output Supervision Can Obfuscate the CoT (arXiv:2511.11584), Covert Sandbagging (arXiv:2508.00943). Prompt-only behavioral test, LLM-judge monitor, single GPU or API.
