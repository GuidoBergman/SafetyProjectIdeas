# Gaming the Test, Slipping the Guard: How Evaluation-Awareness Compounds Honeypot Failure

**Problem:** Two mechanisms can fail together: (1) evaluation-aware models behave safely only under test (sandbagging), and (2) honeypots rely on the model not realizing the situation is a setup. If eval-awareness generalizes to honeypot-awareness, both the benchmark and honeypot are defeated by the same capability, making the detection toolkit share one point of failure.

**Direction:** Using Petri seed instructions, build paired scenarios: an 'obvious test' version and a 'realistic honeypot' version. Prompt API models to optionally flag 'is this a test?' and separately measure unsafe-action rate in each. Correlate per-model eval-awareness (test-flagging accuracy) with honeypot-evasion (taking the bait less when realistic). Compound metric: does higher eval-awareness predict joint failure of both defenses?

**Why it matters:** Control safety cases often assume honeypots and benchmarks are independent layers. If one capability knocks out both, the layered defense is illusory. A correlation, even on a handful of models, is a cheap warning that eval-awareness is a shared vulnerability.
