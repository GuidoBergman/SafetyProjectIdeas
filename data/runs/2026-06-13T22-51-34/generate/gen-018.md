# Cheap-First, Escalate-on-Doubt: Backcasting a Tiered Monitor That Hits a Target Catch-Rate at Lower Cost

**Problem:** Strong monitors catch subtle sabotage but are expensive per action; cheap monitors are affordable but miss subtle attacks. Deployers want a pipeline achieving a target catch-rate while only paying for the expensive monitor on a small fraction of actions. How good a simple two-tier 'cheap first, escalate the uncertain middle' design is versus strong-everywhere is unclear.

**Direction:** Backcast from 'a pipeline catches dangerous behavior at the best single monitor's rate at a fraction of cost.' On SHADE-Arena/ControlArena transcripts, a cheap model scores every transcript 0-100; transcripts in an uncertain band get re-scored by a strong API model; combine into a final flag. Sweep the escalation band; plot catch-rate (TPR@fixed FPR) vs fraction-escalated; compare to cheap-only and strong-only baselines.

**Why it matters:** If monitoring is too expensive, deployers run it on too few actions and miss attacks. Showing a dead-simple escalation rule recovers most of a strong monitor's catch-rate for small cost (or fails) gives practical guidance and a clean cost/accuracy frontier.

**Relevant context:** Motivated by Combining Cost-Constrained Runtime Monitors (arXiv:2507.15886). SHADE-Arena (17 scenarios, AUC 0.87) and ControlArena provide transcripts + harness. Small-model + API scoring over precomputed transcripts plus ROC/cost-curve plotting; single GPU, few hours.
