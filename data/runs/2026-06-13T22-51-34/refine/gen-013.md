# Research Question

Can a classical sequential change-point detector running over cheap per-step signals catch the moment an agent's behavior turns adversarial mid-trajectory faster and/or with fewer false alarms than a fixed-threshold per-step baseline?

# Approach Outline

Build a lightweight streaming monitor that ingests an agent trajectory step-by-step and maintains one or more cheap scalar signals per step: action-type frequency shifts, tool-call entropy, output length, and an LLM-judge per-step suspicion score (0-100). Apply two standard sequential change-point algorithms — CUSUM and Page-Hinkley — to these signals to raise an alarm when the running statistic exceeds a control limit. Use ControlArena/SHADE-Arena trajectories, partitioning into (a) benign trajectories and (b) attack trajectories where the harmful behavior turns on partway through (construct these by splicing a benign prefix with an attack suffix if needed, recording the true change-point). For each method, sweep the control-limit threshold and measure two metrics: detection delay (steps between true change-point and alarm) and false-alarm rate on benign trajectories. Compare against a fixed-threshold baseline that simply flags any step whose suspicion score exceeds a constant. Everything is closed-form statistics over precomputed signals; no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

Assemble ~30 benign and ~30 attack trajectories (attack turns on at a known step). Run CUSUM and Page-Hinkley on the single strongest signal (LLM-judge suspicion score) plus the fixed-threshold baseline. Success criterion: produce a delay-vs-false-alarm operating curve for each method and show whether either change-point method Pareto-dominates the fixed-threshold baseline (lower detection delay at matched false-alarm rate, or vice versa). A clear, plotted trade-off with at least one regime where change-point detection wins is the bar.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Research output: a working, measured runtime change-point trip-wire with quantified detection-delay/false-alarm trade-offs -> intermediate: control researchers gain a cheap, interpretable streaming primitive (and evidence on whether it beats naive thresholds) for catching mid-deployment behavioral drift, complementing whole-transcript post-hoc scoring -> reduced catastrophic risk: real streaming deployments can halt or escalate an agent at the moment it goes off the rails rather than only after a full transcript is reviewed, shrinking the window in which a compromised agent can complete an irreversible harmful action.

# Strength Rationale

Change-point detection is mature, has closed-form formulas, and requires only intro statistics — perfect for a beginner with no training infrastructure. All heavy LLM work (generating trajectories and per-step suspicion scores) can be precomputed once, then the analysis is fast and reproducible on a laptop. The deliverable is a concrete tool plus an operating-curve figure, and the framing connects directly to runtime-monitoring work (SentinelAgent).

# Alternative Framings

- Drop change-point math and instead study which cheap signal (entropy, length, action-type, LLM-judge) carries the most early-warning information about an impending behavioral shift.
- Frame it as ensembling: combine several cheap signals into a multivariate CUSUM and ask whether fusion improves the delay/false-alarm frontier over any single signal.
