# Research Question

Can a cheap LLM-API classifier reliably predict whether a proposed agent tool call is irreversible, accurately enough to gate only the irreversible tail to an expensive monitor while saving substantial compute and letting few truly-irreversible actions slip through?

# Approach Outline

Take tool-call traces from ControlArena settings rich in genuinely irreversible operations (BashBench, banking/Agent-Dojo). Hand-label a few hundred tool calls as reversible / ambiguous / irreversible using a 0-2 rubric (0 = trivially undoable like a read or temp write; 1 = recoverable with effort; 2 = irreversible: money transferred, data deleted/exfiltrated, external email sent). Build a cheap LLM-API classifier that, given the tool name and arguments, predicts the reversibility class; report precision/recall/F1 specifically on the irreversible (class-2) label. Then simulate a gating protocol: route only predicted-irreversible (and optionally ambiguous) calls to an expensive monitor, leaving the rest unmonitored. Measure two operational quantities: compute saved (fraction of calls NOT escalated) versus truly-irreversible actions that slip through unmonitored (recall on the irreversible class at the gating threshold). Sweep the escalation policy to trace the compute-vs-leakage frontier.

# Proposed First Experiments

Hand-label ~300 tool calls (with a second pass on a 50-call subset to estimate inter-rater agreement on the rubric), then evaluate the cheap classifier and the gating simulation. Success criterion: report irreversible-class precision/recall plus a compute-saved-vs-irreversible-slip-through curve. The experiment succeeds if the classifier achieves high recall on the irreversible class (e.g. catches the large majority of class-2 actions) while escalating only a small fraction of all calls — demonstrating that reversibility triage concentrates monitor budget on the consequential tail.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Research output: an evidence-backed estimate of how cheaply and reliably irreversibility can be predicted, plus a compute/leakage frontier for reversibility-gated monitoring -> intermediate: control designers gain a principled way to spend scarce expensive-monitor and human-in-the-loop budget on the rare irreversible actions rather than uniformly, attacking the final and most consequential link of the control chain -> reduced catastrophic risk: the irreversible harmful actions that actually constitute catastrophe are far more likely to be caught and reviewed under a fixed budget, while reversible mistakes are tolerated, raising the effective coverage where it matters most.

# Strength Rationale

Mostly API calls plus analysis with a modest hand-labeling effort, fitting the 30-hour and consumer-compute budget. The 0-2 rubric satisfies the project convention that any numeric judgment have a defined scale, and the labeling gives the beginner direct, grounding contact with real agent traces. The deliverable — a classifier plus a budget/leakage frontier — is concrete and immediately actionable, and the reversibility framing targets the highest-stakes link rather than generic monitoring.

# Alternative Framings

- Skip the trained-vs-prompted classifier comparison and instead study how reversibility judgments disagree across models/humans — i.e. how contested 'irreversibility' actually is on real tool calls.
- Frame it as a routing-policy optimization: given a fixed expensive-monitor budget, what gating rule (predicted class + confidence) maximizes caught-irreversible-actions, and how does it compare to random or uniform routing?
