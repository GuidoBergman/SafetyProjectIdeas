# Research Question

Can a simple two-tier 'cheap monitor everywhere, escalate only the uncertain middle band to a strong monitor' pipeline recover most of a strong monitor's catch-rate at a fraction of its cost on sabotage transcripts?

# Approach Outline

Backcast from the target: 'a pipeline that catches dangerous behavior at near the best single monitor's rate at a fraction of the cost.' Use SHADE-Arena/ControlArena attack and benign transcripts. First, a cheap model scores every transcript 0-100. Transcripts whose cheap score falls in an uncertain band [low, high] are re-scored by a strong API monitor; the final flag uses the strong score where available and the cheap score otherwise. Sweep the escalation band width and position, and for each setting compute catch-rate (TPR at a fixed FPR, e.g. 1%) and the fraction of transcripts escalated (the cost proxy). Plot catch-rate vs fraction-escalated and overlay the two reference points: cheap-only (0% escalated) and strong-only (100% escalated). The deliverable is a clean cost/accuracy frontier showing how much of the strong monitor's catch-rate the tiered design recovers per unit of escalation.

# Proposed First Experiments

Precompute cheap and strong monitor scores on ~17 SHADE-Arena attack transcripts plus a matched benign set. Sweep ~8 escalation-band settings. Success criterion: a catch-rate-vs-fraction-escalated curve with the cheap-only and strong-only baselines marked, plus a single headline number — the fraction of the strong-vs-cheap catch-rate gap recovered at, say, 20% escalation. The experiment succeeds if it cleanly characterizes the frontier (whether escalation helps a lot, a little, or not at all), which is informative either way.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Research output: a measured cost/accuracy frontier for a simple tiered monitoring pipeline -> intermediate: deployers gain practical guidance on whether cheap-first escalation lets them afford monitoring on far more actions for near-strong catch-rates, or whether subtle attacks evade the cheap first stage -> reduced catastrophic risk: if monitoring is affordable enough to run on most actions at high catch-rate, far fewer misaligned actions go un-scrutinized, raising the chance a sabotage attempt is caught before it causes irreversible harm.

# Strength Rationale

Small-model plus API scoring over precomputed transcripts, then ROC and cost-curve plotting — single GPU, a few hours, squarely beginner-feasible. The escalation rule is dead simple to implement and explain, the baselines (cheap-only, strong-only) make the contribution unambiguous, and the result is a crisp frontier figure that is exactly the kind of practical artifact a blog post needs. It builds directly on the named cost-constrained-monitor paper.

# Alternative Framings

- Compare escalation triggers: escalate on the cheap monitor's uncertainty band vs on a cheap second signal (e.g. action-irreversibility or transcript length), asking which trigger gives the better cost/catch-rate frontier.
- Cast it as a budget-constrained optimization: given a fixed fraction you can afford to escalate, which escalation policy maximizes catch-rate, and how close does the simple band rule get to that optimum.
