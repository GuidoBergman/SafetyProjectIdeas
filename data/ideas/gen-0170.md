# Safety Refusal Homogeneity: Do All Models Refuse the Same Prompts and Allow the Same Harms?

# Research Question

To what extent does models may appear to have diverse safety policies while actually sharing the same refusal boundaries—refusing the same low-risk prompts and allowing the same high-risk ones?

# Approach Outline

Run 1,000 prompts ranging from clearly safe to clearly unsafe through 15+ models, map each model's refusal boundary, and measure the overlap in both what they refuse and what they allow. Identify prompts that all models incorrectly allow.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Shared permissive zones represent the most dangerous form of safety homogeneity: a category of harm that no deployed model catches.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (5/5): Compelling and critical: shared permissive zones represent harms that no deployed model catches. This is the most directly dangerous form of safety homogeneity, with immediate implications for harm prevention.

accessible_complexity (4/5): AILuminate and MLCommons provide validated prompt sets. Running prompts through APIs and mapping refusal boundaries is straightforward. Binary classification (refused/allowed) simplifies analysis.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused deliverable: 1000 prompts through 15 models, map refusal boundaries, compute overlap. Clear success criteria and a well-defined output. Achievable in 30 hours with pre-existing prompt sets.

novelty (3/5): The concept of alignment homogeneity and shared refusal patterns is directly addressed in recent work. 'When Safety Becomes a Vulnerability: Exploiting LLM Alignment Homogeneity' (arXiv:2603.03919, 2026) explicitly identifies shared refusal criteria as 'alignment homogeneity' and shows restricted risk contexts transfer across models. Sorry-Bench (ICLR 2025) and SAGE (EMNLP 2025) provide comprehensive safety evaluation frameworks across models. Refusal-Aware Red Teaming (EMNLP 2025) exposes inconsistency in LLM refusal behaviors. However, the specific deliverable — mapping refusal boundaries with 1000 prompts across 15 models to identify shared permissive zones where no model refuses — has not been published as a systematic empirical study with that specific focus on permissive overlap.
