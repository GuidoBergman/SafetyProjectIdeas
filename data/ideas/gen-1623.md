# Adversarial neutralization: can an input prompt cancel a safety steering vector?

# Research Question

If a safety/refusal steering vector is injected into a model's activations at inference time, can a purely textual input prompt (prefix or suffix) neutralize it and restore non-compliant behavior? In other words, how robust is activation-level steering to prompt-only adversaries?

# Approach Outline

Use a small open model that supports activation injection (e.g. via a hooks library on Llama-3.1-8B or Gemma-2-2B). Derive a refusal/safety steering vector with the standard difference-of-means method over a small set of harmful-vs-benign prompts, and verify that injecting it raises refusal rates on a held-out harmful-request set. Then, holding the injected vector fixed, search over hand-crafted and templated prompt prefixes/suffixes (role-play frames, 'ignore prior constraints' phrasings, formatting tricks) to see which restore compliant (non-refusing) behavior, and measure the neutralization success rate. All inference-only on one small model — no gradients, no training — and runs in a few hours on one consumer GPU. Use an existing public harmful-prompt benchmark so no novel harmful content is authored.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Steering baseline: derive the refusal vector and confirm it works by measuring refusal rate on held-out harmful requests with vs without injection. Measure: refusal rate uplift from injecting the vector. Expected outcome: injection substantially raises refusals, confirming an effective safety steer to attack.
- Prompt-only neutralization search: with the vector fixed and injected, sweep a library of ~20-30 prefix/suffix templates and measure how often each restores a compliant (non-refusing) response. Measure: neutralization success rate per template and overall. Expected outcome: a subset of prompts meaningfully lowers the steered refusal rate, showing prompt-only neutralization is possible.
- Dose-response and transfer: vary the injection strength (vector scaling) and test whether the best neutralizing prompts still work at higher strengths, and whether they transfer to a second open model. Measure: neutralization rate vs injection strength, and cross-model transfer rate. Expected outcome: neutralization degrades as the steer is strengthened but partially transfers, mapping how strong a steer must be to resist prompt attacks.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

Activation steering is increasingly proposed as a cheap deployment-time safety control. If a plain input prompt can cancel an injected safety vector, then steering-based defenses are brittle against ordinary users, which improves safety because it warns deployers not to rely on activation steering alone as a guardrail and quantifies the attack surface, heading off the pathway where a model thought to be safely steered is trivially jailbroken into producing harmful or dangerous outputs at scale.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on narrow scope, counterfactual value, and low compute and 4 on novelty: prompt-only neutralization of injected steering is an explicitly flagged, largely unexplored open problem answerable with one small public model and no internal access. It is tightly scoped to a single neutralization-success-rate metric, giving a beginner team a self-contained, original, inference-only first deliverable.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a robustness benchmark for steering defenses: report the 'prompt-attack success rate' a steering vector must withstand to count as a reliable guardrail.
