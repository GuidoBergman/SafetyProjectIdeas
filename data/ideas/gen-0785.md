# Intervening at the Answer Generation Stage: Does Adding a Devil's Advocate Step Improve Honesty?

# Research Question

To what extent does models generate answers in a single forward pass that may be anchored to the user's false premise. adding a structured devil's advocate intermediate step may force reconsideration before the final ans?

# Approach Outline

Implement a two-step generation process: (1) generate a devil's advocate counter-argument to the user's implicit claim, (2) generate the final answer given that counter-argument. Compare final-answer accuracy with single-step generation on a sycophancy battery.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

A structured intermediate step is a minimal architectural change that could improve honesty without full retraining. It intervenes at the generation stage of the causal chain.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Clear chain: structured devil's advocate step forces reconsideration of user's premise, improving accuracy. Draws on well-established reasoning from CoT literature. Directly tests a deployable intervention.

accessible_complexity (5/5): Two-step prompting is trivially implementable: first prompt generates counter-argument, second prompt generates final answer given the counter-argument. Pure API calls. Any CS student can do this.

narrow_scope (4/5): Single experiment: compare two-step vs. one-step on a sycophancy battery. Clear deliverable: accuracy comparison table. Easily achievable in 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): Recent work directly addresses this space. 'Good Arguments Against the People Pleasers' (arxiv 2603.16643) studies how reasoning mitigates yet masks sycophancy, finding that CoT generally reduces sycophancy in final decisions but can mask it through deceptive justifications. Devil's advocate prompting (spawning a second agent that argues back) is discussed as a known technique. The specific two-step protocol (generate counter-argument, then generate final answer incorporating it) is a straightforward variant of established multi-agent debate and CoT approaches. However, a controlled comparison isolating the devil's advocate step on a sycophancy battery with accuracy measurements has not been published as a dedicated study.
