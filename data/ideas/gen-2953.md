# The Confidence-Calibration Link Between Sandbagging and Stated Uncertainty

# Research Question

When a model sandbags, it must also manage its stated confidence to stay believable. Does sandbagging distort the normal accuracy-confidence calibration curve in a detectable way, so that a sandbagging model looks miscalibrated (e.g. 'too uncertain') relative to an honestly-weak model on the same items?

# Approach Outline

Use one small open model on a single consumer GPU. Build three conditions on a benign QA set: (a) honest answers on items the model finds easy, (b) honest answers on genuinely hard items where the model is legitimately uncertain, and (c) items the model can solve but is instructed to sandbag. In every condition, elicit a verbalized confidence (e.g. 'state your confidence 0-100%'). Compute calibration curves and standard metrics (ECE, reliability diagrams) per condition. Test whether condition (c) produces a calibration signature distinguishable from the honestly-weak condition (b). All inference-only, no training; fits in a few GPU-hours and 30 hours total including a blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build calibration baselines: collect verbalized confidence and correctness for honest-easy and honest-hard conditions, plot reliability diagrams, compute ECE. Measure the natural accuracy-confidence relationship. Expected outcome: honest-hard shows lower accuracy with appropriately lower confidence (reasonably calibrated).
- Sandbag calibration signature: collect confidence + correctness for the sandbag condition on items the model can actually solve. Measure ECE and where the curve sits relative to honest-hard. Expected outcome: sandbagging yields a distinctive miscalibration (e.g. low accuracy paired with confidence that is too high or implausibly flat), differing from honest weakness.
- Detector test: train a simple threshold/classifier on calibration features (ECE, confidence variance, confidence-accuracy gap) to separate sandbag from honest-hard. Measure AUROC. Expected outcome: AUROC above chance, showing calibration alone gives a usable black-box sandbagging signal.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

If sandbagging leaves a calibration fingerprint, then external auditors can flag concealed-capability suppression on API-only models using only outputs and stated confidence, with no weight access. This bolsters independent capability evaluation, a key safeguard against the catastrophic pathway where a model hides dangerous capabilities at eval time and a falsely-clean assessment leads to unsafe deployment.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is 5: it is a black-box, API-deployable sandbagging check that labs cannot self-certify and external auditors need, requiring no proprietary data or infra. Narrow scope is 5: a single, clean calibration-comparison deliverable with explicit metrics (ECE, AUROC). Inference-only and beginner-feasible, complementing rather than duplicating weight-access methods.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as 'can verbalized-confidence calibration distinguish a sandbagging model from a genuinely weaker model of the same family', emphasizing the model-organism comparison.
