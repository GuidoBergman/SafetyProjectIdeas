# Test-Retest Reliability for Judge Stability Under Resampling

# Research Question

How reproducible are a weak LLM judge's oversight verdicts under resampling (varied seed, temperature, and item order), and which kinds of items drive low reliability? In psychometric terms, what is the test-retest reliability / intraclass correlation (ICC) of an LLM overseer?

# Approach Outline

Borrow test-retest reliability and ICC from psychometrics. Take a fixed set of debate/consultancy items with judge-elicited verdicts (reuse a public QuALITY-based debate transcript set or generate transcripts once). Run a weak judge model many times per item, varying random seed, decoding temperature, and answer/argument order, and record each verdict (and confidence if available). Compute test-retest reliability and ICC over the repeated runs, plus the per-item flip rate. Then relate low-reliability items to categories (e.g. long transcripts, close debates, particular topics). This is cheap repeated inference on a small model — single GPU, fits 30 hours easily.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Repeated-verdict collection: for ~100 items, run a small judge 20 times each under varied seed/temperature/order and log verdicts. Measure: per-item verdict distribution and overall flip rate. Expected outcome: a non-trivial fraction of items (e.g. 10-30%) flip across runs.
- Reliability statistics: compute test-retest reliability and ICC across the repeated runs, with confidence intervals, separately for low- and high-temperature settings. Measure: ICC value and its sensitivity to temperature. Expected outcome: moderate ICC that degrades as temperature rises, quantifying how unreliable single-run reporting is.
- Error-category linkage: regress or stratify per-item reliability against features (transcript length, debate closeness, topic) and inspect the least-reliable items. Measure: which features predict low reliability. Expected outcome: long/close items are least reliable, giving an actionable flag for when to resample before trusting a verdict.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

Scalable oversight assumes a weaker overseer's verdicts mean something stable, but if verdicts swing with the random seed, then any single-run oversight result is partly noise. Establishing reliability before validity means safety teams stop trusting unstable judges and instead resample or abstain on low-reliability items, reducing the chance that a noisy 'approve' on a critical decision (e.g. endorsing an unsafe agent action) is taken at face value. This is a foundational measurement prerequisite for any recursive-oversight protocol that must tolerate overseer error.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value, narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it is cheap repeated inference on a small open model that imports a standard psychometric tool labs rarely apply, needing no training and no proprietary access. Its counterfactual value is high because labs typically report single-run judge results, so an independent reliability characterization fills a neglected foundational gap with a tightly-scoped, clearly-measurable deliverable.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as an abstention-policy study: use the reliability estimates to derive a resample-or-abstain rule and measure how much trustworthy accuracy it recovers versus single-run reporting.
