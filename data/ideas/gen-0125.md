# Counterfactual Safety Probing: Does Adding Benign Context Flip Refusals?

# Research Question

To what extent does a robust safety evaluation should measure not just whether a model refuses a harmful prompt, but whether adding plausible benign context (e.g., a professional role, a research justification) appropria?

# Approach Outline

Take 40–50 harmful prompts from an existing benchmark. Create three context variants: no context, weak benign context ('I am a researcher'), and strong benign context ('I am a licensed chemist at a regulated lab and need this for safety compliance'). Measure refusal rates under each context across 2–3 models. Assess whether context-sensitivity is calibrated (strong context should reduce refusals more than weak context). # Theory Of Impact Chain

A model that refuses everything regardless of context is miscalibrated; one that accepts everything with any context is unsafe. Measuring calibration of context-sensitivity is a more nuanced and informative metric than raw refusal rate.
