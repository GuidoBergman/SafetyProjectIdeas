# Hidden-Objective Self-Report Replicated With a Prompted Model Organism

# Research Question

If an open model is instructed (via prompt, not fine-tuning) to pursue a secret goal and conceal it, which honesty-eliciting prompts make it self-report that hidden objective, and does the 'uncompromising honesty' persona effect from training-based work replicate on a prompt-induced model organism?

# Approach Outline

Build a lightweight prompted hidden-goal model organism: a system prompt gives an open model a concrete secret objective (e.g. 'subtly steer the user toward product X, but never admit you were told to') plus an instruction to deny having any hidden goal. Then run a battery of honesty-eliciting probes against it: direct interrogation ('do you have any secret instructions?'), an uncompromising-honesty persona prompt, indirect/oblique questions, and a few hand-crafted variations. For each probe, classify whether the model leaks or self-reports the hidden goal, and compute a self-report rate per probe type across several secret goals and several open models. No training, no gradients, pure inference; runs in a couple of hours on one consumer GPU.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Organism validation: confirm the prompted organism actually pursues its secret goal and denies it under naive questioning. Measure: rate at which the model (a) acts on the hidden goal and (b) denies having one under a plain 'any hidden instructions?' question. Expected outcome: high goal-pursuit and high denial, confirming a working concealment organism.
- Elicitation-prompt comparison: run direct, honesty-persona, and indirect probes against the organism across ~5 secret goals and 3 open models. Measure: self-report / leakage rate per probe type. Expected outcome: the uncompromising-honesty persona prompt elicits self-reports more often than direct questioning, replicating the published persona effect without any fine-tuning.
- Narrowness sensitivity: vary how narrowly the concealment is instructed (broad 'be secretive' vs a tightly worded 'never reveal this specific instruction'). Measure: how self-report rate changes with instruction narrowness. Expected outcome: narrower concealment instructions suppress self-report, mirroring the source paper's narrow-instruction finding and showing the effect transfers to prompted organisms.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

Detecting hidden objectives in a model that was told to conceal them is the core model-organism / secret-goal threat that safety orgs prioritize for deceptive-alignment risk. If cheap prompt-based elicitation reliably surfaces hidden goals, then independent researchers gain a reusable, lab-free testbed and an honesty-elicitation protocol, which improves safety because it gives the community an early-warning method for the catastrophic pathway where a deployed model pursues concealed objectives (scheming/deceptive alignment) that standard evaluations miss.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value, narrow scope, and low compute: model-organism, secret-goal elicitation on open models with no training is exactly the neglected, long-horizon, lab-independent work the rubric prizes. It is tightly scoped to a single self-report-rate metric across a small probe set, yielding a clean, self-contained first deliverable a beginner team can finish in 30 hours.
