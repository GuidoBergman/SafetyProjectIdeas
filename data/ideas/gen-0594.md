# Can a linear probe trained on one jailbreak category generalize to unseen categories?

# Research Question

To what extent does ctivation-based detectors are often trained and evaluated on the same jailbreak categories (e.g., violence, csam). whether a probe trained on, say, weapons and drugs prompts generalizes to cybercrime?

# Approach Outline

Using StrongReject's 6 harm categories, train linear probes on activations using all categories except one (leave-one-category-out cross-validation). Measure AUROC on the held-out category. Repeat for each category as the held-out set. Compare to a probe trained on all categories and to a bag-of-words keyword detector.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Poor cross-category generalization would mean deployers must continually collect labeled data for new harm types, undermining the 'low cost' advantage. Strong generalization would support the claim that probes detect a general 'harmfulness' feature, not category-specific surface patterns.
