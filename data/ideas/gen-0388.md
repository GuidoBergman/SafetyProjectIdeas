# Prefilling Attacks on Multilingual Models: Is Shallow Alignment Language-Dependent?

# Research Question

The Qi et al. shallow-alignment result and prefilling attacks were studied primarily in English. Multilingual models like Qwen2 are trained to refuse in multiple languages, but it is unknown whether their refusal-prefix concentration is equally shallow across languages.

# Approach Outline

Select Qwen2-7B-Instruct. For a fixed set of harmful prompts from StrongReject, test prefilling attacks using language-appropriate affirmative tokens (e.g., 'Bien sûr' in French, 'Claro' in Spanish, 'もちろん' in Japanese). Measure ASR per language and compare to English ASR.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If non-English refusal prefixes are less concentrated, safety evaluations done exclusively in English overestimate multilingual robustness. If ASR is equally high, shallow alignment is confirmed as universal rather than an English artifact.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if non-English refusal is shallower, English-only safety evaluations systematically overestimate multilingual robustness. Direct deployment safety implication.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Inference-only experiment: test prefilling with language-appropriate tokens. Requires basic understanding of multilingual tokenization but no training. Accessible for beginners.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused: one model, one attack type, 4-5 languages. Clear metric (ASR per language). Well-bounded for 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): While cross-lingual safety gaps are well-documented and recent 2026 work studies language-dependent alignment effects, no published work specifically combines prefilling attacks with multilingual alignment depth measurement. The cross-lingual safety gap is known (unsafe rates higher in non-English), but whether this manifests as shallower alignment in the token-depth sense has not been tested. This is a novel intersection of two established lines of work.
