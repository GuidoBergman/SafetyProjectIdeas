# Replicating Homogeneity Findings on Legal Reasoning Tasks

# Research Question

Legal AI tools are being widely deployed for contract review, case research, and compliance. If models give homogeneous (and wrong) legal advice, every system using AI assistance would share the same legal error.

# Approach Outline

Replicate the Artificial Hivemind pairwise similarity analysis on legal reasoning tasks from LEGALBENCH or similar, measuring both output similarity and accuracy correlation across models.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Correlated legal errors across all major AI tools would undermine the diversity of legal advice in society and could create systemic legal risk for organizations relying on AI.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: correlated legal errors across AI tools undermine diversity of legal advice in society. Legal AI is widely deployed, and shared errors create liability risk.

accessible_complexity (4/5): LEGALBENCH is publicly available and well-documented. The replication methodology from Artificial Hivemind is directly applicable. CS students can run this with standard API access.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused deliverable: apply existing methodology to existing benchmark, compute metrics, analyze results. Minimal adaptation needed. Completable in 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): LegalBench exists as a well-established benchmark and the Artificial Hivemind methodology exists, but no one has yet combined them to measure inter-model homogeneity on legal reasoning. The legal domain is a natural and important extension that has not been done.
