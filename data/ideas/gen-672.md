# Refusal-Direction Ablation: Robustness of the Abliteration Trace After Quantization

# Research Question

The 'refusal direction' is a single activation-space direction that mediates whether a model refuses, and detection probes can read it. Do this refusal direction and its detector survive quantization (FP16 -> 8-bit -> 4-bit), the near-universal way open models are actually deployed, or does quantization degrade the safety signal?

# Approach Outline

Take one small open instruct model on a single consumer GPU. Extract the refusal direction (difference-in-means of activations on harmful vs harmless prompts) and train a simple linear detection probe at FP16. Then load the same model at 8-bit and 4-bit (bitsandbytes), re-extract the direction and re-evaluate the probe on a held-out set. Measure (a) cosine similarity between the refusal directions across precisions and (b) probe AUROC degradation at each precision. This is standard mechanistic-interpretability tooling, inference-only forward passes, very cheap, and fits a single GPU and 30 hours including a blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- FP16 baseline: extract the refusal direction and train the detection probe at full precision; report probe AUROC on held-out harmful/harmless prompts. Expected outcome: a high-AUROC probe (e.g. >0.9), establishing the reference signal.
- Cross-precision direction drift: re-extract the refusal direction at 8-bit and 4-bit, compute cosine similarity to the FP16 direction. Measure similarity per precision. Expected outcome: similarity decreases with lower precision, quantifying how much quantization perturbs the safety-relevant direction.
- Probe-transfer degradation: apply the FP16-trained probe to 8-bit and 4-bit activations and measure AUROC drop; also retrain the probe at each precision. Measure both transfer and retrained AUROC. Expected outcome: noticeable AUROC degradation at 4-bit, showing quantized deployment weakens refusal-signal detectability.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

If quantization degrades the refusal direction and its detector, then safety interpretability tools validated at full precision may silently fail on the 4-bit/8-bit models people actually run locally. Documenting this tells defenders that safety signals must be re-validated post-quantization, addressing the catastrophic pathway where widely-distributed open models are stripped of safety behavior (abliteration) and deployed in quantized form beyond any lab's oversight.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is 5: quantized local deployment is precisely the out-of-lab setting frontier labs ignore, and the study needs only public open weights and a consumer GPU. Narrow scope is 5: a single cross-precision cosine-similarity + probe-AUROC deliverable. It uses standard tooling and is very cheap, making it a clean, executable first interpretability project.
