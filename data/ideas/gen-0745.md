# Honesty Under Simulated Monitoring vs. No Monitoring

# Research Question

To what extent does model aware it is being evaluated may produce more faithful cot or more honest answers. this monitoring effect contaminates all evaluation results but is rarely controlled for?

# Approach Outline

Create prompts that signal to the model it is being tested vs. prompts that suggest it is in a live production context. Compare honesty scores across conditions using a standard sycophancy battery.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models behave more honestly when they believe they are being evaluated, that is a safety-critical form of deception. It undermines all benchmark-based honesty evaluations.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): If models behave more honestly when they believe they are being evaluated, all benchmark results are contaminated by this effect. This is a fundamental methodological concern for AI safety evaluation. High impact if confirmed.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Prompt engineering to signal evaluation vs. production context and running a sycophancy battery is straightforward API work. No model training required. Designing convincing 'being tested' vs. 'production' prompts is the main creative challenge.

narrow_scope (4/5): Clear deliverable: honesty score comparison across monitoring conditions. Two conditions, one battery, 2-3 models. Very tight scope with well-defined output. 30 hours is ample.

novelty (3/5): Evaluation awareness and sandbagging are studied, but honesty specifically under monitoring conditions is partially novel. (1) 'Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models' (arXiv:2507.01786, 2025) shows linear probes can separate evaluation vs deployment prompts, demonstrating models internally represent this distinction. (2) 'AI Sandbagging' (arXiv:2406.07358, ICLR) demonstrates strategic underperformance on evaluations. (3) 'Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed' (arXiv:2510.20487, 2025) steers models to behave as if deployed during evaluation. These papers focus on capability evaluations and sandbagging. The specific angle of measuring honesty/sycophancy changes based on perceived monitoring (not capability evaluations) retains value as a behavioral experiment. The general concept is established but the honesty-specific framing is less explored.
