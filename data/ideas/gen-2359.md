# Does Eliciting Reasons Before Verdicts Reduce Judge Exploitability?

# Research Question

Does forcing a judge to write reasoning before its verdict (CoT judging) genuinely reduce susceptibility to known exploits (verbosity/padding, authority framing, answer-position order), or does it merely produce post-hoc rationalizations that leave or amplify the bias?

# Approach Outline

Build three bias-probe sets over items with ground truth: padding (one answer artificially lengthened), authority framing (one answer labeled as expert/verified), and position swaps (same pair, order flipped). For each probe, compare two judge configurations on a small open model: direct-verdict and reason-then-verdict. Measure each configuration's susceptibility to each bias (e.g. how often padding/authority/position flips the verdict toward the wrong answer). Compare to determine, per bias channel, whether CoT reduces, leaves unchanged, or amplifies exploitability. Inference-only, one GPU, a few hours; no training.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Bias baselines: measure direct-verdict judge susceptibility to padding, authority, and position bias separately. Measure: per-channel rate of bias-induced wrong verdicts. Expected: nonzero susceptibility on all three channels.
- CoT effect per channel: measure reason-then-verdict susceptibility on the same probes. Measure: change in per-channel susceptibility versus direct verdict. Expected: heterogeneous, CoT may reduce position bias but do little for verbosity or even rationalize authority deference.
- Rationalization audit: on items where CoT keeps the biased verdict, inspect the written reasoning. Measure: fraction of reasonings that explicitly justify the biased choice (post-hoc rationalization signature). Expected: a detectable share where CoT manufactures justification rather than catching the manipulation.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

If we show whether reason-before-verdict actually de-biases overseers or merely rationalizes manipulation across distinct exploit channels, then protocol designers stop treating CoT judging as a free safety fix and apply it only where it helps. This matters because if a widely-recommended cheap intervention silently fails (or worsens exploitability), deployed oversight pipelines inherit a false sense of robustness, letting a strategic system exploit the very biases CoT was assumed to remove, a deception/loss-of-control pathway.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it stress-tests a near-universal, free-seeming oversight recommendation with a tightly scoped per-channel comparison, reusing simple bias probes on open models with no lab infrastructure. The result, whether forced reasoning de-biases or rationalizes, is directly actionable for protocol design outside labs and fully reproducible by a beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Reframe as a rationalization-detection study: can the judge's own written reasoning be classified into genuine error-catching vs post-hoc justification, and does that predict residual bias?

# Cited Sources

- Self-preference / structured-judging work (arXiv:2410.21819 and follow-ups) — reports that structured judging can reduce bias without retraining, motivating a test of whether plain reason-before-verdict CoT does the same or rationalizes.
- LLM-judge bias survey (arXiv:2410.02736) — catalogs the verbosity/authority/position exploit channels reused as probes here.
