# Consistency Cracks: Detecting Deception via Cross-Question Contradiction

# Research Question

When an open model is instructed to conceal a known fact, does probing the secret from many indirect angles produce a higher contradiction rate than an honest baseline, and can a consistency-based score separate concealing from honest behavior with no ground-truth labels at probe time?

# Approach Outline

Design a small set of toy scenarios, each with a single known secret fact and a roster of ~15 indirect probe questions/rephrasings that bear on that fact without asking it directly. Run two conditions on an open instruct model: a concealing condition (instructed to hide the secret) and an honest baseline. For each condition, collect answers to all probes, then use an LLM-judge plus spot manual checks to count cross-question contradictions (mutually inconsistent claims). Define a consistency-based deception score (contradiction rate) and measure its separation between conditions across several secrets/seeds. API/single-GPU inference only.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build one scenario and its probe set; run honest and concealing conditions and manually count contradictions on the outputs. Measure: contradiction count per condition and judge-vs-human agreement. Expected: concealing condition shows more contradictions; judge agrees with humans (>0.7).
- Scale to ~5 secrets x several seeds in both conditions. Measure: contradiction-rate distribution per condition. Expected: concealing runs have systematically higher contradiction rates.
- Build and evaluate the detector: threshold the contradiction-rate score and measure separation between honest and concealing runs. Measure: AUC / detection rate of the consistency score. Expected: meaningful separation (AUC > 0.7), showing consistency probing flags concealment without ground-truth labels.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

If a consistency-probe score separates concealment from honesty, then third parties with only API access and no deployment-time labels gain a cheap monitor for covert deception -> a model that lies to advance a goal can be caught when it fails to maintain a coherent false story under multi-angle probing -> the scheming/strategic-deception pathway, where a misaligned model conceals its aims or capabilities, gains a lab-independent detection primitive that does not require interpretability access.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual-value gold: API-only, no ground-truth labels needed at deployment, and squarely the kind of independent monitoring labs deprioritize. Narrow scope gives a single deliverable -- a consistency-based deception score and its separation -- and the conceal-a-secret toy setup is pure prompting, comfortably executable by a beginner team in 30 hours.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a self-consistency monitor: rather than a one-off detector, design it as a continuous deployment-time check that samples redundant probes and flags rising contradiction rates.
