# Defense-in-Depth Layer Independence

# Research Question

When several cheap LLM monitors are stacked in a defense-in-depth control protocol, do their misses occur independently, or do they fail on the same attacks? If misses are correlated, the true stacked false-negative rate is much higher than the independence assumption predicts.

# Approach Outline

Treat each monitor as a binary classifier over the same set of attack trajectories and import common-cause failure analysis from reliability engineering. Obtain a public set of malign control trajectories (e.g. SHADE-Arena or ControlArena transcripts), run 3-5 distinct cheap monitors over each one (different open models and/or different prompt framings), and record a per-trajectory hit/miss matrix. Compute pairwise miss-correlation (phi coefficient / Q-statistic) and compare the empirically observed stacked FNR against the FNR a naive independence assumption would predict. The whole pipeline is inference-plus-statistics on existing transcripts, with no training and no proprietary infrastructure, so a beginner team can complete it in ~30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Build the hit/miss matrix: run 3 cheap monitors (e.g. two small open models plus one with a reworded prompt) over ~50-100 known-malign trajectories at a fixed audit threshold, recording for each trajectory whether each monitor caught it. Measure: per-monitor FNR. Expected outcome: each monitor individually misses a meaningful fraction (20-40%) of attacks.
- Compute miss correlation: for each pair of monitors compute the phi coefficient on their miss indicators and visualize the miss-overlap as a heatmap. Measure: pairwise correlation of misses and the fraction of attacks missed by ALL monitors simultaneously. Expected outcome: positive correlation (monitors tend to miss the same 'hard' attacks), not zero as independence assumes.
- Naive-vs-true stacked FNR: predict stacked FNR under the independence assumption (product of individual FNRs) and compare to the observed all-miss fraction. Measure: ratio of true stacked FNR to independence-predicted FNR. Expected outcome: the true stacked FNR is several times higher than the independence prediction, quantifying how much defense-in-depth over-promises.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

If monitor misses are strongly correlated, then stacking monitors buys far less safety than a safety case assumes, so control designers who rely on the independence assumption will deploy systems they wrongly believe are robust. Surfacing and quantifying this correlation lets them either add genuinely decorrelated layers or discount their safety margin, which directly improves the soundness of safety cases gating the deployment of untrusted, potentially scheming models that could otherwise execute undetected sabotage at scale.

# Strength Rationale

The idea scores top marks on counterfactual value and narrow scope: it is a cheap meta-analysis over already-public trajectories needing no training, no proprietary data, and no lab-internal infrastructure, yet it probes a load-bearing assumption (layer independence) that underpins essentially every layered control safety case and is rarely tested publicly. Its deliverable, a measured ratio of true-to-assumed stacked FNR, is concrete and self-contained.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame it as a reliability-engineering audit of AI control: import the 'common-cause failure' literature wholesale and present the monitor stack as a redundant safety system whose beta-factor (common-cause fraction) is being estimated.
