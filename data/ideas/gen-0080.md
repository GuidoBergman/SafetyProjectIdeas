# Cross-Model Autograder Bias Audit Using Safety Evaluation Prompts

# Research Question

To what extent does llm judges used for safety evaluation show systematic family-bias — they score outputs from the same model family higher. this inflates safety scores for models evaluated by their own family's judge?

# Approach Outline

Select 3 open-source judge models (e.g., LLaMA-3-based, Mistral-based, Qwen-based) and have each grade the same set of 100–200 safety-relevant model outputs drawn from AILuminate or TrustLLM. Compute inter-judge agreement (Cohen's Kappa) and measure whether each judge systematically favors its own family. Visualize score distributions and report a 'judge bias score'.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Safety evaluation pipelines often use a single LLM judge. If that judge is biased, the entire evaluation is compromised. Documenting this for safety-specific prompts is more urgent than documenting it for general tasks.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: documenting family bias in safety judges motivates cross-family evaluation protocols. Direct practical impact on how safety evaluations are conducted.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Running 3 models as judges on the same dataset and computing agreement statistics is straightforward. Uses standard Python and statistical libraries accessible to beginners with guidance.

narrow_scope (5/5): Tightly scoped single experiment: 3 judges, one dataset, compute agreement metrics and bias scores. Very well-defined deliverable easily achievable within 30 hours.

novelty (3/5): LLM-as-judge bias is well-studied. The paper 'Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge' identified 12 key bias types and proposed the CALM framework. BiasScope automates bias detection. Research confirms family bias exists (models favor outputs with lower perplexity under their own policy). However, the specific focus on safety evaluation prompts (rather than general tasks) has not been the subject of a dedicated study. The idea of computing a 'judge bias score' specifically for safety-relevant outputs and using Cohen's Kappa for inter-judge agreement in the safety domain provides partial novelty.
