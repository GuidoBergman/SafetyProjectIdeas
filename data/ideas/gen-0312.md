# Transparency Theater + Misaligned Incentives: When More Documentation Means Less Safety

# Research Question

To what extent does compounding risk: governance failure (standards require documentation but not quality) + technical failure (documentation is produced for compliance rather than safety). together these produce an il?

# Approach Outline

Measure the correlation between documentation quantity (page count, indicator count, number of model card fields filled) and documentation quality (assessed by a rubric for specificity, testability, and actionability) across a sample of AI safety reports. Test whether quantity predicts quality or whether they are decoupled.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If documentation quantity and quality are decoupled, governance standards that require only documentation volume create compliance without safety—a governance design failure.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: directly tests whether governance standards requiring documentation volume create compliance without safety. A decoupled finding would be an actionable critique of current governance design.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Guided: rubric-based coding of documents for specificity, testability, and actionability is accessible. CS students can apply a well-defined rubric to safety reports with moderate training.

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused deliverable: sample of AI safety reports, count quantity metrics, apply quality rubric, compute correlation. Clear methodology with bounded inputs and outputs.

novelty (3/5): The concept of transparency without genuine safety is discussed in several sources. 'The Explainability Illusion: Why AI Transparency Requirements Miss the Point' (National Law Review, 2025) argues organizations create the appearance of transparency without understanding. 'A Trustworthy AI Reality-Check: The Lack of Transparency of AI Products in Healthcare' (Frontiers in Digital Health, 2024) found major documentation gaps in ethical considerations and safety monitoring. Medium's 'Why Most AI Safety Policies Fail in Practice' (2026) addresses compliance without actual safety. However, the specific empirical approach of sampling AI safety reports and correlating documentation volume metrics with quality rubric scores (specificity, testability, actionability) to test whether quantity is decoupled from quality has not been done. This quantitative test of the hypothesis is a novel contribution.
