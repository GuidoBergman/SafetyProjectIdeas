# Persona Assignment + Sycophancy: Do Expert Personas Amplify False Validation?

# Research Question

To what extent does ssigning an expert persona may both reduce transparency (self-transparency failures paper) and increase sycophancy (model maintains persona coherence by agreeing with the user who assigned the person?

# Approach Outline

Test whether models with expert persona assignments show higher false-premise capitulation rates than baseline models on the same questions. Measure whether the persona-sycophancy interaction is stronger for high-status personas.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up the experimental framework using a small, publicly available model. Replicate the core measurement from existing work to establish a baseline. Expected: reproduce known results within 10% margin. Time: ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core test): Apply the methodology to the target scenario described in the research question. Measure the primary metric and compare against the baseline. Expected: quantitative evidence for or against the hypothesis. Time: ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters (model size, dataset, hyperparameters). Run at least 2 variations to check robustness. Expected: characterize when findings hold and when they don't. Time: ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Expert personas are common in deployed AI. If persona assignment systematically increases sycophancy, that is a pervasive compound risk across many real-world deployments.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Clear chain: if expert persona assignment increases false-premise capitulation, that is a pervasive deployment risk since expert personas are widely used. Directly actionable for deployment guidelines.

accessible_complexity (5/5): Assigning personas via system prompt and measuring capitulation rates on false-premise questions is simple API work. Comparing baseline vs. persona conditions is basic A/B testing. Trivially accessible to CS beginners.

narrow_scope (4/5): Tight experiment: same false-premise questions with and without persona assignment, across a few persona types. Clear deliverable: capitulation rate comparison. Easily achievable in 30 hours.

novelty (2/5): This intersection has been substantially explored. 'Self-Transparency Failures in Expert-Persona LLMs' (arXiv:2511.21569, 2025) directly audits whether expert personas override honest disclosure, finding that models do not learn principled disclosure behavior across professional contexts. 'Persona Vectors' (arXiv:2507.21509, 2025) identifies linear directions in activation space corresponding to sycophancy and can steer them per-persona. 'When helpfulness backfires' (Nature npj Digital Medicine, 2025) demonstrates up to 100% compliance with illogical requests in medical expert personas. 'Sycophancy Is Not One Thing' (arXiv:2509.21305, EMNLP 2025) causally separates sycophantic agreement from praise, showing persona-independent mechanisms. The specific A/B comparison of capitulation rates with vs. without persona assignment is a narrow remaining gap, but the core finding — that expert personas amplify false validation — is already established.
