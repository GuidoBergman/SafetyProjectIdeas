# Consistency of Safety Across Decoding Parameters: Temperature, Top-p, and Repetition Penalty

# Research Question

To what extent does qi et al. identified decoding parameter attacks as a vulnerability where shallow alignment is bypassed by adjusting sampling parameters. the sensitivity of safety behavior to decoding parameters acros?

# Approach Outline

Evaluate attack success rate on StrongREJECT prompts across a grid of decoding parameters: temperature (0.1 to 2.0), top-p (0.5 to 1.0), repetition penalty (1.0 to 1.5) for 3-5 open-source instruction-tuned models. Identify parameter regions where safety collapses (high ASR) vs. is preserved. Test whether the collapse regions correlate with shallow alignment depth (measured by prefilling attacks).

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

Many applications expose decoding parameters to users or use non-default settings. If safety collapses at commonly used high-temperature settings, this is a practical deployment risk with an easy fix (constrain parameter ranges).

# Strength Rationale

narrow_scope (4/5): Focused deliverable: a heatmap of ASR across parameter grid for each model. Can be scoped to 2-3 models. Grid search is systematic and automatable. Fits well in 30 hours.

novelty (2/5): This idea is largely addressed by 'Catastrophic Jailbreak of Open-source LLMs via Exploiting Generation' (Huang et al., ICLR 2024), which demonstrated that varying decoding hyper-parameters (temperature, top-p, top-k sampling) increases attack success rate from 0% to 95%+ across 11 language models. The paper systematically sweeps generation configurations and measures safety degradation, which is essentially the proposed experiment. SafeDecoding (ACL 2024) provides the defense counterpart. The proposed heatmap deliverable would replicate existing findings with minor extensions (repetition penalty parameter, updated models).
