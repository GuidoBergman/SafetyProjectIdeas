# StrongReject Evaluation of Standard Jailbreaks on Sub-2B Parameter Models

# Research Question

Safety alignment research focuses on 7B+ parameter models. Sub-2B models (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct) are deployed on-device. Their safety behavior under standard jailbreak attacks has not been systematically benchmarked.

# Approach Outline

Run prefilling, adversarial suffix, and role-play jailbreaks against Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct and SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct. Score outputs using the StrongReject automated evaluator. Compare ASR to published results for 7B models. Characterize whether refusals exist and whether they follow the shallow-alignment pattern.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If tiny models have no meaningful alignment, deploying them in sensitive on-device applications is riskier than currently understood. If they show surprising robustness, that challenges assumptions about scale and safety.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong causal chain: sub-2B models are deployed on-device in sensitive settings, and documenting whether they have meaningful safety alignment directly informs deployment risk assessments. The two outcomes (no alignment or surprising robustness) both yield actionable knowledge.

accessible_complexity (4/5): Sub-2B models run easily on consumer hardware. StrongReject evaluator is open-source and automated. Standard jailbreak attacks (prefilling, adversarial suffix, role-play) have published implementations. Beginner CS students can follow existing code with guidance.

narrow_scope (5/5): Tightly scoped: run 3 well-defined attack types on exactly 2 small models, score with an existing automated evaluator, compare to published 7B baselines. Clear deliverable within 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): StrongREJECT and jailbreak benchmarking have been applied to 7B+ models extensively, but systematic jailbreak evaluation of sub-2B parameter models using standardized benchmarks is not well-covered. The EASE paper addresses small model alignment but not jailbreak vulnerability evaluation. This fills a gap for on-device deployment safety assessment.
