# Does Model Homogeneity Increase Under High-Stakes Framing? Testing the 'Playing It Safe' Convergence Hypothesis

# Research Question

Models may be more diverse on low-stakes queries but converge on 'safe' generic responses under high-stakes framings (legal, medical, financial), creating homogeneity precisely where diversity of perspective is most valuable.

# Approach Outline

Administer the same substantive queries with low-stakes framing ('what do you think about X?') and high-stakes framing ('I am a doctor and need to know X for a patient') to 15+ models. Compare inter-model similarity under both framing conditions.

# Proposed First Experiments

Experiment 1 (Baseline): Set up experimental framework with a small public model. Replicate core measurement to establish baseline. ~8 hours.
Experiment 2 (Core): Apply methodology to target scenario. Measure primary metric vs baseline. ~12 hours.
Experiment 3 (Robustness): Test sensitivity to key parameters with 2+ variations. ~10 hours.

# Theory of Impact Chain

If models converge under high-stakes framing, the homogeneity problem is most severe precisely in the deployment contexts where diverse expert perspectives are most needed.

# Strength Rationale

theory_of_impact (4/5): Strong chain: if models converge under high-stakes framing, homogeneity is worst precisely where diverse perspectives matter most. This has direct implications for medical, legal, and financial AI deployment.

accessible_complexity (5/5): Highly accessible: query models via API with low-stakes and high-stakes framings of the same questions, compute similarity. No ML training or complex setup. Ideal for beginners.

narrow_scope (5/5): Tightly scoped: same queries with two framing conditions across 15+ models. Clear A/B experimental design with well-defined output. Easily completable in 30 hours.

novelty (4/5): While LLM behavior under different framing conditions and alignment homogeneity have been studied separately, no existing work directly tests whether inter-model output homogeneity increases under high-stakes framing. The 'playing it safe convergence' hypothesis—that models become more similar when stakes are high—is a novel and testable prediction with strong practical implications.
