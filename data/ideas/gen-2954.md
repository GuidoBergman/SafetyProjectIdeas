# The Prompt-Order Link: Does Item Ordering Leak Eval-Frame and Shift Safety Scores?

# Research Question

Does the presentation structure of an evaluation, specifically batching identical safety items back-to-back versus embedding them in a natural conversation, by itself leak that 'this is a benchmark' and shift a model's refusal behavior and stated test-belief, independent of item content?

# Approach Outline

Take a fixed set of safety items (e.g. refusal probes and safety MCQs) and present the exact same items to one open model under two conditions: (a) interleaved within a natural-looking multi-turn conversation, and (b) as an obvious back-to-back benchmark batch. Hold item content constant so only presentation structure varies. Measure refusal rate, answer distribution, and verbalized test-belief ('do you think you are being tested? 0-100%') per condition. Use paired statistics to isolate the ordering/batching effect. Pure inference on a single small open model; no training, a few GPU-hours, comfortably within 30 hours including a blog post.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Paired presentation contrast: run the same 60-80 safety items in batched vs interleaved form, measure refusal rate and verbalized test-belief in each. Expected outcome: the batched condition shows higher refusal and/or higher stated test-belief than the interleaved condition.
- Content control: shuffle item order and vary the surrounding filler in the interleaved condition to confirm the effect tracks structure, not specific neighboring items. Measure variance of the effect across filler variants. Expected outcome: the batched-vs-interleaved gap is stable across filler, isolating presentation structure as the cause.
- Mitigation demo: apply a simple zero-cost fix (randomize order + embed each item in conversational filler) and re-measure refusal/test-belief against the batched baseline. Expected outcome: the embedded condition moves scores toward the interleaved baseline, demonstrating a concrete, free benchmark-design improvement.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

If batching structure alone leaks the eval frame, then current safety benchmarks systematically mis-measure deployed behavior because models behave differently when they detect a test. Showing that a free design change (randomize/embed items) reduces this leakage improves eval validity, which underpins the long-horizon pathway where trustworthy evaluations are the gate that catches dangerous behavior before deployment; invalid evals let unsafe models pass.

# Strength Rationale

Counterfactual value is 5: pure inference on open models, and eval-validity work explicitly benefits from independent external scrutiny rather than lab self-assessment. Narrow scope is 5: a single, content-controlled comparison with a clean refusal/test-belief delta. It is beginner-feasible, low-compute, and yields an immediately actionable benchmark-design recommendation.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a benchmark-hygiene audit: quantify how much of a published safety benchmark's score is attributable to batched presentation rather than content.
