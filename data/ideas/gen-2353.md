# Self-Play Debate Training-Free Replication: Does Adversarial Pressure Alone Sharpen Judges?

# Research Question

How much of the judge-accuracy gain that self-play debate training produces can be recovered with prompting alone? Specifically, does escalating inference-time adversarial structure (rebuttal rounds, forced strongest-counterargument prompts) improve a weak judge's accuracy, and where do the returns saturate?

# Approach Outline

On QuALITY-style reading-comprehension items (where ground truth is known), set up a weak judge reading debater arguments produced by a stronger open model. Compare escalating no-training adversarial conditions: (1) single statement from one side, (2) one rebuttal round, (3) multi-round debate with prompts that force each debater to give its strongest counterargument. For each condition, measure judge accuracy against ground truth. Estimate the marginal accuracy gain per added adversarial round and identify where it plateaus. This is pure prompting/orchestration on open models — single GPU, inference-only, fits 30 hours.

# Proposed First Experiments

- Baseline vs single-rebuttal: on ~150 QuALITY items, measure weak-judge accuracy with a single statement vs one rebuttal round. Measure: judge accuracy in each condition. Expected outcome: a measurable accuracy bump from adding one rebuttal.
- Escalating rounds: extend to 2-3 debate rounds with forced strongest-counterargument prompts and record accuracy at each depth. Measure: judge accuracy as a function of number of adversarial rounds. Expected outcome: diminishing returns, with most of the gain captured by the first 1-2 rounds.
- Comparison to reported training gains: compare the best prompting-only accuracy to the self-play-trained gains reported in the literature on a comparable setup. Measure: fraction of the trained gain recovered by prompting alone. Expected outcome: prompting recovers a substantial but partial share (e.g. half), quantifying what low-resource teams can achieve.

# Theory Of Impact Chain

Debate is a leading scalable-oversight protocol, but if its benefits require expensive self-play RL, most of the safety community cannot study or use it. If prompting-only adversarial structure recovers much of the judge-accuracy gain, then independent groups can study, stress-test, and deploy debate-style oversight without lab-scale training, accelerating community work on whether weak overseers can reliably supervise stronger models — the core mechanism we'd rely on to catch a superhuman model arguing for an unsafe action.

# Strength Rationale

Scores 5 on counterfactual value, narrow scope, accessible complexity, and low compute: it isolates a single causal link (does inference-time adversarial pressure alone sharpen judges?) using only prompting on open models, no training and no lab infra. Its counterfactual value is high because it directly tells under-resourced researchers how much of a key oversight result is reachable without the lab-scale RL barrier, and the scope is a clean dose-response measurement with ground-truth success criteria.

# Alternative Framings

- Frame as a cost-effectiveness curve: plot judge accuracy against inference cost (rounds x tokens) to give practitioners an explicit accuracy-per-dollar guide for prompting-only debate.
